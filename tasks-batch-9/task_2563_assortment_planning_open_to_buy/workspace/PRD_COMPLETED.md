# Assortment Planning & Open-to-Buy API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retail merchandise planning fails in the gap between a financial target and a purchase order. A department is handed an annual sales, margin and turn goal; a category merchant must turn that goal into a seasonal receipt budget, spread it across fiscal months, split it across good-better-best price tiers and store clusters, decide how many distinct options to carry and how deep to buy each one, and then place vendor orders that respect lead times, minimums and capacity — all while the season is already selling and the numbers are moving underneath them. Today that reconciliation happens in spreadsheets that nobody can audit, with the result that buyers overspend months that were already overbought, chase decisions get approved by the people who proposed them, and localized assortment overlays quietly double-count.

This document specifies the Assortment Planning & Open-to-Buy API, a server that holds the entire planning chain as one governed, arithmetically closed system. Every collection is mounted under the `/otb` prefix. The system owns the reference spine (departments, categories, store clusters, vendors and vendor authorizations), the seasonal plan hierarchy (category plans, fiscal plan months, breadth-and-depth option plans, size curves and assortment items), the money that flows out of it (receipt commitments and their receipts), and the in-season governance loop (weekly actuals, variance verdicts, reforecast cycles, and chase-or-cancel decisions).

Two design commitments shape the whole specification. First, **every derived number is the server's to compute, never the client's to assert**: receipt budgets, markdown reserves, monthly open-to-buy, option slot counts, size allocations, sell-through forecasts, localization multipliers, posture gates and variance verdicts are all computed from pinned formulas with pinned evaluation order and pinned rounding, so that two implementations of this document produce the same cents. Second, **spending authority is always segregated and always gated**: the operator who submits a plan may not approve it, the operator who proposes an in-season correction may not adjudicate it, a supplier may only touch its own orders, and a plan whose open-to-buy has been consumed or whose sales have broken plan freezes itself against further buying until a governance step reopens it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API: authentication, response shape, pagination, audit fields, arithmetic conventions, the logical clock, resolution order, the validation precedence ladder, concurrency control, and the cascade and conservation invariants that tie the entities together.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, empty or holds only whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). If the role is declared but is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403). These two checks run before anything else in the system, in that order.

The five roles are `MerchOpsAdmin`, `MerchandisePlanner`, `PlanningDirector`, `CategoryBuyer`, and `VendorRep`.

A `MerchOpsAdmin` is the merchandise systems operator. It maintains the reference spine — departments, categories, store clusters, vendors and vendor authorizations — posts the weekly actuals that drive in-season reforecasting, and drives the server's logical clock. A `MerchandisePlanner` builds the plan: category plans, fiscal months, option plans and size curves, localization overlays, requested reforecast cycles, and proposed chase-or-cancel decisions; it may never approve its own work. A `PlanningDirector` is the financial gatekeeper: it approves, rejects and closes plans, activates localization overlays, adjudicates chase-or-cancel decisions, and closes reforecast cycles. A `CategoryBuyer` spends the open-to-buy: it adopts, activates and closes assortment items, and creates, commits and cancels receipt commitments. A `VendorRep` is the supplier-side actor: it marks committed orders as shipped and posts received units. Every role may read: all four planning roles are authorized on every read endpoint, and a `VendorRep` may additionally read vendors, individual receipt commitments and the receipt-commitment list.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the distinction matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `MerchOpsAdmin`, `MerchandisePlanner`, `PlanningDirector` or `CategoryBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition captures an actor — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a category plan, and as `proposed_by_user_id` on a chase-or-cancel decision — and compares it only to other recorded operator identifiers, never to any row's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` row; ownership checks on a receipt commitment compare the commitment's `vendor_id` field to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch on either the ship or the receipt-posting operation is rejected with `ERR_NOT_COMMITMENT_VENDOR` (403).

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing recorded operator identifiers. Approving or rejecting a submitted category plan requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the plan's recorded `submitted_by_user_id`; approving or rejecting a chase-or-cancel decision requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the decision's recorded `proposed_by_user_id`. Any of those four operations attempted by the originator is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the host's real UTC wall clock and never from the logical clock of §2.6.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Two transport-level rejections use this same envelope with a status other than the one §5 lists for `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a request whose path matches no declared route is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under **404**, and a request whose method is not declared for an otherwise existing path is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under **405**. Every other `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carries the 400 of §5.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer of at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative or non-integer `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Query parameters other than `limit`, `offset` and the filters a given list endpoint declares are ignored rather than rejected.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending; because `created_at` is only millisecond-resolution, rows sharing one timestamp are ordered by ascending server insertion sequence, so the effective ordering is always the order in which the rows were created. Three lists override that default because their domain ordering is the one planners read them in: the plan-month list is ordered by `month_index` ascending — and because that list spans every plan rather than one, two months of different plans may share an index, in which case they are ordered by ascending server insertion sequence — the size-curve-cell list of an option plan is ordered by `size_sort_order` ascending, and the weekly actuals list of a plan month is ordered by `week_index` ascending; the latter two keys are unique inside the single parent each of those lists is scoped to, so neither ever needs that tie-break.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Version Semantics

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits (for example `2027-02-14T09:00:00.000Z`). Both `created_at` and the `updated_at` described below are read from the host's real UTC wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.6, which governs only the temporal tests §2.6 names and the fields those tests explicitly stamp. Clients never supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version`; any attempt to send one is an unknown body field and is rejected as an invalid request.

Most resources — departments, categories, store clusters, vendors, vendor authorizations, category plans, plan months, option plans, assortment items, receipt commitments, reforecast cycles, chase-or-cancel decisions and localization overlays — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A mutation includes the rewriting of a purely derived field: when the server recomputes a month's open-to-buy figures or a plan's posture, that is a mutation of the month and of the plan, and each affected row's `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly.

Two resources are append-only records rather than governed rows, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them at all. Those two are the size-curve cell (a row of an option plan's size curve, replaced wholesale rather than edited) and the weekly actuals post (an immutable record of one week's trading).

Because several operations mutate more than one row, a single request frequently advances several `version` counters. Where a cascade touches the same row twice — the approval of a category plan writes the plan's budget and then, later in the same cascade, its posture — the counter advances once per write, so the plan's `version` rises by 2 across that request. Each such multi-write case is called out explicitly in §3.

### 2.5. Integer Money, Basis Points and the Rounding Convention

No floating-point money is ever stored or returned. Every monetary field in the system is an integer count of cents, and every rate, share, index, margin and multiplier is an integer count of basis points, where 10,000 basis points is exactly one whole (100%). Inventory turn is likewise carried as an integer scaled by one hundred, so a target of 3.50 turns is stored as 350.

Wherever a formula in §3 calls for rounding, the method is **round half up away from zero**: a rational value is taken to the nearest integer, and a value ending in exactly one half is taken away from zero. So 5469.5 becomes 5470 and −1166.5 becomes −1167. Rounding is applied at exactly the points each formula names and nowhere else; collapsing two named rounding steps into one, or deferring a named rounding to the end of a chain, produces a different and incorrect answer. Where a formula calls for a floor instead, the value is truncated toward zero, which is how a partially funded option or a partially funded unit is discarded rather than acquired.

One conversion recurs throughout the system and is stated once here: **retail dollars are converted to cost dollars by retaining the complement of the plan's planned margin.** The cost equivalent of a retail figure is that figure multiplied by ten thousand less `planned_margin_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. Every retail-to-cost conversion in the system — the seasonal cost of goods, a month's planned cost of sales, and the projected and actual cost of sales during the season — uses the owning category plan's `planned_margin_bps` in exactly this way.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Every temporal test in this system — vendor lead-time feasibility, cancellation cutoffs, overlay effective windows, chase-window bucketing — reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any client has moved it, every temporal comparison uses that instant, and the server never falls back to real time.

A `MerchOpsAdmin` may reposition the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a single `to_iso8601` instant and responds with the resulting `current_utc` reading and an `expired_overlay_count`. The clock may be set to any instant, later or earlier than its current reading; a backwards reposition is accepted and is never refused on the grounds of direction, and it un-expires nothing. Every ISO-8601 instant the API accepts — `to_iso8601` here and every dated body field elsewhere — is normalized to UTC on the way in: a value carrying an explicit offset is converted, a value carrying none is read as already UTC, and a date-only value is read as midnight UTC. Repositioning the clock has one governed side effect: every `ACTIVE` localization overlay whose `effective_end` now lies strictly in the past transitions to `EXPIRED`, and the assortment items that were carrying it have their localization multiplier and sell-through forecast recomputed without it. `expired_overlay_count` reports how many overlays that request expired.

### 2.7. Path and Payload Resolution Order

Every collection in this API is mounted directly beneath `/otb`; no resource is addressed through a nested path, and every link between rows is an explicit foreign-key field carried in the request body. Path placeholders are therefore always a single `{id}`. A trailing slash is insignificant: any path is matched identically with or without one.

Identifier resolution runs in a fixed order. Path identifiers are resolved first, left to right, and the first one that matches no row decides the response with that entity's own not-found code: `ERR_DEPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_CLUSTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATEGORY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_MONTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPTION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSORTMENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECEIPT_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFORECAST_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHASE_CANCEL_DECISION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LOCALIZATION_OVERLAY_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with 404. Body foreign keys are resolved next, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and again the first unresolvable one decides the response with the corresponding not-found code. A list filter, by contrast, is never resolved: its value is compared verbatim against the stored field, so a filter naming an identifier that matches no row returns an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a not-found error.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule could reject a request, the outcome is never ambiguous: the system evaluates rules **strictly in ascending priority order and the first rule that fails decides the response**. Implementers must not treat the checks below as an unordered set — a request that violates three of them returns the code of the lowest-numbered one. The complete ladder is:

1. Both authentication headers present with a declared role — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorized for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403), on every endpoint.
3. Request-body shape: every required field present, every value of its declared JSON type, every enum value a declared member, every integer within its declared range, every string within its declared length bounds, every ISO-8601 field parseable, and no unknown body field — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every POST and PUT endpoint. Three shape conventions are fixed here. A field present with a JSON `null` counts as absent: a required field so sent is rejected, an optional one takes its documented default. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose declared fields are all optional accepts it, while a body that fails to parse as JSON or parses to anything other than a JSON object is rejected here. And a JSON boolean never satisfies a field declared as an integer.
4. Pagination bounds — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400), on every list read.
5. Path identifiers resolve, left to right — the matching 404 not-found code, on every endpoint carrying a path `{id}`.
6. Body foreign keys resolve, in declared field order — the matching 404 not-found code, on every POST and PUT endpoint that carries foreign-key fields.
7. Vendor ownership: a `VendorRep` acts only on a commitment whose `vendor_id` equals its `X-User-ID` — `ERR_NOT_COMMITMENT_VENDOR` (403), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/ship` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/receipts`.
8. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), on `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/approve`, `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/reject`, `POST /otb/chase-cancel-decisions/{id}/approve` and `POST /otb/chase-cancel-decisions/{id}/reject`.
9. The target row currently sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), on every action endpoint: plan submit, approve, reject and close; month open-season; item adopt, activate and close; commitment commit, cancel, ship and receipt posting; decision approve and reject; overlay activate; and reforecast-cycle close.
10. The referenced plan month is in the status the operation requires — `ERR_PLAN_MONTH_NOT_OPEN` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit` (which require `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON`) and on `POST /otb/actuals-posts` (which requires `IN_SEASON`).
11. The option plan behind the item being adopted is `SLOTTED` — `ERR_OPTION_PLAN_NOT_SLOTTED` (409), on `POST /otb/assortment-items/{id}/adopt`.
12. The parent category plan is `APPROVED` — `ERR_CATEGORY_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` (409), on `POST /otb/plan-months/{id}/open-season` and `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/reforecasts`.
13. The commitment's vendor is `AUTHORIZED` — `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`.
14. No decision of the cycle is still `PROPOSED` — `ERR_OPEN_DECISIONS_REMAIN` (409), on `POST /otb/reforecast-cycles/{id}/close`.
15. The submitted optimistic-concurrency version matches the stored one — `ERR_STALE_PLAN_MONTH_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CATEGORY_PLAN_VERSION` (412), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/reforecasts` respectively.
16. The governing plan's `otb_posture` is not `FROZEN` — `ERR_OTB_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`. This gate is deliberately **not** applied to the commitments the server itself generates from an approved chase decision, which is the sanctioned in-season correction route.
17. When the governing plan's `otb_posture` is `RESTRICTED`, the item's `lifecycle_stage` is not `NEW` — `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED_NEW_ITEM` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`.
18. The plan's `reforecast_round` is below 3 before a new cycle opens — `ERR_REFORECAST_ROUND_LIMIT` (409), on `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/reforecasts`. The same cap governs the automatic breach-triggered cycle raised while posting weekly actuals, but there the cap is silently skipped rather than failed: the actuals post still succeeds and simply opens no cycle.
19. An `ACTIVE` vendor authorization joins the commitment's vendor to the category of the plan that owns the target month — `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_CATEGORY` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`.
20. Plan-scope agreement: the option plan behind the referenced assortment item and the referenced plan month belong to the same category plan, and the body's `vendor_id` equals the item's own `vendor_id` — `ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments`.
21. The overlay's cluster is actually planned: at least one option plan exists for the overlay's `category_plan_id` and `store_cluster_id` pair — `ERR_CLUSTER_NOT_IN_PLAN` (422), on `POST /otb/localization-overlays`.
22. A category plan is unique per `category_id`, `season_code` and `fiscal_year` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_PLAN` (409), on `POST /otb/category-plans`.
23. A plan month is unique per `category_plan_id` and `month_index` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_MONTH` (409), on `POST /otb/plan-months`.
24. An option plan is unique per `category_plan_id`, `store_cluster_id` and `tier` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPTION_PLAN` (409), on `POST /otb/option-plans`.
25. A weekly actuals post is unique per `plan_month_id` and `week_index` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTUALS_WEEK` (409), on `POST /otb/actuals-posts`.
26. The submitted `cells` array is non-empty — `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_EMPTY` (422), on `PUT /otb/option-plans/{id}/size-curve`.
27. The submitted cells' `pct_bps` values sum to exactly 10000, and `size_label` and `size_sort_order` are each unique within the submitted set — `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_NOT_BALANCED` (422), on `PUT /otb/option-plans/{id}/size-curve`.
28. No submitted cell resolves to zero `allocated_units` after per-cell rounding and remainder reconciliation — `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_ZERO_CELL` (422), on `PUT /otb/option-plans/{id}/size-curve`.
29. The option plan has at least one open slot — `ERR_NO_OPTION_SLOT` (409), on `POST /otb/assortment-items/{id}/adopt`.
30. The commitment's `units` is at least the vendor's `min_order_units` — `ERR_BELOW_VENDOR_MINIMUM` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments`.
31. Vendor capacity: the candidate passes both the overlapping-ship-window capacity test against `weekly_unit_capacity` and the category open-unit test against `max_open_units` — `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`.
32. The overlay's window, expanded by its mandatory separation buffer, does not collide with another overlay of the same type on the same cluster — `ERR_OVERLAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on `POST /otb/localization-overlays`.
33. `ship_window_end` is strictly after `ship_window_start` — `ERR_SHIP_WINDOW_INVERTED` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments`.
34. `ship_window_start` is no earlier than the logical clock plus the vendor's `lead_time_days` — `ERR_LEAD_TIME_INFEASIBLE` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments`.
35. The cancellation cutoff has not passed — `ERR_CANCEL_CUTOFF_PASSED` (409), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/cancel` when the commitment's `commit_status` is `COMMITTED`.
36. `effective_end` is greater than or equal to `effective_start` — `ERR_OVERLAY_WINDOW_INVERTED` (422), on `POST /otb/localization-overlays`.
37. The commitment's cost does not exceed the month's open balance — `ERR_OTB_EXCEEDED` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments` and `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/commit`.
38. The computed seasonal receipt budget is strictly greater than zero — `ERR_RECEIPT_BUDGET_ZERO` (422), on `POST /otb/category-plans/{id}/approve`.
39. When and only when the decision's `action` is `CHASE`, the chase-or-cancel allocation funds at least one unit — `ERR_NO_FEASIBLE_RECEIPT_MONTH` (422), on `POST /otb/chase-cancel-decisions/{id}/approve`.
40. `received_units_delta` is at least 1 and does not push cumulative receipts above the committed units — `ERR_RECEIPT_OVER_COMMITMENT` (422), on `POST /otb/receipt-commitments/{id}/receipts`.

The business meaning of each rule, and the exact arithmetic several of them depend on, is developed in §3; this ladder fixes only their relative precedence and their scope.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Month Budgets and Plan Governance

Exactly two write operations in this API carry an optimistic-concurrency version field, and on both of them the field is **required**, not optional.

Creating a receipt commitment must carry `expected_plan_month_version`, holding the `version` of the target plan month as the client last observed it. The server compares that value to the stored `version` of the plan month named by `plan_month_id`; if the two differ, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_MONTH_VERSION` (412). The rationale is fiscal: a buyer sizes a buy from the month's `otb_open_cents`, and any earlier write that consumed part of that month's open budget — another commitment reaching `COMMITTED`, a receipt being posted, an approved chase allocation landing units in the month — has already advanced the month's `version`, which means the buy the client computed no longer matches the budget the server holds. The check is purely a comparison of the submitted number against the stored number; it never inspects request timing.

Opening a reforecast cycle against a category plan must carry `expected_category_plan_version`, holding the `version` of the plan as the client last observed it. The server compares that value to the stored `version` of the addressed category plan; if the two differ, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CATEGORY_PLAN_VERSION` (412). A new cycle supersedes the governance state the client believed it was acting on, and a plan's `version` advances whenever a posture recomputation, a variance verdict, or an automatic breach-triggered cycle has already moved the plan forward.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Sending one anywhere else is an unknown body field and is rejected as an invalid request.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Consistency in this system is immediate: by the time a request returns, every derived field it touched has been rewritten. Two named cascades carry most of that weight, and both are described in full in §3 where their business context lives.

**Cascade B** runs when a submitted category plan is approved. Its five links fire in a fixed order, each caused by the one before it: the plan's own approval and receipt-budget pinning; then the promotion of every `DRAFT` plan month of that plan to `LOCKED_PLAN` with its markdown reserve and open-to-buy fields written; then the plan-level posture recomputation over the newly locked months; then the promotion of every `OPEN` option plan of that plan to `SLOTTED` with its slot counts written; and finally the cluster-and-size allocation that writes each slotted cell's per-option unit target and each of its size cells' unit numbers.

**Cascade A** runs when a `COMMITTED` receipt commitment is cancelled. Its four links likewise fire in order: the commitment releases its open cost; the owning plan month's committed and open figures move by the released amount; the owning category plan's open ratio and posture are recomputed; and if that recomputation leaves the plan in any posture other than frozen, previously deferred assortment items are restored to proposed status subject to their lifecycle stage.

Every link of either cascade is a mutation of the rows it touches, so `updated_at` and `version` advance on each of them per §2.4. A cascade never partially applies: if the triggering operation is accepted, all of its links are applied before the response is produced.

### 2.11. The Open-to-Buy Conservation Identity

Open-to-buy is a conserved quantity, and the system's fiscal correctness rests on one identity holding exactly on every plan month at the end of every request that touches it, the one exception being the rejection unwind of §3.4, which zeroes a month's planned figure without clearing its buckets: **a month's open open-to-buy, plus its committed open-to-buy, plus its received open-to-buy, equals its planned open-to-buy.** In field terms, `otb_open_cents` plus `otb_committed_cents` plus `otb_received_cents` equals `otb_planned_cents`.

Two consequences follow and are both normative. First, `otb_open_cents` is **never clamped at zero**: a month whose commitments and receipts exceed its plan carries a negative open balance, which is the honest representation of an overbought month and is what makes the identity exact rather than approximate. Second, money moving between the committed bucket and the received bucket — which is what happens when a vendor posts receipts — leaves the open balance untouched, because both buckets are subtracted from the same planned figure.

Every operation that could disturb the identity recomputes the whole month rather than adjusting a single field, and then recomputes the owning plan's posture from the refreshed months. §3.6 and §3.7 give the arithmetic.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Spine: Departments, Categories and Store Clusters

The server holds no data at all when it starts: nothing is seeded, preloaded or hardcoded, and every row in the system — reference spine included — is minted through a declared endpoint. Before any planning can happen, therefore, a `MerchOpsAdmin` establishes the reference data the plans hang from. A **Department** is created with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters that must be unique across all departments — the uppercase test, here and on the category and vendor `code` fields alike, is that the string is unchanged by uppercasing, so digits are permitted while any lowercase letter is rejected — and the `fiscal_year` between 2000 and 2100 that its financial targets belong to. Its three targets are the annual net sales target at retail in cents, `sales_target_cents`, which must be at least 1; the gross margin target in basis points of retail sales, `margin_target_bps`, between 0 and 9999; and the inventory turn target scaled by one hundred, `turn_target_x100`, which must be at least 1, so a 3.50-turn goal is submitted as 350. A duplicated department code violates the field's declared validity and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the same holds for any of the range bounds above.

A **Category** rolls up to exactly one department, named by `department_id` in the creation payload. It carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters that must be unique within its department rather than globally, and `sales_share_bps` between 1 and 10000, expressing the share of the department's sales target this category is expected to carry. The category's `role` is the merchandising posture that will later drive its receipt appetite, and it is one of `TRAFFIC_DRIVER`, `DESTINATION`, `CONVENIENCE`, `SEASONAL` or `OCCASIONAL`. Categories can be listed and, when listing, narrowed to a single `department_id` or a single `role`.

A **StoreCluster** groups stores that should receive the same assortment. It is created with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `climate_zone` of `COLD`, `TEMPERATE` or `HOT`, a `volume_tier` of `A`, `B` or `C`, a `store_count` of at least 1, and two productivity indices expressed in basis points against a chain average of 10000. `sales_index_bps`, between 3000 and 25000, is the cluster's sales productivity and drives both its share of the option-slot budget and its unit allocation. `demographic_index_bps`, between 5000 and 20000, is the cluster's demographic productivity and is the sole scaling factor applied to a carryover option's own sell-through history. Cluster listings can be narrowed by `climate_zone` or by `volume_tier`.

### 3.2. Vendor Onboarding, Authorization and Capacity Discipline

Vendors enter the system in an unusable state on purpose. A `MerchOpsAdmin` creates a **Vendor** with a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters unique across all vendors, and four supply-side parameters: `lead_time_days` between 1 and 365, which is the combined production and transit time used by every temporal feasibility test in the system; `weekly_unit_capacity` of at least 1, the maximum units the vendor can ship across all commitments whose ship windows overlap; `min_order_units` of at least 1, the smallest quantity accepted on a single commitment; and `cancel_cutoff_days` between 0 and 180, the notice a committed order requires before its ship window in order to be cancellable. The vendor's `status` is server-owned and is initialised to `PENDING`.

The vendor status endpoint accepts a target `status` and drives a three-state, cyclic lifecycle. From `PENDING` the vendor may be moved to `AUTHORIZED`, which simply flips the status and advances the vendor's version. An `AUTHORIZED` vendor may be moved to `SUSPENDED`, which additionally revokes every one of that vendor's authorizations: each vendor authorization whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is set to `REVOKED`. A `SUSPENDED` vendor may be reinstated to `AUTHORIZED`, and reinstatement is the exact mirror of suspension — every vendor authorization of that vendor whose `status` is `REVOKED` is restored to `ACTIVE`, so reinstatement returns precisely the authorizations the suspension took away. There is no direct move from `PENDING` to `SUSPENDED`; requesting a transition the vendor's current status does not declare is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. All three status names are accepted as body values, so a request naming `PENDING` — never a declared target from any status — or naming the vendor's own current status is refused as an undeclared transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than as a body-shape error. The vendor's `version` advances by 1 on every accepted transition, and each vendor authorization a suspension or a reinstatement flips advances its own `updated_at` and `version` too. Authorization matters because no receipt commitment against a vendor may ever reach `COMMITTED` unless that vendor's status is `AUTHORIZED` at that moment, and a commit attempted against a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` vendor is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

A **VendorAuthorization** is the many-to-many join that licenses one vendor to supply one category. It is created with `vendor_id`, `category_id` and `max_open_units` of at least 1, which is the ceiling on the units this vendor may hold open — neither received nor cancelled — for that category. Its `status` is server-owned, initialised to `ACTIVE`, and only ever `ACTIVE` or `REVOKED` thereafter, moving between the two only through the vendor's suspension and reinstatement described above. Creation itself is unconditional in two respects: the named vendor may be in any status, including `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED`, and the row is still created `ACTIVE`; and the `vendor_id`/`category_id` pair is not constrained to be unique, so the same vendor and category may be joined by more than one authorization row. Authorizations can be listed and narrowed by `vendor_id`, by `category_id` or by `status`.

Two capacity tests are evaluated together whenever a buyer proposes a commitment against a vendor and again whenever a proposed commitment is committed, and both report the same code, `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The first is a **ship-window capacity** test with aggregate semantics: a ship window runs from `ship_window_start` inclusive to `ship_window_end` exclusive, and two windows overlap when the existing window's start is strictly before the candidate's end and the candidate's start is strictly before the existing window's end. Windows that merely touch — where one ends exactly when the other starts — do not overlap and are always permitted. The system collects every other commitment of the same vendor whose `commit_status` is `PROPOSED` or `COMMITTED` and whose window overlaps the candidate's, sums each one's `units` less its `cancelled_units`, and rejects the candidate when that sum plus the candidate's own `units` exceeds the vendor's `weekly_unit_capacity`. Overlap alone is legal; it is only the summed load against capacity that is enforced. The second is a **category open-unit** test: the system sums, over every other commitment of the same vendor whose `commit_status` is `PROPOSED` or `COMMITTED` and whose month belongs to a plan in the authorization's category, that commitment's `units` less its `received_units` and less its `cancelled_units`, and rejects the candidate when that sum plus the candidate's `units` exceeds the authorization's `max_open_units`. On a commit, where the candidate is itself an already-stored `PROPOSED` row, that row is excluded from both sums, so its units are counted exactly once — through the candidate term. When more than one `ACTIVE` authorization joins that vendor to that category, the earliest-created one is the authorization whose `max_open_units` governs. Commitments that are `WITHDRAWN`, `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED` release their capacity and contribute nothing to either sum.

### 3.3. Authoring a Seasonal Category Plan

A `MerchandisePlanner` opens the planning season by creating a **CategoryPlan** for one category, naming `category_id`, a `season_code` of `SPRING`, `SUMMER`, `FALL` or `HOLIDAY`, and a `fiscal_year` between 2000 and 2100. Only one plan may exist for a given combination of those three values; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_PLAN`. The plan's own financial intent is carried by `planned_sales_cents`, the planned net sales at retail for the season in cents, at least 1; `planned_margin_bps`, the planned gross margin in basis points, between 0 and 9999, which is the margin every retail-to-cost conversion under this plan uses per §2.5; and `planned_turn_x100`, the planned inventory turn scaled by one hundred, at least 1.

Everything else on a new plan is server-owned and initialised without client input. `status` starts at `DRAFT`. `reforecast_round` starts at 0 and is capped at 3. `receipt_budget_cents` starts as null and stays null until the plan's first approval computes it. `otb_posture` starts at `OPEN`, `open_ratio_bps` at 10000, `variance_status` at `ON_PLAN` and `sales_variance_bps` at 0. Both actor records, `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id`, start null and are written by the submit and approve steps respectively. Plans can be listed and narrowed by `category_id` or by governance `status`.

The plan's money is spread over fiscal months. A **PlanMonth** is added with `category_plan_id`, a `month_index` between 1 and 12 that must be unique within the plan and is the sole ordering key for every waterfall in the system, and a `fiscal_month_label` of exactly seven characters in `YYYY-MM` form; that form test is purely syntactic — four digits, a hyphen and two digits — and no calendar validity is checked, so a label naming month 13 is accepted. A duplicate month index within one plan is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_MONTH`. `first_receipt_date` is the earliest date on which receipts may land in the month and is the anchor for every vendor lead-time feasibility test the month participates in. Four monetary inputs, each an integer count of cents of at least 0, describe the month: `bom_inventory_cents`, the beginning-of-month inventory at cost; `planned_sales_cents`, the planned net sales at retail; `planned_markdown_cents`, the planned markdown dollars at cost; and `planned_eom_cents`, the planned end-of-month inventory at cost. A new month is created in `DRAFT` with `active_basis` set to `PLANNED_BASIS` and with every computed open-to-buy figure — `provisional_sell_through_bps`, `reserve_rate_bps`, `markdown_reserve_cents`, `otb_planned_cents`, `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents` and `otb_open_cents` — at 0. Months may be added while the plan sits at any governance status; a month added after an approval starts in `DRAFT` and remains `DRAFT` until a subsequent approval locks it. Month listings can be narrowed by `category_plan_id` or by `status`.

Breadth and depth are planned per cell of a three-way grid. An **OptionPlan** is one such cell, created with `category_plan_id`, `store_cluster_id` and a `tier` of `GOOD`, `BETTER` or `BEST`; the triple must be unique, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPTION_PLAN`. `breadth_target`, between 1 and 200, is the merchant's requested ceiling on how many distinct options the cell may carry. `depth_units_per_option`, between 1 and 500, is how many units of each option are carried per store. `assumed_unit_cost_cents`, at least 1, is the planning cost per unit used to convert budget dollars into option slots. A new cell is created with `status` `OPEN`, `option_slots_assigned` at 0, and `option_slots_total`, `option_slots_open` and `target_units_per_option` all null. Cell listings can be narrowed by `category_plan_id`, `store_cluster_id`, `tier` or `status`.

A cell's **size curve** is submitted as a whole set, not edited cell by cell: the size-curve replacement operation takes a `cells` array, deletes every existing size-curve row of that option plan, and persists the submitted set in its place, advancing the option plan's own version by 1. It answers with the replacement set itself rather than with the option plan: an array of the newly persisted size-curve rows ordered by `size_sort_order` ascending, and, because this is not a list read, carrying no pagination fields in `meta`. Each submitted element carries a `size_label` of 1 to 12 characters, a `size_sort_order` between 1 and 99, and `pct_bps` between 1 and 10000 expressing that size's share of one option's units. Each element is validated as a body object in its own right, in array order and under priority 3 of §2.8: an element that is not a JSON object, or that carries any key other than those three, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the addressed option plan's `{id}` is even resolved, whereas the emptiness, balance and zero-cell checks below all run after it has resolved. An empty `cells` array is rejected with `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_EMPTY`. The submitted shares must sum to exactly 10000 basis points, and both `size_label` and `size_sort_order` must be unique within the submitted set; any of those three failures is rejected with `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_NOT_BALANCED`. `size_sort_order` is the ascending display order of the curve and is also the sole tie-break in the remainder reconciliation described next.

Submitting a curve immediately runs the **cluster-and-size allocation**, whose output is per-cell unit counts. The rule computes, first, the cell's total unit target for one option across the whole cluster: the option plan's `depth_units_per_option` multiplied by the cluster's `store_count`, multiplied by the cluster's `sales_index_bps` and divided by ten thousand, with the result rounded half up to a whole unit. Then, for each size row, it computes an unrounded demand in ten-thousandths of a unit: the exact demand for that size is `depth_units_per_option` times `store_count` times the cluster's `sales_index_bps` divided by ten thousand times the row's `pct_bps` divided by ten thousand; multiplying that exact demand by ten thousand and rounding half up to an integer gives the row's `raw_units_x10000`, which is retained on the row precisely so the remainder tie-break is auditable. The row's `allocated_units` is then `raw_units_x10000` divided by ten thousand and rounded half up to a whole unit. **The order is pinned: each size row is rounded on its own and only then are the rows summed.** Summing the raw demands and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and yields different per-size counts for the same curve.

Because per-row rounding does not in general reproduce the cell's total, a remainder reconciliation follows and is fully deterministic. The system sums every row's `allocated_units` and takes the difference between the cell's total unit target and that sum. Each row's fractional part is the remainder of `raw_units_x10000` divided by ten thousand. When the difference is negative — the rows collectively over-allocated — rows are visited in ascending order of fractional part, ties broken by ascending `size_sort_order`, and one unit is subtracted from each row visited. When the difference is positive, rows are visited in descending order of fractional part, ties again broken by ascending `size_sort_order`, and one unit is added to each row visited. If the magnitude of the difference exceeds the number of rows, the visit order is cycled through again from its start. No row identifier and no item identifier ever participates in the ordering. Should any row end this reconciliation with zero or fewer `allocated_units` — the subtraction pass carries no floor, so a thin row visited twice by a cycled walk can land below zero — its share is too thin to stock and the whole submission is rejected with `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_ZERO_CELL`, the check running across the whole reconciled set before any row's numbers are written anywhere.

One asymmetry between the two callers of this rule is deliberate and must be honoured. The size-curve replacement persists `raw_units_x10000` and `allocated_units` on every submitted row and uses the cell's total unit target only for the zero-row check and the remainder reconciliation; it does **not** write the option plan's `target_units_per_option`, which stays null for as long as the cell's `status` is `OPEN`. The slotting cascade described in §3.4 re-runs the identical formula and additionally persists `target_units_per_option`. The per-row numbers it re-derives are necessarily identical to the ones the replacement wrote, because the cell's total unit target depends only on the option plan's own depth and its store cluster and never on the plan's receipt budget.

### 3.4. Governance: Submission, Approval and the Approval Cascade

A plan leaves the planner's hands by being submitted, which advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and records the submitting operator's `X-User-ID` into `submitted_by_user_id`. Submission is refused unless the plan has at least one plan month and at least one option plan; an empty shell has nothing to budget. An optional `submission_note` may accompany the request. The same operation also serves the reforecast loop: a plan sitting in `REFORECAST_OPEN` advances `REFORECAST_OPEN → SUBMITTED` through it, and that path additionally requires that every reforecast cycle of the plan has reached `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, so a plan cannot be resubmitted while a cycle is still live. Submission from any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `PlanningDirector` may send a submitted plan back with a mandatory `rejection_reason`, moving it `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`. Rejection unwinds the previous approval's structural work: every plan month of the plan whose `status` is `LOCKED_PLAN` reverts `LOCKED_PLAN → DRAFT` and has `otb_planned_cents`, `otb_open_cents`, `markdown_reserve_cents`, `provisional_sell_through_bps` and `reserve_rate_bps` all reset to 0 — while `otb_committed_cents` and `otb_received_cents` are left exactly as they stand, so a month that had already drawn commitments before the plan re-entered the revision loop carries those buckets into `DRAFT` against a zeroed planned figure, and the conservation identity of §2.11 is restored on it only when a later approval re-locks it — and every option plan of the plan whose `status` is `SLOTTED` reverts `SLOTTED → OPEN` with `option_slots_total`, `option_slots_open` and `target_units_per_option` all reset to null, its `option_slots_assigned` untouched and the items holding those slots left in whatever status they had reached. A rejection recomputes neither posture nor variance: the plan's `open_ratio_bps`, `otb_posture`, `sales_variance_bps` and `variance_status` keep the values the last recomputation left them at, even though the months feeding the posture sums have just left the posture month set. The revision loop `SUBMITTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` may repeat without limit and never consumes a reforecast round.

Approval, also a `PlanningDirector` act and optionally carrying an `approval_note`, is where the plan's money is actually created. Before anything is written, the **seasonal receipt budget** is computed, and unless it resolves to strictly more than zero the approval is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_BUDGET_ZERO` and nothing changes — the gate tests the sign rather than equality with zero, so a figure of zero or below is refused alike. The computation reads exactly one category plan, its category, and that category's department — no month, option plan or commitment data participates — and proceeds in a pinned order. First, the season's cost of goods is the plan's `planned_sales_cents` converted from retail to cost by the plan's own margin per §2.5, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, a **role capacity** is that cost of goods multiplied by the receipt multiplier of the category's `role` and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up: a `TRAFFIC_DRIVER` category is allowed 11000 basis points of its cost of goods, a `DESTINATION` category 10000, a `CONVENIENCE` category 9000, a `SEASONAL` category 12500 and an `OCCASIONAL` category 8500. Third, a **turn capacity** is computed against a three-turn reference: the turn basis is the greater of the plan's `planned_turn_x100` and a floor of 200 (two turns), and the turn capacity is the cost of goods multiplied by 300 and divided by that turn basis, rounded half up — so a plan targeting fewer than two turns is treated as if it targeted exactly two. Fourth, a **department ceiling** is the department's `sales_target_cents` multiplied by 1200 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up: no single category may claim more than 12% of its department's annual sales target as receipts. Finally, `receipt_budget_cents` is the smaller of the department ceiling and the larger of the role capacity and the turn capacity. The inner comparison lets whichever of merchandising appetite and turn discipline is more generous win; the outer comparison then applies the department's hard ceiling. All four intermediates are rounded to whole cents before the comparisons run, and the comparisons themselves operate on those already-rounded integers.

Once the budget is positive, approval fires **cascade B**, whose five links run in order, each caused by the one before it.

**Link 1** sets the plan's `status` to `APPROVED`, records the approving operator's `X-User-ID` into `approved_by_user_id`, pins `receipt_budget_cents` to the figure just computed, and advances the plan's version by 1.

**Link 2** is caused by link 1 pinning the budget: every plan month of the plan whose `status` is `DRAFT` advances `DRAFT → LOCKED_PLAN`, and on each of them the markdown reserve is computed and then the month's open-to-buy is computed on the planned basis, writing `provisional_sell_through_bps`, `reserve_rate_bps`, `markdown_reserve_cents`, `otb_planned_cents`, `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents` and `otb_open_cents`. Each locked month's version advances by 1.

The **markdown reserve** is a three-step rule and its evaluation is deliberately pinned to a single pass. Step one produces a provisional sell-through. Under `PLANNED_BASIS` the month's cost of sales is its `planned_sales_cents` converted to cost by the plan's margin and rounded half up, and the inventory base is the authored `bom_inventory_cents`; under `PROJECTED_BASIS` the cost of sales is the projected cost of sales and the inventory base is the projected beginning-of-month inventory, both defined in §3.7. To either base the system adds the month's already-committed receipt cost — the sum, over the month's commitments whose `commit_status` is `COMMITTED` or `CLOSED`, of each one's `open_cost_cents` plus its `received_cost_cents`; commitments that are `PROPOSED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. The denominator is that total, or 1 if the total is smaller than 1. `provisional_sell_through_bps` is then ten thousand multiplied by the cost of sales, divided by that denominator, rounded half up. Step two selects a reserve rate from the provisional sell-through, with every band's lower edge inclusive: below 4000 basis points the rate is 900, from 4000 up to but not including 6500 it is 600, from 6500 up to but not including 8500 it is 350, and at 8500 or above it is 150. A slow month therefore reserves six times as much for markdowns as a fast one. Step three writes `markdown_reserve_cents` as the denominator multiplied by `reserve_rate_bps` and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. Rounding occurs at steps one and three only.

The single-pass pin deserves emphasis because the rule looks circular: the reserve feeds the month's planned open-to-buy, the planned open-to-buy bounds the receipts a buyer may place, and receipts feed the sell-through that selects the reserve rate. **The reserve is recomputed on exactly two events and no others** — when a month advances `DRAFT → LOCKED_PLAN`, and when a week of actuals is posted against the month. Committing a commitment, cancelling one, or posting receipts against one changes only `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents` and `otb_open_cents`; `provisional_sell_through_bps`, `reserve_rate_bps`, `markdown_reserve_cents` and `otb_planned_cents` all retain the values written by the previous reserve event until the next reserve event fires. Newly committed dollars first enter the sell-through denominator at that next event, and they enter it exactly once. The formula must never be iterated to a fixed point.

The **month's open-to-buy** is then computed. Under the planned basis, the month's planned cost of sales is its `planned_sales_cents` converted to cost by the plan's margin, rounded half up, and `otb_planned_cents` is that planned cost of sales plus `planned_eom_cents` plus `planned_markdown_cents` plus `markdown_reserve_cents`, less `bom_inventory_cents`. The authored plan is taken at face value here and no week of actuals is read. Two sums follow, identical under either basis: `otb_committed_cents` is the sum of `open_cost_cents` over the month's commitments whose `commit_status` is exactly `COMMITTED`, and `otb_received_cents` is the sum of `received_cost_cents` over the month's commitments whose `commit_status` is `COMMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. Withdrawn commitments contribute nothing to either, because their open cost is zero and a row that never left `PROPOSED` never received anything. A cancelled commitment likewise contributes nothing to the committed sum, because its open cost has been released to zero, but its `received_cost_cents` — the cost of the units genuinely received against it — stays in the received sum, so a cancellation hands back exactly the open cost it released and never the money already spent on goods in hand. Finally `otb_open_cents` is `otb_planned_cents` less `otb_committed_cents` less `otb_received_cents`, never clamped, per §2.11.

**Link 3** is caused by link 2 creating the plan's first locked months: the plan's **posture** is recomputed and `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` are written, advancing the plan's version by 1 a second time — so a single approval request raises the plan's `version` by 2 in total. Posture is derived from the plan's months whose `status` is `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON`; `DRAFT` and `CLOSED` months are excluded from both sums. If that month set is empty, `open_ratio_bps` is 10000 and `otb_posture` is `OPEN` — but that shortcut settles the ratio only, and the breach override below still runs on top of it. Otherwise the system sums `otb_open_cents` across those months and sums `otb_planned_cents` across the same months, and `open_ratio_bps` is ten thousand multiplied by the open sum, divided by the planned sum or by 1 if that planned sum is smaller than 1, rounded half up. At 1500 basis points or above the posture is `OPEN`; at 500 or above but below 1500 it is `RESTRICTED`; below 500 it is `FROZEN`. One override is applied last and unconditionally: if the plan's `variance_status` is `BREACH`, `otb_posture` is `FROZEN` regardless of the ratio.

**Link 4** is caused by link 3 leaving the posture at something other than `FROZEN`: every option plan of the plan whose `status` is `OPEN` advances `OPEN → SLOTTED`, provided that cell has at least one size-curve row. If link 3 produced `FROZEN`, **no** option plan is slotted at all. For each cell that does slot, the **option slot capacity** is computed with its two rounding steps in pinned order. The tier's share of the plan's `receipt_budget_cents` is taken first — 4500 basis points for a `GOOD` cell, 3500 for `BETTER` and 2000 for `BEST` — divided by ten thousand and rounded half up. That tier share is then multiplied by the store cluster's `sales_index_bps`, divided by ten thousand and rounded half up again; collapsing the two multiplications into a single rounding step is a different and incorrect answer. The cost of carrying one option in that cell is `depth_units_per_option` multiplied by the cluster's `store_count` multiplied by `assumed_unit_cost_cents`. The number of affordable options is the cell's budget share divided by that per-option cost, floored, because a partially funded option cannot be carried. `option_slots_total` is then the larger of 2 and the smaller of the cell's `breadth_target` and that affordable count — every slotted tier is guaranteed a minimum of two options even when the arithmetic funds fewer. `option_slots_open` is `option_slots_total` less `option_slots_assigned`, and each slotted cell's version advances by 1.

**Link 5** is caused by link 4 slotting the cells: for every cell slotted in link 4, the cluster-and-size allocation of §3.3 re-runs, writing `target_units_per_option` on the option plan and `raw_units_x10000` plus `allocated_units` on each of its size-curve rows.

### 3.5. Assortment Adoption, Localization Overlays and Sell-Through Forecasting

Options are proposed against a breadth-and-depth cell before they are adopted into it. Either a `MerchandisePlanner` or a `CategoryBuyer` may propose an **AssortmentItem**, naming the `option_plan_id` of the cell that will hold its slot and the `vendor_id` of the supplier that will make it. Its identity is a `sku_code` of 3 to 24 characters, unique across all assortment items — a duplicate violates the field's declared validity and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, though that uniqueness test runs only once `option_plan_id` and `vendor_id` have both resolved, so a duplicate SKU quoted alongside an unknown vendor yields the vendor's 404 instead — together with a `color_code` of 2 to 8 characters. Its commercial parameters are `unit_cost_cents`, the landed unit cost of at least 1 that chase allocation and generated commitments will use, and `retail_price_cents`, the planned first-ticket retail price of at least 1. Its `lifecycle_stage` — one of `NEW`, `CARRYOVER`, `CORE` or `EXIT` — is the single most consequential field on the row: it selects which sell-through variant applies, it decides whether a lifted freeze restores the item, and it decides whether the item may be bought while its plan is under a restricted posture. The optional `prior_season_sell_through_bps`, between 0 and 10000 and defaulting to 0 when omitted, is the option's own history; it is mandatory in substance for a carryover-style forecast and is ignored outright by the new-item forecast. A newly proposed item has `status` `PROPOSED`, `sell_through_basis` and `sell_through_bps` and `planned_units` all null, and `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps` at 10000. Proposal is unconditional on structure: the named cell may sit in any `status`, `OPEN` and `CLOSED` included, and its governing plan in any governance status, because nothing about the slot is tested until adoption. Item listings can be narrowed by `option_plan_id`, `vendor_id`, `lifecycle_stage` or `status`.

Adoption is a `CategoryBuyer` act and it is where the option's numbers come to life. It advances the item `PROPOSED → ADOPTED` and requires, in exactly this order, that the item itself is still `PROPOSED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), that the cell holding the slot is `SLOTTED` (otherwise `ERR_OPTION_PLAN_NOT_SLOTTED`), that the governing category plan's `status` is either `APPROVED` or `REFORECAST_OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), and that the cell has at least one open slot (otherwise `ERR_NO_OPTION_SLOT`) — so the plan-status refusal outranks the empty-slot one, and a null `option_slots_open` counts as no slot rather than as an unbounded one. On success the item's `planned_units` is set to the cell's `target_units_per_option`, its `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps` is resolved, and its `sell_through_basis` and `sell_through_bps` are forecast. The cell's slot counters move as a conserved pair — `option_slots_assigned` rises by 1 and `option_slots_open` falls by 1, while `option_slots_total` is untouched — and both the item's and the cell's versions advance by 1. A cell's `option_slots_assigned` is by definition the number of its items whose `status` is `ADOPTED`, `IN_SEASON`, `CHASED` or `CANCELLED_IN_SEASON`.

An adopted option becomes live for the season by being activated, which advances the item `ADOPTED → IN_SEASON` and requires that at least one plan month of the item's category plan has already reached `IN_SEASON`. Only an `IN_SEASON` item can be chased or cancelled later.

**Localization overlays** are how one plan carries different intensity in different clusters. A `MerchandisePlanner` authors a **LocalizationOverlay** for one `category_plan_id` and one `store_cluster_id`, declaring an `overlay_type` of `CLIMATE`, `DEMOGRAPHIC` or `VOLUME` — which also fixes the overlay's position in the application order — a window running from `effective_start` to `effective_end`, and a `unit_multiplier_bps` between 5000 and 15000. Overlay windows are **closed** intervals: both endpoints are inside the window, so a single-day overlay whose `effective_end` equals its `effective_start` is perfectly valid, while an `effective_end` earlier than `effective_start` is rejected with `ERR_OVERLAY_WINDOW_INVERTED`. The overlay must name a cluster the plan actually plans for: at least one option plan must exist for the same `category_plan_id` and `store_cluster_id` pair, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CLUSTER_NOT_IN_PLAN`. Overlays are created in `DRAFT` and can be listed narrowed by `category_plan_id`, `store_cluster_id`, `overlay_type` or `status`.

Overlays of the same dimension on the same cluster must be kept well apart, and the separation is enforced with a mandatory seven-day buffer on each side. A candidate is rejected with `ERR_OVERLAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when any other overlay with the same `store_cluster_id` and the same `overlay_type`, whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, satisfies both of these: the candidate's `effective_start` is on or before that overlay's `effective_end` advanced by seven days, and that overlay's `effective_start` is on or before the candidate's `effective_end` advanced by seven days. Windows that merely touch are rejected, and so are windows separated by anything from one to seven days; a gap of eight days or more on either side is accepted. The test is deliberately plan-agnostic — an overlay belonging to some other category plan collides just the same, so long as the cluster and the dimension match — which is exactly what makes the claim below, that a second applicable overlay of one dimension on one cluster can never exist, hold across the whole system rather than only inside one plan.

Activation is reserved for a `PlanningDirector`. It advances the overlay `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and requires that the parent category plan's `status` is `APPROVED` or `REFORECAST_OPEN` and that the logical clock has not yet passed the overlay's `effective_end`. Activation immediately re-resolves the affected assortment: every item whose `status` is `ADOPTED` or `IN_SEASON`, and whose cell belongs to both the overlay's plan and the overlay's cluster, has its `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps` re-resolved and its `sell_through_bps` re-forecast, with each affected item's version advancing by 1. An overlay leaves `ACTIVE` in two ways. Repositioning the logical clock strictly past an `ACTIVE` overlay's `effective_end` advances it `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and recomputes the multiplier and sell-through of every item that had been carrying it, now without it; the sweep is plan-agnostic, so one reposition expires every qualifying overlay in the system, and when several expire together all of the expiries are applied first and each affected item is then re-resolved exactly once from the fully expired state — an item that had been carrying two of them advances its `version` by 1, not by 2. Opening a reforecast round on the plan advances every `ACTIVE` overlay of that plan `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`; a superseded overlay is finished, and a new round needs freshly authored overlays activated against it. Unlike expiry, supersession re-resolves nothing: the items that had been carrying the overlay keep the `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps` and `sell_through_bps` it last gave them, and those fields move again only at that item's next adoption, at the activation of another overlay, or at a clock-driven expiry.

The **effective multiplier** of an item is resolved from the overlays that apply to its cell at the current instant. An overlay applies when its `category_plan_id` matches the plan of the item's cell, its `store_cluster_id` matches the cluster of the item's cell, its `status` is `ACTIVE`, and the logical clock falls inside its closed window — at or after `effective_start` and at or before `effective_end`. Overlays in `DRAFT`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` never participate. The application order is pinned by dimension and by nothing else — not by creation time, not by identifier: `CLIMATE` first, then `DEMOGRAPHIC`, then `VOLUME`. Starting from 10000, for each of those three dimensions in that order, if an applicable overlay of that dimension exists the running value is multiplied by that overlay's `unit_multiplier_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and **rounded half up before the next dimension is applied**; multiplying all the factors together and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule. Because the window-conflict rule makes a second `ACTIVE` overlay of the same dimension on the same cluster impossible, no tie-break is ever needed. The resulting value is then clamped into the range 5000 to 15000 — floored at 5000 and ceilinged at 15000 — and stored as `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps`.

The **sell-through forecast** then runs, and which of its two variants applies depends only on the item's `lifecycle_stage`. For an item whose stage is `NEW`, the **new-item basis** applies: the forecast is the tier benchmark of the item's cell — 6200 basis points for a `GOOD` cell, 5500 for `BETTER`, 4300 for `BEST` — multiplied by the cluster's `sales_index_bps` and by the item's `effective_overlay_multiplier_bps`, divided by one hundred million (that is, each of those two basis-point factors is divided by ten thousand), rounded half up, and then capped at 9800 basis points. Under this basis the item's `prior_season_sell_through_bps` is ignored even when it is populated, and `sell_through_basis` is recorded as `NEW_ITEM_BASIS`. For an item whose stage is `CARRYOVER`, `CORE` or `EXIT`, the **carryover basis** applies: the forecast is the item's own `prior_season_sell_through_bps` multiplied by the cluster's `demographic_index_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, and then capped at 9800 basis points. Under this basis the tier benchmark, the cluster's sales index and the overlay multiplier are all ignored — a carryover option is forecast from its own history scaled by the cluster's demographic productivity — and `sell_through_basis` is recorded as `CARRYOVER_BASIS`. Exactly one rounding occurs in whichever branch runs, and the 9800 cap is applied to the already-rounded value. The same authored row therefore yields two entirely different forecasts depending on nothing but its lifecycle stage.

### 3.6. Spending the Open-to-Buy: Receipt Commitments

A **ReceiptCommitment** is the instrument through which a `CategoryBuyer` converts a month's open-to-buy into an order. Proposing one names the `plan_month_id` of the month whose budget is being drawn down, the `assortment_item_id` of the option being bought, and the `vendor_id` of the supplier — denormalized onto the row deliberately, so that a supplier-side ownership check is a single-field comparison. The buy itself is `units`, at least 1, at a negotiated `unit_cost_cents`, at least 1, which may legitimately differ from the item's own `unit_cost_cents`. The delivery promise is the half-open window from `ship_window_start` inclusive to `ship_window_end` exclusive. And the request must carry `expected_plan_month_version` per §2.9.

A proposal is subjected to the fullest validation stack in the system, and §2.8 fixes the order in which its members are consulted. The target month must be `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON`, or the buy is refused with `ERR_PLAN_MONTH_NOT_OPEN`. The governing plan's posture must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_OTB_POSTURE_FROZEN`), and when that posture is `RESTRICTED` the item's `lifecycle_stage` must not be `NEW` — a restricted plan may still flow goods on carryover, core and exit options, but it may not open new ones, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED_NEW_ITEM`. An `ACTIVE` vendor authorization must join the commitment's vendor to the category of the plan that owns the month (`ERR_VENDOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED_FOR_CATEGORY`). Scope must agree: the cell behind the referenced item and the referenced month must belong to the same category plan, and the body's `vendor_id` must equal the item's own vendor, or the buy is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The quantity must reach the vendor's `min_order_units` (`ERR_BELOW_VENDOR_MINIMUM`) and must pass both vendor capacity tests of §3.2 (`ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Temporally, `ship_window_end` must fall strictly after `ship_window_start`, so a zero-length window is refused with `ERR_SHIP_WINDOW_INVERTED`, and `ship_window_start` must fall no earlier than the logical clock advanced by the vendor's `lead_time_days`, or the buy is refused as physically impossible with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_INFEASIBLE`.

The last gate is fiscal. The commitment's `cost_cents` — its `units` multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents` — is compared against the month's `otb_open_cents`: a cost exactly equal to the open balance is accepted, and one cent more is refused with `ERR_OTB_EXCEEDED`. On success the commitment is stored with `commit_status` `PROPOSED`, `fulfillment_status` `AWAITING_SHIP`, `origin` `BUYER`, `received_units` and `cancelled_units` at 0, `cost_cents` computed as above, `open_cost_cents` computed as `units` less `received_units` less `cancelled_units`, multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`, and `received_cost_cents` computed as `received_units` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`. Creating the commitment advances the target plan month's version by 1 even though a `PROPOSED` commitment has not yet entered the month's committed figure — which is exactly why the next buyer to size a buy from that month needs a fresh `expected_plan_month_version`. Commitments can be listed narrowed by `plan_month_id`, `assortment_item_id`, `vendor_id`, `commit_status` or `fulfillment_status`.

Every commitment carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `commit_status` is the financial dimension and walks `PROPOSED`, `COMMITTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CANCELLED` and `CLOSED`, of which the last three are terminal. `fulfillment_status` is the physical dimension and walks `AWAITING_SHIP`, `SHIPPED`, `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`, `RECEIVED` and `VOID`, of which the last two are terminal. The two dimensions evolve independently and couple at exactly two points, both named below.

Committing a proposal advances `PROPOSED → COMMITTED` on the financial dimension and leaves `fulfillment_status` untouched at `AWAITING_SHIP`. It re-checks, at commit time, that the vendor's `status` is `AUTHORIZED`, that an `ACTIVE` authorization still links that vendor to the plan's category, that the month is still `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON`, that the governing plan's posture is not `FROZEN`, that the restricted-posture rule on `NEW` items still holds, that vendor capacity still admits the units, and that `cost_cents` still fits inside the month's `otb_open_cents`. On success the commitment's `open_cost_cents` is recomputed, the month's `otb_committed_cents` and `otb_open_cents` are recomputed and the month's version advances by 1, and the plan's `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` are recomputed and the plan's version advances by 1. If that recomputation lands the plan in `FROZEN`, every assortment item under that plan whose `status` is `PROPOSED` is demoted `PROPOSED → DEFERRED`, each with its own version advancing by 1; items already `ADOPTED` or `IN_SEASON` are untouched, because they already hold slots. That demotion sweep is bound to this one operation: of every posture recomputation in the system, only the commit of a proposal carries it, exactly as only the cancellation of a `COMMITTED` commitment carries the conditional restore of cascade A described below. A posture recomputation fired by the approval cascade of §3.4, by a month opening for the season, by a weekly actuals post or by a chase-or-cancel adjudication rewrites `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` and touches no item's `status` at all — so a plan driven into `FROZEN` down any of those routes defers nothing, and a plan lifted out of `FROZEN` down any of those routes restores nothing. The commitment's own version advances by 1.

Cancellation covers both financial dimensions of retreat and an optional `cancel_reason` may accompany it. A commitment that is still `PROPOSED` is **withdrawn**: it advances `PROPOSED → WITHDRAWN`, its `fulfillment_status` moves to `VOID`, its `cancelled_units` is set equal to its `units` and its `open_cost_cents` to 0. Because a proposal had never entered the month's committed figure, withdrawal touches no month and no plan field whatsoever — only the commitment's own version advances.

A commitment that has reached `COMMITTED` is **cancelled**, and that is a materially different event. It is permitted only while the logical clock is at or before the commitment's `ship_window_start` less the vendor's `cancel_cutoff_days`; a later attempt is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_CUTOFF_PASSED`. When permitted it drives **cascade A**. Link 1 sets `commit_status` to `CANCELLED`, sets `cancelled_units` to `units` less `received_units`, zeroes `open_cost_cents`, and moves `fulfillment_status` to `VOID` when — and only when — `received_units` is 0; the commitment's version advances by 1. Link 2, caused by link 1 zeroing the open cost, drops the month's `otb_committed_cents` by the released amount and raises the month's `otb_open_cents` by that same amount through a full recomputation of the month, advancing the month's version by 1; after link 2 the conservation identity of §2.11 holds exactly on that month. Link 3, caused by link 2 changing the open balance, recomputes the plan's `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` and advances the plan's version by 1. Link 4 fires on the posture link 3 left behind rather than on the move itself: it runs whenever that posture is anything other than `FROZEN`, so a cancellation on a plan that was already unfrozen still sweeps up whatever is still `DEFERRED`, and a plan left `FROZEN` restores nothing. It restores deferred options — but **conditionally**: every assortment item under that plan whose `status` is `DEFERRED` and whose `lifecycle_stage` is `CARRYOVER` or `CORE` advances `DEFERRED → PROPOSED`, while items whose stage is `NEW` or `EXIT` remain `DEFERRED` and must be re-proposed as fresh rows if the merchant still wants them.

The physical dimension belongs to the supplier. A `VendorRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the commitment's `vendor_id` marks an order shipped, advancing `AWAITING_SHIP → SHIPPED`; this requires the commitment's `commit_status` to be `COMMITTED`, and beyond that guard the financial dimension is neither read nor written. The same `VendorRep` posts arrivals by supplying `received_units_delta`, which must be at least 1 and must not push cumulative receipts above the ordered quantity — a delta that would is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_OVER_COMMITMENT`. A posting is accepted while `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED` or `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`, and the financial dimension is not consulted as a gate at all, so a commitment whose `commit_status` has already moved to `CANCELLED` while its physical dimension still stands at `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` still accepts one. When `received_units` plus the delta is still strictly below `units`, the delta is added to `received_units` and the dimension lands in `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`, whether this was the first partial or a later one; `open_cost_cents` and `received_cost_cents` are recomputed, the month's `otb_committed_cents` and `otb_received_cents` are recomputed, and the month's and commitment's versions each advance by 1. When `received_units` plus the delta exactly equals `units`, the order is complete: `received_units` is set to `units`, `fulfillment_status` becomes `RECEIVED`, and this is the **first coupling point between the two dimensions** — `commit_status` simultaneously advances `COMMITTED → CLOSED`, with `open_cost_cents` zeroed and `received_cost_cents` set to `received_units` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`. The month's `otb_committed_cents` and `otb_received_cents` are recomputed while `otb_open_cents` is left unchanged, because the cost simply moved between two buckets that are both subtracted from the same planned figure. Both versions advance by 1. On either branch the recomputation stops at the month: a receipt posting never recomputes the owning plan's `open_ratio_bps` or `otb_posture` and never advances the plan's `version` — which is consistent, since cost moving between two buckets subtracted from one planned figure leaves the plan's open sum exactly where it was. The **second coupling point** is the reverse: a financial retreat with nothing yet received — a withdrawal, or a buyer-driven cancellation whose `received_units` is 0 — voids the physical dimension, `fulfillment_status` becoming `VOID` from whichever state it was holding, so a cancellation of a commitment already marked `SHIPPED` but with `received_units` still 0 advances `SHIPPED → VOID` exactly as an unshipped one advances `AWAITING_SHIP → VOID`.

### 3.7. In Season: Weekly Actuals, the Projected Basis and the Variance Verdict

A locked month starts selling when a `MerchandisePlanner` opens it for the season, advancing it `LOCKED_PLAN → IN_SEASON`. That is permitted only while the parent category plan's `status` is `APPROVED`; a month may not open for selling while its plan sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REFORECAST_OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. Opening a month changes which months feed the posture sums, so the plan's `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` are recomputed in the same request; the month's version and the plan's version each advance by 1. The month's `active_basis` remains `PLANNED_BASIS` until the first week of actuals lands against it.

Weekly trading is posted by a `MerchOpsAdmin` as an **ActualsPost** against a specific `plan_month_id`. The `week_index`, between 1 and 4, is unique within the month and is the sole chronological replay key; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTUALS_WEEK`. The week's figures are `actual_sales_cents`, the actual net sales at retail in cents; `actual_receipts_cents`, the actual receipts at cost in cents; and `actual_units_received`, the actual units received — each an integer of at least 0. The target month must be `IN_SEASON`, or the post is refused with `ERR_PLAN_MONTH_NOT_OPEN`. An actuals post is an immutable record and, per §2.4, carries only `id` and `created_at`.

Posting a week has wide consequences. The month's `active_basis` flips to `PROJECTED_BASIS`, and the markdown reserve is recomputed — this is the second and only other event that recomputes it — followed by a full recomputation of the month's open-to-buy on the projected basis. The plan's variance verdict is then evaluated, rewriting `sales_variance_bps` and `variance_status`, and the plan's posture is recomputed, rewriting `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture`. The month's version advances by 1 and the plan's version advances by 1, or by 2 when the verdict additionally forces the plan into a reforecast round as described below.

Under the **projected basis** the month is rebuilt from the weeks actually posted rather than taken at face value, and the walk proceeds in ascending `week_index` order, which is a total order because the index is unique within the month. First, the number of weeks posted is the count of actuals rows for the month. Second, the actual sales to date are the sum of `actual_sales_cents` across those rows, and the actual receipts to date are the sum of `actual_receipts_cents` across the same rows. Third, the weekly run rate is the actual sales to date divided by the number of weeks posted, kept as an exact rational and **not rounded**; projected sales for the month are the actual sales to date plus that run rate multiplied by the number of unposted weeks, where a fiscal month always contains four weeks, and that sum is rounded half up to whole cents; `projected_sales_cost_cents` is then the projected sales converted from retail to cost by the plan's margin and rounded half up. When no week has been posted at all — a case this basis is reached in only through the on-demand `basis` of the two computed reads below, since a month with no actuals otherwise sits on the planned basis — the run rate is undefined and both the projected sales and `projected_sales_cost_cents` are 0, which leaves the projected cost of sales feeding the reserve at 0 and its provisional sell-through at 0. Fourth, the actual cost of sales to date is the actual sales to date converted to cost by the plan's margin and rounded half up, and `projected_bom_cents` is the month's authored `bom_inventory_cents` plus the actual receipts to date less that actual cost of sales. Fifth, `otb_planned_cents` is `projected_sales_cost_cents` plus `planned_eom_cents` plus `planned_markdown_cents` plus `markdown_reserve_cents`, less `projected_bom_cents`. The markdown reserve used in that fifth step is itself the one just recomputed on the projected figures, and its own first step reads the projected cost of sales and the projected beginning-of-month inventory in place of the authored ones. The committed, received and open figures are then derived exactly as they are under the planned basis. The same authored month therefore yields a different `otb_planned_cents` under the two bases: sales are re-projected from the posted run rate, and the opening inventory is rebuilt chronologically from actual receipts net of the actual cost of sales.

Which basis governs a month is not a choice: a month whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `LOCKED_PLAN`, and a month whose `status` is `IN_SEASON` with no actuals posted yet, is on `PLANNED_BASIS`; a month whose `status` is `IN_SEASON` with at least one actuals row is on `PROJECTED_BASIS`.

The **variance verdict** compares trading to plan across the whole plan, and it prorates the plan side rather than the actual side. The month set is every plan month of the plan whose `status` is `IN_SEASON` or `CLOSED`; months still `DRAFT` or `LOCKED_PLAN` contribute neither actuals nor plan, and an `IN_SEASON` month with no posts contributes zero to both sides. Actual sales to date are the sum of `actual_sales_cents` over the actuals rows of those months, replayed in ascending `month_index` and then ascending `week_index` order. For each month in the set, the weeks credited are the count of its actuals rows, capped at the four weeks a fiscal month holds, and that month's contribution to the plan side is its `planned_sales_cents` multiplied by the weeks credited and divided by four, **rounded half up per month before the months are summed**. A month with three of its four weeks posted therefore contributes exactly three quarters of its planned sales; comparing to-date actuals against a whole month's plan is explicitly not the rule, and summing the raw prorations and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer whenever two months are mid-flight simultaneously. `sales_variance_bps` is then ten thousand multiplied by the difference between the actual sales to date and the prorated plan to date, divided by that prorated plan to date or by 1 if it is smaller than 1, rounded half up — and since rounding is half away from zero, a value of −1166.67 becomes −1167.

The verdict itself reads that signed variance against three boundaries, all inclusive on the breach edges. At −1000 basis points or below, `variance_status` is `BREACH`. Otherwise, at −400 basis points or below, it is `WATCH`. Otherwise, at +1500 basis points or above, it is `BREACH` again — running far ahead of plan is as much a planning failure as running behind it. Otherwise it is `ON_PLAN`. Exactly −1000 is therefore `BREACH` and not `WATCH`, and exactly +1500 is `BREACH` and not `ON_PLAN`. A `BREACH` verdict has two distinct consequences: it unconditionally forces the plan's `otb_posture` to `FROZEN` as the last step of the posture computation regardless of how much open budget remains, and, when the plan's `status` is `APPROVED` and its `reforecast_round` is still below 3, it drives the plan `APPROVED → REFORECAST_OPEN` and opens a reforecast cycle whose `trigger_reason` is `VARIANCE_BREACH`. When the round cap has already been reached, the actuals post still succeeds and simply opens no cycle.

Two computed reads expose this arithmetic without persisting anything. The month-level open-to-buy ledger accepts an optional `basis` and returns `plan_month_id`, the `basis` it evaluated, `bom_inventory_cents`, `projected_bom_cents`, `planned_sales_cost_cents`, `projected_sales_cost_cents`, `provisional_sell_through_bps`, `reserve_rate_bps`, `markdown_reserve_cents`, `otb_planned_cents`, `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents`, `otb_open_cents` and `weeks_posted`. Passing `PLANNED_BASIS` or `PROJECTED_BASIS` in `basis` returns that variant on demand for the same month; omitting `basis` evaluates the month's own `active_basis`. On both of these computed reads a `basis` that is present but names neither of those two values is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that rejection is decided before the addressed row is resolved, so it outranks the not-found code an unknown `{id}` would otherwise produce. Either way the ledger derives every figure it returns from the basis it evaluated afresh — `provisional_sell_through_bps`, `reserve_rate_bps`, `markdown_reserve_cents` and `otb_planned_cents` included — rather than echoing whatever the month currently has persisted: a `PLANNED_BASIS` reading of a month that has already flipped to `PROJECTED_BASIS` re-derives the reserve from the authored figures and lets no week of actuals into either the reserve or `otb_planned_cents`, and a reading taken after a commitment has moved the sell-through denominator may legitimately differ from the stored month. The authored and the projected columns are nonetheless both reported under either basis — `bom_inventory_cents` and `planned_sales_cost_cents` alongside `projected_bom_cents` and `projected_sales_cost_cents`, each computed by its own definition, with `weeks_posted` always the true count of posted weeks — the requested basis deciding only which of the two pairs feeds the reserve and `otb_planned_cents`. The two-events rule of §3.4 governs the month's persisted reserve fields, not this non-persisting read. The plan-level summary likewise accepts an optional `basis` and rolls the plan's months whose `status` is `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON` — the same month set the posture computation uses — into `category_plan_id`, the `basis` evaluated, `receipt_budget_cents`, the summed `otb_planned_cents`, `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents` and `otb_open_cents`, plus `open_ratio_bps`, `otb_posture`, `variance_status`, `sales_variance_bps` and `month_count`, the number of months that contributed. When no `basis` is requested the summary sums those months' persisted open-to-buy figures and reports the plan's persisted `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture`, and the `basis` it reports back is read off the contributing months themselves: `PROJECTED_BASIS` when at least one of them carries `PROJECTED_BASIS` as its own `active_basis`, and `PLANNED_BASIS` otherwise — so a plan with no contributing month at all reports `PLANNED_BASIS`. When a `basis` is requested it re-evaluates each of those months under it exactly as the month-level ledger would, echoes that requested value back as the reported `basis`, and derives the reported `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` from the re-evaluated sums by the posture rule of §3.4, the breach override included. Under either branch `receipt_budget_cents`, `variance_status` and `sales_variance_bps` are the plan's persisted values, never re-derived. The weekly actuals of a single month can also be listed on their own, ordered by `week_index` ascending.

### 3.8. Reforecast Governance: Cycles, Chase and Cancel

When a season stops behaving like its plan, the correction runs inside a governed **ReforecastCycle** rather than by editing the plan in place. A cycle is opened in one of two ways, distinguished on the row by `trigger_reason`. A `MerchandisePlanner` may request one explicitly, supplying a `reason_note` and the `expected_category_plan_version` of §2.9; such a cycle records `trigger_reason` `DIRECTOR_REQUEST`. Alternatively the system opens one itself when a weekly actuals post produces a `BREACH` verdict, and that cycle records `trigger_reason` `VARIANCE_BREACH`. Either way the plan advances `APPROVED → REFORECAST_OPEN`, its `reforecast_round` rises by 1, and the plan's version advances by 1.

An explicit request is refused unless the plan's `status` is `APPROVED` (`ERR_CATEGORY_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`), unless `reforecast_round` is still below the cap of 3 (`ERR_REFORECAST_ROUND_LIMIT`), and unless no cycle of that plan is currently `OPEN` or `DECIDED` — one live cycle at a time, and a request that finds one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Those gates are consulted in a pinned order: the plan's status first, then the `expected_category_plan_version` comparison of §2.9, then the round cap, and the live-cycle test last of all, so the live-cycle refusal never masks a stale-version rejection. The new cycle is created with `round_index` set to the round number the plan has just moved to, `status` `OPEN`, `opened_at_utc` set to the current logical clock reading, and `snapshot_sales_variance_bps` frozen to the plan's `sales_variance_bps` at the moment of opening, so the cycle preserves the evidence it was opened on even as later trading moves the live figure. Opening a round also invalidates localization: every `ACTIVE` overlay of that plan advances `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and the new round must author and activate its own. Cycles can be listed narrowed by `category_plan_id` or `status`.

Inside an open cycle, a `MerchandisePlanner` or a `CategoryBuyer` proposes **ChaseCancelDecision** rows, one per option being corrected. A decision names the `reforecast_cycle_id` it belongs to and the `assortment_item_id` it corrects, declares an `action` of `CHASE`, `CANCEL` or `HOLD`, states `requested_units` of at least 0 — which must be exactly 0 when the action is `HOLD`, a cross-field constraint enforced as a body-shape failure with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at priority 3 of §2.8, before either of the row's two foreign keys is resolved — and carries a `rationale` of 1 to 400 characters retained for the governance audit. The proposing operator's `X-User-ID` is captured into `proposed_by_user_id`, `approved_units` starts null, and `status` starts `PROPOSED`. Proposing into a cycle that has already bubbled to `DECIDED` reopens it, advancing the cycle `DECIDED → OPEN` and advancing its version by 1; that reopening requires the parent plan to still be in `REFORECAST_OPEN`, and the `OPEN → DECIDED → OPEN` loop may repeat for as many decision rounds as one reforecast round needs. Decisions can be listed narrowed by `reforecast_cycle_id`, `assortment_item_id`, `action` or `status`.

Adjudication belongs to a `PlanningDirector`, who may attach an optional `decision_note`. Approving requires that the decision is still `PROPOSED`, that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `proposed_by_user_id`, that the parent cycle's `status` is `OPEN`, and that the referenced assortment item's `status` is `IN_SEASON`. Rejecting requires the first three of those. A rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED` and `approved_units` to 0, changes no plan month and no item field at all, and advances the decision's version by 1. A successful approval, on every one of the three branches below, advances the decision `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and writes the `approved_units` that branch computed — including the `HOLD` branch, whose `approved_units` is 0 and which is nonetheless approved rather than refused.

An approval runs the **chase-or-cancel waterfall**, whose output is `approved_units`, and its three branches are disjoint. A `HOLD` decision allocates nothing: `approved_units` is 0, no commitment is created and none is reduced, and the item's `status` is unchanged.

A `CHASE` decision buys more. The candidate months are the plan's plan months whose `status` is `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON`, whose `otb_open_cents` is strictly greater than 0, and whose `first_receipt_date` falls no earlier than the logical clock advanced by the vendor's `lead_time_days` — a month that cannot physically be shipped into is not a candidate. Candidate months are visited in ascending `month_index` order, which is total because the index is unique within the plan, so no tie-break is needed. The units still to place start at `requested_units`. For each month visited, the units that month can fund are its `otb_open_cents` divided by the item's `unit_cost_cents`, floored, because a partially funded unit cannot be bought; the vendor's remaining headroom is its `weekly_unit_capacity` less the load already carried by that vendor's `PROPOSED` and `COMMITTED` commitments whose ship windows overlap the chase window that begins at that month's `first_receipt_date` and runs 14 days — a load that counts the commitments this same walk has already written for earlier months, since each is `COMMITTED` from the moment it is created. The units taken from the month are the smallest of the units still to place, the units the month can fund, and that headroom. When the units taken exceed 0, the server creates one receipt commitment for that month with `plan_month_id` set to the month, `assortment_item_id` set to the item, `vendor_id` set to the item's vendor, `units` set to the units taken, `unit_cost_cents` copied from the item's `unit_cost_cents`, `ship_window_start` set to the month's `first_receipt_date` and `ship_window_end` set to 14 days after it, `origin` `CHASE_DECISION`, `commit_status` `COMMITTED`, `fulfillment_status` `AWAITING_SHIP`, `received_units` and `cancelled_units` at 0, and `cost_cents`, `open_cost_cents` and `received_cost_cents` derived from those figures by the §3.6 formulas; the units still to place fall by the units taken. A month whose funding or headroom admits nothing is simply skipped and the walk carries on to the next candidate. The walk stops as soon as nothing remains to place. `approved_units` is then `requested_units` less whatever still remains. A shortfall is a legitimate **partial approval**: `approved_units` may be less than `requested_units` and the decision still becomes `APPROVED`. Only when `approved_units` resolves to 0 is the approval itself refused, with `ERR_NO_FEASIBLE_RECEIPT_MONTH`. Note that the commitments the server generates this way are the sanctioned in-season correction route and are deliberately exempt from the frozen-posture gate that blocks buyer-originated commitments. They are exempt from the rest of the proposal stack of §3.6 too: no vendor minimum, no vendor `status` test, no category-authorization test, no ship-window or lead-time test beyond the candidate-month feasibility already applied, and no optimistic-concurrency field are consulted, and the month's open balance never has to be re-checked because the funding floor already bounds each take. The vendor's `weekly_unit_capacity` is the only supply-side ceiling the walk honours.

A `CANCEL` decision gives units back. The candidate rows are the item's own receipt commitments whose `commit_status` is `COMMITTED` and whose open units — `units` less `received_units` less `cancelled_units` — are strictly greater than 0. They are visited in descending `month_index` of their plan month, with ties broken by ascending `created_at` of the commitment, so the latest months are unwound first. The units still to remove start at `requested_units`; from each row the system takes the smaller of the units still to remove and that row's open units, adds the taken amount to the row's `cancelled_units`, and reduces the units still to remove accordingly. Every row the walk touches has its `open_cost_cents` rewritten as its remaining open units multiplied by its own `unit_cost_cents`, while its `received_cost_cents` is left alone, and no cancellation cutoff is consulted anywhere in this walk — the director's adjudication is not bound by the vendor's `cancel_cutoff_days`. Any row whose open units reach 0 advances `COMMITTED → CANCELLED` on its financial dimension, and on that dimension only: its `fulfillment_status` is left exactly as it stands, so this governed unwind never voids the physical dimension the way a buyer-driven cancellation of §3.6 does. The walk stops when nothing remains to remove, and `approved_units` is `requested_units` less whatever still remains.

The item follows the branch. On an approved `CHASE` with `approved_units` above 0 the item advances `IN_SEASON → CHASED` and its `planned_units` rises by `approved_units`. On an approved `CANCEL` with `approved_units` above 0 the item advances `IN_SEASON → CANCELLED_IN_SEASON` and its `planned_units` falls by `approved_units`. The two branches write disjoint side-effect sets — one adds units and debits open-to-buy, the other removes units and credits it — and both eventually converge on the same terminal state. On either allocating branch — including a `CANCEL` that found nothing to release and settled on `approved_units` 0 — every plan month the approval touched has `otb_committed_cents`, `otb_received_cents` and `otb_open_cents` recomputed, and the plan's `open_ratio_bps` and `otb_posture` are recomputed afterwards, advancing the plan's version by 1. A `HOLD` approval recomputes neither: it touches no month and no plan field, so the plan's `version` does not move. On every branch the decision's version advances by 1.

Cycles close by aggregation. As soon as an approval or a rejection leaves no decision of the cycle still `PROPOSED`, and provided the cycle holds at least one decision at all, the cycle bubbles `OPEN → DECIDED` and its version advances by 1 — the parent's state is derived from the aggregate state of its children. A `PlanningDirector` then closes it, advancing `DECIDED → CLOSED`. A cycle that was opened and then judged to need no correction whatsoever can also be closed directly from `OPEN`, which is the deliberate liveness escape: because the bubble to `DECIDED` requires at least one decision, an empty cycle would otherwise be unclosable and its plan could never leave `REFORECAST_OPEN`. Either close path is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DECISIONS_REMAIN` while any decision of the cycle is still `PROPOSED`. Once every cycle of the plan is `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED`, the planner may resubmit the plan, and the governance loop closes: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED → REFORECAST_OPEN → SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, with the plan re-entering `APPROVED` through the very same approval transition and cascade described in §3.4.

### 3.9. Retiring Options and Closing the Season

An option is retired by a `CategoryBuyer`, and retirement is the moment its slot returns to the pool. Closing is accepted from any of four states — `ADOPTED`, `IN_SEASON`, `CHASED` or `CANCELLED_IN_SEASON` — and it is the single join point at which all of them converge on `CLOSED`. It is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` from `PROPOSED`, from `DEFERRED` and from `CLOSED`, since none of those hold a live slot to release or are already finished. Closing also requires that the item has no financial loose ends: if any receipt commitment of the item still has a `commit_status` of `PROPOSED` or `COMMITTED`, the close is refused. On success `status` becomes `CLOSED` and the cell's slot counters move as the conserved pair they are — `option_slots_assigned` falls by 1 and `option_slots_open` rises by 1, with `option_slots_total` untouched — and the item's and the cell's versions each advance by 1. Where a plan rejection has meanwhile unwound the cell back to `OPEN` and left its `option_slots_open` null, only `option_slots_assigned` falls and the null is left null rather than being read as zero. The slot release is identical on every one of the four inbound paths.

The season ends when a `PlanningDirector` closes the plan, and the same operation serves two business purposes with one identical rule set: closing an `APPROVED` plan at season end, and closing a `REFORECAST_OPEN` plan whose reforecast round the director has decided to abandon outright. Both sources are accepted, and both require that no receipt commitment belonging to any plan month of the plan is still `PROPOSED` — dangling proposals must be committed or withdrawn first.

Closure cascades downward. The plan's `status` becomes `CLOSED`, a terminal state from which nothing further is possible. Every plan month of the plan whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `LOCKED_PLAN` or `IN_SEASON` moves to `CLOSED`, and every option plan of the plan whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SLOTTED` moves to `CLOSED`, each with its version advancing by 1. Every reforecast cycle of the plan whose `status` is `OPEN` advances `OPEN → ABANDONED`, and each of that cycle's decisions still sitting at `PROPOSED` is set to `REJECTED` with `approved_units` written as 0. A cycle that had already bubbled to `DECIDED` is deliberately **not** swept: only cycles in `OPEN` are abandoned, so a cycle whose decisions were all adjudicated keeps its `DECIDED` status when the plan closes. Nothing else is swept: the plan's assortment items keep whatever status they had reached, including `ADOPTED`, `IN_SEASON`, `CHASED` and `CANCELLED_IN_SEASON`, its receipt commitments keep both of their state dimensions, so a `COMMITTED` order survives the season's close, its localization overlays keep their status, and neither the closed months' open-to-buy figures nor the plan's `open_ratio_bps`, `otb_posture`, `sales_variance_bps` and `variance_status` are recomputed, even though every closed month has just left the posture month set. The plan's own version advances by 1. Because a cycle is only ever `OPEN` or `DECIDED` while its plan sits in `REFORECAST_OPEN`, the abandonment path is in practice reached exclusively from the reforecast source — no `APPROVED` plan ever carries an open cycle.
