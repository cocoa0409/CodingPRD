# Heavy Civil Bidding Unit Price Quantity Reconciliation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Public-works contracting is a business of two clocks that refuse to agree. The first clock runs backwards from the moment an owner's bid window slams shut: estimators race a hard deadline to complete a quantity takeoff, develop unit prices from crew-productivity assumptions, and collect subcontractor and supplier quotes, all while a statutory prohibition on bid shopping forbids them from playing one trade against another. The second clock runs forwards for years after award, because a unit-price contract pays for measured field quantity rather than a lump sum, and the money owed does not settle until the last measurement has been reconciled against the engineer's original estimate. This API exists to hold both clocks in one system of record, so that the price committed at bid time and the quantity paid at closeout can be compared, disputed, and finally squared.

The pain this product addresses is concrete and expensive. Quantity overruns and underruns beyond a contractual variance band entitle either party to renegotiate the rate, and a contractor who cannot prove which pay items crossed that band — or what the renegotiated rate was — loses the adjustment entirely. Simultaneously, bonding capacity is a finite, conserved resource: a surety's aggregate capacity is consumed by every outstanding bond and every reserved commitment it carries, so a firm that cannot see its true available capacity will either overcommit and lose a bid, or refuse work it could have bonded. A third pressure comes from compliance: disadvantaged business enterprise participation goals must be met or excused by documented good-faith effort, and any differing site condition must be documented and submitted inside a thirty-day notice period or the claim is barred.

The result is a system where a single field measurement can move a pay item's variance across a threshold, engage a renegotiated rate, alter a retention balance, and change how much bonding capacity a firm still has available. Because those couplings are exactly where disputes and audit findings originate, the API enforces them immediately rather than eventually: every derived figure is recomputed the moment its inputs change, every mutation is version-stamped so that a writer acting on stale state is rejected rather than silently corrupting the ledger, and every rejection carries a machine-readable identifier so that an owner's auditor and a contractor's estimating desk can agree on what went wrong.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section bind every operation in the system. They cover who may act, what every response looks like, how collections are paged, the order in which rejections are evaluated, how time is governed, what every resource carries by default, how concurrent writes are serialized, how numbers are rounded, and how bonding capacity is conserved.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request except the two open capabilities named below must carry two HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting party, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which that party is acting. A request that omits either header, or supplies an empty value for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and an HTTP 401 status. A request whose declared role is not permitted on the operation being attempted is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` and an HTTP 403 status. Both of these checks precede every other rule in the system, so an unauthenticated or unauthorized caller learns nothing about whether the resource it named exists.

The only request that requires neither header is the session exchange, which lets a caller declare a role and an optional `user_id`, and which is answered with a `session_token` alongside the resolved `role` and `user_id`. The logical-clock advance capability is also exempt from both the authentication and the permission checks at the header level, although the acting role is still recorded. Every other endpoint in the catalogue is guarded.

The system recognises seven roles. `PlatformAdmin` is the back-office operator, responsible for the surety registry, surety suspension and reinstatement, the calling of an executed performance bond, the finalization of engineer determinations, and the logical clock. `Estimator` is the bid preparer, who owns the takeoff, the unit pricing, and the collection of quotes. `ContractingOfficer` is the owner-side authority, who opens and recalls bids, selects or rejects quotes, releases capacity commitments, finalizes the quantity reconciliation, and releases retention. `ProjectManager` is the field-side delivery role, who mobilizes the work, approves, pays and voids measurements, closes pay items, and reports and documents differing site conditions. `Engineer` is the owner's engineer, who issues determinations on differing site conditions, returns claims for documentation, and denies claims. `Surety` is the bonding firm, which issues its own bonds against its own capacity. `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated caller permitted only to open a session.

The identity carried by `X-User-ID` is interpreted differently depending on the declared role. When `X-User-Role` is `Surety`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Surety`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Surety` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Estimator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Engineer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paged through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many records are returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and any supplied value must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. The `offset` parameter states how many records to skip from the front of the result set; when it is omitted it defaults to 0. Unless a description says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records ordered by creation timestamp ascending, so the oldest record appears first.

Paged responses carry four bookkeeping fields inside the `meta` object of the success envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before paging is applied, and `has_more` is true when further records remain beyond the returned window. The same four fields appear on both paged and non-paged list responses.
### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every primary resource in the system — `Project`, `BidItem`, `Bond`, `Surety`, `SubcontractorQuote`, `ProgressMeasurement`, `QuantityReconciliation`, `DifferingSiteCondition`, `EngineerDetermination` and `SuretyCommitment` — carries a standard set of system-managed fields, because all of them are declared with common fields enabled. The `id` field is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded entity name. The `created_at` field is an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with exactly three fractional digits, recording when the resource was first created. The `updated_at` field is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed whenever any property of the resource changes, including changes to its derived fields. The `version` field is an integer that begins at its initial value and is incremented by one on every mutation of that resource.

No entity in this system is declared without common fields, so there are no resources that carry only `id` and `created_at` while omitting `updated_at` and `version`. Parent entities are incremented independently of their children: a change to a pay item that also refreshes its parent project bumps both the pay item's own `version` and the project's `version`, because they are separate resources with separate concurrency tokens.

### 2.5. Evaluation Order and Rejection Precedence

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which the checks are evaluated is itself part of the contract, and it is fixed. The system evaluates a request against the following bands in strict sequence, and the first band that produces a violation determines the error returned; a later band is never consulted once an earlier band has failed.

The first band is authentication, and within it the presence check outranks the permission check: a request missing the `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` header is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the role is ever compared against the operation, and only once both headers are present and non-empty is the role compared, a failure producing `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The second band is request well-formedness: if the body does not parse, a required field is absent, an unknown field is present, or an enum-valued field holds a member outside its declared set, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

The third band is referential existence, and within it the checks run in a fixed order: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a `project_id` path or body value resolves to no `Project`; then `ERR_BID_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a `bid_item_id` or a `{id}` bound to a `BidItem` resolves to nothing or to an item belonging to a different project than the path declares; then `ERR_SURETY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a `surety_id` resolves to no `Surety`; then `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a quote identifier resolves to no `SubcontractorQuote`; then `ERR_BOND_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a bond identifier resolves to no `Bond`; then `ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a measurement identifier resolves to no `ProgressMeasurement`; then `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a reconciliation identifier resolves to no `QuantityReconciliation` or to one belonging to a different project; then `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a condition identifier resolves to no `DifferingSiteCondition`; then `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a determination identifier resolves to no `EngineerDetermination`; and finally `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when a commitment identifier resolves to no `SuretyCommitment`. Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, so the existence of an outer resource is always settled before the system attempts to locate anything nested beneath it.

The fourth band is concurrency: the optimistic-concurrency checks described in §2.9 are evaluated here, ahead of any state or business rule, so a stale writer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) or `ERR_STALE_BID_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) even if it also named a resource in the wrong state.

The fifth band is state: if the target resource is not in the state that the requested transition requires, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and if the dependent sibling resource is in the wrong state — for example when a measurement is approved against a pay item that is not in `ACTIVE` status — the request is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

The sixth band is business safety, where a suspended surety is rejected first with `ERR_SURETY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422) before any capacity arithmetic is attempted, followed by the bid-shopping prohibition which is rejected with `ERR_BID_SHOPPING_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422).

The seventh band is business constraint uniqueness and overlap: `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409) when a project already holds a reconciliation that is not `CLOSED`, `ERR_RESERVATION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when two issued or executed bonds of the same type on one project have intersecting coverage intervals, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROJECT_CODE` (HTTP 409) when a `project_code` already exists on a project that is not `COMPLETE`, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CODE` (HTTP 409) when an `item_code` already exists within the same project.

The eighth band is temporal and referential business rules, evaluated in this order: `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_BID_NOT_YET_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_QUOTE_AFTER_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_RATE_BELOW_COST` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_BOND_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_NO_LIEN_CLAIM` (HTTP 422), and then `ERR_AGGREGATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) for both the available-capacity test and the temporally-intersecting-commitment test. When two of these rules apply to the same request — most commonly at `seal-bid`, where both `ERR_RATE_BELOW_COST` and `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`-style temporal rules may fire — the one listed earlier in this paragraph wins.

The ninth and final band is business fiscal closeout: `ERR_DBE_GOOD_FAITH_MISSING` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_QUANTITY_RENEGOTIATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_CLOSEOUT` (HTTP 422), then `ERR_MEASUREMENTS_PENDING` (HTTP 422), and finally `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_RELEASABLE` (HTTP 422). The bulk of this band is concentrated on the reconciliation finalization, where the open-claim test outranks the pending-measurement test, and on the retention release, which carries only the final check.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Time-Dependent Rules

The system runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, and it is fully deterministic. The server's startup default time is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time before the first call that advances it. Time advances only through the administrative capability that accepts a `to_iso8601` instant, which sets the current logical time to that value; advancing the clock recomputes the elapsed-notice figure on every differing site condition in the system, because those figures are measured against the clock.

Four time-dependent behaviours hang off the clock. A project records the moment it became sealable, at creation, as its bid-window-open instant. Sealing a bid, recalling a sealed bid, and carrying a subcontractor quote are all pre-deadline operations: each requires the logical clock to be strictly before the project's bid deadline (before `bid_deadline_at`), and a violation is rejected with `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Opening a sealed bid and selecting a carried quote are post-deadline operations: each requires the logical clock to be at or after the project's bid deadline, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_BID_NOT_YET_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Recording a subcontractor quote and carrying a quote each additionally require the logical clock to be strictly before the project's bid deadline, and a violation of that specific rule is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_AFTER_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Nested Resource Retrieval

Requests that address a resource nested within a parent put more than one identifier in the path, and the system resolves them strictly left to right. The parent is located first; if it does not exist the request stops with the parent's own not-found error, established by the third band of §2.5, before the system spends any effort on the child. Only once the parent is confirmed does the system attempt to resolve the child identifier inside that parent, and a child that exists but belongs to a different parent is treated as absent rather than as a mismatch: a pay item reached through one project's path that actually belongs to another project yields `ERR_BID_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, and a reconciliation reached through one project's path that belongs to another yields `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`. The same rule governs the measuring of a pay item whose parent project has since been recalled or reset.

### 2.8. Measurement Units, Money and Rounding

Money in this system is carried in integer cents, never in floating-point dollars, and every cent-valued field is an integer. Field quantities, variance percentages, participation percentages, rates and thresholds are instead carried as numbers measured in their own unit — the unit of measure declared on the pay item, or a percentage — and they may carry a fractional part.

Two rounding conventions are fixed and must not be varied. First, there is no fractional cent anywhere: every computation whose result is a cent amount is rounded to a whole cent, and the method is round half up, meaning a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Second, percentage-valued derived figures that are exposed with fractional precision are rounded to exactly 2 decimal places using the same round half up method; in particular the credited DBE participation percentage is rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the signed quantity variance percentage is rounded half up to 2 decimal places. When a formula subtracts money totals, it subtracts one integer cent amount from another, so the result is itself an exact integer and is never rounded a second time. Rounding is applied at the point named in each calculation below, and applying it twice, or applying it at a sum of already-rounded values, is wrong.

The measurement unit of a pay item is one of a fixed set: `LF` for linear feet, `CY` for cubic yards, `TON` for tons, `EA` for each, and `SY` for square yards. Every quantity recorded against a pay item is expressed in that same unit, and the per-unit bid price is expressed in cents per that same unit.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in the system are guarded against stale writes, and only those two take a version field; every other endpoint in the catalogue accepts no version field of any kind. The guards are declared as explicit input fields, not optional ones, and their absence is a well-formedness failure rather than a bypass.

Recording a new progress measurement against a pay item requires the body to carry `expected_bid_item_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of that pay item. If the value supplied does not equal the pay item's current `version` at the moment the write is processed, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BID_ITEM_VERSION` and an HTTP 412 status. This guard exists because another writer may have approved or voided a measurement on the same item since the client last looked, which legitimately moves the item's running measured quantity and its signed variance percentage; the stale writer's view of the item is therefore no longer true.

Finalizing a quantity reconciliation requires the body to carry `expected_project_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent project. If the value supplied does not equal the project's current `version` at the moment the write is processed, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` and an HTTP 412 status. This guard exists because another writer may have issued an engineer determination or approved a measurement on the same project since the client last looked, both of which legitimately advance the project's version; the stale writer is therefore acting on a project state it has not observed.

### 2.10. Precision and Immutability of Determinations

Determination records are the system's only explicitly immutable artifacts. An engineer determination begins its life in `PENDING`, moves to `ISSUED` when the engineer issues it, and reaches `FINAL` only when a `PlatformAdmin` finalizes it. Once a determination reaches `FINAL` it may not be altered by any route in the system, and a later measurement approval against the pay item it re-set must adopt the rate the determination fixed rather than the item's original bid rate.

### 2.11. Conservation of Surety Bonding Capacity

A surety's aggregate capacity is a conserved quantity. The `aggregate_capacity_cents` of a `Surety` never changes after registration; what moves is the split between what has been consumed and what remains available. The consumed amount is the sum of the capacity consumed by that surety's bonds plus the sum of the committed amounts of that surety's commitments that are either reserved or active, where a bond consumes capacity only while its status is `ISSUED` or `EXECUTED` and consumes nothing at all while it is `PENDING` or `RELEASED`. The available amount is the aggregate capacity minus the consumed amount, and it is floored at zero so that it can never be reported as a negative number.

Conservation is enforced in both directions. Issuing or executing a bond adds its face amount to the consumed total and therefore removes that amount from the available total; releasing the bond sets its consumed amount to zero and therefore returns exactly that amount to the available total. A suspension freezes the surety so that no new bonds may be issued, but it does not by itself move the available figure; reinstatement restores the surety to active without altering the arithmetic. Because registration initialises the consumed amount to zero and the available amount to the aggregate capacity, the sum of the two figures always equals the aggregate capacity.

### 2.12. Response Visibility of Computed Fields

Derived fields are part of every resource representation and are returned to clients on reads as well as on writes. A `Project` response carries its computed `total_bid_cents`, `contract_value_cents`, `unbalanced_bid_score`, `dbe_participation_pct`, `retention_balance_cents` and the `claim_window_open` flag alongside its stored attributes. A `BidItem` response carries its computed `balanced_unit_price_cents`, `measured_quantity`, `variance_pct` and `renegotiation_required` flag. A `Bond` response carries its computed `required_bond_cents`, `lien_claim_amount_cents` and `capacity_consumed_cents`. A `Surety` response carries its computed `capacity_consumed_cents`, `available_capacity_cents` and `unavailable_capacity_cents`. A `SubcontractorQuote` response carries its computed `dbe_credit_cents`, `carried_in_bid` and `shopping_flagged` flags. A `ProgressMeasurement` response carries its computed `applied_unit_price_cents` and `payable_amount_cents`. A `QuantityReconciliation` response carries its computed `overrun_item_count`, `underrun_item_count`, `net_adjustment_cents`, `final_contract_value_cents`, `retention_held_cents`, `pace_basis_retention_cents` and `retention_released_cents`. A `DifferingSiteCondition` response carries its computed `documentation_complete`, `notice_days_elapsed` and `claim_window_engaged` values. An `EngineerDetermination` response carries its computed `entitles_adjustment` flag.

When an operation returns a parent resource after a mutation, that representation reflects the mutation: the `updated_at` timestamp is refreshed and the `version` integer is advanced by one. Reads that recompute a roll-up before answering refresh those computed figures on the returned representation as well, which means a read of a project's summary may legitimately advance the freshness of the values it reports without altering the project's stored inputs.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Contract and Assembling the Takeoff

A public-works engagement begins when an `Estimator` opens a project record for a bid. The request must supply the `project_code`, which is the owner-agency contract number such as `CALTRANS-04-1J5604`, and the `owner_agency_name`. The `project_code` must be unique across every project whose `project_status` is not `COMPLETE`; a second project that reuses a code already held by an in-flight project is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROJECT_CODE` (HTTP 409), and a code is released for reuse only once its project has reached `COMPLETE`. The `bid_deadline_at` instant is mandatory and states when the owner's bid window shuts.

Three further attributes are optional at creation and take documented defaults. The `contract_type` records how the owner will pay, and it is one of exactly two values: `UNIT_PRICE`, which pays by measured quantity, and `LUMP_SUM`, which pays a fixed sum; when the caller omits it the value `UNIT_PRICE` is assumed. The `retention_rate_pct` states the percentage withheld from each progress payment; it defaults to 5.0 when omitted and must be a number between 0.0 and 10.0 inclusive. The `advertised_dbe_goal_pct` states the disadvantaged business enterprise participation percentage the owner advertised; it defaults to 0.0 when omitted and must be a number between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive. A body that omits a mandatory attribute, that carries an unknown attribute, or that puts a value outside the declared set into an enumerated attribute such as `contract_type` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A `project_id` that resolves to no project at all is reported as `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Opening the record sets the project's bid-window-open instant to the current logical clock, stamps the sealed-bid dimension to `DRAFT`, and stamps the delivery dimension to `PRE_BID`. From that moment the project is addressable, and every subsequent bid-preparation action is scoped to it.

The takeoff is then captured as pay items. An `Estimator` adds a pay item to a project by supplying the `item_code`, which is the owner's pay-item number such as `2025-390102` and must be unique within the project — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CODE` (HTTP 409) — together with the `item_description` in plain language, the `unit_of_measure` in which the owner will pay, and the `estimated_quantity` the engineer derived from the takeoff. The `estimated_quantity` must be strictly greater than zero. The `unit_of_measure` must be one of `LF`, `CY`, `TON`, `EA` or `SY`. Each new pay item starts its life in `DRAFT` status, which marks it as part of the takeoff but outside the bid total.

Two variance bands are attached to the item at creation, because the contract entitles the parties to revisit the price once field quantity drifts far enough from the estimate. The `overrun_threshold_pct` is the measured-over-estimated percentage above which price renegotiation is triggered, and it defaults to 15.0 when omitted, within the range 0.0 to 100.0 inclusive. The `underrun_threshold_pct` is the measured-under-estimated percentage below which the same renegotiation is triggered, and it also defaults to 15.0, within the same range. The two bands are independent, so a project may carry an asymmetric pair — for instance a tighter underrun band than overrun band where the contractor's margin is thin.

### 3.2. Unit Pricing, Crew Productivity and the Unbalanced-Bid Score

Pricing a pay item is the act that converts a takeoff quantity into a bid. An `Estimator` prices a `DRAFT` item by supplying its `bid_unit_price_cents`, and along with it the crew-productivity assumption that justifies the price: the `crew_day_labor_cents` for one crew day, the `crew_day_equipment_cents` for the same crew day, and the `daily_productivity_units` the crew installs in one day. Productivity sheets in this trade are conventionally built around a standard eight-hour crew day. The `crew_day_labor_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, the `crew_day_equipment_cents` must be at least zero, and the `daily_productivity_units` must be strictly greater than zero; the `bid_unit_price_cents` may be zero or more at the field level but a price of exactly zero cannot be committed, as described below.

From those assumptions the system derives the item's balanced unit price, which is the at-cost reference against which the bid price will be judged. The crew-day cost is the labor figure plus the equipment figure. The `balanced_unit_price_cents` is that crew-day cost divided by the `daily_productivity_units`, rounded half up exactly once, at the per-unit quotient, to a whole cent. The balanced price is stored on the item and is not the price that will be bid; it is the floor that the bid price must clear and the denominator of the unbalanced-bid score.

Pricing is only accepted on an item that is still `DRAFT`. Attempting to price an item in any other status is a state violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A price that is not strictly greater than zero is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_PRICE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), and a price that falls below the item's balanced unit price is rejected with `ERR_RATE_BELOW_COST` (HTTP 422), because bidding work below the crew-productivity cost is treated as a materially unbalanced bid rather than a legitimate commercial choice. Note that these two rules are checked in that relative order — a non-positive price is reported as `ERR_UNIT_PRICE_REQUIRED` rather than as a below-cost rate, since a price of zero is below every cost floor and would otherwise mask the real complaint.

Applying the price moves the item from `DRAFT` to `PRICED`, stores the freshly computed balanced unit price, recomputes the project-level unbalanced-bid score across the project's priced items, and advances the project's `version`.

A priced item does not enter the bid total until it is submitted. An `Estimator` submits a `PRICED` item, which moves it to `SUBMITTED`, and submission is permitted only while the project's bid dimension is `DRAFT` or `SEALED`; once the bid has been opened the composition of the bid is frozen and the attempt is refused as a state violation. A `SUBMITTED` item can be pulled back to `PRICED` again while the project's bid dimension is anything other than `OPEN`, which is how an estimator reacts to a late change in scope before the deadline. Both directions recompute the project's bid extension, so that the total always reflects the items currently in the bid.

The project's bid extension is computed in a pinned evaluation order. The population of included pay items is every item on the project whose `item_status` is not `DRAFT`. For each included item, the extended amount is the item's `bid_unit_price_cents` multiplied by its `estimated_quantity`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and rounding is applied here, at the item level, and only here. The items subtotal is the exact integer sum of those per-item extended amounts. The subcontract subtotal is the exact integer sum of the `quoted_amount_cents` of every subcontractor quote on the project whose `quote_status` is `CARRIED` or `SELECTED`. The project's `total_bid_cents` is the items subtotal plus the subcontract subtotal. Because both subtotals are sums of already-rounded integers, the total is never rounded a second time. This calculation therefore has two variants that must both be honoured: when the project has at least one carried or selected quote the total includes the subcontract subtotal, and when no quote has been carried the total equals the items subtotal alone — meaning the identical takeoff yields a strictly smaller total in the second case.

Separately from the actual bid total, the system maintains a scrutiny score that measures how far the bid prices have been pushed away from their at-cost reference. The `unbalanced_bid_score` is computed over every included pay item whose `item_status` is not `DRAFT` and whose `balanced_unit_price_cents` is strictly greater than zero. For each such item the system forms the ratio of the item's `bid_unit_price_cents` to its `balanced_unit_price_cents`, and assigns the whole item — not a marginal slice of it — to one of four bands. When the ratio is strictly less than 0.80 the item sits in the front-loaded band and takes a multiplier of 1.00. When the ratio is at least 0.80 but strictly less than 1.20 the item sits in the at-cost band and also takes a multiplier of 1.00. When the ratio is at least 1.20 but strictly less than 2.00 the item sits in the moderately unbalanced band and takes a multiplier of 1.005. When the ratio is 2.00 or greater the item sits in the materially unbalanced band and takes a multiplier of 1.015. An item whose ratio falls exactly on a band boundary is assigned wholly to the higher band, because band assignment is whole-item rather than marginal. The item's contribution is then its `estimated_quantity` multiplied by its `balanced_unit_price_cents` and then by that band multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the `unbalanced_bid_score` is the exact integer sum of those contributions. A straddling item is therefore never split across bands — that whole-item assignment is the pinned interpretation.

### 3.3. Quote Collection, Good-Faith Effort and the Bid Shopping Prohibition

Subcontractor and supplier pricing is captured as quotes while the bid is being prepared. An `Estimator` records a quote by supplying the `project_id` it belongs to, the `trade_code` naming the scope of work such as `SWPPP`, `SIGNAL` or `STRIPING`, the `contact_name` of the person at the quoting firm, the `quoted_amount_cents` in cents, and the two ends of the interval during which the quoted price is held: `quote_window_start`, which is inclusive, and `quote_window_end`, which is exclusive and must fall strictly after the start. The `quoted_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero. Recording the quote stamps its received-at instant from the logical clock and starts it in `RECEIVED` status with a negotiation round of 1.

Two attributes on the quote drive compliance rather than price. The `unicap_registered` flag records whether the quoting firm is a certified Unified Certification Program disadvantaged business enterprise; it defaults to false when omitted. The `good_faith_effort_basis` records how the estimator attempted to obtain disadvantaged-business participation on this trade, and it must be one of `NONE`, `SOLICITED_DBE`, `DOCUMENTED_UNAVAILABLE` or `OUTREACH_EVENT`, defaulting to `NONE` when omitted.

Carrying a quote is the act of folding its price into the bid. An `Estimator` carries a quote that is in `RECEIVED` status, which moves it to `CARRIED` and sets its carried-in-bid flag true. Carrying is a pre-deadline action: the logical clock must be strictly before the project's bid deadline, and a later attempt is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_AFTER_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422) rather than with the generic window error, because a quote that arrives after the window shuts is a distinct and more specific failure than a bid operation attempted after the window shuts. Carrying recomputes the quote's credited disadvantaged-business cents, the project's credited participation percentage, and the project's bid extension, and advances the project's `version`.

The prohibition that gives this stage its commercial edge is bid shopping. At most one quote per trade may be carried: if any other quote on the same project and the same `trade_code` already sits in `CARRIED` or `SELECTED` status, the new carry is refused with `ERR_BID_SHOPPING_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422). The rule is deliberately framed against the hardware, not against intent — a second carried quote on the same trade is the visible signature of a firm using one subcontractor's number to beat down another's, and the system will not record it. A quote may nonetheless be withdrawn from the bid: an `Estimator` withdraws a `CARRIED` quote back to `RECEIVED`, provided the project's bid dimension is not `OPEN`, and each withdrawal opens a new negotiation round by incrementing the quote's `version_round` by one, which is the audit trail proving that the price was renegotiated rather than merely dropped. Withdrawal clears the carried-in-bid flag and recomputes the project's bid extension.

Once the bid has been opened, the owner's side takes over the same quotes. A `ContractingOfficer` selects a carried quote, moving it from `CARRIED` to `SELECTED`, but selection is a post-deadline action: it requires the project's bid dimension to be `OPEN`, which itself can only be true after the deadline, and the attempt before that point is rejected with `ERR_BID_NOT_YET_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Selection recomputes the project's credited participation percentage, since a selected quote remains credited. A carried or selected quote may instead be rejected by a `ContractingOfficer`, moving it to `REJECTED`, clearings its carried-in-bid flag; rejection of a carried quote also recomputes the project's bid extension, because removing a carried price changes the total that had included it. `REJECTED` and `SELECTED` are terminal states — a quote that has reached either cannot be carried again.

Credited disadvantaged-business participation is a weighted score over the project's live quotes, and its denominator is pinned to those quotes rather than to the bid total. The numerator is the exact sum of the `quoted_amount_cents` of every quote on the project whose `quote_status` is `CARRIED` or `SELECTED` and whose `unicap_registered` flag is true. The denominator is the exact sum of the `quoted_amount_cents` of every quote on the project whose `quote_status` is `CARRIED` or `SELECTED`, regardless of registration. The project's `dbe_participation_pct` is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places. When the denominator is zero — a project with no carried or selected quotes at all — the participation percentage is exactly 0 rather than undefined or null.

### 3.4. Sealing, Recalling and Resetting the Bid

Sealing freezes the bid. An `Estimator` seals the project's bid, moving the sealed-bid dimension from `DRAFT` to `SEALED`, and the act is permitted only while those six conditions hold together. The project's bid dimension must still be `DRAFT`. The logical clock must be strictly before the project's `bid_deadline_at`; a seal attempted at or after the deadline is rejected with `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The project must have at least one pay item in `PRICED` or `SUBMITTED` status, so that an empty bid cannot be sealed. No pay item on the project may still sit in `DRAFT`, because a takeoff line that was never priced has no place in a sealed bid. Every pay item on the project must satisfy the cost-floor rule that its `bid_unit_price_cents` is at least its `balanced_unit_price_cents`; a single below-cost item blocks the seal and is reported as `ERR_RATE_BELOW_COST` (HTTP 422), which is why an estimator cannot repair a below-cost item by simply omitting it from the bid. And no subcontractor quote on the project may carry a true shopping flag, because a sealed bid must not contain a price obtained after the owner's bid was opened.

A successful seal recomputes the project's scrutiny score and its bid extension, so that both figures are frozen alongside the bid, and advances the project's `version`.

Recalling is the owner-side remedy for a bid that should not be opened as it stands. A `ContractingOfficer` recalls a sealed bid, moving the dimension from `SEALED` to `RECALLED`, and must supply a `reason_code` drawn from exactly `ERROR_IN_BID`, `OWNER_ADDENDUM` or `PROTESTS_RECEIVED`. A recall is a pre-deadline action, so the logical clock must be strictly before the project's bid deadline. Recalling unwinds the bid's composition: every pay item on the project in `SUBMITTED` status returns to `DRAFT`, every quote on the project in `CARRIED` status returns to `RECEIVED` with its `version_round` incremented by one — reopening the negotiation round exactly as an explicit withdrawal would — and the project's `total_bid_cents` is reset to 0. The `version` of the project is advanced by one. Recall is not terminal: the recalled bid can be re-prepared at leisure and sealed again once the deadline allows.

Resetting closes the loop. A `ContractingOfficer` returns a `RECALLED` bid to `DRAFT`, and the same act returns the delivery dimension to `PRE_BID`, so that the project is back in the state it held before any bid was prepared. The project's `version` advances by one. This four-step cycle — `DRAFT` to `SEALED`, `SEALED` to `RECALLED`, `RECALLED` to `DRAFT` — is the only permitted route back from a sealed bid to preparation; once the bid has been opened the cycle is closed and the direction of travel is forwards only.

### 3.5. Bid Opening, Award and the Shopping Flag

Opening the bid is the hinge between preparation and delivery, and it is reserved to a `ContractingOfficer`. The bid dimension must be `SEALED` and the logical clock must be at or after the project's `bid_deadline_at`; an attempt before the deadline is rejected with `ERR_BID_NOT_YET_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The opening is recorded with a `bid_open_at` instant taken from the logical clock, which is the timestamp every later shopping determination is measured against.

Opening the bid drives a five-stage award cascade in a fixed order. First, the sealed-bid dimension becomes `OPEN` and the delivery dimension becomes `AWARDED`, so that the project has moved out of preparation on both of its parallel dimensions at once. Second, the project's awarded `contract_value_cents` is set equal to its `total_bid_cents`, capturing the bid total as the starting contract value that reconciliation will later adjust. Third, every pay item on the project in `SUBMITTED` status is promoted to `AWARDED`, and for each pay item that enters `AWARDED` the system recomputes the quantity variance adjustment on the project's non-closed quantity reconciliation covering that item. Fourth, those same reconciliations have their retention settlement recomputed — the signed net adjustment, the final contract value and the retention held — and the recomputed contract value and retention balance are written back onto the project. Fifth, and separately from the money chain, the credited participation percentage is recomputed across all of the project's quotes, and any quote whose received-at instant falls after the project's `bid_open_at` has its `shopping_flagged` flag set true.

That last step is the compliance counterpart of the bid-shopping prohibition described earlier in §3.3: a firm may not carry a late quote into a sealed bid, and a firm may not receive a late quote after opening without the system recording it. The flag is computed, not asserted — it is set whenever the received instant is later than the open instant, and a quote whose received instant is at or before the open instant is never flagged. The project's `version` advances by one at the end of the opening.

A quote that arrives after the bid has been opened is flagged the moment it is recorded, because the recording rule compares its received instant against the project's `bid_open_at` at creation time.

### 3.6. Bonding, Surety Capacity and the Bond Lifecycle

Bonding is the second commerce in this system, and it runs on a conserved pool of capacity. A `PlatformAdmin` registers a surety firm by supplying its `surety_name`, which must be unique, its `aggregate_capacity_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, and its `single_job_limit_cents`, which states the largest face amount the surety will carry on one project's performance bond and which must not exceed the aggregate capacity. Registration initialises the surety's consumed capacity to zero and its available capacity to the full aggregate capacity, and starts the firm in `ACTIVE` status. From that point the aggregate capacity is immutable, and only the split between consumed and available moves, following the conservation rule in §2.11.

A `ContractingOfficer` or an `Estimator` may reserve capacity ahead of a bond by recording a surety commitment, supplying the `surety_id` whose capacity is being reserved, the `project_id` the reservation covers, and the `committed_amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero. A reservation starts in `RESERVED` status and records its `reserved_at` instant from the logical clock. Reservations are refused when the sum of the committed amounts of every commitment on that surety whose coverage interval intersects the new commitment's interval, plus the requested amount, would exceed the surety's aggregate capacity; that refusal is reported as `ERR_AGGREGATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The intersection test is what makes the check meaningful: two reservations that will not overlap in time do not compete for the same capacity, while two that do overlap must both fit inside it.

Releasing a commitment is how reserved capacity goes back. A `ContractingOfficer` releases a commitment that is in `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` status, which moves it to `RELEASED` and stamps its `released_at` instant from the logical clock; because a `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` commitment counts against the surety, its release immediately recomputes the surety's consumed and available capacity and advances the surety's `version`. Once released, the reservation no longer consumes capacity at all.

Bonds themselves are recorded against a project and a surety. A `Surety` or an `Estimator` records a bond by supplying the `project_id`, the `surety_id`, the `bond_type`, the `bond_amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, and the coverage interval `effective_from` inclusive and `effective_to` exclusive, where the end must fall strictly after the start. The `bond_type` must be one of exactly four values: `BID`, which is ten percent of the bid, `PERFORMANCE`, which is one hundred percent of the contract, `PAYMENT`, also one hundred percent, and `MAINTENANCE`, fifty percent. The optional `maintain_bond_horizon_days` states the maintenance-bond horizon in days and matters when the project's `contract_type` is `LUMP_SUM`; it defaults to 30 when omitted and must fall between 1 and 365 inclusive. Recording a bond starts it in `PENDING` status and computes its statutory `required_bond_cents` under the tiered requirement described below. A `surety_id` that resolves to no surety is reported as `ERR_SURETY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

The statutory requirement is tiered by contract size, and the whole tier applies rather than a marginal rate. The size basis is the project's current `contract_value_cents`. When the size is at most 1,000,000 cents the statutory percentage is 0.50. When the size is greater than 1,000,000 cents but at most 5,000,000 cents the statutory percentage is 0.75. When the size is greater than 5,000,000 cents but at most 10,000,000 cents the statutory percentage is 1.00. When the size is greater than 10,000,000 cents the statutory percentage is 1.25. For a performance bond the `required_bond_cents` is the size multiplied by that statutory percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent. For a payment bond the `required_bond_cents` is the size multiplied by 1.00, rounded half up. For every other bond type — bid and maintenance bonds — the `required_bond_cents` is the project's `total_bid_cents` multiplied by 0.10, rounded half up. The tier selection is whole-tier: a contract whose size falls under a tier's upper bound takes that tier's percentage in full, and no portion of the contract is priced at a lower tier's rate.

Issuing a bond is the act that commits capacity against it, and it is reserved to the acting `Surety`, which may only issue against the `surety_id` named in its own `X-User-ID`. A bond must be in `PENDING` status to be issued, and issuing runs a fixed sequence of five checks. The bond's statutory `required_bond_cents` must not exceed its `bond_amount_cents`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_BOND_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The backing surety must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SURETY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). The surety's available capacity must be at least the bond's face amount, and when the bond is a performance bond the face amount must additionally not exceed the surety's single-job limit; either failure is rejected with `ERR_AGGREGATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). And no other bond on the same project with the same `bond_type` that is in `ISSUED` or `EXECUTED` status may have a coverage interval intersecting the interval that runs from this bond's `effective_from` up to but excluding its `effective_to`; an intersection is rejected with `ERR_RESERVATION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The overlap rule reads on the half-open interval deliberately: one bond whose `effective_to` equals another's `effective_from` does not overlap, because the earlier interval is exclusive at its end and the later is inclusive at its start.

Issuing a bond moves it to `ISSUED`, records its issued-at instant from the logical clock, sets its consumed capacity to its full face amount, recomputes the surety's consumed and available capacity, recomputes the statutory requirement when the bond is a performance bond, and advances the surety's `version`.

A performance bond may be called when the contractor has failed, and that is the `EXECUTED` arm. A `PlatformAdmin` executes a performance bond that is in `ISSUED` status, and the move requires the project's payment bond to show an outstanding lien claim — a `lien_claim_amount_cents` strictly greater than zero — or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NO_LIEN_CLAIM` (HTTP 422). The lien-claim figure is itself derived from unpaid progress measurements on the project, so the call cannot be made before subcontractors have gone unpaid long enough to file. Execution moves the bond to `EXECUTED`, keeps its consumed capacity at its face amount, recomputes the surety's consumed capacity and advances the surety's `version`.

A bond leaves the books by being released, and the release rule depends on where the bond currently sits. A `PENDING` bond may be released at any time by a `ContractingOfficer`, which is the route for a bond that was recorded and then abandoned before issue. An `ISSUED` bond may be released only once the project's delivery dimension has reached `COMPLETE` and the project's non-closed reconciliation has reached `CLOSED`, so that a live bond cannot be shed while the work it backs is still open. An `EXECUTED` bond may be released once the project has reached `COMPLETE`, without the reconciliation test, because a called bond has already been drawn down. Every release sets the bond to `RELEASED`, stamps its released-at instant from the logical clock, sets its consumed capacity to zero, recomputes the surety's exposure, and advances the surety's `version`. `RELEASED` is terminal.

Sureties themselves can be taken out of service. A `PlatformAdmin` suspends an `ACTIVE` surety, moving it to `SUSPENDED`, which freezes it so that no further bonds may be issued against it while leaving its available capacity figure untouched; a `PlatformAdmin` reinstates a `SUSPENDED` surety, returning it to `ACTIVE` and recomputing its available capacity. Suspension and reinstatement are a cycle rather than a one-way door, because a firm may be suspended over a dispute and later cleared.

### 3.7. Mobilization and the Compliance Gate on Starting Work

Awarding a contract does not put a crew in the field; mobilization does, and mobilization is gated on compliance and on bonding rather than on paperwork alone. A `ProjectManager` starts work on a project whose delivery dimension is `AWARDED`, and four conditions must hold. The project must carry a performance bond — that is, a `Bond` with `bond_type` `PERFORMANCE` — that is in `ISSUED` status. The surety backing that bond must itself be `ACTIVE`, so a surety suspended between issue and mobilization will hold the project at the gate. And the project must satisfy its disadvantaged-business obligation, which is satisfied when the project's credited `dbe_participation_pct` is at least the project's `advertised_dbe_goal_pct`, or, failing that, when any subcontractor quote on the project documents a good-faith-effort basis of `SOLICITED_DBE`, `DOCUMENTED_UNAVAILABLE` or `OUTREACH_EVENT`. A mobilization that fails the disadvantaged-business test is rejected with `ERR_DBE_GOOD_FAITH_MISSING` (HTTP 422).

The last condition is the one that changes commercial behaviour. A project with an advertised goal of zero credits trivially, since participation of 0.0 is at least a goal of 0.0. A project with a real goal must either reach it in carried or selected quote value or leave behind documentary evidence of the attempt, and a `NONE` basis satisfies nothing — a quote whose good-faith-effort basis is `NONE` and whose firm is not registered as a disadvantaged business contributes nothing toward the test.

Passing the gate starts a four-stage mobilization cascade. First, the project's delivery dimension becomes `ACTIVE` and its claim window is opened by setting the `claim_window_open` flag true, which is the switch that later permits differing site conditions to be recorded at all. Second, every pay item on the project in `AWARDED` status is promoted to `ACTIVE`, and each such item has its running measured quantity reset to zero, because a pay item that has just gone into the field has measured nothing yet. Third, every surety commitment on the project that is in `RESERVED` status becomes `ACTIVE`, so that the reservation stops being a contingency and becomes a live draw on capacity. Fourth, for each surety touched by that third step, the surety's consumed and available capacity are recomputed. The project's `version` advances by one at the end of the sequence.

The delivery dimension then moves in one direction only. A project that is `ACTIVE` becomes `COMPLETE` when the delivery lifecycle reaches its natural end, and it is the combination of item closure and reconciliation closure that produces that end rather than an explicit mobilization-like command. From `PRE_BID` a project can reach `AWARDED` only through bid opening, and from `AWARDED` it can reach `ACTIVE` only through the mobilization gate; a project that is `COMPLETE` cannot be moved back.

### 3.8. Field Measurement and the Running Quantity Ledger

Once work is active, payment begins to track measured field quantity rather than the estimate. A `ProjectManager` or an `Estimator` records a progress measurement against a pay item by supplying the `measured_quantity` observed for the period and the `measured_at` instant at which the field measurement was taken. The `measured_quantity` must be strictly greater than zero, and the `measured_at` instant is what places the measurement in the running quantity ledger's chronological order. A new measurement starts in `DRAFT` status, which means it is recorded but not yet payable, and recording it advances the parent pay item's `version`.

The ledger is the key to this stage, because the quantity a pay item has actually earned is the sum of its live measurements and nothing else. The population of counted measurements is every measurement on the item whose `measurement_status` is anything other than `VOIDED`, and the system orders that population chronologically by `measured_at` ascending, breaking any tie by `created_at` ascending and never by `id`. Ordering matters because the ledger is a running figure and because the tie-break is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail: two measurements taken on the same day are ordered by the instant they were recorded, which is a stable and reproducible sequence. The item's derived `measured_quantity` is the sum of the counted measurements' quantities.

Voiding is the subtraction from that ledger. A `ProjectManager` voids a measurement that is in `APPROVED` or `PAYABLE` status, and the void is permitted only while the project's non-closed reconciliation is not `CLOSED`, because a closed reconciliation has already frozen the quantities it settled. Voiding moves the measurement to `VOIDED`, zeroes its payable amount, recomputes the item's running measured quantity, its signed variance percentage and its renegotiation flag, recomputes the project's retention settlement, and advances the project's `version`. Because the counted population excludes `VOIDED` rows entirely, a voided measurement is invisible to the ledger once voided, which is what makes the reversal exact rather than an estimate.

Approval is the act that turns a draft observation into a payable one, and it is where the applied rate is pinned. A `ProjectManager` approves a measurement that is in `DRAFT` status, and the approval requires the measurement's `measured_quantity` to be strictly greater than zero and the parent pay item to be in `ACTIVE` status; an approval against a pay item that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Approval moves the measurement to `APPROVED`, recomputes the item's running measured quantity, signed variance percentage and renegotiation flag, and then sets the measurement's `applied_unit_price_cents`: when a final renegotiated rate covers this pay item the applied rate is that renegotiated rate, and when no renegotiated rate is in force the applied rate is the item's original `bid_unit_price_cents`. The measurement's `payable_amount_cents` is then the measurement's `measured_quantity` multiplied by its `applied_unit_price_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Approval also recomputes the project's retention settlement and the owner's pace-based retention figure, refreshes the project's retention balance, and advances the project's `version`.

A `ProjectManager` marks an approved measurement payable, which moves it from `APPROVED` to `PAYABLE` and freezes its payable amount at the value computed at approval; paying is permitted only while the project's non-closed reconciliation is still `OPEN`, so that a reconciliation already being finalized cannot have new money added underneath it. `PAYABLE` is not terminal, because a payable measurement can still be voided, but `VOIDED` is terminal in the sense that a voided measurement is never revived — a correction is recorded as a new measurement, which is exactly the behaviour an owner's auditor expects to see.

Two population variants of the ledger must both be honoured, because they produce genuinely different numbers from identical data. While a reconciliation is still `OPEN`, the running ledger counts both `APPROVED` and `PAYABLE` measurements, so a paid row continues to contribute to the item's measured quantity and its variance. Once the reconciliation has been `FINALIZED`, only `APPROVED` rows count, and `PAYABLE` rows are dropped from the population entirely. The consequence is deliberate: an item whose variance sat just above its overrun band can fall back below the band at finalization, because the paid rows that pushed it over are no longer counted, and its renegotiation requirement can therefore disappear. An implementation that uses one population for both phases will misprice every item that has been paid.

### 3.9. Quantity Variance and Price Renegotiation Beyond the Bands

The central commercial mechanic of a unit-price contract is that drifting field quantity reopens the price. For each pay item the system computes a signed quantity variance against the engineer's estimate. The variance percentage is the item's committed measured quantity minus its `estimated_quantity`, divided by its `estimated_quantity`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places. The sign is preserved: an item measured over its estimate carries a positive variance and an item measured under its estimate carries a negative one.

Whether that variance opens the price depends on the item's own two bands, and the boundary behaviour is strict in both directions. Renegotiation is required when the variance percentage is strictly greater than the item's `overrun_threshold_pct`, or when the variance percentage is strictly less than the negative of the item's `underrun_threshold_pct` — that is, when it falls below minus that band. A variance exactly equal to the overrun band does not trigger, and a variance exactly equal to minus the underrun band does not trigger either. This is the single most consequential boundary in the system: an item measured exactly 15 percent over its estimate on the default bands is not renegotiable, and an implementation that uses an inclusive comparison at either edge will disagree with the contract on a boundary case the field will hit regularly.

The flag that records this outcome is the item's `renegotiation_required`, a flag that is true when either band is crossed and false otherwise, and it is recomputed every time the item's running measured quantity moves — on an approval, on a void, when the item is awarded through bid opening, when a quantity adjustment is determined, and when the reconciliation is finalized.

Crossing a band is a claim on the contract, not an automatic payment. A `ContractingOfficer` finalizes a reconciliation whose status is not `CLOSED`, and the finalization is refused with `ERR_QUANTITY_RENEGOTIATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) when an item whose variance crosses a configured band has no final renegotiated rate in force. The remedy is the engineer's quantity adjustment described in §3.11, which sets the rate the item will be paid at; until that determination is final, the overrun simply cannot be settled.

The money consequence of renegotiation is computed as a signed net adjustment over the project. Only items whose `renegotiation_required` flag is true contribute; every other item contributes nothing at all. For a contributing item the system first forms the economic variance figure, which is the item's variance percentage rounded half up to a whole number of points. It then forms the uplift: the economic variance clamped to the range from zero up to a cap of 300 points — so a negative economic variance contributes nothing and an economic variance above 300 contributes only the 300-point cap — multiplied by 200 cents per point. Each contributing item's uplift is therefore an exact integer cent amount with a maximum of 60,000 cents, and the project's `net_adjustment_cents` is the exact integer sum of the uplifts of the contributing items. Items that did not cross a band contribute zero, not their raw variance, which means the net adjustment is never diluted by well-behaved items.

Renegotiation is also what makes the pay item's applied rate move. When a final renegotiated rate is in force for an item, every non-closed measurement on that item is re-priced at the renegotiated rate — its `applied_unit_price_cents` becomes the determined rate — rather than at the original `bid_unit_price_cents`, and every subsequent approval applies the renegotiated rate as well. This is the mechanism by which the overrun is actually paid at the renegotiated price rather than merely flagged.

### 3.10. Retention, the Pace Grade and Final Contract Value

Retention is money the owner withholds from progress payments and returns at closeout, and this system tracks it as a running balance on both the project and its open reconciliation. The base for retention is the gross payable figure: the exact sum of the `payable_amount_cents` of every progress measurement on the project whose `measurement_status` is `APPROVED` or `PAYABLE`. The retention held is that gross payable figure multiplied by the project's `retention_rate_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up exactly once, on the gross payable base, to a whole cent. The project's `retention_balance_cents` is then the retention held minus the retention released, and the retention released is zero until a release succeeds, so while a project is in progress the balance equals the retention held.

The final contract value is the awarded value adjusted by the settlement. The `final_contract_value_cents` on the reconciliation is the project's `contract_value_cents` plus the reconciliation's `net_adjustment_cents` — the signed renegotiation figure derived in §3.9 — and because both operands are whole-cent integers the result is exact. This figure is written back onto the project's `contract_value_cents` when the reconciliation finalizes, so that the project's awarded value becomes the reconciled value and a later read of the project shows the settled number rather than the bid number.

The owner's closeout template grades retention by work-in-place pace rather than by the percentage base alone, and the system computes that alternative alongside the contractual figure. The alternative uses the same gross payable base as the contractual computation and applies an absolute annual pace grade of 4,000,000,000 cents — that is, forty million dollars of work per year — divided by twelve to give a monthly pace of 333,333,333 cents. When the gross payable figure is at least that monthly pace, the pace-based retention held is zero, because a project moving work at or above the graded pace releases retention entirely. When the gross payable figure falls below the monthly pace, the pace-based retention held is the monthly pace multiplied by the project's `retention_rate_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the contractual figure: the comparison to the pace is at or above, so a project whose gross payable exactly equals the monthly pace releases retention in full under this grade while the contractual percentage basis would still hold it. The pace-based figure is reported on the reconciliation as `pace_basis_retention_cents` and is materially different from the contractual held figure for lightly measured projects.

The waterfall has two release points, and the release is all-or-nothing per the reconciliation rather than incremental. While the reconciliation is not `CLOSED` no retention has been released and the released figure is zero. When the reconciliation is `CLOSED` the released figure equals the full retention held, and the project's retention balance is written to zero. There is no partial release: the entire withheld balance moves at the single closing event.

### 3.11. Differing Site Conditions, the Notice Clock and the Engineer's Determination

A unit-price contract only functions if the site turns out to be the site that was bid, and when it does not, the contractor's remedy is a differing site condition claim. A `ProjectManager` reports a condition against a project by supplying the `reported_at` instant at which the condition was discovered — the instant that starts the thirty-day notice clock — and an optional `claimed_amount_cents`, the contractor's claimed cost impact, which defaults to 0 when omitted and must be zero or greater when supplied. Two gates stand in front of the report. The project's claim window must be open, which it is only while the project's delivery dimension is `ACTIVE`, and a report attempted while the window is shut is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). A `project_id` that resolves to nothing is reported as `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before any window test is attempted. The new condition starts in `REPORTED` status and the elapsed-notice figure is recomputed on it.

The notice clock is the discipline that makes these claims a matter of record rather than of argument. The elapsed notice figure is the whole number of days between the condition's `reported_at` instant and the current logical clock, obtained by taking the difference in seconds, dividing by 86,400 to convert to days, and discarding the fractional part so that only whole days count. The notice is late only when that whole-day figure is strictly greater than 30, which means that a claim submitted on exactly the thirtieth day is still timely and only the thirty-first day and beyond is barred. The figure is recomputed whenever the condition is documented, whenever a claim is submitted, and on every read of the condition, so that a condition that has been sitting untouched for a month is seen to have aged the moment the clock passes the limit.

Documenting a condition is how the contractor stops the notice clock from mattering and assembles the claim package. A `ProjectManager` documents a `REPORTED` condition by supplying `documentation_package_urls`, which must be a non-empty array; an empty or absent array is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). The project's claim window must still be open, and a documentation attempt against a shut window is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Documenting moves the condition to `DOCUMENTED` and sets its `documentation_complete` flag true, and recomputes the elapsed-notice figure.

Submitting a documented claim to the engineer is the step that starts the determination. A `ProjectManager` submits a condition that is in `DOCUMENTED` status, and four conditions must hold. The condition's documentation must be complete. The project's delivery dimension must be `ACTIVE`. The elapsed-notice figure must not exceed 30 days — that is, the claim must be submitted while the notice is still timely — and a claim submitted past the limit is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). And the condition's `claimed_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, because a claim for nothing is not a claim; a zero amount is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Submission moves the condition to `UNDER_REVIEW` and advances the project's `version`, and while the condition sits `UNDER_REVIEW` the project's open reconciliation is frozen against finalization, because a claim under review is a claim that has not been settled.

An `Engineer` may return a claim for documentation rather than determine it. Returning a condition that is in `UNDER_REVIEW` status moves it back to `DOCUMENTED` and clears its `documentation_complete` flag, reopening the documentation round. No determination record is created on this path — the return is a rework instruction, not a decision, and this is the deliberate asymmetry between the return and the determination.

A claim may also be denied from any non-terminal position. An `Engineer` denies a condition that is in `REPORTED`, `DOCUMENTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` status, moving it directly to `DENIED`; no adjustment of any kind is applied on a denial, and `DENIED` is terminal.

The determination itself is where the money moves. An `Engineer` issues a determination against a condition that is in `UNDER_REVIEW` status, supplying a `determination_type` drawn from exactly `TIME_EXTENSION`, `COST_EQUITABLE_ADJUSTMENT`, `QUANTITY_ADJUSTMENT` or `REJECTED`. When the type is `QUANTITY_ADJUSTMENT` the request must also supply `target_bid_item_id`, the pay item whose rate is being re-set, and that field is required for this type and for this type only; the other types may omit it, and when supplied on another type it is ignored. Two further optional fields accompany the determination: `determined_unit_rate_cents`, the unit rate the engineer sets, which defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater, and `days_granted`, the calendar days of time extension, which defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. A determination whose parent condition is not `UNDER_REVIEW` is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` (HTTP 422).

Issuing a determination sets the condition to `DETERMINED` and creates a determination record that moves straight to `ISSUED`, stamped with its issued-at instant from the logical clock. The determination's `entitles_adjustment` flag is a derived value, true exactly when the determination type is `COST_EQUITABLE_ADJUSTMENT` or `QUANTITY_ADJUSTMENT`, and false for `TIME_EXTENSION` and `REJECTED`. When the type is `QUANTITY_ADJUSTMENT`, the pay item named by `target_bid_item_id` has its applied rate re-set: every non-closed measurement on that item has its `applied_unit_price_cents` set to the `determined_unit_rate_cents` the engineer supplied, which is the mechanism described in §3.9 by which a renegotiated rate reaches the money.

A determination is not final when it is issued, and the distinction carries real weight. A `PlatformAdmin` finalizes a determination that is in `ISSUED` status, moving it to `FINAL`, and a final determination is immutable thereafter. Finalizing a quantity adjustment also engages the project's claim-window flag by setting the parent condition's `claim_window_engaged` flag true, and that engagement is exclusive: once any quantity adjustment determination on a project has been finalized, no further determination of the same type on that project may be finalized, and a duplicate finalization attempt is rejected. Only a final determination supplies the rate that satisfies the renegotiation gate described in §3.9, so a project cannot be closed out on the strength of an issued-but-unfinalized determination. The determination carries its own lifecycle independent of the condition's: a condition reaches its terminal `DETERMINED` in one step, while its determination travels `PENDING` to `ISSUED` to `FINAL` on its own schedule.

### 3.12. Reconciliation, Closeout and the Cascades

The final act of a project is the quantity reconciliation, and it is the point where every loose end in the previous subsections is tied. A `ContractingOfficer` or an `Estimator` opens the reconciliation for a project, which starts it in `OPEN` status and immediately recomputes the project's quantity variance adjustment and retention settlement. At most one reconciliation may be open on a project at a time: opening a second one while the project already holds a reconciliation that is not `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409).

Finalizing is the freeze, and it is guarded on four fronts. A `ContractingOfficer` finalizes a reconciliation that is in `OPEN` status, and the finalization requires, in the precedence order established in §2.5, that no differing site condition on the project sits in `REPORTED`, `DOCUMENTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — an open claim blocks closeout and is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_BLOCKS_CLOSEOUT` (HTTP 422) — and then that every progress measurement on every pay item of the project sits in `APPROVED`, `PAYABLE` or `VOIDED`, since a measurement still in `DRAFT` means the quantity ledger has not settled; a draft measurement is rejected with `ERR_MEASUREMENTS_PENDING` (HTTP 422). The reconciliation also carries the renegotiation gate: an item whose variance crosses a band with no final renegotiated rate blocks the finalization with `ERR_QUANTITY_RENEGOTIATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). And because finalization is one of the two version-guarded writes, the request must carry `expected_project_version` matching the project's current version, or it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A successful finalization sets the reconciliation to `FINALIZED` and its closeout marker to `PENDING_RETENTION`, recomputes the quantity variance adjustment over all of the project's pay items — producing the overrun item count, the underrun item count and the signed net adjustment — and recomputes the retention settlement, producing the final contract value and the retention held. It then writes the reconciled contract value and the retention balance back onto the project, and advances the project's `version`. The count of overrun items is the number of included pay items whose variance percentage is strictly greater than that item's `overrun_threshold_pct`, and the count of underrun items is the number of included pay items whose variance percentage falls strictly below minus that item's `underrun_threshold_pct`; the same strict boundaries that govern the renegotiation flag govern these counts, so the two are always consistent with one another.

Releasing retention is the closing cascade, and it is where a single request unwinds the whole project structure. A `ContractingOfficer` releases retention on a reconciliation that is in `FINALIZED` status; a release attempted while the reconciliation is still `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_RELEASABLE` (HTTP 422), which is the only guard on this operation beyond the state check. The release sets the reconciliation to `CLOSED`, sets its released amount equal to the full retention held, sets its closeout marker to `CLOSED`, and writes the project's retention balance to zero.

The release then cascades in three stages. First, every pay item of the project that is in `ACTIVE` status is moved to `CLOSED`, which is the pay item's terminal state. Second, when every pay item of the project has reached `CLOSED`, the project's delivery dimension becomes `COMPLETE` and its claim window is shut by clearing the `claim_window_open` flag. Third, every surety commitment on the project that is in `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` status becomes `RELEASED` with its released-at instant taken from the logical clock, and every bond on the project that is in `ISSUED` status becomes `RELEASED` with its released-at instant taken from the logical clock; the surety exposure is then recomputed for every surety bound to the project. The project's `version` advances by one. This is the deepest cascade in the system, and it is the reason a project that closes out cleanly ends with its items closed, its project complete, its commitments released, its bonds released and its sureties' available capacity restored to what it was before the project began.

Pay items can also be closed individually once the reconciliation supports it. A `ProjectManager` closes a pay item that is in `ACTIVE` status, and the closure requires the project's non-closed reconciliation to have reached `FINALIZED` and every measurement on that item to sit in `APPROVED`, `PAYABLE` or `VOIDED`; a measurement still in `DRAFT` blocks the item's closure. Closing the item freezes its running measured quantity at its reconciled value, recomputes the project's retention settlement, and advances the project's `version`; when the closure leaves every pay item on the project `CLOSED`, the project's delivery dimension becomes `COMPLETE` in the same step. This is the route by which a project completes without the release-retention cascade — a project whose items are closed one by one reaches `COMPLETE` as soon as the last item closes, and its reconciliation can then be released afterwards. Both routes to `COMPLETE` must be supported, because a field operation that closes the last pay item and an administrative release of retention are two genuinely different sequences that happen to reach the same terminal state.

The project's `COMPLETE` status is also what unlocks the final bond releases. A bond in `ISSUED` status may only be released once the project has reached `COMPLETE` and its reconciliation has reached `CLOSED`, and a bond in `EXECUTED` status may be released once the project has reached `COMPLETE`. Both routes recompute the surety exposure, so that the capacity consumed by a project returns to its surety exactly once, at whichever release event actually fires.
