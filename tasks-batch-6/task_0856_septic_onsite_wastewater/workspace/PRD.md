# Septic & On-Site Wastewater Permit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

County health departments carry a peculiar regulatory burden: they must license the sewage system of every property that is not connected to a municipal sewer, and they must keep licensing it for the life of the building. A single parcel generates a decades-long paper trail — a soil evaluation with percolation readings cut into the ground, an engineered system design sized from those readings, a construction permit naming a bonded installer, a cover inspection performed before the trenches are backfilled, an operating permit that obliges the owner to pump the tank on a schedule, a failure report when effluent surfaces in the yard, and finally a transfer certificate when the property is sold. Today that trail lives in filing cabinets, spreadsheets and the institutional memory of two or three long-serving specialists. When groundwater goes bad, nobody can answer the question that matters — *which permitted systems sit on parcels whose wells are under advisory, and are any of them overdue for pumping?*

This document specifies the REST API for the department's on-site wastewater register: a single system of record that holds parcels, wells, water samples, site evaluations, system designs, contractor licences, construction permits, field inspections, operating permits, pumping records, repair orders and real-estate transfer inspections, and that enforces the statutory gates between them rather than trusting staff to remember them. The register is a compliance instrument first and a workflow tool second. Every fee assessed against a parcel is conserved when the underlying permit is voided; every hydraulic allowance consumed by a permit is released when that permit lapses; every advisory posted against a well propagates outward to the operating permits, pumping deadlines and risk ratings that depend on it. Where the statute distinguishes an inclusive threshold from an exclusive one, the API distinguishes them too.

Two design constraints deserve early mention. First, the department operates a **segregation-of-duties** control: the specialist who approves a system design on paper may not be the specialist who signs off the buried-works cover inspection built from that design, because the cover inspection is the last moment anyone can see what was actually installed. Second, the entire system is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that multi-year validity windows, five-year permit terms and calendar-month pumping intervals can be exercised deterministically. Every deadline, expiry, window and interval described below is measured against that clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Departmental Actors and Access Control

Every request to the register must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the seven roles below exactly, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles the target operation authorises, the refusal is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorisation, and both are evaluated before the server looks for any resource named in the path.

The seven roles are `Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `Installer`, `Pumper`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`.

An `Applicant` is the property owner or their agent. They register parcels and wells, pay permit fees and request transfer certificates. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not the primary key of any persisted entity; the server records it on the parcels the applicant creates, as `owner_user_id`, and ownership checks on parcel-scoped operations compare that stored `owner_user_id` against the caller's `X-User-ID`.

A `DesignProfessional` is the certified soil evaluator and system designer who cuts site evaluations, logs percolation holes, and draws and submits system designs. When `X-User-Role` is `DesignProfessional`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `evaluator_user_id` on the site evaluations that professional opens and as `designer_user_id` on the designs they draw, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

An `Installer` is a licensed installation contractor. When `X-User-Role` is `Installer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor` row; ownership and licence checks on any resource that references a contractor compare the resource's contractor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A `Pumper` is a licensed septage hauler who files pump-out records. When `X-User-Role` is `Pumper`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Contractor` row, and the pumping record the hauler files stores that identifier as its `contractor_id` without the client supplying it.

An `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` is field and plan-review staff: they review evaluations and designs, schedule and result inspections, post water samples, open and close repair orders, and sign transfer certificates. A `HealthOfficer` is the registrar and signing authority: they license contractors, grant setback variances, issue and void construction permits, issue operating permits, and issue and extend repair orders. A `RecordsClerk` is front-counter staff who takes payments, opens transfer-certificate requests on behalf of walk-ins and runs departmental reports. For each of these three roles `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `review_ehs_user_id` on an approved design, as `inspector_user_id` on a scheduled inspection, as `issued_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a repair order — and it may compare two such recorded identifiers to each other for the segregation-of-duties control, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

Two role-scoped restrictions cut across the ordinary role list. An `Applicant` acting on a parcel-scoped resource — recording a fee payment against a parcel's ledger, or opening a transfer-certificate request — must be the parcel's owner; if `X-User-ID` differs from the parcel's `owner_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_PARCEL_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This restriction binds only the `Applicant` role: a `RecordsClerk` performing either of the same two operations at the front counter is not subject to it. Separately, licence class is enforced against the acting or named contractor: a `Pumper` filing a pump-out record must be a `Contractor` whose `license_class` is `PUMPER` or `DUAL`, and a construction permit naming an installer requires that contractor's `license_class` to be `INSTALLER` or `DUAL`. Either mismatch is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_CLASS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Collections are always returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest record first — so that paging through a parcel's wells or a permit's inspections yields a stable chronological sequence. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters, where a collection offers them, are combined conjunctively: a listing narrowed by both `status` and `contractor_id` returns only records matching both. An omitted filter imposes no restriction.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and Timestamp Rendering

Every persisted record is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no client-supplied component. Clients never propose an `id`; they read it back from the creation response and use it thereafter.

Most records also carry three housekeeping attributes. `created_at` is the logical-clock instant at which the record came into existence. `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical-clock instant of the most recent mutation of that record. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations that arrive as the cascade of an operation performed on some other record. Two entities are exceptions and are deliberately immutable once written: a water sample and a pumping record are laboratory and field evidence, never revised. Both carry `id` and `created_at` only — neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`.

All datetime values in all responses are rendered in ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. This rendering applies uniformly to three families of value: system stamps (`created_at`, `updated_at`); server-computed instants (`valid_until`, `next_pumping_due_on`, `compliance_deadline`, `certified_at`, `certificate_valid_until`, `expires_on`, `issued_on`, `result_recorded_at`, `cover_inspection_passed_at`, `last_sample_collected_at`, `last_pumped_on`); and client-supplied instants echoed back (`evaluated_on`, `collected_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `pumped_on`, `reported_on`, `requested_on`, `license_expires_on`). A client that submits `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` therefore reads back `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The register never consults wall-clock time. It holds a logical clock which, before any client has moved it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. Every temporal rule in this specification — licence expiry, site-evaluation validity, construction-window membership, pumping deadlines, repair deadlines, certificate validity — is evaluated against that clock, and every server-stamped instant is taken from it.

A test-driving capability, available to all seven roles, sets the clock to a supplied instant carried in the request as `to_iso8601`, and answers with the new value under the key `current_utc`. Moving the clock is not a passive act: the register re-evaluates four families of derived state in the same request. Every construction permit whose status is `ISSUED` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and whose `valid_until` falls strictly before the new clock moves to `EXPIRED`, which releases that permit's `design_flow_gpd` from its parcel's hydraulic ledger and cancels every inspection on it that was still `SCHEDULED`. Every operating permit whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `expires_on` falls strictly before the new clock moves to `EXPIRED`. Every operating permit then has its `compliance_status` re-derived against the new clock. Finally every parcel has its `risk_tier` re-derived. A clock that lands exactly on a `valid_until` or an `expires_on` does not expire anything — expiry requires the clock to be strictly beyond the instant.

### 2.6. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A request that offends several rules at once must always be refused for the *first* reason in the department's fixed evaluation ladder, never for whichever check the implementation happens to reach first. Twenty-seven checks are ordered as follows, and the first one that fails determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status of the response.

**Checks 1 and 2 — identity.** The headers are validated as described in §2.1 (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401), then the acting role is matched against the operation's authorised roles (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403). Both apply to every operation in the register.

**Check 3 — path resolution.** Every resource named by a path segment must exist, and path segments are resolved strictly left to right, so a request against a well nested under a non-existent parcel reports the parcel as missing rather than the well. The refusal names the entity that could not be found: `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SYSTEM_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATING_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUMPING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRANSFER_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. This check applies to every operation that carries a path parameter.

**Check 4 — body references.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row, reported with the same 404 family — `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SYSTEM_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_OPERATING_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`. It guards the nine creation operations that carry references in their payloads: recording a well, opening a site evaluation, drawing a system design, issuing a construction permit, scheduling an inspection, issuing an operating permit, filing a pumping record, opening a repair order and requesting a transfer inspection. Note that this precedes body-shape validation, so an unknown parcel reference is reported as a missing parcel even when some other field of the same payload is also malformed.

**Check 5 — request shape.** The payload must be well formed: required fields present, enumerated fields naming a declared member, numbers inside their declared bounds, `scheduled_end` strictly after `scheduled_start`, `valid_until` strictly after `valid_from`, and pagination parameters inside their declared range. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and it applies to every operation.

**Checks 6, 7 and 8 — the three fine-grained authorisation controls**, in this order: parcel ownership for an acting `Applicant` (`ERR_NOT_PARCEL_OWNER`, 403) on the two parcel-scoped writes, fee payment and transfer-inspection request; licence class (`ERR_LICENSE_CLASS_MISMATCH`, 403) on pumping-record filing and construction-permit issue; and segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403) when an inspection result is recorded.

**Check 9 — stale-read protection.** The optimistic-concurrency comparison described in §2.7 runs next, on the two operations that carry an expected-version field: drawing a system design and requesting a transfer inspection. It carries `ERR_STALE_SITE_EVALUATION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` with HTTP 412. Because this check sits above every state and business check, a client working from a stale view is told so before it is told anything about the target's state.

**Checks 10, 11 and 12 — state.** First, the target resource must currently sit in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); this applies to every action operation. Then the cross-entity state gates run (HTTP 409 throughout): a design submit requires its cited site evaluation to be `APPROVED` (`ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_NOT_APPROVED`); a construction permit requires its cited design to be `APPROVED` (`ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`); an operating permit requires its cited construction permit to be `COMPLETED` (`ERR_CONSTRUCTION_NOT_COMPLETED`); a transfer certification requires its cited operating permit to be `ACTIVE`, and a repair-order issue requires its cited operating permit to be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_OPERATING_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`); and recording an inspection result requires the permit's contractor to have an `effective_status` of `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`). Those five gates guard, respectively, design submit, construction-permit issue, operating-permit issue, transfer certification and inspection result. Last in the state group comes inspection sequencing (`ERR_INSPECTION_SEQUENCE`, HTTP 409) on the inspection-result operation.

**Checks 13, 14 and 15 — engineering safety**, all HTTP 422. Soil suitability (`ERR_SOIL_UNSUITABLE`) guards the site-evaluation review decision and the system-design review decision. Well setback (`ERR_SETBACK_VIOLATION`) guards three operations: design submit, design review and construction-permit issue. The water-quality hold (`ERR_WATER_QUALITY_HOLD`) guards the setback variance decision alone.

**Checks 16 through 19 — temporal rules**, all HTTP 409. Site-evaluation lapse (`ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_EXPIRED`) guards design submit and construction-permit issue. The parcel construction-window conflict (`ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`) guards construction-permit issue. The contractor calendar conflict (`ERR_CONTRACTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`) guards inspection scheduling and inspection rescheduling. Then the pair at priority 19: an inspection may only be scheduled while the logical clock lies inside the permit's construction window (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_IN_WINDOW`), and a repair order may only be resolved as repaired while its deadline stands (`ERR_REPAIR_DEADLINE_PASSED`).

**Check 20 — uniqueness**, HTTP 409: the pair of `apn` and `county_code` must be unique across parcels (`ERR_DUPLICATE_APN`) and `license_number` must be globally unique across contractors (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_NUMBER`), guarding parcel registration and contractor registration respectively.

**Checks 21 and 22 — referential holds**, HTTP 409: a transfer certificate may not be signed while the parcel's `transfer_hold` is true (`ERR_TRANSFER_HOLD_ACTIVE`, on the certification operation), and an operating permit may only be reinstated when the parcel carries no repair order in `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED` (`ERR_OPEN_REPAIR_ORDER`, on the administrative operating-permit status operation).

**Checks 23, 24, 25 and 26 — business constraints**, all HTTP 422, in this order: a site evaluation may not be submitted while no percolation hole has been marked valid (`ERR_NO_VALID_PERC_HOLES`); a field-verified transfer request must carry both field conditions and a desk-review request must carry neither (`ERR_FIELD_CONDITIONS_REQUIRED`); an extension must respect both the extension cap and the imminent-hazard prohibition (`ERR_MAX_EXTENSIONS_REACHED` and `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_ALLOWED`, on the repair-order extension operation); and a parcel's aggregate hydraulic capacity must not be exceeded (`ERR_DESIGN_FLOW_EXCEEDS_PARCEL_CAP`, on construction-permit issue).

**Check 27 — money, last of all.** A transfer certificate may only be signed against a parcel whose fee ledger is settled; an outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_FEE_BALANCE`, and this is the register's only HTTP 402. Because it sits at the bottom of the ladder, a parcel that is both fee-encumbered and under a transfer hold reports the hold, not the balance.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Cross-Aggregate Writes

Two creation operations write a new record whose correctness depends on a *parent* record the client has previously read, and in both the parent can legitimately be advanced by someone else in the meantime. These two operations therefore require an explicit optimistic-concurrency token. No other operation in the register accepts or requires a version field.

Drawing a system design must carry `expected_site_evaluation_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the cited site evaluation as the client last observed it. If that number does not equal the evaluation's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_EVALUATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The gate exists because a newer design legitimately supersedes the previous design drawn from the same evaluation, so state alone cannot distinguish an intended supersession from a writer who has not noticed that the evaluation's certified percolation rate and soil loading rate were re-derived underneath them. A successful draw itself advances the evaluation's `version` by 1, so two designs drawn back to back from one evaluation require the second client to re-read the evaluation first.

Requesting a transfer inspection must carry `expected_parcel_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the cited parcel as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the parcel's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Here too a fresh request legitimately supersedes an earlier one for the same parcel — a re-listed sale — so the department cannot resolve the conflict by state, and instead insists that the requester be working from a current view of the parcel's holds, fee balance and open repair-order count. A successful request advances the parcel's `version` by 1.

### 2.8. Measurement, Rounding and Money

Unless a rule below says otherwise, every rounding in the register is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and money is held in whole cents as an integer. Monetary results are rounded to whole cents; percolation rates and site-plan distances are rounded to two decimal places; hydraulic flows and drainfield areas are whole integers; risk points are whole integers.

Three precision rules are load-bearing and are stated here once because they are easy to get wrong. First, wherever a value is divided and then multiplied by rate factors — the drainfield sizing of §3.4 is the case in point — the arithmetic must be performed in exact decimal arithmetic rather than binary floating point, so that an exact quotient such as three hundred and sixty gallons per day divided by a loading rate of 0.60 gallons per day per square foot yields exactly six hundred square feet and is never pushed to six hundred and one by representation error. Second, where a total is assembled from several line items each of which carries a statutory surcharge, the surcharge is applied to each line item and *that line* is rounded to whole cents before the lines are summed; summing raw line items and applying the surcharge once at the end produces a different and incorrect total. Third, thresholds are inclusive or exclusive exactly as written, and the two are never interchangeable: a percolation rate exactly at a tier ceiling stays in the lower tier, a well exactly at the required setback distance is compliant, a licence whose expiry instant equals the clock is still live, and a permit window is checked as a closed interval while a contractor's occupied inspection window is checked as a half-open one.

### 2.9. Derived State and Cascade Discipline

A large fraction of this register is derived rather than supplied. Parcels carry a hydraulic ledger, a fee ledger, an open-order count, a water-quality flag, a transfer hold and a risk tier — none of which a client ever writes. Site evaluations carry two percolation-rate variants and a soil loading rate. Designs carry a design flow, a required drainfield area, a nearest-well distance, a required setback and a setback posture. Operating permits carry a pumping interval, a next-pumping deadline and a compliance posture. The rule for all of them is the same: whenever an operation changes any input a derived value depends on, that derived value is recomputed inside the same request, before the response is written, and the record carrying it has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

Cascades propagate transitively and are completed within the triggering request. When one record's change forces a second record to change state, and that second change forces a third, all three are settled before the response is returned; there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in the register. Each cascade step bumps the `version` and `updated_at` of every record it touches, including records the client never named. The most consequential chains are spelled out where they belong in §3 — a well entering advisory drives its parcel's flag, which drives every active operating permit's monitoring obligation, which halves the pumping interval, which pulls the next deadline earlier, which can move the compliance posture; and a repair order being issued suspends its operating permit, which blocks that permit's outstanding transfer requests.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Parcel: Hydraulic Allowance, Ledgers and Risk

Everything in the register hangs off a parcel. An `Applicant` opens one by supplying its assessor parcel number as `apn`, the two-letter `county_code` of the county it sits in, its street address as `site_address`, its lot area in square feet as `lot_size_sqft`, and the potable water source serving it as `water_source`, which is either `PUBLIC_WATER` or `PRIVATE_WELL`. The parcel number, county code and street address must each be non-empty, and the lot area must be at least 1 square foot. The pair of `apn` and `county_code` is the parcel's legal identity and no two parcels may share it; a second registration of the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APN`. On success the register copies the creating caller's `X-User-ID` into `owner_user_id`, sets all four fee-ledger counters to zero, sets `risk_tier` to `LOW` and `transfer_hold` to false, and derives the parcel's hydraulic allowance.

That allowance, `max_design_flow_gpd`, is the ceiling on how much sewage flow the department will ever permit onto the lot. It is derived from lot area alone: divide `lot_size_sqft` by 20 square feet per gallon per day, discard the fractional part, and then cap the result at 1500 gallons per day. A quarter-acre lot of ten thousand square feet therefore earns an allowance of 500 gallons per day, while a ten-acre lot is still held to 1500.

Against that ceiling stands `permitted_design_flow_gpd`, the flow already committed. It is not a running counter that the department nudges up and down; it is re-derived from scratch as the sum of `design_flow_gpd` over every construction permit on the parcel whose status is `ISSUED`, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, `COVER_PASSED` or `COMPLETED`. Permits that have gone `VOIDED` or `EXPIRED` are excluded from that sum, which is what gives the register its **flow-conservation property**: issuing a permit for some quantity of flow and then voiding that same permit must leave `permitted_design_flow_gpd` exactly equal to its value before the issue, not merely close to it.

The fee ledger works on the same principle of separately-tracked lifetime totals. `fees_assessed_cents` is the lifetime total of permit fees charged to the parcel and grows by a permit's `fee_cents` when that permit is issued. `fees_paid_cents` is the lifetime total of payments and grows by `amount_cents` each time a payment is recorded. `fees_credited_cents` is the lifetime total of reversals and grows by a permit's `fee_cents` when that permit is voided. The outstanding `fee_balance_cents` is then the assessed total less the paid total less the credited total. It may legitimately be negative when a parcel has overpaid. The **money-conservation property** mirrors the flow one: issuing a permit whose fee is some number of cents and then voiding that permit must leave `fee_balance_cents` exactly where it started, while `fees_assessed_cents` and `fees_credited_cents` have each grown by that same number of cents.

Payments are taken at the counter or online. Recording one is a parcel-scoped write available to the `Applicant` who owns the parcel and to any `RecordsClerk`; it carries a single `amount_cents` figure, adds that figure to `fees_paid_cents`, re-derives `fee_balance_cents`, increments the parcel's `version` by 1, and answers with the refreshed parcel.

Three further parcel attributes are pure roll-ups of what is happening elsewhere. `open_repair_order_count` is the number of repair orders on the parcel whose status is `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`. `water_quality_flag` is true exactly when at least one well on the parcel has an `advisory_status` of `ADVISORY` or `CONDEMNED`. `transfer_hold` is true when any of three conditions holds: the parcel has at least one open repair order, or any operating permit on the parcel is `REVOKED`, or any well on the parcel is `CONDEMNED`; while it is true, no transfer certificate may be signed for that parcel.

`risk_tier` is the department's triage rating, computed as a point score. A parcel scores 40 points when its `water_quality_flag` is true. It scores 25 points for each open repair order, counting at most 2 of them, so the repair-order contribution never exceeds 50 points. It scores a further 20 points when any operating permit on the parcel has a `compliance_status` of `DELINQUENT`, and 35 points when any operating permit on the parcel has a `status` of `REVOKED` — both of these are one-off awards regardless of how many permits qualify, and every operating permit on the parcel is considered regardless of its own status. A total of 60 points or more makes the tier `HIGH`; a total of 25 points or more, but under 60, makes it `ELEVATED`; anything below 25 leaves it `LOW`. Both comparisons are at-or-above, so exactly 25 points is `ELEVATED` and exactly 60 points is `HIGH`. The rating is re-derived whenever the water-quality flag moves, the open-order count moves, or any operating permit on the parcel changes either of its two state dimensions.

Reads of the register are broad. Any of the seven roles may list parcels, optionally narrowed by `county_code`, by `risk_tier` or by `transfer_hold`, and any of the seven may read a single parcel with its derived ledgers and postures. Narrower listings exist for reaching a parcel's children: its wells, optionally narrowed by `well_type`, and its site evaluations, optionally narrowed by `status`; both are available to `Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`. Finally the department maintains a one-page **compliance dossier** for a parcel, readable by `Applicant`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`. It is a computed object, not a stored one, and reports eleven fields: `parcel_id`, `risk_tier`, `transfer_hold`, `fee_balance_cents`, `permitted_design_flow_gpd`, `max_design_flow_gpd`, `open_repair_order_count` and `water_quality_flag` are copied straight off the parcel; `well_count` is the number of wells recorded on it; `active_operating_permit_count` is the number of operating permits on it whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; and `latest_certificate_grade` is the `certificate_grade` of the parcel's transfer inspection with the greatest `certified_at`, or null when the parcel has no certified inspection at all.

### 3.2. Wells, Water Samples and the Groundwater Advisory Cascade

A well is recorded on a parcel by an `Applicant` or a `DesignProfessional`, who supply the `parcel_id` it sits on, its `well_type` — `DOMESTIC`, `IRRIGATION` or `MONITORING` — its site-plan position as `location_x_ft` east-west and `location_y_ft` north-south in feet, and its total drilled `depth_ft`, which must be at least 1 foot. A new well always starts with `advisory_status` of `CLEAR` and with `last_sample_collected_at` null.

Adding a well is not an inert act, because the presence of a well is what makes the setback rule bite. The moment a well is recorded, every system design on that parcel whose status is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` or `APPROVED` has its `nearest_well_distance_ft`, `required_setback_ft` and `setback_status` re-derived from scratch. This re-derivation deliberately discards any earlier variance verdict: a design that had been granted a variance, or denied one, is returned to whatever posture the geometry now dictates. Designs already in `PERMITTED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left untouched. This is the *only* event in the register that clears a variance verdict; ordinary resubmission of a design does not.

Laboratory results arrive as water samples, posted against a specific well — the sample records that well as its `well_id`, taken from the path rather than from the payload — by an `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` with three values: the `collected_at` instant the sample was drawn, the measured nitrate-as-nitrogen concentration as `nitrate_mg_l` in milligrams per litre, which may not be negative, and `coliform_present`, true when total coliform bacteria were detected. Samples are evidence and are never revised; each one is stored with an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of housekeeping.

The verdict is computed from that one sample alone — prior samples are never consulted — and lands in `result`. If `coliform_present` is true the result is `UNSAFE`, whatever the nitrate reading. Otherwise, if `nitrate_mg_l` is at or above the maximum contaminant level of 10.0 milligrams per litre the result is `EXCEEDANCE`. Otherwise the result is `PASS`. The comparison against the contaminant level is inclusive: a reading of exactly 10.0 is an `EXCEEDANCE`, while 9.9 is a `PASS`. Alongside the verdict the register writes `exceedance_reasons`, a sorted array of reason codes drawn from `COLIFORM_DETECTED` and `NITRATE_MCL_EXCEEDED`. The coliform code appears whenever `coliform_present` is true; the nitrate code appears whenever `nitrate_mg_l` is at or above 10.0. Both may appear together on a sample that is `UNSAFE` and also over the contaminant level, and the array is empty for a `PASS`.

Posting a sample also stamps the well's `last_sample_collected_at` with the sample's `collected_at` and moves the well's advisory posture. A `CLEAR` well that receives an `EXCEEDANCE` moves `CLEAR → ADVISORY`. A `CLEAR` well that receives an `UNSAFE` moves `CLEAR → CONDEMNED`, and an `ADVISORY` well that receives an `UNSAFE` moves `ADVISORY → CONDEMNED`. A clean resample lifts an advisory: an `ADVISORY` well that receives a `PASS` returns `ADVISORY → CLEAR`. Condemnation, by contrast, is not clearable through this API — no result returns a `CONDEMNED` well to any other posture.

Each of those moves opens a four-step cascade that must complete inside the same request. Step one is the well's own transition, just described. Step two is the parcel: a well entering `ADVISORY` or `CONDEMNED` sets the parcel's `water_quality_flag` to true and re-derives `risk_tier`; a well entering `CONDEMNED` additionally sets `transfer_hold` to true; and a well returning to `CLEAR` causes the flag to be re-derived across *all* the parcel's wells, so it only falls to false when no well on the parcel remains under advisory or condemnation, with `risk_tier` re-derived either way. An `ADVISORY → CONDEMNED` move leaves the flag already true and simply re-derives `risk_tier`. Step three reaches the operating permits: once the parcel's flag has become true, every operating permit on that parcel whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has `monitoring_required` set to true, which halves its `pumping_interval_months` and pulls its `next_pumping_due_on` earlier. Step four follows from that: because the deadline moved, each affected permit has its `compliance_status` re-derived, which may carry it from `COMPLIANT` to `DUE_SOON` or, if the newly-shortened deadline has already passed, straight from `COMPLIANT` to `DELINQUENT`.

Samples are readable per well by `Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`, optionally narrowed by `result`; the same five roles may read an individual well.

### 3.3. Site Evaluation: Cutting the Profile, Certifying the Rate

Before any system can be designed, a `DesignProfessional` must cut a soil profile on the parcel and log what they find. Opening an evaluation names the `parcel_id`, the `evaluated_on` instant at which the profile was cut and logged, the `soil_group` of the limiting horizon — one of `SANDY`, `LOAMY`, `CLAY_LOAM` or `CLAY` — and `depth_to_limiting_layer_in`, the number of inches from finished grade down to bedrock, hardpan or the seasonal water table, which may not be negative. The register stamps `evaluator_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, starts the evaluation in `DRAFT` with an empty `perc_holes` array, and computes `valid_until` as `evaluated_on` advanced by five years. A design may cite this evaluation only while the logical clock stands at or before `valid_until`.

Percolation readings are appended one at a time to a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` evaluation, each carrying its `hole_number`, the observed water-level `drop_inches`, the `elapsed_minutes` over which that drop occurred, and `presoak_ok`, the surveyor's attestation that the required overnight pre-soak was performed. Appending returns the whole refreshed evaluation, since every reading changes the derived rates. The array is append-only; readings are never edited or removed.

Each stored reading carries two derived attributes alongside the four supplied ones. A reading is marked `valid` only when all three of the following hold: `presoak_ok` is true, `drop_inches` is greater than zero, and `elapsed_minutes` is greater than zero. For a valid reading the register also stores `rate_min_per_in`, the `elapsed_minutes` divided by the `drop_inches`, rounded half up to two decimal places. An invalid reading — failed pre-soak, zero drop or zero elapsed time — is still stored, still shown, and carries no rate; it is excluded from every downstream calculation.

From the set of valid readings the register derives **two different percolation rates**, and the distinction between them is the crux of the soil assessment. Take the `rate_min_per_in` of every valid reading. If that set is empty, both `preliminary_perc_rate_min_per_in` and `certified_perc_rate_min_per_in` are null. Otherwise `preliminary_perc_rate_min_per_in` is the arithmetic mean of those per-hole rates, rounded half up to two decimal places — this is the indicative figure shown to the applicant, and one unusually fast hole pulls it down. `certified_perc_rate_min_per_in`, on the other hand, is simply the largest of those per-hole rates: the slowest hole governs. The certified figure is the only one the soil loading tier and the departmental approval gate are permitted to read; the mean is never used for sizing. Note the rounding order — each hole's rate is rounded to two decimals first, and the mean of those already-rounded values is then rounded to two decimals again.

The certified rate selects the **soil loading rate**, `soil_loading_rate_gpd_per_sqft`, in gallons per day per square foot, from a five-band tier table whose bands are each inclusive of their upper bound. A certified rate at or below 5 minutes per inch loads at 1.20. Above 5 and at or below 15, it loads at 0.80. Above 15 and at or below 30, at 0.60. Above 30 and at or below 60, at 0.40. Above 60 and at or below 120, at 0.20. A certified rate above 120 minutes per inch — the department's maximum percolation rate — means the soil will not accept effluent at all, and the loading rate is null; it is likewise null while the certified rate itself is null. The boundaries matter: a certified rate of exactly 15 selects 0.80, while 15.01 selects 0.60.

Submitting the evaluation for departmental review advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and only the `DesignProfessional` whose `X-User-ID` matches the evaluation's `evaluator_user_id` may do so. The submission is refused with `ERR_NO_VALID_PERC_HOLES` unless `perc_holes` contains at least one reading marked valid. On success the register recomputes both percolation-rate variants and the soil loading rate, and increments `revision_round` by 1 — the counter tracks how many times this evaluation has entered review and starts at 0.

Review is recorded by an `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, with an optional `review_note` free-text explanation that is stored on the evaluation and overwrites whatever note the previous review left; before the first review it is null. A rejection moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with no preconditions of its own and no effect beyond recording the note. An approval moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, but only if the soil passes two tests: the `certified_perc_rate_min_per_in` must be at or below 120 minutes per inch, and `depth_to_limiting_layer_in` must be at least 18 inches. Failing either is refused with `ERR_SOIL_UNSUITABLE`.

A `REJECTED` evaluation is not dead. The professional may cut more holes, append more readings and submit again — the `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` edge is the revision loop, it is unbounded, it enforces the same evaluator-identity and valid-hole preconditions, and it recomputes the same derived values and increments `revision_round` exactly as the first submission did.

Approval has a supersession consequence that reaches two levels deep. When an evaluation enters `APPROVED`, every *other* evaluation on the same parcel that was itself `APPROVED` is moved `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`; the parcel may have only one live approved evaluation. And because a superseded evaluation can no longer support a design, every system design citing one of those newly-superseded evaluations, whose own status is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` or `APPROVED`, is moved to `SUPERSEDED` too. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for an evaluation.

Any of `Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk` may read a single evaluation — including its full `perc_holes` array and both rate variants — and may list the designs drawn from it, optionally narrowed by `status`.

### 3.4. The System Design: Sizing the Field and Clearing the Wells

With an approved evaluation in hand a `DesignProfessional` draws the system. The draw names the `site_evaluation_id` it is sized from, carries the `expected_site_evaluation_version` described in §2.7, and supplies the proposed technology as `system_type` — `CONVENTIONAL`, `MOUND`, `ATU` or `DRIP` — the `bedrooms` count the system is sized for, which must be between 1 and 8 inclusive, the septic tank's working `tank_capacity_gal`, which must be at least 750 gallons, and the site-plan coordinates of the drainfield centroid as `drainfield_x_ft` and `drainfield_y_ft` in feet. The register stamps `designer_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, starts the design in `DRAFT` with `review_ehs_user_id` null and `revision_round` at 0, increments the cited evaluation's `version` by 1, and computes the design's sizing and setback posture immediately.

**Design flow.** `design_flow_gpd` is the bedroom count, floored at a minimum of 2 billable bedrooms, multiplied by 120 gallons per day per bedroom. A one-bedroom cottage is therefore sized as though it had two bedrooms, at 240 gallons per day; a four-bedroom house is sized at 480.

**Required drainfield area.** `required_drainfield_sqft` is where four inputs interact, and the order of operations is fixed. Start with the design flow and divide it by the cited evaluation's `soil_loading_rate_gpd_per_sqft`; if that loading rate is null the design cannot be sized at all and the soil-suitability refusal applies. Multiply the quotient by the technology multiplier for the chosen `system_type`: 1.00 for `CONVENTIONAL`, 1.25 for `MOUND`, 0.75 for `ATU` and 0.85 for `DRIP`. Multiply that product by the shallow-soil penalty, which is 1.20 when the evaluation's `depth_to_limiting_layer_in` is strictly less than 36 inches and 1.00 otherwise. The three steps happen in exactly that order — divide by the loading rate, then apply the technology multiplier, then apply the shallow-soil penalty — and in exact decimal arithmetic per §2.8. Round the result *up* to the next whole square foot, and only then clamp it: never below 150 square feet and never above 4000. The clamp is applied last, after both multipliers.

**Well setback.** The department protects drinking-water wells by requiring separation between them and the drainfield. Only wells on the design's parcel whose `well_type` is `DOMESTIC` or `IRRIGATION` qualify; `MONITORING` wells are ignored by this rule entirely. The `required_setback_ft` is a base figure keyed on the technology — 100 feet for `CONVENTIONAL`, 100 for `MOUND`, 75 for `ATU` and 75 for `DRIP` — plus a surcharge of 25 feet when the cited evaluation's `soil_group` is `SANDY`, because sand transmits effluent laterally. That figure is computed whether or not any qualifying well exists.

When the parcel carries no qualifying well, `nearest_well_distance_ft` is null and `setback_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`. When it carries at least one, the register computes the straight-line site-plan distance from the drainfield centroid to each qualifying well head — the square root of the sum of the squared east-west and squared north-south coordinate differences — rounds each distance half up to two decimal places, and takes the smallest as `nearest_well_distance_ft`. If that nearest distance is at or above `required_setback_ft` the posture is `COMPLIANT`; the comparison is inclusive, so a well sitting exactly on the required separation clears. If it falls short, the posture is `VARIANCE_REQUIRED`.

A variance verdict, once recorded, is **sticky**. Neither an ordinary resubmission of the design nor any other recomputation moves `setback_status` away from `VARIANCE_GRANTED` or `VARIANCE_DENIED`; the recomputation on submit re-derives distance and required separation but leaves a granted or denied verdict standing. As noted in §3.2, the single event that wipes a variance verdict and re-derives the posture from bare geometry is the recording of a new well on the parcel.

**The variance decision** itself belongs to a `HealthOfficer`, who answers `GRANT` or `DENY` in a `decision` field on a design whose posture is `VARIANCE_REQUIRED`. The design's own lifecycle status must be `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISIONS_REQUIRED`; a decision on a design in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A grant is additionally barred while the parcel's `water_quality_flag` is true — the department will not relax a well separation on a parcel whose groundwater is already suspect — and that refusal is `ERR_WATER_QUALITY_HOLD`. Granting moves the posture `VARIANCE_REQUIRED → VARIANCE_GRANTED` and adds a variance line item to the eventual construction-permit fee. Denying moves it `VARIANCE_REQUIRED → VARIANCE_DENIED`, which is terminal for that dimension, and carries one further consequence: if the design happened to be `UNDER_REVIEW` at that moment, it is also returned `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, since the reviewer can no longer approve it. A denial is not subject to the water-quality bar.

**Plan review.** Submitting the design moves it `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REVISIONS_REQUIRED → UNDER_REVIEW` on the revision loop; both edges behave identically. Three preconditions guard the submission: the cited evaluation's status must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_NOT_APPROVED`; the logical clock must stand at or before that evaluation's `valid_until`, else `ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_EXPIRED`; and the design's `setback_status` must not be `VARIANCE_DENIED`, else `ERR_SETBACK_VIOLATION`. On success the register recomputes `design_flow_gpd`, `required_drainfield_sqft`, `nearest_well_distance_ft`, `required_setback_ft` and `setback_status`, and increments `revision_round` by 1.

The reviewing `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` answers with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISIONS`. Approval moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and demands two things: `setback_status` must be one of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `COMPLIANT` or `VARIANCE_GRANTED` — a design still requiring a variance, or denied one, is refused with `ERR_SETBACK_VIOLATION` — and the cited evaluation's `soil_loading_rate_gpd_per_sqft` must not be null, else `ERR_SOIL_UNSUITABLE`. Approval stores the reviewer's `X-User-ID` into `review_ehs_user_id`; this single field is the anchor of the segregation-of-duties control described in §3.6. Approval also supersedes siblings: every other design drawn from the same `site_evaluation_id` that was itself `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`. Requesting revisions moves `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISIONS_REQUIRED` with no preconditions and changes nothing but the status, `updated_at` and `version` — the design keeps its computed sizing until the next submission recomputes it.

An approved design leaves `APPROVED` by one of two routes. Issuing a construction permit against it moves it `APPROVED → PERMITTED`, and that route is barred if another construction permit already references this design with a status of `ISSUED`, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, `COVER_PASSED` or `COMPLETED`. Alternatively the design may be superseded — either because a later design drawn from the same evaluation was approved, or because the cited evaluation itself became `SUPERSEDED` — moving `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal. A design that has been permitted and whose permit is subsequently voided returns `PERMITTED → APPROVED`, as described in §3.5.

`Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk` may all read an individual design with its computed sizing and setback posture.

### 3.5. Licensed Firms and the Construction Permit

Only a licensed firm may build or pump. A `HealthOfficer` registers one by supplying its `business_name`, its state-issued `license_number`, the `license_class` the licence authorises — `INSTALLER`, `PUMPER` or `DUAL` — the `license_expires_on` instant at which the licence lapses, and the `bond_amount_cents` of surety posted, which may not be negative. Trade name and licence number must both be non-empty, and the licence number is globally unique across the register; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_NUMBER`. A new firm starts with `status` of `ACTIVE`.

Administrative status is moved by the same officer through an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `REVOKE`. Suspension moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`; reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; revocation moves either `ACTIVE → REVOKED` or `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` and is terminal. None of the four edges carries a precondition, and each re-derives the firm's effective posture.

That posture, `effective_status`, is what every licence gate in the register actually consults, and it is derived rather than stored decisions being read directly. A firm whose `status` is `REVOKED` has an `effective_status` of `REVOKED`. A firm whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` has an `effective_status` of `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if the logical clock has moved strictly past `license_expires_on`, the effective posture is `EXPIRED` even though the administrative status still reads `ACTIVE`. Only a firm that is administratively active and not yet lapsed has an `effective_status` of `ACTIVE`. The expiry comparison is strict: a clock standing exactly on `license_expires_on` still leaves the licence active. Because the derivation is performed at read time and at every gate, a licence can lapse purely through the passage of the logical clock, with no request touching the firm at all. Any of the seven roles may read a contractor record.

Issuing the construction permit is the `HealthOfficer`'s act, and it is the register's busiest single operation. The request names the `system_design_id` being authorised, the `contractor_id` of the installer who will build it, and the construction window as `valid_from`, the first instant work may lawfully begin, and `valid_until`, the last instant the permit authorises work. The window is treated throughout as a **closed** interval — both endpoints belong to it — and `valid_until` must be strictly after `valid_from`.

Six gates stand in front of the issue, and the ladder of §2.6 orders them. The named firm must hold a `license_class` of `INSTALLER` or `DUAL`, else `ERR_LICENSE_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The cited design must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED` — which also excludes a design already carrying a live permit, since that design would be `PERMITTED`. The design's `setback_status` must not be `VARIANCE_REQUIRED` or `VARIANCE_DENIED`, else `ERR_SETBACK_VIOLATION`. The evaluation behind the design must not have lapsed — the logical clock must stand at or before its `valid_until` — else `ERR_SITE_EVALUATION_EXPIRED`. The requested window must not collide with another live permit on the same parcel, else `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the parcel must have the hydraulic headroom, else `ERR_DESIGN_FLOW_EXCEEDS_PARCEL_CAP`.

The **parcel construction window** rule works like this. Every other construction permit on the same parcel whose status is `ISSUED`, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `COVER_PASSED` occupies the parcel over its own closed window; permits that are `COMPLETED`, `VOIDED` or `EXPIRED` release it. Two closed windows collide when the new window's `valid_from` is at or before the existing permit's `valid_until` *and* the existing permit's `valid_from` is at or before the new window's `valid_until`. There is no buffer of any kind, and because both endpoints are included, a new window beginning at the exact instant an existing one ends does collide — deliberately unlike the contractor calendar rule of §3.6, where exact adjacency is permitted.

The **hydraulic headroom** rule compares the parcel's current `permitted_design_flow_gpd` plus the new design's `design_flow_gpd` against the parcel's `max_design_flow_gpd`; if the sum would exceed the allowance the issue is refused. A sum exactly equal to the allowance is permitted.

On success the register snapshots the parcel and the flow onto the permit — `parcel_id` is copied from the cited design's evaluation's parcel and `design_flow_gpd` from the design as it stands at that instant — mints a human-readable `permit_number`, computes the fee, grows the parcel's `fees_assessed_cents` by that fee and re-derives `fee_balance_cents`, re-derives the parcel's `permitted_design_flow_gpd`, moves the cited design `APPROVED → PERMITTED`, and starts the permit in `ISSUED` with `cover_inspection_passed_at` null. The permit number is formed as the parcel's county code, then a hyphen, then the letters `CP`, then a hyphen, then a zero-padded six-digit sequence number; the sequence is the count of construction permits already issued in that same county plus one, so the first permit ever issued in county `WA` is numbered `WA-CP-000001`.

**The permit fee** is assembled from four line items in a fixed order. The first is a base plan-review charge of 45000 cents. The second is a flow charge: the design's `design_flow_gpd` multiplied by 65 cents per gallon per day. The third is a technology surcharge keyed on `system_type` — 0 cents for `CONVENTIONAL`, 30000 for `MOUND`, 42500 for `ATU` and 27500 for `DRIP`. The fourth is a variance charge of 15000 cents, applied only when the design's `setback_status` is `VARIANCE_GRANTED` at the instant of issue, and zero otherwise. Every line item then carries the combined statutory loading: a state surcharge of 4.5 percent and a county technology fee of 2.25 percent, giving a combined multiplier of 1.0675. **The rounding order is pinned:** multiply each line item by 1.0675, round *that line* half up to whole cents, and only then sum the four rounded lines to obtain `fee_cents`. Summing the raw lines and applying the loading once at the end is wrong and produces a different total.

Voiding a permit is the compensating action, available to a `HealthOfficer` on a permit that is `ISSUED` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, carrying a free-text `reason`. It is barred once any inspection on the permit has reached `PASSED` — at that point work has been accepted and the permit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A void moves the permit to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, and carries four consequences. The parcel's `fees_credited_cents` grows by exactly this permit's `fee_cents` and `fee_balance_cents` is re-derived, restoring the money-conservation property of §3.1. The parcel's `permitted_design_flow_gpd` is re-derived, which releases this permit's flow because a `VOIDED` permit is outside the summed set. The cited design returns `PERMITTED → APPROVED`, so it may be permitted afresh. And every inspection on the permit whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `FAILED` moves to `CANCELLED`.

A permit may also die of old age. When the logical clock advances strictly past a permit's `valid_until` while that permit is `ISSUED` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, it moves to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal; its flow is released from the parcel ledger and its `SCHEDULED` inspections are cancelled. Note the asymmetry with a void: expiry cancels only `SCHEDULED` inspections and issues no fee credit, whereas a void cancels `SCHEDULED` and `FAILED` inspections alike and does credit the fee.

Permits are readable individually and as a filtered collection — by `parcel_id`, by `system_design_id`, by `contractor_id` or by `status` — to `Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `Installer`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`.

### 3.6. Field Inspections, the Contractor Calendar and the Four-Eyes Rule

An `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` books a field visit against a permit by naming the `construction_permit_id`, the `inspection_type` — `COVER` for the buried-works check before backfill, `FINAL` for the completion check, or `COMPLAINT` for an unscheduled investigation — and the visit window as `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, with the end strictly after the start. The register snapshots the permit's `contractor_id` onto the inspection, because the firm must be on site, and stamps `inspector_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. The visit starts in `SCHEDULED` with `result_recorded_at` null, an empty `deficiency_codes` array and `reinspection_round` at 0.

Booking is barred unless the logical clock currently lies inside the permit's closed construction window, at or after `valid_from` and at or before `valid_until`; outside it the request is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_IN_WINDOW`. Scheduling the *first* inspection on a permit also moves that permit `ISSUED → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`; subsequent bookings leave the permit's status alone.

**The contractor calendar.** A firm cannot be in two places at once, and it needs travel time between sites. Only inspections whose status is `SCHEDULED` occupy a firm — `PASSED`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED` visits release it. Each occupied window is half-open, running from its `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end`, and a one-sided travel buffer of 45 minutes is appended after each end. A proposed window collides with an existing one when the proposed start falls strictly before the existing end plus 45 minutes *and* the existing start falls strictly before the proposed end plus 45 minutes. Any collision is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The strictness matters: a new visit beginning exactly 45 minutes after an existing visit ends does *not* conflict, while one beginning a single minute earlier does.

Recording the verdict carries a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, and for a failure an accompanying `deficiency_codes` array of code strings which must be present and non-empty — an empty or absent array on a failure is a malformed request and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either verdict requires the parent permit to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `COVER_PASSED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Passing has three further conditions. **Sequencing:** a `COVER` verdict may only be recorded while its permit is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, and a `FINAL` verdict only while its permit is `COVER_PASSED`; recording out of sequence is `ERR_INSPECTION_SEQUENCE`. **Licence:** the permit's contractor must have an `effective_status` of `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` — a firm suspended, revoked or simply lapsed since the permit was issued cannot have its work signed off. **Segregation of duties:** for a `COVER` verdict only, the `X-User-ID` recording the pass must differ from the `review_ehs_user_id` stored on the permit's system design when that design was approved. The cover inspection is the last opportunity anyone has to see what was actually installed before it is buried, so the department refuses to let the same specialist both approve the plan and certify the buried work; a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The rule binds `COVER` verdicts alone: a `FINAL` or `COMPLAINT` verdict may be recorded by the approving specialist.

A pass moves the visit `SCHEDULED → PASSED`, which is terminal, and stamps `result_recorded_at` with the logical clock. A `COVER` pass additionally moves its permit `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → COVER_PASSED` and stamps the permit's `cover_inspection_passed_at` with the same logical-clock instant. A `FINAL` pass moves its permit `COVER_PASSED → COMPLETED`, which is terminal for the permit and is the state that makes it eligible for an operating permit. A `COMPLAINT` pass moves no permit at all.

A failure moves the visit `SCHEDULED → FAILED`, stamps `result_recorded_at`, stores the supplied `deficiency_codes`, and leaves the parent permit's status exactly where it was. Failure is not terminal: the specialist re-books the visit into a fresh window by supplying a new `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, which moves it `FAILED → SCHEDULED`, increments `reinspection_round` by 1 and clears both `result_recorded_at` and `deficiency_codes`. Re-booking runs the same contractor-calendar test as the original booking, and it too requires the parent permit to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `COVER_PASSED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. This is the re-inspection loop, and it is unbounded.

Cancellation is never requested directly; it arrives as a cascade. When a permit is voided, both its `SCHEDULED` and its `FAILED` inspections move to `CANCELLED`. When a permit expires under the clock, only its `SCHEDULED` inspections move to `CANCELLED`. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

`Applicant`, `DesignProfessional`, `Installer`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk` may read an individual visit and may list visits filtered by `construction_permit_id`, by `contractor_id`, by `status` or by `inspection_type`.

### 3.7. The Operating Permit: Two Independent Dimensions

Construction ends and stewardship begins. Once a construction permit reaches `COMPLETED`, a `HealthOfficer` issues the operating permit that licenses the system to run, naming the `construction_permit_id` it follows and the `household_size` the permit is rated for, which must be between 1 and 12 inclusive. A construction permit in any other status is refused with `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_NOT_COMPLETED`. The register copies `parcel_id` from the completed construction permit and snapshots `tank_capacity_gal` from the system design behind it, stamps `issued_on` with the logical clock, sets `expires_on` to five years after `issued_on`, starts `status` at `ACTIVE` with `monitoring_required` false and `last_pumped_on` null, and derives the servicing schedule.

An operating permit carries **two independent state dimensions** that must never be conflated. `status` is the administrative dimension — `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` — and reflects what the department has decided about the permit. `compliance_status` is the pumping dimension — `COMPLIANT`, `DUE_SOON` or `DELINQUENT` — and reflects only where the tank stands against its servicing deadline. An `ACTIVE` permit may perfectly well be `DELINQUENT`, and a `SUSPENDED` permit may be `COMPLIANT`. A change along one dimension never rewrites the other: administratively suspending a permit leaves its compliance posture untouched, and a pump-out that restores compliance does not reinstate a suspended permit.

**The servicing interval.** Divide the permit's `tank_capacity_gal` by its `household_size` to obtain the gallons of tank capacity per resident, as an exact quotient with no rounding. At 500 gallons per person or more, the base interval is 60 months. At 300 or more but under 500, 48 months. At 200 or more but under 300, 36 months. At 125 or more but under 200, 24 months. Below 125, 12 months. Each band is inclusive of its lower bound, so exactly 200.0 gallons per person selects 36 months while 199.99 selects 24. If `monitoring_required` is true the base interval is halved and the fractional part discarded, giving `pumping_interval_months`; otherwise `pumping_interval_months` is the base interval unchanged. The deadline `next_pumping_due_on` is then the anchor advanced by `pumping_interval_months` **calendar months**, where the anchor is `last_pumped_on` when a pump-out has been recorded and `issued_on` when none has. Advancing by calendar months preserves the day of month and the time of day; when the target month is too short to contain that day, the date clamps to the last day of that month.

**The compliance posture** is derived from the deadline and the clock alone; the permit's administrative status is never consulted. Take the whole days from the logical clock to `next_pumping_due_on`, flooring the exact difference, so that the figure is negative once the deadline has passed. A negative figure makes the posture `DELINQUENT`. A figure from 0 through 90 inclusive makes it `DUE_SOON`. Anything above 90 makes it `COMPLIANT`. The boundaries are inclusive on both sides of the due-soon window: exactly 90 days out is `DUE_SOON`, 91 days out is `COMPLIANT`, and exactly 0 days out — the deadline day itself — is still `DUE_SOON`.

Movement along the compliance dimension has two causes. The passage of the logical clock carries a permit `COMPLIANT → DUE_SOON` as the deadline approaches, `DUE_SOON → DELINQUENT` once the clock stands strictly past the deadline, and `COMPLIANT → DELINQUENT` directly when a single clock advance jumps clean over the due-soon window. The other cause is the groundwater cascade of §3.2: when `monitoring_required` flips to true the interval halves, the deadline is pulled earlier, and the same edges fire without the clock having moved at all.

**Filing a pump-out.** A `Pumper` records the service with the `operating_permit_id` of the system serviced, the `pumped_on` instant the tank was emptied, the `gallons_removed`, which must be at least 1, and the two pre-pumping measurements `sludge_depth_in` and `scum_depth_in`, neither of which may be negative. The `pumped_on` instant must be at or before the logical clock — a pump-out dated in the future is a malformed request and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The register takes `contractor_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` rather than from the payload, and marks the record `accepted` true; a submission that fails any gate produces an error rather than a rejected row, so no stored record ever has `accepted` false. Like water samples, pumping records are immutable evidence carrying only `id` and `created_at` for housekeeping.

Filing refreshes the permit: `last_pumped_on` becomes the record's `pumped_on`, `pumping_interval_months` and `next_pumping_due_on` are re-derived from the new anchor, and `compliance_status` is re-derived, which ordinarily carries the permit `DELINQUENT → COMPLIANT` or `DUE_SOON → COMPLIANT`. The parcel's `risk_tier` is re-derived as well, since a delinquent permit was worth points.

**Administrative moves.** A `HealthOfficer` may suspend, reinstate or revoke an operating permit directly through an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `REVOKE`, and the same three moves also arrive as cascades from the repair-order workflow of §3.8. Suspension moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and, as a cascade, moves every transfer inspection citing this permit whose status is `REQUESTED` to `BLOCKED`. Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and returns every `BLOCKED` transfer inspection on this permit to `REQUESTED`; it is barred while the parcel still carries any repair order in `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`, refused with `ERR_OPEN_REPAIR_ORDER`. Revocation moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED` or `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`, is terminal, sets the parcel's `transfer_hold` to true and re-derives its `risk_tier`, and moves every transfer inspection citing this permit whose status is `REQUESTED` or `BLOCKED` to `DENIED` with a `certificate_grade` of `UNSATISFACTORY`. Separately, when the logical clock advances strictly past `expires_on`, a permit that is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `EXPIRED` with no further side effects.

Operating permits are readable individually and as a filtered collection — by `parcel_id`, by `construction_permit_id`, by `status` or by `compliance_status` — to all seven roles. Pumping records are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `operating_permit_id` or by `contractor_id`, to `Applicant`, `Pumper`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`.

### 3.8. Failures, Repair Orders and the Enforcement Cascade

When effluent surfaces in a yard, a toilet backs up, a tank collapses or a well goes bad, the department opens a failure investigation. An `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist` starts one against the failing system by naming the `operating_permit_id`, the observed `failure_type` — `SURFACING_EFFLUENT`, `BACKUP`, `STRUCTURAL` or `GROUNDWATER_CONTAMINATION` — and the `reported_on` instant the failure reached the department. The order is snapshotted with the operating permit's `parcel_id` and begins life in `DRAFT`, with `severity`, `compliance_deadline`, `issued_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` all null and `extension_count` at 0. A `DRAFT` order commits nobody to anything and holds nothing.

Issuing it is the `HealthOfficer`'s act, and it starts the enforcement cascade. The cited operating permit must be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_OPERATING_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`. On issue the order moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, `issued_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and severity and deadline are computed. **Severity** turns on two things: if the `failure_type` is `GROUNDWATER_CONTAMINATION`, or if the parcel's `water_quality_flag` stands true at that moment whatever the failure type, the severity is `IMMINENT_HAZARD`. Otherwise a `SURFACING_EFFLUENT` or `BACKUP` failure is `URGENT`, and a `STRUCTURAL` failure is `ROUTINE`. The `compliance_deadline` is then the logical clock advanced by whole days according to that severity: 10 days for `IMMINENT_HAZARD`, 30 days for `URGENT` and 90 days for `ROUTINE`, preserving the time of day.

Issuing cascades three levels outward, all inside the same request. First the parcel: `open_repair_order_count` is re-derived and rises by one, `transfer_hold` becomes true, and `risk_tier` is re-derived. Second the operating permit: it moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. Third the transfer certificates hanging off that permit: because the permit entered `SUSPENDED`, every transfer inspection citing it whose status is `REQUESTED` moves `REQUESTED → BLOCKED`.

An `ISSUED` order may be granted more time. Extension moves it `ISSUED → EXTENDED`, or `EXTENDED → EXTENDED` on the self-loop, advancing `compliance_deadline` by a further 30 whole days and incrementing `extension_count` by 1 each time. Two limits bound the loop: an extension is refused with `ERR_MAX_EXTENSIONS_REACHED` once `extension_count` has reached 2, so an order may be extended at most twice for a total of 60 additional days; and an extension is refused outright with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_ALLOWED` when the order's severity is `IMMINENT_HAZARD`, because a ten-day hazard deadline is not negotiable.

Closing the order is the specialist's act again, and it is the register's sharpest **branch**. The `outcome` is either `REPAIRED` or `ESCALATE`, from either `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`, and the two branches diverge in their consequences. Both stamp `closed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and both re-derive the parcel's `open_repair_order_count`, which falls by one.

A `REPAIRED` outcome moves the order to `RESOLVED`, which is terminal, and is permitted only while the logical clock stands at or before `compliance_deadline`; once the deadline has passed the department will not accept a repair closure and the request is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The cited operating permit returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, but only when no other repair order on the parcel remains in `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`; and every transfer inspection on that permit whose status is `BLOCKED` returns `BLOCKED → REQUESTED`. The parcel's `transfer_hold` and `risk_tier` are then re-derived, so the hold falls away if nothing else is holding the parcel.

An `ESCALATE` outcome moves the order to `ESCALATED`, likewise terminal, and carries no deadline precondition — the department may always escalate, including after the deadline has lapsed. Where the repaired branch reinstates, this branch revokes: the cited operating permit moves to `REVOKED`, and every transfer inspection on that permit whose status is `REQUESTED` or `BLOCKED` moves to `DENIED` with `certificate_grade` set to `UNSATISFACTORY`. The parcel's `transfer_hold` stays true — a revoked operating permit holds the parcel on its own account, independently of the now-closed order — and `risk_tier` is re-derived, picking up the 35 points that a revoked permit contributes.

Repair orders are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `parcel_id`, by `operating_permit_id`, by `status` or by `severity`, to `Applicant`, `Installer`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`.

### 3.9. Real-Estate Transfer Certificates and Departmental Reporting

When a property changes hands, the buyer wants a departmental verdict on the septic system. A request is opened by the owning `Applicant` or by a `RecordsClerk` at the counter, naming the `parcel_id` changing hands, the `operating_permit_id` whose system is being certified, the `expected_parcel_version` of §2.7, the `requested_on` instant the certificate was asked for, the `buyer_name` of the purchasing party and the `seller_name` of the selling party — both non-empty — and the `inspection_mode`. That mode is either `DESK_REVIEW`, a records-only assessment, or `FIELD_VERIFIED`, in which a specialist actually looks at the tank and the field. An optional `expedited` flag, false when omitted, buys faster turnaround for a surcharge.

The mode governs which field observations the request must carry. A `FIELD_VERIFIED` request must supply both `tank_condition` and `drainfield_condition`, each `GOOD`, `FAIR` or `POOR`. A `DESK_REVIEW` request must supply neither. Omitting either on a field-verified request, or supplying either on a desk review, is refused with `ERR_FIELD_CONDITIONS_REQUIRED`.

**The charges** are settled at request time. The `subtotal_cents` is a base transfer fee of 18500 cents, plus 9500 cents when the mode is `FIELD_VERIFIED`, plus a records-research fee of 4000 cents when the parcel's `open_repair_order_count` is greater than zero at that moment. When `expedited` is false, `expedite_fee_cents` is zero. When it is true, an expedite rate is selected **from the subtotal alone**: 30 percent when `subtotal_cents` is strictly below 20000, 20 percent when it is strictly below 30000, and 15 percent otherwise. The `expedite_fee_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to whole cents. `total_due_cents` is then the subtotal plus the expedite fee. The evaluation order here is pinned and deliberately non-obvious: although the rate is described as keyed on a total, the tier is chosen from the pre-expedite subtotal and is never re-selected after the expedite fee is added, and the calculation is never iterated to a fixed point.

A new request starts in `REQUESTED` with `certificate_grade`, `certified_at` and `certificate_valid_until` all null, and it advances the parcel's `version` by 1. One exception: if the cited operating permit is already `SUSPENDED` at the moment the request is opened, the request starts in `BLOCKED` instead. Thereafter the request tracks its permit — suspension moves it `REQUESTED → BLOCKED`, reinstatement moves it `BLOCKED → REQUESTED`, and revocation moves it from either `REQUESTED` or `BLOCKED` straight to `DENIED` with a grade of `UNSATISFACTORY`.

**Signing the certificate** belongs to an `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, who answers a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `DENY`. A denial moves the request `REQUESTED → DENIED` or `BLOCKED → DENIED`, sets `certificate_grade` to `UNSATISFACTORY`, and leaves both `certified_at` and `certificate_valid_until` null; it carries no preconditions. A certification moves `REQUESTED → CERTIFIED` and requires three things in the ladder order of §2.6: the cited operating permit must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_OPERATING_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the parcel's `transfer_hold` must be false, else `ERR_TRANSFER_HOLD_ACTIVE`; and the parcel's `fee_balance_cents` must be exactly zero, else `ERR_OUTSTANDING_FEE_BALANCE`. A `BLOCKED` request cannot be certified at all — only denied — because its permit is by definition not active. On success `certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `certificate_valid_until` is set to 180 days after `certified_at`. Both `CERTIFIED` and `DENIED` are terminal.

**The grade** is computed in two steps, and the second step differs by mode. Step one produces a records-only base grade, identically for both modes. If the parcel's `open_repair_order_count` is greater than zero, or the cited operating permit's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`, the base is `UNSATISFACTORY`. Otherwise, if the permit's `compliance_status` is `DUE_SOON` or `DELINQUENT`, or the parcel's `water_quality_flag` is true, the base is `CONDITIONAL`. Otherwise the base is `SATISFACTORY`.

Under `DESK_REVIEW`, step two does nothing: `certificate_grade` is the base grade unchanged, and field conditions are neither collected nor consulted.

Under `FIELD_VERIFIED`, step two lets what the specialist actually saw move the verdict in either direction. If either `tank_condition` or `drainfield_condition` is `POOR`, the grade is `UNSATISFACTORY` regardless of the base. Otherwise, if either is `FAIR`, the grade is `CONDITIONAL` when the base was `SATISFACTORY`, and otherwise the base unchanged. If both are `GOOD`, field verification may *clear* a records-only `CONDITIONAL`, but only in one narrow circumstance: when the base is `CONDITIONAL`, the permit's `compliance_status` is `DUE_SOON`, and the parcel's `water_quality_flag` is false — that is, when the sole defect was an approaching pumping deadline — the grade is upgraded to `SATISFACTORY`. In every other case with both conditions `GOOD`, the grade is the base unchanged. The consequence is deliberate: two requests against identical records yield `CONDITIONAL` under `DESK_REVIEW` and `SATISFACTORY` under `FIELD_VERIFIED` whenever the only blemish is a due-soon deadline.

Transfer inspections are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `parcel_id`, by `operating_permit_id`, by `status` or by `inspection_mode`, to `Applicant`, `EnvironmentalHealthSpecialist`, `HealthOfficer` and `RecordsClerk`.

**Departmental reporting.** A `HealthOfficer` or a `RecordsClerk` may pull a computed, department-wide compliance roll-up, optionally scoped to a single county by supplying `county_code`; with no scope the report covers every parcel in the register. It reports eight figures. `parcel_count` is the number of parcels in scope. `active_operating_permit_count` is the number of operating permits whose parcel is in scope and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `delinquent_permit_count` and `due_soon_permit_count` are the numbers of in-scope operating permits whose `compliance_status` is `DELINQUENT` and `DUE_SOON` respectively, counted regardless of their administrative status. `open_repair_order_count` is the number of in-scope repair orders whose status is `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`. `high_risk_parcel_count` is the number of in-scope parcels whose `risk_tier` is `HIGH`. `total_fee_balance_cents` is the sum of `fee_balance_cents` over the in-scope parcels. And `advisory_well_count` is the number of wells on in-scope parcels whose `advisory_status` is `ADVISORY` or `CONDEMNED`.
