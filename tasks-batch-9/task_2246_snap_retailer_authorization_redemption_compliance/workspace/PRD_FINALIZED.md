# SNAP Retailer Authorization & Redemption Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The Food and Nutrition Service authorizes roughly a quarter of a million retail firms to accept Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program benefits, and every one of those firms sits somewhere on a single long arc: it applies, it is inspected, it is authorized, it redeems benefits, its redemption pattern is watched, and — for a small but consequential minority — it is investigated, charged, sanctioned and disqualified. Today that arc is administered across disconnected systems. Store-visit findings arrive on paper and are reconciled against self-reported inventory by hand. Redemption anomalies are spotted by analysts eyeballing spreadsheets against a mental model of how much a two-lane convenience store *should* redeem. Charge letters, retailer replies, civil-money-penalty elections and administrative-review decisions are tracked in a case-management tool that has no idea whether the underlying store is still allowed to swipe an EBT card. The result is inconsistent enforcement, missed statutory deadlines, and — worst of all — permanently disqualified operators reappearing under a new store name because nobody joined the ownership records.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that carries a retail firm from first application through terminal enforcement. It has four jobs. First, **eligibility**: assess staple-food stock and staple-sales share against the two statutory criteria, both at the desk from self-reported inventory and on site from what an inspector actually measures, and refuse to paper over the difference between the two. Second, **redemption integrity**: split every basket into SNAP-eligible and SNAP-ineligible tender at the point of sale, aggregate authorized redemptions into monitoring periods, reconcile those aggregates against the EBT processor's settlement file, and score each closed period for anomalies against what the store's own footprint and sales can plausibly support. Third, **due process**: run the charge-letter, reply, determination, administrative-review and enforcement pipeline on a logical clock with hard statutory windows, with the independence of the reviewing officer enforced by the server rather than by policy memo. Fourth, **conservation**: EBT terminal hardware, penalty dollars and inspector hours are finite, physically real quantities, and the API must never manufacture or lose any of them.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. All time is read from an explicitly settable **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time, so that deadline behaviour, evidence decay and interval conflicts are exactly reproducible. And all money is integer cents, all ratios are integer basis points; no floating-point percentage is ever stored, because a rounding drift of one cent on a monthly penalty becomes a drift of ten cents on a trafficking penalty and a defensible determination becomes an indefensible one.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API is mounted directly at the root: there is no shared URL prefix, and `Owner`, `Store`, `Application`, `Inspector`, `StoreVisit`, `PosTransaction`, `RedemptionPeriod`, `Investigation`, `SanctionCase`, `EquipmentDepot`, `EbtTerminal`, `TerminalDeployment` and `EquipmentOrder` are peer top-level collections wired together by references rather than by nesting. Only two resources are addressed beneath a parent: a case's civil-money-penalty elections and a case's penalty ledger.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is carried by two request headers on every call: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capability set that principal is exercising. Both headers are mandatory everywhere with exactly one exception — owner registration, which is how an unregistered firm bootstraps itself into the program. A call that omits either header anywhere else is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` (HTTP 400). A call whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the target endpoint permits is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Anonymous`, `RetailerOwner`, `StoreClerk`, `Inspector`, `FnsAdmin`, `FnsAnalyst`, and `AdminReviewOfficer`.

- `Anonymous` is an unregistered caller and may do exactly one thing: create an owner record. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque caller identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- `RetailerOwner` is the firm owner. When `X-User-Role` is `RetailerOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Owner`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Owner` compare the resource's owning-firm reference to `X-User-ID`. For store-scoped resources the comparison walks one hop — the resource's store's `owner_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. A `RetailerOwner` files applications, supplies corrected documents, withdraws applications and stores, replies to charge letters, files and pays civil money penalties, requests administrative review, and orders equipment, always and only for stores its own firm controls. It is the exclusive holder of four operations: filing a charge-letter reply, requesting administrative review, filing a penalty election, and remitting a penalty payment.
- `StoreClerk` is a cashier acting for one authorized store. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreClerk`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; a clerk may ring up or void a sale only for the store whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`. Ringing up and voiding EBT sales are exclusive to `StoreClerk` and `FnsAdmin`.
- `Inspector` is an FNS field inspector. When `X-User-Role` is `Inspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector`; a visit-scoped action is permitted only when the visit's `inspector_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Starting a visit, submitting findings and recording no-access are exclusive to `Inspector` and `FnsAdmin`.
- `FnsAdmin` is the retailer-operations administrator and holds the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `FnsAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands attribution — as `charge_issued_by_user_id` and `determined_by_user_id` on a `SanctionCase`, and as `opened_by_user_id` or `concluded_by_user_id` on an `Investigation` when an administrator acts there — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. `FnsAdmin` may exercise every write endpoint in this specification and every read endpoint, with exactly one exclusion: it may not decide an administrative review.
- `FnsAnalyst` is the redemption-monitoring analyst. When `X-User-Role` is `FnsAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `opened_by_user_id` and `concluded_by_user_id` on investigations but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. An analyst opens monitoring periods, imports settlements, closes periods, opens investigations, appends evidence and concludes investigations, and schedules or cancels store visits. An analyst may not issue a charge letter, a determination, a finalization or an enforcement.
- `AdminReviewOfficer` is the independent review officer. When `X-User-Role` is `AdminReviewOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `admin_review_decided_by_user_id` and compares it against the recorded charge and determination attributions, but never against any entity's `id`. Deciding an administrative review is the officer's sole write capability; beyond that it holds read access to owners, stores, applications, store visits, transactions, monitoring periods, investigations, sanction cases, elections and penalty ledgers.

When a caller holds a permitted role but is acting outside its own scope — a `RetailerOwner` touching a store its firm does not own, a `StoreClerk` ringing up a sale for a different store, or an `Inspector` acting on a visit assigned to someone else — the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no lower than 1 and no higher than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no lower than 0. Results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states a different ordering for a specific collection.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`.

List endpoints additionally accept the domain filters named in their §4 rows; every filter is optional and multiple filters combine conjunctively. Owners may be filtered by `sanction_flag` and `status`. Stores may be filtered by `owner_id`, `predecessor_store_id`, `authorization_status`, `sanction_status`, `state_code`, `store_type` and `redemption_flag_level`. Applications may be filtered by `store_id`, `status`, `application_type` and `claimed_criterion`. Inspectors may be filtered by `state_code` and `status`. Store visits may be filtered by `application_id`, `inspector_id`, `status` and `visit_type`. Point-of-sale transactions may be filtered by `store_id`, `terminal_deployment_id`, `status` and `split_tender_applied`. Monitoring periods may be filtered by `store_id`, `status`, `flag_level` and `reconciliation_status`. Investigations may be filtered by `store_id`, `trigger_period_id`, `status` and `opened_reason`. Sanction cases may be filtered by `store_id`, `investigation_id`, `predecessor_case_id`, `status`, `sanction_type` and `violation_type`. Penalty-ledger reads may be filtered by `entry_type` and election reads by `status`. Depots may be filtered by `state_code`. Terminals may be filtered by `depot_id`, `status`, `model` and `serial_number`. Deployments may be filtered by `store_id`, `terminal_id` and `status`. Equipment orders may be filtered by `store_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Deadline Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every deadline comparison, every evidence-decay computation, every interval conflict test and every timestamp the server writes takes its value from a **logical clock** that the API exposes for test-driving. Before any caller sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time, and it never advances on its own. An administrator-only capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and every request served afterwards sees that instant.

Deadlines are computed once, at the moment of the triggering event, and stored on the row rather than recomputed on read. A charge letter fixes `reply_due_at` and `cmp_election_due_at` at ten days after `charge_letter_sent_at`. A determination fixes `admin_review_due_at` at ten days after `determined_at`. Finalization of a disqualification fixes `effective_date` at thirty days after `final_decision_at`. All three windows are **exclusive at the closing edge**: an action permitted "before" a deadline is refused when the logical clock has reached the deadline instant exactly. Conversely, actions gated on a start instant are **inclusive**: a visit may be started when the clock is at or after `window_start`, a monitoring period may be closed when the clock is at or after `period_end`, and enforcement may proceed when the clock is at or after `effective_date`.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics — an application id and a sanction-case id are indistinguishable by inspection. Clients never supply an `id`.

Almost every resource also carries three bookkeeping fields. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, both read from the logical clock; `created_at` is fixed at insertion and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of that row. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade from another resource's transition rather than by a direct call against the row itself.

There is exactly one exception. A `PenaltyLedgerEntry` is append-only: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because a ledger row is never edited — a correction is a new compensating row.

The one type of value that never appears as a top-level resource field is a foreign key the client did not supply. `store_id` on a `SanctionCase`, `sanction_case_id` on a `CmpElection` and `sanction_case_id` on a `PenaltyLedgerEntry` are all copied by the server from the parent context, not accepted from the request body.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Store and Case Snapshots

Two write operations file a derived artefact against a snapshot of a parent row, and both therefore demand that the caller prove which snapshot it read.

Filing an application requires `expected_store_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Store`. An application is filed against a snapshot of the store's eligibility profile — its `store_type`, `checkout_lane_count`, `staple_shelf_linear_feet`, `annual_gross_sales_cents`, `annual_staple_sales_cents` and `hardship_designated` flag. Every store profile edit and every application creation increments `Store.version`, so a caller that read the store and then saw the profile edited must re-read before filing. If `expected_store_version` does not equal the store's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The same field and the same rejection apply to a store profile edit, which likewise names `expected_store_version` and likewise refuses a stale value with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Filing a civil-money-penalty election requires `expected_sanction_case_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SanctionCase`. An election quotes an amount derived from the case's charge date and the store's closed redemption history; charge-letter issuance, retailer replies, determinations and review decisions all increment `SanctionCase.version`. If `expected_sanction_case_version` does not equal the case's current `version`, the election is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SANCTION_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than silently re-quoted against a case the client has not observed.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field. Filing a reply, requesting review, deciding a review, finalizing, enforcing, remitting a payment, activating a deployment and every other action operate without an expected-version input.

### 2.7. Integer Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities are integers denominated in cents; the API neither accepts nor emits fractional cents. All shares and ratios that are persisted are integers denominated in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is 100 percent and 5,000 basis points is exactly 50.00 percent. No floating-point percentage is ever stored on a row.

Wherever a computation produces a non-integral cent or point value, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole integer — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero, upward. Where a formula converts a duration or a ratio to an integer count, the operation is a **floor** (truncation toward zero for the non-negative quantities used here), and the individual calculation subsections in §3 say which of the two applies at each step.

Three formulas pin the *order* of rounding rather than merely its direction, because rounding at the wrong step produces a defensible-looking but wrong answer:

- Sales tax on a basket is computed by summing the extended amounts of the ineligible lines **first** and applying the tax rate **once** to that sum. Applying the rate per line and summing rounded per-line taxes is incorrect.
- A period's anomaly score keeps its three weighted components as exact values and applies a **single** half-up rounding to the weighted sum. Rounding each weighted term first drifts by up to one point and flips a flag band.
- A monthly civil money penalty is rounded to whole cents **before** it is multiplied by the month count. Multiplying an unrounded average by the rate and by the months and rounding once at the end differs by up to one cent per month.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence: The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A single malformed or ill-timed request routinely breaks several rules at once. The server evaluates its checks in a fixed ascending order and returns the error of the **first** rule violated, so the response is deterministic no matter how many rules a request breaks. The order is:

1. **User context.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present on every endpoint except owner creation — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` (400).
2. **Role permission.** `X-User-Role` must appear in the called endpoint's role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Path existence, left to right.** Every identifier in the URL path must resolve to an existing row, checked in the order the segments appear, yielding `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POS_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SANCTION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_EQUIPMENT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate (all 404). This applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Body reference existence.** Every reference supplied in the request body must resolve, checked in the field order the endpoint declares, yielding `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND` (all 404). This guards `POST /stores`, `POST /applications`, `POST /store-visits`, `POST /pos-transactions`, `POST /redemption-periods`, `POST /investigations`, `POST /sanction-cases`, `POST /ebt-terminals`, `POST /terminal-deployments` and `POST /equipment-orders`.
5. **Resource ownership.** A `RetailerOwner` acting on a store-scoped resource must be the owner of that store, a `StoreClerk` must be scoped to the transacting store, and an `Inspector` must be the visit's assigned inspector — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This guards nine operations: `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/reply`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-elections`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/administrative-review`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-payments`, `POST /pos-transactions`, `POST /store-visits/{visitId}/start`, `POST /store-visits/{visitId}/findings`, `POST /store-visits/{visitId}/no-access` and `POST /equipment-orders`.
6. **Segregation of duties.** The three independence rules of §2.11 are checked next, all yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). They guard exactly three operations: `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/administrative-review-decision`, `POST /store-visits/{visitId}/findings` and `POST /investigations/{investigationId}/conclude`.
7. **Schema.** Required fields present, types correct, enum values within their declared sets, numeric bounds honoured, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, `deploy_end` at or after `deploy_start`, `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, and arrays non-empty where an array is required — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This applies to every write endpoint.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied expected-version value must equal the parent's current `version`, yielding `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SANCTION_CASE_VERSION` (412). This guards `POST /applications`, `PATCH /stores/{storeId}` and `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-elections`.
9. **State.** The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); a transaction naming a deployment that is not active is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) and a sanction case opened from a non-substantiated investigation with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (409). This applies to every action endpoint.
10. **Trading authorization.** A redemption, a terminal activation or an equipment placement requires the store's `authorization_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (403). Only the authorization dimension gates trading: a store whose `sanction_status` is `CHARGED`, `UNDER_ADMIN_REVIEW`, or `SANCTION_FINAL` under a penalty-in-lieu determination keeps redeeming. This guards `POST /pos-transactions`, `POST /terminal-deployments/{deploymentId}/activate` and `POST /equipment-orders/{orderId}/place`.
11. **Owner-level bars.** An owner carrying a permanent disqualification may not be approved for a new authorization (`ERR_OWNER_PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED`, 403) and an owner carrying two or more may not file at all (`ERR_OWNER_BARRED`, 403). This guards `POST /applications/{applicationId}/submit` and `POST /applications/{applicationId}/decision`.
12. **Temporal gates.** Replies before `reply_due_at`, review requests before `admin_review_due_at`, penalty elections before `cmp_election_due_at`, determinations only once the reply window has closed, enforcement only at or after `effective_date`, visit starts only at or after `window_start`, and period closes only at or after `period_end` — yielding `ERR_REPLY_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_REPLY_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_ADMIN_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CMP_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_NOT_DUE`, `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` and `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (all 409). These guard `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/reply`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/determination`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/administrative-review`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-elections`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/enforce`, `POST /store-visits/{visitId}/start` and `POST /redemption-periods/{periodId}/close`.
13. **Interval conflicts.** The three interval families of §2.10 are checked, yielding `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TERMINAL_ALREADY_COMMITTED` or `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (all 409). These guard `POST /store-visits`, `POST /terminal-deployments` and `POST /redemption-periods`.
14. **Uniqueness.** A terminal `serial_number` is unique across all non-retired terminals (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TERMINAL_SERIAL`), a `period_index` is unique per store (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_INDEX`), and a store may hold at most one application in a non-terminal status (`ERR_APPLICATION_ALREADY_OPEN`) — all 409. These guard `POST /ebt-terminals`, `POST /redemption-periods` and `POST /applications`.
15. **Aggregate capacity.** Concurrent terminal commitments per store against `max_terminal_slots` (`ERR_STORE_TERMINAL_SLOTS_EXCEEDED`), committed visit minutes per inspector per UTC calendar day against `max_daily_minutes` (`ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), and depot unit availability (`ERR_DEPOT_STOCK_EXHAUSTED`) — all 409. These guard `POST /terminal-deployments` and `POST /store-visits`.
16. **Staple eligibility.** Desk staple qualification at submit, the strict sales-share test at submit, and the depth-of-stock floor at decision — yielding `ERR_STAPLE_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_CRITERION_B_NOT_MET` or `ERR_DEPTH_OF_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (all 422). These guard `POST /applications/{applicationId}/submit` and `POST /applications/{applicationId}/decision`.
17. **Evidence sufficiency.** Concluding for trafficking requires a decayed score of at least 60 plus a live direct-evidence anchor; concluding for ineligible-item sales requires at least one live evidence item; and a sanction case may be opened only from a substantiated investigation whose trigger period actually reached the top flag band — yielding `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` or `ERR_TRIGGER_PERIOD_NOT_FLAGGED` (both 422). These guard `POST /investigations`, `POST /investigations/{investigationId}/conclude` and `POST /sanction-cases`.
18. **Penalty gates.** Hardship designation plus a timely election plus no owner-level trafficking history (`ERR_CMP_NOT_AVAILABLE`, 409); a payment that never exceeds the outstanding balance (`ERR_CMP_OVERPAYMENT`, 422); and a penalty that cannot be computed without closed redemption history (`ERR_NO_REDEMPTION_HISTORY`, 422). These guard `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/determination`, `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-payments` and `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-elections`.
19. **Tender feasibility.** A basket must contain at least one SNAP-eligible line (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ITEMS`, 422) and the offered other tender must cover the ineligible subtotal plus its tax plus any eligible shortfall (`ERR_SPLIT_TENDER_SHORTFALL`, 422). These guard `POST /pos-transactions`.
20. **Monitoring immutability and transfer integrity.** An authorized transaction cannot be voided once its covering period is closed and a settlement cannot be imported into a closed period (`ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`, 409); an ownership-change reauthorization cannot be approved while the predecessor owner carries penalty debt (`ERR_TRANSFER_CMP_OUTSTANDING`, 409); and the successor owner must actually differ from the predecessor (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_UNCHANGED`, 422). These guard `POST /pos-transactions/{transactionId}/void`, `POST /redemption-periods/{periodId}/settlement-import` and `POST /applications/{applicationId}/decision`.
21. **Remand limit.** A case may be remanded at most once, and a determination may not be re-issued while a review is pending — otherwise `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). This guards `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/administrative-review-decision`.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: because role permission (rule 2) precedes path existence (rule 3), a forbidden role calling an endpoint with a nonexistent identifier gets 403, not 404. Because schema validation (rule 7) precedes the concurrency check (rule 8), a request with both a malformed body and a stale version gets 400, not 412. And because the state check (rule 9) precedes the trading-authorization check (rule 10), an attempt to activate an already-returned deployment at an unauthorized store reports the state problem.

### 2.9. Path Resolution, Reference Integrity and Sub-Resource Reads

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Reading the elections of a nonexistent case reports `ERR_SANCTION_CASE_NOT_FOUND` and never a generic empty list. Where a nested read succeeds at the parent but the parent has no children, the response is a normal empty array with a `total_count` of zero.

Body references follow the same discipline in declared field order. When a store registration names both an `owner_id` and a `predecessor_store_id`, the owner is resolved first; a bad owner reports `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` even if the predecessor is also bad. When a store visit names an `application_id` and an `inspector_id`, the application is resolved before the inspector. When a point-of-sale transaction names a `store_id` and a `terminal_deployment_id`, the store is resolved before the deployment, and a deployment reference that does not resolve reports `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Two collections are addressed under a parent case: its elections and its penalty ledger. Both are read-only listings scoped to that one case, and both accept the standard `limit` and `offset` pagination.

### 2.10. Interval Conventions and Aggregate Capacity Ceilings

Three families of intervals live in this system, and — deliberately — they do not share endpoint semantics. Implementers must not generalise from one to another.

**Inspector visit windows are half-open**, covering `window_start` inclusive through `window_end` exclusive, and are further padded by a **sixty-minute travel buffer added to both ends of every existing window**. A newly requested visit conflicts with an existing `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` visit of the same inspector when the new window's start is earlier than the existing window's end plus one hour **and** the existing window's start minus one hour is earlier than the new window's end. Conflict is reported as `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Concretely: against an existing window from 09:00 to 11:00, a new window from 12:00 to 13:00 is accepted, because 12:00 is not earlier than 12:00; a new window from 11:59 to 12:59 is refused.

**Terminal deployment windows are closed on both ends**, covering `deploy_start` through `deploy_end` inclusive, so touching endpoints do collide. A newly requested deployment conflicts with an existing `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE` or `RECALL_PENDING` deployment of the same terminal when the new start is at or before the existing end **and** the existing start is at or before the new end. Conflict is reported as `ERR_TERMINAL_ALREADY_COMMITTED` (409). Concretely: against an existing commitment ending 2026-09-30, a new commitment starting 2026-09-30 is refused and one starting 2026-10-01 is accepted.

**Monitoring periods are half-open per store**, covering `period_start` inclusive through `period_end` exclusive, and may not overlap for the same store. A new period conflicts when its start is earlier than an existing period's end **and** the existing start is earlier than the new end, reported as `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Abutting periods — one beginning exactly where the previous ends — are accepted, which is precisely how consecutive monitoring periods are built.

Two capacity ceilings are **aggregate rather than pairwise**. A store's terminal slots are measured as the maximum number of that store's `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE` or `RECALL_PENDING` deployments whose closed windows overlap any single instant within the new deployment's window; that maximum plus one must be at or below the store's `max_terminal_slots`, with equality allowed, otherwise `ERR_STORE_TERMINAL_SLOTS_EXCEEDED` (409). An inspector's daily ceiling is measured over the inspector's `SCHEDULED` and `IN_PROGRESS` visits whose `window_start` falls on the **same UTC calendar date** as the new visit's `window_start`: the sum of those visits' minutes plus the new visit's minutes must be at or below the inspector's `max_daily_minutes`, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Note that the travel buffer widens the *conflict* test but is never added to the *minute* total.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties Across the Enforcement Pipeline

Three independence rules are enforced by the server, all reporting `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

The first protects the fairness of administrative review: the officer deciding a review must be neither the user recorded as having issued the charge letter nor the user recorded as having issued the determination on that same case. The second protects re-verification: the inspector submitting findings on a `REVERIFICATION` visit must not be the inspector who submitted findings on that store's `PRE_AUTHORIZATION` visit — and this comparison consults the *historical* visit record, so a visit whose status has already moved to `FINDINGS_SUBMITTED` still counts for the purposes of this rule even though it no longer contributes to any capacity total. The third protects investigative independence: the analyst concluding an investigation must not be the user who opened it. An investigation opened automatically by the monitoring engine records the literal string `system` as its opener, which no human `X-User-ID` can collide with, so an automatically opened case may be concluded by any analyst.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants: Hardware, Money and Inspector Time

Three quantities in this system are physically conserved, and the invariant is a contract rather than a nicety.

**Terminal hardware.** For every depot at every instant, `units_in_stock` plus `units_reserved` plus `units_deployed` plus `units_pending_return` plus `units_retired` equals `total_units_owned`. Each of the four named counters is the count of that depot's terminals in the matching status, and `units_in_stock` is the residual. Reserving, activating, recalling, returning and releasing each move exactly one unit between two counters and never change the sum. A reserve-then-activate-then-return round trip restores `units_in_stock` to its original value with `units_reserved`, `units_deployed` and `units_pending_return` all back to zero. Provisioning cannot manufacture or lose hardware: a commitment released before it is ever activated returns its unit to stock, not to pending-return.

**Penalty money.** A case's `cmp_outstanding_cents` always equals the sum of `amount_cents` over that case's ledger entries. An owner's `cmp_outstanding_cents` always equals the sum of `cmp_outstanding_cents` over every sanction case attached to any store that owner controls. Each ledger row's `balance_after_cents` is the running sum of `amount_cents` over that case's entries ordered by `created_at` ascending, up to and including that row. Entry signs are fixed: a `CMP_ASSESSED` entry is positive, a `CMP_PAYMENT` entry is negative, and a `CMP_ABATEMENT` entry is negative. The balance may never go negative, so a payment whose absolute value exceeds the outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_CMP_OVERPAYMENT` (422). Reversal is a *compensating pair*, never a deletion: a determination for a penalty in lieu writes a positive assessment, and a reversal on review writes a negative abatement of the same magnitude, restoring the balance to exactly zero with both rows preserved.

**Inspector time.** An inspector's `committed_minutes` is the sum of `window_minutes` over that inspector's `SCHEDULED` and `IN_PROGRESS` visits. Scheduling a visit raises it by exactly that visit's `window_minutes`; cancelling the visit, submitting its findings, or recording no-access lowers it by exactly the same amount. A schedule-then-cancel round trip therefore restores the prior value exactly. Visits in `FINDINGS_SUBMITTED`, `NO_ACCESS` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing to the total.

### 2.13. The Two Independent Store Status Dimensions

A store row carries **two state dimensions simultaneously**, and they move independently. `authorization_status` governs the right to redeem benefits and takes the values `NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `SUSPENDED`, `DISQUALIFIED` and `WITHDRAWN`; it begins at `NOT_AUTHORIZED` and `DISQUALIFIED` is terminal. `sanction_status` governs where the store sits in the enforcement pipeline and takes the values `NONE`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `CHARGED`, `UNDER_ADMIN_REVIEW`, `SANCTION_FINAL` and `SANCTION_REVERSED`; it begins at `NONE` and has no terminal value, since a reversal returns a store to a clean slate.

The distinction matters commercially. Investigation and charging move only the sanction dimension: a store under investigation, or one that has received a charge letter, or one whose determination is under administrative review, is still `AUTHORIZED` and still redeems benefits normally. Only the authorization dimension gates trade. A store therefore stops redeeming at exactly two moments — when finalization of a disqualification sanction moves `authorization_status` from `AUTHORIZED` to `SUSPENDED`, and when the store voluntarily withdraws. A finalized penalty-in-lieu determination leaves `authorization_status` untouched, which is the whole point of paying a penalty instead of being disqualified.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding: Firm Owners, Retail Locations and the Owner-Level Rollup

Everything begins with a firm. Registering an owner is the single unauthenticated entry point into the system, and the payload is deliberately thin: the `legal_name` of the applicant firm or ownership entity, an `ownership_type` drawn from `SOLE_PROPRIETOR`, `PARTNERSHIP`, `CORPORATION` and `LLC`, and `tax_id_last4` — the last four characters of the employer identification or social security number on file, which must be exactly four characters long. A freshly registered owner starts with `permanent_disqualification_count` at 0, `cmp_outstanding_cents` at 0, `sanction_flag` at `CLEAR` and `status` at `ACTIVE`.

Those last four fields are never written directly by a caller; they are a **rollup over the owner's entire portfolio**, re-derived whenever anything in that portfolio moves. `permanent_disqualification_count` is the number of sanction cases, across every store the owner controls, whose case status is either `SANCTION_FINAL` or `ENFORCED` and whose `sanction_type` is `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION`. `cmp_outstanding_cents` is the sum of the outstanding penalty balances of every sanction case attached to any of those stores. `sanction_flag` is then decided by an **ordered** test, not an independent one: it reads `PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED` when the permanent-disqualification count is at least 1, otherwise `HAS_ACTIVE_CMP_DEBT` when the outstanding penalty total is greater than zero, otherwise `CLEAR`. The ordering matters — a permanently disqualified owner reports `PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED` even while it still owes money. Finally, `status` reads `BARRED` when the permanent-disqualification count reaches 2 or more, and `ACTIVE` otherwise; a barred owner is locked out of the program entirely and cannot even submit a new application.

A retail location is registered under an owner and carries the profile from which every later eligibility judgement is made. The caller supplies the controlling `owner_id`, a trade `store_name`, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `store_type` drawn from `SUPERMARKET`, `GROCERY`, `CONVENIENCE`, `SPECIALTY`, `FARMERS_MARKET` and `MEAL_SERVICE`, a `checkout_lane_count` of staffed lanes that may be zero or more, a self-reported `staple_shelf_linear_feet` that may be zero or more, an `annual_gross_sales_cents` figure that must be strictly greater than zero, an `annual_staple_sales_cents` figure that must be at least zero and no greater than the gross figure, and a `max_terminal_slots` ceiling of at least 1 governing how many EBT terminal commitments may overlap at that location. Three optional inputs round out the profile: `tax_rate_bps`, the state and local sales tax in basis points, which defaults to 0 and may not exceed 2000; `hardship_designated`, which defaults to false and is set true when FNS has designated this store the sole authorized retailer selling a substantial variety of staple foods in its area; and `predecessor_store_id`, supplied only when this record succeeds an existing store after a change of ownership and left null otherwise.

At registration the server derives two figures and initialises the rest. `staple_sales_bps` is the store's staple share of sales in basis points: take the annual staple sales figure, multiply by 10,000, divide by the annual gross sales figure, and take the floor of the result. `criterion_b_pass` is true exactly when `staple_sales_bps` is **strictly greater** than 5,000 — that is, strictly more than 50.00 percent of sales are staple foods. A store sitting at exactly 5,000 basis points **fails**; the boundary is not inclusive, and this single fact is the most common source of rejected applications. A new store lands with `authorization_status` at `NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `sanction_status` at `NONE`, `redemption_flag_level` at `FLAG_L0`, `active_terminal_count` at 0, and with `fns_number`, `authorized_at`, `disqualified_until` all null and `permanently_disqualified` false.

The eligibility profile can be edited afterwards, and the edit is the second place optimistic concurrency bites: the caller must present `expected_store_version` alongside any subset of `store_name`, `checkout_lane_count`, `staple_shelf_linear_feet`, `annual_gross_sales_cents`, `annual_staple_sales_cents`, `max_terminal_slots`, `tax_rate_bps` and `hardship_designated`. A successful edit recomputes `staple_sales_bps` and `criterion_b_pass` from the new sales figures and advances the store's `version` by one, which is exactly what invalidates any application snapshot a client was holding.

A firm may also leave the program voluntarily. Voluntary withdrawal moves `authorization_status` from `AUTHORIZED` to `WITHDRAWN`, and it is permitted only while the store is not caught mid-proceeding: if `sanction_status` is `CHARGED`, `UNDER_ADMIN_REVIEW` or `SANCTION_FINAL`, the withdrawal is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When it succeeds, every one of the store's terminal commitments in `ACTIVE` or `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED`, each affected unit returns to `IN_STOCK`, the owning depot's counters are recomputed, and the store's `version` advances by one. Withdrawal is not terminal for the store: a withdrawn store may be the subject of a fresh application later, because the submit gate admits stores whose `authorization_status` is `NOT_AUTHORIZED` or `WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.2. Filing an Application and the Two Eligibility Routes

An application is filed against one store and declares which statutory route the applicant is claiming. The payload names the target `store_id`, the `expected_store_version` the caller last observed, an `application_type` drawn from `INITIAL`, `REAUTHORIZATION_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE` and `RENEWAL`, a `claimed_criterion` of either `CRITERION_A` or `CRITERION_B`, and `reported_inventory_lines` — the applicant's own account of what staple food it stocks. Each inventory line names a `category` from `BREAD_CEREAL`, `DAIRY`, `FRUIT_VEG` and `MEAT_POULTRY_FISH`, a `variety_key` naming the distinct product variety, a `unit_count` of stocking units on hand, and an `is_perishable` boolean. An optional `reported_staple_sales_pct` records the applicant's own stated staple share of sales as a percentage; it is captured for the record and is **informational only** — the sales-share route is decided from the store's own derived `staple_sales_bps`, never from this number.

The filing lands in `DRAFT` with `deficiency_cycle_count` at 0, `hold_reason_code` and `decision_reason_code` null, and `submitted_at` and `decided_at` null. It also immediately runs the desk staple assessment described below to populate `desk_qualifying_categories`, `desk_perishable_categories` and `desk_criterion_pass`, and it advances the target store's `version` by one — filing an application is itself a mutation of the store's snapshot. `verified_criterion_pass` stays null until a field visit produces findings. A store may hold only one live application: if the store already has an application in any status other than `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`, the filing is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_ALREADY_OPEN`.

**The desk staple assessment.** Statutory Criterion A asks whether a store stocks enough breadth and depth of staple food. The desk evaluation — the variant used whenever *self-reported* stock is judged, meaning at filing, at document resubmission and at submit — groups the reported lines by `category` and asks two questions of each of the four staple categories. First, how many *distinct* `variety_key` values does the category contain; second, what is the sum of `unit_count` across the whole category. The category qualifies when it holds at least 3 distinct varieties **and** its aggregate unit count is at least 9. Crucially, in the desk variant a variety contributes to the distinct count even when its own `unit_count` is only 1 or 2 — breadth is counted first and depth only in aggregate. The number of categories that qualify becomes `desk_qualifying_categories`. Among those qualifying categories, the number that carry at least one perishable variety among the varieties that made them qualify becomes `desk_perishable_categories`. `desk_criterion_pass` is true when `desk_qualifying_categories` equals 4 — all four staple categories, with no partial credit — **and** `desk_perishable_categories` is at least 2.

**Submitting.** Submission moves the application from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and is where the eligibility gate first bites. Four preconditions are checked. The application must actually be in `DRAFT`. The store's owner must not be `BARRED`, or the submit is refused with `ERR_OWNER_BARRED`. The store's `authorization_status` must be `NOT_AUTHORIZED` or `WITHDRAWN`, or the submit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — an already-authorized store cannot re-apply while authorized. And then the claimed route is tested: an application claiming `CRITERION_B` requires the store's `criterion_b_pass` to be true, failing which the submit is refused with `ERR_CRITERION_B_NOT_MET`, while an application claiming `CRITERION_A` requires `desk_criterion_pass` to be true, failing which the submit is refused with `ERR_STAPLE_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. Only one of the two route tests is applied — the one the applicant claimed. On success the server sets `submitted_at` to the current logical-clock instant, recomputes the three desk staple figures from the stock currently on file, and clears `hold_reason_code`.

**Correcting a deficiency.** An application that a field visit has pushed back into `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` returns to `SUBMITTED` when the applicant supplies corrected stock. The payload replaces `reported_inventory_lines` wholesale — this is a replacement, not a merge — and may restate `reported_staple_sales_pct`. The resubmission is permitted while the application is in `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` and its `deficiency_cycle_count` is at most 2. On success the desk staple figures are recomputed from the replacement lines, `submitted_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant, and `hold_reason_code` is cleared. Because the application returns to `SUBMITTED` rather than to `PENDING_VISIT`, a fresh field visit must be scheduled to move it forward again — which is what makes the application machine cyclic: `PENDING_VISIT` to `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` to `SUBMITTED` to `PENDING_VISIT`.

**Abandoning.** An applicant may withdraw at any point before the file closes. Withdrawal is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `PENDING_VISIT`, `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` and `UNDER_REVIEW` — in other words from any status not already in `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` — and drives the application to `WITHDRAWN` with `decision_reason_code` set to `WITHDRAWN_BY_APPLICANT`. It also cancels every one of the application's still-`SCHEDULED` field visits, which releases each cancelled visit's minutes back from the assigned inspector's `committed_minutes`.

### 3.3. Field Verification: Inspectors, Windows, Findings and Deficiency Cycles

FNS field inspectors are registered with a `full_name`, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the territory they cover, and a `max_daily_minutes` ceiling of at least 60 minutes that caps how much visit time may be committed to them on any one UTC calendar day. A new inspector starts with `committed_minutes` at 0 and a `status` of `ACTIVE`, though `INACTIVE` may be supplied at registration. Both `status` and `max_daily_minutes` may be adjusted afterwards, and any such adjustment advances the inspector's `version` by one. An `INACTIVE` inspector keeps whatever visits are already booked but may not start one.

Scheduling a field visit is the act that moves a `SUBMITTED` application to `PENDING_VISIT`. The scheduling payload names the `application_id` being verified, the `inspector_id` assigned, a `visit_type` of `PRE_AUTHORIZATION`, `COMPLIANCE` or `REVERIFICATION`, and the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the visit window, where the end must be strictly after the start. From those two instants the server derives `window_minutes` as the whole number of minutes in the window: take the difference between end and start in seconds, divide by 60, and take the floor. That figure is immediately added to the assigned inspector's `committed_minutes`, and the new visit lands in `SCHEDULED`. Both the half-open-plus-travel-buffer conflict test and the daily-minutes ceiling described in §2.10 are applied here, so a booking may be refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` or with `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` before any application state moves.

An inspector arriving on site marks the visit as begun, advancing it from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. Two gates apply beyond the state check: the assigned inspector's own `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the start is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and the logical clock must have reached `window_start`, or the start is refused with `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The window's opening edge is inclusive — a start at exactly `window_start` is permitted.

**Recording findings.** Submitting findings advances the visit from `IN_PROGRESS` to `FINDINGS_SUBMITTED` and carries two inputs: `observed_inventory_lines`, using exactly the same line shape as the applicant's self-report, and `observed_staple_shelf_linear_feet`, the linear feet of staple shelving the inspector actually measured. Before anything is computed, the re-verification independence rule applies: on a `REVERIFICATION` visit the acting inspector must differ from the inspector who submitted findings on that store's `PRE_AUTHORIZATION` visit, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The on-site staple assessment reads the same four categories but applies a **stricter** variant than the desk. For each category the server counts only the distinct `variety_key` values whose own `unit_count` is at least 3 — the deep varieties — and the category qualifies when it holds at least 3 such deep varieties. The aggregate-units shortcut that rescues a broad-but-shallow desk filing does **not** apply on site: three varieties of two units each qualify the category at the desk and fail it at the store. The count of qualifying categories becomes `verified_qualifying_categories`; the count of those qualifying categories that carry at least one perishable variety among the deep varieties that made them qualify becomes `verified_perishable_categories`; and `verified_criterion_a_pass` is true when `verified_qualifying_categories` equals 4 and `verified_perishable_categories` is at least 2. The divergence between the two variants is intentional: every reconciliation between the desk figure and the verified figure exercises it.

Two shelf measurements are derived at the same moment. `depth_of_stock_pass` compares `observed_staple_shelf_linear_feet` against a floor that depends on the store's format, and the comparison is **inclusive**: a supermarket must reach 120 linear feet, a grocery 60, a convenience store 24, a specialty store 16, and a farmers' market or meal service has no floor at all. Twenty-four feet therefore passes for a convenience store and twenty-three fails. Separately, `shelf_discrepancy_bps` measures how far the inspector's measurement diverges from what the store self-reported: take the absolute difference between `observed_staple_shelf_linear_feet` and the store's own `staple_shelf_linear_feet`, multiply by 10,000, divide by the store's self-reported figure — or by 1 when that figure is zero, so the division is always defined — and take the floor. `shelf_discrepancy_flag` is true when `shelf_discrepancy_bps` is **strictly greater** than 2,500 basis points, meaning a divergence of more than 25 percent. Exactly 2,500 does not flag; 2,501 does. Note the asymmetry with the shelf floor: one boundary is inclusive and the other is strict.

Submitting findings also sets `findings_submitted_at` to the current logical-clock instant and releases the visit's `window_minutes` from the inspector's `committed_minutes`.

**Where the application goes next.** Findings drive the application, and the branch is decided by exactly two of the derived figures. When `verified_criterion_a_pass` is true **and** `shelf_discrepancy_flag` is false, the application advances from `PENDING_VISIT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, its `verified_criterion_pass` is set to true, and its `hold_reason_code` is cleared. `depth_of_stock_pass` is deliberately **not** consulted at this point — a store that fails the shelf floor still reaches `UNDER_REVIEW`, and its depth failure is refused later, at the decision. That is the intended serialization point: the depth question belongs to the decision-maker, not to the inspector.

When either the verified staple criterion failed or the discrepancy flag was raised, the branch depends on how many deficiency cycles the application has already burned. While `deficiency_cycle_count` is less than 2, the application moves from `PENDING_VISIT` to `PENDING_DOCUMENTS`: `deficiency_cycle_count` increases by 1, `verified_criterion_pass` is set to the failing `verified_criterion_a_pass` value, and `hold_reason_code` is set to name the check that failed — `STAPLE_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` when the on-site staple criterion failed, `SHELF_DISCREPANCY` when the criterion held but the discrepancy flag was raised, `DEPTH_OF_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` when the shelf floor was the failing measure, and `NO_ACCESS` when the inspector could not get in at all. When `deficiency_cycle_count` is already 2, the applicant has exhausted the three permitted attempts and the application moves from `PENDING_VISIT` straight to `DENIED`: `deficiency_cycle_count` becomes 3, `decision_reason_code` is set to `DEFICIENCY_CYCLES_EXHAUSTED`, `decided_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, and every one of the store's `RESERVED` terminal commitments is driven to `RELEASED`.

Note carefully that a failing on-site assessment is **not** an error response. Findings that fail the on-site variant return 200 and route the application through the deficiency-cycle rule; `ERR_STAPLE_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` is raised only when a `CRITERION_A` application is submitted with a failing desk figure.

**No access, and cancellation.** When an inspector cannot gain entry, the visit advances from `IN_PROGRESS` to `NO_ACCESS`, optionally recording a free-text `note`. This releases the visit's minutes from the inspector's `committed_minutes` and consumes a deficiency cycle exactly as a failed finding does — pushing the application to `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` with `hold_reason_code` set to `NO_ACCESS` while cycles remain, or to `DENIED` with `decision_reason_code` set to `DEFICIENCY_CYCLES_EXHAUSTED` when they do not. A visit that has not yet started may instead be cancelled outright, moving from `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED` with an optional `note`; cancellation releases the minutes and consumes no deficiency cycle. `FINDINGS_SUBMITTED`, `NO_ACCESS` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal for a visit.

### 3.4. The Authorization Decision, FNS Numbering and Ownership-Change Transfers

An application sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW` awaits a decision from FNS retailer operations. The decision payload carries an `outcome` and, for a denial, an optional `reason_code` naming why. Approving is the most heavily guarded operation in the authorization half of the system, and the checks run in the order given here.

The application must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, and its `verified_criterion_pass` must be true. The latest `FINDINGS_SUBMITTED` visit of that application must have `depth_of_stock_pass` true — this is where the shelf floor deferred at findings time is finally enforced, and a failure is refused with `ERR_DEPTH_OF_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. The store's owner must have `permanent_disqualification_count` of exactly 0, or the approval is refused with `ERR_OWNER_PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED` — note that a single permanent disqualification blocks *new authorizations* while two are required to bar the owner from *filing*, so an owner with exactly one may file and be screened and still be refused at the decision. Then, for an application whose `application_type` is `REAUTHORIZATION_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE`, two transfer-integrity checks apply: the predecessor store's owner must carry `cmp_outstanding_cents` of exactly 0, or the approval is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_CMP_OUTSTANDING`, because an outstanding civil money penalty cannot be shed by selling the firm; and the successor store's `owner_id` must actually differ from the predecessor store's `owner_id`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_UNCHANGED`, because a paper transfer to oneself is not a change of ownership.

An approval writes `decided_at` from the logical clock and sets `decision_reason_code` from the route the applicant claimed — `APPROVED_CRITERION_A` for a `CRITERION_A` filing and `APPROVED_CRITERION_B` for a `CRITERION_B` filing. It then reaches across to the store: when the store has no `fns_number` yet, one is minted; the store's `authorization_status` moves from `NOT_AUTHORIZED` (or from `WITHDRAWN`, for a store re-entering the program) to `AUTHORIZED`; `authorized_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant; and the store's `version` advances by one. An `fns_number` is the literal prefix `FNS-` followed by a seven-digit sequence number beginning at 1000001 and allocated strictly in the order applications are approved, so the first store ever authorized by the system carries `FNS-1000001` and the second `FNS-1000002`. A store that already carries a number keeps it across a re-authorization — the number identifies the location, not the episode.

A denial writes `decided_at` and sets `decision_reason_code` from the supplied `reason_code`, and it releases the store's speculative hardware: every `RESERVED` terminal commitment of that store moves to `RELEASED`. The full vocabulary of `decision_reason_code` is `APPROVED_CRITERION_A`, `APPROVED_CRITERION_B`, `STAPLE_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`, `DEFICIENCY_CYCLES_EXHAUSTED`, `OWNER_PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED`, `TRANSFER_CMP_OUTSTANDING` and `WITHDRAWN_BY_APPLICANT`; `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the three terminal statuses of an application.

Consider how an ownership change actually plays out. A convenience store operating as `FNS-1000042` is sold. The buyer registers a *new* store record naming its own firm as `owner_id` and the old store's identifier as `predecessor_store_id`, then files an application whose `application_type` is `REAUTHORIZATION_OWNERSHIP_CHANGE`. The successor record is screened on its own merits — its own staple stock, its own field visit — but the decision additionally reaches back through `predecessor_store_id` to the *seller's* firm and refuses the approval while that firm still owes a civil money penalty. This is the cross-aggregate read that makes the ownership chain enforceable, and it is why `predecessor_store_id` is a first-class filter on store listings.

### 3.5. EBT Terminal Provisioning and Equipment Ordering

Hardware lives in state depots. A depot is registered with the `state_code` it serves and a `total_units_owned` count of EBT terminals it owns, which may be zero or more and is the conserved quantity of §2.12. On registration `units_reserved`, `units_deployed`, `units_pending_return` and `units_retired` are all 0 and `units_in_stock` equals `total_units_owned`.

Individual units are added to a depot with a `depot_id`, a manufacturer `serial_number`, and a `model` of `EBT_ONLY_WIRELESS`, `EBT_ONLY_DIAL` or `COMMERCIAL_INTEGRATED`. A serial number must be unique across all terminals that are not `RETIRED`, so a retired unit's serial may be reused but a live one's may not; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERMINAL_SERIAL`. A new unit lands `IN_STOCK` and the owning depot's counters are recomputed. The five terminal statuses are `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED`, `PENDING_RETURN` and `RETIRED`, and `RESERVED` carries a specific meaning: it means at least one open, not-yet-active commitment exists for the unit, so a unit booked for several non-overlapping windows still counts exactly once in the depot ledger.

A **terminal deployment** is the junction that commits one unit to one store for one date interval. The payload names the `store_id`, the `terminal_id`, and the `deploy_start` and `deploy_end` bounding the commitment, where the end must be at or after the start. The interval is closed on both ends, so the collision rules of §2.10 apply and a same-unit overlap is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_ALREADY_COMMITTED` while breaching the store's `max_terminal_slots` is refused with `ERR_STORE_TERMINAL_SLOTS_EXCEEDED`. A unit whose status is `PENDING_RETURN` or `RETIRED` is not available to commit at all, and naming one is refused with `ERR_DEPOT_STOCK_EXHAUSTED`. A new commitment lands in `RESERVED`. If this is the unit's first open commitment, the unit moves from `IN_STOCK` to `RESERVED` and the depot counters are recomputed; a unit already `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` keeps its status, which is precisely how back-to-back non-overlapping bookings of the same unit avoid double-counting it.

Activating a reserved commitment is what lets a store actually transact. Activation moves the deployment from `RESERVED` to `ACTIVE`, and it requires the store's `authorization_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_STORE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Activation sets `activated_at` to the current logical-clock instant, moves the unit from `RESERVED` to `DEPLOYED` with the depot counters recomputed, and increments the store's `active_terminal_count`.

Three exits close a deployment. **Recall** moves an `ACTIVE` deployment to `RECALL_PENDING` without waiting for enforcement — after damage, or to freeze hardware during an investigation — moving the unit from `DEPLOYED` to `PENDING_RETURN`, recomputing the depot counters, and decrementing the store's `active_terminal_count`; the caller may supply a `close_reason_code`, and the enforcement cascade described in §3.10 supplies `AUTHORIZATION_LOST` on the store's behalf. **Return** records physical arrival of an `ACTIVE` or `RECALL_PENDING` unit back at the depot, moving the deployment to `RETURNED`, setting `closed_at` to the current logical-clock instant, moving the unit to `IN_STOCK` when no other open commitment remains, recomputing the depot counters, and decrementing `active_terminal_count` only when the deployment was still `ACTIVE` — a unit already recalled has had its count taken off once and must not be double-decremented. **Release** disposes of a `RESERVED` commitment that will never be activated, moving the deployment to `RELEASED`, setting `closed_at`, and moving the unit from `RESERVED` back to `IN_STOCK` when no other open commitment remains. `RETURNED` and `RELEASED` are the two terminal statuses of a deployment. A release also arrives by cascade, from an application denial, a store withdrawal or the opening of an investigation; all three sweep the store's `RESERVED` commitments. The optional `close_reason_code` on any of the three exits is drawn from `AUTHORIZATION_LOST`, `INVESTIGATION_FREEZE`, `RETAILER_REQUEST`, `END_OF_TERM` and `DAMAGED`.

**Ordering equipment.** A store may order hardware and consumables. The order payload names the `store_id` and a `lines` array in which each element carries an `item_code` and a `qty`. Six item codes exist with fixed catalogue prices: an `EBT_ONLY_WIRELESS_TERMINAL` at $425.00, an `EBT_ONLY_DIAL_TERMINAL` at $310.00, a `COMMERCIAL_POS_INTEGRATION` at $1,850.00, a `MANUAL_VOUCHER_KIT` at $45.00, a `SIGNAGE_PACK` at $22.00, and a `RECEIPT_PAPER_CASE` at $40.00. `subtotal_cents` is the sum, over every line, of the line's catalogue unit price multiplied by its `qty`.

USDA funds EBT-only hardware for small retailers, and the subsidy test has two conjuncts: the store's `authorization_status` must be `AUTHORIZED` **and** its `annual_gross_sales_cents` must be at or below $5,000,000.00. When both hold, `subsidy_cents` is the sum of unit price multiplied by `qty` over only those lines whose `item_code` is one of the four subsidized codes — `EBT_ONLY_WIRELESS_TERMINAL`, `EBT_ONLY_DIAL_TERMINAL`, `MANUAL_VOUCHER_KIT` and `SIGNAGE_PACK`. Commercial point-of-sale integration and receipt paper are never subsidized. When either conjunct fails, `subsidy_cents` is 0. `net_equipment_cents` is then `subtotal_cents` less `subsidy_cents`.

The handling fee is where implementations most often go wrong, because the fee appears to depend on a total that itself includes the fee. The circularity is broken by pinning the threshold to the **pre-fee net**: `handling_fee_cents` is 0 when `net_equipment_cents` is 0, 0 when `net_equipment_cents` is at least $250.00, and $15.00 otherwise. `total_due_cents` is `net_equipment_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents`. The threshold is read off `net_equipment_cents` and **never** off `total_due_cents`: an order whose net is $240.00 pays the $15.00 fee for a total of $255.00, whereas reading the threshold off the total would wrongly waive the fee and return $240.00.

An order is created in `DRAFT` with its five money figures computed. Placing it moves it from `DRAFT` to `PLACED`, requires the store's `authorization_status` to be `AUTHORIZED` — otherwise `ERR_STORE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` — sets `placed_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and **freezes** `subtotal_cents`, `subsidy_cents`, `net_equipment_cents`, `handling_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` as they stood. A placed order may then be marked shipped, moving to `FULFILLED`. An order in either `DRAFT` or `PLACED` may be cancelled, which moves it to `CANCELLED` and records the supplied `cancel_reason_code`, drawn from `RETAILER_REQUEST`, `AUTHORIZATION_LOST` and `DUPLICATE_ORDER`. `FULFILLED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.6. Point-of-Sale Eligibility Screening and Split Tender

A cashier rings up a basket at an EBT terminal, and the system's job is to decide which lines benefits may lawfully pay for, how the rest is settled, and whether the sale can complete at all. The transaction payload names the `store_id`, the `terminal_deployment_id` identifying the commitment the sale ran on, the `household_ebt_card_last4` — exactly four digits — a `line_items` array, the `snap_benefit_available_cents` balance snapshot the processor presented, and the `other_tender_offered_cents` the household is putting up for the ineligible remainder. Both money inputs must be at least zero. Each line item carries a `sku`, an `item_class`, a `unit_price_cents` and a `qty`.

Two gates precede any arithmetic. The store's `authorization_status` must be `AUTHORIZED`, or the sale is refused with `ERR_STORE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; a store under investigation, under charge, or under administrative review is still authorized and still trades. And the named deployment's status must be `ACTIVE`, or the sale is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — a merely `RESERVED` or already `RECALL_PENDING` terminal cannot take a swipe.

Nine item classes exist, split into two buckets. The SNAP-eligible classes are `STAPLE_FOOD`, `NONSTAPLE_FOOD` and `SEED_PLANT` — seeds and food-producing plants are eligible by statute. The SNAP-ineligible classes are `HOT_PREPARED`, `ALCOHOL`, `TOBACCO`, `NONFOOD`, `ENERGY_DRINK_SUPPLEMENT` and `LIVE_ANIMAL`. `eligible_subtotal_cents` is the sum, over every line whose `item_class` is one of the three eligible classes, of `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `qty`. `ineligible_subtotal_cents` is the same sum over all remaining lines. `flagged_line_skus` is the list of `sku` values of every ineligible line, in the order the lines appeared in the request.

If `eligible_subtotal_cents` comes out at 0 — every line in the basket is ineligible — the sale is declined immediately: the transaction is created with `status` `DECLINED` and `decline_reason_code` `NO_ELIGIBLE_ITEMS`, all tender figures are recorded as 0, and the call returns `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ITEMS`.

Otherwise tax is computed on the ineligible bucket **only** — benefits never pay tax, and eligible food is not taxed here. `tax_cents` is the ineligible subtotal multiplied by the store's `tax_rate_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents. The order is pinned as described in §2.7: the ineligible extended amounts are summed first and the rate is applied once to that sum, never per line.

Benefits then fill the eligible bucket first and may never spill over. `ebt_tender_cents` is the **lesser** of `eligible_subtotal_cents` and `snap_benefit_available_cents`. Whatever eligible amount the benefits could not cover is the eligible shortfall — the eligible subtotal less the EBT tender. The household's own money must therefore cover three things: the ineligible subtotal, the tax on that subtotal, and the eligible shortfall. If `other_tender_offered_cents` is **less than** that required sum, the sale is declined: the transaction is created with `status` `DECLINED` and `decline_reason_code` `INSUFFICIENT_SPLIT_TENDER`, both `ebt_tender_cents` and `other_tender_cents` are recorded as 0, and the call returns `ERR_SPLIT_TENDER_SHORTFALL`.

When the offer is sufficient the transaction is created `AUTHORIZED`. `other_tender_cents` is set to the **required** amount, not to the amount offered — the register takes what it needs and no more, so a household offering $50.00 against a $12.34 requirement is charged $12.34. `split_tender_applied` is true exactly when `other_tender_cents` on an authorized sale is greater than zero. `AUTHORIZED` is the initial status of a successful sale, `DECLINED` is terminal from birth, and `void_reason_code` stays null until a void.

An authorized sale immediately updates the point-of-sale aggregates of whichever of the store's `OPEN` monitoring periods has a half-open window containing the transaction's `created_at`. That linkage is what makes the void rule sharp: an authorized sale may be voided, moving it to `VOIDED` with the supplied `void_reason_code` drawn from `CLERK_ERROR`, `CUSTOMER_ABANDONED` and `PROCESSOR_REVERSAL`, and the void withdraws the transaction from that period's point-of-sale aggregates — but only while the covering period is still `OPEN`. Once the covering period has been `CLOSED`, the void is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`, because a closed period's figures are evidence and evidence does not move.

### 3.7. Redemption Monitoring, Settlement Reconciliation and Anomaly Scoring

Monitoring works in **periods**. An analyst opens a period for a store by naming the `store_id`, a `period_index` of at least 1 that must be unique within that store, and the `period_start` and `period_end` bounding it, where the end must be strictly after the start. A duplicate index for the same store is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_INDEX`, and an overlap with another period of the same store is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` — though abutting periods are accepted, since that is how a continuous monitoring history is built. A new period lands `OPEN` with `reconciliation_status` `NOT_IMPORTED`, and it immediately computes its point-of-sale aggregates from whatever authorized transactions of that store already fall inside its window, so a period opened retroactively is not born empty.

The **point-of-sale-derived** figures are taken over a precisely defined set: transactions belonging to that store, whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` — declined and voided rows are excluded outright — and whose `created_at` falls at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. Over that set, `pos_derived_total_cents` is the sum of `ebt_tender_cents` and `pos_derived_transaction_count` is the row count. Two further point-of-sale figures are derived for scoring purposes: the large-transaction total is the sum of `ebt_tender_cents` over those included transactions whose own `ebt_tender_cents` is at least $100.00, and the even-dollar count is the number of included transactions whose `ebt_tender_cents` is a whole multiple of $10.00 and is also greater than zero — a zero-benefit sale is not an even-dollar sale.

The EBT processor eventually reports its own settlement for the period, and once it does, **the processor is authoritative**. A settlement import carries four figures: `imported_total_cents`, the settlement total; `imported_transaction_count`, the settlement transaction count; `imported_large_transaction_cents`, the settlement total restricted to transactions of at least $100.00; and `imported_even_dollar_transaction_count`, the settlement count of transactions that are a non-zero whole multiple of $10.00. Importing into a period that has already been `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Four reported figures on a period are then resolved by a single switch. When `imported_total_cents` is non-null, `redemption_total_cents` takes `imported_total_cents`, `transaction_count` takes `imported_transaction_count`, `large_transaction_cents` takes `imported_large_transaction_cents`, and `even_dollar_transaction_count` takes `imported_even_dollar_transaction_count`. When `imported_total_cents` is null, all four take their point-of-sale-derived equivalents instead. The switch is all-or-nothing and is driven only by whether the total has been imported — the four figures never come from a mixture of sources.

Reconciliation is reported alongside. `reconciliation_variance_cents` is `imported_total_cents` less `pos_derived_total_cents`, and stays null until a settlement has been imported. `reconciliation_status` reads `NOT_IMPORTED` while `imported_total_cents` is null, `VARIANCE` when the absolute value of the variance is **strictly greater** than $5.00, and `MATCHED` otherwise — so a variance of exactly $5.00 in either direction still counts as matched. A variance does not block anything; it is a signal for the analyst.

**Closing a period** freezes it and scores it. A close is permitted only once the logical clock has reached `period_end`; before that it is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The close freezes `redemption_total_cents`, `transaction_count`, `large_transaction_cents` and `even_dollar_transaction_count` at their then-current resolved values, sets `closed_at` to the current logical-clock instant, moves `status` from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`, and computes the three scoring fields that were null until now.

`size_expectation_cents` asks how much a store of this size could plausibly redeem in one period, and it is the **smaller** of two independent estimates, floored so it can never be zero. The footprint estimate allows $4,000.00 for each staffed checkout lane in `checkout_lane_count` plus $150.00 for each linear foot of `staple_shelf_linear_feet`. The sales estimate is one twelfth of the store's `annual_gross_sales_cents`, floored to whole cents. `size_expectation_cents` is the lesser of those two, but never below $1,000.00 — the floor exists so that a tiny store cannot produce a zero denominator.

`anomaly_score` is a weighted blend of three components, each a value between 0 and 1 inclusive. The **excess** component measures redemption volume against expectation: compute the ratio in basis points as `redemption_total_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `size_expectation_cents`, floored to a whole number; subtract 10,000 from that ratio and treat any negative result as zero; divide the remainder by 20,000; and cap the result at 1. In business terms the excess component is 0 while a store redeems no more than its expectation and reaches its full value of 1 once the store redeems three times its expectation or more. The **large-transaction** component measures dollar concentration: take `large_transaction_cents` divided by `redemption_total_cents` — or by 1 when the total is zero — divide that share by 0.50, and cap at 1, so the component maxes out once at least half the redeemed dollars come from transactions of at least $100.00. The **even-dollar** component measures suspiciously round baskets: take `even_dollar_transaction_count` divided by `transaction_count` — or by 1 when the count is zero — divide that share by 0.40, and cap at 1, so it maxes out once at least two in five transactions land on a whole multiple of $10.00.

The three components are then weighted by store format. A `CONVENIENCE` or `SPECIALTY` store — the small formats where trafficking concentrates — weights excess at 45, large transactions at 35 and even dollars at 20. A `FARMERS_MARKET` inverts the emphasis, weighting excess at 20, large transactions at 30 and even dollars at 50, because round-number benefit conversions are the characteristic pattern there. Every other format — `SUPERMARKET`, `GROCERY` and `MEAL_SERVICE` — weights excess at 50, large transactions at 30 and even dollars at 20. `anomaly_score` is the sum of each weight multiplied by its component, rounded half up **once** to a whole number, and then capped at 100. The three components stay exact until that single rounding; rounding each weighted term separately drifts by up to a point and can flip a flag band.

`flag_level` is read off the score in four inclusive bands: `FLAG_L3` at a score of 70 or more, `FLAG_L2` at 50 or more, `FLAG_L1` at 25 or more, and `FLAG_L0` below that. All four bands are inclusive at their lower bound — 69 is `FLAG_L2` and 70 is `FLAG_L3`. Closing a period also refreshes the store's own `redemption_flag_level`, which mirrors the `flag_level` of the store's `CLOSED` period bearing the highest `period_index`; since `period_index` is unique per store this is unambiguous, and should two closed rows of a store ever share an index the earlier row by `created_at` wins. A store with no closed period reports `FLAG_L0`.

Reaching `FLAG_L3` at close has teeth. The close itself opens an investigation against the store, with `opened_reason` set to `ANOMALY_FLAG_L3` and `trigger_period_id` set to the period just closed. That in turn moves the store's `sanction_status` from `NONE` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and releases every one of the store's `RESERVED` terminal commitments to `RELEASED`, each affected unit returning to `IN_STOCK` with the depot counters recomputed. Note what does **not** happen: the store's `authorization_status` is untouched, so the store keeps redeeming while the investigation runs.

Worked example, to fix the arithmetic: a `CONVENIENCE` store with two staffed lanes, 30 linear feet of staple shelving and $360,000.00 in annual gross sales has a footprint estimate of $12,500.00 and a sales estimate of $30,000.00, so its `size_expectation_cents` is $12,500.00. If that store redeems $43,750.00 in a period, its ratio is 3.5 times expectation.

### 3.8. Investigations, Evidence Decay and Substantiation

An investigation may be opened by an analyst directly or by the monitoring engine automatically. The payload names the `store_id`, an `opened_reason` of `ANOMALY_FLAG_L3`, `TIP` or `ALE_REFERRAL`, an optional `trigger_period_id`, and an optional initial `evidence_items` array. The trigger period is **required** when `opened_reason` is `ANOMALY_FLAG_L3` and must be null otherwise; and when supplied it must name a period belonging to that same store which is `CLOSED` at `flag_level` `FLAG_L3`. A trigger period that is not closed at the top band, or that belongs to a different store, is refused with `ERR_TRIGGER_PERIOD_NOT_FLAGGED`.

Opening lands the investigation in `OPEN` with `trafficking_confidence_score` at 0, `substantiated_violation` null, `concluded_at` and `concluded_by_user_id` null, and `opened_by_user_id` recording the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` — or the literal string `system` when the monitoring engine opened it automatically at `FLAG_L3`. Opening also has three side effects that reach outside the investigation. The store's `sanction_status` moves to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, which is permitted from `NONE` and also from `SANCTION_REVERSED`, so a store cleared once may be investigated again. Every `RESERVED` terminal commitment of the store is driven to `RELEASED`, with the units returned to `IN_STOCK` and the depot counters recomputed — speculative hardware is not extended to a store under investigation. And any application of that store sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW` is pushed back to `PENDING_DOCUMENTS` with `hold_reason_code` set to `INVESTIGATION_HOLD` and its `deficiency_cycle_count` incremented by 1. That last cascade is easy to miss and consequential: an investigation opened mid-review consumes one of the applicant's three attempts.

**Evidence and its decay.** Each evidence item carries an `evidence_type` drawn from `UNDERCOVER_BUY`, `INVENTORY_PURCHASE_MISMATCH`, `EBT_ONLY_TRANSACTION_PATTERN`, `INFORMANT_STATEMENT` and `HOUSEHOLD_INTERVIEW`, an `observed_at` instant recording when the conduct was observed, and an optional free-text `note`. Appending an item moves the investigation to `EVIDENCE_REVIEW` — a move permitted from either `OPEN` or `EVIDENCE_REVIEW`, so the second and subsequent items are accepted without complaint — and rescores the case against the current logical clock.

Scoring is a decay function. Each evidence type carries a base weight: an `UNDERCOVER_BUY` is worth 40 points, an `INVENTORY_PURCHASE_MISMATCH` 25, an `EBT_ONLY_TRANSACTION_PATTERN` 20, an `INFORMANT_STATEMENT` 15, and a `HOUSEHOLD_INTERVIEW` 10. Each item's age in whole days is the difference between the current logical clock and the item's `observed_at`, expressed in seconds, divided by 86,400 and floored. That age selects a multiplier: 1.00 for an age of at most 180 days, 0.75 for an age from 181 through 365 days inclusive, 0.50 for an age from 366 through 730 days inclusive, and 0.00 for an age beyond 730 days. Each item's weighted contribution is its base weight multiplied by its multiplier; the contributions stay exact and a **single** half-up rounding is applied to their sum; and `trafficking_confidence_score` is that rounded total capped at 100. Evidence older than two years contributes exactly nothing, so a case holding five stale items still scores 0.

**Concluding.** A conclusion records a `substantiated_violation` of `TRAFFICKING`, `INELIGIBLE_ITEM_SALES` or `NONE`, and sets `concluded_at` and `concluded_by_user_id`. Whichever branch is taken, the concluding user must differ from `opened_by_user_id`, or the conclusion is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The two substantiating branches carry different evidentiary bars and both refuse with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. Concluding for `TRAFFICKING` moves the investigation from `EVIDENCE_REVIEW` to `SUBSTANTIATED` and requires two things together: a `trafficking_confidence_score` of at least 60, **and** at least one `UNDERCOVER_BUY` or `INVENTORY_PURCHASE_MISMATCH` item whose own decay multiplier is still greater than zero — a live direct-evidence anchor. A case can clear 60 on informant statements and pattern analysis alone and still be refused for want of that anchor. Concluding for `INELIGIBLE_ITEM_SALES` likewise moves the investigation from `EVIDENCE_REVIEW` to `SUBSTANTIATED` and requires at least one evidence item — of any type — whose decay multiplier is still greater than zero, regardless of the score.

Concluding with `NONE` drives the investigation to `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, and is permitted from either `OPEN` or `EVIDENCE_REVIEW`, so a case that never accumulated evidence can be closed out. It also returns the store's `sanction_status` from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to `NONE`, but only when no other investigation of that store is still sitting in `OPEN` or `EVIDENCE_REVIEW`; a store with two live investigations stays under investigation until the last one closes. `SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED` are the two terminal statuses of an investigation.

### 3.9. Charge Letters, Determinations and the Civil Money Penalty

A sanction case is the vehicle for due process. It is opened from a substantiated investigation by naming the `investigation_id` and the `violation_type` — `TRAFFICKING`, `INELIGIBLE_ITEMS_HARDSHIP` or `STOCK_FAILURE`. The referenced investigation must be `SUBSTANTIATED`, or the case is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`; and where that investigation was opened on an anomaly flag, its trigger period must genuinely have closed at `FLAG_L3`, or the case is refused with `ERR_TRIGGER_PERIOD_NOT_FLAGGED`. Opening copies `store_id` from the investigation rather than accepting it from the caller, lands the case in `CHARGE_DRAFT`, sets `predecessor_case_id` to the store's most recent `SANCTION_FINAL` or `ENFORCED` case when one exists — forming the repeat-violation chain — and computes `prior_final_sanction_count`.

`prior_final_sanction_count` counts **other** sanction cases whose status is `SANCTION_FINAL` or `ENFORCED` and whose `sanction_type` is `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` or `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION`, drawn from this store **or from any other store belonging to the same owner**. Repeat violations follow the owner across locations, which means a second store's enforcement history lengthens the first store's sanction.

**Issuing the charge letter.** The charge letter carries a `charge_summary` narrative, which must be present. Issuance moves the case from `CHARGE_DRAFT` to `CHARGE_ISSUED`, sets `charge_letter_sent_at` to the current logical-clock instant, records the issuing administrator's `X-User-ID` as `charge_issued_by_user_id`, and fixes both `reply_due_at` and `cmp_election_due_at` at ten days after `charge_letter_sent_at`. It also moves the store's `sanction_status` from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to `CHARGED` and advances the case's `version` by one. The store remains `AUTHORIZED` and keeps redeeming.

**The retailer's reply.** Within the reply window the retailer may file a `reply_text`, moving the case from `CHARGE_ISSUED` to `REPLY_RECEIVED` and recording `reply_received_at` from the logical clock. The window's closing edge is exclusive: a reply filed at or after `reply_due_at` is refused with `ERR_REPLY_WINDOW_CLOSED`. This is one of the four operations exclusive to the retailer's own firm alongside the administrator.

**Electing to pay instead.** A retailer facing disqualification may elect to pay a civil money penalty in lieu of it. The election payload carries `expected_sanction_case_version` and an optional `hardship_ownership_change_pending` flag, defaulting to false, which is set true when the election rests on a pending sale of the firm rather than on a hardship designation alone. An election filed at or after `cmp_election_due_at` is refused with `ERR_CMP_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A successful election creates a row whose `sanction_case_id` is copied from the path, whose `elected_at` is the current logical-clock instant, whose `status` is `ELECTED`, and which quotes the penalty as it stands at election time: `quoted_amount_cents` and `quoted_basis_variant` are the amount and the basis variant the penalty formula yields against the election-time history. That history is the same eligible pool the determination uses — the store's monitoring periods that are `CLOSED` with a `period_end` at or before `charge_letter_sent_at` — so an election against an empty pool cannot be quoted at all and is refused with `ERR_NO_REDEMPTION_HISTORY`, exactly as a determination would be; this is the gate rule 18 of §2.8 attaches to `POST /sanction-cases/{caseId}/cmp-elections`, and it applies whatever the case's `violation_type` and independently of the hardship and owner-history gates, which are tested only at the determination. Filing a second election on the same case drives the previous `ELECTED` row to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one election on a case is ever `ELECTED`. Each election advances the case's `version` by one. Election statuses are `ELECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `PAID`, of which `PAID`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

**Sizing the sanction.** The determination is the pivot of the whole case. It carries a `sanction_type` and moves the case to `DETERMINED` from `CHARGE_ISSUED`, from `REPLY_RECEIVED`, or from `REMANDED` after a review sent it back. When the case is still in `CHARGE_ISSUED` — that is, the retailer never replied — the determination is permitted only once the reply window has **closed**; issuing it while the logical clock is still before `reply_due_at` is refused with `ERR_REPLY_WINDOW_OPEN`, because the retailer's right to respond has not expired. A determination reached from `REPLY_RECEIVED` or `REMANDED` carries no such wait.

The supplied `sanction_type` must be permitted for the case's `violation_type`, otherwise the determination is refused with `ERR_SANCTION_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`. The three violation types have three distinct allowed sets, and each also sizes `disqualification_months` from `prior_final_sanction_count`:

- For `TRAFFICKING`, the only permitted sanctions are `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` and `CMP_IN_LIEU`. A permanent disqualification leaves `disqualification_months` null, since it never ends.
- For `INELIGIBLE_ITEMS_HARDSHIP`, the only permitted sanctions are `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION` and `CMP_IN_LIEU`. `disqualification_months` is 3 when `prior_final_sanction_count` is 0, 6 when it is exactly 1, and 12 for any higher count.
- For `STOCK_FAILURE`, the only permitted sanctions are `WARNING` and `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION`. `disqualification_months` is 0 — a warning with no exclusion period — when `prior_final_sanction_count` is 0, and 6 for any higher count.

`disqualification_months` is also null for a `WARNING`.

**Computing the penalty.** When the determined `sanction_type` is `CMP_IN_LIEU`, the amount is computed here and written to the ledger. Three gates must hold together, and any failure is refused with `ERR_CMP_NOT_AVAILABLE`: the store must be `hardship_designated`; there must be an `ELECTED` election on the case whose `elected_at` is strictly before `cmp_election_due_at`; and the owner must hold no sanction case anywhere in its portfolio whose status is `SANCTION_FINAL` or `ENFORCED` and whose `violation_type` is `TRAFFICKING`. That third condition sweeps the whole owner, so a repeat trafficker cannot buy its way out of a second permanent disqualification.

The basis for the amount is the store's own redemption history: the eligible pool is every monitoring period of that store whose status is `CLOSED` and whose `period_end` is at or before `charge_letter_sent_at`. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_NO_REDEMPTION_HISTORY` — a penalty cannot be sized without history. Which periods of that pool enter the basis depends on how long the store has been in the program at the moment the charge letter issued. Take the whole number of days between `charge_letter_sent_at` and the store's `authorized_at`. When that span is **365 days or more** — a store with a full year of authorized history — the variant is `TRAILING_12_PERIOD_AVERAGE` and the basis is the most recent 12 periods of the pool ordered by `period_index` descending, or the whole pool when it holds fewer than 12. When the span is **fewer than 365 days** — a newly authorized store whose short history must be annualized from its busiest stretch — the variant is `PEAK_3_PERIOD_AVERAGE` and the basis is the 3 periods with the highest `redemption_total_cents`, or the whole pool when it holds fewer than 3, with ties broken first by the higher `period_index` and then by the earlier `created_at`. The chosen variant is recorded as `cmp_basis_variant` and the basis rows as `cmp_basis_period_ids`, listed in the variant's own ordering.

From the basis, `avg_monthly_redemption_cents` is the sum of `redemption_total_cents` over the basis periods divided by the number of basis periods, rounded half up to whole cents. `monthly_penalty_cents` is ten percent of that average — the average multiplied by 1,000 basis points, divided by 10,000 — rounded half up to whole cents. The month multiplier is 10 months flat for a `TRAFFICKING` case, and otherwise the `disqualification_months` the previous paragraph derived. `cmp_amount_cents` is the monthly penalty multiplied by the month multiplier, capped at the statutory maximum of $69,000.00, and `cmp_capped` is true exactly when the uncapped product would have exceeded that maximum. The rounding order is pinned: the monthly ten-percent figure is rounded to whole cents **before** the multiplication by the month count, because multiplying an unrounded average by the rate and by the months and rounding once at the end differs by as much as one cent per month.

**Everything the determination writes.** A determination sets `determined_at` from the logical clock, records the issuing administrator as `determined_by_user_id`, fixes `admin_review_due_at` at ten days after `determined_at`, recomputes `prior_final_sanction_count` and `disqualification_months`, and — for `CMP_IN_LIEU` only — computes `cmp_basis_variant`, `cmp_basis_period_ids`, `avg_monthly_redemption_cents`, `monthly_penalty_cents`, `cmp_amount_cents` and `cmp_capped`, then writes a `CMP_ASSESSED` ledger entry for the full `cmp_amount_cents` and re-derives the case's `cmp_outstanding_cents`, the owner's `cmp_outstanding_cents` and the owner's `sanction_flag`. It also disposes of the election: the `ELECTED` row moves to `APPROVED` when the determined sanction is `CMP_IN_LIEU` and to `REJECTED` for every other sanction type. The case's `version` advances by one.

**The penalty ledger.** Every movement of penalty money is an append-only row carrying an `entry_type` of `CMP_ASSESSED`, `CMP_PAYMENT` or `CMP_ABATEMENT`, a signed `amount_cents` — positive for an assessment, negative for a payment or an abatement — a `reason_code` of `DETERMINATION`, `RETAILER_REMITTANCE` or `REVIEW_REVERSAL`, and a `balance_after_cents` giving the running balance through that row. A retailer remits against the balance by supplying an `amount_cents`; the server appends a `CMP_PAYMENT` row for the negation of that amount with `reason_code` `RETAILER_REMITTANCE`, computes `balance_after_cents`, and re-derives the case's `cmp_outstanding_cents`, the owner's `cmp_outstanding_cents` and the owner's `sanction_flag`. A remittance greater than the outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_CMP_OVERPAYMENT`; the balance may never go negative. When a payment brings the case's outstanding balance to exactly 0, the `APPROVED` election on the case moves to `PAID`, and the owner's `cmp_outstanding_cents` and `sanction_flag` are re-derived accordingly — an owner whose only debt has just been cleared drops from `HAS_ACTIVE_CMP_DEBT` back to `CLEAR`.

### 3.10. Administrative Review, Finality and Enforcement

A retailer that disagrees with a determination has ten days to say so. Requesting review carries a `grounds_text` and moves the case from `DETERMINED` to `ADMIN_REVIEW_REQUESTED`, recording `admin_review_requested_at` from the logical clock and moving the store's `sanction_status` from `CHARGED` to `UNDER_ADMIN_REVIEW`. A request filed at or after `admin_review_due_at` is refused with `ERR_ADMIN_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The case's `version` advances by one.

The review decision belongs exclusively to the independent review officer, and the independence is enforced: the deciding officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from both `charge_issued_by_user_id` and `determined_by_user_id` on that case, otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The decision carries an `outcome` of `SUSTAINED`, `REVERSED` or `REMANDED` and an optional `reason_code` drawn from `EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT`, `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`, `PROCEDURAL_DEFECT` and `NEW_EVIDENCE`. Whichever outcome is chosen, the server records `admin_review_outcome`, `admin_review_decided_by_user_id`, the decision instant and `reason_code`, and advances the case's `version` by one. The three outcomes then diverge sharply, and each is specified in full.

**Sustained.** The case stays in `ADMIN_REVIEW_REQUESTED` — this is the one self-transition in the case machine — with `admin_review_outcome` recorded as `SUSTAINED`. Nothing about the sanction changes; the case simply becomes eligible for finalization on the strength of the review having upheld it.

**Reversed.** The case moves from `ADMIN_REVIEW_REQUESTED` to `SANCTION_REVERSED`. A `CMP_ABATEMENT` ledger entry is written for the negation of whatever balance remains, driving the case's `cmp_outstanding_cents` to exactly 0 while leaving both the original assessment row and the abatement row on the ledger — the compensating pair of §2.12, never a deletion. The store's `sanction_status` moves to `SANCTION_REVERSED`, and its `authorization_status` is restored to `AUTHORIZED` when it had been driven to `SUSPENDED`. `SANCTION_REVERSED` is one of the two terminal statuses of a case.

**Remanded.** The case moves from `ADMIN_REVIEW_REQUESTED` to `REMANDED`, sending the matter back for a fresh determination. `remand_count` increments by 1, and `sanction_type`, `disqualification_months` and every computed penalty figure — `cmp_basis_variant`, `cmp_basis_period_ids`, `avg_monthly_redemption_cents`, `monthly_penalty_cents`, `cmp_amount_cents` and `cmp_capped` — are all cleared, because the determination they described no longer exists. Any outstanding balance is abated with a `CMP_ABATEMENT` entry. A case may be remanded **at most once**: a second remand on a case whose `remand_count` is already 1 is refused with `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED`. From `REMANDED` the administrator issues a fresh determination, which is what makes the case machine cyclic.

**Finalization.** A sanction becomes final by one of two routes: from `DETERMINED` once the administrative-review window has closed without a request, or from `ADMIN_REVIEW_REQUESTED` when `admin_review_outcome` is `SUSTAINED`. Either way the case moves to `SANCTION_FINAL`, `final_decision_at` is set from the logical clock, and `effective_date` is set to thirty days after `final_decision_at` **for disqualification sanctions only** — for `CMP_IN_LIEU` and for `WARNING` it stays null, because there is nothing to enforce on a date. The store's `sanction_status` moves to `SANCTION_FINAL`, and for a `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` or `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION` its `authorization_status` moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `SUSPENDED`. The store's `ACTIVE` terminal commitments are deliberately **left untouched** at this point; the hardware stays in place through the thirty-day notice period. Because the owner rollup of §3.1 counts a case as soon as its status is `SANCTION_FINAL`, and not only at `ENFORCED`, finalization also re-derives the owning firm's `permanent_disqualification_count`, `cmp_outstanding_cents`, `sanction_flag` and `status` from the definitions in §3.1 — so a finalized `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` already raises that count and already flips the owner to `PERMANENTLY_DISQUALIFIED`, before any enforcement. The case's `version` advances by one.

**Enforcement.** Enforcement is the last act and the widest cascade in the system. It is permitted only from `SANCTION_FINAL`, only for a `sanction_type` of `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` or `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION` — attempting it on a `WARNING` or a `CMP_IN_LIEU` case is refused with `ERR_SANCTION_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED` — and only once the logical clock has reached `effective_date`, before which it is refused with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_NOT_DUE`. The edge is inclusive: enforcement at exactly `effective_date` is permitted.

A successful enforcement moves the case to `ENFORCED`, its second terminal status, and sets `enforced_at` from the logical clock. It then cascades four hops outward. First, the store's `authorization_status` moves from `SUSPENDED` to `DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal for that dimension; for a `PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION` the store's `permanently_disqualified` flag is set true and `disqualified_until` is left null, while for a `TEMPORARY_DISQUALIFICATION` `disqualified_until` is set to `enforced_at` plus the case's `disqualification_months` and `permanently_disqualified` stays false. Second, every one of the store's `ACTIVE` terminal commitments moves to `RECALL_PENDING` with `close_reason_code` set to `AUTHORIZATION_LOST`, and the store's `active_terminal_count` is decremented for each. Third, each unit behind those commitments moves from `DEPLOYED` to `PENDING_RETURN` and every affected depot's counters are recomputed. Fourth, the owner rollup is re-derived: `permanent_disqualification_count` rises for a permanent disqualification, and `sanction_flag` and `status` are recomputed from it — which is how a second permanent disqualification anywhere in an owner's portfolio flips that owner to `BARRED` and locks it out of filing ever again. The case's `version` advances by one.

The interaction with redemption is worth restating from the store's point of view. A store trades normally through investigation, charge letter, reply, determination and administrative review. It stops trading the moment a disqualification is finalized, because finalization suspends it. If review then reverses the sanction, it trades again. If review sustains it and the thirty days elapse, enforcement disqualifies it terminally and recalls its hardware. And if the determination was a civil money penalty in lieu of disqualification, the store never stops trading at all — it simply owes money, tracked to the cent on an append-only ledger, and its owner carries the `HAS_ACTIVE_CMP_DEBT` flag until the balance reaches zero.
