# Corporate Video & Brand Film Production API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A corporate video and brand film production company lives or dies on the gap between what was scoped and what was shot. A signed creative brief promises a hero film and a handful of channel cutdowns; six weeks later the client has asked for a fourth round of changes, the union performer's reuse cycles have doubled because the social cutdown was licensed for three years, a permit lapsed the night before the shoot, and nobody can say whether the production is still inside its not-to-exceed number. This API is the system of record that closes that gap. It carries a production from creative scoping through budget construction, casting, location scouting, permitting, crew and equipment booking, the shoot itself, the client review loop, and finally the archive handover that starts the raw-footage retention clock.

Two properties shape every requirement that follows. The first is that money is always visible in two forms at once: a **provisional** figure that includes everything anyone has planned, and a **committed** figure that includes only what the company is contractually on the hook for. Every write that adds, confirms, releases or voids a cost-bearing row moves one or both of these figures, and a not-to-exceed allowance is checked against the provisional figure before any such row is persisted. The second is that creative work is entitlement-metered: a project buys a fixed number of client revision rounds per deliverable, and the moment that entitlement is spent the system prices a change order, freezes the review loop across the whole project until the client decides on it, and only then releases the extra round.

The API is written for four production-side operators, the client, and the booked crew, and it enforces segregation of duties between them: the supervisor who submits a cut cannot record its review decision, and the reviewer whose decision spawned a change order cannot approve that change order. Everything is deterministic and driven by a logical clock, so permit expiry, call-sheet lead time, rush tiering and retention windows are all reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning conventions, the logical clock, monetary precision, the two cost ledgers, the fiscal allowance gate, cascade semantics, stale-version protection, identifier resolution, and the order in which rules are evaluated.

### 2.1. Production Roles, Headers and Segregation of Duties

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's operational capacity. Six roles exist and their spellings are exact: `ProductionCoordinator`, `LineProducer`, `PostSupervisor`, `ClientApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `CrewMember`. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything outside those six values, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the acting role is not listed among the authorized roles of the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The roles divide the production along duty lines. A `ProductionCoordinator` runs the physical production — locations, shoot days, call sheets, crew bookings and equipment rentals. A `LineProducer` owns the money — opening the project, building the budget, casting talent, and voiding an approved change order. A `PostSupervisor` delivers the cuts: uploading versions, recording technical QC, and submitting cuts for client review. A `ClientApprover` represents the client: approving the budget, judging each cut, and paying for extra revision rounds by approving change orders. A `ComplianceOfficer` files film permits and certificates of insurance and records the issuing authority's or carrier's decision. A `CrewMember` is a booked crew person who may read only its own bookings plus the shoot days and roster entry those bookings reference, and may write nothing.

Five of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProductionCoordinator`, `LineProducer`, `PostSupervisor`, `ClientApprover`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a cut, as `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a change order — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The sixth role is different: when `X-User-Role` is `CrewMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CrewMember` roster row, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `CrewMember` compare that resource's `crew_member_id` to `X-User-ID`. Consequently a `CrewMember` caller reading a single booking whose `crew_member_id` differs from its own `X-User-ID`, or listing bookings without supplying a `crew_member_id` filter equal to its own `X-User-ID`, is refused with `ERR_CREW_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

Segregation of duties is enforced on `X-User-ID` in three places, all of them yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The user recorded as having submitted a cut may not record that cut's review decision. The user recorded as having requested a change order may not decide that change order. The officer who filed a compliance certificate may not record its issue-or-deny decision.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it defaults to 10 when omitted, and must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); this pagination check is declared for the cut list, the shoot-day list, the compliance-certificate list and the crew-booking list, and the same 1-to-100 and non-negative rules are applied wherever pagination parameters are accepted.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two lists override that default because operational readers scan them chronologically: shoot days are ordered by `call_time` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending as a tie-break, and crew bookings are ordered by `call_time` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Change orders raised against a deliverable are returned newest last, which is the `created_at` ascending default.

Every list response sets `response_type` to `"array"`, returns the rows as the envelope's `data` array, and carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource in this system — projects, budget lines, deliverables, cuts, change orders, talent engagements, locations, shoot days, compliance certificates, crew members, crew bookings and equipment reservations — carries the same four system-managed properties, with no exceptions and no entity omitting any of them:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was first written, with three fractional digits. This stamp is read from real wall-clock UTC time, not from the logical production clock of §2.5, so it advances of its own accord and moving the logical clock neither changes it nor is reflected in it.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent modification, refreshed on every mutation; like `created_at` it is read from real wall-clock UTC time rather than from the logical clock. The no-wall-clock rule of §2.5 governs the logical clock's own value and the derived rules that read it, not these two record stamps.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.

Recomputation of a purely derived column for the purpose of answering a read is not a mutation and does not advance `version` or `updated_at`. Writes that change stored state do — and when a write cascades into a second row, that second row's `version` and `updated_at` advance too. Locking a project's scope, approving its budget, handing it over, and every ledger movement caused by a child are all mutations of the project row.

### 2.5. The Logical Production Clock

Every window, expiry, lead-time and rush-tier rule in this specification reads a server-side logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. An operator in any of the five production-side roles may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. `CrewMember` callers may not move the clock.

Moving the clock has no stored side effects, but every derived value that reads it changes meaning immediately: a certificate's `effective_status`, a shoot day's `compliance_posture`, a project's `archive_status`, the rush tier applied to a newly priced change order, the call-sheet lead-time gate, and the wrap gate that requires the clock to have reached a day's planned wrap time.

### 2.6. Cents, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All money in this system is expressed in whole cents as integers. Percentages and multipliers (`markup_pct`, `rush_pct`, `producer_fee_pct`, the buyout multipliers, the agent and pension rates) are decimal numbers and keep their input precision. Wherever a monetary amount is derived from a multiplication, the result is converted to whole cents by rounding half up — that is, to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half rounding away from zero.

Where the rounding happens is a contract, not an implementation detail, because two orders give different cents. Three rules pin it:

- **Budget lines round per line, then sum.** A line's markup is rounded to whole cents on that line, the rounded markup is added to that line's exact raw amount, and only then are the line totals added together for a project roll-up. Summing raw amounts across lines and applying the markup percentage to the sum is forbidden: three lines whose raw amounts are each 22,220 cents at a markup of 0.075 produce 1,667 cents of markup each and therefore 23,887 cents per line and 71,661 cents for the roll-up, whereas summing raw first (66,660 cents) and rounding a single markup of 4,999.5 cents to 5,000 would produce 71,660 cents — one cent short of the sanctioned answer.
- **Crew bookings sum first, then round once.** Deliberately the opposite order: a booking's straight-time, first-tier overtime and second-tier overtime components are computed as exact values, added together, and the single total is rounded half up exactly once.
- **Talent compensation rounds per component, then sums.** Each money component of an engagement is rounded to whole cents on its own, and the components are then added; the resulting total is never re-rounded.

Because every addend of a project-level roll-up is already a whole-cent integer, no rounding occurs at roll-up time, and both roll-ups are order-independent — no tie-break rule is needed.

### 2.7. The Twin Cost Ledgers: Provisional and Committed

Each project publishes two live cost figures, recomputed from its children on every mutation that adds, removes or re-states a budget line, a talent engagement, a crew booking, an equipment reservation or a change order, and again whenever the project is read.

`provisional_cost_cents` is the forward-looking estimate. It is the sum of five contributions: every budget line of the project whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `COMMITTED`, contributing its `extended_cents`; every talent engagement of the project whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, contributing its `total_comp_cents`; every crew booking on any shoot day of the project whose `status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, contributing its `cost_cents`; every equipment reservation on any shoot day of the project whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `RETURNED`, contributing its `total_cost_cents`; and every change order on any deliverable of the project whose `status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED`, contributing its `amount_cents`.

`committed_cost_cents` is the contractually binding figure and is built from the same five families with strictly tighter status filters: budget lines only when `COMMITTED`, talent engagements only when `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, crew bookings only when `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, equipment reservations when `RESERVED` or `RETURNED` (the same filter as the provisional side), and change orders only when `APPROVED`. Because those four families exclude the not-yet-committed statuses — `PLANNED` lines, `DRAFT` engagements, `HELD` bookings and `PENDING_APPROVAL` change orders — the committed figure is strictly smaller than the provisional figure whenever any such row exists, and equal to it when none does.

Three exclusions are absolute and prevent double counting. A shoot day's `day_cost_cents` never enters either figure: it is a display-only day total, and a location's daily fee reaches the project ledger only through a budget line of category `LOCATION`. A compliance certificate's `fee_cents` never enters either figure. And rows that have left the production — crew bookings and equipment reservations in `RELEASED`, change orders in `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` — are excluded from both.

`budget_variance_cents` is `provisional_cost_cents` minus `approved_budget_cents`, and is reported as 0 for as long as `approved_budget_cents` is null. It is recomputed on every event that moves the provisional figure.

### 2.8. The Not-To-Exceed Allowance Gate

Four writes create a cost commitment: adding a budget line, casting a talent engagement, booking crew, and reserving equipment. Before any of them is persisted, the server computes an allowance and compares it against the projected provisional cost. The allowance depends on where the project stands: while the project's `status` is `DRAFT` or `SCOPED` the allowance equals `budget_ceiling_cents` exactly, with no tolerance; once the budget has been approved — that is, for `IN_PRODUCTION`, `IN_POST` and any later status — the allowance is `budget_ceiling_cents` multiplied by 1.05, rounded half up to whole cents, so the production may run five percent hot against its contracted ceiling. A ceiling of 300,000 cents therefore yields a post-approval allowance of 315,000 cents.

The projected figure is the project's current `provisional_cost_cents` plus the provisional contribution the new row would add — nothing else; no other pending write participates. If the projected figure is strictly greater than the allowance the write is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); a projected figure exactly equal to the allowance is accepted. This gate is evaluated **last** of all checks on those four endpoints, so a state or safety defect on the same request always reports its own error first.

### 2.9. Cascades, Bubble-Ups and Derived Column Refresh

State in this system propagates in two directions, and both are the server's own responsibility rather than something a client requests. **Cascade down** means a parent's transition rewrites its children: approving a budget commits every planned line, cancelling a shoot day releases its bookings and reservations, wrapping a day completes its confirmed crew and returns its reserved kit, and handing a project over marks its approved deliverables as handed over. **Bubble up** means a child's transition forces its parent forward: submitting a cut moves its deliverable into review, a review approval can move a deliverable to approved and, if it was the last one outstanding, move the whole project to delivered, and the disappearance of the last non-terminal shoot day moves the project from production into post.

Cascades are transitive and each hop is a real mutation with its own ledger consequences; the workflow narratives in §3 state each hop explicitly, in order, together with which figures move and which deliberately do not. Whenever a cascade rewrites a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented.

Derived columns — a project's ledger figures, `budget_variance_cents`, `round_entitlement_balance`, `review_gate`, `archive_status` and `retention_expires_at`; a deliverable's `consumed_review_rounds`, `granted_extra_rounds`, `round_entitlement` and `version_count`; a location's `scheduled_shoot_day_count` and `scouting_status`; a shoot day's `compliance_posture`, `confirmed_crew_count` and `day_cost_cents`; a certificate's `effective_status` — are recomputed from live data before being returned by a read, so a read never serves a stale derivation.

### 2.10. Stale-Version Protection on Cut Uploads

One write in the system takes optimistic concurrency control: the cut upload, `POST /deliverable-versions`. That request must carry `expected_deliverable_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent deliverable. The server compares it against the parent `Deliverable.version` stored at the moment the create is processed; if the two differ, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DELIVERABLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The purpose is not to arbitrate between requests but to stop a writer from acting on a read it has outgrown. Two successive uploads are both legitimate under the product rules — the second cut retires the first through `supersedes_version_id` — so a writer working from a stale read would otherwise silently branch the cut history and corrupt the ordering of `round_index`. Each accepted upload increments the parent deliverable's `version`, which is exactly why the next upload must present the new number. No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.11. Reverse-Reachability Reads and Identifier Resolution Order

Write paths in this API are flat: each collection sits at the top level and a child names its parent through a foreign-key field in the request body rather than through a nested URL. Reads are the exception — a handful of sub-resource read paths exist purely so that a client can walk from a parent to its children: a project's budget lines, deliverables and talent engagements; a deliverable's change orders (the only channel through which a change order created by a cascade can be discovered); and a shoot day's equipment reservations.

Where a path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the error. A request for the change orders of a deliverable that does not exist reports `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND` and never proceeds to look at anything else. The same discipline applies to identifiers supplied in a request body: each referenced row must exist, and each family has its own 404 — `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` for a `project_id`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND` for a `deliverable_id`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_VERSION_NOT_FOUND` for a cut identifier in the path or a `supersedes_version_id` in the payload, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for a change-order identifier, `ERR_SHOOT_DAY_NOT_FOUND` for a `shoot_day_id`, `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND` for a `location_id`, `ERR_CREW_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` for a `crew_member_id`, `ERR_CREW_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` for a booking identifier, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CERT_NOT_FOUND` for a certificate identifier, and `ERR_TALENT_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an engagement identifier on the engagement-confirmation call. Every one of these is HTTP 404.

### 2.12. Rule Precedence: Safety Before Schedule, Schedule Before Money

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined. The domain ordering is deliberate: a shoot that is illegal or unstaffable must be refused for *that* reason even when it also blows the budget, so production safety and compliance rank above temporal feasibility, temporal feasibility above scope and entitlement, scope above referential rights coherence, and money last of all. The complete precedence order, highest first, is:

- Priority 1: **Authentication** — headers present and naming one of the six roles (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
- Priority 2: **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
- Priority 3: **Crew read scoping** — a `CrewMember` caller may only read crew-booking rows whose `crew_member_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; a booking list without a `crew_member_id` filter, or with a different one, is refused (`ERR_CREW_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`), and this applies to both the crew-booking list and the single crew-booking read.
- Priority 4: **Request shape** — the body parses, every required field is present and of the declared type, enum fields carry a declared member, monetary and count fields are non-negative integers, and interval fields satisfy start strictly before end (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400).
- Priority 5: **Pagination shape** — `limit` and `offset` within their bands (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`).
- Priorities 6 through 15: **Existence** of every referenced row, in this order of identifier families — project (priority 6), deliverable (7), cut (8), change order (9), shoot day (10), location (11), crew member (12), crew booking (13), compliance certificate (14), talent engagement (15) — each reporting the 404 code listed in §2.11.
- Priority 16: **Stale parent version** on a cut upload (`ERR_STALE_DELIVERABLE_VERSION`).
- Priority 17: **Project status permits the operation** (`ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409). Scope lock requires `DRAFT`; budget approval requires `SCOPED`; budget-line creation requires `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`; talent-engagement creation requires `DRAFT`, `SCOPED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; deliverable creation requires `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`; shoot-day creation requires `SCOPED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; shoot-day confirmation requires `IN_PRODUCTION`; cut submission and review decisions require `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`; handover requires `DELIVERED`.
- Priority 18: **Deliverable status permits the operation** (`ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`): a cut may only be uploaded against a deliverable in `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; an `APPROVED` or `HANDED_OVER` deliverable accepts no further cuts.
- Priority 19: **Cut review status permits the operation** (`ERR_VERSION_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`): submission requires `DRAFT`, a review decision requires `SUBMITTED`, and a `SUPERSEDED`, `APPROVED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` cut accepts neither.
- Priority 20: **QC recorded at most once** — the cut's `technical_status` must still be `PENDING_QC` (`ERR_QC_ALREADY_RECORDED`).
- Priority 21: **QC passed before submission** — a cut may only be submitted while its `technical_status` is `QC_PASSED`; both `PENDING_QC` and `QC_FAILED` are refused (`ERR_VERSION_QC_NOT_PASSED`).
- Priority 22: **Shoot-day status permits the operation** (`ERR_SHOOT_DAY_STATE_INVALID`): confirmation requires `PLANNED`; call-sheet publication requires `CONFIRMED`; wrap requires `CONFIRMED`; cancellation requires `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`; crew-booking and equipment-reservation creation require the day to be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`.
- Priority 23: **Crew-booking status permits the operation** (`ERR_CREW_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`): confirmation requires `HELD`, release requires `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, and a `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED` booking accepts neither.
- Priority 24: **Certificate status permits a decision** — must be `SUBMITTED`; `ISSUED` and `DENIED` are terminal (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_CERT_STATE_INVALID`).
- Priority 25: **Change-order status permits the decision** — `PENDING_APPROVAL` for an approve or reject, `APPROVED` for a void (`ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`).
- Priority 26: **Engagement status permits confirmation** — must be `DRAFT` (`ERR_TALENT_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`).
- Priority 27: **Handover happens once** — the project's `archive_status` must still be `NOT_PACKAGED` (`ERR_ARCHIVE_ALREADY_HANDED_OVER`).
- Priority 28: **Segregation of duties** on the three decision calls (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403).
- Priority 29: **Film permit window** (`ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422), checked on both shoot-day confirmation and call-sheet publication.
- Priority 30: **Insurance coverage** (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COI_COVERAGE`, HTTP 422), checked on both shoot-day confirmation and call-sheet publication.
- Priority 31: **Live compliance posture not blocked** (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, HTTP 422), checked on call-sheet publication and on wrap.
- Priority 32: **Minimum confirmed crew** (`ERR_MIN_CREW_NOT_MET`, HTTP 422), checked on call-sheet publication.
- Priority 33: **Call-sheet lead time** (`ERR_CALL_SHEET_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422), checked on call-sheet publication.
- Priority 34: **Crew double booking** (`ERR_CREW_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, HTTP 409), checked on crew-booking creation.
- Priority 35: **Crew turnaround** (`ERR_CREW_TURNAROUND_VIOLATION`, HTTP 409), checked on crew-booking creation after the double-booking check.
- Priority 36: **Equipment prep and return windows** (`ERR_EQUIPMENT_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE`, HTTP 422), checked on equipment-reservation creation.
- Priority 37: **Location stage capacity** (`ERR_LOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409), checked on shoot-day creation.
- Priority 38: **Revision-round entitlement** (`ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409), checked on cut submission.
- Priority 39: **Change-order freeze** (`ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING`, HTTP 409), checked on cut submission after the entitlement check.
- Priority 40: **Scope completeness** (`ERR_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE`, HTTP 422), checked on scope lock.
- Priority 41: **Channel within scope** (`ERR_CHANNEL_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, HTTP 422), checked on deliverable creation.
- Priority 42: **Usage rights within the master term** (`ERR_USAGE_RIGHTS_EXCEED_TERM`, HTTP 422), checked on deliverable creation after the channel check.
- Priority 43: **Union status matches compensation model** (`ERR_TALENT_COMP_MODEL_INVALID`, HTTP 422), checked on engagement creation.
- Priority 44: **Supersede target coherence** (`ERR_VERSION_DELIVERABLE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422), checked on cut upload.
- Priority 45: **Certificate scope coherence** (`ERR_CERT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422), checked on certificate filing.
- Priority 46: **Fiscal allowance** (`ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422), checked last on the four commitment-creating writes described in §2.8.

Two consequences deserve emphasis. Because the film-permit check outranks the insurance check, a shoot day that lacks both reports `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`. And because the entitlement check outranks the change-order freeze, a cut submitted against a deliverable that has both exhausted its rounds and a pending change order reports `ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Scoping the Project: Brief, Channels and Locked Scope

A production begins when a `LineProducer` opens a project from a signed creative brief. The payload names the commissioning `client_name` and the internal `campaign_title` — each a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters — and carries a `creative_brief_summary`, the condensed brief agreed with the client at scoping, which must be a non-blank string of at least 10 and at most 2000 characters. Licensing is declared up front: `channel_scope` is a non-empty array of distinct channel names drawn from `BROADCAST`, `OLV`, `SOCIAL`, `INTERNAL` and `OOH` (duplicates are rejected), and `usage_term_months` is the licensed usage term applying to every deliverable, an integer from 1 to 60. The commercial envelope is `budget_ceiling_cents`, the contracted not-to-exceed production budget in whole cents, which must be greater than zero.

Three inputs are optional and carry defaults that the contract assumes when the payload is silent. `contracted_revision_rounds` is the number of client review rounds included per deliverable; it defaults to 2 and must be an integer from 0 to 10. `raw_footage_retention_months` is how long the company must keep camera negative after handover; it defaults to 12 and must be an integer from 0 to 120. `raw_footage_terabytes` declares the raw volume destined for the archive package; it defaults to 0 and must be a number of at least 0.

A newly opened project starts with `status` `DRAFT`, both `provisional_cost_cents` and `committed_cost_cents` at 0, `approved_budget_cents` null (so `budget_variance_cents` reads 0), `review_gate` `OPEN`, `archive_status` `NOT_PACKAGED`, `handover_at` and `handover_recipient` null, `retention_expires_at` null, and `round_entitlement_balance` equal to `contracted_revision_rounds`.

Deliverables are the units of creative work, and either a `ProductionCoordinator` or a `LineProducer` may register one while the project is `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`. Registration names the owning project through a `project_id` in the payload and describes the format completely: a `title` such as "Hero film 60s" (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), the single `channel` this cut is licensed for, the `aspect_ratio` every accepted version must deliver — one of `16:9`, `9:16`, `1:1`, `4:5` or `2.39:1` — the contracted `target_duration_seconds` (an integer greater than 0 and at most 3600), the `caption_requirement` (`NONE`, `CLOSED_SRT`, `BURNED_IN_SUBTITLES` or `OPEN_CAPTIONS`), the deliverable's own `usage_rights_months` (1 to 60), and the contracted first-air instant `air_date`, which later drives rush tiering on change orders. Two rights checks guard the registration, in this order: the `channel` must be a member of the project's `channel_scope`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; and `usage_rights_months` must not exceed the project's `usage_term_months`, or the request is refused with `ERR_USAGE_RIGHTS_EXCEED_TERM`. A registered deliverable starts at `status` `PENDING` with `consumed_review_rounds` at 0, `granted_extra_rounds` at 0, `round_entitlement` equal to the project's `contracted_revision_rounds`, and `version_count` at 0.

Freezing the scope is the `LineProducer`'s move and advances the project `DRAFT → SCOPED`. It is permitted only from `DRAFT`, and it requires the project to carry at least one deliverable **and** at least one budget line; a project missing either, or both, is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE`. The lock also re-checks rights containment across the whole scope: every deliverable of the project must satisfy the rule that its `usage_rights_months` does not exceed the project's `usage_term_months`. Because the creative brief text is validated when the project is created, the lock does not revisit it. Locking the scope increments the project's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, and it makes the scope commercially binding: from `SCOPED` onwards — that is, whenever the project's `status` is anything other than `DRAFT` — registering a further deliverable raises a change order with `trigger_reason` `DELIVERABLE_ADDED_AFTER_SCOPE_LOCK`, as described in §3.9.

### 3.2. The Budget Top Sheet and the Client's Sign-Off

A `LineProducer` builds the budget one line at a time, and each line rolls up to the top sheet under a `category` of `CREW`, `EQUIPMENT`, `LOCATION`, `TALENT` or `POST`. Every line names its project with a `project_id`, carries a `description` as it will read on the top sheet (non-blank, 1 to 200 characters), a billable `quantity` in days, weeks or each — a number strictly greater than 0 — and a `unit_rate_cents` of at least 0. The production company's uplift is `markup_pct`, an optional number that defaults to 0 and may not be less than 0 or greater than 0.35; the ceiling of 35 percent is the maximum uplift the company applies to any single line.

The line's total, `extended_cents`, is derived in a fixed order. First the raw amount is the `quantity` multiplied by the `unit_rate_cents`, kept exact with no rounding. Then the markup amount is that raw amount multiplied by `markup_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents. Finally `extended_cents` is the exact raw amount plus the rounded markup amount. A project roll-up adds the already-rounded `extended_cents` of each included line, per the pinned order in §2.6.

Where a new line lands depends on how far the project has travelled. A line created while the project is `DRAFT` or `SCOPED` starts at `status` `PLANNED` and joins the provisional figure only. A line created while the project is `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST` is auto-committed in the very same request: it is written directly as `COMMITTED`, and the project's `committed_cost_cents` increases by that line's `extended_cents` immediately, alongside the provisional increase. Either way, the fiscal allowance of §2.8 is the last thing evaluated before the line is persisted.

The client's sign-off is the `ClientApprover`'s move and advances the project `SCOPED → IN_PRODUCTION`. It is permitted only from `SCOPED`, requires the project to carry at least one budget line, and enforces a strict ceiling with no tolerance whatsoever: the project's `provisional_cost_cents` at that instant must be no greater than its `budget_ceiling_cents`. The request carries `approved_budget_cents`; the value the server stores in `approved_budget_cents` is the project's `provisional_cost_cents` evaluated at the instant of approval, which becomes the baseline against which variance is measured from then on. Sign-off then cascades down: every budget line of the project still in `PLANNED` — each one under a project that is `SCOPED` at that moment — transitions `PLANNED → COMMITTED`. Caused by that line cascade, `committed_cost_cents` is recomputed and now includes those lines, and `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed, which necessarily reads 0 at this instant because the baseline was just snapshotted from the same provisional figure. The project's `version` increments. From this point the fiscal allowance widens from the bare ceiling to the ceiling grossed up by five percent, so a commitment that would have been refused an hour earlier may now be accepted.

### 3.3. Casting Talent: Buyout Loads and Union Reuse Fees

Casting is the `LineProducer`'s responsibility and is permitted while the project is `DRAFT`, `SCOPED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`. A deal memo names the project through `project_id`, the performer through `talent_name` (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), the performer's `union_affiliation` (`UNION` or `NON_UNION`), the `compensation_model` that will price the engagement (`USAGE_BUYOUT` or `UNION_SESSION_REUSE`), the agreed `session_fee_cents` day rate in whole cents (greater than 0), and `session_days`, the number of shooting days on the memo (an integer from 1 to 30). The two enumerations are not independent: a `UNION` performer may never be bought out, so a `UNION` engagement must use `UNION_SESSION_REUSE` and a `NON_UNION` engagement must use `USAGE_BUYOUT`; any other pairing is refused with `ERR_TALENT_COMP_MODEL_INVALID`. A new engagement is written with `status` `DRAFT` and `comp_basis` `PROVISIONAL`, and — like every other commitment-creating write — it is subject last of all to the fiscal allowance of §2.8.

Compensation is computed twice in an engagement's life, from the same inputs but on two different bases, and the two figures normally differ. Under the `PROVISIONAL` basis, `channel_basis_count` is the number of distinct channels in the project's `channel_scope` and `term_basis_months` is the project's `usage_term_months` — the widest rights the master agreement allows. Under the `FINAL` basis, evaluated at confirmation, `channel_basis_count` is the count of distinct `channel` values across the deliverables actually registered on the project and `term_basis_months` is the largest `usage_rights_months` among those deliverables, regardless of what state each deliverable is in. In both cases `base_fee_cents` is the `session_fee_cents` multiplied by `session_days`.

For a `USAGE_BUYOUT` engagement, the buyout load prices the breadth and the length of the licence. A channel multiplier is selected by the channel count, treating any count above four as four: one channel gives 0.50, two channels 0.85, three channels 1.20, and four or more channels 1.60. A term multiplier is selected by the term in months: 1.00 when the term is 12 months or fewer, 1.35 when it is more than 12 and at most 24, 1.65 when it is more than 24 and at most 36, and 2.00 for any longer term. `usage_load_cents` is then the base fee multiplied by the channel multiplier and by the term multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. `reuse_cycles`, `reuse_fee_cents` and `pension_health_cents` are all 0 for a buyout. The agent's commission, `agent_fee_cents`, is ten percent of the sum of the base fee and the rounded usage load, itself rounded half up. `total_comp_cents` is the base fee plus the rounded usage load plus the rounded agent fee. Worked through: a session fee of 250,000 cents over 2 days against a three-channel scope and a 24-month term gives a base fee of 500,000 cents, a usage load of 810,000 cents, an agent fee of 131,000 cents and a total of 1,441,000 cents; the same row re-priced on the `FINAL` basis when the project's deliverables cover only two distinct channels with a longest usage window of 24 months gives a usage load of 573,750 cents, an agent fee of 107,375 cents and a total of 1,181,125 cents — 259,875 cents lower on identical inputs.

For a `UNION_SESSION_REUSE` engagement, `usage_load_cents` is 0 and the money is in session and reuse. Reuse is metered in thirteen-week cycles: `reuse_cycles` is the term in months multiplied by 30 days per month, divided by 91 days per cycle, rounded up to a whole cycle. The first cycle is covered by the session fee itself, so `reuse_fee_cents` is thirty-five percent of the base fee multiplied by the number of cycles beyond the first — using zero whenever the cycle count is one or fewer — rounded half up. `pension_health_cents`, the union pension and health contribution, is 19.75 percent of the sum of the base fee and the rounded reuse fee, rounded half up. `agent_fee_cents` is ten percent of that same sum of base fee and rounded reuse fee, rounded half up. `total_comp_cents` is the base fee plus the rounded reuse fee plus the rounded pension-and-health contribution plus the rounded agent fee. Worked through with the same session inputs and a 24-month term: 8 reuse cycles, a reuse fee of 1,225,000 cents, a pension and health contribution of 340,688 cents, an agent fee of 172,500 cents and a total of 2,238,188 cents; re-priced on the `FINAL` basis when the longest deliverable usage window is 12 months: 4 cycles, a reuse fee of 525,000 cents, 202,438 cents of pension and health, an agent fee of 102,500 cents and a total of 1,329,938 cents.

Countersigning the deal memo is the `LineProducer`'s move and advances the engagement `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`. It is permitted only from `DRAFT`, requires the project to be `SCOPED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`, and requires the project to carry at least one deliverable — otherwise there is no `FINAL` basis to price against. Confirmation flips `comp_basis` from `PROVISIONAL` to `FINAL` and rewrites `channel_basis_count`, `term_basis_months`, `reuse_cycles` and every money component on the `FINAL` basis. The engagement then enters the committed set: the project's `committed_cost_cents` increases by the `FINAL` `total_comp_cents`, and `provisional_cost_cents` is recomputed with the `FINAL` total replacing the `PROVISIONAL` total for this engagement — which may move the provisional figure in either direction — after which `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed.

A confirmed engagement's last transition is not requested by anyone. When the project reaches delivery, every engagement still `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, and this movement is deliberately ledger-neutral: `total_comp_cents` is untouched and `COMPLETED` sits inside both the provisional and the committed inclusion sets, so neither project figure changes.

### 3.4. Scheduling the Shoot: Locations, Stage Capacity and Shoot Days

Scouted venues are registered by either a `ProductionCoordinator` or a `LineProducer` and are shared across projects. A location carries a `name` and an `address` as they will appear on permits and call sheets (non-blank; up to 120 and 200 characters respectively), a `stage_count` of independently bookable stages or areas (an integer from 1 to 12), a `daily_fee_cents` per shoot day (at least 0), an `insurance_minimum_cents` naming the minimum general-liability coverage the venue demands before any shoot day there may be confirmed (at least 0), and the `permit_authority` — the jurisdiction that issues film permits for that address (non-blank, up to 120 characters). A fresh location reports `scouting_status` `SCOUTED` and `scheduled_shoot_day_count` 0.

Both of those columns are derived and are refreshed whenever the days at that venue change. `scheduled_shoot_day_count` is the number of shoot days at the location whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`. `scouting_status` is `CONFIRMED` when at least one shoot day there is `CONFIRMED`; it is `HOLD` when at least one is `PLANNED` and none is `CONFIRMED`; and it is `SCOUTED` in every other case, including a venue with no days at all and a venue whose only days have wrapped or been cancelled.

Scheduling a shoot day is reserved to a `ProductionCoordinator` and is permitted only while the project is `SCOPED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`. The request names the project with a `project_id` and the venue with a `location_id`, and describes the occupation window: `shoot_date` is the calendar date of the shoot as a UTC `YYYY-MM-DD` string and must equal the UTC date part of `call_time`; `call_time` is the general crew call and opens the occupation interval; `wrap_time` is the planned wrap and closes it, and must be strictly later than `call_time`.

Stage capacity is what makes a venue finite. Before the day is written, the server counts the existing `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` shoot days at the same location whose occupation interval overlaps the requested one, where the interval is treated as **half-open** — it includes its call time and excludes its wrap time. That count must be strictly less than the location's `stage_count`; if it is not, the request is refused with `ERR_LOCATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the interval is half-open, back-to-back days that merely touch at an endpoint — one wrapping exactly when the next is called — do not overlap and do not consume a second stage.

A new shoot day starts at `status` `PLANNED` with `call_sheet_status` `DRAFT`, `call_sheet_published_at` null and `cancellation_reason` null. Its `compliance_posture` is computed immediately and will read `BLOCKED` until both a permit and a certificate of insurance are in force, and its location's `scheduled_shoot_day_count` and `scouting_status` are recomputed on the spot — a first `PLANNED` day moves the venue from `SCOUTED` to `HOLD`.

### 3.5. Permits, Certificates of Insurance and the Compliance Posture

Paperwork is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s domain, and one filing form covers two very different documents. Every filing names the project it is filed under through `project_id`, identifies the issuer through `carrier_or_authority` — the insurer for a certificate of insurance, the permitting authority for a permit (non-blank, up to 120 characters) — carries a `reference_code` holding the policy number or permit application reference (non-blank, up to 64 characters), and declares a coverage interval with `effective_from` (inclusive start) and `effective_to` (inclusive end, which must be strictly later than the start). An optional `fee_cents` records the filing fee or premium, defaults to 0 and may not be negative; it is retained for reference only and is never summed into either project ledger.

What `cert_type` the officer chooses decides which of the remaining fields are mandatory. A `FILM_PERMIT` covers exactly one shooting day and must carry a `shoot_day_id`, and that shoot day must belong to the project named in the filing; a permit that omits the identifier or points at a day of a different project is refused with `ERR_CERT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. A `LIABILITY_COI` covers the whole project and must omit `shoot_day_id` entirely; supplying one is the same violation and the same error. A certificate of insurance must also declare its `coverage_amount_cents`, the general-liability coverage in whole cents, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative but must be strictly greater than 0 for a `LIABILITY_COI` to be a well-formed filing — a `LIABILITY_COI` filed with zero or absent coverage is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `FILM_PERMIT` may leave the coverage amount at its default.

A filing lands at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `decision_note` and `decided_at` null, and while it sits there it satisfies nothing: a submitted permit leaves its shoot day's `compliance_posture` `BLOCKED`. The officer's identity is recorded at filing time so that the later decision can enforce segregation of duties. Recording the authority's or carrier's verdict is also a `ComplianceOfficer` action, must come from a different `X-User-ID` than the filer's (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and is permitted only while the certificate is `SUBMITTED` — `ISSUED` and `DENIED` are terminal, and a second decision is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CERT_STATE_INVALID`. The request carries a `decision` of either issue or deny, plus an optional `decision_note` that is stored verbatim, and the transition — `SUBMITTED → ISSUED` or `SUBMITTED → DENIED` — stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock. Either verdict cascades across aggregates: for a `FILM_PERMIT` the linked shoot day's `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and for a `LIABILITY_COI` the `compliance_posture` of every shoot day of the project is recomputed. A `DENIED` certificate never satisfies a posture gate.

Expiry is derived rather than transitioned. `effective_status` reads `EXPIRED` when the stored `status` is `ISSUED` and the logical clock has passed `effective_to`; in every other case it simply equals the stored `status`, so `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED`, and an `ISSUED` certificate still inside its window all report themselves. No stored transition ever performs that flip, which is why moving the clock forward can silently disarm a day that was compliant an hour ago.

`compliance_posture` is the gate that all of this feeds, and it is recomputed on every shoot-day read, immediately before every confirmation and call-sheet publication, and as a cascade of any certificate decision or crew-booking status change. It is built from three tests. The permit test passes when there exists a certificate whose `shoot_day_id` is this day, whose `cert_type` is `FILM_PERMIT`, whose `status` is `ISSUED`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the day's `call_time`, whose `effective_to` is at or after the day's `wrap_time`, and whose `effective_to` is at or after the logical clock — that last clause being exactly the condition that its `effective_status` has not become `EXPIRED`. The insurance test passes when there exists a certificate on the same `project_id`, of `cert_type` `LIABILITY_COI`, with `status` `ISSUED`, whose `coverage_amount_cents` is at least the location's `insurance_minimum_cents`, whose `effective_from` is at or before `call_time`, whose `effective_to` is at or after `wrap_time`, and whose `effective_to` is at or after the logical clock. The third test counts `confirmed_crew_count`, the crew bookings on the day whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`. The posture is `BLOCKED` whenever either the permit test or the insurance test fails; it is `RESTRICTED` when both pass but fewer than three crew bookings are confirmed; and it is `OPEN` only when both pass and at least three are confirmed. A day with no bookings at all has a confirmed crew count of 0 and is therefore `RESTRICTED`, never `OPEN`.

Locking the day is the `ProductionCoordinator`'s move and advances the shoot day `PLANNED → CONFIRMED`. It requires the day to be `PLANNED`, the project to be `IN_PRODUCTION`, and the posture evaluated on the pre-transition state to be either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` — in other words the permit and insurance tests must both already pass, though the crew need not yet be assembled. A missing, unissued, expired or badly windowed film permit reports `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`; insurance that is missing, unissued, expired, badly windowed or below the venue's `insurance_minimum_cents` reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COI_COVERAGE`. Confirmation recomputes the posture and sets the venue's `scouting_status` to `CONFIRMED`. It deliberately does **not** sweep the day's children: every crew booking keeps whatever `status` it already had, and no equipment reservation is touched.

### 3.6. Crewing and Equipping a Shoot Day

The crew roster is maintained by either a `ProductionCoordinator` or a `LineProducer`. A roster entry carries the `full_name` as booked (non-blank, up to 120 characters), the `department` the person is booked into — one of `CAMERA`, `GRIP_ELECTRIC`, `SOUND`, `ART`, `PRODUCTION` or `POST` — an `hourly_rate_cents` straight-time rate greater than 0, an optional `union_local` designation that is null for non-union crew, and an optional `min_turnaround_hours` giving the contractual rest hours required between the wrap of one booking and the call of the next, which defaults to 10 when omitted and must be an integer from 0 to 24.

Booking a person onto a day is reserved to a `ProductionCoordinator` and requires the day to be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`; a `WRAPPED` or `CANCELLED` day accepts no new bookings and reports `ERR_SHOOT_DAY_STATE_INVALID`. The request names the day with a `shoot_day_id` and the person with a `crew_member_id`, states the `department_role` as it will read on the call sheet (non-blank, up to 80 characters, for example "1st AC"), and gives the person's own `call_time` and `wrap_time`, which need not match the day's and must satisfy wrap strictly after call.

Two scheduling rules protect the individual, and they are checked in this order. First, the person may hold no other `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` booking whose interval intersects the requested one, and here the interval is treated as **closed** — both endpoints included — so a booking that starts at the exact instant another ends counts as an intersection and is refused with `ERR_CREW_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. This is deliberately stricter than the half-open rule that governs stage capacity in §3.4. Second, for every other `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` booking of the same person that does not overlap, the rest gap between the two intervals — the requested call time minus the other booking's wrap time, or the other booking's call time minus the requested wrap time, whichever of the two is the positive quantity — must be at least that person's `min_turnaround_hours`. A gap of exactly the minimum passes; a gap of 8.5 hours against a 10-hour minimum is refused with `ERR_CREW_TURNAROUND_VIOLATION`.

The booking's cost is frozen at creation and reused by every later ledger movement of that booking, so releasing it later credits back exactly what confirming it debited. `scheduled_hours` is the span from `call_time` to `wrap_time` expressed in hours, exact to two decimals. Three components are then formed against the roster entry's `hourly_rate_cents`: `straight_time_cents` is the lesser of the scheduled hours and 10 hours, multiplied by the rate; `overtime_tier1_cents` covers the hours from the tenth to the twelfth — the lesser of two hours and however much of the span exceeds 10 hours, never below zero — multiplied by the rate and by 1.5; and `overtime_tier2_cents` covers everything beyond the twelfth hour — the span minus 12 hours, never below zero — multiplied by the rate and by 2.0. `cost_cents` is the sum of those three components, rounded half up to whole cents exactly once at the end, per §2.6. A new booking is written at `status` `HELD`, which puts its cost inside the provisional figure but not the committed one, and refreshes the day's `day_cost_cents`. The fiscal allowance of §2.8 is evaluated last, after both the double-booking and the turnaround checks.

Confirming a booking is the coordinator's next move and advances it `HELD → CONFIRMED`. It requires the booking to be `HELD` and its shoot day to be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`. The ledger syncs at that moment: the project's `committed_cost_cents` increases by the booking's `cost_cents` and `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed. The day's `confirmed_crew_count` rises by one and, caused by that count change, the day's `compliance_posture` is recomputed — which is how a day sitting at `RESTRICTED` becomes `OPEN` as soon as the third booking is confirmed.

Dropping a booking is the compensating move and advances it `HELD → RELEASED` or `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, optionally recording a `release_reason`. Release is permitted only from `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; a `COMPLETED` or already `RELEASED` booking reports `ERR_CREW_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. Because `RELEASED` sits outside both inclusion sets, the project's `provisional_cost_cents` drops by the booking's `cost_cents`; and when the booking was `CONFIRMED`, the project's `committed_cost_cents` drops by exactly the amount that confirmation credited, leaving no drift. Releasing a booking that was only ever `HELD` moves the committed figure not at all, because a held booking was never inside the committed set. Either way `budget_variance_cents`, the day's `confirmed_crew_count`, its `compliance_posture` and its `day_cost_cents` are recomputed, and the vacated interval stops occupying the person, so a later booking may reuse it.

Equipment is reserved by a `ProductionCoordinator` against a `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` day. A reservation names the day with a `shoot_day_id`, the `vendor_name` supplying the kit and the `kit_label` describing it (each non-blank, up to 120 characters, for example "A-cam package + 4 primes"), the `unit_count` of identical kits (an integer from 1 to 50), the `daily_rate_cents` per kit per rental day (greater than 0), and the `pickup_at` and `return_at` instants. The rental must sit inside the prep and return windows around the day: `pickup_at` may be no earlier than the day's `call_time` minus 48 hours, and `return_at` must be later than `pickup_at` and no later than the day's `wrap_time` plus 48 hours. A pickup or return outside those two days of slack is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE`. `rental_days` is the span from pickup to return in hours divided by 24 and rounded up to a whole day, with a floor of one day, so any partial day bills as a full day and a same-morning pickup and return still bills one. `total_cost_cents` is the `unit_count` multiplied by the `daily_rate_cents` and by `rental_days`. A reservation is written at `status` `RESERVED`, and because `RESERVED` sits inside *both* inclusion sets, the reservation lands on the provisional **and** the committed figure in the same request; the day's `day_cost_cents` is refreshed, and the fiscal allowance is evaluated last.

A day's `day_cost_cents` is a display-only total, recomputed on every shoot-day read and after any booking or reservation status change on that day: it is the venue's `daily_fee_cents` plus the `cost_cents` of every crew booking on the day whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` plus the `total_cost_cents` of every reservation on the day whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `RETURNED`. It is never added into either project figure — the venue's fee reaches the project ledger only through a budget line of category `LOCATION`.

### 3.7. Running the Day: Call Sheets, Wrap and Cancellation

Publishing the call sheet is the coordinator's signal that the day is real, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the physical production. The day must already be `CONFIRMED`; a `PLANNED`, `WRAPPED` or `CANCELLED` day reports `ERR_SHOOT_DAY_STATE_INVALID`. Then four production checks run in the order fixed by §2.12. The film-permit test and the insurance test are re-run exactly as they were at confirmation, reporting `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH` and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COI_COVERAGE` respectively. The posture is re-evaluated live immediately before publishing, and if it now reads `BLOCKED` the publish is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` — which is precisely what happens when a certificate that was in force at confirmation has since expired against the logical clock. The day must carry at least three crew bookings in `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, counted as `confirmed_crew_count`; fewer, including the empty case counted as zero, is refused with `ERR_MIN_CREW_NOT_MET`. Finally the lead-time rule: a call sheet may only go out while the logical clock is at or before the day's `call_time` minus twelve hours, so publishing inside the last half-day before call is refused with `ERR_CALL_SHEET_WINDOW_CLOSED`. When all of it passes, `call_sheet_status` advances `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and `call_sheet_published_at` is stamped with the logical clock.

Wrapping the day is also the coordinator's move and advances the shoot day `CONFIRMED → WRAPPED`. It requires the day to be `CONFIRMED`, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the day's `wrap_time`, and re-evaluates the posture, refusing with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` if it reads `BLOCKED` at that instant. Wrap then cascades down through the day's children. Every crew booking still `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, and the committed figure does not move because `COMPLETED` stays inside the committed inclusion set. Every crew booking still `HELD` moves `HELD → RELEASED` — never worked, never billed — so it leaves the provisional set and the project's `provisional_cost_cents` drops by that booking's `cost_cents` while the committed figure is untouched, since a held booking was never committed. Every equipment reservation still `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → RETURNED`, and neither figure moves because `RETURNED` stays inside both inclusion sets. The day's `day_cost_cents` and the project's ledger figures are recomputed. Finally the day bubbles up: when no shoot day of the project remains in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` and at least one shoot day exists, and the project is `IN_PRODUCTION`, the project advances `IN_PRODUCTION → IN_POST`, incrementing its `version` and refreshing its `updated_at`.

Cancelling a day may be done by either a `ProductionCoordinator` or a `LineProducer`, is permitted from `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, requires a `cancellation_reason` to be recorded on the day, and unwinds the day completely. The cascade runs in hops. Hop one: every crew booking of the day whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`. Hop two, caused by hop one: each booking that left `CONFIRMED` debits the project's `committed_cost_cents` by its own `cost_cents`, while bookings released out of `HELD` debit the committed figure nothing; every released booking, from either state, leaves the provisional set, so `provisional_cost_cents` drops by its `cost_cents`. Hop three, caused by hop two: `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed as the provisional figure minus `approved_budget_cents`. In parallel, every equipment reservation of the day whose `status` is `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`, each one debiting *both* `committed_cost_cents` and `provisional_cost_cents` by its `total_cost_cents`, because a reservation had been inside both sets. Also in parallel, the venue's `scheduled_shoot_day_count` and `scouting_status` are recomputed — a cancelled day no longer holds a stage, and a venue whose only confirmed day was just cancelled falls back to `HOLD` or `SCOUTED`. And exactly as with wrap, when no shoot day of the project remains in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` and at least one exists, the project bubbles `IN_PRODUCTION → IN_POST`. The cascade from `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` is identical in every respect to the cascade from `PLANNED → CANCELLED`; the only difference is how much of the committed figure there is to unwind.

### 3.8. Post-Production: Cuts, Technical QC and the Client Review Loop

Cuts are uploaded by a `PostSupervisor`, and each upload is a new row rather than an edit of an old one. The request names its parent with a `deliverable_id`, carries the mandatory `expected_deliverable_version` described in §2.10, gives an editorial `cut_label` such as "RC2 client cut" (non-blank, 1 to 80 characters), and declares what was actually delivered: the `aspect_ratio` of this cut, its `duration_seconds` (an integer greater than 0 and at most 3600), whether a caption asset accompanies it in `has_captions` (optional, defaulting to false), the `caption_format` of that asset (optional, defaulting to `NONE`, drawn from the same four values as a deliverable's caption requirement), and the master `file_size_gb` destined for the archive package (optional, defaulting to 0, at least 0). An optional `supersedes_version_id` retires the cut this one replaces.

Uploading is permitted only against a deliverable in `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; an `APPROVED` or `HANDED_OVER` deliverable accepts no further cuts and reports `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. When `supersedes_version_id` is present it must reference a cut of the *same* deliverable, and that cut's `review_status` must be `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; anything else — a cut of a different deliverable, or a cut in the wrong review state — is refused with `ERR_VERSION_DELIVERABLE_MISMATCH`. The retirement itself is a system cascade admitting `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` and `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUPERSEDED` on the referenced row, incrementing that row's `version` and refreshing its `updated_at`; because the referential check requires a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` target, that is the edge reachable through this API. A cut whose `review_status` is `SUBMITTED` may never be superseded, since the client still owes a decision on it.

An accepted upload increments the parent deliverable's `version` by one — which is what the next uploader must present as `expected_deliverable_version` — and writes the new row with `review_status` `DRAFT`, `technical_status` `PENDING_QC`, `qc_failure_reasons` as an empty array, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `decided_at` all null. Its `round_index` is set to one more than the parent deliverable's `consumed_review_rounds` at that moment, which is the round this cut is competing for. The deliverable's `version_count` is recomputed as the number of cuts carrying its identifier.

Every cut carries two independent state axes on the same row, and this separation matters: `review_status` tracks the client conversation (`DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `SUPERSEDED`) while `technical_status` tracks the technical conversation (`PENDING_QC`, `QC_PASSED`, `QC_FAILED`). Neither axis's transitions touch the other's value.

Technical QC is recorded by a `PostSupervisor` and the verdict is derived from the cut against its parent's spec, never supplied by the caller. Exactly one QC record is permitted per cut: the `technical_status` must still be `PENDING_QC`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_QC_ALREADY_RECORDED`. Four findings are evaluated and appended in a fixed order. `ASPECT_RATIO_MISMATCH` is recorded when the cut's `aspect_ratio` differs from the deliverable's `aspect_ratio`. `DURATION_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` is recorded when the absolute difference between the cut's `duration_seconds` and the deliverable's `target_duration_seconds` is greater than one second — a difference of exactly one second passes, a difference of two seconds fails. `CAPTION_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` is recorded when the deliverable's `caption_requirement` is anything other than `NONE` and either the cut's `has_captions` is false or its `caption_format` differs from that requirement. `UNREQUESTED_CAPTION_ASSET` is recorded when the deliverable's `caption_requirement` is `NONE` and yet the cut's `has_captions` is true. If the findings list is empty the cut advances `PENDING_QC → QC_PASSED` and `qc_failure_reasons` is written as an empty array; otherwise the cut advances `PENDING_QC → QC_FAILED` and `qc_failure_reasons` holds the findings in the order above. A failing QC is a recorded outcome and a successful call, not a request error — the response is a 200 carrying the failed cut. Because the `review_status` axis is untouched, a `QC_FAILED` cut simply can never be submitted; the only way forward is a new cut.

Sending a cut to the client is the supervisor's last move on it and advances the cut `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Six conditions must hold: the cut's `review_status` is `DRAFT`; its `technical_status` is `QC_PASSED` — the cross-axis guard, with both `PENDING_QC` and `QC_FAILED` refused as `ERR_VERSION_QC_NOT_PASSED`; the parent deliverable is `PENDING` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; the project is `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`; the project's `review_gate` is `OPEN`, or the submission is frozen with `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING`; and the deliverable's `consumed_review_rounds` is strictly less than its `round_entitlement`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Submission stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `submitted_at` with the logical clock, then bubbles up: the deliverable advances `PENDING → IN_REVIEW` on a first cut, or `CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` on a later one, incrementing the deliverable's `version` and refreshing its `updated_at`. That second edge is what closes the review cycle `PENDING → IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW`, and it carries the same entitlement precondition — the deliverable's `consumed_review_rounds` must still be strictly below its `round_entitlement` — so the number of laps the loop can run is bounded by the entitlement, which only an approved change order can raise.

The client's verdict is recorded by a `ClientApprover` and carries a `decision` of either approve or request-changes plus an optional `review_note`. It requires the cut's `review_status` to be `SUBMITTED`, requires the project to be `IN_PRODUCTION` or `IN_POST`, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the cut's `submitted_by_user_id` — a supervisor may not sign off their own cut, and an attempt reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either verdict stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` with the logical clock.

An approval advances the cut `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and bubbles up in two hops. First the deliverable advances `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`. Then, if that leaves every deliverable of the project in `APPROVED` and the project is `IN_POST`, the project advances `IN_POST → DELIVERED`, which in turn completes every `CONFIRMED` talent engagement as described in §3.3.

A request for changes advances the cut `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and sets off a four-hop cascade that is the single most consequential chain in the system. Hop one: the deliverable advances `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and its `consumed_review_rounds` increases by one. Hop two, caused by hop one rather than directly by the request: if the incremented `consumed_review_rounds` has reached or passed the deliverable's `round_entitlement` — the entitlement is now spent — a change order row is created against that deliverable with `trigger_reason` `REVISION_ROUND_EXCEEDED`, `additional_rounds_granted` 1, `status` `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `requested_by_user_id` set to the reviewing `X-User-ID`, and every money column priced as described in §3.9. Hop three, caused by hop two: the new `PENDING_APPROVAL` change order enters the provisional inclusion set, so the project's `provisional_cost_cents` increases by that change order's `amount_cents`, and the project's `review_gate` flips `OPEN → CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING`, which blocks every further cut submission across the *whole* project, not merely on the deliverable that triggered it. Hop four, caused by hop three: `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed as `provisional_cost_cents` minus `approved_budget_cents`, and `round_entitlement_balance` is recomputed.

The project-wide round balance is a conserved quantity that makes the entitlement economy auditable. `round_entitlement_balance` is the project's `contracted_revision_rounds`, plus the `additional_rounds_granted` of every change order on any deliverable of the project whose `status` is `APPROVED`, minus the `consumed_review_rounds` of every deliverable of the project. Change orders in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing to it. Per deliverable the same arithmetic is expressed as two columns: `granted_extra_rounds` is the sum of `additional_rounds_granted` across that deliverable's `APPROVED` change orders, and `round_entitlement` is the project's `contracted_revision_rounds` plus that sum. The balance may go negative, but only when a void withdraws rounds that were already consumed; the invariant tracks that honestly rather than clamping it.

### 3.9. Change Orders and the Economics of Extra Rounds

A change order is the commercial instrument that turns a scope or entitlement breach into a priced, client-approvable line. The system raises it; no client of this API creates one directly. There are exactly two reasons, recorded on `trigger_reason`. `REVISION_ROUND_EXCEEDED` is the cascade described in §3.8, raised when a request for changes exhausts a deliverable's round entitlement, and it grants `additional_rounds_granted` of 1. `DELIVERABLE_ADDED_AFTER_SCOPE_LOCK` is raised when a deliverable is registered while the project's `status` is anything other than `DRAFT`, and it grants `additional_rounds_granted` of 0 — the client is buying a new deliverable, not another lap of an existing review. In both cases the order is written with `status` `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `approved_by_user_id` null, and `requested_by_user_id` set to the `X-User-ID` whose action spawned it; that user may not later decide it. In both cases the order's `amount_cents` immediately enters the project's `provisional_cost_cents` and flips the project's `review_gate` to `CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING`.

Pricing happens once, at creation, and is never recomputed afterwards even if the clock or the deliverable moves. It runs in a pinned order. `labor_cents`, the post-production labour component, is 480,000 cents (4,800.00 in currency units) for every round granted when the trigger is `REVISION_ROUND_EXCEEDED` — so 480,000 cents for the single round such an order grants — and a flat 620,000 cents when the trigger is `DELIVERABLE_ADDED_AFTER_SCOPE_LOCK`, where no rounds are granted at all. Next the rush tier: the number of whole days from the logical clock to the deliverable's `air_date` is computed by taking the difference in seconds, dividing by 86,400 seconds per day and discarding any fraction, and that day count may legitimately be negative when the air date has already passed. `rush_pct` is 0.25 when the day count is 7 or fewer, 0.15 when it is more than 7 and 14 or fewer, and 0.00 for anything further out. `rush_cents` is `labor_cents` multiplied by `rush_pct`, rounded half up. `subtotal_cents` is `labor_cents` plus `rush_cents`.

The producer's fee tier is keyed on `subtotal_cents` — the pre-fee figure — and never on the invoiced total, even though the invoiced total is what the client pays. `producer_fee_pct` is 0.12 when `subtotal_cents` is at least 1,000,000 cents, 0.15 when it is at least 500,000 but below 1,000,000, and 0.18 below 500,000. `producer_fee_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `producer_fee_pct`, rounded half up, and `amount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `producer_fee_cents`. Consider an extra-round order with no rush: labour of 480,000 cents and a rush percentage of 0.00 give a subtotal of 480,000 cents, which sits in the below-500,000 band and therefore attracts 18 percent, a producer fee of 86,400 cents and an amount of 566,400 cents. Keying the tier on the 566,400-cent total instead would have selected the at-least-500,000 band, produced a fee of 72,000 cents and an amount of 552,000 cents — a 14,400-cent divergence on identical inputs, which is exactly why the order of evaluation is fixed.

Deciding a change order is shared between the `ClientApprover` and the `LineProducer`, and the request carries a `decision` of approve, reject or void plus an optional `decision_note`. Approve and reject are permitted only from `PENDING_APPROVAL`; void is permitted only from `APPROVED`; anything else reports `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. In all three cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`, and the decision records `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`.

Approval advances `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and releases both the round and the money. The deliverable's `granted_extra_rounds` increases by the order's `additional_rounds_granted`, so its `round_entitlement` rises by the same amount; caused by that entitlement rise, the project's `round_entitlement_balance` increases by the same amount. The project's `committed_cost_cents` increases by `amount_cents`, because `APPROVED` enters the committed inclusion set, while `provisional_cost_cents` is unchanged — the row was already inside the provisional set as `PENDING_APPROVAL`. The project's `review_gate` returns to `OPEN` once no `PENDING_APPROVAL` change order remains anywhere on the project, which is what unblocks cut submission.

Rejection advances `PENDING_APPROVAL → REJECTED` and does the opposite: the project's `provisional_cost_cents` decreases by `amount_cents` as the row leaves the provisional set, `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed, and the `review_gate` returns to `OPEN` once no `PENDING_APPROVAL` order remains. No extra rounds are granted, so a deliverable sitting at its entitlement stays blocked from further submissions — the client declined to pay for another lap.

Voiding advances `APPROVED → VOIDED` and is the exact compensating pair for approval, which is what keeps the round-entitlement invariant honest. The deliverable's `granted_extra_rounds` decreases by `additional_rounds_granted` and its `round_entitlement` falls by the same amount; caused by that fall, the project's `round_entitlement_balance` decreases by the same amount, restoring the value it held before the approval. Both `committed_cost_cents` and `provisional_cost_cents` are debited by `amount_cents`, and `budget_variance_cents` is recomputed. Because a cascade-created order is not announced anywhere else, the change-order listing for a deliverable — filterable by `status` and by `trigger_reason` — is the only channel through which such an order can be discovered.

### 3.10. Delivery, Archive Handover and Raw-Footage Retention

Delivery is never requested; it is the consequence of the last approval, as described in §3.8. Handover is the opposite: it is a deliberate `LineProducer` action that advances the project `DELIVERED → ARCHIVED`. It requires the project to be `DELIVERED` — any other status reports `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires `handover_at` to still be null, expressed operationally as `archive_status` still reading `NOT_PACKAGED`, which is the check that reports `ERR_ARCHIVE_ALREADY_HANDED_OVER`. Because the handover itself advances the project `DELIVERED → ARCHIVED`, a second handover on the same project no longer satisfies the `DELIVERED` precondition, and the precedence of §2.12 — priority 17 ahead of priority 27 — therefore refuses it with `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The request names the client-side custodian in `handover_recipient`, which is stored on the project alongside `handover_at`, stamped from the logical clock.

The retention clock starts at that instant. `retention_expires_at` is `handover_at` plus the project's `raw_footage_retention_months` multiplied by 30 days, using whole days added to the handover instant; calendar month lengths are deliberately not used, so a 12-month retention term is exactly 360 days. `archive_status` is derived from those two columns: it reads `NOT_PACKAGED` while `handover_at` is null, `RETENTION_EXPIRED` once the logical clock has passed `retention_expires_at`, and `HANDED_OVER` in every other case. The declared `raw_footage_terabytes` volume travels with the package as the retention obligation's subject, alongside the `file_size_gb` of each master cut. Handover also cascades down: every deliverable of the project whose `status` is `APPROVED` advances `APPROVED → HANDED_OVER`, each such row incrementing its `version` and refreshing its `updated_at`, and the project's own `version` increments as well.

Reporting surfaces exist so that operators can reconstruct any of this without guessing. A project read returns its live ledger figures, `budget_variance_cents`, `round_entitlement_balance`, `review_gate`, `archive_status` and `retention_expires_at`. A project's budget lines can be filtered by `category` and `status`; its deliverables by `status` and `channel`; its talent engagements by `status` and `compensation_model`. A deliverable read returns `consumed_review_rounds`, `granted_extra_rounds`, `round_entitlement` and `version_count`. The cut listing accepts `deliverable_id`, `supersedes_version_id`, `review_status` and `technical_status` filters, which is how the self-referential supersede chain is walked. Shoot days can be filtered by `project_id`, `location_id` and `status`; a day's equipment reservations by `status`; certificates by `project_id`, `shoot_day_id`, `cert_type` and `status`; crew bookings by `shoot_day_id`, `crew_member_id` and `status`. Every one of these listings honours the `limit` and `offset` parameters of §2.3.
