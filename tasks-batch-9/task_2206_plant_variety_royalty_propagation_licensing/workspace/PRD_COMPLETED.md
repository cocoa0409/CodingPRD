# Plant Variety Royalty & Propagation Licensing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A plant breeding programme earns nothing from the germplasm it creates until three separate machines are turning at once. The first is legal: plant patents and plant variety certificates must be applied for jurisdiction by jurisdiction, granted, and then kept alive through a maintenance calendar of annuities that lapse without mercy when a grace window closes. The second is commercial: licensed propagators must be brought under agreement, given production quotas per variety and per season, issued traceability tags that let the licensor verify what was actually grown, and billed a royalty per plant or per acre that depends on their certified quality tier and on where the plants are destined. The third is defensive: market surveys and genetic marker testing must catch unauthorized propagation, contractual audit rights must be exercised against filed royalty reports, and the brand under which a variety is sold must be managed as a trademark asset entirely separate from the variety's denomination of record.

This API is the system of record for all three. It is deliberately built as a graph rather than a tree: varieties, trial sites and propagators are peer roots, and the junction records that join them — production quotas, trials, sublicense grants, royalty line items, audit engagements and surveillance cases — are discovered by filtering lists on the foreign keys they carry rather than by walking a deep URL hierarchy. That shape matters because almost every number in the system is derived across aggregate boundaries. A single royalty line's amount depends on the quality tier recorded on an agreement, the territory class recorded on a quota, the tag activations reconciled against that quota, and the protection posture of a variety that is itself derived from filings living in a third aggregate entirely.

Two properties are non-negotiable for the engineering team. First, **determinism**: time is a server-held logical clock that only moves forward under explicit instruction, every sweep it performs runs in a pinned order, and no computation may consult wall-clock time. Second, **conservation**: quota units, royalty balances and recovered damages are all recomputed by full re-aggregation rather than by incremental adjustment, so that compensating actions — voiding an issued tag order, reversing a settlement, revoking a grant — return the ledgers to exactly the values they held before, with no accumulated drift.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply across every endpoint in the API. Where a rule is stated here in general terms, §3 supplies the business narrative that determines when it fires.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails immediately with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); no statement about the role's permissions has been made at that point. Once both headers are present, the server checks that `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles **and** that the role is authorized for the endpoint being called. A role that is not declared, or is declared but not authorized for that endpoint, is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). This check runs before the request body is parsed, so an unauthorized caller never learns whether its payload was well formed.

The five roles are `BreederAdmin`, `LicensingOfficer`, `Propagator`, `TrialCoordinator` and `ComplianceAuditor`.

- The `BreederAdmin` is the breeding programme's rights office. It owns the variety record itself, the whole protection portfolio, the trademark dimension, trial certification, and commercial release and retirement. It holds no billing authority at all and can never accept or settle a royalty report.
- The `LicensingOfficer` is the licensing and billing desk: propagator onboarding, agreements and their suspension lifecycle, quota allocation, tag issuance and voiding, royalty acceptance, dispute, settlement and reversal, and sublicensing.
- The `Propagator` role acts for a licensed nursery on its own behalf: requesting and reconciling tags, and opening, amending and submitting its own royalty reports. It can neither issue nor void tags, and it can never accept, dispute, settle or reverse a report.
- The `TrialCoordinator` runs the regional trialing network — trial sites, the trial calendar, planting and evaluation scoring. Certification, the decision that a trial counts toward release, is reserved to `BreederAdmin`, so scoring and certifying are deliberately separated.
- The `ComplianceAuditor` is the enforcement and audit function: market-survey surveillance cases, genetic testing, case resolution with damages allocation, and royalty audit engagements. It can issue findings that dispute an accepted report and suspend an agreement, but it can never accept, settle or reverse a report, nor issue tags.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `Propagator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Propagator` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Propagator` compare the propagator reached from that resource to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for a royalty report or one of its line items the comparison is against the `propagator_id` recorded on the report's agreement, and for a tag order it is against the `propagator_id` on the agreement of the tag order's quota. When the acting propagator is not the one reached that way, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) — another propagator's resources are indistinguishable from forbidden ones, never from missing ones. For the other four roles — `BreederAdmin`, `LicensingOfficer`, `TrialCoordinator` and `ComplianceAuditor` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a business rule requires an audit trail (as `countersigned_by_user_id` and later `accepted_by_user_id` or `settled_by_user_id`, and as `finalized_by_user_id` on an audit engagement) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, rendered from the logical clock of §2.5 and never from wall-clock time — so every response produced while the clock holds one instant carries that same timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. A value that is not an integer, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an empty-string value for either parameter is read as if the parameter had been omitted.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, which stands still between explicit advances, records routinely share an identical `created_at`; ties are broken by creation order, never by `id`.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page.

Each list endpoint additionally accepts a set of equality filters drawn from the fields of the resource it returns; those filters are named in the workflow narratives of §3 and again, endpoint by endpoint, in the descriptions of §4. A filter value is compared as the raw string it arrived as against the stored field, an empty-string filter value is read as absent, and any query parameter that is neither `limit`, `offset` nor a declared filter of that endpoint is ignored rather than rejected. The one non-string filter is `overrun_flagged`, which accepts `true`/`1`/`yes` and `false`/`0`/`no` case-insensitively and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource in this system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Fields the server keeps purely for internal bookkeeping — those whose names begin with an underscore, such as the creation-order sequence every record carries and the pre-resolution conclusion a surveillance case remembers — are stripped out of every response body and are never observable.

Almost every resource also carries three system-managed audit fields:

- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the record is first written and never changed afterwards.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

A recomputation of a derived field is a mutation. When accepting a royalty report re-derives a propagator's royalty ledger, the propagator's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though no client sent a payload naming that propagator. The same holds for a variety whose protection posture or sublicense count is re-derived, a quota whose tag inventory is re-derived, and a trial site whose occupancy is re-derived — subject always to the qualification of §2.9 that a re-derivation which leaves every derived value exactly as it found it is not a mutation at all, unless the workflow that owns it pins the increment outright.

There is exactly one exception. A `GeneticTest` is an immutable laboratory record: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no endpoint that modifies it, and nothing in the system ever mutates one after creation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Deterministic Time Sweep

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All temporal reasoning — lateness, grace windows, trial calendars, expiry — is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock** whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The clock is advanced only by an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant. That instant must be strictly later than the clock's present value; an equal or earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), which guarantees that a sweep already performed is never replayed.

Advancing the clock also runs a deterministic sweep over three classes of overdue record, in this pinned order:

1. Protection filings sitting in `MAINTENANCE_DUE` whose grace window has closed move to `LAPSED`. Within this class, filings are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `jurisdiction_code` ascending.
2. Varieties whose `brand_status` is `BRAND_REGISTERED` and whose renewal instant has arrived move to `BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE`. Within this class, varieties are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `denomination` ascending.
3. Sublicense grants that are `ACTIVE` and past their expiry move to `EXPIRED`. Within this class, grants are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `territory_code` ascending, then by `sublicensee_name` ascending.

After all three classes have been swept, every propagator's rolling compliance score is recomputed, because the rolling window has slid — propagators are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `legal_name` ascending. No ordering anywhere in the sweep is ever decided by `id`. Each class drives its own cascades as it runs: a lapse revokes that jurisdiction's active sublicense grants and re-derives the variety's protection posture, protected jurisdiction list and active sublicense count, and a compliance recomputation can auto-suspend active agreements and freeze their active quotas.

The clock capability answers with the resulting `current_utc` — the instant the clock now holds, rendered like the server-stamped timestamps of §2.4 as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits — plus four counters describing what the sweep did: `lapsed_filing_count`, `brand_renewal_due_count`, `expired_grant_count` and `compliance_recompute_count`. The first three count only the records that class actually moved; `compliance_recompute_count` instead counts every propagator the sweep visited, because every propagator is recomputed on every advance whether or not its scores change. Those five keys are the entire `data` object of the response, which is an `object` envelope with HTTP 200 and carries no record of its own. Every entity the sweep mutates has its `version` incremented in the ordinary way.

All five roles may advance the clock, and all five roles may read every list and fetch endpoint in the API.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is always an integer number of whole cents. There are no fractional-cent balances and no decimal money fields. Rates, scores and indices are the only decimal quantities, and each has a fixed scale. Every decimal quantity is emitted in JSON as a bare number rather than a string, so the trailing zeros of its fixed scale are not preserved on the wire — an `effective_rate_cents` of 0.0000 reads back as `0.0` and a `regional_performance_index` of 0.00 reads back as `0.0` — and each is therefore to be compared numerically. The scales are:

- Trial scores and indices — `performance_index` and `regional_performance_index` — carry **2 decimal places**, rounded **half up**.
- `propagation_pass_rate` and `match_confidence` are fractions between 0 and 1 carrying **at most 4 decimal places**: a value supplied with fewer is normalized up to 4 and stored at that scale, and one supplied with more is rejected in the body tier. They are inputs and are otherwise never rounded.
- `effective_rate_cents` is a per-unit royalty rate carrying **4 decimal places**, rounded **half up**. It is the one place in the system where a monetary rate is not an integer; the amounts it produces are integers.
- `compliance_score` and `lifetime_compliance_score` are integers clamped to the range 0 through 100 inclusive. No rounding is involved.
- Unit quantities — plots, plants, acres, tags, reported units — are non-negative integers.

Three rounding-order rules are pinned and observable, and getting them wrong produces answers that differ by a cent or by a hundredth:

1. **Component-then-sum for trial scoring.** Each weighted evaluation component is rounded to 2 decimal places on its own *before* the four are added together. Adding unrounded components and rounding the sum can differ by 0.01.
2. **Per-line-then-sum for royalty totals.** Each line's money amount is rounded to whole cents individually, and the report total is the sum of those already-rounded integers. Summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different — and wrong — answer.
3. **Step-by-step rounding for annuities.** The late surcharge and the volume credit are each rounded to whole cents independently before entering the next step of the annuity calculation.

Derived money and quantity fields are always recomputed by full re-aggregation over their source rows, never adjusted by delta. That is what makes the compensating actions in this system exact: voiding an issued tag order restores a quota's available units to precisely their pre-issue value, and reversing a settlement restores a propagator's payable balance to precisely its pre-settlement value, no matter how many issue/void or settle/reverse laps have been run.

### 2.7. Stale-View Rejection on Repeatable Child Writes

Three creation operations in this system are legitimately repeatable against the same parent — a quota may carry many tag orders, a report may carry many line items, an agreement may carry many sublicense grants — and in all three cases the parent's own state determines whether the new child is sound. Because no state precondition would otherwise reject the second create, these three operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent's `version`.

- Requesting a tag order requires `expected_quota_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductionQuota`. A quota's `version` advances on every tag issuance, void, freeze, thaw and closure, so a client that read `units_available` before any of those and then requests tags is working from a stale view of the remaining authorization. If `expected_quota_version` does not equal the quota's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_QUOTA_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Adding a royalty line item requires `expected_report_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RoyaltyReport`. A report's `version` advances when another line is added, when it is submitted, and when a revision resets its totals, so a stale view means the client is appending to a report whose provisional total it can no longer predict. A mismatch against the report's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Proposing a sublicense grant requires `expected_agreement_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PropagatorAgreement`. The agreement's `version` advances on countersign, suspend, reinstate and terminate, each of which changes whether and where a sublicense may be granted. A mismatch against the agreement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

All three fields are required, not optional: omitting one — or supplying it as an explicit `null` — is a malformed body, and so is a value that is negative, fractional or not a number at all. No other endpoint in the API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Note that "no state precondition would otherwise reject the second create" is true of the quota and the agreement but not quite of the report: adding a line item additionally requires the report to be in `review_status` `DRAFT`, a check that sits *after* the stale-view guard so the guard still decides first.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

When a URL carries a templated identifier, the server resolves it before doing anything else, and it resolves identifiers **strictly left to right** so that the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides the error. Each entity has its own 404 code: `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND` for a `variety_id`, `ERR_PROTECTION_FILING_NOT_FOUND` for a `filing_id`, `ERR_TRIAL_SITE_NOT_FOUND` for a `trial_site_id`, `ERR_VARIETY_TRIAL_NOT_FOUND` for a `trial_id`, `ERR_PROPAGATOR_NOT_FOUND` for a `propagator_id` or a `suspected_propagator_id`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an `agreement_id`, `ERR_QUOTA_NOT_FOUND` for a `quota_id`, `ERR_TAG_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for a `tag_order_id`, `ERR_ROYALTY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` for a `report_id` or a `royalty_report_id`, `ERR_LINE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for a `line_item_id`, `ERR_SUBLICENSE_GRANT_NOT_FOUND` for a `grant_id`, `ERR_SURVEILLANCE_CASE_NOT_FOUND` for a `case_id`, `ERR_GENETIC_TEST_NOT_FOUND` for a `test_id`, and `ERR_AUDIT_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an `engagement_id`. Every one of these carries HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in the request **body** are resolved next, using the same per-entity 404 codes, and they are resolved in the order the fields are listed for that operation. An unknown `variety_id` on a quota creation therefore yields `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND`, while an unknown `quota_id` on a line-item creation yields `ERR_QUOTA_NOT_FOUND`.

The full order in which a request is judged is fixed, and it is the order that decides which of several simultaneously-true failures the client is told about:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role declaration and endpoint authorization — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Body shape and field-level constraints — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The body must parse, must carry every required field, must carry no unknown field, and every value must satisfy its declared constraints: enum values must be declared members, integer quantities must be at least zero (or at least one where the field says so), decimal fields must carry no more precision than declared, and timestamps must be ISO 8601. A field present with an explicit `null` counts as omitted, so a required field set to `null` is rejected and an optional one falls back to its stated default. Integer fields reject booleans and any JSON number carrying a fractional part; decimal fields accept either a JSON number or a numeric string and are normalized to their declared scale. Code fields the workflows describe as uppercase — `breeder_code`, `jurisdiction_code`, `region_code` and `lab_code` — admit uppercase letters and digits, and territory codes admit uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen. A body that is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON but is not an object, is rejected here as well, while an entirely absent body is read as an empty object. This check happens before any database lookup and applies to every operation that takes a body.
4. Path identifier resolution, left to right, with the per-entity 404 codes above.
5. Body foreign-key resolution, in field order, with the same 404 codes.
6. Propagator ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. The optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_QUOTA_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION`. Placing it here, after existence and ownership but before any state or business check, means a client working from a stale view learns that fact rather than receiving a business verdict computed against data it has never seen.
8. Terminal-state rejection — `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` (HTTP 409). Before the generic transition check, the server asks whether the target is already in a terminal state of the machine this action drives, so that a terminal resource yields the specific terminal code. The terminal states are: a `RETIRED` variety lifecycle; a `BRAND_ABANDONED` brand; a filing that is `LAPSED` or `ABANDONED`; a trial that is `PASSED` or `FAILED`; a `TERMINATED` agreement; a `CLOSED` quota; a tag order that is `RECONCILED` or `VOIDED`; a report whose settlement dimension is `REVERSED`, for settlement actions; a grant that is `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`; a `RESOLVED` surveillance case; and an audit engagement that is `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `CLOSED_NO_FINDING`.
9. Generic transition legality — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), raised when the target's current state is not a declared origin state for the action being attempted. This covers non-terminal but wrong-state attempts — granting a `DRAFT` filing, paying an annuity on a `GRANTED` filing, evaluating a `PLANNED` trial, reconciling a `REQUESTED` tag order, accepting a `DRAFT` report, settling a `SUBMITTED` report, disputing an already-`DISPUTED` report, activating an already-`ACTIVE` sublicense, concluding testing on a `SAMPLED` case. It is also the code returned by the handful of gates that §3 expresses as timing or companion-dimension preconditions on a transition rather than as errors of their own: countersigning or reinstating an agreement whose `term_end` has already passed, activating a sublicense grant whose `expiry_date` has already passed, adding a line item to a report that is no longer `DRAFT`, and disputing or revising a report whose settlement dimension is not `UNSETTLED`. And it is what makes every action safe against accidental repetition: a second identical trigger finds the record past its origin state and is rejected.
10. Frozen-quota rejection — `ERR_QUOTA_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), when the quota an operation consumes is `FROZEN`.

Everything after that is business logic proper, and §2.9 through §2.11 and §3 describe it. Within the business tier the ordering is likewise fixed: agreement-activity gating, then segregation of duties, then the sublicensing and release gates, then reinstatement eligibility, then referential cross-checks, then uniqueness constraints, then temporal constraints, then fiscal and capacity constraints. Each subsection below and each workflow in §3 states the relative position of the checks it owns.

### 2.9. Parallel State Dimensions, Cascades and Version Propagation

Two records in this system carry **two independent state dimensions on a single row**, and this is a deliberate design choice rather than an accident of modelling.

A `Variety` has a `lifecycle_state` describing its journey toward commercial release — `CANDIDATE`, `IN_TRIAL`, `TRIAL_COMPLETE`, `RELEASED`, `RETIRED` — and a completely orthogonal `brand_status` describing its trademark — `BRAND_NONE`, `BRAND_REGISTERED`, `BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE`, `BRAND_ABANDONED`. Neither dimension constrains the other. A variety still in `CANDIDATE` may hold a registered mark, and a `RETIRED` variety may still hold one; abandoning the mark leaves the lifecycle untouched, and retiring the variety leaves the mark untouched.

A `RoyaltyReport` likewise has a `review_status` — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DISPUTED` — and a `settlement_status` — `UNSETTLED`, `SETTLED`, `REVERSED`. Settling a report never touches its `review_status`, and disputing a report never touches its `settlement_status`.

Three of the state machines are **cyclic**. A protection filing laps `GRANTED → MAINTENANCE_DUE → GRANTED` once per scheduled annuity, and `installments_paid_count` counts the completed laps. An agreement laps `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` any number of times before termination, and `reinstatement_count` counts those laps. A report laps `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and `revision_no` counts those. A variety may also re-enter trialing, moving `TRIAL_COMPLETE → IN_TRIAL` when a further regional trial is planted, until it is released.

Three transitions are **server-chosen forks**: the caller supplies no branch selector and the server reads stored data to decide the outcome. Certifying an evaluated trial lands on `PASSED` or `FAILED`; concluding testing on a surveillance case lands on `MATCH_CONFIRMED` or `NO_MATCH`; finalizing an audit engagement lands on `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `CLOSED_NO_FINDING`. In each case the two branches have deliberately **divergent side-effect sets**, and the difference is externally observable — §3 spells out both branches of all three.

Cascades run **breadth-first by level**, and every level is caused by the level above it. When a level touches multiple sibling records, they are processed in a pinned order stated with the workflow that owns the cascade; that order is never by `id`. Every record a cascade mutates — at any depth — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. A record that a cascade evaluates but does not actually change gets neither: when certification fails a trial, the variety is untouched and its `version` does not move, and when a proposed grant is revoked before it ever became active the variety's `active_sublicense_count` is unchanged and its `version` does not move. The converse also holds, and it is the rule wherever the two collide: where a workflow in §3 states outright that a particular record's `version` increments — the parent of one of the three repeatable child creates of §2.7, the variety under a lapse or a passing certification, the trial site under a certification, the propagator under an acceptance, dispute, settlement or reversal — that increment lands unconditionally, even when the re-derivation it carries happens to leave every value exactly as it was.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties on the Licensing Desk

Running a royalty report from acceptance through to payment deliberately requires at least two distinct `LicensingOfficer` user identifiers. Two barriers enforce this, and both compare the acting `X-User-ID` against a user id the system recorded earlier:

- The officer whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `countersigned_by_user_id` when the agreement was activated may not accept a royalty report filed under that agreement.
- The officer whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `accepted_by_user_id` when a report was accepted may not settle that same report.

Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Within the business tier this pair of checks is evaluated immediately after the agreement-must-be-active gate and before any of the sublicensing, release, reinstatement, referential, uniqueness, temporal or fiscal checks — so an officer who is barred from acting learns that fact rather than receiving a computed billing verdict. The same principle separates functions across roles: the audit function can force a report back into dispute but can never accept, settle or reverse it, and the breeding rights office can release a variety but can never bill for it.

### 2.11. Compliance Scoring and Automatic Suspension

Every propagator carries two scores derived from the same set of penalty events: `compliance_score`, computed over a rolling 120-day window, and `lifetime_compliance_score`, computed over every penalty event ever derived regardless of age. Both start from a baseline of 100, both are clamped so they can never fall below 0 nor rise above 100, and neither is stored as an event log — the penalty events are **derived on demand** from persisted rows.

Five sources contribute penalty points, each with its own event instant:

- A royalty report that was submitted late and has been accepted contributes **6 points**, attributed to the instant the report was submitted.
- A royalty line item flagged as an overrun on an accepted report contributes **15 points**, attributed to the instant the report was accepted.
- An audit engagement that issued a `MINOR` finding contributes **10 points**, attributed to the instant the engagement was finalized.
- An audit engagement that issued a `MATERIAL` finding contributes **25 points**, attributed to the instant the engagement was finalized.
- A surveillance case whose testing concluded in a confirmed genetic match against a named suspect contributes **30 points**, attributed to the instant testing was concluded. A case that has since been resolved still contributes, so long as its pre-resolution conclusion was a confirmed match.

Report-sourced and audit-sourced events reach the propagator through the agreement chain: the report or engagement names an agreement, and the agreement names the propagator.

Only rows in those exact statuses contribute. A late report that has been submitted but not yet accepted contributes nothing. A report that has been disputed stops contributing both its late-filing and its overrun points. An audit engagement still open contributes nothing, and one closed with no finding contributes nothing. A surveillance case concluded as no match contributes nothing, and a case with no named suspect is attributed to no propagator at all.

The rolling window is **half-open**. Take the logical clock's present instant, subtract 120 days, and call that the lower bound; an event contributes when its event instant is **strictly after** that lower bound and **at or before** the present instant. An event landing exactly 120 days before now has therefore aged out, while an event landing exactly at now is in. `compliance_score` is 100 minus the sum of the points of the in-window events, clamped into 0 through 100. `lifetime_compliance_score` is 100 minus the sum of the points of **all** derived events, clamped the same way; because it ignores age, it never rises when the clock advances and is monotonically non-increasing for a fixed set of source rows.

Both scores are recomputed at four points: when a royalty report is accepted, when an audit engagement is finalized, when a surveillance case's testing concludes in a confirmed match, and on every advance of the logical clock. Whenever `compliance_score` is recomputed and lands **strictly below 70**, every `ACTIVE` agreement of that propagator is suspended with `suspension_reason` set to `COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BREACH`, and each suspension in turn freezes that agreement's `ACTIVE` quotas. Recovery is never automatic in the other direction: a later recomputation that lifts the score back to 70 or above reinstates nothing. Reinstatement is always an explicit act by a `LicensingOfficer`, and it has its own eligibility gates.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Variety and Filing for Protection

A `BreederAdmin` opens the record for a new breeding candidate by supplying its **denomination** — the official variety name of record, between 1 and 80 characters — together with the botanical `species` (1 to 60 characters, for example `Rubus idaeus`), the `breeder_code` of the owning breeding programme (2 to 12 uppercase characters), and the two published base royalty rates: `base_royalty_cents_per_plant` and `base_royalty_cents_per_acre`, each a whole number of cents of at least 1. The `royalty_basis_default` may also be supplied, choosing between `PER_PLANT` and `PER_ACRE`; when omitted it is `PER_PLANT`. That default is not itself a billing rule — it is the value that production quotas inherit when they do not state a basis of their own.

The denomination is unique across every variety in the system, compared case-insensitively after trimming surrounding whitespace. A second registration under a name that already exists in any letter case is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DENOMINATION` (HTTP 409). This uniqueness check sits in the constraint tier, after the referential checks and before the temporal ones.

A newly registered variety begins in a fully clean state: `lifecycle_state` is `CANDIDATE`, `brand_status` is `BRAND_NONE`, `protection_posture` is `UNPROTECTED`, `protected_jurisdictions` is an empty list, `regional_performance_index` is 0.00, `passed_trial_region_count` is 0, `active_sublicense_count` is 0, `brand_renewal_count` is 0, and `version` is 1. `brand_mark_text`, `brand_registry_class`, `brand_registered_on`, `brand_renewal_due_at` and `released_at` are all null. Varieties can be browsed with filters on `species`, on `lifecycle_state` and on `protection_posture`.

**Filing for protection.** Legal protection is per jurisdiction, so a variety accumulates a portfolio of filings rather than a single one. A `BreederAdmin` opens each application against a `variety_id`, choosing the `instrument_type` — either `PLANT_PATENT` or `PLANT_VARIETY_CERTIFICATE` — and naming the `jurisdiction_code` (2 to 4 uppercase characters, for example `US`, `EU` or `CA`). The application also fixes the money and duration of the future maintenance obligation: `annuity_base_cents`, the base amount of each maintenance instalment, is a whole number of cents of at least 1000, and `term_years`, the protection term measured from grant, is an integer between 5 and 25 that defaults to 20 when omitted. A new filing starts in `DRAFT` with `annuity_sequence_no` 0, `installments_paid_count` 0, `annuity_days_late` 0, `annuity_surcharge_cents` 0, `annuity_volume_credit_cents` 0, `annuity_total_due_cents` 0, `version` 1, and null values for `filed_on`, `granted_on`, `annuity_due_date` and `lapse_reason`. `annuity_grace_days` starts at its default of 90. Opening the filing also re-derives the parent variety's `protection_posture` and `protected_jurisdictions` over the portfolio the new `DRAFT` row has just joined, and on this operation the variety's `updated_at` and `version` move only when that re-derivation actually changed one of the two — which is exactly what happens for a variety's very first filing, whose posture goes `UNPROTECTED → PENDING`, and which does not happen when the variety already sits at the posture the new row implies.

At most one filing may exist for any given combination of variety, instrument type and jurisdiction while it is still live — that is, while its status is `DRAFT`, `FILED`, `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE`. An attempt to open a second one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 409). Once a filing has reached `LAPSED` or `ABANDONED` the triple is released and a replacement filing for the same variety, instrument and jurisdiction may be opened. Filings are browsable filtered by `variety_id` to walk one variety's portfolio, or by `status` or `jurisdiction_code`. Opening a filing is not gated on the variety's lifecycle in any way: a filing may be opened against a variety in any `lifecycle_state`, `RETIRED` included.

The filing then walks a well-defined path. Lodging the drafted application with the registry moves it `DRAFT → FILED` and stamps `filed_on` with the logical clock's present instant. Recording the registry's decision moves it `FILED → GRANTED` and stamps `granted_on`. Abandoning an application before grant is available from either `DRAFT` or `FILED` and moves the filing to `ABANDONED`; nothing else about it changes, and no jurisdiction is affected. Every one of these three transitions increments the filing's own `version`, and every one of them also re-derives the parent variety's protection posture and increments the variety's `version` as well.

**How protection posture is derived.** `protection_posture` and `protected_jurisdictions` are never set directly; they are re-derived from the variety's whole filing portfolio every time a filing joins it or changes status — on opening a filing, on lodging, on grant, on scheduling an annuity, on paying one, on a voluntary lapse, on abandonment, and on the clock sweep's grace expiry. The derivation walks a ladder of four branches and the **first matching branch wins**:

1. If the variety has at least one filing whose status is `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, the posture is `PROTECTED`.
2. Otherwise, if it has at least one filing whose status is `DRAFT` or `FILED`, the posture is `PENDING`.
3. Otherwise, if it has at least one filing whose status is `LAPSED`, the posture is `LAPSED_PROTECTION`.
4. Otherwise the posture is `UNPROTECTED`.

Filings in status `ABANDONED` contribute to none of the four branches: an abandoned application is not in force, not pending, and not lapsed. `protected_jurisdictions` is then the list of distinct `jurisdiction_code` values taken from exactly those filings whose status is `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, sorted lexicographically ascending by the code itself — never by row creation order and never by `id`. Note what follows from the first branch: `MAINTENANCE_DUE` keeps protection in force, so an annuity that is outstanding but still inside its grace window neither drops the posture below `PROTECTED` nor removes its jurisdiction from `protected_jurisdictions`.

The practical consequences are visible immediately. A variety with a single fresh `DRAFT` filing sits at `PENDING` with an empty jurisdiction list. Lodging it changes nothing about the posture, because `DRAFT` and `FILED` are the same branch. Granting it moves the posture to `PROTECTED` and inserts the jurisdiction code into `protected_jurisdictions` at its lexicographic position. Abandoning the only remaining pending filing of a variety that has never held a grant drops the posture from `PENDING` back to `UNPROTECTED`, because no filing of that variety is then in `DRAFT`, `FILED`, `GRANTED`, `MAINTENANCE_DUE` or `LAPSED`.

### 3.2. The Maintenance Calendar: Annuities, Grace, Payment and Lapse

Protection survives only if it is maintained, and maintenance is a repeating obligation. A `BreederAdmin` posts the next instalment onto a granted filing's calendar by supplying `annuity_due_date`, the calendar instant at which the instalment falls due, and optionally `annuity_grace_days`, the number of days after that instant during which a late payment is still accepted — an integer between 0 and 90 that defaults to 90. That default is re-applied on every lap, so omitting `annuity_grace_days` on a later instalment resets an earlier lap's narrower window back to 90 rather than retaining it. The supplied due date must be strictly later than the logical clock's present instant; a due date at or before now is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

Posting the instalment drives the filing `GRANTED → MAINTENANCE_DUE`. It increments `annuity_sequence_no` by 1, giving each lap of the maintenance cycle a 1-based ordinal; it writes `annuity_due_date` and `annuity_grace_days` from the payload; and it resets `annuity_days_late`, `annuity_surcharge_cents`, `annuity_volume_credit_cents` and `annuity_total_due_cents` all back to 0. The figures from an earlier lap are overwritten, never accumulated — the filing always describes the obligation currently outstanding. The filing's `version` increments. The variety's `protection_posture` is deliberately unchanged, because `MAINTENANCE_DUE` is still in force — the posture is re-derived all the same, but since the re-derivation moves neither `protection_posture` nor `protected_jurisdictions` the variety's `updated_at` and `version` do not move either.

**The amount due.** When the `BreederAdmin` pays, it must supply `paid_amount_cents` and that figure must equal, to the cent, the amount the server computes at that very instant. The computation runs in a pinned order of five steps, and the tier lookups in it are not interchangeable.

*Step one — lateness.* Take the number of seconds between `annuity_due_date` and the logical clock's present instant, divide by the 86,400 seconds in a day, discard any fraction, and take the larger of that figure and zero. The result is `annuity_days_late`. Because the day count is floored, a delay of five days and twenty-three hours is five days late, and a payment made at or before the due date is zero days late.

*Step two — the late surcharge, assessed on the base amount only.* Zero days late attracts no surcharge at all. From 1 through 30 days late inclusive the surcharge is 5 percent of `annuity_base_cents`. From 31 through 60 days late inclusive it is 12 percent of `annuity_base_cents`. At 61 days late or beyond it is 20 percent of `annuity_base_cents`. The resulting figure is rounded half up to a whole number of cents and stored as `annuity_surcharge_cents`.

*Step three — the pre-credit total.* Add `annuity_base_cents` and `annuity_surcharge_cents`. This intermediate figure is what the next step is keyed on.

*Step four — the portfolio volume credit.* A credit percentage is chosen by looking up **the pre-credit total from step three**, not the final amount payable. When the pre-credit total is below 200,000 cents the credit percentage is zero. When it falls between 200,000 and 499,999 cents inclusive the credit is 3 percent of the pre-credit total. When it is 500,000 cents or more the credit is 6 percent of the pre-credit total. The result is rounded half up to a whole number of cents and stored as `annuity_volume_credit_cents`. This keying is load-bearing: a surcharge can be what pushes a filing across a credit boundary, and the credit band must be read from the total that includes the surcharge. Keying the credit on the final payable figure instead would be circular and would land straddling cases in a lower band.

*Step five — the amount payable.* Subtract `annuity_volume_credit_cents` from the pre-credit total. That is `annuity_total_due_cents`.

If `paid_amount_cents` differs from that figure in either direction, the payment is rejected with `ERR_ANNUITY_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Underpayment and overpayment are both refused outright rather than partially applied, because a partial payment would leave the filing's lapse date ambiguous.

**The grace boundary.** Payment is only accepted while the logical clock's present instant is at or before `annuity_due_date` plus `annuity_grace_days` days. The boundary is **inclusive**: a payment landing at exactly that instant is accepted, and the very next instant is not. A late attempt is rejected with `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422), and past that boundary the filing's only remaining path is the clock sweep's move into `LAPSED`. Within the business tier the grace check is a temporal check and is therefore evaluated before the fiscal amount check, so a payment that is both far too late and for the wrong amount reports the expired grace period.

A successful payment drives the filing `MAINTENANCE_DUE → GRANTED`. It writes all four computed annuity figures, increments `installments_paid_count` by 1, re-stamps `granted_on` with the present instant, and increments the filing's `version`. The variety's posture is re-derived on payment too and, exactly as on scheduling, the variety's `updated_at` and `version` move only if that re-derivation actually changed one of the two — which on a payment it never does, since `MAINTENANCE_DUE` and `GRANTED` sit in the same branch of the ladder. `installments_paid_count` therefore counts the completed laps of the `GRANTED → MAINTENANCE_DUE → GRANTED` cycle, and that cycle may repeat once per instalment in the maintenance calendar.

**Lapse, and what it takes down with it.** There are two ways a filing lapses, and both run the same three-level cascade. The first is the clock sweep: a filing still in `MAINTENANCE_DUE` when the logical clock passes strictly beyond `annuity_due_date` plus `annuity_grace_days` days is moved to `LAPSED` with `lapse_reason` set to `GRACE_EXPIRED`. The second is deliberate: a `BreederAdmin` may voluntarily stop maintaining a filing that is either `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, which moves it to `LAPSED` with `lapse_reason` set to `VOLUNTARY_LAPSE`.

The cascade below either lapse has three levels. Level one, caused by the lapse itself, re-derives the variety's `protection_posture` and `protected_jurisdictions`; the posture becomes `LAPSED_PROTECTION` when no filing of that variety remains in `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, and the lapsed filing's jurisdiction code drops out of `protected_jurisdictions`. Level two, caused by that jurisdiction dropping out, revokes every sublicense grant of the same variety and the same `jurisdiction_code` that is currently `ACTIVE`, setting each one's `revocation_reason` to `PROTECTION_LAPSED`; those grants are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `territory_code` ascending and then by `sublicensee_name` ascending. Level three, caused by each revocation, re-derives the variety's `active_sublicense_count`, decrementing it once per revoked grant. The filing's `version`, the variety's `version`, and the `version` of every revoked grant all increment by 1.

### 3.3. The Brand Dimension: Registering, Renewing and Abandoning the Mark

The name a variety is sold under is a commercial asset in its own right, and the licensor manages it separately from the variety's legal name. The reason is unromantic: once protection lapses, the denomination becomes generic and anyone may use it, so the denomination must never double as the trademark. The `brand_status` dimension tracks that mark and moves entirely independently of `lifecycle_state`.

A `BreederAdmin` registers the mark on a variety whose `brand_status` is still `BRAND_NONE` by supplying `brand_mark_text` (1 to 60 characters), `brand_registry_class` — the Nice classification class, an integer between 1 and 45 — and `brand_renewal_due_at`, the instant at which the mark will next need renewing. The renewal instant must be strictly later than the logical clock's present instant; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

The mark text is subjected to a separation check that is deliberately broad. Compared case-insensitively after trimming, the proposed `brand_mark_text` must not equal this variety's own `denomination`, must not equal any other variety's `denomination`, and must not equal any other variety's `brand_mark_text`. Any of those three collisions is rejected with `ERR_BRAND_CONFLICTS_DENOMINATION` (HTTP 422). The same check applies, in exactly the same three-way form, when a mark is renewed with restated text. Within the business tier this check sits among the referential cross-checks, after the territory and report-to-agreement cross-checks and before the uniqueness constraints.

Registration drives `BRAND_NONE → BRAND_REGISTERED`, writes `brand_mark_text`, `brand_registry_class` and `brand_renewal_due_at` from the payload, stamps `brand_registered_on` with the logical clock's present instant, and increments the variety's `version`. Nothing about `lifecycle_state`, `protection_posture` or `protected_jurisdictions` moves: a `CANDIDATE` variety may perfectly well hold a registered mark.

Renewal is a cycle rather than a one-off. When the logical clock reaches or passes `brand_renewal_due_at`, the sweep moves the variety `BRAND_REGISTERED → BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE` and increments its `version`. A `BreederAdmin` then renews by supplying `brand_mark_text` and a fresh `brand_renewal_due_at`, which again must be strictly later than the present instant. Both of those fields are required — a renewal cannot omit the mark text and merely push the date out — and `brand_registry_class` is not restatable at renewal, so offering it is an unknown field and a malformed body. Renewal drives `BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE → BRAND_REGISTERED`, writes the new `brand_renewal_due_at`, re-stamps `brand_registered_on` with the present instant, increments `brand_renewal_count` by 1, and increments the variety's `version`. `brand_renewal_count` therefore records how many laps of `BRAND_REGISTERED → BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE → BRAND_REGISTERED` the mark has run, and that cycle turns once per renewal term, completely independently of the protection filing's maintenance cycle.

Abandonment is terminal for this dimension and is available from either of the two live brand states. Abandoning a mark that is `BRAND_REGISTERED` or `BRAND_RENEWAL_DUE` moves `brand_status` to `BRAND_ABANDONED` and increments the variety's `version`; nothing else changes, and in particular the variety's lifecycle, protection posture and sublicense count are all untouched.

### 3.4. Regional Trialing, Evaluation Scoring, and the Road to Commercial Release

**Sites.** A `TrialCoordinator` registers each regional trialing site with a `site_name` (1 to 60 characters), a `region_code` identifying the growing region (2 to 6 uppercase characters, for example `PNW` or `SE`), and a `plot_capacity` of at least 1 — the number of plots the site can host at any single instant. A new site starts with `occupied_plot_count` 0 and `version` 1. `occupied_plot_count` is derived, never supplied: it is the sum of `plot_count` over the trials hosted at that site whose status is `PLANNED` or `PLANTED`. The stored figure is refreshed on exactly three events — when a trial is scheduled at the site, when one is planted, and when one is certified — and recording an evaluation is deliberately not one of them, so between a trial's evaluation and its certification the site's stored `occupied_plot_count` still counts that trial's plots even though its status has left `PLANNED`/`PLANTED`. The capacity rule below never reads the stored figure; it re-counts the overlapping trials at the instant of planting. Sites can be browsed filtered by `region_code`.

**Scheduling a trial.** A `TrialCoordinator` books a regional performance trial by naming the `variety_id` under evaluation and the `trial_site_id` hosting it, declaring the `plot_count` the trial will occupy (at least 1), and fixing the occupancy window with `plant_date` and `harvest_date`. The window is **half-open**: `plant_date` is the inclusive start and `harvest_date` the exclusive end, and `harvest_date` must be strictly later than `plant_date` or the booking is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A new trial starts in `PLANNED` with `performance_index` 0.00, `version` 1, and null values for `yield_index`, `vigor_score`, `uniformity_score`, `disease_resistance_score`, `propagation_pass_rate` and `evaluated_at`. Booking the trial also re-derives the hosting site's `occupied_plot_count` immediately, because a `PLANNED` trial already occupies its plots, so the site's `updated_at` and `version` move on scheduling as well as on planting. Trials are browsable filtered by `variety_id` to walk one variety's trialing history, by `trial_site_id` to walk a site's booking calendar, or by `status`.

**Planting, and the site capacity rule.** Planting drives the trial `PLANNED → PLANTED`. It is refused before the calendar has caught up: the logical clock's present instant must be at or after `plant_date`, and an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_TRIAL_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). It is also refused unless the variety's `lifecycle_state` is one of `CANDIDATE`, `IN_TRIAL`, `TRIAL_COMPLETE` or `RELEASED` — a `RETIRED` variety is past trialing, and because that is a terminal lifecycle the refusal is `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` (HTTP 409), evaluated immediately after the trial's own terminal and transition checks and before the plant-date check.

Capacity is then checked as an aggregate over overlapping windows rather than as a simple running total. Take every trial at the same `trial_site_id` whose status is `PLANNED` or `PLANTED` and whose half-open occupancy window overlaps the window of the trial being planted, count this trial among them, and add up their `plot_count` values. If that sum exceeds the site's `plot_capacity`, the planting is rejected with `ERR_TRIAL_SITE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Because the windows are half-open, **adjacency is not overlap**: a trial whose `harvest_date` is exactly another trial's `plant_date` does not co-occupy the site within one shared window, and the two may both be planted at a site with room for only one of them.

A successful planting has three effects beyond the trial's own status. It re-derives the site's `occupied_plot_count`. It reaches across into the variety's lifecycle dimension: a `CANDIDATE` variety moves to `IN_TRIAL`, and a `TRIAL_COMPLETE` variety whose `released_at` is still null moves back to `IN_TRIAL` — the revision loop by which a variety re-enters trialing for further regions until it is released. A variety already `RELEASED` is left exactly as it is, since planting a further trial after release no longer moves the lifecycle. And it increments versions: the trial's `version` and the site's `version` always, and the variety's `version` **only when the lifecycle state actually changed**.

**Evaluation.** After harvest, the `TrialCoordinator` records the observations. The logical clock's present instant must be at or after `harvest_date`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_TRIAL_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. The payload carries five observations: `yield_index`, `vigor_score`, `uniformity_score` and `disease_resistance_score`, each a number from 0 to 100 with at most 2 decimal places, and `propagation_pass_rate`, the fraction of propagated plants meeting the quality standard, supplied as a number from 0 to 1 inclusive carrying at most 4 decimal places, which the server normalizes to exactly 4 decimal places on storage.

From those five the server computes the trial's `performance_index`, and the evaluation order is pinned. Multiply `yield_index` by 0.40 and round that product half up to 2 decimal places. Multiply `vigor_score` by 0.20 and round half up to 2 decimal places. Do the same for `uniformity_score` at 0.20 and for `disease_resistance_score` at 0.20. Add the four already-rounded components together to get a raw score. Multiply that raw score by `propagation_pass_rate` and round the result half up to 2 decimal places. That final figure is `performance_index`. Rounding the four components individually before summing is not optional decoration — summing unrounded components and rounding only at the end can shift the answer by 0.01. The four weights add to exactly 1.00, so a trial that scored 100 on every observation with a pass rate of 1.0000 would index at 100.00.

Evaluation stores the five observations, writes `performance_index`, stamps `evaluated_at` with the present instant, drives the trial `PLANTED → EVALUATED`, and increments the trial's `version`.

**Certification: the first server-chosen fork.** Certification is reserved to a `BreederAdmin`, and it is the point at which the licensor decides whether a trial counts toward release. The caller supplies no branch selector at all; the server reads the trial's stored figures and chooses. A trial passes when its `performance_index` is at least 72.00 **and** its `propagation_pass_rate` is at least 0.9500 — both boundaries inclusive, so a trial indexing exactly 72.00 with a pass rate of exactly 0.9500 passes. Otherwise it fails, which is to say it fails when the `performance_index` is below 72.00 or the `propagation_pass_rate` is below 0.9500.

The two branches share one effect and diverge on everything else. Both move the trial to a terminal status — `EVALUATED → PASSED` or `EVALUATED → FAILED` — both re-derive the site's `occupied_plot_count`, since the trial's plots are released either way, and both increment the trial's `version` and the site's `version`.

On the **`PASSED`** branch the variety is re-derived. `passed_trial_region_count` becomes the number of distinct `region_code` values among the trial sites of that variety's trials whose status is `PASSED`. `regional_performance_index` becomes the mean of `performance_index` over exactly those `PASSED` trials, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — and 0.00 when the variety has no passed trials at all. Then, if the freshly recomputed `passed_trial_region_count` has reached 2 or more **and** the variety is at that moment in `IN_TRIAL`, the variety's lifecycle moves `IN_TRIAL → TRIAL_COMPLETE` and its `regional_performance_index` is re-derived as part of that transition; a variety already `RELEASED`, or still sitting in `CANDIDATE` or `TRIAL_COMPLETE`, keeps its lifecycle and takes only the two recomputed aggregates. The variety's `version` increments on this branch unconditionally, even when both aggregates happen to land on the values they already held.

On the **`FAILED`** branch none of that happens. `passed_trial_region_count` is not recomputed, `regional_performance_index` is not recomputed, `lifecycle_state` is not changed, and the variety's `version` is **not** incremented. This asymmetry is the observable signature of the fork. It also has a substantive consequence: because only `PASSED` trials feed the aggregate, a failed trial with a high `performance_index` never lifts the variety's index, and trials still in `PLANNED`, `PLANTED` or `EVALUATED` are excluded from both the region count and the mean.

**Release.** A `BreederAdmin` releases a variety for commercial propagation, driving `lifecycle_state` `TRIAL_COMPLETE → RELEASED`. Three release criteria must all hold at that moment: `passed_trial_region_count` must be at least 2, so that performance has been demonstrated in at least two distinct growing regions; `regional_performance_index` must be at least 72.00; and `protection_posture` must be either `PENDING` or `PROTECTED`. A variety whose posture is `UNPROTECTED` or `LAPSED_PROTECTION` is never released, because there would be no enforceable right behind the royalty. Any of the three failing yields `ERR_RELEASE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). A successful release stamps `released_at` with the logical clock's present instant and increments the variety's `version`.

**Retirement.** Retirement drives `RELEASED → RETIRED` and is the terminal state of the lifecycle dimension. It stamps no instant of its own: the variety carries no retirement timestamp field at all, and `released_at` keeps exactly the value the release wrote. It is blocked while the variety's `active_sublicense_count` is greater than 0: live nursery or retail sublicenses must be revoked or allowed to expire first, and an attempt otherwise is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVE_SUBLICENSES_EXIST` (HTTP 409). Retirement then runs a two-level cascade. Level one, caused by the retirement, closes every production quota of that variety whose status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, moving each to `CLOSED`; those quotas are processed by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `season_code` ascending. Level two, caused by each quota closure, voids every tag order of that quota whose status is `REQUESTED`, setting each one's `voided_units` to its `requested_units`. The variety's `version`, the `version` of every closed quota and the `version` of every voided tag order each increment by 1.

### 3.5. Propagators, Head Licences, and the Suspension Cycle

**Onboarding a counterparty.** A `LicensingOfficer` registers each licensed nursery as a propagator with its `legal_name` — the registered legal name, 1 to 80 characters, unique across all propagators when compared case-insensitively after trimming, a collision being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than with any dedicated duplicate code — and its `home_territory_code`, the primary territory it operates from (2 to 8 uppercase characters, which may contain a hyphen, for example `US-OR`). Every one of a new propagator's derived fields starts at its clean baseline: `compliance_score` 100, `lifetime_compliance_score` 100, `royalty_payable_cents` 0, `royalty_settled_cents` 0, `damages_credit_cents` 0, `material_audit_finding_count` 0, and `version` 1. Propagators can be browsed filtered by `home_territory_code`.

Two of those derived fields deserve their definitions stated here rather than in the workflows that move them. `material_audit_finding_count` is the number of audit engagements reached through this propagator's agreements whose status is `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and whose `finding_severity` is `MATERIAL`. `damages_credit_cents` is the cumulative total of the propagator-credit bucket allocated to this propagator across resolved surveillance cases; it is kept deliberately **outside** the royalty ledger so that the payable-plus-settled conservation identity of §3.7 stays exact.

**Drafting the head licence.** A `PropagatorAgreement` is the head licence under which everything downstream hangs. A `LicensingOfficer` drafts one against a `propagator_id`, declaring the `quality_tier` the propagator has certified to — one of `TIER_A`, `TIER_B` or `TIER_C`, defaulting to `TIER_B` when omitted — the `territory_codes` the propagator may produce and sublicense in, and the term bounds `term_start` and `term_end`. `territory_codes` is a non-empty array of territory codes, each 2 to 8 uppercase characters possibly containing a hyphen, with no duplicates in the array. The term is half-open: `term_start` is the inclusive first instant and `term_end` the exclusive last, and `term_end` must be strictly later than `term_start` or the draft is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A drafted agreement starts in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` with `reinstatement_count` 0, `version` 1, and null `countersigned_by_user_id`, `countersigned_at` and `suspension_reason`. It authorizes nothing at all in that state. Agreements are browsable filtered by `propagator_id` to walk one propagator's licence history, or by `status`.

**Countersigning.** A `LicensingOfficer` countersigns on the licensor's side, driving `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN → ACTIVE`. Countersigning is only available while the logical clock's present instant is strictly before `term_end`; an agreement whose term has already run out cannot be brought to life, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). The transition records the acting `X-User-ID` in `countersigned_by_user_id`, stamps `countersigned_at` with the present instant, and increments the agreement's `version`. That recorded user id is not decoration: as §2.10 states, the officer who countersigned is permanently barred from accepting royalty reports filed under this agreement.

An `ACTIVE` agreement is the licence to operate, and its absence is felt in seven distinct places. The governing agreement must be `ACTIVE` in order to allocate a production quota, to request a tag order, to issue tags, to add a royalty line item, to submit a royalty report, to accept a royalty report, and to activate a sublicense grant. In all seven the rejection is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), and it applies equally to an agreement still `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` and to one that is `SUSPENDED` — neither authorizes any downstream production or billing activity. Within the business tier this gate is evaluated first, before segregation of duties and before everything after it.

**Suspension, from three directions.** An agreement laps `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` any number of times before it is finally terminated, and suspension arrives from three directions that differ only in the reason they record.

A `LicensingOfficer` may suspend an `ACTIVE` agreement outright, which records `suspension_reason` as `MANUAL`. The system suspends automatically whenever a recomputation of the propagator's rolling `compliance_score` lands strictly below 70, recording `suspension_reason` as `COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BREACH`. And the system suspends automatically when an audit engagement is finalized with a `MATERIAL` finding, recording `suspension_reason` as `AUDIT_FINDING`.

The precedence between the last two is pinned. When a `MATERIAL` finding also drops the rolling score below 70, `suspension_reason` ends up as `AUDIT_FINDING`: the audit reason is applied first, and the compliance recomputation that follows finds the agreement already `SUSPENDED` and leaves `suspension_reason` exactly as it is.

Whichever direction it arrives from, suspension carries the same cascade. Every production quota of that agreement whose status is `ACTIVE` moves to `FROZEN`, processed by `created_at` ascending with ties broken by `season_code` ascending. The agreement's `version` increments by 1, and so does the `version` of every frozen quota.

`FROZEN` is a gate, not a terminal state. A frozen quota blocks three operations: requesting a tag order against it, issuing an already-requested tag order on it, and adding a royalty line item that reports against it. All three are rejected with `ERR_QUOTA_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), and that check sits at the end of the state tier, after the generic transition check and before any business check.

**Reinstatement.** Recovery is never automatic. A later recomputation that lifts a propagator's `compliance_score` back to 70 or above does not reinstate anything; a `LicensingOfficer` must act. Reinstatement drives `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and has three eligibility conditions. The propagator's `compliance_score` must be at least 70, and a lower score is rejected with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 422). The propagator's `material_audit_finding_count` must be exactly 0, and any outstanding material finding is rejected with `ERR_MATERIAL_FINDING_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 422) — a propagator carrying an unresolved material finding cannot be reinstated even when its rolling score has fully recovered. And the logical clock's present instant must still be strictly before `term_end`, a failure there being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) and evaluated last of the three, after both coded eligibility gates. Of those two the compliance-score gate is evaluated first, so a propagator failing both is told about its score.

A successful reinstatement clears `suspension_reason` back to null, increments `reinstatement_count` by 1 — the count of completed `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` laps — and cascades in the mirror image of suspension: every `FROZEN` quota of that agreement moves back to `ACTIVE`. The agreement's `version` and the `version` of every thawed quota each increment by 1.

**Termination.** Termination is the terminal state of the agreement machine and is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. It is blocked by one financial condition: no royalty report of this agreement may be sitting with `review_status` `ACCEPTED` and `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED` at the same time. Accepted-but-unpaid royalties must be settled or disputed before the licence is closed out, and an attempt otherwise is rejected with `ERR_UNSETTLED_ROYALTY_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409).

Termination then runs the widest cascade in the system, and it has two independent branches beneath it. On the quota branch, level one closes every production quota of the agreement whose status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, moving each to `CLOSED`; level two, caused by each closure, voids every tag order of that quota whose status is `REQUESTED`, setting each one's `voided_units` to its `requested_units`. On the sublicensing branch, level one revokes every sublicense grant of the agreement whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`, setting each one's `revocation_reason` to `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`; level two, caused by each revocation, re-derives `active_sublicense_count` on each affected variety. The agreement's `version` increments, and so does the `version` of every quota, tag order, sublicense grant and variety the cascade actually mutated.

### 3.6. Production Quotas, Traceability Tags, and the Committed-Unit Invariant

**Allocating a quota.** A production quota is the authorization to propagate one variety, in one territory, for one season, under one head licence. A `LicensingOfficer` allocates it by naming the `agreement_id` and the `variety_id`, the `season_code` identifying the production season (4 to 8 characters, for example `2026S1`), the `basis` on which royalty will be charged, the destination `territory_code` (2 to 8 uppercase characters, possibly containing a hyphen), the `territory_class` of that destination, and the `allocated_units` authorized — at least 1, counted in plants when the basis is `PER_PLANT` and in whole acres when it is `PER_ACRE`.

Two of those fields have defaults worth stating precisely. `basis` may be `PER_PLANT` or `PER_ACRE`; when the payload omits it, the quota copies the variety's `royalty_basis_default` at the moment of creation. `territory_class` may be `DOMESTIC` or `EXPORT` and defaults to `DOMESTIC`; it is a pricing classification of the destination and, as §3.7 shows, it moves the royalty rate directly.

The destination territory must sit inside the agreement's licensed footprint: the supplied `territory_code` must be a member of the agreement's `territory_codes`, and a territory outside that list is rejected with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_LICENSED` (HTTP 422). The same footprint rule, with the same code, guards sublicense proposals. Uniqueness is scoped to live quotas only: at most one quota may exist for a given combination of agreement, variety, season code and territory code while its status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, and a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUOTA_SEASON` (HTTP 409). A `CLOSED` quota does not block a replacement for the same combination. Allocation is gated on nothing about the variety itself — neither its `lifecycle_state` nor its `protection_posture` is consulted, so a quota may be allocated for a variety that has never been released — and the three gates that do apply fire in the order agreement-must-be-`ACTIVE`, then licensed footprint, then uniqueness.

A new quota starts `ACTIVE` with `tags_committed_units` 0, `tags_activated_units` 0, `units_available` equal to `allocated_units`, and `version` 1. Quotas can be browsed filtered by `agreement_id` to walk one agreement's allocations, by `variety_id` to walk one variety's licensed production, or by `status` or `season_code`. `CLOSED` is the quota machine's terminal state, and quotas reach it only through the cascades of agreement termination or variety retirement.

**The tag inventory derivation.** Three fields on the quota are derived from its tag orders and are re-derived in full on every write that touches them — never adjusted by delta. `tags_committed_units` is the sum of `requested_units` over the quota's tag orders whose status is `ISSUED` or `RECONCILED`. `units_available` is `allocated_units` minus `tags_committed_units`. `tags_activated_units` is the sum of `activated_units` over the quota's tag orders whose status is `RECONCILED` alone.

The membership rules there carry the whole design. A `REQUESTED` order is excluded from the committed sum, because a request is not yet an authorization to print tags. A `VOIDED` order is excluded from both sums, which is exactly what makes voiding the compensating action for issuing. A `RECONCILED` order stays inside the committed set, so reconciling moves no committed units. And only `RECONCILED` orders contribute activated units, so an order that has been issued but not yet reconciled contributes to committed units and nothing at all to activated units.

That yields an invariant which must hold for every quota at every observable instant: **`allocated_units` always equals `units_available` plus `tags_committed_units`.** It holds after an issuance, where `units_available` falls by exactly the order's `requested_units`. It holds after a reconciliation, where neither side moves. It holds after voiding an issued order, where `units_available` rises by exactly `requested_units` and returns to its precise pre-issuance value. And it holds trivially after voiding a merely requested order, where neither side moves because the order was never committed. Because the sums are re-aggregated rather than adjusted, an arbitrary number of issue-and-void laps leaves no drift whatsoever.

**Requesting tags.** A `Propagator` requests a batch of traceability tags against a quota by supplying the `quota_id`, the `requested_units` wanted (at least 1), and `expected_quota_version` carrying the quota `version` it last observed — the stale-view guard of §2.7, rejected with `ERR_STALE_QUOTA_VERSION` when it does not match. The acting propagator must be the one reached through the quota's agreement, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; the agreement must be `ACTIVE`; and the quota must not be `FROZEN`. A quota that has already reached `CLOSED` refuses the request with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` (HTTP 409), and the four checks run in the pinned order of §2.8: ownership, then the stale-view guard, then the terminal `CLOSED` check, then the frozen check, then the agreement-active gate. A new order starts in `REQUESTED` with `activated_units` 0, `voided_units` 0, `version` 1, and null `serial_prefix` and `issued_at`. Creating it increments the quota's `version` by 1 even though none of the quota's derived tag fields change — a `REQUESTED` order is not yet committed — which is precisely why the next requester's `expected_quota_version` must be refreshed. Tag orders can be browsed filtered by `quota_id` to walk a quota's issuance history, or by `status`.

**Issuing tags.** A `LicensingOfficer` issues the requested tags, driving the order `REQUESTED → ISSUED`. The governing agreement must be `ACTIVE` and the quota must not be `FROZEN`. The fiscal ceiling is then checked: the order's `requested_units` must not exceed the quota's `units_available` as freshly re-derived, and an over-request is rejected with `ERR_QUOTA_UNITS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Tags are the traceability instrument that makes royalties verifiable, so no more of them may exist than the quota authorizes.

Issuance mints the order's `serial_prefix`, the string printed on every tag of the batch. It is assembled from three parts joined by hyphens: the `breeder_code` of the quota's variety, then the `season_code` of the quota, then a four-digit zero-padded ordinal. That ordinal is the count of this quota's tag orders already in status `ISSUED` or `RECONCILED`, plus one — so the first batch issued against a quota ends in `0001`, the second in `0002`, and so on. Issuance also stamps `issued_at` with the logical clock's present instant, re-derives the quota's `tags_committed_units` and `units_available`, and increments both the order's `version` and the quota's `version`.

**Reconciling tags.** The propagator that holds the licence reports how many of the issued tags were actually affixed to saleable plants, driving the order `ISSUED → RECONCILED`. The payload carries `activated_units`, a non-negative integer that must not exceed the order's `requested_units`; more activations than were issued is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVATION_EXCEEDS_ISSUED` (HTTP 422). Fewer is perfectly legitimate. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `propagator_id` on the agreement of the order's quota, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Reconciliation writes `activated_units`, re-derives the quota's `tags_activated_units` while leaving `tags_committed_units` and `units_available` untouched, and increments both the order's `version` and the quota's `version`. `tags_activated_units` is the audited royalty floor: §3.7 shows how it can raise a report's billed quantity above what the propagator declared.

**Voiding.** A `LicensingOfficer` voids an order that is either `REQUESTED` or `ISSUED`, moving it to `VOIDED` and setting `voided_units` to the order's `requested_units` — the compensating quantity captured at the moment of voiding. From `ISSUED`, the order drops out of the committed set and `units_available` rises by exactly `voided_units`, restoring the invariant with no drift. From `REQUESTED`, neither `tags_committed_units` nor `units_available` moves, because the order was never committed in the first place. Both branches increment the order's `version` and the quota's `version`. Voiding is deliberately not gated on the quota at all: an order sitting on a `FROZEN` quota can still be voided, and so can one on a `CLOSED` quota. `RECONCILED` and `VOIDED` are the terminal states of the tag order machine.

### 3.7. Royalty Reporting: from the Reported Basis to the Booked Amount

Royalty reporting is the financial heart of the system, and it is deliberately built as two independent state dimensions over one record. `review_status` tracks the editorial life of the report — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DISPUTED` — and cycles. `settlement_status` tracks the money — `UNSETTLED`, `SETTLED`, `REVERSED` — and does not. Neither dimension has any influence on the other's transitions except where a precondition explicitly reads across, and settling a report never touches its `review_status`. `review_status` has no terminal state at all; `settlement_status` has exactly one, `REVERSED`.

**Opening a report.** A `Propagator` opens a report for one reporting period of one agreement, naming the `agreement_id`, the `period_code` identifying the period (4 to 10 characters, for example `2026-Q3`), the `period_start` that inclusively begins it, the `period_end` that exclusively ends it, and the `due_at` filing deadline. Three temporal rules apply together: `period_end` must be strictly later than `period_start`, and `due_at` must be at or after `period_end` — violating either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). Separately the period must sit inside the licence term: `period_start` must be at or after the agreement's `term_start` and `period_end` must be at or before its `term_end`, and a period straying outside is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (HTTP 422). At most one report may exist for a given combination of agreement and `period_code`, regardless of either state dimension, and a second one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD` (HTTP 409): corrections travel the dispute-and-revise loop below, never a duplicate report for the same period. A new report begins with `review_status` `DRAFT`, `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED`, `is_late` false, `revision_no` 0, `submitted_at`, `accepted_at`, `accepted_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` all null, all four money fields — `provisional_total_cents`, `final_total_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `booked_amount_cents` — at 0, and `version` 1. Opening the report is deliberately *not* one of the seven places §3.5 gates on an `ACTIVE` agreement, so a report may be opened under an agreement that is still `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`; the gate bites at submission. The checks that do apply fire in the order ownership, then the per-period uniqueness check, then the two interval rules, then the term containment rule. Reports are browsable filtered by `agreement_id` to walk an agreement's reporting history, by `period_code`, or by `review_status` and `settlement_status` independently of each other.

**Declaring production.** A `Propagator` adds one line item per quota being reported, supplying the `quota_id`, the `reported_units` declared for that quota over the period (a non-negative integer, counted in plants or acres according to the quota's `basis`), and `expected_report_version` carrying the report `version` last observed. That last field is the stale-view guard of §2.7 — every added line changes the report, so the client must be looking at the current one — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The quota must belong to the same agreement as the report: a quota whose `agreement_id` differs from the report's `agreement_id` is rejected with `ERR_QUOTA_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), because units produced under one licence may not be billed on another's report. At most one line may exist per combination of report and quota, and a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_ITEM_QUOTA` (HTTP 409), since reporting the same quota twice on one report would double-count the same production. The report itself must still be in `review_status` `DRAFT`: a line offered to a `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED` report is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), evaluated after the stale-view guard and before everything that follows. The governing agreement must be `ACTIVE` and the quota must not be `FROZEN` — the frozen check first, then the agreement gate, then the quota-to-agreement cross-check, then the per-quota uniqueness check. A new line is created with `billable_units` 0, `effective_rate_cents` 0.0000, `provisional_amount_cents` 0, `final_amount_cents` 0, `overrun_flagged` false and `version` 1 — all of its money is computed later, at submission and again at acceptance — and creating it increments the report's `version` by 1. Line items are browsable filtered by `report_id` to read one report's lines, by `quota_id` to trace every report that has billed a given quota, or by `overrun_flagged` to sweep up the lines that failed tag verification.

**The effective rate.** Every line's `effective_rate_cents` is the published base rate for the variety, pushed through four multipliers and then held inside a collar. It carries exactly 4 decimal places.

The base rate is chosen by the quota's `basis`: when the basis is `PER_PLANT` it is the variety's `base_royalty_cents_per_plant`, and when the basis is `PER_ACRE` it is the variety's `base_royalty_cents_per_acre`.

The first multiplier is the quality tier of the agreement: 1.15 for `TIER_A`, 1.00 for `TIER_B`, 0.85 for `TIER_C`. The second is the territory class of the quota: 1.25 for `EXPORT`, 1.00 for `DOMESTIC`. The third is a volume band read off the line's `billable_units`: 1.00 when `billable_units` is 10000 or fewer, 0.95 when it is between 10001 and 50000 inclusive, and 0.90 when it exceeds 50000. The fourth is a regional performance premium derived from the variety's `regional_performance_index`: take that index, subtract 72.00, divide by 100, then hold the result inside the range from 0.00 to 0.10 inclusive — anything below 0.00 becomes 0.00 and anything above 0.10 becomes 0.10 — and add 1. A variety whose index is 72.00 or lower therefore earns a premium multiplier of exactly 1.00, and one whose index is 82.00 or higher earns exactly 1.10.

Multiply the base rate by all four multipliers and round the product half-up to 4 decimal places. Then apply the collar, which keeps the effective rate from straying far from the published base. The floor is the base rate times 0.75, and the cap is the base rate times 1.50, each rounded half-up to 4 decimal places before use. Raise the rate to the floor if it is below it, lower it to the cap if it is above it, and round the collared result half-up to 4 decimal places once more. That final number is `effective_rate_cents`.

Note carefully that the rate is not a property of the line alone: because the volume band reads `billable_units`, and because `billable_units` is computed differently at submission and at acceptance, the very same stored line can be priced at two different rates in the two phases.

**Submitting on the reported basis.** The `Propagator` that holds the licence — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the agreement's `propagator_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — submits the report, driving `review_status` `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`, and the report must carry at least one line item; an empty report is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_HAS_NO_LINES` (HTTP 422), because a zero total computed from nothing is indistinguishable from a genuine zero-production period. Submission stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock's present instant and sets `is_late` to true exactly when `submitted_at` is strictly later than `due_at` — a submission landing exactly at `due_at` is on time.

Submission then prices the whole report on the **reported basis**. Every line of the report is processed in `created_at` ascending order, with ties broken by the `season_code` of the line's quota ascending and then by that quota's `territory_code` ascending — never by `id`. For each line, `billable_units` is set to `reported_units` verbatim, `overrun_flagged` is left false, `effective_rate_cents` is computed as described above, and `provisional_amount_cents` is set to `billable_units` multiplied by `effective_rate_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents. The report's `provisional_total_cents` is then the sum of those already-rounded per-line amounts. The rounding order here is pinned and is not interchangeable: each line is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded integers are summed. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different, wrong answer. On the reported basis no late fee and no booked amount are produced, and `final_total_cents` stays 0. The report's `version` and the `version` of every recomputed line each increment by 1.

**Accepting on the audited basis.** A `LicensingOfficer` accepts the report, driving `review_status` `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the agreement's `countersigned_by_user_id`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Acceptance reprices the report on the **audited basis**, using the same line ordering. For each line, `billable_units` becomes the larger of the line's `reported_units` and the quota's `tags_activated_units` — tag activations above the self-declared figure raise the billed quantity, and can simultaneously push the line into a lower volume band and thus change `effective_rate_cents` as well. `overrun_flagged` is set to true exactly when the quota's `tags_activated_units` is strictly greater than the line's `reported_units`, marking the lines where the propagator under-declared. `effective_rate_cents` is recomputed from the audited `billable_units`, `final_amount_cents` is set to `billable_units` multiplied by `effective_rate_cents` rounded half-up to whole cents, and `final_total_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-line amounts under the same pinned per-line-then-sum order. `provisional_total_cents` and the provisional per-line amounts are left exactly as submission left them, so the two bases remain independently readable side by side.

Acceptance alone produces the late fee and the booked amount. When `is_late` is true, `late_fee_cents` is `final_total_cents` multiplied by 0.02, rounded half-up to whole cents; when `is_late` is false, `late_fee_cents` is 0. `booked_amount_cents` is then `final_total_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`. Acceptance also stamps `accepted_at` with the present instant and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `accepted_by_user_id` — the officer who is thereby barred from settling this report.

Acceptance then runs a four-level cascade. Level one, caused by the acceptance itself, re-derives the propagator's `royalty_payable_cents`. Level two, caused by the `LATE_REPORT` and `QUOTA_OVERRUN` penalty events that the acceptance materializes, recomputes the propagator's `compliance_score` and `lifetime_compliance_score`. Level three, caused by a recomputed `compliance_score` strictly below 70, moves every `ACTIVE` agreement of that propagator to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BREACH`. Level four, caused by each of those suspensions, moves that agreement's `ACTIVE` quotas to `FROZEN`. The `version` of the report, of every line item, of the propagator, and of every suspended agreement and frozen quota each increment by 1. It is entirely possible for a single acceptance to suspend the very agreement it was filed under.

**Disputing.** A `LicensingOfficer` disputes an accepted report, driving `review_status` `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED`, optionally supplying a `dispute_note` explaining the objection — at most 500 characters, read only for the caller's own convenience, persisted nowhere and returned in no response field. The report's `settlement_status` must still be `UNSETTLED`: a report already paid out cannot be disputed, and because the two dimensions are tested together as a single origin condition a `SETTLED` or `REVERSED` report is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) rather than with a terminal code. Disputing sets `booked_amount_cents` to 0 and, at cascade level one caused by that un-booking, re-derives the propagator's `royalty_payable_cents`; the report's `version` and the propagator's `version` each increment by 1.

The same `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED` transition can also be raised by the system rather than by an officer, when an audit engagement over this report is finalized with a `MINOR` or `MATERIAL` finding — described in §3.9. The system-raised route carries identical conditions and identical effects: the report must be `ACCEPTED` and `UNSETTLED`, the engagement's `royalty_report_id` must be this report, `booked_amount_cents` goes to 0, `royalty_payable_cents` is re-derived, and both versions increment. A report already `SETTLED` or `REVERSED` is never disputed by an audit finding — but the engagement still reaches its findings state and the compliance penalty still lands.

**Revising.** The `Propagator` that holds the licence returns a disputed report to draft, driving `review_status` `DISPUTED → DRAFT`; the report's `settlement_status` must be `UNSETTLED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the agreement's `propagator_id`. Revision increments `revision_no` by 1, and the fields it clears are exactly specified: `submitted_at`, `accepted_at` and `accepted_by_user_id` return to null, `is_late` returns to false, and `final_total_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `booked_amount_cents` return to 0. `provisional_total_cents` and the existing line items are deliberately **retained**, so the propagator can amend the return by adding lines for further quotas before submitting again. The report's `version` increments by 1. No propagator field is touched and the propagator's `version` does not move, because a `DISPUTED` report was already carrying `booked_amount_cents` of 0 and already contributed nothing to the ledger. The loop `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` may repeat any number of times, and `revision_no` counts the laps.

**Settling and reversing.** A `LicensingOfficer` settles the booked royalty, driving `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED → SETTLED`. Three conditions apply beyond the state itself: `review_status` must be `ACCEPTED`, `booked_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than 0 — a zero booked amount, including one zeroed by a dispute, has nothing to disburse and is rejected with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_SETTLE` (HTTP 422) — and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `accepted_by_user_id`, or the settlement is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Settling records the acting `X-User-ID` in `settled_by_user_id`, re-derives both `royalty_payable_cents` and `royalty_settled_cents` on the propagator at cascade level one, and increments the report's `version` and the propagator's `version` by 1. It does not touch `review_status`.

Reversal is the compensating action. A `LicensingOfficer` reverses a settled report, driving `settlement_status` `SETTLED → REVERSED`, optionally supplying a `reversal_note` — like `dispute_note`, at most 500 characters and persisted nowhere; `review_status` must still be `ACCEPTED`. Reversal clears `settled_by_user_id` back to null, re-derives `royalty_payable_cents` and `royalty_settled_cents` — the reversed report re-enters the payable set rather than dropping out of the ledger — and increments both versions by 1. `REVERSED` is terminal on the settlement dimension.

**The propagator's ledger.** Two fields on the propagator summarize all of this, and both are re-derived by full re-aggregation on every write that touches them, never by incremental delta. Consider every royalty report reached through that propagator's agreements whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED`; `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `DISPUTED` reports are excluded entirely — and a disputed report additionally carries `booked_amount_cents` of 0, so it would contribute nothing even if it were included. Among those accepted reports, `royalty_payable_cents` is the sum of `booked_amount_cents` over the ones whose `settlement_status` is `UNSETTLED` or `REVERSED`, and `royalty_settled_cents` is the sum of `booked_amount_cents` over the ones whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`.

This yields the second conservation invariant, which must hold for every propagator at every observable instant: **`royalty_payable_cents` plus `royalty_settled_cents` always equals the sum of `booked_amount_cents` over that propagator's accepted reports.** Settling moves one report's booked amount from the payable side to the settled side and reversing moves the identical amount back, so `royalty_payable_cents` must return to its exact pre-settlement value after a settle-and-reverse lap. Disputing is emphatically **not** a compensation for settling: it zeroes `booked_amount_cents` and therefore shrinks both sides of the identity at once.

### 3.8. Sublicensing to Nurseries and Retail Programmes

A sublicense grant is the downstream permission a propagator's head licence carries: the right to supply a named nursery or retail programme with a released variety, in one territory, under one jurisdiction's protection.

**Proposing.** A `LicensingOfficer` proposes a grant against the `agreement_id` it hangs off and the `variety_id` being sublicensed, naming the `sublicensee_name` receiving it (1 to 80 characters), the `territory_code` the grant is restricted to (2 to 8 uppercase characters, possibly containing a hyphen), the `jurisdiction_code` whose protection filing must back it (2 to 4 uppercase characters), the `exclusivity` of the grant, the `effective_date` that inclusively begins its window and the `expiry_date` that inclusively ends it, plus `expected_agreement_version` carrying the agreement `version` last observed. `exclusivity` is either `EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE` and defaults to `NON_EXCLUSIVE` when omitted.

The `expected_agreement_version` field is the third stale-view guard of §2.7, and it exists because countersigning, suspending, reinstating and terminating all change what may legitimately be granted; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The window must be well-ordered — `expiry_date` strictly later than `effective_date`, failing which the proposal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — and it must sit inside the licence term, with `effective_date` at or after the agreement's `term_start` and `expiry_date` at or before its `term_end`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`. The `territory_code` must be a member of the agreement's `territory_codes`, exactly as for production quotas, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_LICENSED`. Proposing is otherwise not gated on the agreement's status at all — only on the `version` the guard compares — so a grant may be proposed under an agreement that is still `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, or is `SUSPENDED`, or has already been `TERMINATED`; the `ACTIVE` requirement bites only at activation. The checks fire in the order stale-view guard, then licensed footprint, then the interval rule, then term containment. A new grant is created in `PROPOSED` with null `activated_at` and null `revocation_reason` and `version` 1, and creating it increments the agreement's `version` by 1. Grants are browsable filtered by `agreement_id` to walk an agreement's downstream programmes, by `variety_id` to see a variety's territory coverage, or by `territory_code`, `status` and `exclusivity`.

**Activating.** A `LicensingOfficer` activates a proposed grant, driving `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`. Five business conditions must hold beyond the state itself, and they are evaluated in the order they are given here. The governing agreement must be `ACTIVE`, or the activation is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The grant's `jurisdiction_code` must be a member of the variety's `protected_jurisdictions` — that is, the variety must hold a protection filing in status `GRANTED` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE` for that jurisdiction — because no royalty-bearing sublicense may be activated where the variety carries no enforceable right; failing this it is rejected with `ERR_PROTECTION_NOT_IN_FORCE` (HTTP 422). The variety's `lifecycle_state` must be `RELEASED`, since a variety still in trialling, or already retired, may not be sublicensed to nurseries or retail programmes; failing this it is rejected with `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 422). And the logical clock's present instant must be at or before the grant's `expiry_date`, so a window that has already closed cannot be activated; that failure is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) and is evaluated after the protection and release checks and before the exclusivity check. Of the two coded safety checks the protection check is evaluated first, so a grant failing both is told about the missing right.

The fifth and last condition is exclusivity. No other sublicense grant with the same `variety_id` and the same `territory_code`, in status `ACTIVE`, may occupy an overlapping window when either that grant or the candidate has `exclusivity` `EXCLUSIVE`; the clash is rejected with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The window arithmetic is precise. Each grant's own window is the closed interval running from its `effective_date` to its `expiry_date`, both endpoints included. The already-active grant's interval is then extended by 30 days — the exclusivity buffer — on **both** ends, so its occupied span starts 30 days before its `effective_date` and ends 30 days after its `expiry_date`. Overlap is tested between that buffered span and the candidate's **un-extended** closed interval: the buffer is applied once, to the incumbent, and never to the applicant. Because both intervals are closed, touching endpoints do count as overlap. Two `NON_EXCLUSIVE` grants may overlap freely and never conflict with each other; the check only bites when at least one side of the pair is `EXCLUSIVE`.

Activation stamps `activated_at` with the present instant, re-derives the variety's `active_sublicense_count` — the count of that variety's grants whose status is `ACTIVE` — and increments both the grant's `version` and the variety's `version` by 1.

**Revoking.** A `LicensingOfficer` revokes a grant that is either `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`, moving it to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` `MANUAL`. The two source states differ in one respect only. From `ACTIVE`, the variety's `active_sublicense_count` is re-derived and drops, and both the grant's `version` and the variety's `version` increment by 1. From `PROPOSED`, `active_sublicense_count` is unchanged because a proposed grant was never counted in it, so only the grant's `version` increments; the variety's `version` increments only when the count actually changed.

**The two system routes out.** A grant can also leave `ACTIVE` without anyone acting on it. When a protection filing transitions into `LAPSED` and its `jurisdiction_code` matches the grant's, so that the jurisdiction drops out of the variety's `protected_jurisdictions`, the system revokes the grant with `revocation_reason` `PROTECTION_LAPSED`, re-derives `active_sublicense_count`, and increments the grant's and the variety's `version` by 1 — the enforcement right vanished, so the downstream permission cannot stand. And when the logical clock is advanced past a grant's `expiry_date`, the sweep of §2.5 moves the grant from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, re-derives `active_sublicense_count`, and increments both versions. Only grants strictly past their `expiry_date` expire; one whose `expiry_date` equals the new clock value stays `ACTIVE`, matching the closed upper bound of the interval. Both `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

`active_sublicense_count` is what makes retirement of a variety conditional: as §3.4 states, a variety may not be retired while that count is above 0, and grants must be revoked or allowed to expire first.

### 3.9. Enforcement: Surveillance, Genetic Testing, Audits and the Damages Waterfall

Enforcement runs on two parallel tracks, both driven by the `ComplianceAuditor`. Surveillance chases unauthorized propagation observed in the market; audit engagements re-examine royalty reports the licensor has already accepted. Both feed the compliance score, and both can suspend a licence.

**Opening a surveillance case.** A `ComplianceAuditor` opens a case against the `variety_id` suspected of being propagated without authority, citing the `market_survey_ref` of the market survey that raised the suspicion (1 to 40 characters), and optionally naming a `suspected_propagator_id`. That last field is genuinely optional, and its absence is meaningful: a null `suspected_propagator_id` denotes an unlicensed third party, and it changes what the case can do downstream — with no named suspect there is no propagator to penalize and none to credit. A new case starts in `OPENED` with `sampled_unit_count` 0, `max_match_confidence` 0.0000, `recovered_damages_cents`, `enforcement_cost_cents`, `enforcement_recovery_cents`, `breeder_pool_cents` and `propagator_credit_cents` all 0, null `concluded_at`, and `version` 1. Cases are browsable filtered by `variety_id`, by `suspected_propagator_id` to walk one propagator's enforcement exposure, or by `status`.

**Sampling.** The auditor records the sample collection, driving `OPENED → SAMPLED`, supplying `sampled_unit_count` — the number of plants sampled in the market survey, a **required** field that must be at least 1, so omitting it is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The transition stores the count and increments the case's `version` by 1.

**Genetic testing.** The auditor records laboratory results against the case's samples, each carrying the `lab_code` of the accredited laboratory that ran the panel (2 to 12 uppercase characters), the `marker_panel` identifier used (2 to 16 characters, for example `SSR-24`), and the `match_confidence` — the probability that the sample is genetically identical to the protected variety, a number from 0 to 1 inclusive carrying at most 4 decimal places, which the server normalizes to exactly 4 decimal places on storage.

A genetic test is an immutable laboratory record and is the one entity in this system that departs from the common field set of §2.4: it carries `id` and `created_at` but has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update or delete capability whatsoever. Its `result` is derived by the server, never supplied: it is `MATCH` when `match_confidence` is greater than or equal to 0.9000, and `NO_MATCH` otherwise. The threshold boundary is **inclusive** — a confidence of exactly 0.9000 is a `MATCH`.

Recording a test re-derives the case's `max_match_confidence` as the highest `match_confidence` among all of that case's tests, and increments the case's `version` by 1. The **first** test recorded on a case also drives the case `SAMPLED → TESTING`; every subsequent test leaves the status at `TESTING` while still refreshing `max_match_confidence`. Tests are browsable filtered by `case_id` to read a case's full marker-test record, by `result`, or by `lab_code`.

**Concluding testing: a server-chosen fork.** The auditor closes out testing, and the server — not the caller — decides which of two states the case lands in. There is no branch selector in the payload; the server reads `max_match_confidence` against the same 0.9000 threshold, inclusive at exactly 0.9000. When `max_match_confidence` is 0.9000 or higher, the case moves `TESTING → MATCH_CONFIRMED`; when it is strictly below 0.9000, the case moves `TESTING → NO_MATCH`. Both branches stamp `concluded_at` with the logical clock's present instant and increment the case's `version` by 1.

What separates the two branches is the compliance cascade, and it is present on exactly one side. On the `MATCH_CONFIRMED` branch, and only when the case names a `suspected_propagator_id`, a `CONFIRMED_UNAUTH_MATCH` penalty event materializes at `concluded_at` and a three-level cascade runs: level one recomputes that propagator's `compliance_score` and `lifetime_compliance_score`; level two, caused by a recomputed `compliance_score` strictly below 70, moves the propagator's `ACTIVE` agreements to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BREACH`; level three, caused by each suspension, moves that agreement's `ACTIVE` quotas to `FROZEN`. The `version` of every mutated propagator, agreement and quota increments by 1. A confirmed match against an unnamed suspect penalizes nobody — there is no propagator record to charge. On the `NO_MATCH` branch no penalty event materializes and no propagator field changes at all: the divergent side-effect set of that branch is precisely the absence of the cascade.

**Resolving and the damages waterfall.** The auditor resolves the case from either `MATCH_CONFIRMED` or `NO_MATCH` — both are legitimate sources for `MATCH_CONFIRMED → RESOLVED` and `NO_MATCH → RESOLVED` — supplying `recovered_damages_cents`, the damages actually recovered, and `enforcement_cost_cents`, the documented cost of enforcement, each a non-negative integer. `RESOLVED` is the terminal state of the case machine.

Resolution allocates the recovery across three buckets in a strict waterfall, in integer cents throughout. The first bucket, `enforcement_recovery_cents`, fills first and is the smaller of `recovered_damages_cents` and `enforcement_cost_cents` — enforcement cost is recovered before anything else, but never beyond what actually came in. Subtract it from `recovered_damages_cents` to get the first remainder. The second bucket, `breeder_pool_cents`, is the breeder royalty pool's share, and it is bounded by two caps that interact: a target computed as `recovered_damages_cents` multiplied by 0.70 and rounded half-up to whole cents, and the first remainder itself. `breeder_pool_cents` is the smaller of those two. Note carefully that the 70 percent target is a share of the **gross** recovery, not of the remainder. Subtract the second bucket from the first remainder to get the second remainder, and the third bucket, `propagator_credit_cents`, is exactly that remainder — it absorbs every rounding residue so the allocation is exact.

The waterfall therefore satisfies its own conservation rule: **`enforcement_recovery_cents` plus `breeder_pool_cents` plus `propagator_credit_cents` always equals `recovered_damages_cents` exactly.** No bucket is ever negative. When enforcement costs exceed the recovery, the first bucket takes the entire recovery, the first remainder is 0, and the second and third buckets are both 0 — the enforcement cost is simply only partly recovered. Each resolution allocates only its own `recovered_damages_cents`; figures are never pooled across cases.

When the case names a `suspected_propagator_id`, `propagator_credit_cents` is added to that propagator's `damages_credit_cents` and the propagator's `version` increments by 1; with no named suspect no credit is applied and no propagator is touched. The case's `version` increments by 1 either way. Resolution from `NO_MATCH` runs the identical waterfall on whatever `recovered_damages_cents` the payload carries, which is 0 in the common case.

**Opening an audit engagement.** A `ComplianceAuditor` opens a royalty audit under the agreement's contractual audit rights, naming the `agreement_id` whose rights are being exercised and the `royalty_report_id` of the accepted report under audit. The two must belong together: the report's `agreement_id` must equal the supplied `agreement_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_ENGAGEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). That pairing check is the only cross-record condition: opening an engagement is not gated on the report's `review_status` or `settlement_status` — a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `SETTLED` or `REVERSED` report may all be put under audit — and it is not gated on the agreement's status either. The `agreement_id` is resolved before the `royalty_report_id`. A new engagement starts in `OPENED` with `underreported_units` 0, null `finding_severity`, null `finalized_at` and `finalized_by_user_id`, and `version` 1. Engagements are browsable filtered by `agreement_id` to walk an agreement's audit history, by `royalty_report_id` to see every audit of one report, or by `status` and `finding_severity`.

**Finalizing: a caller-selected fork.** The auditor finalizes the engagement, supplying `finding_severity` and optionally `underreported_units` — the units the audit determined were propagated beyond the report's declaration, a non-negative integer. Unlike the surveillance fork, this one is selected by the payload, and the two branches are terminal in opposite directions.

When `finding_severity` is `NONE`, the engagement moves `OPENED → CLOSED_NO_FINDING`, with `finding_severity` recorded as `NONE` and `underreported_units` forced to 0. No penalty event materializes; the audited report keeps its `review_status` of `ACCEPTED` and keeps its `booked_amount_cents` untouched. Only the engagement's `version` increments by 1. The divergent side-effect set of this branch is exactly the absence of the dispute and compliance cascades.

When `finding_severity` is `MINOR` or `MATERIAL`, the engagement moves `OPENED → FINDINGS_ISSUED`, and `underreported_units` must be at least 1 — a finding with no underreported units is not a finding. Because that is a payload-only constraint it is judged in the body tier of the §2.8 ladder, *ahead of* resolving `engagement_id`: a `MINOR` or `MATERIAL` finalize that omits `underreported_units` or sends 0 answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) even when the `engagement_id` in the path does not exist and even when the engagement is already terminal. Both branches stamp `finalized_at` with the present instant and record the acting `X-User-ID` in `finalized_by_user_id`.

Issuing findings then runs a cascade with several independent limbs. When and only when `finding_severity` is `MATERIAL`, the propagator's `material_audit_finding_count` is re-derived — the field that blocks reinstatement in §3.5. Independently of severity within the findings branch, when the audited report is still `ACCEPTED` and still `UNSETTLED` it moves to `DISPUTED` with `booked_amount_cents` set to 0, and that un-booking in turn causes the propagator's `royalty_payable_cents` to be re-derived; a report already `SETTLED` or `REVERSED` is left alone, though the engagement still reaches `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and the penalty still lands. The `MINOR_AUDIT_FINDING` or `MATERIAL_AUDIT_FINDING` penalty event then causes the propagator's `compliance_score` and `lifetime_compliance_score` to be recomputed.

Suspension follows, and the two severities differ in how they reach it. A `MATERIAL` finding suspends the agreement outright, moving it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `AUDIT_FINDING`, regardless of what the recomputed score is. A `MINOR` finding suspends only when the recomputed `compliance_score` falls strictly below 70, and in that case `suspension_reason` is `COMPLIANCE_SCORE_BREACH`. Either way the suspension then freezes that agreement's `ACTIVE` quotas. The `version` of the engagement and of every report, propagator, agreement and quota the cascade actually mutated each increment by 1. Both `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and `CLOSED_NO_FINDING` are terminal: an engagement is finalized exactly once.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All paths below are relative to the API root. Creation endpoints — those that bring a new record into existence — respond with HTTP 201; every other successful call responds with HTTP 200, including all of the state-transition endpoints, which return the updated resource. Every list endpoint accepts the `limit` and `offset` query parameters of §2.3 alongside the filters named in its description, and every list endpoint is readable by all five roles. Path parameters are resolved left to right as described in §2.8, and a trailing slash on any path is tolerated as equivalent to its absence. The state-transition endpoints that §3 does not describe as carrying a payload accept **no body fields whatsoever** — `file`, `grant`, `abandon`, `lapse`, `release`, `retire`, `brand-abandon`, `plant`, `certify`, `countersign`, `suspend`, `reinstate`, `terminate`, `issue`, `void`, `submit`, `accept`, `revise`, `settle`, `activate`, `revoke` and `conclude-testing` — so sending any field at all to one of them is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at rung 3 of the ladder, while an absent, empty or `{}` body is accepted. `GET` requests ignore any body entirely. Alongside the table there is one unauthenticated liveness probe, `GET /health`, which requires neither header and answers HTTP 200 with the bare object `{"status": "ok"}` outside the §2.2 envelope; it is the only endpoint in the service that is not wrapped by the envelope and the only one exempt from §2.1.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every error response uses the envelope of §2.2, carrying the `error_id` from the table below in its `data` object. The order in which competing errors are chosen is governed entirely by the rejection ladder of §2.8: authentication before role, role before schema, schema before ownership, ownership before existence, existence before stale-view rejection, stale-view rejection before state, and state before every business rule. The table names only each identifier and the HTTP status it is returned with; the conditions that raise each one are stated in §2 and §3. Three transport-level identifiers sit outside the business catalogue and are raised by the framework rather than by any rule below: a request to a path that matches no route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a request to a routed path with an unsupported method answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unhandled server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). All three still use the §2.2 error envelope, and all three are decided before authentication, so they surface even without the two identity headers.
