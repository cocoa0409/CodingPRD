# Coupon Clearinghouse & Redemption Settlement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A coupon clearinghouse sits between two parties who never speak directly: the manufacturers who print offers and the retailers who accept them at the register. The manufacturer wants to reimburse only the coupons it actually authorised — the right offer, inside the right dates, backed by a real qualifying purchase, and never the same one-time-use serial twice. The retailer wants to be paid quickly for a shipment of paper it has already handed over, and wants a way to appeal when a clerk's judgement goes against it. Between those two positions sits a settlement ledger that must balance to the cent, survive dispute reversals and funding claw-backs, and produce an auditable answer to the question "who was paid, out of whose money, on what evidence?"

This document specifies the backend service that runs that clearinghouse. The service registers coupon designs and mints the barcode payload that encodes each offer's terms; takes in retailer submissions through both the mailroom and the electronic channel and verifies the counts the retailer declared; sorts each shipment into per-manufacturer, per-offer lots and captures coupon images; adjudicates every captured coupon against the offer's terms through a fixed, strictly ordered ladder of checks — counterfeit, expiry, duplicate serial, purchase-data misredemption — and then values and prices each lot. Downstream of adjudication it scores retailers for fraud, opens and resolves disputes over rejected lots, issues retailer settlement statements with a risk-sensitive holdback, bills manufacturers, tracks their funding, and clears digital coupons by matching redemption events against consumer clips.

Two properties are non-negotiable and shape almost every rule below. The first is **determinism**: every sweep, every cascade and every tie-break is ordered explicitly, and the server runs on a settable logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so a given sequence of requests always produces byte-identical money. The second is **segregation of duties**: the analyst who condemned a lot may not be the analyst who hears the appeal against it, and the officer who issued a statement may not be the officer who releases its cash. Both walls are enforced in the request path, not by convention.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is missing or blank, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint listed in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Manufacturer`, `Retailer`, `IntakeClerk`, `FraudAnalyst`, `SettlementOfficer` and `ClearinghouseAdmin`.

- A `Manufacturer` is the coupon issuer. It designs and registers offers, receives invoices and wires funding.
- A `Retailer` is the submitting retailer or banner. It sends coupons in for clearing, supplies point-of-sale data, appeals rejections and reports digital redemptions.
- An `IntakeClerk` is the mailroom and electronic-intake operator: opens submissions, performs count verification, sorts coupons into manufacturer and offer lots, and captures images.
- A `FraudAnalyst` is the validation and anti-fraud operator: adjudicates individual coupons against offer terms, finalizes lots, rescores retailers and decides disputes.
- A `SettlementOfficer` is the money operator: issues retailer statements, schedules payments, releases them, and bills manufacturers.
- A `ClearinghouseAdmin` administers the clearinghouse: onboards and suspends both sides of the market, activates offers, and performs the compensating funding reversal.

Two of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the caller's `X-User-ID` is itself the primary key of a stored row. When `X-User-Role` is `Manufacturer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Manufacturer; ownership checks on any resource that references a Manufacturer compare the resource's `manufacturer_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Retailer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Retailer; ownership checks on any resource that references a Retailer compare the resource's `retailer_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Retailer` may only act on the submissions, submission lots, coupon items, purchase-data records, disputes and digital redemption events whose owning retailer is itself, and a `Manufacturer` may only act on the offers and manufacturer invoices whose owning manufacturer is itself. A violation is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeClerk`, `FraudAnalyst`, `SettlementOfficer` or `ClearinghouseAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque staff identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a submission lot, as `issued_by_user_id` or `released_by_user_id` on a settlement statement, or as `resolved_by_user_id` on a dispute — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and these four roles are therefore exempt from the ownership check. Recording those identifiers is what makes segregation of duties enforceable: an operator identifier stored by one action becomes a bar on a later action, and a breach of either wall is rejected with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

`ClearinghouseAdmin` is deliberately **not** a hierarchical override. It cannot verify counts, sort lots, capture images, adjudicate items, finalize lots, rescore retailers, resolve disputes, issue statements, schedule or release payments, or fund an invoice; those capabilities remain exclusive to `IntakeClerk`, `FraudAnalyst`, `SettlementOfficer` and `Manufacturer` respectively. Conversely, onboarding manufacturers and retailers, suspending and reinstating them, activating offers and reversing invoice funding are exclusive to `ClearinghouseAdmin`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Both are echoed back in `meta`, alongside `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is still below `total_count`).

Unless a listing endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two listings are documented exceptions and return newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending: the manufacturer listing and the retailer listing.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no domain-flavoured encoding. Clients never supply an `id`, and no endpoint accepts one in a request body. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Whenever a rule below says a field is written, recomputed or cleared on a row, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed — including when the row is touched only as the secondary target of a cascade, never directly by the caller.

`PurchaseDataRecord` is the single exception. A point-of-sale basket is immutable: once ingested it is never updated, there is no update or delete endpoint for it, and the entity carries **only** `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `PurchaseDataRecord` payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Its Sweeps

The server does not read wall-clock time. It keeps a **logical clock** whose value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real time before the clock has first been set. Every date and instant the system derives — adjudication timestamps, filing instants, clip dates, payment schedules, expiry comparisons — is taken from that clock. The clock is advanced through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant and is the only endpoint exempt from the authentication and role checks of §2.1. The clock is strictly monotonic: a requested `current_utc` that is less than or equal to the stored instant is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), so the clock never moves backward and never repeats an instant.

A successful clock advance runs three deterministic sweeps, in exactly this order, and reports how many rows each one touched.

1. **Offer expiry.** Every `Offer` whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose `expiration_date` is strictly earlier than the new clock's calendar date moves from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The count is returned as `expired_offer_count`.
2. **Digital-clip expiry.** Every `DigitalClip` whose status is `CLIPPED` and whose `clip_expires_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock's calendar date moves from `CLIPPED` to `EXPIRED_UNUSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The count is returned as `expired_clip_count`.
3. **Dispute SLA withdrawal.** Every `Dispute` whose status is `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, and whose filing date plus the dispute service-level allowance of 30 days is strictly earlier than the new clock's calendar date, moves to `WITHDRAWN` and returns its lot to `LOT_REJECTED`; processed in `created_at` ascending order. The count is returned as `withdrawn_dispute_count`.

The response body also echoes the newly stored `current_utc`.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal. All percentage and rate fields are decimals. Wherever rounding is required, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and never bankers' rounding or truncation.

The number of decimal places is fixed per field family. The three retailer component rates (`duplicate_rate`, `misredemption_rate`, `counterfeit_rate`), the three fraud scores (`fraud_score_rolling_window`, `fraud_score_lifetime_weighted`, `fraud_score`) and the intake variance `count_variance_pct` all carry 2 decimal places. The statement holdback percentage `holdback_pct` carries 4 decimal places. Extrapolated unit-accept rates used during lot valuation are computed to 4 decimal places.

Two ordering rules about rounding are load-bearing and appear again in the workflows that use them. First, when a rate is rounded and then compared against a tolerance, the rounding happens **before** the comparison. Second, when per-lot money is summed to a submission or statement total, each lot's own amount is rounded to whole cents **first** and the totals are then plain integer sums of already-rounded lot amounts — rounding once over a combined base is explicitly wrong and produces different cents.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations are optimistically concurrent on a parent row, and both take a mandatory version field in the request body. Neither field is optional, and no other endpoint in the system accepts a version field of any kind.

**Issuing a settlement statement** requires `expected_submission_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of the target `Submission` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the submission's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBMISSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a closed submission legitimately accepts a fresh statement that supersedes an earlier `DRAFT` one, so no state precondition stands in the way; the stale-version check is the only thing that stops an officer from settling a submission whose money fields have since been rewritten by a dispute reinstatement or an offer-withdrawal cascade. Note that a successful issuance itself increments `Submission.version` by 1, so a client that wants to issue twice must re-read the submission in between.

**Re-running a retailer fraud score** requires `expected_retailer_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of the target `Retailer` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the retailer's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RETAILER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A rescore is idempotent under unchanged data and carries no state precondition, so nothing else prevents an analyst from writing a score derived from a retailer snapshot that a submission-close cascade has already superseded.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Ordering

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. Where a cascade iterates over a set of rows, the iteration order is always `created_at` ascending on the entity being swept — offers, submission lots, coupon items, settlement statements, digital clips and disputes alike — so that counters, tie-breaks and resulting money are reproducible. Cascades are described hop by hop in §3; every hop is mandatory, and a partially applied cascade is never an acceptable outcome. A cascade that recomputes a derived field always recomputes it from the current stored rows rather than adjusting it incrementally, and it bumps the `version` and refreshes the `updated_at` of every row it touches.

Bulk request bodies follow the same discipline in the opposite direction: a bulk image-capture call either creates every element or creates none, and is refused as a whole if any element is invalid.

### 2.9. Ledger Identities

Three conservation identities hold at all times and are the fastest way to detect a broken cascade.

`Manufacturer.funding_balance_cents` always equals `funded_total_cents` minus `disbursed_total_cents`. `funded_total_cents` is the sum of `funded_amount_cents` over that manufacturer's invoices whose status is `FUNDED`, `SHORT_FUNDED` or `RECONCILED`; `disbursed_total_cents` is the sum of `net_payable_cents` over that manufacturer's linked settlement statements whose status is `PAID`. The balance is never negative by construction, because a statement's `net_payable_cents` can never exceed its invoice's `billed_total_cents`, so disbursement never overtakes funding.

`Retailer.unpaid_balance_cents` is the sum of `net_payable_cents` over that retailer's settlement statements whose status is `SCHEDULED` — and only `SCHEDULED`. A statement moved to `HELD` is not payable and therefore drops out of the balance; a statement that has reached `PAID` has already left it.

`Submission.adjudicated_face_value_cents` is the sum, over that submission's coupon items whose status is anything other than `CAPTURED`, of the item's offer face value multiplied by the item's `claimed_units`. It is the conserved quantity of adjudication: reinstating rejected items after an overturned dispute shifts value between `accepted_item_face_value_cents` and `rejected_item_face_value_cents` but leaves `adjudicated_face_value_cents` numerically unchanged.

### 2.10. Read Access, Filters and Nested Creation Paths

Every entity is addressed at its own top-level collection, and parentage is expressed through foreign-key fields in bodies and query strings rather than through nested read paths. The only nested paths in the system are two creation-under-context shortcuts — creating a lot under a submission, and capturing items under a lot — and the rows they create are addressed flatly afterwards. Path parameters are validated left to right: the `{id}` segment must resolve to an existing row of that collection before the body is examined, and an unresolvable segment yields the not-found code for that collection (§2.11).

Listing endpoints expose the reverse-reachability filters that make every foreign key discoverable, alongside `limit` and `offset`. Manufacturers may be filtered by `status`. Retailers may be filtered by `status` and `handling_fee_tier`. Offers may be filtered by `manufacturer_id`, `status` and `digital_enabled`. Submissions may be filtered by `retailer_id`, `intake_status` and `settlement_status` — the two dimensions are filterable independently. Submission lots may be filtered by `submission_id`, `offer_id`, `manufacturer_id` and `status`. Coupon items may be filtered by `lot_id`, `status`, `reject_code`, `duplicate_of_item_id` and `matched_purchase_record_id`. Purchase-data records may be filtered by `retailer_id`, `offer_id` and `pos_transaction_ref`. Settlement statements may be filtered by `submission_id`, `retailer_id`, `manufacturer_invoice_id` and `status`. Manufacturer invoices may be filtered by `manufacturer_id` and `status`. Disputes may be filtered by `lot_id`, `retailer_id` and `status`. Digital clips may be filtered by `offer_id`, `consumer_ref`, `status` and `matched_event_id`. Digital redemption events may be filtered by `offer_id`, `retailer_id`, `consumer_ref`, `status` and `matched_clip_id`.

One read is computed rather than stored. A submission valuation read recomputes that submission's face value **both** ways from the same stored rows — once forcing every lot through the full-count valuation variant and once forcing every lot through sampled extrapolation — and returns the two totals side by side without writing anything. Its response body carries `submission_id`, `valuation_method`, `full_count_gross_face_value_cents`, `sampled_gross_face_value_cents`, `settled_gross_face_value_cents`, `settled_handling_fee_cents` and `lot_count`.

### 2.11. The Validation Ladder

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract: the **first** failing check decides the response, and later checks are not evaluated. The ordering reflects clearinghouse priorities — identity and authority first, then referential integrity, then the anti-fraud and regulatory-safety checks that protect manufacturers from paying for coupons they never authorised, then temporal and contractual windows, then ordinary constraint checks, and finally the fiscal checks, which are the cheapest to retry and therefore lose every conflict. The ladder is as follows, from highest precedence to lowest.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-blank and the role is one of the six names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint except the clock endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint except the clock endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** The `{id}` path segment resolves to an existing row of its own collection. This level holds thirteen sibling rules, one per addressable collection, each with its own code: `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the manufacturer read and suspend routes; `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the retailer read, suspend, reinstate and fraud-rescore routes; `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the offer read, register, activate and withdraw routes; `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the submission read, submission-valuation, verify-count and lot-creation routes; `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the lot read, item-capture and finalize routes; `ERR_COUPON_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the coupon-item read and adjudicate routes; `ERR_PURCHASE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the purchase-data-record read route; `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the statement read, schedule and release-payment routes; `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the invoice read, fund and reverse-funding routes; `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the dispute read, request-info, resubmit and resolve routes; `ERR_CLIP_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the digital-clip read route; and `ERR_REDEMPTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the digital-redemption-event read route.
4. **Resource ownership.** An entity-backed caller acts only on its own rows, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Retailer` or `Manufacturer`; the four opaque staff roles are exempt.
5. **Request shape.** The body parses as JSON; every required field for that endpoint is present and non-null; no unknown field is present; every value matches its declared type; every enum value is in range; every integer is within its declared bounds; every date parses as an ISO 8601 value; and no computed or system-owned field is supplied by the caller. A failure at this level is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
6. **Body foreign keys.** Six sibling rules, each resolving one body reference: `manufacturer_id` on offer creation, lot creation and invoice creation, otherwise `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND` (404); `retailer_id` on submission creation, purchase-record ingest and digital-redemption reporting, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND` (404); `offer_id` on lot creation, purchase-record ingest, clip creation and digital-redemption reporting, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` (404); `submission_id` on statement issuance, otherwise `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` (404); `lot_id` on dispute filing, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` (404); and every element of the `statement_ids` array on invoice creation, checked in array order, otherwise `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_submission_version` on statement issuance must equal the submission's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUBMISSION_VERSION` (412); `expected_retailer_version` on fraud rescoring must equal the retailer's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_RETAILER_VERSION` (412). The two rules apply to disjoint endpoints.
8. **Own-entity state.** The target row's current state admits the requested transition, judged against that entity's own transition table only — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). This covers every from-state guard in the system: registering an offer that is not `DRAFT`, activating an offer that is not `REGISTERED`, withdrawing an already-`WITHDRAWN` offer, verifying the count of a submission whose `intake_status` is not `RECEIVED`, adding lots to a submission whose `intake_status` is not `COUNT_VERIFIED`, capturing items into a lot that is not `SORTED`, adjudicating an item that is not `CAPTURED`, finalizing a lot that is not `SORTED`, scheduling a statement that is not `DRAFT`, releasing a statement that is not `SCHEDULED`, funding an invoice that is neither `ISSUED` nor `SHORT_FUNDED`, reversing an invoice that is neither `FUNDED` nor `SHORT_FUNDED`, requesting information on a dispute that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, resubmitting a dispute that is not `INFO_REQUESTED`, resolving a dispute that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, and every attempt to move a row out of a terminal state or to repeat an already-applied trigger.
9. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the disputed lot's `adjudicated_by_user_id` when resolving a dispute over that lot, and must not equal the statement's `issued_by_user_id` when releasing that statement's payment — otherwise `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (403). Because this level outranks every cross-entity readiness check below it, an analyst who tries to resolve their own adjudication receives the segregation-of-duties error even when the dispute is unresolvable for other reasons as well.
10. **Retailer standing.** The retailer owning the affected submission chain must not have status `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_SUSPENDED` (403). This rule guards seven operations: submission creation, count verification, lot creation, item capture, lot finalization, dispute filing and digital-redemption reporting.
11. **Manufacturer standing.** The manufacturer owning the affected offer or invoice must not have status `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED` (403). This rule guards six operations: offer creation, offer registration, offer activation, lot creation, invoice creation and invoice funding.
12. **Sort-bucket referential agreement.** On lot creation, the body's `manufacturer_id` must equal the offer's own `manufacturer_id` — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_MANUFACTURER_MISMATCH` (422).
13. **Cross-entity readiness.** Four sibling rules over disjoint endpoint sets, so they can never conflict with one another. On lot creation, the offer's status must be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` — a `DRAFT`, `REGISTERED` or `WITHDRAWN` offer is not clearable — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_CLEARABLE` (422); because this sits above the grace-window check at level 20, a lot sorted against a withdrawn offer past its grace period reports the offer problem, not the date problem. On statement scheduling, no dispute in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` may reference any lot of that statement's submission — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN` (409). On payment release, the statement's `manufacturer_invoice_id` must be non-null and that invoice's status must be `FUNDED` — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FUNDED` (422); this outranks the pay-date check at level 35 and is itself outranked by the segregation-of-duties wall at level 9. On funding reversal, no statement billed onto the invoice may have status `PAID` — otherwise `ERR_STATEMENT_ALREADY_PAID` (409).
14. **Statement billability.** On invoice creation, every statement named in `statement_ids` must have status `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, must have a null `manufacturer_invoice_id`, and must belong to a submission whose `VALIDATED` and `REINSTATED` lots all carry the invoice's `manufacturer_id` — otherwise `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_BILLABLE` (422).
15. **Digital eligibility and activation dating.** Two sibling rules over disjoint endpoints. On clip creation and on digital-redemption reporting, the offer's `digital_enabled` must be true — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_DIGITAL` (422). On offer activation, the offer's `expiration_date` must be on or after the logical clock's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (422); an offer whose redemption date has already passed is refused outright rather than becoming an `ACTIVE` offer that the next sweep would immediately expire.
16. **Clock monotonicity.** `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422) on the clock endpoint, as described in §2.5.
17. **Postmark-window contention.** `ERR_POSTMARK_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) on submission creation, as described in §3.3.
18. **Billing-period buffer.** `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409) on invoice creation, as described in §3.8.
19. **Clip concurrency.** `ERR_CLIP_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT` (409) on clip creation, as described in §3.9.
20. **Redemption grace window.** `ERR_SUBMISSION_PAST_GRACE` (422) on lot creation, as described in §3.3.
21. **Dispute window.** `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422) on dispute filing, as described in §3.7.
22. **Uniqueness.** Two sibling rules over disjoint endpoints: `offer_code` must be unique within its manufacturer on offer creation, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_OFFER_CODE` (409); and `gs1_company_prefix` must be globally unique on manufacturer creation, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_GS1_PREFIX` (409).
23. **Channel capability.** `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_SUPPORTED` (422) on submission creation, as described in §3.3.
24. **Something to settle.** `ERR_EMPTY_SUBMISSION` (422) on statement issuance, as described in §3.8.
25. **Count variance tolerance.** `ERR_COUNT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (422) on count verification, as described in §3.3.
26. **Lot count overflow.** `ERR_LOT_COUNT_OVERFLOW` (422) on lot creation, as described in §3.3.
27. **Mandatory imaging.** `ERR_IMAGE_REQUIRED` (422) on item capture, as described in §3.3.
28. **Mandatory register reference on the electronic channel.** `ERR_POS_REF_REQUIRED` (422) on item capture, as described in §3.3.
29. **Capture ceiling.** `ERR_LOT_CAPTURE_COMPLETE` (409) on item capture, as described in §3.3.
30. **Sample completeness.** `ERR_SAMPLE_INCOMPLETE` (422) on lot finalization, as described in §3.5.
31. **Information-round cap.** `ERR_INFO_ROUND_LIMIT` (422) on dispute information requests, as described in §3.7.
32. **Invoice line integrity.** `ERR_STATEMENT_LINES_EMPTY` (422) on invoice creation when `statement_ids` is empty or contains a repeated element.
33. **Redemption ceiling.** `ERR_MAX_REDEMPTIONS_EXCEEDED` (422) on lot finalization and on digital-redemption reporting, as described in §3.5 and §3.9.
34. **Overfunding.** `ERR_FUNDING_EXCEEDS_BILLED` (422) on invoice funding. Because this loses to the state check at level 8, funding a `RECONCILED` invoice with an overfunding amount returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not the overfunding code.
35. **Pay-date arrival.** `ERR_PAY_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (422) on payment release, as described in §3.8.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Market Onboarding, Standing and Administrative Gates

Onboarding a coupon issuer is the first act in the system. The administrator supplies the issuer's registered `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters and never blank — together with its `gs1_company_prefix`, which must be exactly six characters, all digits, and which no other issuer may already hold; two issuers sharing a prefix would mint colliding barcode payloads, which is why the collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GS1_PREFIX` rather than tolerated. Two commercial terms may be supplied at the same time and both have defaults. `handling_fee_policy` selects which per-coupon fee basis the clearinghouse will apply to that issuer's lots and is either `CONTRACT_RATE` or `TIERED_RATE`, defaulting to `CONTRACT_RATE`. `dispute_window_days` sets how many days after a lot's rejection the retailer may still appeal, must be at least 1 and at most 180, and defaults to 45. A newly created issuer is `ACTIVE` and its three ledger fields — `funded_total_cents`, `disbursed_total_cents` and `funding_balance_cents` — all start at 0.

Suspending an issuer is terminal. The administrator supplies a `reason`, the issuer moves `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`, and from that point it may no longer have offers created, registered or activated, may not have new lots sorted against its offers, may not be billed a new invoice and may not fund an existing one — every such attempt is refused with `ERR_MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED`. Invoices already issued survive untouched. Suspension is not, however, a passive gate: as a side effect, every offer of that issuer whose status is `DRAFT`, `REGISTERED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` set to the literal value `MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED`, processed in offer `created_at` ascending order, and each of those withdrawals runs the full offer-withdrawal cascade described in §3.2. Attempting to suspend an issuer that is already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Retailer onboarding takes a `legal_name` under the same 1-to-200-character rule and four optional commercial settings. `channel_capability` records which intake channels the retailer is contracted for and is `MAIL_ONLY`, `ELECTRONIC_ONLY` or `BOTH`, defaulting to `BOTH`. `payment_terms` is `NET_15`, `NET_30` or `NET_45`, defaulting to `NET_30`, and drives payment scheduling. `base_fee_tier` is the contractual handling-fee tier, either `PREFERRED` or `STANDARD` and defaulting to `STANDARD`; it is the tier the retailer returns to whenever its fraud score falls back under the watchlist line. `fraud_scoring_mode` chooses which of the two fraud-score variants the clearinghouse treats as authoritative for this retailer and is either `ROLLING_WINDOW` or `LIFETIME_WEIGHTED`, defaulting to `ROLLING_WINDOW`. A newly created retailer is `ACTIVE`, its effective `handling_fee_tier` is initialised from `base_fee_tier`, all three fraud scores (`fraud_score_rolling_window`, `fraud_score_lifetime_weighted` and `fraud_score`) are `0.00`, all three component rates (`duplicate_rate`, `misredemption_rate` and `counterfeit_rate`) are `0.00`, `rejected_submission_count` and `unpaid_balance_cents` are 0, and `last_rescored_at` is null until the first rescore writes a logical-clock instant into it.

A retailer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `WATCHLIST` or `SUSPENDED`, and unlike an issuer's it is not terminal in either direction. Administrative suspension takes a `reason` and applies from `ACTIVE` or `WATCHLIST`, driving the retailer to `SUSPENDED` and forcing its effective `handling_fee_tier` to `PROBATION`; it deliberately does not touch `fraud_score`, so the next rescore can re-derive the gate from evidence rather than from the administrator's judgement. Reinstatement, also taking a `reason`, is available only from `SUSPENDED`: the retailer returns to `ACTIVE` and its `handling_fee_tier` is restored to `base_fee_tier`, again leaving `fraud_score` untouched — which means the very next rescore may push it straight back to `WATCHLIST` or `SUSPENDED`. The pair closes an `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` administrative cycle that a retailer may traverse any number of times. While a retailer is `SUSPENDED`, seven operations are blocked with `ERR_RETAILER_SUSPENDED`: opening a submission, verifying its count, sorting it into lots, capturing images into a lot, finalizing a lot, filing a dispute and reporting a digital redemption.

### 3.2. Coupon Design, Barcode Minting and the Offer Lifecycle

An offer is the coupon design: the terms the manufacturer promises to honour. Creating one requires the issuing `manufacturer_id`, an `offer_code` of 1 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase alphanumerics and the hyphen and unique within that issuer (compared case-sensitively against every offer of that issuer, **including** ones already `WITHDRAWN`, so a code is never recycled), a `face_value_cents` between 1 and 100000 that is what the manufacturer reimburses per cleared unit, a `handling_fee_cents` between 0 and 1000 that is the contractual per-unit fee basis, an `expiration_date` as a calendar date, a `redemption_instructions` free-text block of 1 to 500 characters carrying the retailer-facing clearing instructions printed on the coupon, a `barcode_family` of either `GS1_DATABAR` or `UPC_A_WITH_EXT5`, and a `max_redemptions` between 1 and 100000000 that ceilings the cumulative cleared units across every lot and every digital redemption of the offer. Four further inputs are optional: `purchase_requirement_units`, the qualifying units a shopper must buy per cleared unit, at least 1 and at most 24 and defaulting to 1; `redemption_grace_days`, the number of days after expiry during which a retailer may still open a lot against the offer, from 0 to 365 and defaulting to 30; `digital_enabled`, defaulting to false; and `clip_validity_days`, from 1 to 365 and defaulting to 60. A newly created offer sits in `DRAFT` with a null `barcode_payload`, an `accepted_redemption_count` of 0 and a null `withdrawn_reason`.

Registration is the manufacturer's own act — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the offer's `manufacturer_id`, the issuer must be `ACTIVE`, and the offer must still be `DRAFT`. Registration advances the offer `DRAFT` → `REGISTERED` and, in the same call, mints `barcode_payload` once and for all; it is never recomputed afterwards. The payload is built by exact digit concatenation, with no separators and no trimming of leading zeros, from five fixed-width components: the issuer's `gs1_company_prefix` exactly as stored (six digits); the `face_value_cents` rendered as a decimal string and left-padded with zeros to five characters; the `purchase_requirement_units` rendered and left-padded with zeros to two characters; the `redemption_grace_days` rendered and left-padded with zeros to three characters; and the `expiration_date` rendered as a six-digit year-month-day string using the last two digits of the year, so that 2026-12-31 becomes `261231`. A sixth component, used only by the second family, is the sum of the `purchase_requirement_units` multiplied by one thousand and the `redemption_grace_days`, rendered and left-padded with zeros to five characters.

Which components are used depends on the family, and the two shapes are deliberately different. For a `GS1_DATABAR` offer the payload is the family code `811`, followed by the six-digit prefix, then the five-digit face value, then the two-digit purchase requirement, then the six-digit expiry, then the three-digit grace — twenty-five digits in all. For a `UPC_A_WITH_EXT5` offer the payload is the coupon number system digit `5`, followed by the six-digit prefix, then the five-digit face value, then the five-digit combined units-and-grace extension — seventeen digits in all. Only the databar family carries the expiration date, so a `UPC_A_WITH_EXT5` coupon's expiry can only ever be checked against the offer row and never re-derived from the barcode. This matters more than it may appear: a captured coupon's `scanned_barcode` is compared byte for byte against `barcode_payload` during adjudication, so a payload minted under the wrong family makes every coupon in the lot counterfeit.

Activation is the clearinghouse's act, not the issuer's. From `REGISTERED`, and only when the offer's `expiration_date` is on or after the logical clock's calendar date, the offer advances `REGISTERED` → `ACTIVE`, which opens it both to lot creation and to digital clipping. An offer whose expiry has already passed cannot be activated at all and is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. Once `ACTIVE`, the offer leaves that state by itself: the offer-expiry sweep inside a clock advance moves every `ACTIVE` offer whose `expiration_date` is strictly earlier than the new calendar date to `EXPIRED`. Expiry is not the end of clearing — an `EXPIRED` offer still accepts new lots until its grace period runs out — and both `ACTIVE` and `EXPIRED` are clearable states, while `DRAFT`, `REGISTERED` and `WITHDRAWN` are not.

Withdrawal is the destructive act, and its cascade is the widest in the system. The manufacturer that owns the offer (its `manufacturer_id` matching the acting `X-User-ID`) may withdraw it from `REGISTERED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, supplying a `withdrawn_reason` of 1 to 200 characters that is stored on the offer; the offer becomes `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. Then the cascade runs in three hops. In the first hop, every submission lot pointing at this offer whose status is still `SORTED` is set to `LOT_REJECTED` with `reject_reason` set to `OFFER_WITHDRAWN`, processed in lot `created_at` ascending order; those lots are never valued, so their `gross_face_value_cents` and `handling_fee_cents` remain 0, and — importantly — every coupon item inside them stays in `CAPTURED`, because nothing is adjudicated by a withdrawal. In the second hop, each submission that owned one of those lots recomputes `rejected_lot_count`, `declared_face_value_cents`, `settled_gross_face_value_cents` and `settled_handling_fee_cents`. In the third hop, a submission that now has every one of its lots in `VALIDATED` or `LOT_REJECTED` advances its `intake_status` from `SORTED` to `CLOSED`; and if every one of its lots is `LOT_REJECTED`, its `settlement_status` becomes `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `FRAUD_REVIEW` and the owning retailer's `rejected_submission_count` increments by 1. Also in the third hop, any settlement statement over an affected submission that is still in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` has its money recomputed from the submission's new figures, and each affected retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` is recomputed. Every row the cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 3.3. Intake: Receipt, Count Verification, Sorting and Image Capture

A submission is one shipment of paper or one electronic file arriving from one retailer, and it advances along **two independent state dimensions**. The `intake_status` dimension runs `RECEIVED` → `COUNT_VERIFIED` → `SORTED` → `CLOSED`; the `settlement_status` dimension runs over `PENDING`, `STATEMENT_ISSUED`, `PAID` and `HELD`. The two dimensions never read one another during intake — verifying a count leaves `settlement_status` entirely untouched — and a fully finished submission is one that has reached `CLOSED` on the first dimension and `PAID` on the second.

Opening a submission requires the `retailer_id`, the `channel` (either `MAIL` or `ELECTRONIC`), the `declared_count` the retailer claims to have sent (from 0 to 1000000), and the two bounds of the postmark interval this shipment occupies: `postmark_window_start`, the inclusive lower bound, and `postmark_window_end`, the exclusive upper bound. Both bounds are calendar dates, both are **required on every submission of either channel** — the interval models the retailer's intake stream rather than a mail-only artefact, so neither is ever null — and the start must be strictly earlier than the end. The chosen channel must be permitted by the retailer's `channel_capability`: a `MAIL` submission requires `MAIL_ONLY` or `BOTH`, an `ELECTRONIC` submission requires `ELECTRONIC_ONLY` or `BOTH`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_SUPPORTED`. The clearinghouse can reconcile only one postmark window of a given retailer at a time, so the new half-open interval must not overlap the interval of any existing submission of the same retailer whose `intake_status` is not yet `CLOSED`; the rule is channel-independent, so a mail window and an electronic window of one retailer contend exactly the same way. Because the interval is half-open, exact adjacency is legal — an existing window ending 2026-07-31 and a new window starting 2026-07-31 do not overlap — while any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_POSTMARK_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A freshly opened submission is `RECEIVED` on the intake dimension and `PENDING` on the settlement dimension, with `verified_count` 0, `count_variance_pct` `0.00`, a null `hold_reason` and every money field 0.

Count verification is the clerk's physical or electronic recount. The clerk supplies `verified_count`, the submission must still be `RECEIVED`, and the retailer must not be `SUSPENDED`. The variance is then computed as the absolute difference between the verified count and the `declared_count`, multiplied by one hundred, divided by the `declared_count`, and rounded half up to two decimal places — which also means the `declared_count` must be at least 1 for the variance to be computable, so a submission declared with zero coupons can never be count-verified. The rounded figure is compared against a tolerance of 2.00 percent, and the comparison is **strictly greater than**, so a variance of exactly 2.00 passes. Rounding happens before the comparison, and this ordering is observable: 63 declared against 62 verified gives 1.5873, rounds to 1.59 and passes; 63 declared against 61 verified gives 3.1746, rounds to 3.17 and is refused; 400 declared against 408 verified is exactly 2.00 and passes. On success the rounded figure is stored as `count_variance_pct`, the clerk's figure is stored as `verified_count`, and the submission advances `RECEIVED` → `COUNT_VERIFIED`. On failure the request is rejected with `ERR_COUNT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` and the submission stays `RECEIVED`, so the clerk can recount and try again.

Sorting is the act of dividing a verified submission into buckets, one per manufacturer-and-offer pair. Each bucket is a submission lot, created under its submission with three inputs: the `offer_id` all coupons in the bucket claim, the `manufacturer_id` the clerk sorted the bucket to, and the `counted_qty`, the physical coupon count the clerk placed in the bucket, which must be at least 1. The submission must be `COUNT_VERIFIED`; the retailer must not be `SUSPENDED`; the offer's issuer must not be `SUSPENDED`; the supplied `manufacturer_id` must be exactly the offer's own issuer, or the bucket is refused with `ERR_OFFER_MANUFACTURER_MISMATCH`; the offer must be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, or the bucket is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_CLEARABLE`; and the offer must still be inside its redemption window, meaning the clock's calendar date is on or before the offer's `expiration_date` plus its `redemption_grace_days`. That last comparison is inclusive on the final grace day: a lot sorted exactly on the last grace day is accepted and one day later is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_PAST_GRACE`. Finally, the running sum of `counted_qty` across the submission's existing lots plus the new lot's `counted_qty` must not exceed the submission's `verified_count`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LOT_COUNT_OVERFLOW` — a clerk cannot sort more coupons than were counted.

Creating a bucket also fixes how much of it must be imaged. When the `counted_qty` is at most 40, the whole bucket is imaged: `sample_size` equals `counted_qty` and `valuation_variant` is `FULL_COUNT`. Above 40, only a tenth is imaged: `sample_size` is the `counted_qty` multiplied by ten, divided by one hundred, and rounded **up** to the next whole number, computed entirely in integers, and `valuation_variant` becomes `SAMPLED_EXTRAPOLATION`. The threshold is inclusive on the full-count side, and the step at the boundary is dramatic: a bucket of 40 coupons stays `FULL_COUNT` and demands 40 images, while a bucket of 41 coupons flips to `SAMPLED_EXTRAPOLATION` and demands only 5 (the ceiling of 4.1). Because `counted_qty` is always at least 1, `sample_size` is never 0. A new lot is created in status `SORTED` with `captured_item_count`, `accepted_qty`, `rejected_qty`, `projected_accepted_units`, `gross_face_value_cents` and `handling_fee_cents` all 0, a null `reject_reason`, and a null `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_at`. Creating the lot also increments the submission's `sorted_lot_count` and recomputes its `declared_face_value_cents`, which is the sum over the submission's lots of each lot's `counted_qty` multiplied by that lot's offer face value — the notional value of the shipment before anyone has adjudicated anything. And at the end of every lot creation the system tests one thing: once the running sum of `counted_qty` over the submission's lots exactly equals the submission's `verified_count`, the submission advances `COUNT_VERIFIED` → `SORTED`, which closes it to any further lots.

Image capture is a bulk operation against one lot, which must be in `SORTED` and whose retailer must not be `SUSPENDED`. The caller supplies `items`, an array whose elements are each an object carrying `scanned_barcode`, an optional `serial_number`, an `image_uri`, a `redeemed_at`, an optional `pos_transaction_ref` and an optional `claimed_units`; the owning lot comes from the path, never from the body. The `scanned_barcode` is 1 to 64 characters, all digits, read off the coupon. The `serial_number` is 1 to 32 characters when present and is the one-time-use serial carried in the barcode's serial field; mass-print coupons have none, and a null serial skips the duplicate check entirely. The `image_uri` is 1 to 300 non-blank characters locating the captured coupon image and is **mandatory on every element**, because validation against offer terms is image-based — a blank or missing one refuses the whole batch with `ERR_IMAGE_REQUIRED`. The `redeemed_at` is the calendar date the shopper redeemed at the register. The `pos_transaction_ref` is 1 to 40 characters when present and is the register transaction reference; it is required on every element when the owning submission's `channel` is `ELECTRONIC`, and its absence there refuses the batch with `ERR_POS_REF_REQUIRED`, whereas a `MAIL` submission may omit it and let the item fail adjudication instead. The `claimed_units` says how many offer instances this single coupon clears, is between 1 and 5, and defaults to 1. The batch is refused as a whole and never partially applied: if `captured_item_count` plus the number of supplied elements would exceed the lot's `sample_size`, the call fails with `ERR_LOT_CAPTURE_COMPLETE`. On success one coupon item is created per element **in the supplied array order**, each in status `CAPTURED` with a null `reject_code`, `duplicate_of_item_id`, `matched_purchase_record_id` and `adjudicated_at`, and element number *k* receives a `created_at` of the logical clock plus *k* milliseconds — so `created_at` ascending is exactly the order of the request body, and the adjudication ladder's processing order is fully determined by how the clerk arranged the batch. The lot's `captured_item_count` increases by the number of elements created.

Point-of-sale data arrives on its own path and is the evidence adjudication will match against. Ingesting one basket requires the `retailer_id` whose register produced it, the `offer_id` the basket qualifies for, the `pos_transaction_ref` that is the join key back to the coupon, the `transaction_date` of the basket as a calendar date, and `qualifying_units`, the number of units in the basket that satisfy the offer's purchase requirement, from 0 to 1000. A basket is immutable once ingested and its creation changes nothing else in the system.

### 3.4. Adjudicating Coupons Against Offer Terms

Adjudication is the moment a captured coupon stops being a photograph and becomes either money or evidence of fraud. A `FraudAnalyst` may adjudicate a single coupon individually, ahead of any lot decision, which is useful for pinning a specific outcome before the bulk pass runs; the same ladder also runs automatically over every still-`CAPTURED` coupon of a lot when the lot is finalized (§3.5). The two entry points apply **exactly** the same ladder, and in both cases the item must be in `CAPTURED` — an already-adjudicated item cannot be adjudicated again and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A coupon leaves `CAPTURED` for exactly one of five outcomes: `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT`, `REJECTED_EXPIRED`, `REJECTED_DUPLICATE`, `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED` or `ACCEPTED`. There is a sixth status, `REINSTATED`, but it is reachable only through an overturned dispute (§3.7).

The ladder has four tests, evaluated in a fixed order, and the first one that fires decides the outcome; if none fires the coupon clears. **Step one — counterfeit.** The coupon's `scanned_barcode` is compared byte for byte against the offer's `barcode_payload`, with no normalisation of any kind. If they differ, the coupon becomes `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT` with `reject_code` `BARCODE_MISMATCH`. This step outranks every later one, so a coupon that is both counterfeit and expired lands in `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT`, not in `REJECTED_EXPIRED`. **Step two — expiry at the register.** The coupon's `redeemed_at` is compared against the offer's `expiration_date`; only a `redeemed_at` **strictly after** the expiration date is late, so a coupon redeemed exactly on the expiration date is on time. A late coupon becomes `REJECTED_EXPIRED` with `reject_code` `EXPIRED_AT_POS`. **Step three — duplicate serial.** This step is skipped entirely when the coupon's `serial_number` is null, because a mass-print coupon carries no one-time-use serial to duplicate. Otherwise the system looks for an earlier cleared instance of the same serial, and if it finds one the coupon becomes `REJECTED_DUPLICATE` with `reject_code` `DUPLICATE_SERIAL` and `duplicate_of_item_id` pointing at the instance that owns the serial. **Step four — purchase-data misredemption.** The system tries to match the coupon against an ingested point-of-sale basket, and a coupon that cannot be backed by a qualifying basket becomes `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED`. **If no step fires**, the coupon becomes `ACCEPTED`, and if the purchase-data check actually ran, the winning basket is recorded on the coupon as `matched_purchase_record_id`. Step three outranks step four: a coupon that is both a duplicate serial and unbacked by purchase data lands in `REJECTED_DUPLICATE`, not in `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED`.

Duplicate detection is deliberately global. The candidate pool is **every** coupon item in the system — across all retailers, all submissions and all lots, because duplicate redemption of a one-time-use serial is a cross-retailer fraud — restricted to items other than the coupon being adjudicated whose `scanned_barcode` and `serial_number` are both exactly equal to the candidate's, compared as exact strings with no case folding and no whitespace trimming, and whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `REINSTATED`. Only an already-cleared coupon can own a serial: items still in `CAPTURED` and items already rejected never win. If the pool is empty the candidate is the original and the step does not fire. Otherwise the pool is ranked by `adjudicated_at` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the earliest such item wins; the item's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. The consequence worth internalising is that the outcome follows adjudication order rather than capture order: capture coupon X and then coupon Y, but adjudicate Y first, and X becomes the duplicate. And because the pool contains only cleared items, reinstating a previously rejected duplicate can retroactively hand a third instance of the same serial a different owner.

Purchase-data matching is scoped and arithmetic. First the system computes how many qualifying units the coupon needs: the coupon's `claimed_units` multiplied by the offer's `purchase_requirement_units`. This multiplication is the trap — a coupon with `claimed_units` of 3 against an offer requiring 2 units per coupon needs a basket with at least 6 qualifying units, not 2 and not 3. Then it gathers the reference pool: every ingested basket whose `retailer_id` is the submitting retailer of this coupon's submission, whose `offer_id` is the lot's offer, and whose `pos_transaction_ref` equals the coupon's `pos_transaction_ref`. If that pool is empty — which includes the case where the coupon's `pos_transaction_ref` is null, since a null reference joins no basket at all and therefore earns no free pass — the coupon becomes `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED` with `reject_code` `NO_PURCHASE_MATCH` and `matched_purchase_record_id` left null. Otherwise the system filters the reference pool down to the baskets whose `qualifying_units` is **at least** the required number, so a basket with exactly the required units qualifies. If at least one basket qualifies, the qualifying baskets are ranked by `transaction_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — the basket `id` is never consulted — and the earliest one is recorded as `matched_purchase_record_id` while the coupon proceeds to clear. If the reference exists but **every** basket sharing it falls short, the coupon becomes `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED` with `reject_code` `INSUFFICIENT_QUALIFYING_UNITS`, and the earliest reference-matched basket under the same ranking is still recorded as `matched_purchase_record_id` so the retailer can see which basket fell short. Baskets are never consumed: one basket may back more than one coupon, because the clearinghouse only requires that a qualifying transaction existed.

Whatever the outcome, adjudicating one coupon writes `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock — that timestamp is the primary sort key of duplicate detection, which is why it is written on every branch — and then recomputes the surrounding derived values. The owning lot's `accepted_qty` (the sum of `claimed_units` over its items in `ACCEPTED` or `REINSTATED`) and `rejected_qty` (the same sum over its items in `REJECTED_EXPIRED`, `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT`, `REJECTED_DUPLICATE` or `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED`) are recomputed. So are three submission-level totals: `adjudicated_face_value_cents`, which sums the offer face value multiplied by `claimed_units` over every item of the submission not in `CAPTURED`; `accepted_item_face_value_cents`, which sums the same product over items in `ACCEPTED` or `REINSTATED`; and `rejected_item_face_value_cents`, which sums it over items in the four rejected statuses.

### 3.5. Lot Finalization, Valuation and Handling Fees

Finalizing a lot is the analyst's decision to close the bucket and put a number on it. The lot must be in `SORTED`; its `captured_item_count` must equal its `sample_size`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_INCOMPLETE` because a lot cannot be valued until the full sample has been imaged; and the retailer that owns the lot's submission must not be `SUSPENDED`. Finalization then runs the four-test ladder of §3.4 over **every** coupon of the lot still in `CAPTURED`, processing them in coupon `created_at` ascending order — which, per §3.3, is exactly the order the clerk supplied them in.

After the bulk pass, the lot's `accepted_qty` and `rejected_qty` are recomputed and the outcome forks on a single test. If at least one coupon cleared — that is, the recomputed `accepted_qty` is at least 1 — the lot goes `SORTED` → `VALIDATED`. If nothing cleared at all — the recomputed `accepted_qty` is exactly 0 — the lot goes `SORTED` → `LOT_REJECTED` with `reject_reason` set to `ALL_ITEMS_REJECTED`. The two branches share a trigger, an actor and every precondition except that one test, and their side effects diverge materially. The rejected branch leaves `gross_face_value_cents` and `handling_fee_cents` at 0, because a rejected lot is never valued and never earns a handling fee, and it does **not** touch the offer's `accepted_redemption_count`. Both branches write `adjudicated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock — this is the segregation-of-duties wall that will later bar this same analyst from resolving a dispute over this lot — then recompute the owning submission's derived money fields and evaluate whether the submission can close.

On the validated branch the lot is valued, and which of two valuation variants runs was already fixed at sort time by the bucket's size (§3.3). The offer's `face_value_cents` is the unit price in both variants, and both work in whole cents throughout.

Under the **full-count** variant — the one selected when `counted_qty` is at most 40 — every physical coupon in the bucket was imaged and adjudicated, so the adjudicated units are used directly: `projected_accepted_units` is set equal to `accepted_qty`, and `gross_face_value_cents` is `accepted_qty` multiplied by the offer's face value. There is no rate, no extrapolation and no rounding, because both operands are integers.

Under the **sampled-extrapolation** variant — the one selected when `counted_qty` exceeds 40 — only `sample_size` coupons were imaged, so the sample's accepted units are scaled up to the whole physical count in two rounding steps whose order is load-bearing. First the unit accept rate is the `accepted_qty` divided by the `sample_size`, rounded half up to **four** decimal places. Then `projected_accepted_units` is the `counted_qty` multiplied by that already-rounded rate, rounded half up to a whole unit. Only then is `gross_face_value_cents` computed as `projected_accepted_units` multiplied by the offer's face value. Rounding the rate to fewer places, or multiplying the face value in before rounding the units, produces a different cent total. Worked through: a bucket of 60 coupons with a `sample_size` of 6, five cleared coupons each claiming one unit and one coupon rejected as misredeemed, against an offer whose face value is 50 cents, gives a rate of 0.8333, `projected_accepted_units` of 50, and `gross_face_value_cents` of 2500 — where the same stored rows run through full-count would give 5 projected units and 250 cents.

The denominator of that rate is the `sample_size`, not the `captured_item_count` and not the number of items actually adjudicated. Those three coincide at finalization by construction, since incompleteness is refused outright, but a lot whose items are later reinstated still divides by the original `sample_size`, so a reinstatement **raises** the projection rather than re-basing it.

Immediately after valuation, the handling fee for the lot is priced from the projected units. Three inputs decide the fee. The unit count is the lot's `projected_accepted_units`. The per-unit basis comes from the issuing manufacturer's `handling_fee_policy`: under `CONTRACT_RATE` it is simply the offer's `handling_fee_cents`, while under `TIERED_RATE` it is banded by **this lot's** unit count and not by the submission's — 8 cents per unit for up to 99 units, 7 cents per unit for 100 through 499 units, and 6 cents per unit at 500 units and above. The multiplier comes from the retailer's **current** effective `handling_fee_tier`, read at the moment finalization runs rather than the tier in force at intake: a `PREFERRED` retailer earns a 1.15 multiplier, a `STANDARD` retailer 1.00, and a retailer on `PROBATION` only 0.80. The lot's `handling_fee_cents` is the unit count multiplied by the per-unit basis multiplied by the tier multiplier, rounded half up to a whole number of cents **at the lot level**.

That per-lot rounding is the whole point, and the submission's `settled_handling_fee_cents` is a plain integer sum of already-rounded lot fees over the submission's lots in `VALIDATED` or `REINSTATED`; lots in `SORTED`, `LOT_REJECTED` or `DISPUTED` contribute nothing. Consider a submission with two lots, a `PREFERRED` retailer and a contract rate of 7 cents. Lot A's 50 units price at 402.5, which rounds to 403; lot B's 13 units price at 104.65, which rounds to 105; and `settled_handling_fee_cents` is 508. Summing the units first and rounding once would give 507.15 rounding to 507 — one cent short, and that cent would propagate into the statement's provisional net, the invoice's billed total and the retailer's net payable. Under `TIERED_RATE` the divergence is larger still, because per-lot banding can put lot A and lot B in different bands while their combined unit count sits in a third. In the same way, `settled_gross_face_value_cents` is the plain integer sum of `gross_face_value_cents` over the submission's `VALIDATED` and `REINSTATED` lots, and it is the billable gross a statement will copy.

Validation of a lot also advances the offer's cumulative redemption counter, and that counter has a ceiling. Before the lot is validated, the offer's `accepted_redemption_count` plus the units this lot would clear must not exceed the offer's `max_redemptions`, or the operation is refused with `ERR_MAX_REDEMPTIONS_EXCEEDED`; on success `accepted_redemption_count` increases by the cleared units and the offer's `version` is bumped.

Finally, every finalization — validated or rejected — asks whether the submission is done with intake. When the submission is in `SORTED` and every one of its lots has reached `VALIDATED` or `LOT_REJECTED`, the submission advances `SORTED` → `CLOSED`. Closing recomputes `adjudicated_face_value_cents`, `accepted_item_face_value_cents`, `rejected_item_face_value_cents`, `settled_gross_face_value_cents`, `settled_handling_fee_cents`, `rejected_lot_count` (the count of the submission's lots in `LOT_REJECTED`) and `valuation_method`, which is `FULL_COUNT` unless at least one of the submission's lots resolved to `SAMPLED_EXTRAPOLATION`, in which case it is `SAMPLED_EXTRAPOLATION`. One further cascade runs **only** in the total-loss case, where `rejected_lot_count` equals `sorted_lot_count`: the submission's `settlement_status` is set to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `FRAUD_REVIEW`, the retailer's `rejected_submission_count` increments by 1, the retailer's fraud score is re-derived from scratch, and the resulting status and tier gate of §3.6 is applied. A submission that closes with at least one validated lot does none of that and stays `PENDING` on the settlement dimension, waiting for an officer.

### 3.6. Fraud Scoring and Retailer Risk Standing

A retailer's standing is evidence-driven. An analyst may re-derive it at any time, supplying `expected_retailer_version` as described in §2.7, and the same derivation runs automatically when a submission closes with every one of its lots rejected. Either way the computation is identical and it always writes `last_rescored_at` from the logical clock.

The evidence pool is every adjudicated coupon of that retailer: the coupon items reachable from the retailer through its submissions and their lots, restricted to items whose status is **not** `CAPTURED`. `REINSTATED` items are part of the pool and count toward the denominator, but they count toward none of the three numerators — which is why an overturned dispute lowers every rate rather than merely moving value around. Three component rates come out of that pool, each computed as the count of matching items multiplied by one hundred, divided by the pool size, rounded half up to two decimal places: `duplicate_rate` counts items in `REJECTED_DUPLICATE`, `misredemption_rate` counts items in `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED`, and `counterfeit_rate` counts items in `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT`. When the pool is empty, all three rates are `0.00` rather than undefined.

The score is a severity-weighted sum, not an average. The `duplicate_rate` is multiplied by 1.40, the `misredemption_rate` by 1.00 and the `counterfeit_rate` by 2.00; the three products are added; the total is rounded half up to two decimal places; and the result is clamped so that it can never exceed `100.00`. The weights do not sum to one and the clamp is the only ceiling, which is what makes the thresholds reachable — a `duplicate_rate` of 50.00 on its own yields exactly 70.00, precisely the watchlist boundary. Note the rounding discipline: each component rate is rounded to two decimals **before** it is weighted, and the weighted sum is rounded to two decimals again.

Two variants of that computation exist, they disagree about which adjudicated items enter the pool and with what weight, and **both are always computed and stored on every rescore**. The `ROLLING_WINDOW` variant applies a hard cut-off: only items whose `adjudicated_at` falls on or after the logical clock minus 90 days enter the pool, each with weight one, and anything older is invisible — a retailer with a terrible history and a clean last 90 days scores `0.00`. The window is inclusive on its low end, so an item adjudicated exactly 90 days ago is still inside it. Its result is stored in `fraud_score_rolling_window`. The `LIFETIME_WEIGHTED` variant applies no cut-off at all: every adjudicated item the retailer has ever produced enters the pool, weighted by a **step** decay over whole half-life buckets of 180 days. An item's age in whole days is divided by 180 and truncated to a whole bucket number, and its weight is one half raised to that bucket. Decay is a step, never an interpolation: an item 179 days old carries weight 1.0 and an item 180 days old carries weight 0.5. Both numerator and denominator use those weights, so each component rate is the summed weight of matching items multiplied by one hundred, divided by the summed weight of the whole pool, rounded half up to two decimals. Its result is stored in `fraud_score_lifetime_weighted`.

The gap between the two variants is real money. With the clock at 2027-04-01, consider two items adjudicated on 2026-08-01 — 243 days old, therefore bucket one and weight 0.5 — one of which is `REJECTED_DUPLICATE`, plus four items adjudicated on 2027-04-01 in bucket zero at full weight, all `ACCEPTED`. Rolling window sees only the four recent items, finds a `duplicate_rate` of `0.00` and scores `0.00`. Lifetime weighted sees a weighted duplicate mass of 0.5 against a weighted pool of 5.0, giving a `duplicate_rate` of `10.00`, a weighted total of 14.00 and therefore a score of `14.00`. The same stored rows, 14 points apart, and only the retailer's `fraud_scoring_mode` decides which number lands in `fraud_score` — and therefore which number drives status and tier. The three component rates stored on the retailer are the components of whichever variant `fraud_scoring_mode` selects, so `misredemption_rate` — which later feeds the settlement holdback — is consistent with the authoritative score.

The threshold ladder is then evaluated from the top down against `fraud_score`. At `90.00` or above the retailer's target status is `SUSPENDED`. At `70.00` or above but below `90.00` it is `WATCHLIST`. Below `70.00` it is `ACTIVE`. Separately and on the same boundary, the effective `handling_fee_tier` becomes `PROBATION` at `70.00` or above and reverts to the retailer's `base_fee_tier` below it — so the tier always returns to the contracted base and never to a remembered `PROBATION`. Concretely, a rescore may drive `ACTIVE` → `WATCHLIST`, `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`, `WATCHLIST` → `SUSPENDED`, or `WATCHLIST` → `ACTIVE`, and a retailer may oscillate `ACTIVE` → `WATCHLIST` → `ACTIVE` across successive rescores as its evidence pool changes. Every such move bumps the retailer's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`. Because `PROBATION` drops the handling-fee multiplier to 0.80, a watchlisted retailer immediately earns less on every lot finalized from that moment on — the tier is read at finalization time, not at intake time.

### 3.7. Disputes, Information Rounds and Reinstatement

A retailer whose lot was condemned may appeal it, and only the retailer that owns the lot's submission may do so — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal that submission's retailer. The appeal names the `lot_id` under appeal, a `reason_code` drawn from `WRONG_EXPIRY_CALL`, `IMAGE_QUALITY`, `POS_DATA_LATE` and `DUPLICATE_MISMATCH`, and a `narrative` of 1 to 1000 characters carrying the retailer's argument. The lot must currently be in `LOT_REJECTED`, which by construction means no appeal over it is already live, since an open appeal parks the lot in `DISPUTED`. The appeal must also be timely: the logical clock's calendar date must be on or before the lot's `adjudicated_at` date plus the issuing manufacturer's `dispute_window_days`, inclusive on the final day, and a late filing is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A suspended retailer may not file at all.

Filing creates the dispute in `UNDER_REVIEW` with `info_request_count` 0, a null `resolution_note` and a null `resolved_by_user_id`, `retailer_id` copied from the lot's submission, `filed_at` taken from the logical clock, and `claimed_face_value_cents` copied from the lot's rejected face value — the sum, over the lot's coupons in any of the four rejected statuses, of the offer's `face_value_cents` multiplied by that coupon's `claimed_units`. The lot moves `LOT_REJECTED` → `DISPUTED`. And because money over contested paper must not move, any settlement statement over the lot's submission that is still in `DRAFT` or in `SCHEDULED` is put into `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `DISPUTE_OPEN`. On this dispute-driven path the submission itself is **not** touched: a submission whose statement was already `STATEMENT_ISSUED` stays `STATEMENT_ISSUED`, because the intake batch really is settled and only the payment is frozen. The owning retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` is nevertheless recomputed over its `SCHEDULED` statements only, so the held amount leaves the payable balance.

Review is a bounded negotiation. An analyst may bounce the appeal back for more evidence, supplying an `info_request_note`; that moves the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` and increments `info_request_count` by 1. The retailer answers by resubmitting with a fresh `narrative`, which is appended to the existing `narrative` and returns the dispute `INFO_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW`; only the retailer named on the dispute may resubmit. That closes one turn of an `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` cycle, and the cycle is capped at two information requests per dispute: a third attempt, when `info_request_count` has already reached 2, is refused with `ERR_INFO_ROUND_LIMIT`, which terminates the negotiation and forces the analyst to decide on what is in front of them.

Resolution takes an `outcome` of either `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` together with a `resolution_note` of 1 to 1000 characters, and the dispute must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`. The segregation-of-duties wall applies here and outranks every other readiness check on this operation: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the disputed lot's `adjudicated_by_user_id`, so two distinct analyst identities are required to take a lot from rejection to resolution, and a self-review is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION`. Whichever way it goes, resolution writes `resolution_note` and sets `resolved_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`.

An `UPHELD` outcome confirms the rejection. The dispute becomes `UPHELD` and the lot returns `DISPUTED` → `LOT_REJECTED`, this time with `reject_reason` set to `DISPUTE_UPHELD`. Every coupon of the lot keeps the status it already had, so the submission's `adjudicated_face_value_cents` is unchanged. This closes a `LOT_REJECTED` → `DISPUTED` → `LOT_REJECTED` revision cycle, and the retailer may file again as long as the manufacturer's dispute window is still open.

An `OVERTURNED` outcome reverses the rejection completely, and its cascade is the mirror image of adjudication. Every coupon of the lot in `REJECTED_EXPIRED`, `REJECTED_COUNTERFEIT`, `REJECTED_DUPLICATE` or `REJECTED_MISREDEEMED` is moved to `REINSTATED`, with its `reject_code` cleared and its `duplicate_of_item_id` cleared, processed in coupon `created_at` ascending order. Each reinstated coupon re-enters the accepted set at its full original `claimed_units`. The lot's `accepted_qty` and `rejected_qty` are recomputed, and both lot valuation and the lot handling fee are re-run from §3.5 — still dividing by the original `sample_size`, which is why a reinstatement raises the projection. The lot becomes `REINSTATED` and its `reject_reason` is cleared. The offer's `accepted_redemption_count` advances by the newly cleared units and the offer's `version` is bumped. The owning submission then recomputes `accepted_item_face_value_cents`, `rejected_item_face_value_cents`, `settled_gross_face_value_cents`, `settled_handling_fee_cents` and `rejected_lot_count`, while `adjudicated_face_value_cents` stays numerically identical — value moves from the rejected column to the accepted column and the conserved total does not budge.

An appeal that nobody decides does not sit open forever. The dispute service-level allowance is 30 days from `filed_at`, and the sweep inside a clock advance moves every dispute still in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` whose filing date plus 30 days is strictly earlier than the new calendar date to `WITHDRAWN`, returning its lot `DISPUTED` → `LOT_REJECTED` with `reject_reason` `DISPUTE_UPHELD` — the same outcome as an upheld decision, reached by silence. Disputes are swept in `created_at` ascending order so the reported count is deterministic. One asymmetry deserves emphasis: a hold placed on a statement by a dispute has **no** automatic release path. It is cleared only through the invoice funding cycle of §3.8, so a `DISPUTE_OPEN` hold survives the dispute's resolution and the practical remedy is to issue a fresh statement over the submission once the appeal is settled.

### 3.8. The Settlement and Funding Chain

Settlement is where paper becomes cash, and it runs as a chain of four operator acts: a `SettlementOfficer` issues a statement over a closed submission, schedules it, bills it onto a manufacturer invoice, and — once the manufacturer's money has actually arrived — releases the payment. The chain is deliberately breakable in the middle, because the manufacturer may underpay or an administrator may claw funding back.

**Issuing a statement.** The officer names the `submission_id` and supplies the `expected_submission_version` of §2.7, optionally together with `prior_chargeback_offset_cents`, a non-negative operator-supplied carry-forward deduction that defaults to 0 and represents face value the clearinghouse already paid this retailer on lots that a later dispute pushed back to `LOT_REJECTED` with `reject_reason` `DISPUTE_UPHELD`, on statements that have already been paid. The submission's `intake_status` must be `CLOSED`; a submission still in intake cannot be settled and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The submission must also have something to settle: at least one lot in `VALIDATED` or `REINSTATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_SUBMISSION`, since a submission whose every lot was rejected has no value to pay out. A closed submission may legitimately receive a fresh statement that supersedes an earlier `DRAFT` one, which is exactly why the stale-version check rather than a state guard is what protects the operation.

Every money field on the statement is minted in that one call, in a strict six-step waterfall. **Step one**: `gross_face_value_cents` is the sum of `gross_face_value_cents` over the submission's lots in `VALIDATED` or `REINSTATED` — already-rounded lot values, summed as integers. Lots in `LOT_REJECTED` and `DISPUTED` are excluded from this sum and the next, so a statement issued while an appeal is live values the disputed lot at zero. **Step two**: `handling_fee_total_cents` is the sum of `handling_fee_cents` over the same lots, again already rounded per lot. **Step three**: the caller's `prior_chargeback_offset_cents` is recorded on the statement as supplied. **Step four**: `provisional_net_cents` is the gross plus the handling fee total minus the offset; if that figure would be negative the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. **Step five**: `holdback_pct` is chosen from `provisional_net_cents` in three sub-steps whose order is pinned — a base band, then a risk add-on, then a cap. The base band is 0.00 when `provisional_net_cents` is below 100000 cents, 0.02 when it is at least 100000 but below 1000000 cents, and 0.05 at 1000000 cents and above. The risk add-on is 0.03 when the retailer's current `misredemption_rate` is at least 5.00 percent and 0.00 otherwise. `holdback_pct` is then the lesser of the band plus the add-on and the hard cap of 0.06, stored to four decimal places. **Step six**: `misredemption_holdback_cents` is `provisional_net_cents` multiplied by `holdback_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — the single rounding point in the whole waterfall — and `net_payable_cents` is `provisional_net_cents` minus `misredemption_holdback_cents`, a pure integer subtraction that never rounds.

Three of those orderings are load-bearing because every plausible alternative gives a different answer. The offset is subtracted **before** the holdback band is chosen, so a large chargeback can drop a statement into a lower band and thus a smaller holdback. The risk add-on is applied to the band rate and only **then** capped, never capped and then added. And the holdback is a percentage of `provisional_net_cents` — gross plus fee minus offset — not of gross alone and not of gross plus fee. Worked through: a gross of 3150 and a fee of 508 with no offset give a `provisional_net_cents` of 3658; 3658 is below 100000 so the base band is 0.00; the retailer's `misredemption_rate` of 11.11 is at least 5.00 so the add-on is 0.03; `holdback_pct` is the lesser of 0.03 and 0.06, namely 0.03; the holdback is 109.74 rounded half up to 110; and `net_payable_cents` is 3548. Applying the same percentage to gross alone would give 95 and a net of 3563.

The statement is created in `DRAFT` with `retailer_id` copied from the submission, `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the second segregation-of-duties wall — a null `manufacturer_invoice_id`, a null `scheduled_pay_date` and a null `hold_reason`. Issuance also increments the submission's `version` by 1, so a client wanting to issue again must re-read the submission first.

**Scheduling a statement** puts it on the payment calendar. The statement must be in `DRAFT`, and no dispute in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` may reference any lot of the statement's submission, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN`. Scheduling computes `scheduled_pay_date` from the retailer's `payment_terms` and the logical clock, and nothing else: the base date is the calendar date of the clock **at scheduling time**, not the statement's issue date, so a statement that sits in `DRAFT` across a clock advance schedules later. Neither the submission's postmark window nor the statement's own creation timestamp is an input. The term is 15 days for `NET_15`, 30 days for `NET_30` and 45 days for `NET_45`. After adding the term, a weekend result rolls **forward** to the following Monday — a Saturday advances by two days, a Sunday by one — and the roll is applied once and never iterated. From a clock of 2026-08-01, `NET_30` lands on 2026-08-31, a Monday, with no roll; `NET_15` from the same clock lands on 2026-08-16, a Sunday, and rolls to 2026-08-17. Scheduling then moves the statement `DRAFT` → `SCHEDULED`, moves the submission's `settlement_status` `PENDING` → `STATEMENT_ISSUED`, and increases the retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` by the statement's `net_payable_cents`, bumping the retailer's `version`.

**Billing a manufacturer.** An invoice bundles a batch of retailer statements onto one issuer for a closed billing period. The officer supplies the `manufacturer_id` being billed, the inclusive `billing_period_start` and inclusive `billing_period_end` of the period (the start on or before the end), and `statement_ids`, an ordered list of 1 to 200 statement identifiers with no repeats — an empty list or a repeated element is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_LINES_EMPTY`. Billing periods of one manufacturer must not crowd each other: the new closed period must be separated from the period of every non-`REVERSED` invoice of the same manufacturer by at least one clear day. Unlike the postmark rule of §3.3, adjacency is **rejected** here — an existing period ending 2026-08-31 and a new period starting 2026-09-01 violate the buffer, and the new period must start no earlier than 2026-09-02 — with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_BUFFER_VIOLATION` on a violation. Each named statement must be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, must not already carry a `manufacturer_invoice_id`, and must belong to a submission whose `VALIDATED` and `REINSTATED` lots all point at this same manufacturer; statements in `HELD` or `PAID` are not billable at all, and a violation is `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_BILLABLE`. The issuer must not be `SUSPENDED`.

The invoice total is then aggregated in one pass. `billed_face_value_cents` is the sum of `gross_face_value_cents` over the named statements; `billed_handling_fee_cents` is the sum of their `handling_fee_total_cents`. `billed_clearing_fee_cents` is the clearinghouse's own cut: 1.5 percent charged on the **summed face value only** and never on the handling fee, rounded half up to whole cents — and that single rounding point is over the sum, not per statement. `billed_total_cents` is the face value plus the handling fee plus the clearing fee. `funded_amount_cents` starts at 0 and `funding_shortfall_cents` starts equal to `billed_total_cents`, which is the general identity: the shortfall is always the billed total minus the funded amount, floored at zero. Worked through: one statement with a gross of 3150 and a fee of 508 gives a clearing fee of 47.25 rounded half up to 47 and a `billed_total_cents` of 3705. Charging the clearing fee on gross plus fee would give 55 and a total of 3713; and two statements with face values of 3150 and 3151 bill 6301 at 1.5 percent, which is 94.515 rounding to 95 — not the 94 that rounding each statement separately would produce. The invoice is created in status `ISSUED`, and each named statement has its `manufacturer_invoice_id` set to the new invoice.

**Funding the invoice.** Only the billed manufacturer wires money against its own invoice, and only while it is `ACTIVE`; a suspended issuer cannot fund, and a user whose `X-User-ID` is not the invoice's `manufacturer_id` is refused as a non-owner. The invoice must be in `ISSUED` or `SHORT_FUNDED`, and the wire's `amount_cents` — a positive integer — must not push `funded_amount_cents` past `billed_total_cents`; overfunding is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_EXCEEDS_BILLED`. Because that arithmetic check sits below the state check in the ladder of §2.11, an attempt to overfund an already-`RECONCILED` invoice reports the state problem, not the amount problem.

The same call, from the same role, forks on one comparison, and the two branches are wildly asymmetric in consequence. **Branch A — full funding**: when the running total after the wire exactly equals `billed_total_cents`, `funding_shortfall_cents` becomes 0, the invoice moves to `FUNDED`, the manufacturer's `funded_total_cents` grows by `amount_cents` with `funding_balance_cents` recomputed and its `version` bumped — and **no statement is touched at all**. The invoice's statements were already `SCHEDULED`; full funding simply makes them releasable. **Branch B — short funding**: when the running total falls short, `funding_shortfall_cents` becomes `billed_total_cents` minus the new `funded_amount_cents`, the invoice moves to `SHORT_FUNDED`, the manufacturer's `funded_total_cents` grows by exactly the same `amount_cents` — and a three-hop cascade that branch A never runs freezes the whole downstream chain. Hop one: every statement of this invoice that is `SCHEDULED` moves to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `FUNDING_SHORTFALL`, processed in statement `created_at` ascending order. Hop two: because each of those statements changed status, its submission's `settlement_status` moves `STATEMENT_ISSUED` → `HELD`, also with `hold_reason` `FUNDING_SHORTFALL`. Hop three: because those statements are no longer `SCHEDULED`, each affected retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` is recomputed over that retailer's `SCHEDULED` statements only, so the held amounts drop out of the payable balance, and each such retailer's `version` is bumped. Note what branch B does **not** do: it does not reduce `funded_total_cents`, and it does not skip the manufacturer ledger update. A short payment is real money received; it is simply not enough money.

Topping the invoice up closes the cycle. A second wire on a `SHORT_FUNDED` invoice that brings `funded_amount_cents` exactly to `billed_total_cents` sets `funding_shortfall_cents` to 0, moves the invoice `SHORT_FUNDED` → `FUNDED`, adds that wire's `amount_cents` to `funded_total_cents`, and runs the same three hops in reverse: every statement of the invoice that is `HELD` with `hold_reason` `FUNDING_SHORTFALL` returns to `SCHEDULED` with its `hold_reason` cleared, each such statement's submission returns to `STATEMENT_ISSUED` with its `hold_reason` cleared, and each affected retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` is recomputed. One detail matters for auditability: on release from hold the statement's `scheduled_pay_date` is **not** recomputed. A statement whose payment date passed while funding was short becomes immediately releasable rather than sliding another term into the future. Statements held for the other reason — `DISPUTE_OPEN` — are not released by this cascade, because it selects only statements whose `hold_reason` is `FUNDING_SHORTFALL`.

**Releasing payment.** This is the second segregation-of-duties wall and the point at which cash actually leaves. The statement must be in `SCHEDULED`. It must carry a non-null `manufacturer_invoice_id` whose invoice is in status `FUNDED`; an unfunded, short-funded, reversed or merely-issued invoice blocks release with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FUNDED`. The logical clock's calendar date must have reached `scheduled_pay_date` — the comparison is inclusive, so release on the pay date itself is legal, and an earlier date is refused with `ERR_PAY_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the statement's `issued_by_user_id`, so two distinct `SettlementOfficer` user identities are required to move money; a self-release is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION`. The relative precedence of these three is fixed by §2.11: the funding gate outranks the pay-date gate, so an early release attempt against an unfunded invoice reports the funding problem. Release sets the statement to `PAID` — a terminal state — records `released_by_user_id` as the acting user, moves the submission's `settlement_status` `STATEMENT_ISSUED` → `PAID` and clears its `hold_reason`, increases the manufacturer's `disbursed_total_cents` by the statement's `net_payable_cents` with `funding_balance_cents` recomputed and the manufacturer's `version` bumped, and decreases the retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents` by the same `net_payable_cents` with the retailer's `version` bumped. Finally, the same call evaluates a bubble-up: once **every** statement carrying this `manufacturer_invoice_id` is `PAID`, the invoice advances `FUNDED` → `RECONCILED`, its own terminal state. An invoice with three statements therefore reconciles on the third release and not before, and the check is over all statements of the invoice, not merely the ones settled in this request.

**Reversing funding** is the compensating action for a wire sent in error, and only a `ClearinghouseAdmin` may perform it. The invoice must be in `FUNDED` or `SHORT_FUNDED` — both source states behave identically — and no statement of the invoice may yet be `PAID`, since disbursed cash cannot be unwound; an attempt against an invoice with a paid statement is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_ALREADY_PAID`. The administrator supplies a `reason` string for the audit trail. Reversal decreases the manufacturer's `funded_total_cents` by the invoice's entire `funded_amount_cents`, recomputes `funding_balance_cents` and bumps the manufacturer's `version`; resets the invoice's `funded_amount_cents` to 0 and `funding_shortfall_cents` back to the full `billed_total_cents`; sets the invoice to `REVERSED`, which is terminal and admits no further funding; moves every `SCHEDULED` statement of the invoice to `HELD` with `hold_reason` `FUNDING_SHORTFALL` and each such statement's submission to `HELD`; and recomputes each affected retailer's `unpaid_balance_cents`. The reversal restores `funded_total_cents`, and therefore `funding_balance_cents`, to exactly its pre-funding value — that exact restoration is the point of the operation, and it is why the decrement uses the invoice's accumulated `funded_amount_cents` rather than any single wire amount.

### 3.9. Digital Coupon Clearing: Clips and Redemption Events

Paper is only half the clearinghouse's business. Digitally clipped offers never physically arrive, so they bypass submissions, lots, statements and invoices entirely and clear through their own two-record pipeline: a clip, recording that a shopper loaded an offer onto a loyalty account, and a redemption event, recording that a register honoured it. The two are matched at ingest and never re-matched.

**Clipping.** A clip names the `offer_id` and the `consumer_ref`, an opaque loyalty-account handle of 1 to 40 characters. Both a `Retailer` acting for its own banner and a `ClearinghouseAdmin` may record clips. The offer must have `digital_enabled` set to true; an offer that exists but was never enabled for digital distribution is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_DIGITAL`, which is the sole gate the digital pipeline shares with redemption ingest. The clip's `clipped_at` is the logical clock's calendar date at creation and is **never** supplied by the caller — a client cannot backdate a clip to widen its own eligibility window — and it is the inclusive lower bound of the clip's occupancy interval.

`clip_expires_at`, the inclusive upper bound, is computed once at creation and never recomputed afterwards, even if the offer is later amended. It is the **earlier** of two dates: the clip date plus the offer's `clip_validity_days`, and the offer's `expiration_date` plus its `redemption_grace_days`. That second ceiling is the interesting half — a clip can never outlive the offer's redemption grace period, so clipping a nearly-expired offer yields a clip that dies far sooner than `clip_validity_days` alone would suggest, and the matcher will then pass it over even though its nominal validity has not elapsed. Both operands are whole calendar dates in UTC and the comparison is taken on dates, not on datetimes. Worked through: an offer expiring 2026-08-10 with a `redemption_grace_days` of 5 and a `clip_validity_days` of 30, clipped on 2026-08-01, gives a validity date of 2026-08-31 and an offer ceiling of 2026-08-15, so `clip_expires_at` is 2026-08-15.

One consumer may hold only so many live clips on one offer at a time, and the limit is 2. Before a new clip is created, the count of that same `offer_id` and `consumer_ref` pair's clips that are still `CLIPPED` **and** whose `clip_expires_at` has not fallen behind the clock's calendar date must be below 2; a third live clip is refused with `ERR_CLIP_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT`. This is an aggregate refusal, not an individual one: each of the two existing clips is perfectly valid on its own, and it is only their collective count that blocks the request — so the error message should speak about occupancy, not about any one clip being wrong. Slots free up two ways. A clip the clock sweep has already moved to `EXPIRED_UNUSED` no longer occupies a slot, and neither does one consumed into `MATCHED`. A consumer who clips twice, redeems once and clips again is therefore acting legally.

**Redemption ingest and the matcher.** Only a `Retailer` reports a redemption, supplying the `offer_id`, its own `retailer_id` and the `consumer_ref` the register read. As with clips, `redeemed_at` is the logical clock's calendar date at ingest and never caller-supplied, and `face_value_cents` is copied from the offer at ingest so that the historical event keeps the value it cleared at even if the offer's own face value is later amended.

The matcher runs once, at ingest. Its candidate pool is every clip that satisfies **all** of five conditions: the same `offer_id` as the event; the same `consumer_ref` as the event; a `status` of `CLIPPED`, which excludes clips already `MATCHED` or swept to `EXPIRED_UNUSED`; a `clipped_at` at or before the event's `redeemed_at`; and a `clip_expires_at` at or after the event's `redeemed_at`. That last comparison is inclusive, so a clip redeemed on its own expiry date still matches — and because the clock's clip-expiry sweep uses a strict comparison in the other direction, the two rules agree exactly on the boundary day and there is no date on which a clip is both sweepable and matchable. When the pool holds more than one clip the winner is the one with the **earliest** `clip_expires_at`, with the earliest `created_at` breaking a tie; the identifier is never consulted. Soonest-to-expire wins, which is deliberately **not** first-clipped-wins: of two live clips on the same offer, the one clipped later can be selected if a nearer offer ceiling shortened its life.

The fork here is unusual in that only one arm is an error and the other is not. When the matcher returns a winner the event is created `MATCHED` with `matched_clip_id` set to the winner, that clip moves `CLIPPED` → `MATCHED` with its `matched_event_id` pointing back at the new event, and the offer's `accepted_redemption_count` increases by 1 — one unit per digital redemption, unlike the multi-unit lot clearances of §3.5. When the pool is empty the event is still created, with status `UNMATCHED`, a null `matched_clip_id` and a `reject_code` of `NO_ELIGIBLE_CLIP`; no clip is consumed and `accepted_redemption_count` is unchanged. An unmatchable redemption is recorded as a successful creation rather than raised as an error, because the register's report is a fact the clearinghouse must retain for fraud analysis whether or not a clip backs it. The one thing that **does** raise an error at digital ingest is the offer's own redemption ceiling: if the offer's `accepted_redemption_count` plus the single unit this redemption would clear would exceed its `max_redemptions`, the request is refused with `ERR_MAX_REDEMPTIONS_EXCEEDED` and no event is created at all. That ceiling check is evaluated before the matcher, so a redemption against an exhausted offer never reaches the matching logic and never records an `UNMATCHED` row. Both records are read-only once written: clips and events can be listed and fetched, filtered by their offer, their consumer, their status and their counterpart identifier — the reverse-reachability channel that lets a `FraudAnalyst` walk from a suspect event back to the clip it consumed — but neither is ever updated or deleted.
