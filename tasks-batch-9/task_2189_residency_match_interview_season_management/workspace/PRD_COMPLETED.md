# Residency Match Season API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every autumn a residency program's administrative office becomes a bottleneck for thousands of career decisions. Applications arrive through ERAS faster than faculty can read them, interview invitations must be released in waves against a fixed number of seats, an interview committee must converge on a single ordered preference list, and the whole apparatus is bound by NRMP participation rules that forbid asking a candidate how they intend to rank the program. Programs that manage this on spreadsheets routinely double-book interviewers, certify rank lists containing gaps, or discover on match day that a position they believed filled was never underwritten by a signed contract.

The Residency Match Season API is the system of record for one NRMP participating program's entire recruitment season, from paper screening through contract activation. It models the season as an explicit phase machine so that no operation can happen out of order: reviews only during paper review, invitations only while interviews are open, ballots only during ranking, SOAP offers only after match day has published its results. It tracks two conserved quantities — the program's certified positions and each interview block's seats — and guarantees that every compensating action (a declined invitation, a cancelled slot, a rescinded contract) returns exactly what the corresponding forward action consumed. And because the segregation-of-duties expectations of a rank committee are the part most often violated in practice, the system encodes them as hard refusals rather than as policy documents: the program director who wrote a candidate's score card cannot be the one who certifies the list that ranks that candidate.

This document specifies the complete contract: the resources, their state machines, the arithmetic behind every derived score, the ordered set of refusals that guard each operation, and the role each actor plays. Deadlines, expiry windows and interval comparisons are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than the host wall clock, so an entire season can be replayed deterministically in a test harness.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, audit fields, precision, concurrency guards, and the fixed order in which the server refuses a bad request. Everything here applies to every endpoint unless a later section narrows it.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight recognised role names, or is a recognised role that is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles, spelled exactly as the header must carry them, are `ProgramDirector`, `Reviewer`, `Interviewer`, `CommitteeMember`, `ProgramCoordinator`, `Applicant`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `NrmpAdmin`.

Their authority divides as follows. A `ProgramDirector` owns the program's preference order and its irreversible commitments — opening the rank list, appending entries to it, sending it to committee, reviving a returned list, certifying it, withdrawing it, extending SOAP offers, rescinding a contract, and sending a submitted score card back for amendment. A `Reviewer` writes paper-screening reviews of applications and does nothing else that mutates state. An `Interviewer` opens, submits and revises rubric score cards for the applications their own panel seats hosted. A `CommitteeMember` casts one ballot per deliberation round on a rank list under review. A `ProgramCoordinator` is the operational staff role that runs the season's logistics: application intake, faculty registration, screening decisions, applicant withdrawal, wave planning, release and closure, invitation issuance, slot creation, completion and cancellation, panel staffing and release, phase advancement, running the match, and contract issuance and activation. An `Applicant` is the candidate: they answer invitations, book and re-book slots, accept or decline SOAP offers, sign the contract and submit credential documents. A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates flagged pre-match communications and verifies or rejects onboarding credentials, holding no program-operational authority of any kind. An `NrmpAdmin` is the national match operator: it registers participating programs, owns the logical clock, and is otherwise read-only across every program's season artifacts. The authoritative per-endpoint role list is the table in §4; where this paragraph and that table could be read differently, the table governs.

Four of the roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the primary key of a persisted row and ownership checks compare that value against the resource's own references:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and the segregation-of-duties rules therefore bind that individual personally.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Reviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Interviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` — an invitation, a SOAP offer, a contract, a credential item — compare the resource's chain back to that applicant against `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, a coordinator who logs a pre-match contact leaves `author_faculty_id` null on the resulting `CommunicationLog` precisely because their identity resolves to no `FacultyMember` row) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. A consequence worth stating plainly: because a coordinator is not a `FacultyMember`, coordinator actions never trip the conflict-of-interest rule.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `NrmpAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

When an entity-backed actor addresses a resource that does not belong to the entity whose `id` the header carries — an applicant answering another applicant's invitation, an interviewer scoring a panel seat that is not theirs, a faculty member acting on a target belonging to a different program than their own `program_id` — the request is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The same code covers the degenerate case: when an entity-backed actor's `X-User-ID` resolves to no persisted row at all — a `ProgramDirector`, `Reviewer`, `Interviewer` or `CommitteeMember` header value naming no `FacultyMember`, or an `Applicant` header value naming no `Applicant` — every ownership check on that request fails with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` rather than with a not-found.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the window begins; it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). A `limit` or `offset` that is omitted entirely, or supplied as an empty string, takes its default instead; a value that will not parse as an integer is likewise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Boolean-valued list filters accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` case-insensitively and reject anything else with `ERR_VALIDATION`, while an unrecognised query parameter is simply ignored.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The one documented exception is the entry listing of a single rank list, which is returned in ascending `rank_position` order because the list's meaning *is* that ordering. Because the logical clock of §2.7 only moves when an operator moves it, many rows share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the server first wrote the rows — a monotonic per-process creation sequence — and never by `id`. The rank-list entry listing breaks any `rank_position` tie the same way.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` counts all rows matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Season Phase: The Master Gate

The `Program` resource carries a computed `season_phase` that gates essentially every child operation in the API. It takes exactly one of eight values: `PRE_SEASON`, `REVIEW_OPEN`, `INTERVIEWS_OPEN`, `RANKING`, `LIST_CERTIFIED`, `MATCH_COMPLETE`, `SOAP_ACTIVE`, `SEASON_CLOSED`. A newly registered program begins in `PRE_SEASON`. The phase is never set directly by a client; it advances only along the transitions declared for the `Program` machine in §3, either because an operator asked for a specific `to_phase` or because a system cascade moved it.

`SEASON_CLOSED` is the intended resting phase, but it is **not** terminal. The `Program` machine has no terminal phases at all, because rescinding a contract vacates a filled position and re-opens a closed season back to `SOAP_ACTIVE`. The cycle `SEASON_CLOSED → SOAP_ACTIVE` is therefore a legitimate, declared edge and implementations must not model `SEASON_CLOSED` as an absorbing state.

When the governing `Program` is in a phase that does not permit the operation being requested, the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — the same code used when a resource's own state field does not admit the requested transition.

### 2.5. Hierarchical Consistency and Cascading Side Effects

Many operations in this system mutate more than one resource. A declined invitation frees a seat, which promotes a waitlisted candidate, which advances that candidate's interview posture, which changes the program's invitation counter. A rescinded contract vacates a match result, which restores a position, which can re-open a closed season. These chains are not best-effort: each hop named in §3 is part of the operation's contract, and an implementation that performs only the primary mutation is incorrect.

Whenever the server writes a resource — whether because a client addressed it directly or because a cascade reached it — that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by one. This applies to derived counters as much as to state fields: recomputing `Program.invited_count`, `Program.matched_count`, `Program.unfilled_positions`, `InterviewWave.issued_count`, `InterviewSlot.booked_count`, `FacultyMember.scheduled_load`, `FacultyMember.realized_load`, `Application.review_count`, `Application.committee_score` or `RankList.entry_count` is a mutation of the owning row and bumps that row's `version`. Because `version` is what the concurrency guards in §2.8 compare against, a client that reads a wave, watches a coordinator issue an invitation, and then writes against its stale reading will be refused. The increment is nevertheless idempotent within a single request: however many times one request and its cascades write the same row, that row's `version` rises by exactly one and its `updated_at` is stamped once, and a row the request itself created is observable at `version` 1 no matter how much later work in that same request touches it.

The server is also permitted to move a resource on its own initiative when the criteria for a declared system transition become true — a slot filling to capacity, a quorum being reached, an invitation lapsing — without any client request naming that resource.

### 2.6. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in this system — `Program`, `Applicant`, `Application`, `FacultyMember`, `ApplicationReview`, `InterviewWave`, `InterviewSlot`, `InterviewInvitation`, `InterviewAssignment`, `ScoreCard`, `RankList`, `RankListEntry`, `CommitteeVote`, `MatchResult`, `SoapOffer`, `Contract`, `CredentialItem` and `CommunicationLog` — carries the same four system-managed fields. No resource is exempt; there is no immutable sub-resource in this domain that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured formatting; clients never supply it.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation.

All other datetime-valued fields — `invite_release_at`, `response_deadline_at`, `start_at`, `end_at`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `responded_at`, `approved_at`, `rank_list_certified_at`, `published_at`, `offered_at`, `signed_at`, `verified_at`, `sent_at` — use the identical ISO 8601 UTC representation. A malformed instant supplied by a client is a shape violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 2.7. The Logical Season Clock

No comparison in this API reads the host wall clock. Every deadline, expiry, window and interval test reads a single server-held **logical current UTC instant**, which an `NrmpAdmin` sets by supplying a `current_utc` value. On process startup the logical clock is initialised to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; a freshly started server that never receives a clock call behaves exactly as though the current instant were `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A clock call answers 200 OK with a `data` object holding the single field `current_utc`, echoing the instant now in force in the same three-fraction-digit form; it mints no row and touches no resource's `version`.

Two absolute instants are fixed by national policy rather than by any program's data. The rank list certification deadline is `2027-02-25T21:00:00Z`; certification is permitted while the logical clock is at or before that instant and refused once the clock has passed it. That is the only hard calendar deadline in the system; every other window is relative — an invitation's response window, a SOAP offer's response window, the mandatory gap between two waves.

### 2.8. Score Precision and Pinned Rounding Order

Four derived numbers in this system are decimal: `Application.provisional_screen_score`, `Application.final_screen_score`, `Application.committee_score`, and the two `FacultyMember` load figures `scheduled_load` and `realized_load`. All five are carried to exactly **2 decimal places** using **round half up**, and in each case the rounding is applied **once**, to the final total, not progressively to intermediate terms. `ScoreCard.domain_average` is the one exception to the two-place rule: it is carried to exactly **1 decimal place**, also round half up. The percentages in the pipeline report are carried to exactly **1 decimal place**, round half up.

One rounding order in this system is deliberately counter-intuitive and is pinned by contract. The committee roll-up rounds **each score card's four-domain mean to one decimal place first**, and only then averages those already-rounded per-card figures and rounds that average to two decimal places. The alternative — summing all four domain scores across all cards, dividing by four times the card count, and rounding once — produces a different answer for most inputs and **must not** be used. §3.6 states the arithmetic in full.

The other pinned order concerns the SOAP offer budget, where the reserve credit appears to depend on the very budget it feeds. It does not: the tier is selected from the raw vacancy count computed before any reserve is considered, and is never re-derived from the resulting budget. Implementations must not iterate to a fixed point. §3.8 states the arithmetic in full.

Decimal values are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, not strings. Integer-valued fields — counters, quotas, scores out of 300, rubric domains out of 10, `salary_cents`, `soap_round`, `rank_position`, `wave_index`, `review_round` — are integers throughout, with no rounding step at all.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Wave and List Writes

Exactly two write endpoints in this API take a version guard. Both are append-style operations against a parent whose ordering a second writer could legitimately supersede, which is why nothing in the parent's own state can reject them and the stale-version check is the only protection available.

**Appending a rank list entry.** A `POST /rank-lists/{id}/entries` request must carry `expected_rank_list_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the `version` of the target `RankList` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the list's current `version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RANK_LIST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second writer appending a different application at a different `rank_position` would otherwise succeed against the natural product semantics — nothing in the list's state forbids it — so this check is what protects a client whose view of the list's ordering has gone out of date.

**Issuing an interview invitation.** A `POST /interview-waves/{id}/invitations` request must carry `expected_wave_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the `version` of the target `InterviewWave` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the wave's current `version`, the issue is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WAVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Issuing an invitation consumes a seat and can supersede the wave's waitlist ordering, so a second issue for a different application would itself succeed under the natural semantics; the guard protects a coordinator whose seat-count view predates the earlier issue.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is neither required nor honoured. On both guarded endpoints the guard field is an integer of 0 or above, so a missing, non-integer or negative value is a shape violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` before any version comparison is attempted.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and every forward action that consumes one must be matched exactly by the compensating action that releases it.

**Program positions.** For every `Program` at every observable moment, `quota_positions` equals `matched_count` plus `unfilled_positions`. `matched_count` is the number of `MatchResult` rows for the program whose `outcome` is `MATCHED`; `unfilled_positions` is `quota_positions` minus `matched_count`. A program is registered with `matched_count` at 0 and `unfilled_positions` equal to `quota_positions`. Running the match fills positions; accepting a SOAP offer fills one more; rescinding a contract vacates one and returns it to the unfilled pool. No path may leave the identity broken.

**Slot seats.** For every `InterviewSlot`, `applicant_capacity` minus `booked_count` is the free seat pool, where `booked_count` is the number of `InterviewInvitation` rows whose `slot_id` is this slot and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Every accept consumes one seat; every decline-after-accept, reschedule-away, applicant withdrawal and slot cancellation restores exactly what it consumed. The slot's `status` follows occupancy in both directions: it moves `OPEN → BOOKED` when the pool empties and back `BOOKED → OPEN` the moment the pool is non-empty again.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Conflict of Interest

Two independent integrity rules constrain who may act, and they are distinct — one is about the same person holding two incompatible roles in one decision, the other is about a declared personal relationship with a candidate.

**Segregation of duties** governs rank list certification only. The `FacultyMember` certifying a list must have authored no `ScoreCard` for any `Application` ranked on that list, and must have cast no `CommitteeVote` for that list in its current `review_round`. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). Note the asymmetry deliberately built in: the ballot bar looks only at the current round, so a director who voted in a round that was later returned and superseded is not barred, whereas the score-card bar has no round scoping at all.

**Conflict of interest** operates on the `conflict_applicant_ids` array that each `FacultyMember` declares. A faculty member whose array contains the subject `Applicant`'s `id` may neither author a review of that applicant's application nor take a panel seat on a slot that applicant is booked into. Either violation is refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` (HTTP 422). Because a `ProgramCoordinator` resolves to no faculty row, coordinator-driven operations never consult this array.

### 2.12. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and the Ordered Refusal Table

Requests whose URL carries more than one resource identifier are resolved strictly left to right. A request against the credential packet of a contract, for example, confirms that the contract exists before it looks at anything else, and the 404 returned names the first identifier in the path that resolves to nothing. After path identifiers are resolved, foreign keys supplied in the request body are resolved. Every such failure carries the single code `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

More generally, when a request would fail several checks at once, the server reports the **first** failure in the following fixed priority order. This ordering is normative: it determines which of several plausible error codes a client actually observes, and implementations that evaluate checks in a different order will report the wrong code.

1. **Priority 1 — authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Priority 2 — authorisation.** `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles *and* that role is listed for the endpoint being called. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Priority 3 — existence.** Every resource named in the URL path exists, checked left to right; then every foreign key supplied in the request body exists. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Priority 4 — request shape.** Every field the endpoint requires is present, every field has its declared type, every enum-valued field holds a declared member, and every numeric field lies inside its declared range — including `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at 0 or above on list endpoints. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 422. Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Priority 5 — state admissibility.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and the governing `Program.season_phase` permits the operation. This covers both a wrong current state and an already-terminal resource. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409. Applies to every state-transition endpoint. **Scope boundary:** this priority evaluates only state-field admissibility plus the phase gate. Where a transition's stated precondition restates a rule that a *later* priority in this table owns under its own code — the invitation and SOAP expiry instants, seat and budget capacity, evaluation counts, ownership, conflict of interest, segregation of duties, the certification deadline — the specific code at the later priority is the one reported, and priority 5 does not pre-empt it.
6. **Priority 6 — stale parent version, rank list.** On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/entries`, the supplied `expected_rank_list_version` equals the addressed `RankList`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_RANK_LIST_VERSION`, HTTP 412.
7. **Priority 6 — stale parent version, wave.** On `POST /interview-waves/{id}/invitations`, the supplied `expected_wave_version` equals the addressed `InterviewWave`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_WAVE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. This is the same check as the row above, instantiated on the other parent; the two share priority 6.
8. **Priority 7 — segregation of duties.** On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/certify`, the acting program director authored no `ScoreCard` for any `Application` ranked on the list and cast no `CommitteeVote` for the list in its current `review_round`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 422.
9. **Priority 7 — conflict of interest.** On `POST /applications/{id}/reviews` and on `POST /interview-assignments`, the acting or targeted `FacultyMember` does not list the subject `Applicant`'s `id` in `conflict_applicant_ids`. Otherwise `ERR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`, HTTP 422. Those two endpoints are the complete scope.
10. **Priority 7 — unresolved communication flag.** On `POST /soap-offers` and on `POST /programs/{id}/run-match`, the subject `Application` has no `CommunicationLog` row whose `compliance_status` is `FLAGGED`. Otherwise `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMUNICATION_FLAG`, HTTP 422. Those two endpoints are the complete scope.
11. **Priority 8 — acting-identity ownership.** For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to the entity that owns the target: the `Applicant` behind the `Application` of an invitation, offer, contract or credential item; the `FacultyMember` behind an assignment's score card; the `FacultyMember` whose `program_id` matches the target's program. Otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, HTTP 403. Applies to every `Applicant`-acting and every `FacultyMember`-acting endpoint.
12. **Priority 8 — cross-aggregate coherence.** On `POST /interview-invitations/{id}/accept` the chosen slot's `wave_id` equals the invitation's own `wave_id`; on `POST /score-cards` the supplied `application_id` names an application booked into the assignment's slot; on `POST /rank-lists/{id}/entries` a supplied `tie_group_anchor_entry_id` references an entry on the same `rank_list_id`, and the ranked application belongs to the list's own program. Otherwise `ERR_CROSS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422. Those three endpoints are the complete scope.
13. **Priority 9 — invitation not lapsed.** On `POST /interview-invitations/{id}/accept`, the logical clock is strictly before the invitation's `expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_INVITATION_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409.
14. **Priority 9 — SOAP offer not lapsed.** On `POST /soap-offers/{id}/accept` and on `POST /soap-offers/{id}/decline`, the logical clock is strictly before the offer's `expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SOAP_OFFER_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409. Both endpoints are in scope.
15. **Priority 9 — interviewer double-booking.** On `POST /interview-assignments`, the assigned `FacultyMember` holds no other `ASSIGNED` `InterviewAssignment` whose slot interval intersects the target slot's interval, where intervals are **half-open** — they include `start_at` and exclude `end_at` — and **no buffer** is applied. Exact adjacency, one slot ending at the instant the next begins, does not intersect and is permitted. Otherwise `ERR_INTERVIEWER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, HTTP 409. An existing assignment on the *target slot itself* is never counted as an intersecting other assignment, so a repeat staffing of the same slot-and-faculty-member pair falls through to the uniqueness rule below and is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, never as a double booking.
16. **Priority 9 — wave window separation.** On `POST /interview-waves`, the new wave's **closed** window from `invite_release_at` through `response_deadline_at` inclusive must be separated from every existing wave of the same program by at least 48 hours (two full days). Concretely, the create succeeds only when the new wave's `invite_release_at` is at or after the other wave's `response_deadline_at` plus 48 hours, or the new wave's `response_deadline_at` plus 48 hours is at or before the other wave's `invite_release_at`. Exact adjacency is **rejected** here, unlike the interviewer rule. Otherwise `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409.
17. **Priority 9 — slot timing.** On `POST /interview-slots`, `end_at` is strictly after `start_at` and the whole slot interval lies inside its wave's closed window from `invite_release_at` through `response_deadline_at`. On `POST /interview-slots/{id}/cancel` and on `POST /interview-invitations/{id}/reschedule`, the logical clock is strictly before the slot's `start_at`. On `POST /interview-slots/{id}/complete`, the logical clock has reached the slot's `end_at` or later. Otherwise `ERR_SLOT_TIMING_VIOLATION`, HTTP 409. Those four endpoints are the complete scope.
18. **Priority 9 — certification deadline.** On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/certify`, the logical clock is at or before `2027-02-25T21:00:00Z`. Otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, HTTP 409.
19. **Priority 10 — uniqueness.** `Program.nrmp_program_code` is unique across all programs; `Applicant.eras_id` is unique across all applicants; `InterviewWave.wave_index` is unique within a `program_id`; at most one `Application` exists per program-and-applicant pair; at most one `ApplicationReview` exists per application-and-reviewer pair; at most one `InterviewAssignment` exists per slot-and-faculty-member pair regardless of that assignment's `status`, so an already-`RELEASED` seat still blocks a re-staffing of the same pair; at most one `ScoreCard` exists per assignment-and-application pair; at most one `CommitteeVote` exists per rank-list, faculty-member and `review_round` triple; at most one `RankListEntry` exists per rank-list-and-application pair; `rank_position` is unique within a `rank_list_id`; at most one non-`WITHDRAWN` `RankList` exists per `program_id`; at most one `Contract` exists per `match_result_id`. Any breach is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, HTTP 409. Applies to every create endpoint.
20. **Priority 10 — screening filters.** On `POST /applications/{id}/screen` with an advance decision, the applicant's `step2_score` is at least the program's `min_step2_score`, an `IMG` applicant is admitted only when the program's `img_accepted` is true, and an applicant whose `visa_status` needs sponsorship is admitted only when the program's `visa_sponsorship_allowed` is true. Otherwise `ERR_SCREENING_FILTER_FAILED`, HTTP 422.
21. **Priority 10 — evaluation sufficiency.** On `POST /applications/{id}/screen`, the application's `review_count` is at least 2. On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/submit-to-committee`, every ranked application carries at least 2 `ScoreCard` rows whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED`. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATIONS`, HTTP 422. Both endpoints are in scope.
22. **Priority 10 — rank position contiguity.** On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/entries`, the supplied `rank_position` equals the list's current `entry_count` plus one exactly, so the list is built append-only. On `POST /rank-lists/{id}/submit-to-committee`, the set of `rank_position` values on the list is exactly the whole numbers from 1 through `entry_count` with no gap and no repeat. Otherwise `ERR_RANK_POSITION_SEQUENCE`, HTTP 422. Both endpoints are in scope.
23. **Priority 11 — slot seat available.** On `POST /interview-invitations/{id}/accept`, the chosen slot's `booked_count` is strictly less than its `applicant_capacity`. Otherwise `ERR_SLOT_FULL`, HTTP 409. This rule, not the slot's state field, owns the full-seat case.
24. **Priority 11 — wave headroom.** On `POST /interview-waves/{id}/invitations`, the wave must be able to absorb the request either as an issued seat or as a waitlist place. Reaching `seat_capacity` does not reject the create — it changes the created invitation's `status` to `WAITLISTED`. `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_REACHED` (HTTP 409) fires only when no waitlist place can absorb the request either: the wave's waitlist has itself reached `seat_capacity`, or the wave is `CLOSED`, or the program has moved past `INTERVIEWS_OPEN`.
25. **Priority 11 — SOAP budget.** On `POST /soap-offers`, the count of `SoapOffer` rows for the program whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` must remain at or below the program's `soap_offer_budget` after the create. Otherwise `ERR_SOAP_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409.
26. **Priority 11 — rank list length.** On both `POST /rank-lists/{id}/entries` and `POST /rank-lists/{id}/submit-to-committee`, the list's `entry_count` must be at or below `quota_positions` multiplied by 10. Otherwise `ERR_RANK_LIST_TOO_LONG`, HTTP 409. Both endpoints are in scope.
27. **Priority 11 — complete onboarding packet.** On `POST /contracts/{id}/activate`, the contract's `verified_credential_count` equals its `mandatory_credential_count`. Otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIALS_INCOMPLETE`, HTTP 409.
28. **Priority 11 — position availability.** On `POST /soap-offers/{id}/accept`, the program's `unfilled_positions` is at least 1, so no program fills more than `quota_positions` positions. Otherwise `ERR_NO_POSITIONS_AVAILABLE`, HTTP 409. Match day never raises this code, because the match algorithm stops promoting entries once the quota is reached and publishes the remainder as unmatched instead.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Season

A season begins with registration by the national operator. An `NrmpAdmin` registers a participating program by supplying its display `name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), its NRMP-assigned `nrmp_program_code` (non-empty and unique across every program on the platform), its ACGME `specialty_code` abbreviation such as IM, EM or PEDS (non-empty), and `quota_positions` — the number of PGY-1 positions the program is certified to fill, which must be at least 1. Four screening and policy switches are optional at registration and carry defaults when omitted: `visa_sponsorship_allowed` defaults to false, `soap_participating` defaults to false, `min_step2_score` defaults to 220 and must lie between 1 and 300 inclusive, and `img_accepted` defaults to false. The program materialises in `PRE_SEASON` with `matched_count` at 0, `unfilled_positions` equal to `quota_positions`, `invited_count` at 0, `soap_offer_budget` at 0, and `rank_list_certified_at` null. The registration response therefore reports `soap_offer_budget` as exactly 0, even though the §3.8 derivation applied to a fresh program would already yield a positive figure; that derived figure governs every later program response body instead. Programs can afterwards be listed with an optional `specialty_code` or `season_phase` filter.

The applicant registry is likewise owned by the national operator. Each `Applicant` carries an `eras_id` (non-empty and unique across the registry), a non-empty legal `full_name`, a `medical_school_type` that is one of `US_MD`, `US_DO` or `IMG`, a `visa_status` that is one of `US_CITIZEN`, `PERMANENT_RESIDENT`, `NEEDS_J1` or `NEEDS_H1B`, and a three-digit USMLE Step 2 CK `step2_score` between 1 and 300 inclusive. Two further attributes are optional and exist for the pipeline report described in §3.9: `pipeline_program_alum` records whether the candidate came through a recognised pipeline or outreach programme and defaults to false, and `self_identified_group` is a free-text demographic self-description that is stored as null when the applicant declines to report. The registry can be filtered on `medical_school_type` and on `visa_status`.

Faculty are registered per program, by a coordinator or by the national operator, against the program's `program_id` and with a non-empty `full_name`. Three optional attributes shape what that member may later do: `is_program_director` defaults to false and exactly the members carrying it may certify the program's rank list, subject to §2.11; `is_committee_member` defaults to false and gates the right to cast a deliberation ballot; and `conflict_applicant_ids` defaults to the empty array and holds the `Applicant` identifiers this member has declared a conflict with — every identifier in it is resolved as a body foreign key, so an entry naming no `Applicant` row is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, while an array holding a non-string or empty-string element is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Two derived load figures, `scheduled_load` and `realized_load`, both start at 0.0 and are described in §3.5. Faculty listings accept `program_id`, `is_program_director` and `is_committee_member` filters.

An `Application` is created by a coordinator or the national operator with a payload naming the `program_id` and the `applicant_id`, and it comes into being with `screening_status` `RECEIVED` and `interview_status` `NOT_INVITED`. Those two enumerations are **independent state dimensions on the same row**: they share no state names, and no movement on one dimension writes the other except where a side effect explicitly says so. `screening_status` tracks paper review only, taking one of `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ADVANCED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, of which `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal. `interview_status` tracks interview progress and takes one of `NOT_INVITED`, `INVITED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, of which `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; it evolves freely once `screening_status` has reached `ADVANCED`. Applications can be listed filtered by `program_id`, by `applicant_id`, or by either dimension using `screening_status` and `interview_status`, and a single application read exposes both dimensions together with all three computed scores.

Phase advancement is requested by naming the desired phase in a `to_phase` field, and each hop has its own gate. A coordinator moves the program `PRE_SEASON → REVIEW_OPEN` only when at least one `FacultyMember` exists for that `program_id`; the only side effect is that the program's `version` increments by one. A coordinator moves it `REVIEW_OPEN → INTERVIEWS_OPEN` only when at least one `Application` under that program has reached `screening_status` `ADVANCED`; again the program's `version` increments. A coordinator moves it `INTERVIEWS_OPEN → RANKING` only when **every** `InterviewWave` belonging to the program has `status` `CLOSED` — a single wave still `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` blocks the hop — and once more the `version` increments. The remaining phases are reached from ranking, match day and SOAP, and are described in §3.7 and §3.8. Any requested hop that is not one of the declared edges, or whose gate is unmet, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The edges a client may name in `to_phase` are exactly these six: `PRE_SEASON → REVIEW_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN → INTERVIEWS_OPEN`, `INTERVIEWS_OPEN → RANKING`, `MATCH_COMPLETE → SOAP_ACTIVE`, `MATCH_COMPLETE → SEASON_CLOSED` and `SOAP_ACTIVE → SEASON_CLOSED`. The machine's three remaining edges — `RANKING → LIST_CERTIFIED`, `LIST_CERTIFIED → MATCH_COMPLETE` and `SEASON_CLOSED → SOAP_ACTIVE` — are driven only by certification, by match day and by the rescission cascade respectively, so naming one of them on this endpoint is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the program is sitting in the source phase and the underlying conditions hold. The edge-admissibility test runs before the hop's own gate, so an undeclared hop reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` without any gate being consulted.

### 3.2. Paper Review, the Composite Screen Score, and the Screening Decision

Paper review runs only while the program sits in `REVIEW_OPEN`. A `Reviewer` submits a review of an application by supplying three sub-scores: `academic_points` between 0 and 40 inclusive, `experience_points` between 0 and 30 inclusive, and `holistic_points` between 0 and 30 inclusive. The authoring reviewer is **not** taken from the request body — the resulting `ApplicationReview` stores its `reviewer_id` from the `X-User-ID` header of the acting faculty member, alongside the `application_id` from the path. Two further gates apply beyond the phase: the acting `FacultyMember`'s `program_id` must equal the application's `program_id`, and the application's `applicant_id` must not appear in that member's `conflict_applicant_ids`, the latter failing as `ERR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. At most one review may exist per application-and-reviewer pair; a second attempt by the same reviewer is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Reviews can be listed filtered by `application_id` or by `reviewer_id`, and a single review read exposes its three domain point awards.

The first submitted review moves the paper dimension `RECEIVED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Every subsequent review is a self-transition `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`. Both carry the identical trio of side effects: an `ApplicationReview` is created in a submitted state, the application's `review_count` increments by one, and `Application.provisional_screen_score` is recomputed. `review_count` is by definition the number of submitted `ApplicationReview` rows for the application.

The **composite screen score** is a weighted roll-up with two variants that differ only in their bonus treatment. Each individual review contributes a raw figure equal to its `academic_points` multiplied by 0.45, plus its `experience_points` multiplied by 0.30, plus its `holistic_points` multiplied by 0.25. The **base** is the arithmetic mean of those raw figures across every `ApplicationReview` row for the application, and the base is deliberately **not** rounded at this step. Because exactly one review per reviewer per application may exist, the included set never holds a superseded row. When the application has no reviews at all, the base is 0.0 and no division is performed.

Two bonuses sit on top of the base. The **Step 2 band bonus** is selected from the applicant's `step2_score` in a strict cascade: a score of at least 250 earns 6.0; otherwise a score of at least 235 earns 4.0; otherwise a score of at least 220 earns 2.0; anything lower earns 0.0. The **pipeline bonus** is 3.0 when the applicant's `pipeline_program_alum` is true and 0.0 otherwise.

`provisional_screen_score` is **Variant A**: the base alone, rounded half up to two decimal places, with the rounding applied once at the very end. Neither bonus is applied to it and its ceiling never binds; it defaults to 0.0 on a fresh application and is refreshed on every review submission. `final_screen_score` is **Variant B**: the base plus the Step 2 band bonus plus the pipeline bonus, then capped at a ceiling of 40.0, then rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end. It defaults to null and is written exactly once, by the screening decision transition. For identical review data the two variants therefore differ by precisely the sum of the two bonuses, unless the ceiling binds and truncates the difference. `final_screen_score` is also the ranking key for waitlist promotion, which is why it matters that it is written at screening time rather than lazily.

A `ProgramCoordinator` records the screening decision by supplying a `decision` field. An advance decision drives `UNDER_REVIEW → ADVANCED` and a reject decision drives `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`; both require the program to be in `REVIEW_OPEN` and both require `review_count` to be at least 2, the shortfall reported as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATIONS`. Both branches carry the identical single side effect: `Application.final_screen_score` is written using Variant B. The advance branch additionally enforces the program's three declared filters — the applicant's `step2_score` at or above the program's `min_step2_score`, an `IMG` applicant admitted only when `img_accepted` is true, and an applicant whose `visa_status` is `NEEDS_J1` or `NEEDS_H1B` admitted only when `visa_sponsorship_allowed` is true — and reports any shortfall as `ERR_SCREENING_FILTER_FAILED`. The reject branch applies no filter of its own; a candidate who fails a filter is refused advancement, not silently rejected.

Withdrawal is the applicant's own prerogative and is permitted from three paper states — `RECEIVED → WITHDRAWN`, `UNDER_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN`, and `ADVANCED → WITHDRAWN` — in every case only when the acting applicant's `id` equals the application's `applicant_id`. All three store the optional free-text `withdrawn_reason` from the request body, which is null when omitted. Withdrawing from `RECEIVED` or from `UNDER_REVIEW` reaches no invitation and no slot seat, because no invitation can exist yet; it nevertheless runs the same counter-recomputation tail as the `ADVANCED` case, so the owning `Program`'s row is rewritten and its `version` increments by one on every withdrawal whatever paper state it started from. Withdrawing from `ADVANCED` fires a full cascade: every `InterviewInvitation` for the application whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `WAITLISTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`; an invitation that was `ACCEPTED` also becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its booked `InterviewSlot`'s `booked_count` decrements by one, and that slot returns `BOOKED → OPEN` once `booked_count` is below `applicant_capacity`; the application's `interview_status` is set to `CANCELLED` when it was `INVITED` or `SCHEDULED`, which is exactly the pair of edges `INVITED → CANCELLED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` on the interview dimension; and `Program.invited_count` is recomputed. Withdrawal also decrements the affected wave's `issued_count` by one for each `ISSUED` and each `ACCEPTED` invitation it terminates, while a `WAITLISTED` invitation moving to `WITHDRAWN` has no counter effect, because a waitlist place never consumed an issued seat.

### 3.3. Planning Invitation Waves and Interview Blocks

Interview season is organised into **waves** — batches of invitations released together against a fixed number of seats. A `ProgramCoordinator` plans a wave by supplying the owning `program_id`, a `wave_index` that is a 1-based ordinal at least 1 and unique within the program, an `invite_release_at` instant at which the wave's invitations become releasable, a `response_deadline_at` instant that must fall strictly after `invite_release_at` and after which an issued invitation in this wave lapses, and a `seat_capacity` of at least 1 that caps how many invitations the wave may hold in `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED` state. A `response_deadline_at` that is not strictly after `invite_release_at` is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`, not as a window or timing code. The wave materialises with `status` `PLANNED` and `issued_count` at 0, where `issued_count` is by definition the number of invitations in the wave whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`. Waves are listed with optional `program_id` and `status` filters, and a single wave read exposes `issued_count` and the current `version` that the concurrency guard of §2.9 compares against.

Wave windows may not crowd each other. The window a wave occupies is the **closed** interval running from `invite_release_at` through `response_deadline_at` inclusive, and a new wave's window must stand clear of every existing wave of the same program by at least 48 hours — two full days of separation. The create succeeds only when the new wave's `invite_release_at` falls at or after the other wave's `response_deadline_at` plus that 48-hour gap, or the new wave's `response_deadline_at` plus that gap falls at or before the other wave's `invite_release_at`. Windows that merely touch are **rejected**, which is the opposite convention to the interviewer overlap rule in §3.5, and a violation is reported as `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Every wave already registered for the program is compared, including waves that have since reached `CLOSED`, and the separation test runs ahead of the `wave_index` uniqueness test, so a create that breaches both reports `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Releasing a wave drives `PLANNED → RELEASED` and requires two things: the owning program must be in `INTERVIEWS_OPEN`, and the logical clock must have reached the wave's `invite_release_at` or passed it — a release attempted before that instant is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not a slot-timing code, because §2.12 confines the timing code to the four endpoints it names there. The wave's `version` increments by one. Closing a wave drives `RELEASED → CLOSED` and has no preconditions at all — a coordinator may close a released wave at any moment — but it is the single most consequential operation in the interview phase, and its side-effect set is described in §3.4. `CLOSED` is terminal for a wave, and because the program cannot leave `INTERVIEWS_OPEN` until every wave is closed, closure is the gate on the whole ranking phase.

Inside a wave a coordinator creates **interview blocks**. Each `InterviewSlot` names its parent `wave_id`, a `start_at` and an `end_at` where `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`, a non-empty `room_label` identifying a physical or virtual room, and an `applicant_capacity` of at least 1 stating how many applicants the block can host. The slot's whole interval must also lie wholly inside its wave's closed window from `invite_release_at` through `response_deadline_at`; a block that pokes out at either end, or whose `end_at` is not strictly after its `start_at`, is refused with `ERR_SLOT_TIMING_VIOLATION`. A new block starts with `status` `OPEN` and `booked_count` at 0. Block creation is gated on nothing beyond those two timing rules: neither the parent wave's `status` nor the program's `season_phase` is consulted, so a block may legitimately be added to a wave that is still `PLANNED` or already `CLOSED`. Slots are listed with optional `wave_id` and `status` filters, and a single slot read exposes `booked_count`.

A slot's `status` is one of `OPEN`, `BOOKED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, the last two terminal. `OPEN` and `BOOKED` form a genuine two-way edge driven purely by seat occupancy, which is what makes the seat-conservation invariant of §2.10 observable from the outside: an accept that raises `booked_count` to `applicant_capacity` drives `OPEN → BOOKED`, and any release that leaves `booked_count` strictly below `applicant_capacity` drives `BOOKED → OPEN`. Both edges are system-driven; no client requests them.

### 3.4. Issuing, Answering and Promoting Invitations

Issuing an invitation is a coordinator action against a specific wave, carrying the `application_id` to invite and the `expected_wave_version` guard described in §2.9. The named application must belong to the wave's own `program_id`; a cross-program application is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since §2.12 confines `ERR_CROSS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` to three other endpoints. Whether the invitation lands issued or waitlisted is decided by the wave's headroom at that instant. While `issued_count` is strictly below `seat_capacity`, the invitation is created with `status` `ISSUED`, its `issued_at` stamped with the current logical clock instant, and its `expires_at` set to **the earlier of** the wave's `response_deadline_at` and `issued_at` plus 120 hours — a five-day response window. Once `issued_count` has reached `seat_capacity`, the invitation is created with `status` `WAITLISTED` instead, with both `issued_at` and `expires_at` left null, and no seat consumed. The waitlist is not unbounded: it may itself grow only until the number of `WAITLISTED` invitations in the wave reaches `seat_capacity`. When both the issued seats and the waitlist are exhausted, or the wave is already `CLOSED`, or the program has moved past `INTERVIEWS_OPEN` so no waitlist can absorb the request, the create is refused with `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_REACHED`. Reaching `seat_capacity` alone is never a refusal — it is a routing decision.

Creating an issued invitation carries four further effects: `InterviewWave.issued_count` increments by one, the invited application advances `NOT_INVITED → INVITED` on its interview dimension (which requires the application's `screening_status` to be `ADVANCED` — an unadvanced application cannot be invited at all), `Program.invited_count` is recomputed, and the wave's `version` increments by one. `Program.invited_count` is by definition the number of applications under the program whose `interview_status` is anything other than `NOT_INVITED`. A waitlisted create still increments the wave's `version` and still recomputes the program counter, but consumes no seat and leaves the application's interview dimension untouched. Invitations can be listed filtered by `application_id`, `wave_id`, `slot_id` or `status`, and a single invitation read exposes `expires_at` and the booked `slot_id`. An invitation's `slot_id` is null whenever its `status` is `ISSUED`, `WAITLISTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`; it holds a value only while the invitation is `ACCEPTED`.

An `Applicant` **accepts** an invitation by naming the `slot_id` they wish to book, which drives `ISSUED → ACCEPTED`. Six conditions gate it: the acting applicant's `id` equals the `applicant_id` of the invitation's application; the logical clock is strictly before the invitation's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVITATION_EXPIRED`; the wave's `status` is `RELEASED`; the requested slot's `wave_id` equals the invitation's own `wave_id`, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`; the requested slot's `status` is neither `COMPLETED` nor `CANCELLED`; and no other invitation for the same `application_id` is already `ACCEPTED`. Note carefully that the full-seat case is **not** owned by the slot's state check — a slot whose seats are gone is refused by the seat-availability rule as `ERR_SLOT_FULL`, at the last priority tier, not as an invalid state. Acceptance stores the chosen `slot_id` and stamps `responded_at` with the current logical clock instant, increments the slot's `booked_count` by one, moves the slot `OPEN → BOOKED` when `booked_count` reaches `applicant_capacity`, and advances the application `INVITED → SCHEDULED` on its interview dimension — which itself requires that the accepted invitation's `slot_id` is no longer null.

**Rescheduling** lets an applicant hand back a booked seat to choose another, driving `ACCEPTED → ISSUED`. It requires that the acting applicant owns the application, that the wave's `status` is `RELEASED`, that the logical clock is strictly before the wave's `response_deadline_at`, and that the logical clock is strictly before the currently booked slot's `start_at` — the last of these reported as `ERR_SLOT_TIMING_VIOLATION`. Four effects follow: the previously booked slot's `booked_count` decrements by one and that slot returns `BOOKED → OPEN` when `booked_count` falls below `applicant_capacity`; the invitation's `slot_id` and `responded_at` are both cleared to null; `issued_at` is re-stamped with the current logical clock instant and `expires_at` is recomputed as the earlier of the wave's `response_deadline_at` and the new `issued_at` plus 120 hours, so the response window genuinely restarts; and the application reverts `SCHEDULED → INVITED`.

**Declining** has two shapes with deliberately different consequences. Declining an invitation that is merely `ISSUED` drives `ISSUED → DECLINED`, requires only that the acting applicant owns the application and that the wave is `RELEASED`, and produces five effects: `responded_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant; the wave's `issued_count` decrements by one; the waitlist promotion winner, if the wave has any eligible waitlisted candidate, is promoted `WAITLISTED → ISSUED`; the declining application reverts `INVITED → NOT_INVITED` when it retains no invitation whatsoever in `ISSUED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED`; and `Program.invited_count` is recomputed, which rewrites the program row and increments its `version` on every decline whether or not the counter's value actually moves. Declining an invitation that is already `ACCEPTED` drives `ACCEPTED → DECLINED`, adds the requirement that the logical clock be strictly before the booked slot's `start_at` — reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because §2.12 does not place decline in the slot-timing scope — and fires the system's flagship four-hop cascade: **hop 1** decrements the booked slot's `booked_count` by one and returns that slot `BOOKED → OPEN` when `booked_count` falls below `applicant_capacity`; **hop 2** treats that freed seat as an opening and promotes the waitlist winner `WAITLISTED → ISSUED`; **hop 3** advances the promoted invitation's own application `NOT_INVITED → INVITED`; **hop 4** recomputes `Program.invited_count` from the changed set of invited applications. The declining application itself reverts `SCHEDULED → NOT_INVITED` when it retains no invitation in `ISSUED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED`.

The **waitlist promotion selector** decides which waitlisted invitation wins a freed seat, and it runs whenever a seat frees in a `RELEASED` wave — on a decline, on a reschedule, or on an applicant withdrawal. Its candidate set is every `InterviewInvitation` in that wave whose `status` is `WAITLISTED`, whose application's `screening_status` is `ADVANCED`, and whose application's `interview_status` is `NOT_INVITED`; that last clause is what excludes an application already re-invited elsewhere in the same wave. Candidates are ordered by their application's `final_screen_score` in **descending** order, and ties are broken by the invitation's own `created_at` in **ascending** order — earliest waitlisted first. The winner is the first candidate in that order, and exactly one invitation is promoted per freed seat. The random `id` field must not participate in this ordering, nor in any other selector in this specification. A candidate whose application carries a null `final_screen_score` sorts as though that score were −1, below every written score, and when two waitlisted invitations share both score and `created_at` the earlier-written row wins, by the same creation sequence that breaks listing ties in §2.3. When the candidate set is empty, no promotion occurs, the freed seat simply stays available, and this is **not** an error. A promotion stamps the promoted invitation's `issued_at` with the current logical clock instant, sets its `expires_at` to the earlier of the wave's `response_deadline_at` and that `issued_at` plus 120 hours, increments the wave's `issued_count` by one, and advances the promoted application `NOT_INVITED → INVITED`. Promotion is itself gated: the wave must be `RELEASED`, and the wave's `issued_count` must be strictly below `seat_capacity` after the seat release.

Invitations lapse in two ways. An `ISSUED` invitation drives `ISSUED → EXPIRED` either because the logical clock has reached or passed its `expires_at` on a lapse sweep, or because its wave is closing; the effects are that the wave's `issued_count` decrements by one and the application reverts `INVITED → NOT_INVITED` when it retains no invitation in `ISSUED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED`. A `WAITLISTED` invitation drives `WAITLISTED → EXPIRED` only because its wave is closing, and that transition has no side effects at all, since a waitlist place held no seat. Closing a wave is therefore the wholesale application of both: it drives the wave `RELEASED → CLOSED`, sets every invitation in the wave whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `WAITLISTED` to `EXPIRED`, stamps `expires_at` with the closing instant on any such invitation that had none — which is every `WAITLISTED` one, since a waitlist place is created with `expires_at` null — reverts `INVITED → NOT_INVITED` for each application whose last active invitation was thereby expired, recomputes the wave's `issued_count`, recomputes `Program.invited_count`, and increments the wave's `version` by one.

Applicant withdrawal, described in §3.2, reaches invitations through three further edges. `ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` fires when the owning application's `screening_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and decrements the wave's `issued_count` by one. `WAITLISTED → WITHDRAWN` fires on the same trigger and has no side effects. `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN` fires on the same trigger and does both jobs: the booked slot's `booked_count` decrements by one and that slot returns `BOOKED → OPEN` when `booked_count` falls below `applicant_capacity`, and the wave's `issued_count` decrements by one. `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the invitation's three terminal states; `WAITLISTED → ISSUED` is a genuine promotion reverse edge and `ISSUED → ACCEPTED → ISSUED` is a genuine reschedule cycle, so this lifecycle is cyclic rather than a pipeline.

### 3.5. Staffing Panels, Interviewer Load, and Running the Block

Staffing an interviewer onto a block is a coordinator action carrying the `slot_id` being staffed, the `faculty_member_id` being assigned, and a `panel_role` that is either `LEAD` or `MEMBER`. The resulting `InterviewAssignment` is created with `status` `ASSIGNED` — the only other value is `RELEASED` — and immediately triggers a recomputation of that faculty member's `scheduled_load`. At most one assignment may exist per slot-and-faculty-member pair, a second attempt being `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Beyond that pair test and the two safety rules below, staffing is gated on nothing: the slot's `status` is not consulted, so a `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` block may still be staffed; the program's `season_phase` is not consulted; the acting coordinator's own program is not compared against the slot's; and the faculty member need not belong to the program that owns the slot. Assignments are listed with optional `slot_id`, `faculty_member_id` and `status` filters, and a single assignment read exposes its `panel_role` and current `status`. Releasing an interviewer drives the assignment `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` and recomputes that member's `scheduled_load`.

Two safety rules guard staffing. The conflict-of-interest rule of §2.11 bars a member from taking a seat on a slot that a conflicted applicant is booked into, refused as `ERR_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. The **double-booking** rule bars a member from holding two `ASSIGNED` seats whose blocks collide in time, and its interval convention is precise: each block's interval **includes** its `start_at` and **excludes** its `end_at`, and **no buffer whatsoever** is added on either side. Two blocks collide when those half-open intervals share any instant. Exact adjacency — one block ending at the very instant the next begins — does **not** collide and is explicitly permitted, which is the opposite convention to the wave separation rule in §3.3. A collision is refused with `ERR_INTERVIEWER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

The **interviewer load index** measures how much panel work a faculty member carries, and it exists in two variants that are computed over deliberately **disjoint** slot sets, so the same assignment contributes to exactly one variant at a time. The building blocks are two: a block's duration in hours, computed as the exact decimal difference between its `end_at` and its `start_at` expressed in hours, and a seat's role weight, which is 1.5 when the assignment's `panel_role` is `LEAD` and 1.0 when it is `MEMBER`. `RELEASED` assignments are excluded from both variants entirely.

`scheduled_load` is the **forward-looking** variant: over every `ASSIGNED` assignment of this faculty member whose slot's `status` is `OPEN` or `BOOKED`, take the block's duration in hours multiplied by that seat's role weight, add those products together, and round the total half up to two decimal places once at the end. No attendance discount is applied, because for a block that has not yet run the attendance is simply unknown. It is refreshed whenever an assignment is created or released, and whenever a slot is cancelled.

`realized_load` is the **retrospective** variant: over every `ASSIGNED` assignment of this faculty member whose slot's `status` is `COMPLETED`, take the block's duration in hours multiplied by that seat's role weight multiplied by that block's **attendance factor**, add those products together, and round the total half up to two decimal places once at the end. A block's attendance factor is its `booked_count` divided by its `applicant_capacity`. Because the completed set and the open-or-booked set never overlap, a half-empty completed block yields a strictly smaller realized contribution than its scheduled contribution had been, and the two figures diverge for identical underlying data by design. `realized_load` is refreshed when a slot completes.

**Completing a block** is a coordinator action once the logical clock has reached the block's `end_at` or passed it; attempting it earlier is `ERR_SLOT_TIMING_VIOLATION`. It drives the slot `OPEN → COMPLETED` or `BOOKED → COMPLETED` — both edges are declared, because a block that never filled still ran. The request may carry an optional `no_show_application_ids` array; every element must be a non-empty string naming an existing application, an unresolvable element being `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` under the foreign-key priority of §2.12, while a named application that holds no `ACCEPTED` invitation on this slot is simply ignored. Every application holding an `ACCEPTED` invitation on this slot is then resolved on its interview dimension: an application listed in `no_show_application_ids` advances `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`, and every other booked application advances `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. Both edges require that the application actually holds an `ACCEPTED` invitation whose `slot_id` is this slot, and neither carries any side effect of its own beyond the state write. Finally, `realized_load` is recomputed for every `FacultyMember` holding any assignment on this slot, `ASSIGNED` or `RELEASED` alike — the refresh sweeps the slot's whole assignment set even though a `RELEASED` seat contributes nothing to the figure, so such a member's row is rewritten and its `version` bumped without its load changing. Reaching `COMPLETED` on the interview dimension is what makes an application scoreable, and reaching `NO_SHOW` is what quietly excludes it from ever being matched.

**Cancelling a block** is permitted only while the logical clock is strictly before the block's `start_at`, again reported as `ERR_SLOT_TIMING_VIOLATION` when late, and drives `OPEN → CANCELLED` or `BOOKED → CANCELLED`. Cancelling an `OPEN` block releases every `InterviewAssignment` on it to `RELEASED` and recomputes `scheduled_load` for every affected faculty member. Cancelling a `BOOKED` block does strictly more, and every additional hop matters: every `ACCEPTED` `InterviewInvitation` on the slot becomes `WITHDRAWN` and the slot's `booked_count` is driven down to 0; each affected application reverts `SCHEDULED → NOT_INVITED` on its interview dimension; every `InterviewAssignment` on the slot is set to `RELEASED`; `scheduled_load` is recomputed for every affected faculty member; and `Program.invited_count` is recomputed. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a block.

### 3.6. Rubric Scoring and the Committee Score Roll-Up

An `Interviewer` opens a rubric card for an application their own panel seat hosted, supplying the `assignment_id` naming that panel seat, the `application_id` being scored, and four rubric domain scores each between 1 and 10 inclusive: `communication_score`, `clinical_reasoning_score`, `professionalism_score` and `teamwork_score`. The card is created with `status` `DRAFT` and `domain_average` null. Two coherence rules bind creation: the supplied `application_id` must name an application that the assignment's slot actually hosted, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`, and at most one card may exist per assignment-and-application pair, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The three tests are applied in that stated order after a prior ownership test — the acting interviewer's `X-User-ID` must equal the assignment's `faculty_member_id`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` — and "the assignment's slot actually hosted" means precisely that the application holds an `ACCEPTED` invitation whose `slot_id` is that slot. No state gate applies at creation: neither the assignment's `status`, nor the slot's `status`, nor the application's `interview_status`, nor the program's `season_phase` is consulted, so a card may be opened before the block has run. Cards are listed with optional `assignment_id`, `application_id` and `status` filters, and a single card read exposes `domain_average`.

A card's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `AMEND_REQUESTED` and `LOCKED`, of which only `LOCKED` is terminal, and `SUBMITTED → AMEND_REQUESTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` is a permitted revision cycle — a card may legitimately be submitted more than once before the rank list locks it.

Submitting a card drives `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires three things: the acting faculty member's `id` equals the `faculty_member_id` of the card's `InterviewAssignment`, that assignment's `status` is still `ASSIGNED`, and the scored application's `interview_status` is `COMPLETED`. An interviewer whose seat was released, or who tries to score a no-show, is refused. Those three requirements are checked in a fixed order after the `DRAFT` gate: the released-seat and not-yet-completed-interview conditions come first and both answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and only then is the identity condition tested, answering `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. Submission writes `ScoreCard.domain_average` as the mean of the four rubric domains rounded half up to **one** decimal place, and then recomputes `Application.committee_score`.

The **committee rubric score** is the post-interview roll-up written to `Application.committee_score`, which defaults to null and stays null until at least one card is submitted. Its evaluation order is pinned and non-negotiable. **Step one:** for each included card, add its `communication_score`, `clinical_reasoning_score`, `professionalism_score` and `teamwork_score`, divide that sum by four, and round the result half up to **one** decimal place — this is exactly the per-card `domain_average` figure. **Step two:** add together those **already-rounded** per-card averages, divide by the number of included cards, and round that result half up to **two** decimal places. The included set is every `ScoreCard` for the application whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED`; cards in `DRAFT` or `AMEND_REQUESTED` are excluded. When the included set is empty, the score is null and no division is performed. The alternative order — summing all four domain scores across all cards, dividing by four times the card count, and rounding once to two decimal places — yields a different value for most inputs and must **not** be implemented; the per-card-first order is the contract.

A `ProgramDirector` may send a submitted card back, driving `SUBMITTED → AMEND_REQUESTED`. This requires that the acting faculty member's `is_program_director` is true, that their `program_id` equals the scored application's program, and that no `RankList` for that program has reached `CERTIFIED` — once the list is certified the scores behind it are frozen. The certified-list condition is evaluated first and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the director-flag and program-match conditions follow, in that order, and both answer `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. The transition itself has no side effects; notably, it does not immediately recompute the committee score, because an `AMEND_REQUESTED` card is excluded from the roll-up only once it actually leaves the submitted set.

The interviewer then revises, driving `AMEND_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, permitted only when the acting faculty member's `id` equals the assignment's `faculty_member_id`, refused as `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` and tested after the `AMEND_REQUESTED` gate; the assignment's own `status` is not consulted here. Revision overwrites all four rubric domain scores from the request body — `communication_score`, `clinical_reasoning_score`, `professionalism_score` and `teamwork_score` are all supplied again — clears `ScoreCard.domain_average` back to null, and recomputes `Application.committee_score` over the remaining `SUBMITTED` and `LOCKED` cards only. If that revision was the application's last submitted card, the committee score returns to null.

The final edge is system-driven: when the program's rank list reaches `CERTIFIED`, every card whose scored application appears as an entry on the certifying list is driven `SUBMITTED → LOCKED`. The transition carries no other side effect, and because `LOCKED` cards remain inside the included set, certification does not disturb any application's `committee_score`.

### 3.7. Rank List Deliberation, Balloting, and Certification

The program's preference order lives in a single `RankList`. A `ProgramDirector` opens it by naming the `program_id`, and it materialises with `status` `DRAFT`, `entry_count` at 0, `review_round` at 0, `approved_at` null and `certified_by_faculty_id` null. At most one non-`WITHDRAWN` list may exist per program, so a second open attempt while a live list exists is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Opening is gated only on the acting faculty member's own `program_id` equalling the named `program_id`, refused as `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` and tested before the uniqueness rule; the member's `is_program_director` flag is not inspected beyond the role header, and the program's `season_phase` is not consulted. Lists are listed with optional `program_id`, `status` and `certified_by_faculty_id` filters, and a single list read exposes `entry_count`, `review_round` and the `version` that the concurrency guard of §2.9 compares against. A list's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, `RETURNED`, `APPROVED`, `CERTIFIED` or `WITHDRAWN`, of which `CERTIFIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

Entries are appended one at a time by the director, in order. Each request carries the `application_id` being ranked, the `rank_position` it occupies, the `expected_rank_list_version` guard, and an optional `tie_group_anchor_entry_id`. `rank_position` is a 1-based preference position of at least 1, unique within the list, and — crucially — it must equal the list's current `entry_count` plus one **exactly**, which makes the list strictly append-only; any other value is `ERR_RANK_POSITION_SEQUENCE`. `tie_group_anchor_entry_id` is a self-referential pointer to the lowest-numbered entry of a deliberation tie group, defaulting to null when the entry stands alone, and it must reference an entry sitting on the **same** `rank_list_id`, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. At most one entry may exist per list-and-application pair. Appending is permitted only while the list is in `DRAFT`, and the whole sequence of tests is fixed: the `DRAFT` gate, then the version guard, then the ownership test — the acting faculty member's `program_id` against the list's, as `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, again without inspecting the director flag — then the anchor cross-reference, then the application cross-reference, then the duplicate-application check, then a duplicate-`rank_position` check that answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` rather than `ERR_RANK_POSITION_SEQUENCE`, then the append-only position rule, and last the length ceiling. Appending an entry creates the row, increments `RankList.entry_count` by one, and increments `RankList.version` by one. The list may not grow past `quota_positions` multiplied by 10 entries, refused as `ERR_RANK_LIST_TOO_LONG`. Entries of one list are read back in ascending `rank_position` order; a flat entry listing is also available filtered by `application_id` or by `tie_group_anchor_entry_id`, and a single entry read exposes its anchor pointer.

Sending the list to committee drives `DRAFT → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and is the most heavily gated transition in the system. Seven conditions must hold: the acting faculty member's `is_program_director` is true; that member's `program_id` equals the list's `program_id`; the owning program is in `RANKING`; `entry_count` is at least 1; `entry_count` is at or below `quota_positions` multiplied by 10; the set of `rank_position` values across the list's entries is exactly the whole numbers from 1 through `entry_count` with no gap and no repeat; and every ranked application carries at least 2 `ScoreCard` rows whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED`. The shortfalls map to distinct codes — the length breach to `ERR_RANK_LIST_TOO_LONG`, the sequence breach to `ERR_RANK_POSITION_SEQUENCE`, the score-card shortfall to `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATIONS` — per the ordering in §2.12. Two side effects follow: `RankList.review_round` increments by one, and `RankList.version` increments by one. `review_round` is by definition the number of times the list has entered committee review, which is why it starts at 0 and is 1 during the first deliberation.

A `CommitteeMember` casts one ballot on a list under review, supplying the `faculty_member_id` casting it, a `ballot` value of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN`, and an optional free-text `comment` that defaults to null. The created `CommitteeVote` is stamped with the list's **current** `review_round`, which the client never supplies. At most one ballot may exist per rank-list, faculty-member and `review_round` triple, so a second ballot by the same member in the same round is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — though the same member may ballot again in a later round. Three ownership conditions bind the ballot, all three refused as `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` and all three tested after the `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` gate but before the duplicate check: the supplied `faculty_member_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`, that member's `is_committee_member` is true, and that member's `program_id` equals the list's `program_id`. No phase gate applies. Ballots on one list are read back across all rounds with optional `review_round` and `ballot` filters; a flat ballot listing is also available filtered by `faculty_member_id` or `rank_list_id`, and a single ballot read exposes the `review_round` it was cast in.

**Quorum resolution** runs after every single ballot is recorded on a list in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`. The eligible electorate is the count of `FacultyMember` rows whose `program_id` equals the list's `program_id` and whose `is_committee_member` is true. The quorum threshold is that electorate size multiplied by 0.60 — sixty percent — and then **rounded up to the next whole number** by the ceiling function; an electorate of four therefore needs three, and an electorate of five also needs three. The effective tally counts only `CommitteeVote` rows for this list whose `review_round` equals the list's **current** `review_round`; ballots from an earlier round survive as history but are excluded, so a list that was returned and reopened starts its next round with an empty effective tally. Within that tally, the approve count is the number of ballots whose `ballot` is `APPROVE` and the return count is the number whose `ballot` is `RETURN`. **The approve branch is evaluated first, and both comparisons are inclusive:** when the approve count is at or above the threshold the list advances `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → APPROVED`; otherwise, when the return count is at or above the threshold the list advances `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → RETURNED`; otherwise the list stays in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and the ballot is simply recorded. `RankList.version` increments on **every** recorded ballot, including the inconclusive ones that leave `status` untouched, because the list row is rewritten each time quorum is evaluated.

The two branches out of committee review carry deliberately different side-effect sets. The approve branch stamps `RankList.approved_at` with the current logical clock instant and increments `RankList.version` by one. The return branch **leaves `RankList.approved_at` null** — that branch writes no approval timestamp at all — and increments `RankList.version` by one. Nothing else distinguishes them, and an implementation that stamps `approved_at` on both is wrong.

A returned list is revived by its director, driving `RETURNED → DRAFT` so that its entries can be reworked. It requires only that the acting faculty member's `program_id` equals the list's `program_id` — the `is_program_director` flag is not re-checked beyond the role header on this edge — and its sole side effect is that `RankList.version` increments by one. Because `review_round` increments again on the next trip to committee, and because only current-round ballots count, the revision cycle `DRAFT → COMMITTEE_REVIEW → RETURNED → DRAFT` may repeat indefinitely.

**Certification** is the irreversible commitment, driving `APPROVED → CERTIFIED`, and the request carries `certified_by_faculty_id` naming the certifying director. Five conditions gate it: the acting faculty member's `is_program_director` is true; that member's `program_id` equals the list's `program_id`; that member authored **no** `ScoreCard` for **any** application ranked on this list; that member cast **no** `CommitteeVote` for this list in its current `review_round`; and the logical clock is at or before the national certification deadline of `2027-02-25T21:00:00Z`. The two segregation clauses are reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and the deadline as `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A sixth condition sits alongside them: the supplied `certified_by_faculty_id` must name the acting faculty member themselves, otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. The order is fixed and puts segregation of duties ahead of identity: the `APPROVED` gate, then the authored-score-card clause, then the balloted-this-round clause, then the director flag, then the program match, then the certifier-is-actor test, and only last the deadline. Certification carries four side effects: `RankList.certified_by_faculty_id` is set to the acting faculty member's `id`; every `ScoreCard` whose scored application is ranked on this list is driven `SUBMITTED → LOCKED`; the owning program advances `RANKING → LIST_CERTIFIED` and `Program.rank_list_certified_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, which also increments the program's `version` by one; and `RankList.version` increments by one. That program-phase hop has its own declared precondition, namely that the certifying list's `program_id` is indeed this program's `id`, and one further guard: the phase is rewritten only when it currently reads `RANKING`, whereas `rank_list_certified_at` is stamped and the program's `version` bumped whatever the phase was.

A list that was never certified can be abandoned, driving either `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN`. Both edges require the same one thing — the acting faculty member's `program_id` equals the list's `program_id`, the `is_program_director` flag again not being re-checked beyond the role header — and both carry the same single side effect, incrementing `RankList.version` by one. Withdrawal ends the program's participation in the match and frees the `program_id` for a fresh list, since the uniqueness rule counts only non-`WITHDRAWN` lists.

### 3.8. Match Day, SOAP, and the Position Conservation Cascade

Match day is run by the national operator against a certified program, and it drives the program `LIST_CERTIFIED → MATCH_COMPLETE`. Two conditions gate it: `Program.rank_list_certified_at` is not null, and no `MatchResult` row exists yet for that program — the match runs exactly once. The subject applications must also carry no unresolved communication flag, per §3.9, refused as `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMUNICATION_FLAG`. The call answers 200 with the updated `Program` object as its `data` — not the published results, which are fetched separately.

The **match day assignment** algorithm processes the entries of the program's `CERTIFIED` list in **ascending `rank_position` order**, which is a total order precisely because sending the list to committee already required the positions to be exactly 1 through `entry_count` with no gap or repeat. An entry is **eligible** when its application's `screening_status` is `ADVANCED` **and** its application's `interview_status` is `COMPLETED`; an application that withdrew, was rejected, or no-showed its interview is therefore ineligible. A running fill counter starts at zero. Walking the entries in that order: when the entry is eligible **and** the fill counter is strictly less than the program's `quota_positions`, a `MatchResult` is created with `outcome` `MATCHED` and `matched_rank_position` set to that entry's `rank_position`, and the fill counter increases by one; in every other case a `MatchResult` is created with `outcome` `UNMATCHED` and `matched_rank_position` left null. Exactly one `MatchResult` is created per `RankListEntry`, so every ranked application receives a published result; applications that were never ranked receive no `MatchResult` at all. Every created row has its `published_at` stamped. Match day never raises a capacity error: once the quota is exhausted the algorithm simply publishes the remainder as unmatched.

After the walk, `Program.matched_count` is set to the number of `MatchResult` rows whose `outcome` is `MATCHED`, `Program.unfilled_positions` is set to `quota_positions` minus `matched_count`, and the program's `version` increments by one. Results are read back filtered by `program_id`, `application_id` or `outcome`, and a single result read exposes `matched_rank_position`. A result's `outcome` is one of `PENDING`, `MATCHED`, `UNMATCHED` or `VACATED`, of which `UNMATCHED` and `VACATED` are terminal; `PENDING` is the declared initial value, and the match-day walk performs `PENDING → MATCHED` or `PENDING → UNMATCHED` as part of publication.

From `MATCH_COMPLETE` the national operator has two exits, and they are mutually exclusive. `MATCH_COMPLETE` → `SOAP_ACTIVE` requires that `Program.soap_participating` is true **and** `Program.unfilled_positions` is at least 1. `MATCH_COMPLETE` → `SEASON_CLOSED` requires that `Program.unfilled_positions` is exactly 0. Both hops increment the program's `version` by one and carry no other side effect. A program in `SOAP_ACTIVE` may later close along `SOAP_ACTIVE` → `SEASON_CLOSED`, which requires that `Program.unfilled_positions` is exactly 0 **or** that at least one `SoapOffer` with `soap_round` 4 exists for the program — the fourth round is the last, so a program that has exhausted it may close even with vacancies still open. That hop additionally sets every `SoapOffer` for the program whose `status` is `OFFERED` to `EXPIRED`, and increments the program's `version` by one.

**SOAP offers** are extended by the `ProgramDirector` for unfilled positions, carrying the `program_id`, the `application_id` receiving the offer, and a `soap_round` ordinal between 1 and 4 inclusive. Five contextual conditions apply, in this order: the program must be in `SOAP_ACTIVE`, `Program.soap_participating` must be true, a `MatchResult` must already exist for this application under this program with `outcome` `UNMATCHED`, the application must carry no unresolved communication flag (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMUNICATION_FLAG`), and the acting director's own `program_id` must equal the offering program's `id` (`ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`); the budget ceiling below is tested last. The offer is created with `status` `OFFERED`, `offered_at` stamped with the current logical clock instant, and `expires_at` set to `offered_at` plus 4 hours — SOAP moves fast. Offers are read back filtered by `program_id`, `application_id`, `soap_round` or `status`, and a single offer read exposes `expires_at`. An offer's `status` is one of `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`, the last three all terminal.

How many offers a program may hold outstanding is capped by its **SOAP offer budget**, a figure recomputed on every read of the program — and likewise on every other program response body, such as the body answering a phase advance or a match run, save only the registration response of §3.1, which reports 0 — so that its pinned evaluation order is directly observable. **Step one:** the base vacancy is `quota_positions` minus `matched_count`, computed **before** any reserve is considered, where `matched_count` is the count of `MatchResult` rows for the program whose `outcome` is `MATCHED` at that moment — `VACATED` and `UNMATCHED` rows do not count. **Step two:** a reserve credit is selected from that base vacancy in a strict cascade — a base vacancy of at least 6 earns a credit of 3; otherwise a base vacancy of at least 3 earns 2; otherwise a base vacancy of at least 1 earns 1; a base vacancy of 0 earns 0. **Step three:** only now are the two combined, and the budget is the base vacancy plus the reserve credit. The definition looks circular because the reserve credit appears to depend on the very budget it contributes to; it does not. The tier is selected from the base vacancy in step two and is never re-derived from the resulting budget, and implementations must not iterate to a fixed point. All of it is integer arithmetic with no rounding. The number of live offers compared against this budget is the count of `SoapOffer` rows for the program whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`, across all rounds; a create that would push that count above the budget is refused with `ERR_SOAP_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

An applicant **accepts** a SOAP offer, driving `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`, subject to two conditions in this order: the acting applicant's `id` equals the `applicant_id` of the offer's application, and the logical clock is strictly before the offer's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_SOAP_OFFER_EXPIRED`. Nothing else is consulted — in particular the program's `season_phase` is **not** re-checked at acceptance, so an offer standing when the program left `SOAP_ACTIVE` may still be accepted, and a second offer may be accepted for an `application_id` that already holds an `ACCEPTED` one. Acceptance also requires the program to have room — `Program.unfilled_positions` at least 1, otherwise `ERR_NO_POSITIONS_AVAILABLE`, tested after the expiry check. Its effects reach across aggregates: the application's existing `MatchResult` for this program, whatever its `outcome`, is driven to `MATCHED`, or, when the application was never ranked and so has no result row, one is created directly with `outcome` `MATCHED` and `published_at` **null**, since a SOAP fill is not a match-day publication; `matched_rank_position` stays null in **both** cases, because a SOAP fill has no rank position; and `Program.matched_count` increments by one while `Program.unfilled_positions` decrements by one, preserving the conservation identity of §2.10. Declining drives `OFFERED → DECLINED`, requires only applicant ownership and that the logical clock is strictly before `expires_at` — a late decline being `ERR_SOAP_OFFER_EXPIRED` too — carries no side effects, and frees one unit of offer budget by removing the row from the live count. An unanswered offer drives `OFFERED → EXPIRED` either because the logical clock has reached or passed its `expires_at` on a lapse sweep, or because the season is closing; that transition carries no side effects.

### 3.9. Contract Issuance, Onboarding Credentials, and Rescission

A filled position is documented by a `Contract`, issued by a `ProgramCoordinator` with a payload naming the `match_result_id` of the filled position and a `salary_cents` figure — the PGY-1 annual stipend in cents, which must be at least 0. The referenced `MatchResult` must have `outcome` `MATCHED`, and at most one contract may exist per `match_result_id`. `DRAFT` is the declared initial state of the contract machine and the create performs the `DRAFT → ISSUED` hop as one atomic operation, so the row is observable in `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_at` stamped at the current logical clock instant. A contract's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `SIGNED`, `ACTIVE`, `VOID` or `RESCINDED`, of which `VOID` and `RESCINDED` are terminal. Contracts are read back filtered by `match_result_id` or `status`, and a single contract read exposes both credential counters.

Issuance also composes the **onboarding credential packet**, and the composition is deterministic. A `STATE_LICENSE` item is always created with `is_mandatory` true. A `BLS` item is always created with `is_mandatory` true. A `DEA` item is **always created**, but its `is_mandatory` is true only when the program's `specialty_code` is anything other than `PATH`, and false for pathology programs — the item still exists, it simply does not gate activation. A `VISA` item is created **only** when the applicant's `visa_status` is `NEEDS_J1` or `NEEDS_H1B`, and when created it is mandatory. The applicant consulted is the `Applicant` behind the contract's `MatchResult` application, and the program consulted is that application's `Program`. A packet therefore holds three items for a citizen or permanent resident and four items for a J-1 or H-1B candidate, of which two, three or four are mandatory depending on `specialty_code` and `visa_status`. `Contract.mandatory_credential_count` is set to the number of created items whose `is_mandatory` is true, and `Contract.verified_credential_count` is set to 0. Every created `CredentialItem` names its `contract_id`, holds a `credential_type` that is one of `STATE_LICENSE`, `DEA`, `VISA` or `BLS`, starts in `status` `PENDING` with `reference_number` null and `verified_at` null. The packet of one contract is read back with optional `status` and `credential_type` filters, and a single item read exposes `is_mandatory` and `verified_at`.

The applicant **signs** the issued contract, driving `ISSUED → SIGNED`, permitted only when the acting applicant is the `Applicant` behind the contract's `MatchResult` application; `signed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant. Meanwhile the applicant works through the packet, supplying a `reference_number` — the license, DEA or visa document number — for each item. That drives `PENDING → SUBMITTED`, and the same operation drives `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` on a second attempt, overwriting the earlier `reference_number`; both edges require that the acting applicant is the `Applicant` behind the owning contract's `MatchResult` application and that the owning contract's `status` is `ISSUED` or `SIGNED`. `reference_number` is required, a non-empty string of at most 200 characters, and the contract-status requirement is checked before the ownership one, answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates each submission. Verifying drives `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` with no preconditions beyond the state itself; it stamps `verified_at` with the current logical clock instant and increments `Contract.verified_credential_count` by one **only when that item's `is_mandatory` is true** — verifying a non-mandatory pathology `DEA` item advances no counter. Because that counter lives on the contract, verifying a **mandatory** item also rewrites the contract row and increments `Contract.version` by one, whereas verifying a non-mandatory item leaves the contract row entirely alone. Rejecting drives `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, carries a required `reason` in the request body — a non-empty string of at most 2000 characters, retained on the item and echoed back as `reason` — has no preconditions and no side effects, and leaves the door open for the resubmission cycle `REJECTED → SUBMITTED`. A `CredentialItem`'s `status` is one of `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED`, of which `VERIFIED` and `VOIDED` are terminal. `Contract.verified_credential_count` is by definition the count of items on the contract that are both mandatory and `VERIFIED`, and `Contract.mandatory_credential_count` the count of items on the contract that are mandatory.

**Activation** drives `SIGNED → ACTIVE` and is refused until the packet is complete: `Contract.verified_credential_count` must equal `Contract.mandatory_credential_count`, otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIALS_INCOMPLETE`. The transition has no side effects of its own.

Two compensating exits exist, and they differ sharply. **Voiding** drives `ISSUED → VOID` — an issued contract the applicant never signed — carries a required `reason`, a non-empty string of at most 2000 characters retained on the contract and echoed back as `reason`, has no preconditions, and voids the packet along three system-driven edges — `PENDING` → `VOIDED`, `SUBMITTED` → `VOIDED` and `REJECTED` → `VOIDED`, none of which has any precondition or side effect of its own — while an already-`VERIFIED` item is left alone. Voiding touches **no** position counter, because a contract that was never signed never took a position out of circulation in the accounting sense the rescission cascade addresses.

**Rescission** is the compensating action that returns a conserved position, and it is the `ProgramDirector`'s prerogative. It drives `SIGNED → RESCINDED` or `ACTIVE → RESCINDED`, carries a required `reason` on the same terms as a void — non-empty, at most 2000 characters, retained and echoed — and has no preconditions beyond the state and one ownership test: the acting director's own `program_id` must equal the `program_id` of the contract's application, refused as `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, with `is_program_director` not inspected beyond the role header. Both edges fire the identical three-hop cascade plus the packet void. **Hop 1:** the referenced `MatchResult` advances `MATCHED → VACATED`, and its `matched_rank_position` is cleared to null. **Hop 2:** because that result is no longer `MATCHED`, `Program.matched_count` decrements by one and `Program.unfilled_positions` increments by one, preserving `quota_positions` equal to `matched_count` plus `unfilled_positions` exactly. **Hop 3:** because the program's `unfilled_positions` has risen above 0, a program sitting in `SEASON_CLOSED` re-opens `SEASON_CLOSED → SOAP_ACTIVE`, gated on `Program.soap_participating` being true and on `unfilled_positions` being at least 1 after the vacating cascade, and that hop increments the program's `version` by one. A program that does not participate in SOAP, or that is not in `SEASON_CLOSED`, simply keeps its restored vacancy. Alongside the cascade, the same three packet-voiding edges fire as on a void — `PENDING` → `VOIDED`, `SUBMITTED` → `VOIDED` and `REJECTED` → `VOIDED` — leaving `VERIFIED` items untouched. `VACATED` is written by this cascade and by nothing else.

### 3.10. Pre-Match Communication Compliance and Pipeline Reporting

NRMP participation rules forbid a program from soliciting a candidate's ranking intentions before the match, and this system records every pre-match contact so that violations are auditable rather than deniable. A `ProgramDirector`, `ProgramCoordinator`, `Interviewer` or `CommitteeMember` logs a contact by supplying the `application_id` the message concerns, a `channel` that is one of `EMAIL`, `PHONE` or `IN_PERSON`, the verbatim message `body` (non-empty, at most 2000 characters), and an optional `author_faculty_id` naming the authoring faculty member. When `author_faculty_id` is omitted it defaults to the acting `X-User-ID` for the three entity-backed authoring roles — `ProgramDirector`, `Interviewer` and `CommitteeMember` — so it is null precisely when the message was logged by a coordinator acting under the opaque operator identity described in §2.1. A supplied `author_faculty_id` must name an existing `FacultyMember`, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, but it need not be the acting member; a value that is not a string is treated as though it were absent.

The **compliance scan** runs on creation. The `body` is lowercased, and `contains_ranking_intent` is set true when the lowercased text contains any of five fixed prohibited phrases as a plain substring: "how will you rank", "rank us", "commit to us", "where will we be on your list", or "ranking intent". The scan is nothing more elaborate than a case-insensitive substring test over the whole body against that fixed list. Separately, the contact is considered **pre-match** when the program's `season_phase` is one of `PRE_SEASON`, `REVIEW_OPEN`, `INTERVIEWS_OPEN`, `RANKING` or `LIST_CERTIFIED`. When the body contains ranking intent **and** the contact is pre-match, `compliance_status` is set to `FLAGGED`; in every other combination it is set to `CLEAN`. A contact whose body contains a prohibited phrase but is logged after match day is therefore `CLEAN`, and `contains_ranking_intent` may be true on a `CLEAN` row. `sent_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant. The row is **always** created and the response is 201 Created even when the verdict is `FLAGGED` — the flag is an audit record, not a rejection. `compliance_status` takes one of `CLEAN`, `FLAGGED` or `CLEARED`.

A flag is not merely decorative: while any `CommunicationLog` row for a subject application carries `compliance_status` `FLAGGED`, that application blocks two operations with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_COMMUNICATION_FLAG` — extending a SOAP offer to it, and running the program's match. Only a `ComplianceOfficer` can clear it, by adjudicating the flagged contact with a required `resolution_note` — a non-empty string of at most 2000 characters, retained on the row and echoed back as `resolution_note`, null until then — which advances `compliance_status` `FLAGGED → CLEARED` and lifts the block. Logged contacts are read back filtered by `application_id`, `author_faculty_id`, `channel` or `compliance_status`, and a single read exposes the scan verdict.

The season's other reporting obligation is the **diversity and pipeline aggregate** for a program. Its cohort is every `Application` under the program whose `screening_status` is `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ADVANCED` or `REJECTED`; `WITHDRAWN` applications are excluded from every figure in the report. `cohort_size` is the number of applications in that cohort. Three subsets are then taken from the same cohort: the advanced set is those whose `screening_status` is `ADVANCED`; the IMG set is those whose applicant's `medical_school_type` is `IMG`; and the alum set is those whose applicant's `pipeline_program_alum` is true. Every percentage in the report is computed the same way — the subset count divided by `cohort_size`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to one decimal place — and is exactly 0.0 whenever `cohort_size` is 0, in which case no division is performed at all. `advanced_rate`, `img_share` and `pipeline_alum_share` are those three percentages in order.

The report also buckets the cohort by self-identified group. Each application's group label is its applicant's `self_identified_group`, or the literal string `UNREPORTED` when that field is null because the applicant declined to report. `group_buckets` is an array holding one object per distinct label present in the cohort, ordered by label in **ascending** order, and each object carries a `group` naming the label, a `count` of cohort applications bearing it, and a `share` computed by the same percentage rule. An empty cohort is an expected state and not an error: every rate is exactly 0.0, `group_buckets` is the empty array, and the endpoint still answers 200 OK.
