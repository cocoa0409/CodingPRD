# Residential Solar Portfolio Servicing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A residential solar portfolio is not a product that ships and is forgotten. Twenty-five years of obligations follow every rooftop: a lease that escalates once a year, or a power-purchase agreement that bills for each metered kilowatt-hour at an escalating rate; a production guarantee that owes the customer money when the array underperforms; inverters that fail silently and must be found by monitoring rather than by a phone call; homeowners who sell the house and hand the contract to a buyer, buy the contract out, or move it to a new roof; utilities that retire a net-metering schedule and replace it with a less generous successor; roofers who need the array off the roof for three weeks; a fixture filing at the county recorder that has to be subordinated or terminated before any closing can happen; and, at the end, a choice between buying the system, renewing, or having it taken away. Each of these obligations is individually simple and collectively unmanageable in a spreadsheet.

This document specifies the servicing API that holds that portfolio. It is a ledger system first: every credit the production guarantee owes is earned, applied and reversed through counters that must reconcile exactly after every write, including after a compensating write. It is a multi-party workflow system second: the credit decision on an assuming buyer, the title clearance ahead of a closing, the field diagnosis behind a warranty claim and the portfolio-level administration of tariffs and end-of-term outcomes are four separate hands, and several operations additionally refuse to let one person perform both halves of a two-step control. The API is mounted under `/servicing`, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §2.5.

Readers should treat the ordering statements in this document as normative. Several rules in this system produce different — but individually defensible — answers depending on when rounding happens, in which order a waterfall's steps run, and which of several simultaneously-violated rules is reported. Wherever such an order is pinned, it is pinned deliberately, and an implementation that reorders the steps will produce numerically different output for many inputs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or is present but carries the empty string, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else — before the role is examined, before the path identifiers are resolved, and before the request body is even parsed.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `TitleAgent`, `FieldOpsCoordinator`, and `PortfolioAdmin`. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles authorised to invoke it. A request whose `X-User-Role` is not in that endpoint's list — including a value that is not one of the six role names at all — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authorisation is decided before the body is validated, so a forbidden role that also submits a malformed payload receives the 403 rather than a schema error.

The six roles divide the work as follows. A `Homeowner` is the customer: they may request roof work and cancel their own roof-work request, submit and withdraw an end-of-term election, and read their own agreement, metered months, true-ups, outage events, transfer cases, elections and savings projection. A `ServicingAgent` performs day-to-day servicing: booking and activating agreements, registering homeowners, recording metered months, reconciling them, computing and posting annual true-ups, opening and closing transfer cases, and quoting and approving roof work; reconciling a metered month and opening a transfer case are operations no other role may perform. A `CreditUnderwriter` owns exactly one decision — running the credit review on an assuming buyer — plus the reads needed to make it, and may not open, close or cancel the transfer it underwrites. A `TitleAgent` owns the fixture-filing book: recording an executed subordination, recording a termination, and clearing title for a closing are exclusive to this role. A `FieldOpsCoordinator` owns monitoring and the physical asset: triaging alarms, dispatching technicians, closing dispatches out with a diagnosis, taking panels off the roof, signing off the roofer's work, reinstalling the array, and opening and submitting manufacturer warranty claims. A `PortfolioAdmin` performs portfolio-level administration and holds four exclusive powers: publishing a utility tariff, moving an agreement onto a different tariff, reopening a settled month, and executing an accepted end-of-term outcome; it shares most servicing and field reads and several field actions, but it does not inherit the `CreditUnderwriter`'s credit review, the `TitleAgent`'s three title operations, or the `FieldOpsCoordinator`'s triage, dispatch, completion, roof-handling and claim-submission operations.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters. When `X-User-Role` is `Homeowner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Homeowner` record; ownership checks compare the target agreement's `homeowner_id` to that value. When `X-User-Role` is `ServicingAgent`, `CreditUnderwriter`, `TitleAgent`, `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `PortfolioAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records such identifiers where a workflow step demands an audit trail — as `posted_by_user_id` and `voided_by_user_id` on a true-up, `dispatched_by_user_id` and `diagnosed_by_user_id` on a dispatch, `approved_by_user_id` on a roof-work order, `credit_decided_by_user_id` and `title_cleared_by_user_id` on a transfer case, `elected_by_user_id` on an end-of-term election, and `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a warranty claim — but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

Ownership is enforced narrowly and consistently. On every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Homeowner`, a request presenting `X-User-Role` of `Homeowner` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_AGREEMENT_HOMEOWNER` (HTTP 403) when the resolved target agreement's `homeowner_id` is not the acting user's own homeowner id. A homeowner may read and act only on their own agreement and that agreement's children; a successor agreement minted by a relocation closing belongs to the same homeowner and is therefore visible to them. Operator roles are not subject to this check.

Four operations are two-person controls, and a fifth kind of separation is structural. Voiding a guarantee true-up, closing out a truck roll, recording an executed subordination, and approving or denying a warranty claim all refuse the acting user when that user performed the paired earlier step: the user recorded in `posted_by_user_id` may not void the true-up they posted; the user recorded in `dispatched_by_user_id` may not close out the dispatch they created; the user who requested a subordination on a filing may not record its execution; and the user recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` may not approve or deny the claim they submitted. All five rejections carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and this check runs ahead of every state check — so the same user attempting to void a true-up that is already `VOID` learns about the duty conflict, not about the state.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Ordering

Every collection read is paginated by the same two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1 and may not exceed 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Unless a subsection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; the metered-month collection is instead ordered ascending by `period_year` and then `period_month` when the caller supplies an `agreement_id` filter.

Each collection accepts the foreign keys and classifying enumerations it carries as optional filters, and filters combine conjunctively. Homeowners may be filtered by `state_code` and `email`. Utility tariffs accept `utility_code` and `generation`. Agreements accept `homeowner_id`, `tariff_id`, `relocated_from_agreement_id`, `product_type`, and each of the three state dimensions individually — `lifecycle_state`, `monitoring_status` and `service_status`. Metered months accept `agreement_id`, `contract_year`, `period_year`, `period_month` and `status`. Guarantee true-ups accept `agreement_id`, `contract_year`, `method` and `status`. Outage events accept `agreement_id`, `status`, `outage_type`, `severity` and `excused`. Truck rolls accept `outage_event_id`, `agreement_id`, `technician_code`, `status`, `billable_to` and `root_cause`. Roof-work orders accept `agreement_id`, `status` and `contract_year`. Transfer cases accept `agreement_id`, `seller_homeowner_id`, `buyer_homeowner_id`, `successor_agreement_id`, `transfer_type` and `status`. UCC filings accept `agreement_id`, `requested_by_transfer_case_id`, `jurisdiction_code`, `filing_type` and `status`. End-of-term elections accept `agreement_id`, `election_type` and `status`. Warranty claims accept `truck_roll_id`, `agreement_id`, `component_type`, `manufacturer_code` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Each stored resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix; clients never supply it. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row is first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation as well. The one exception is the utility tariff: a published tariff is configuration, not state, and it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because no endpoint exists to update or delete it. A utility's successor schedule is therefore always a new tariff row, and an agreement is moved onto it by an explicit administrative action rather than by editing terms underneath the agreement. Homeowners, agreements, metered months, guarantee true-ups, outage events, truck rolls, roof-work orders, transfer cases, UCC filings, end-of-term elections and warranty claims all carry the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

One subtlety governs cascades: when one operation mutates several resources, each mutated resource's own `version` advances by one, and a single logical transition advances a given resource's `version` exactly once no matter how many individual counters the transition moves. A month that both issues a shortfall credit and consumes credit against its invoice, for example, advances the parent agreement's `version` once for the whole reconciliation, never once per ledger movement.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. Where an endpoint's path carries an identifier — `{homeowner_id}`, `{tariff_id}`, `{agreement_id}`, `{period_id}`, `{true_up_id}`, `{outage_event_id}`, `{truck_roll_id}`, `{roof_work_order_id}`, `{transfer_case_id}`, `{ucc_filing_id}`, `{election_id}` or `{warranty_claim_id}` — that identifier must name an existing row of the corresponding collection, or the request fails with that collection's not-found code: `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TARIFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUE_UP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OUTAGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUCK_ROLL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOF_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UCC_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.

References carried in a request body are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved. A body reference that is syntactically acceptable but names no existing row produces the same not-found code for the referenced collection — an unresolvable `homeowner_id` or `buyer_homeowner_id` yields `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `tariff_id` yields `ERR_TARIFF_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `agreement_id` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `outage_event_id` yields `ERR_OUTAGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `truck_roll_id` yields `ERR_TRUCK_ROLL_NOT_FOUND`. When a body carries two unresolvable references, they are checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, so the reported code is deterministic.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is explicit. A logical current UTC instant is held by the server, and every temporal decision — contract-year arithmetic, due dates, election windows, warranty expiry, outage downtime, roof-work window enforcement, closing-date staleness — reads that logical clock and never the host's wall clock. On startup the clock is initialised to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A control endpoint, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, moves the clock to the instant supplied as `current_utc` and echoes back `{ current_utc }`. This endpoint sits deliberately outside the `/servicing` prefix and outside the authentication rules: headers are not required on it, and when they are supplied they are ignored. Moving the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field; derived values are refreshed by the next write or computed read that reads the clock.

### 2.6. Units, Precision and Rounding Discipline

Three unit conventions apply throughout and are never mixed. All money is expressed in whole cents. All energy is expressed in whole kilowatt-hours. All per-unit rates are expressed in whole millicents, where one millicent is one thousandth of a cent — so converting a millicent-denominated rate multiplied by a kilowatt-hour count into cents means dividing by 1000.

Every rounding step in this document uses round-half-up: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Rounding happens at exactly the point where this document says it happens and nowhere else. Intermediate values are carried unrounded unless a step explicitly rounds them, and once a step rounds a value, every later step consumes the rounded form. Almost every rounded figure is rounded to a whole unit — a whole cent, a whole kilowatt-hour, or a whole millicent. Two figures are exceptions and are called out where they arise: a buyer's composite credit score is rounded to exactly two decimal places, and the end-of-term residual percentage is rounded to exactly four decimal places. Where a computation truncates rather than rounds — elapsed minutes of an outage — the text says so.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this system price or consume a decision recorded on a parent record, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency against that parent.

Creating a guarantee true-up must carry `expected_agreement_version`, a mandatory field holding the `version` of the parent agreement as the client last observed it. A true-up prices a whole contract year off the agreement's escalator and its credit ledger, so it must be computed against the agreement revision the client actually read. If `expected_agreement_version` does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create advances the agreement's `version` by one.

Dispatching a truck roll must carry `expected_outage_event_version`, a mandatory field holding the `version` of the parent outage event as the client last observed it, because the dispatch consumes the triage decision recorded on that event. If it does not equal the outage event's current `version`, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_OUTAGE_EVENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create advances the outage event's `version` by one.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and neither of these two fields is optional on its endpoint: a create that omits it is a malformed request. Both checks sit below the state ladder described in §2.10, so a stale version presented against a parent that is also in the wrong state reports the state error instead — the client's whole premise is wrong, and the state is the more useful thing to learn.

### 2.8. Three Independent State Dimensions on an Agreement

An agreement does not have one status; it has three, and they advance independently of one another. Nothing in this system collapses them, and a transition on one dimension never implies a transition on another unless this document says so explicitly.

The contract dimension, `lifecycle_state`, starts at `DRAFT` and takes the values `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `TRANSFER_PENDING`, `END_OF_TERM_PENDING`, `PREPAID_CLOSED`, `PURCHASED_CLOSED`, `REMOVED_CLOSED` and `RELOCATED_CLOSED`. The last four are terminal: an agreement that reaches one of them never leaves it. The monitoring dimension, `monitoring_status`, starts at `MONITORING_OK` and takes the values `MONITORING_OK`, `OUTAGE_OPEN` and `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED`; it has no terminal value and is a projection over the agreement's open alarm set rather than an independently stored flag. The physical-asset dimension, `service_status`, starts at `IN_SERVICE` and takes the values `IN_SERVICE`, `ROOF_WORK_SUSPENDED`, `DECOMMISSION_PENDING` and `DECOMMISSIONED`, of which `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal. A roof suspension therefore does not disturb the contract lifecycle, an open alarm does not block a transfer, and a contract in `END_OF_TERM_PENDING` keeps producing and keeps being monitored.

Cascades between records are always stated explicitly in §3, and they are complete: when an operation moves a child record and also touches its parent's counters, its parent's state, or sibling records, every one of those effects is part of the same operation and every touched record's `version` and `updated_at` advance. Where an operation is described as compensating — reopening a settled month, voiding a posted true-up, cancelling a dispatch, withdrawing an election, cancelling a transfer — it removes exactly what its counterpart added and leaves no residue in any counter.

### 2.9. The Credit Ledger and Its Invariants

Shortfall credit is real money owed to the customer, and the agreement carries three counters to track it: `credit_earned_cents`, `credit_applied_cents` and `credit_balance_cents`. All three start at 0 when an agreement is booked. Their defining identity must hold after every single write in the system, including after a compensating one.

`credit_earned_cents` equals the sum of `credit_cents` over the agreement's metered months whose `status` is `CREDIT_ISSUED`, plus the sum of `net_credit_cents` over the agreement's guarantee true-ups whose `status` is `POSTED`. `credit_applied_cents` equals the sum of `credit_applied_cents` over the agreement's metered months whose `status` is `RECONCILED` or `CREDIT_ISSUED`, plus the credit consumed at closing by any closed prepayment transfer on the agreement. `credit_balance_cents` equals `credit_earned_cents` minus `credit_applied_cents`, and it is never negative.

A second, smaller identity governs the monitoring counters. `excused_downtime_minutes` on the agreement equals the sum of `downtime_minutes` over the agreement's outage events that are both excused and resolved — an event still open, an event closed as a false positive, and an event whose dispatch ended without access all contribute nothing. `open_outage_count` equals the count of the agreement's outage events whose `status` is `OPEN`, `TRIAGED` or `DISPATCHED`. Because both counters are defined by these sums, dispatching a technician and then cancelling the dispatch is a compensating pair that leaves both counters exactly where they started.

### 2.10. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is reported: the one belonging to the lowest-numbered rung of the following ladder. Evaluation stops at the first violated rung. The ladder is total — every rule in this document sits on exactly one rung, and no two rules sharing a rung can be violated by the same request.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` missing or empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control endpoint.
2. **Role permission.** The presented role is not authorised for the endpoint, or is not one of the six role names — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control endpoint.
3. **Payload shape and domain.** `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400, on every endpoint that accepts a body or query parameters. This rung covers a required input field that is missing or null; a field supplied by the client that the endpoint computes or the system owns; a value of the wrong JSON type; an enumeration value outside its declared set; a string outside its declared length; a numeric value outside its declared range — including negative money, a `fico_score` outside 300 through 850, a `dti_ratio` outside 0 through 1, a `guarantee_band_pct` outside 0 through 0.2, an `escalator_pct` outside 0 through 0.05, a `period_month` outside 1 through 12, and a `term_years` that is not 10, 15, 20 or 25; a date or instant that is not valid ISO 8601; and a `limit` outside 1 through 100 or a negative `offset`.
4. **Path resource existence.** HTTP 404 with the not-found code of the collection named by the path segment, as listed in §2.4.
5. **Body reference existence.** HTTP 404 with the not-found code of the collection named by the unresolvable foreign key in the body, evaluated only after every path identifier has resolved and in the endpoint's declared input order.
6. **Homeowner scope.** `ERR_NOT_AGREEMENT_HOMEOWNER`, HTTP 403, on every endpoint whose authorised roles include `Homeowner`.
7. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403, on the five two-person operations listed in §2.1 — voiding a true-up, closing out a truck roll, recording a subordination, and approving or denying a warranty claim.
8. **State-transition legality.** HTTP 409 on every action endpoint whose target is not in a state from which the requested transition exists. The generic code is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and four specific codes take precedence over it wherever they apply: `ERR_TRUE_UP_ALREADY_POSTED` when a true-up is posted for an agreement and contract year that already has a posted true-up, or when a posted true-up is posted again; `ERR_TRANSFER_ALREADY_OPEN` when a transfer case is opened while another for the same agreement is still in a non-terminal status; `ERR_ELECTION_ALREADY_OPEN` when an end-of-term election is created while another for the same agreement is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`; and `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` when a child record is written against an agreement whose `lifecycle_state` is `DRAFT`, `PREPAID_CLOSED` or `PURCHASED_CLOSED`. Two deliberate exclusions matter here. An agreement closed by removal or by relocation is *not* reported on this rung — its panels are physically gone, so `REMOVED_CLOSED` and `RELOCATED_CLOSED` fall through to rung 10 and report the decommissioned-asset code instead. And an agreement sitting in `TRANSFER_PENDING` is *not* reported on this rung when an end-of-term election creation targets it; that case falls through to rung 12 and reports `ERR_TRANSFER_IN_PROGRESS` so the client learns which closing is in the way.
9. **Parent version freshness.** `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_OUTAGE_EVENT_VERSION`, HTTP 412, on true-up creation and truck-roll creation respectively, as specified in §2.7.
10. **Decommissioned-asset lockout.** `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`, HTTP 409, when a new metered month, outage event, roof-work order, transfer case or end-of-term election is written against an agreement whose `service_status` is `DECOMMISSIONED`.
11. **Business-temporal rules.** All carry HTTP 422: `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CLOSING_DATE_IN_PAST`, `ERR_DETECTED_AT_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and `ERR_ROOF_DATES_INVALID`. Their trigger conditions are stated in the workflow subsections they guard.
12. **Business-referential rules.** All carry HTTP 422: `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR`, `ERR_ROOF_WORK_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_TRANSFER_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_PERIODS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_TRUCK_ROLL_NOT_CLAIMABLE`, `ERR_BUYER_FIELD_MISMATCH` and `ERR_UNAPPLIED_CREDIT_BLOCKS_PURCHASE`.
13. **Business-constraint rules.** `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), `ERR_ROOF_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), `ERR_ROOF_WORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), `ERR_CLAIM_REVISION_LIMIT` (HTTP 422), `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD` (HTTP 409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_NUMBER` (HTTP 409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` (HTTP 409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_TARIFF_CODE` (HTTP 409) and `ERR_PRODUCT_TERMS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
14. **Fiscal rules.** `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) when posting a true-up would breach the contract year's credit cap, and `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` (HTTP 422) when a warranty approval allows more than was claimed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Portfolio Foundations: Homeowners, the Tariff Book and Booking an Agreement

Servicing begins with the people. A `ServicingAgent` or a `PortfolioAdmin` registers a homeowner with their legal `full_name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and not blank), an `email` that must contain an at-sign and that doubles as the servicing-portal login handle, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters for the mailing address, a `fico_score` between 300 and 850 inclusive, a `dti_ratio` expressed as a fraction of gross income between 0.0000 and 1.0000 with at most four decimal places, and a verified `monthly_income_cents` that may not be negative. Email uniqueness is enforced across the whole homeowner collection and is compared without regard to letter case; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`. The same registration flow is used twice in this domain: once for the current customer, and again for an assuming buyer before a transfer can be underwritten.

The tariff book is portfolio configuration and only a `PortfolioAdmin` may publish to it. A published schedule carries a `utility_code` of between 2 and 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters, the utility's own `tariff_code` of between 1 and 40 characters, a `generation` of `NEM_1`, `NEM_2` or `NEM_3`, a blended import rate in `retail_rate_per_kwh_millicents` that must be strictly positive, an `export_credit_rate_per_kwh_millicents` that may be zero but not negative, a non-bypassable `monthly_fixed_charge_cents` that may be zero but not negative, the first calendar date the schedule applies as `effective_from_date`, and an optional `grandfathers_predecessors` flag that defaults to false. The pair of `utility_code` and `tariff_code` must be unique across the book; a duplicate pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TARIFF_CODE`. A schedule is immutable once published — there is no way to edit or delete it — which is precisely what makes a successor tariff safe: it is a new row, and an agreement only ever moves onto it through the deliberate administrative action described in §3.2.

Booking an agreement is a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` operation and lands the contract in `DRAFT`. The payload names the `homeowner_id` of the lessee or offtaker of record and the `tariff_id` of the net-metering schedule the host site is billed on today, then describes the site and the deal: a `site_address` of between 1 and 200 characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters for the host site, a `product_type` of either `LEASE` or `PPA`, a `term_years` that must be exactly 10, 15, 20 or 25, a `contract_start_date` whose day-of-month must be exactly the first of the month, an `escalator_pct` between 0.0000 and 0.0500 with at most four decimal places, a `guaranteed_annual_kwh` that must be strictly positive, a nameplate `system_dc_kw` that must be strictly positive with at most two decimal places, and an optional `guarantee_band_pct` between 0.0000 and 0.2000 with at most four decimal places that defaults to 0.05 when omitted. Exactly one of the two base-rate fields is supplied: `base_monthly_lease_cents` carries the contract-year-1 monthly rent in cents and is required for a `LEASE` while it must be absent for a `PPA`; `base_ppa_rate_per_kwh_millicents` carries the contract-year-1 rate in millicents per kilowatt-hour and is required for a `PPA` while it must be absent for a `LEASE`. A payload that breaks that pairing in either direction — a lease without its rent, a lease carrying a PPA rate, a PPA without its rate, a PPA carrying a lease rent — is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_TERMS_MISMATCH`, and the same check is re-applied at activation.

A freshly booked agreement is fully initialised even though it has not yet gone live. Its `lifecycle_state` is `DRAFT`, its `monitoring_status` is `MONITORING_OK` and its `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE`. Its `relocated_from_agreement_id` is null, because only a successor agreement minted by a relocation closing ever carries a predecessor. Its `contract_end_date` is derived as the `contract_start_date` advanced by `term_years` years and then pulled back by one day, and each executed renewal later pushes that date out by a further five years. Its `current_contract_year` is derived from the logical clock: take the clock's year multiplied by twelve plus the clock's month, subtract the `contract_start_date`'s year multiplied by twelve plus its month, divide by twelve discarding any remainder, and add one — then clamp the result so that it is at least 1 and at most `term_years` plus twelve times `renewal_count`. Its `renewal_count`, `open_outage_count` and `excused_downtime_minutes` all begin at 0, as do the three credit counters `credit_earned_cents`, `credit_applied_cents` and `credit_balance_cents`.

### 3.2. Activation, Escalating Billing Terms and Net-Metering Grandfathering

Activation moves the contract from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` and is performed by a `ServicingAgent` or a `PortfolioAdmin`. It takes no payload, and it re-checks the base-rate pairing described above, again reporting `ERR_PRODUCT_TERMS_MISMATCH` when the stored terms do not match the `product_type`. It also refuses to activate a contract whose `contract_start_date` is after the current clock date — a contract cannot go live before it starts, and that refusal is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Activation is where two values are frozen for the life of the agreement: `grandfathered_export_rate_millicents` is snapshotted from the attached tariff's `export_credit_rate_per_kwh_millicents` and never changes again, and `contract_end_date` is (re)computed from `contract_start_date` and `term_years`. The `tariff_grandfathered` flag is evaluated at the same moment, and the agreement's `version` advances by one.

Grandfathering is a three-part test, and every part is a strict comparison. `tariff_grandfathered` is true only when the currently attached tariff has `grandfathers_predecessors` set to true, **and** the agreement's `contract_start_date` falls strictly before that tariff's `effective_from_date`, **and** the current clock date falls strictly before the twentieth anniversary of the `contract_start_date`. A system whose `contract_start_date` is the very same day as the tariff's `effective_from_date` is not grandfathered, and protection lapses on the exact anniversary rather than the day after it.

Only a `PortfolioAdmin` may move a host site onto a different published schedule — for instance onto a `NEM_3` successor after the utility retires the older generation. The payload carries the replacement `tariff_id`. The agreement's `tariff_id` is replaced, `tariff_grandfathered` is re-evaluated by the same three-part test against the new schedule, and the agreement's `version` advances. Crucially, `grandfathered_export_rate_millicents` is **not** re-snapshotted: the legacy export rate captured at activation survives the move, which is the whole point of the protection.

Both billing products price a contract year as a tier, and the escalator compounds across tier boundaries. For a target contract year, the escalation factor is one plus `escalator_pct`, raised to the power of the contract year minus one — so contract year 1 is unescalated. That factor is exact and is never rounded. Under a `PPA`, the escalated per-kilowatt-hour rate for a contract year is the `base_ppa_rate_per_kwh_millicents` multiplied by the factor and then rounded to a whole millicent, recorded on a metered month as `escalated_rate_millicents`; the month's `escalated_monthly_rent_cents` stays 0. The notional monthly payment for that contract year is then the already-rounded escalated rate multiplied by one twelfth of `guaranteed_annual_kwh`, divided by 1000 to convert millicents to cents, and rounded to a whole cent. **This rounding order is pinned:** the per-kilowatt-hour rate is rounded to a whole millicent first, and it is the rounded rate that multiplies the energy figure. Applying the escalator to an unrounded rate and rounding only the final cent figure is wrong and diverges for many combinations of contract year and energy volume. Under a `LEASE`, `escalated_rate_millicents` stays 0, the escalated rent for a contract year is `base_monthly_lease_cents` multiplied by the factor and rounded to a whole cent, recorded on a metered month as `escalated_monthly_rent_cents`, and the notional monthly payment for that contract year is exactly that figure. Under either product, the annual contract value for a contract year is twelve times that contract year's notional monthly payment; several rules downstream — the annual credit cap, the prepayment payoff, the renewal price, the buyer's payment-burden score — are all built on these two derived figures.

A `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` may ask for a savings projection on an agreement, a computed read that replays one year of the host site's energy economics under whichever net-metering terms actually apply and reports `agreement_id`, the projected `contract_year`, `self_consumed_kwh`, `exported_kwh`, `effective_export_rate_millicents`, `avoided_import_cents`, `export_credit_cents`, `utility_fixed_cents`, `solar_cost_cents`, `annual_savings_cents` and `tariff_grandfathered`. The projection assumes annual production equal to `guaranteed_annual_kwh`. Forty-two percent of that production is treated as consumed on site: `self_consumed_kwh` is the annual production multiplied by 0.42 and rounded to a whole kilowatt-hour, and `exported_kwh` is simply the remainder of the annual production after subtracting it, so the two always sum exactly to the annual figure. The export rate that applies — `effective_export_rate_millicents` — is the frozen `grandfathered_export_rate_millicents` when `tariff_grandfathered` is true, and the current tariff's `export_credit_rate_per_kwh_millicents` otherwise; the import side always uses the *current* tariff regardless of grandfathering. `avoided_import_cents` is `self_consumed_kwh` multiplied by the current tariff's `retail_rate_per_kwh_millicents`, divided by 1000 and rounded to a whole cent. `export_credit_cents` is `exported_kwh` multiplied by the effective export rate, divided by 1000 and rounded to a whole cent. `utility_fixed_cents` is the tariff's `monthly_fixed_charge_cents` multiplied by twelve. `solar_cost_cents` is twelve times the notional monthly payment for the agreement's `current_contract_year`. Finally, `annual_savings_cents` is `avoided_import_cents` plus `export_credit_cents`, minus `utility_fixed_cents`, minus `solar_cost_cents` — a figure that is allowed to come out negative, which is exactly the disclosure a successor-tariff conversation needs. The projection is offered for an agreement that is `ACTIVE` or `END_OF_TERM_PENDING`.

### 3.3. The Monthly Cycle: Metered Production, Excused Availability and Settlement

Every billing month is a record of its own. A `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` records one by naming the `agreement_id`, the calendar `period_year` (which must fall between 2020 and 2099) and `period_month` (1 through 12), together with the inverter-reported `metered_kwh` for the month, which must be a whole number of kilowatt-hours and may not be negative. A month lands in `status` `RECORDED`. At most one month may exist for a given agreement and calendar month; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD`. Recording a month against an agreement whose `lifecycle_state` is `DRAFT`, `PREPAID_CLOSED` or `PURCHASED_CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and recording one against an agreement whose `service_status` is `DECOMMISSIONED` is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`.

Two convenience flags may accompany the recording, and neither is ever stored or echoed back as a field of the month: `auto_reconcile`, which when true reconciles the row inside the same request exactly as if the reconciliation described below had immediately followed the recording, and `auto_issue_credit`, which supplies the credit decision that the reconciliation would otherwise have taken from its own payload and which defaults to false. Recording alone derives the month's `contract_year`, its `due_date`, its `gross_expected_kwh`, its `excused_days` and its `adjusted_expected_kwh`. A month's `contract_year` is computed exactly like an agreement's current contract year, except that the month's own `period_year` and `period_month` stand in for the clock: the month's year multiplied by twelve plus its month, minus the `contract_start_date`'s year multiplied by twelve plus its month, divided by twelve discarding the remainder, plus one. The `due_date` is the last calendar day of the period month advanced by twenty-one days.

Expected production is the annual guarantee spread across the calendar by a fixed seasonality curve. The twelve weights, from January through December, are 0.0520, 0.0620, 0.0830, 0.0980, 0.1090, 0.1120, 0.1130, 0.1060, 0.0930, 0.0760, 0.0530 and 0.0430; they sum to exactly 1.0000. `gross_expected_kwh` is `guaranteed_annual_kwh` multiplied by the weight for the month's `period_month` and rounded to a whole kilowatt-hour — and that rounding happens **before** any availability adjustment is applied.

The month is then pro-rated down for the days on which the system was excused from producing, and excused time arrives from two entirely independent sources that must be merged before they are counted, so that a roof suspension overlapping an excused outage is never charged twice. The first source is roof work: every roof-work order on the agreement whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `PANELS_REMOVED`, `ROOF_COMPLETE` or `REINSTALLED` contributes the closed date interval running from its `suspension_start_date` to its `suspension_end_date`, both endpoints included; orders that are still `REQUESTED` or `QUOTED`, and orders that were `CANCELLED`, contribute nothing. Those intervals are unioned, merging not only overlaps but also zero-day-gap adjacencies — an interval ending on one day and another beginning the very next day become a single interval — and the roof contribution is the number of distinct calendar days of that merged set that fall inside the period month. The second source is outages: every outage event on the agreement whose `status` is `RESOLVED` and whose `excused` flag is true contributes the half-open instant interval from its `detected_at` up to but not including its `resolved_at`; unresolved events and resolved events that were not excused contribute nothing. Those intervals are likewise unioned with overlaps and zero-length gaps merged, the merged set is intersected with the month — from the first instant of the period month up to but not including the first instant of the following month — and the outage contribution is the whole minutes of that intersection, truncated toward zero.

`excused_days` is then the smaller of two figures: the number of calendar days in the period month, and the roof-day count plus the outage minutes converted to days by dividing by 1440 and rounding **up** to a whole day. Because that conversion uses a ceiling, a single excused minute costs a whole day of expectation. Availability is the number of days in the month minus `excused_days`, divided by the number of days in the month, floored at zero; it is an exact ratio and is never rounded. `adjusted_expected_kwh` is `gross_expected_kwh` multiplied by that availability and rounded to a whole kilowatt-hour.

Reconciliation is a `ServicingAgent`-only step and it is what turns a metered month into money. It accepts one optional flag, `issue_credit`, which defaults to false. A month may only be reconciled once its month has fully elapsed against the logical clock: if the clock instant is still before the first instant of the month **after** the period month, the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The parent agreement's `lifecycle_state` must be `ACTIVE`, `TRANSFER_PENDING` or `END_OF_TERM_PENDING`.

The guarantee is a floor, not a target. `guarantee_floor_kwh` is `adjusted_expected_kwh` multiplied by one minus `guarantee_band_pct`, rounded to a whole kilowatt-hour, so the comparison boundary is always an integer. If `metered_kwh` is **strictly** below that floor, `shortfall_kwh` is the floor minus the metered figure; otherwise `shortfall_kwh` is 0 — production exactly equal to the floor earns nothing. `credit_rate_millicents` is the contract-year `escalated_rate_millicents` for a `PPA` agreement and the agreement's current tariff `retail_rate_per_kwh_millicents` for a `LEASE` agreement. `credit_cents` is `shortfall_kwh` multiplied by `credit_rate_millicents`, divided by 1000 and rounded to a whole cent.

The invoice is then built by a six-step waterfall, and **the six steps run in exactly this order**, each consuming the rounded output of the one before it. Step one: `gross_due_cents` is, for a `PPA`, the month's `escalated_rate_millicents` multiplied by `metered_kwh`, divided by 1000 and rounded to a whole cent, and for a `LEASE` it is simply `escalated_monthly_rent_cents`. Step two: the available credit is read as the agreement's `credit_balance_cents` **as it stood before this month issued any credit of its own**. Step three: `credit_applied_cents` is the smaller of `gross_due_cents` and that available credit. Step four: `net_due_cents` is `gross_due_cents` minus `credit_applied_cents`. Step five: `late_fee_cents` is 1.5 percent of `net_due_cents`, rounded to a whole cent, but only when the current clock date is past the month's `due_date`; otherwise it is 0. Step six: `total_invoiced_cents` is `net_due_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`. Two order traps live in that sequence and both are deliberate. The late fee is a percentage of the amount left after credit has been applied, never of the gross amount. And the credit available to a month is the balance before that same month's own shortfall credit is issued, so a month cannot pay itself with the credit it just earned.

Reconciliation lands the month in one of two states. When `issue_credit` is false, or when it is true but `credit_cents` resolves to 0, the month moves `RECORDED → RECONCILED`: `excused_days`, `adjusted_expected_kwh`, `guarantee_floor_kwh`, `shortfall_kwh`, `credit_rate_millicents` and `credit_cents` are recomputed, the six settlement figures are computed in the pinned order, the agreement's `credit_applied_cents` is increased by the month's `credit_applied_cents` and its `credit_balance_cents` is reduced by the same amount, and the agreement's `version` advances once. When `issue_credit` is true **and** `credit_cents` resolves to a value strictly greater than 0, the month instead moves `RECORDED → CREDIT_ISSUED`: every effect of the plain reconciliation applies, and in addition the agreement's `credit_earned_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` are each increased by the month's `credit_cents`. Even though several counters move, the agreement's `version` advances exactly once for the whole transition.

Reopening a month is the compensating operation, and only a `PortfolioAdmin` may perform it. It is refused while any guarantee true-up in `POSTED` status covers the month, which surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A month in `RECONCILED` moves back to `RECORDED`: the agreement's `credit_applied_cents` is reduced by the month's `credit_applied_cents`, its `credit_balance_cents` is restored by the same amount, and the month's `gross_due_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, `net_due_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `total_invoiced_cents` are all reset to 0, with the agreement's `version` advancing once. A month in `CREDIT_ISSUED` moves back to `RECORDED` with one additional unwinding: the agreement's `credit_earned_cents` is reduced by the month's `credit_cents` and its `credit_balance_cents` reduced by the same amount, the applied-credit reversal above is performed as well, and the month's `credit_cents` is reset to 0 alongside the five settlement figures. In both cases the reversal removes exactly the cents the settlement added, so the ledger identity of §2.9 holds again immediately.

### 3.4. Annual Guarantee True-Ups and the Credit Cap

Once a full contract year has elapsed, the year can be settled against the production guarantee as a whole. A `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` computes a true-up by naming the `agreement_id`, the `contract_year` to settle — which must be at least 1 — the `method` to use, and `expected_agreement_version` as required by §2.7. The computation is refused with `ERR_PERIODS_INCOMPLETE` unless twelve metered months exist for that agreement and that `contract_year`, and refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` when at least one of those twelve months has not yet fully elapsed against the clock. A successful computation lands in `status` `DRAFT`, moves no money, and advances the parent agreement's `version` by one. Its `posted_by_user_id` and `voided_by_user_id` are both null while it is `DRAFT`.

Aggregation is over those twelve months in ascending order of `period_year` and then `period_month`, and every one of them is aggregated regardless of its own `status`; only the already-issued offset looks at status at all. `total_metered_kwh` is the sum of their `metered_kwh` and `total_adjusted_expected_kwh` is the sum of their `adjusted_expected_kwh`. `covered_period_ids` records the twelve month ids in that same ascending order; it is a record of what was aggregated and nothing more, and posting the true-up never alters those months' `status`.

The two contractual methods are genuinely different instruments and must both be reproduced exactly. Under `MONTHLY_ROLLING`, every monthly dip is paid: `annual_guarantee_floor_kwh` is 0, `credit_rate_millicents` is 0, `annual_shortfall_kwh` is the sum of the twelve months' `shortfall_kwh`, and `gross_credit_cents` is the sum of the twelve months' `credit_cents` — a total that therefore carries twelve independent roundings. Under `ANNUAL_TRUE_UP`, a strong summer is allowed to offset a weak winter before anything is owed: `annual_guarantee_floor_kwh` is `total_adjusted_expected_kwh` multiplied by one minus the agreement's `guarantee_band_pct`, rounded to a whole kilowatt-hour; `annual_shortfall_kwh` is that floor minus `total_metered_kwh`, floored at zero; `credit_rate_millicents` is the `escalated_rate_millicents` for that contract year under a `PPA` and the agreement's current tariff `retail_rate_per_kwh_millicents` under a `LEASE`; and `gross_credit_cents` is `annual_shortfall_kwh` multiplied by that rate, divided by 1000 and rounded once, at the end, to a whole cent. The two methods are expected to disagree on the same twelve months, and that disagreement is the contractual point rather than a defect.

Whichever method was used, `already_issued_credit_cents` is the sum of `credit_cents` over those twelve months whose `status` is `CREDIT_ISSUED`, evaluated at the moment the true-up is computed, and `net_credit_cents` is `gross_credit_cents` minus that offset, floored at zero — the customer is never paid twice for the same shortfall.

Posting is what moves money, and either a `ServicingAgent` or a `PortfolioAdmin` may do it. A `DRAFT` true-up moves `DRAFT → POSTED`, provided no other true-up for the same `agreement_id` and `contract_year` is already `POSTED`; a violation — including an attempt to post a true-up that is itself already `POSTED` — is reported as `ERR_TRUE_UP_ALREADY_POSTED`. Posting is additionally capped for the contract year: the sum of `net_credit_cents` across every `POSTED` true-up for that agreement and contract year, with this true-up's own `net_credit_cents` included in the sum whether or not it is already posted, may not exceed twenty-five percent of that contract year's annual contract value, computed as 0.25 multiplied by the annual contract value for the year and rounded to a whole cent. A breach is reported as `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Because the target's own figure is always inside the sum, re-posting a `POSTED` true-up that consumed more than half of the cap breaches the cap as well as the state ladder — and the ladder decides which the client hears. On success the agreement's `credit_earned_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` each increase by `net_credit_cents`, `posted_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, `posted_at` records the current clock instant, the covered months keep the `status` they already had, and the agreement's `version` advances by one.

Voiding is the compensating operation and is available to a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin`. A `POSTED` true-up moves `POSTED → VOID` only when the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `posted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the agreement's `credit_earned_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` are each reduced by exactly `net_credit_cents`, `voided_by_user_id` is stamped, and the agreement's `version` advances. A `DRAFT` true-up may also be voided, moving `DRAFT → VOID` with `voided_by_user_id` stamped and **no** ledger movement at all, because a draft never credited the agreement in the first place; there is no posting user to conflict with in that case.

### 3.5. Monitoring, Triage and Truck-Roll Dispatch

Faults are found by the monitoring platform, not by the customer. A `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `PortfolioAdmin` records an alarm against an agreement by supplying the `agreement_id`, the `inverter_serial` of the inverter or gateway that reported the fault (between 1 and 40 characters and not blank), an `outage_type` of `INVERTER_FAULT`, `COMMS_LOSS` or `PRODUCTION_ANOMALY`, and the `detected_at` instant at which monitoring first saw the fault. A `detected_at` strictly after the current logical clock instant is refused with `ERR_DETECTED_AT_IN_FUTURE`. An alarm may not be recorded against an agreement in any of the four closed lifecycle states: `DRAFT`, `PREPAID_CLOSED` and `PURCHASED_CLOSED` yield `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, while an agreement decommissioned by removal or relocation yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`.

A new alarm lands in `status` `OPEN` with `resolved_at` null and `excused` false, its `severity` graded immediately, and the agreement's `open_outage_count` incremented, its `monitoring_status` re-derived, and its `version` advanced. `downtime_minutes` is the whole minutes from `detected_at` to `resolved_at` once the event has left the open set, and the whole minutes from `detected_at` to the current clock instant while it is still open, truncated toward zero in both cases. Severity is graded off that same elapsed-minute figure, measured against `resolved_at` when it is set and against the clock otherwise: fewer than 1440 elapsed minutes — that is, less than one full day — grades `LOW`; from 1440 minutes up to but not including 10080 minutes — one full day up to seven full days — grades `MEDIUM`; and 10080 elapsed minutes or more grades `HIGH`. The lower bound of each band is inclusive and the upper bound exclusive, so exactly 1440 minutes is `MEDIUM` and exactly 10080 minutes is `HIGH`. Severity is re-evaluated when the alarm is triaged and frozen when the event resolves.

An agreement's `monitoring_status` is never toggled incrementally; it is re-derived from scratch after every event that adds to or removes from the agreement's open alarm set, where the open set is every outage event whose `status` is `OPEN`, `TRIAGED` or `DISPATCHED`. When `open_outage_count` is 0 the status is `MONITORING_OK`; when at least one event in the open set is `DISPATCHED` the status is `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED`; otherwise it is `OUTAGE_OPEN`. Re-derivation rather than toggling is what makes the concurrent cases come out right: cancelling one of two dispatched rolls must leave `monitoring_status` at `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED`, because the other event is still `DISPATCHED`.

Triage is exclusive to the `FieldOpsCoordinator` and confirms that an alarm is real and dispatchable: the event moves `OPEN → TRIAGED`, its `severity` is recomputed against the clock, and its `version` advances. An alarm that was never a real fault is instead closed out by a `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `PortfolioAdmin`, moving `OPEN → FALSE_POSITIVE`, which is only possible while no truck roll exists for the event at all — an attempt after a dispatch has ever been created reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Closing an alarm as a false positive stamps `resolved_at` with the current clock instant, freezes `downtime_minutes` at 0, leaves `excused` false, decrements the agreement's `open_outage_count`, and advances both the event's and the agreement's `version`; when that decrement takes the count to 0 the agreement's monitoring dimension moves `OUTAGE_OPEN → MONITORING_OK`.

Dispatching a technician is exclusive to the `FieldOpsCoordinator` and answers a `TRIAGED` alarm. The payload names the `outage_event_id`, a `technician_code` identifying the assigned technician (between 1 and 24 characters and not blank), the inclusive `scheduled_window_start` and the exclusive `scheduled_window_end` of the on-site window, which must be strictly after the start, plus the `expected_outage_event_version` demanded by §2.7. Technician windows are half-open, running from `scheduled_window_start` up to but not including `scheduled_window_end`, and no buffer is applied: if another truck roll in `status` `SCHEDULED` carries the same `technician_code` and its window overlaps the requested one, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, while a window beginning at the exact instant another ends does not conflict, and rolls that are `CANCELLED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_ACCESS` never conflict at all. A successful dispatch lands in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `agreement_id` copied from the linked outage event rather than accepted from the payload, `dispatched_by_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, and `disposition`, `root_cause`, `billable_to`, `completed_at` and `diagnosed_by_user_id` all null with `labor_cost_cents` at 0. The linked event moves `TRIAGED → DISPATCHED` and its `version` advances; the agreement's `monitoring_status` is re-derived — moving `OUTAGE_OPEN → TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED` — and its `version` advances too.

Cancelling a dispatch is the compensating operation, available to a `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `PortfolioAdmin`, and it must leave no trace in any counter. The roll moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, its `billable_to` stays null and its `labor_cost_cents` stays 0, and the technician's window is freed for reuse. The linked event falls back `DISPATCHED → TRIAGED` and therefore stays inside the open set, so the agreement's `open_outage_count` is unchanged; the agreement's `monitoring_status` is re-derived, which returns it `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED → OUTAGE_OPEN` when no other event of that agreement remains `DISPATCHED`, and the agreement's `version` advances.

### 3.6. Field Diagnosis, Cost Allocation and Manufacturer Warranty Claims

Closing out a dispatch is exclusive to the `FieldOpsCoordinator`, and it may not be done by the same `X-User-ID` that created the dispatch — the user recorded in `dispatched_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that refusal precedes any state check. The payload carries a `disposition` of either `SERVICE_COMPLETE` or `NO_ACCESS`, and, when and only when the disposition is `SERVICE_COMPLETE`, a `root_cause` of `INVERTER_FAULT`, `MODULE_DEFECT`, `STRING_FAULT`, `COMMS_ONLY` or `HOMEOWNER_CAUSED`. A `SERVICE_COMPLETE` without a `root_cause`, or a `NO_ACCESS` that carries one, does not satisfy either completion transition.

A serviced visit moves the roll `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. Its `completed_at` is stamped with the current clock instant, its `diagnosed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and its `billable_to` and `labor_cost_cents` are set by the allocation rules below. The linked alarm moves `DISPATCHED → RESOLVED`: its `resolved_at` is stamped with the same clock instant, its `downtime_minutes` is frozen at the whole minutes between `detected_at` and `resolved_at`, and its `excused` flag is set to true when the diagnosed `root_cause` is `STRING_FAULT` or `COMMS_ONLY` and to false for every other cause. When — and only when — `excused` came out true, the agreement's `excused_downtime_minutes` is increased by that event's `downtime_minutes`. The agreement's `open_outage_count` is decremented and its `monitoring_status` re-derived, which takes it `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED → MONITORING_OK` once the count reaches 0, and the event's and agreement's `version` values advance.

A visit that could not get on site moves the roll `SCHEDULED → NO_ACCESS` instead. Its `completed_at` is stamped with the current clock instant, its `billable_to` is set to `HOMEOWNER` and its `labor_cost_cents` to 12500 — a flat one-hundred-and-twenty-five-dollar no-access fee — while `diagnosed_by_user_id` stays null because nothing was diagnosed. The linked alarm falls back `DISPATCHED → TRIAGED` so that it can be dispatched again, meaning it never leaves the open set: `resolved_at` stays null, `excused` stays false, the agreement's `open_outage_count` is unchanged and its `excused_downtime_minutes` gains nothing, and the agreement's `monitoring_status` is re-derived to `TRUCK_ROLL_DISPATCHED → OUTAGE_OPEN` when no other event of that agreement is still `DISPATCHED`.

Cost allocation propagates the field diagnosis into an owner and a labour figure in one step. A `NO_ACCESS` disposition bills the `HOMEOWNER` 12500 cents and excuses nothing. Otherwise the `root_cause` decides: `INVERTER_FAULT` and `MODULE_DEFECT` both set `billable_to` to `PENDING_MANUFACTURER` with `labor_cost_cents` of 52000 and leave the outage unexcused; `STRING_FAULT` and `COMMS_ONLY` both set `billable_to` to `SUNRUN_OM` with `labor_cost_cents` of 38000 and mark the outage excused, because those two causes are the operator's own; and `HOMEOWNER_CAUSED` sets `billable_to` to `HOMEOWNER` with `labor_cost_cents` of 12500 and leaves the outage unexcused. Only the two manufacturer-owned causes are claimable against a manufacturer, and only they leave `billable_to` in the `PENDING_MANUFACTURER` state that a warranty decision is later allowed to move. The chain re-points at most once more: while `billable_to` is `PENDING_MANUFACTURER`, a warranty claim on that roll reaching `APPROVED` changes it to `WARRANTY` and a claim reaching `DENIED` changes it to `SUNRUN_OM`. Nothing re-points a second time, `labor_cost_cents` is never re-priced by a warranty decision, and the outage's `excused` flag is never revised by one either.

Opening a manufacturer claim is exclusive to the `FieldOpsCoordinator`. The payload names the `truck_roll_id` of the completed visit that found the failed component, a `component_type` of `MODULE` or `INVERTER`, the `manufacturer_code` the claim is filed against (between 2 and 24 uppercase alphanumeric characters), the failed component's `serial_number` (between 1 and 40 characters and not blank), its `component_install_date`, and a `claim_amount_cents` that must be strictly positive. Four separate conditions make a roll claimable, and failing any one of them yields `ERR_TRUCK_ROLL_NOT_CLAIMABLE`: the roll's `status` must be `COMPLETED`; its `root_cause` must be `INVERTER_FAULT` or `MODULE_DEFECT`; the claim's `component_type` must agree with that cause, so a `MODULE` claim requires `MODULE_DEFECT` and an `INVERTER` claim requires `INVERTER_FAULT`; and no claim that is not `DENIED` may already exist against that roll. A new claim copies `agreement_id` from the linked roll rather than accepting it in the payload, lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0 and `approved_amount_cents`, `info_request_note`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` all null, and derives its warranty window: `warranty_term_years` is 25 for a `MODULE` claim, running against the module manufacturer's twenty-five-year warranty, and 10 for an `INVERTER` claim; `warranty_expires_on` is the `component_install_date` advanced by that many years and then pulled back one day, so the expiry date itself is still inside the window.

Submission is likewise exclusive to the `FieldOpsCoordinator`, and it serves double duty. A `DRAFT` claim moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamping `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. A claim that came back with questions moves `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, incrementing `revision_count` by one, re-stamping `submitted_by_user_id`, and clearing `info_request_note` back to null. Both paths refuse a claim whose warranty window has closed: if the current clock date is strictly after `warranty_expires_on`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The revision path is additionally capped — a claim sitting in `INFO_REQUESTED` whose `revision_count` has already reached 3 cannot be re-submitted, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_REVISION_LIMIT`.

An adjuster's question is recorded by a `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `PortfolioAdmin`, moving the claim `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` and storing the mandatory `info_request_note` (between 1 and 500 characters) that explains what is required. Decisions are open to the same two roles, and both are guarded against the submitting user: the `X-User-ID` recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` may not decide the claim, and an attempt yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An approval carries `approved_amount_cents`, the amount the manufacturer allowed, which may equal but may not exceed `claim_amount_cents`; a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`. Approval moves the claim `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stores the allowed amount, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and re-points the linked roll's `billable_to` from `PENDING_MANUFACTURER` to `WARRANTY`. A denial takes no payload, moves the claim `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, sets `approved_amount_cents` to 0, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and re-points the linked roll's `billable_to` from `PENDING_MANUFACTURER` to `SUNRUN_OM`. Both `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are terminal, and both advance the claim's `version`.

### 3.7. Roof Work: Taking the Array Off and Putting It Back

Roofs outlive neither the shingles under them nor the twenty-five-year contract above them, so a homeowner occasionally needs the array removed and reinstalled. A `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` opens the request by naming the `agreement_id`, the `requested_removal_date` on which the array is to come off, the `reinstall_target_date` on which it is to go back up, and the `roofer_company` performing the work (between 1 and 80 characters and not blank). Three date conditions are enforced together and all three report `ERR_ROOF_DATES_INVALID`: the `requested_removal_date` may not fall strictly before the current clock date, the `reinstall_target_date` must fall strictly after the `requested_removal_date`, and the two dates may be no more than 45 days apart. As with every other child record, an agreement in `DRAFT`, `PREPAID_CLOSED` or `PURCHASED_CLOSED` reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` and a decommissioned agreement reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`.

The order lands in `status` `REQUESTED` with `quote_cents` and `approved_by_user_id` both null, and derives its suspension window immediately: `suspension_start_date` equals the `requested_removal_date`, `suspension_end_date` equals the `reinstall_target_date`, and the interval between them is treated as closed — both endpoints are inside it. `suspension_days` is the number of days from the start to the end plus one, for that inclusivity. `contract_year` is the contract year containing `suspension_start_date`, and it is the unit against which the per-year cap below is counted.

A `FieldOpsCoordinator` or `ServicingAgent` attaches the roofer's price, supplying a `quote_cents` figure that must be present and may not be negative; the order moves `REQUESTED → QUOTED`. Approval belongs to a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` and is the point at which the suspension window becomes a commitment, so two constraints are checked there. First, no other roof-work order on the same agreement whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `PANELS_REMOVED` or `ROOF_COMPLETE` may have a suspension window that overlaps this one once **both** windows are widened by 3 days at each end; because the windows are closed date intervals, mere adjacency conflicts, a three-day gap between two windows still conflicts, and a seven-day gap does not. A conflict is refused with `ERR_ROOF_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Second, approving the order may not push the number of roof-work orders on that agreement in the same `contract_year` whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `PANELS_REMOVED`, `ROOF_COMPLETE` or `REINSTALLED` above 2; orders still `REQUESTED` or `QUOTED`, and orders that were `CANCELLED`, are not counted at all. Exceeding the cap is refused with `ERR_ROOF_WORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A successful approval moves the order `QUOTED → APPROVED` and stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`.

The three physical steps belong to the `FieldOpsCoordinator`. Taking the panels off moves the order `APPROVED → PANELS_REMOVED` and moves the agreement's asset dimension `IN_SERVICE → ROOF_WORK_SUSPENDED`, leaving `lifecycle_state` entirely alone. The array may not come off ahead of its committed window: if the current clock date is strictly before the order's `suspension_start_date`, the request is refused with `ERR_ROOF_DATES_INVALID` — and note the interaction with the ladder, since an order that is not `APPROVED` at all reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` first. Signing off the roofer's work moves the order `PANELS_REMOVED → ROOF_COMPLETE` and changes nothing else. Reinstallation moves the order `ROOF_COMPLETE → REINSTALLED`, returns the agreement's asset dimension `ROOF_WORK_SUSPENDED → IN_SERVICE`, and freezes `suspension_end_date`; from then on, any metered month reconciled for a month that overlaps the window excuses the suspended days exactly as described in §3.3.

Abandonment is available to a `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` from any of the three pre-physical states, moving the order `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`, `QUOTED → CANCELLED` or `APPROVED → CANCELLED`. Cancelling from `APPROVED` additionally releases the suspension window, which stops blocking later approvals and stops contributing excused days. `REINSTALLED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, and every one of these transitions advances the order's `version`.

### 3.8. Transfer at Sale: Assumption, Prepayment and Relocation

When the house sells, the contract has to go somewhere, and there are exactly three destinations. Opening the case is exclusive to the `ServicingAgent`, who names the `agreement_id`, the `transfer_type`, the `closing_date` of the real-estate closing, and — depending on the type — one of two additional fields. `ASSUMPTION` hands the contract to a qualified buyer and requires `buyer_homeowner_id`, the purchasing homeowner, which must not be the agreement's current `homeowner_id`; it must not carry `new_site_address`. `PREPAYMENT` buys out the remaining term and carries neither of those two fields. `RELOCATION` moves the system to a new roof and requires `new_site_address` while forbidding `buyer_homeowner_id`. Any violation of that field matrix — a missing required field, a forbidden field supplied, or a buyer who is already the current homeowner — is rejected with `ERR_BUYER_FIELD_MISMATCH`. A `closing_date` strictly before the current clock date is rejected with `ERR_CLOSING_DATE_IN_PAST`. Only one transfer may be in flight per agreement: a second attempt while an existing case is `INITIATED`, `CREDIT_REVIEW`, `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `TITLE_CLEARED` is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_ALREADY_OPEN`, and a case may not be opened at all against a decommissioned agreement, which reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`.

The case forks inside that single creation request, and the system takes the fork itself rather than letting the caller choose it. An `ASSUMPTION` lands in `status` `CREDIT_REVIEW` with `payoff_method` set to `NONE` and `payoff_quote_cents` at 0, because nothing is being bought out. A `PREPAYMENT` or `RELOCATION` lands in `INITIATED` and is priced later, at title clearance. In every case `seller_homeowner_id` is copied from the agreement's current `homeowner_id`, `successor_agreement_id` starts null, the five credit fields start null with `required_deposit_cents` at 0, `credit_decided_by_user_id` and `title_cleared_by_user_id` start null, and the agreement moves `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING` with its `version` advancing.

Two term figures are derived from the `closing_date` at creation. `elapsed_term_months` is the closing year multiplied by twelve plus the closing month, minus the `contract_start_date`'s year multiplied by twelve plus its month — that is, whole contract months from the start up to but excluding the closing month. The total term in months is `term_years` plus five times `renewal_count`, all multiplied by twelve; `remaining_term_months` is that total minus `elapsed_term_months`, floored at 0.

Credit qualification is exclusive to the `CreditUnderwriter` and applies only to the assumption arm. It snapshots the buyer's `fico_score` into `credit_fico_snapshot` and their `dti_ratio` into `credit_dti_snapshot`, then scores three weighted sub-scores. The FICO sub-score is the buyer's `fico_score` minus 580, divided by 220 — the span from 580 up to 800 — and clamped into the range 0 through 1. The debt sub-score is 0.45 minus the buyer's `dti_ratio`, divided by 0.45, clamped the same way. The burden sub-score starts from a target payment of ten percent of the buyer's `monthly_income_cents`, rounded to a whole cent: if that target comes out as 0 the payment-to-target ratio is taken to be 2, and otherwise it is the notional monthly payment for the agreement's `current_contract_year` divided by that target; the sub-score is then 2 minus that ratio, clamped into 0 through 1. None of the three sub-scores is rounded. The weighted sum is 0.55 times the FICO sub-score plus 0.30 times the debt sub-score plus 0.15 times the burden sub-score — weights that total exactly 1.00 — and `credit_composite_score` is that sum multiplied by 100 and rounded to exactly two decimal places. The tier comparisons then use the **rounded** composite.

The decision tiers are evaluated strictly top to bottom, and the first one that matches wins. A `fico_score` below 600 is a hard gate: `credit_decision` is `DECLINED` and `required_deposit_cents` stays 0, no matter how good the composite is. Otherwise, a `credit_composite_score` of at least 72.00 yields `APPROVED` with `required_deposit_cents` at 0. Otherwise, a composite of at least 50.00 **together with** a `fico_score` of at least 620 yields `APPROVED_WITH_DEPOSIT`, and `required_deposit_cents` is three times the notional monthly payment for the agreement's `current_contract_year`. Anything else is `DECLINED` with `required_deposit_cents` at 0. Both tier thresholds are inclusive, so a composite of exactly 72.00 is approved outright and a composite of exactly 50.00 with a qualifying FICO carries a deposit. The review stamps `credit_decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and populates all five credit fields regardless of outcome. An approval or a deposit-conditioned approval moves the case `CREDIT_REVIEW → CREDIT_APPROVED`; a decline moves it `CREDIT_REVIEW → CREDIT_DECLINED`, which is terminal, and simultaneously returns the agreement `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` because that closing is dead.

Buyouts are priced at title clearance rather than at intake, so that the figure quoted is the figure the closing table sees. The `payoff_method` a case uses is determined by its `transfer_type` and is not a caller choice: `ASSUMPTION` uses `NONE`, `PREPAYMENT` uses `NPV_REMAINING_PAYMENTS`, and `RELOCATION` uses `UNAMORTIZED_SYSTEM_COST`. Under `NONE` the `payoff_quote_cents` is 0, because an assumption transfers the obligation instead of extinguishing it.

The `NPV_REMAINING_PAYMENTS` method values the payments the homeowner would otherwise have made, discounted for time. The remaining term is split into contract-year buckets: the number of buckets is `remaining_term_months` divided by twelve and rounded **up** to the next whole bucket. The first open contract year is `elapsed_term_months` divided by twelve, discarding the remainder, plus one; buckets are numbered from one, and the contract year a bucket covers is the first open contract year plus that bucket's number minus one. Each bucket covers twelve months, except that the final bucket covers only the months that are actually left — the smaller of twelve and `remaining_term_months` minus twelve times the number of buckets already consumed. A bucket's gross value is the notional monthly payment for that bucket's contract year, computed by the same escalating-payment rule as §3.2, multiplied by that bucket's month count. Each gross bucket is then discounted at six percent per year by dividing it by 1.06 raised to the power of that bucket's number minus one, so bucket one is divided by 1.06 raised to the power of zero and is therefore undiscounted, bucket two is divided by 1.06 once, and bucket three is divided by 1.06 multiplied by itself — and **each discounted bucket is rounded to a whole cent on its own** before anything is added up. `payoff_quote_cents` is the sum of those individually rounded buckets, so the rounding order is per bucket and then sum, never sum and then round.

The `UNAMORTIZED_SYSTEM_COST` method values what is left of the hardware instead. The installed cost is the agreement's `system_dc_kw` multiplied by 320000 cents per kilowatt and rounded to a whole cent. The unamortized fraction is one minus `elapsed_term_months` divided by the total term in months, floored at zero and carried at full precision with no intermediate rounding. `payoff_quote_cents` is the installed cost multiplied by that fraction, rounded to a whole cent, plus a flat relocation and re-permitting fee of 850000 cents.

Title clearance is exclusive to the `TitleAgent` and is where the two arms of the workflow rejoin. On the assumption arm the case moves `CREDIT_APPROVED → TITLE_CLEARED`, and every `UccFiling` recorded against the agreement must be either `SUBORDINATED` or `TERMINATED` — a subordination is enough, because the lien survives the transfer under the new owner. On the prepayment and relocation arms the case moves directly `INITIATED → TITLE_CLEARED`, skipping credit review entirely because there is no buyer to underwrite, and a filing that is merely `TERMINATION_REQUESTED` is also acceptable alongside `SUBORDINATED` and `TERMINATED`, because the buyout will finish the terminations itself at closing. Under either arm, a filing still `FILED` or still `SUBORDINATION_REQUESTED` blocks clearance with `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR`. The `closing_date` is re-checked here against the clock and a closing that has slipped into the past is rejected with `ERR_CLOSING_DATE_IN_PAST`. Clearance stamps `title_cleared_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `requested_by_transfer_case_id` on every filing it touches for this closing, computes `payoff_method` and `payoff_quote_cents` on the prepayment and relocation arms, and — for a `RELOCATION` only — moves the agreement's asset dimension `IN_SERVICE → DECOMMISSION_PENDING`, because the array is about to come off this roof for good.

Closing is available to a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin`, moves the case `TITLE_CLEARED → CLOSED`, and may not happen before the clock date reaches the `closing_date`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. What closing does to the agreement depends entirely on the `transfer_type`, and this is the widest fan-out of side effects in the system.

An `ASSUMPTION` returns the agreement `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE` and replaces its `homeowner_id` with the `buyer_homeowner_id`. The three credit counters — `credit_balance_cents`, `credit_earned_cents` and `credit_applied_cents` — carry over to the new owner unchanged, because the obligation and its history are being assumed whole.

A `PREPAYMENT` moves the agreement `TRANSFER_PENDING → PREPAID_CLOSED`. Any outstanding guarantee credit is spent against the buyout first: the smaller of the agreement's `credit_balance_cents` and the case's `payoff_quote_cents` is added to `credit_applied_cents` and subtracted from `credit_balance_cents`, so a balance larger than the payoff is only partly consumed and the payoff is never over-credited. Every `UccFiling` on the agreement that is not already `TERMINATED` is terminated as a cascade, whatever state it was in, with `terminated_date` stamped to the current clock date, and each filing that was `FILED` or `SUBORDINATED` also has `requested_by_transfer_case_id` stamped with the closing case. Metered months still sitting in `RECORDED` are deliberately left alone — prepayment does not retroactively settle unsettled production.

A `RELOCATION` moves the agreement `TRANSFER_PENDING → RELOCATED_CLOSED` and creates a successor `SolarAgreement` in `ACTIVE`, whose `relocated_from_agreement_id` points back at the predecessor and whose `successor_agreement_id` is recorded on the case. The successor's `site_address` is the case's `new_site_address`, and every economic term is copied verbatim from the predecessor: `product_type`, `term_years`, `escalator_pct`, `base_rate_millicents_per_kwh`, `guaranteed_annual_kwh`, `guarantee_band_pct`, `system_dc_kw`, `contract_start_date` and `tariff_id`. Copying `contract_start_date` unchanged is what makes the escalator continue from where it was rather than restarting, so a relocated homeowner keeps paying their year-nine rate at the new house. The successor's three credit counters start at 0 while the predecessor's are frozen at their closing values, and the predecessor's asset dimension moves `DECOMMISSION_PENDING → DECOMMISSIONED`.

Abandonment is available to a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` from `INITIATED`, `CREDIT_REVIEW`, `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `TITLE_CLEARED`, all moving the case to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal alongside `CLOSED` and `CREDIT_DECLINED`. Cancelling returns the agreement `TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE`, and cancelling a `RELOCATION` out of `TITLE_CLEARED` additionally rewinds the asset dimension `DECOMMISSION_PENDING → IN_SERVICE`, because the array is staying put after all.

### 3.9. UCC Fixture Filings and the Title Chain

A leased solar array is a fixture attached to real property, so the portfolio owner perfects its interest by recording a filing with the county. Those filings are what make a house sale complicated, and clearing them is the gating step for every transfer. A `TitleAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` records one against an agreement by supplying the `filing_number` assigned by the recording office (between 1 and 32 characters, not blank, and unique across the whole portfolio — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_NUMBER`), the `jurisdiction_code` of that office (between 1 and 24 characters and not blank), the `filing_type`, which is either `FIXTURE_FILING` for an original recording or `AMENDMENT` for a continuation, and the `filed_date`, which may not fall after the current clock date. The filing lands in `status` `FILED` with `terminated_date` and `requested_by_transfer_case_id` both null.

Subordination is the two-person path that lets a lien coexist with a new mortgage. A `TitleAgent` or `ServicingAgent` requests it, moving the filing `FILED → SUBORDINATION_REQUESTED`, but only while there is an actual closing to serve: a `TransferCase` on the same agreement must be `INITIATED`, `CREDIT_REVIEW` or `CREDIT_APPROVED`, and that case's identifier is stamped into `requested_by_transfer_case_id`. Recording the executed subordination is exclusive to the `TitleAgent` and moves the filing `SUBORDINATION_REQUESTED → SUBORDINATED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the user who requested it — the same person may not both ask for and record a subordination, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Termination is the path that removes the lien altogether. A `TitleAgent` or `ServicingAgent` requests it, and the request is reachable from two places: `FILED → TERMINATION_REQUESTED` for a lien that was never subordinated, and `SUBORDINATED → TERMINATION_REQUESTED` for one that was subordinated for an earlier closing and is now being released. Recording the termination is exclusive to the `TitleAgent`, moves the filing `TERMINATION_REQUESTED → TERMINATED`, and stamps `terminated_date` with the current clock date. It may not be done while a `TransferCase` on the same agreement is sitting in `TITLE_CLEARED` waiting on this filing, since that closing was cleared on the strength of the filing's current state; such an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `TERMINATED` is the only terminal state.

Terminations also arrive as cascades from elsewhere, and those cascades reach states the manual path cannot. A `PREPAYMENT` closing terminates filings out of `FILED`, `SUBORDINATED` and `TERMINATION_REQUESTED` alike, as described in §3.8. Executing a `PURCHASE` or a `REMOVAL` election terminates every filing out of `FILED` or `SUBORDINATED`, stamping `terminated_date` in each case, as described in §3.10. A filing already in `TERMINATED` is never touched twice and never has its `terminated_date` overwritten.

### 3.10. End of Term: Purchase, Renew or Remove

Twenty or twenty-five years in, the homeowner chooses among three futures for the array, and the choice is time-boxed. Every agreement carries an election window derived from its `contract_end_date`: `window_open_date` is 180 days before the end of the contract and `window_close_date` is 30 days before it, and the window is closed at both ends, so an election submitted exactly on either boundary date is in time. A `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` submits one by naming the `agreement_id` and the `election_type`, which is `PURCHASE` to buy the system outright, `RENEW` to extend at a reduced payment, or `REMOVAL` to have it taken away. A submission before `window_open_date` or strictly after `window_close_date` is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Three exclusivity conditions guard the submission. A second election while one is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_ALREADY_OPEN`. A pending sale takes precedence over an end-of-term decision, so a `TransferCase` on the agreement in `INITIATED`, `CREDIT_REVIEW`, `CREDIT_APPROVED` or `TITLE_CLEARED` blocks the election with `ERR_TRANSFER_IN_PROGRESS` — which is also what an agreement sitting in `TRANSFER_PENDING` reports, rather than the generic not-active error. A decommissioned asset has nothing left to elect over and reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_DECOMMISSIONED`. On success the election lands in `status` `SUBMITTED`, records `elected_on_date` as the current clock date and `elected_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, leaves `accepted_at` and `executed_at` null, and moves the agreement `ACTIVE → END_OF_TERM_PENDING`.

All three prices are computed off one depreciation figure at submission. `elapsed_full_years` is the number of whole years from `contract_start_date` to `elected_on_date`, discarding any partial year. `residual_pct` is one minus 0.045 multiplied by `elapsed_full_years` — four and a half percent of straight-line depreciation per year — rounded to exactly four decimal places and then floored at 0.10, so the residual value never falls below ten percent of installed cost however old the system is. The rounding happens before the floor and before the multiplication that follows it. Critically, the depreciation clock runs from the original `contract_start_date` and never resets, so a homeowner who renewed at year twenty and elects again at year twenty-five is priced off twenty-five elapsed years, not five.

The installed cost is the agreement's `system_dc_kw` multiplied by 320000 cents per kilowatt and rounded to a whole cent, the same basis §3.8 uses. A `PURCHASE` sets `fair_market_value_cents` to that installed cost multiplied by `residual_pct`, rounded to a whole cent, and then floored at 150000 cents — a fifteen-hundred-dollar minimum sale price that binds on old, heavily depreciated systems. A `RENEW` sets `renewal_monthly_payment_cents` to the notional monthly payment for the agreement's final contract year — `term_years` plus five times `renewal_count` — multiplied by 0.75 and rounded to a whole cent, a twenty-five percent discount off the last escalated rate. A `REMOVAL` sets `removal_cost_cents` to `system_dc_kw` multiplied by 45000 cents per kilowatt, rounded to a whole cent, plus a flat mobilization and roof-patching charge of 120000 cents. Whichever figure the `election_type` selects is also copied into `elected_amount_cents`, and the two figures the type did not select stay at 0, so exactly one of the three price fields is ever populated.

Acceptance belongs to a `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin`, moves the election `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and stamps `accepted_at` with the current clock instant. A `PURCHASE` may only be accepted when the agreement's `credit_balance_cents` is exactly 0: unspent guarantee credit has to be refunded or applied before the homeowner buys the system, and an outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_UNAPPLIED_CREDIT_BLOCKS_PURCHASE`. Accepting a `REMOVAL` moves the agreement's asset dimension `IN_SERVICE → DECOMMISSION_PENDING`, scheduling the array for removal while leaving `lifecycle_state` in `END_OF_TERM_PENDING`.

Execution is exclusive to the `PortfolioAdmin`, moves the election `ACCEPTED → EXECUTED`, which is terminal, and stamps `executed_at` with the current clock instant. Two conditions block it. Roof work in flight — a roof-work order on the agreement in `APPROVED`, `PANELS_REMOVED` or `ROOF_COMPLETE` — is refused with `ERR_ROOF_WORK_IN_PROGRESS`, because the array's physical disposition is already committed elsewhere. Any non-terminal `TransferCase` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_IN_PROGRESS`. What execution then does depends on the `election_type`. A `RENEW` returns the agreement `END_OF_TERM_PENDING → ACTIVE`, increments `renewal_count` by one, and extends `contract_end_date` by five years, which reopens a fresh election window five years later. A `PURCHASE` moves the agreement `END_OF_TERM_PENDING → PURCHASED_CLOSED` and terminates every `UccFiling` on it that is not already `TERMINATED`. A `REMOVAL` moves the agreement `END_OF_TERM_PENDING → REMOVED_CLOSED`, requires that the asset dimension already be `DECOMMISSION_PENDING` and advances it to `DECOMMISSIONED`, and likewise terminates every non-terminated `UccFiling`.

Withdrawal is available to a `Homeowner`, `ServicingAgent` or `PortfolioAdmin` from either pre-execution state, moving the election `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` or `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. It returns the agreement `END_OF_TERM_PENDING → ACTIVE`, and withdrawing an already-accepted `REMOVAL` additionally rewinds the asset dimension `DECOMMISSION_PENDING → IN_SERVICE`, undoing the scheduling. A withdrawn election frees the agreement for a new submission, subject to the window still being open.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

A successful creation returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in the `data` envelope. Every other successful call — a read, a list, a state transition, a computed projection — returns HTTP 200.
