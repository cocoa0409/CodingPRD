# Master Data Stewardship & Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise data governance offices are asked to answer a deceptively simple question — *which record is the truth?* — and they are asked it about customers, products, vendors and locations simultaneously, across a dozen contributing systems that each believe their own copy is canonical. The Master Data Stewardship & Governance API is the system of record for that adjudication. It holds the domain definitions themselves, the designation of which contributing system is authoritative for each domain, the named human stewards who are accountable for quality in that domain, the quality rules those stewards are measured against, the duplicate-resolution proposals that collapse redundant records into golden ones, and the change-control process that governs every structural alteration to reference data and hierarchies.

The product's central design commitment is that governance is *gated*, not advisory. A domain carries a derived **governance posture** that summarises how much unresolved quality debt it is carrying and whether it still has a certified authoritative source at all; that posture is what blocks a steward from publishing a record, blocks a data owner from accepting a merge, and blocks the governance office from applying an approved change. Because posture is derived rather than declared, no operator can simply flip it — it moves only as findings are opened, remediated, resolved or waived, and as source registrations come and go. A second commitment is segregation of duties: the steward who raises a change request may not review it, the steward who plans a remediation may not waive it, and the steward who proposes a merge may not decide it. A third is downstream courtesy with teeth — every structural change must be impact-assessed against the subscribed downstream consumers, must observe a notification lead time derived from those consumers' own stated requirements, and cannot be applied until the most critical of them have acknowledged.

Engineering-wise the surface is a flat, graph-shaped REST API mounted under `/mdm`. There is no deep URL nesting: domains, source systems, stewards, registrations, assignments, rules, records, proposals, change requests and consumer subscriptions are all top-level collections wired together by identifier fields carried in request payloads, plus action sub-paths that drive state machines. Several of those state machines run two independent dimensions on a single row, several cascades touch four or five entity types in one request, and two calculations select between materially different variants without the client getting a say. The requirements below describe all of it; nothing in this document is optional.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: identity, envelope shape, pagination, shared resource properties, concurrency, and the cross-cutting governance principles that individual workflows in §3 assume without restating.

### 2.1. Governance Identities and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must declare who is acting and in what capacity, using two HTTP headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. The system recognises exactly five roles, spelled precisely as follows: `GovernanceAdmin`, `DataOwner`, `DataSteward`, `ConsumerRep`, and `Auditor`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, or whose value is not one of those five strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same error is returned when `X-User-ID` is absent while the acting role is one of the three entity-backed roles — `DataOwner`, `DataSteward`, `ConsumerRep` — because for those roles the identifier is load-bearing rather than decorative. For the two opaque roles, `GovernanceAdmin` and `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is optional and its absence is not an authentication failure. Once the headers are accepted, the role must additionally appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called (see the table in §4); if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The three entity-backed roles bind `X-User-ID` to a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `DataOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Steward`; ownership and accountability checks compare that identifier against the steward-identifier fields recorded on the target resource — for example a change request's `requester_steward_id` or a finding's `planned_by_steward_id` — and the acting steward must additionally hold an `ACTIVE` `StewardAssignment` on the target domain whose `steward_role` is `DATA_OWNER` and whose accountability interval covers the current logical clock. Failing that entitlement check yields `ERR_NOT_DOMAIN_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `DataSteward`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Steward`, and the acting steward must hold an `ACTIVE` `StewardAssignment` on the target domain whose `steward_role` is either `DATA_STEWARD` or `CUSTODIAN` and whose interval covers the clock; failing that check yields `ERR_NOT_DOMAIN_STEWARD` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ConsumerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ConsumerSubscription` itself rather than of a steward; a consumer representative may act only on its own subscription, and any attempt to act on a subscription whose `id` differs from the supplied `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SUBSCRIPTION_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The two opaque roles carry no such binding. When `X-User-Role` is `GovernanceAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the governance office owns the registries — domain definitions, source systems, steward identities, source registrations, steward assignments and consumer subscriptions — plus source certification, domain activation, change application and the logical clock, and the server never compares its `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. A `GovernanceAdmin` can never make a stewardship decision: it cannot plan, resolve or waive a finding, cannot decide a merge, and cannot review or decide a change request. When `X-User-Role` is `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque identifier not tied to any persisted entity; an auditor may read every resource in the system, including master records and quality findings, and may mutate nothing.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` states how many rows to skip before the returned page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0. A supplied value outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside the `data` array, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set and `has_more` states whether rows remain beyond the returned page.

All declared query filters on a list endpoint are optional and combine conjunctively: a request supplying two filters returns only the rows matching both. A filter value that is not a member of the filtered field's enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than silently returning nothing. Filters never widen what the acting role is permitted to read.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant whenever any of the resource's own fields change, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments on mutation. The single exception is `ScorecardRun`: a scorecard run is an immutable audit artefact, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and nothing in the system ever rewrites a run once it has been recorded.

Time in this system is a *logical* clock, not wall-clock time. The clock begins at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time. A `GovernanceAdmin` repositions it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting position as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and fires no scheduled action implicitly — nothing is applied, expired or notified as a side effect of moving time. Every deadline, accountability interval, freeze window, lead-time comparison and staleness computation described in this document is evaluated against this logical clock, and every timestamp field the server writes — `activated_at`, `certified_at`, `decertified_at`, `superseded_at`, `opened_at`, `resolved_at`, `published_at`, `measured_at`, `decided_at`, `submitted_at`, `applied_at`, `lineage_documented_at`, `notified_at` and `acknowledged_at` — is stamped from it.

### 2.5. Domain Version Anchoring and Optimistic Concurrency

The `Domain` row is the concurrency anchor for the whole governance graph, and its `version` field obeys one binding rule: **`Domain.version` increments exactly once per HTTP request that mutates the domain row or any row the domain's derived posture and counters read — never once per declared side effect.** A single request that creates a source registration, flips the posture from `FROZEN` to `OPEN` and rewrites `authoritative_source_code` therefore moves the version from N to N+1, not to N+3. A request that ends up mutating nothing on the domain leaves `version` untouched. Because cascades make the exact number of intervening bumps hard for a client to predict, any client about to perform a version-checked write should read the domain immediately beforehand and send back the value it just observed.

Two write operations are version-checked. Creating a source registration requires a non-optional `expected_domain_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Domain`. If that value does not equal the domain's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DOMAIN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists precisely because the write would otherwise succeed on its own merits — the product legitimately allows a new `AUTHORITATIVE` registration to supersede the incumbent one, so a stale `expected_domain_version` is the only signal that the caller's picture of the domain's authoritative designation predates another writer's change. Creating a steward assignment is guarded identically: it too requires `expected_domain_version` matching the domain's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DOMAIN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Superseding an open-ended incumbent assignment is a legitimate product operation, so again only the stale version distinguishes a caller acting on an outdated accountability picture. No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.6. Governance Posture — the Domain-Level Gate

Each `Domain` carries `governance_posture`, a derived value that is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never supplied by a client and never set directly. It is recomputed at the end of a scorecard run (exactly once per run), on every mutation of the domain's finding set — planning a remediation, resolving a finding, or waiving one — and on every change to the status of a source registration for the domain.

The recomputation reads two things. The first is `open_finding_weight`: the sum, rounded half-up to two decimal places, of `severity_weight` over every `QualityFinding` whose governing rule belongs to this domain and whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`. Findings in `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` leave that sum. The second is whether the domain still holds a `SourceRegistration` with `role` of `AUTHORITATIVE` and `status` of `ACTIVE`. The posture is then determined in this priority order: it is `FROZEN` if the domain holds no such active authoritative registration, or if `open_finding_weight` is at least 40.00; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` if `open_finding_weight` is at least 15.00, or if at least one finding in the open set is governed by a rule whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. Both thresholds are inclusive lower bounds — a weight of exactly 40.00 is `FROZEN`, and a weight of exactly 15.00 with no open critical finding is `RESTRICTED`.

Posture evolves independently of the domain's own `status` dimension. A domain still in `DRAFT` can already be `FROZEN` (indeed a freshly defined domain always is, because it has no authoritative source yet), and an `ACTIVE` domain moves freely among all three postures without any `status` change. What posture controls is the right to act: publishing a master record requires the domain to be `ACTIVE` **and** its posture to be `OPEN`; deciding a merge requires the posture to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`; applying a change request requires the posture to be anything other than `FROZEN`. A request that trips any of those three gates is rejected with `ERR_DOMAIN_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.7. Pinned Evaluation Order, Precision and Rounding

Every monetary-style and percentage-style quantity in this system is carried to two decimal places and rounded half up. That applies to `open_finding_weight`, `severity_weight`, `gap_pct`, `completeness_pct`, `survivorship_score`, `match_score`, `weighted_score`, `threshold_pct` and `measured_pct`. Lead times are whole integer days. Freeze-window boundaries and every other instant are exact ISO 8601 UTC values, and interval comparisons on them are inclusive.

Three calculations have an evaluation order that is *pinned* — meaning the order of rounding and aggregation is part of the specification, not an implementation detail, and taking the steps in a different order produces a different and incorrect answer.

First, the scorecard rounds **per rule and then sums**: each evaluated rule's earned points are rounded to two decimal places first, and the already-rounded terms are then added together. Summing unrounded points and rounding once at the end is wrong.

Second, posture recomputation resolves what looks like a circular dependency — a finding's weight depends on the posture, and the posture depends on the sum of finding weights — by pinning the order. The posture as it stands at the instant the request begins is read once and captured; the multiplier derived from that captured posture is computed once per request and applied to every finding the request creates; each finding's `severity_weight` is then frozen on its row forever; and only after all of the request's findings exist are `open_finding_weight` and the posture recomputed, exactly once. Recomputing the posture after each individual finding, and thereby using a larger multiplier for the later findings of the same request, is wrong.

Third, the impacted-consumer lead-time calculation applies the domain's notification floor **before** the surcharges are added and applies the 30-day cap **last**. Folding the surcharges inside the floor comparison is wrong and yields too small a number whenever the floor exceeds the largest consumer-required lead time.

### 2.8. Conserved Governance Counters and Compensating Actions

Five derived quantities are treated as conserved invariants rather than as incidental bookkeeping, and every operation that could disturb one — including every compensating action that undoes an earlier operation — must restore it exactly. Each is defined as a whole-table recount under an explicit status filter, so the values are order-insensitive and independent of the path by which the system arrived at its current state.

`Domain.open_finding_weight` is the two-decimal, half-up-rounded sum of `severity_weight` over the domain's findings whose `status` is `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`. `Domain.published_master_record_count` is the exact integer count of the domain's master records whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`. `Domain.scheduled_change_count` is the exact integer count of the domain's change requests whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. `Steward.open_finding_load` is the exact integer count of findings whose `assigned_steward_id` is that steward and whose `status` is `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` — note the filter is `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` only, so an `OPEN` finding never charges anyone's load. Alongside these, `Domain.scorecard_run_seq` is a monotonic epoch counter incremented by one per scorecard run on the domain, and `Domain.reference_data_revision` is incremented by one every time one of the domain's change requests reaches `APPLIED`; neither is ever decremented, not even by a withdrawal.

The distinction matters most for the two compensating flows. Withdrawing a scheduled change request must release its freeze window, decrement `scheduled_change_count`, and clear the notification fan-out it created — while leaving the domain's applied-change history alone, which is to say `reference_data_revision` does not move. Likewise, retracting a publication decrements `published_master_record_count`, and re-publishing the same record increments it again.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Path parameters are validated left to right, and the first identifier in the path that does not resolve to an existing row determines the 404 error returned. The parameter names and their corresponding errors are: `domain_id` → `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_FOUND`; `source_system_id` → `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`; `steward_id` → `ERR_STEWARD_NOT_FOUND`; `registration_id` → `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`; `assignment_id` → `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `rule_id` → `ERR_QUALITY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`; `run_id` → `ERR_SCORECARD_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; `finding_id` → `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`; `record_id` → `ERR_MASTER_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; `proposal_id` → `ERR_MERGE_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`; `change_request_id` → `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; `subscription_id` → `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`.

Because many operations in this system can fail several different checks at once, the order in which checks run is normative. Validations are evaluated in the following fifteen-step priority sequence, and the first failure determines the response — later checks are not reached and their errors are never returned in preference to an earlier one.

1. **Header authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and a declared role, plus `X-User-ID` present for the entity-backed roles. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every resource named in the path exists, checked left to right, yielding the specific 404 listed above. Applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
4. **Body schema.** Required fields present, enum members valid, numeric ranges respected, array shapes valid — a `measurements` array whose entries each carry `rule_code` and `measured_pct`, a `lineage_path` with at least three entries — and string minimum lengths met. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that takes a request body.
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every identifier supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `domain_id`, `source_system_id`, `steward_id`, `owner_steward_id`, `assigned_steward_id`, `survivor_master_record_id` and `duplicate_master_record_id` — yielding the corresponding 404 (`ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOURCE_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STEWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MASTER_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`). This step guards source-registration creation, steward-assignment creation, quality-rule creation, master-record ingestion, merge-proposal creation, change-request creation, consumer-subscription creation, and remediation planning on a finding.
6. **Domain entitlement.** The acting identity is entitled on the target domain, as described in §2.1, yielding `ERR_NOT_DOMAIN_OWNER`, `ERR_NOT_DOMAIN_STEWARD` or `ERR_NOT_SUBSCRIPTION_OWNER` (403). Applies to every `DataOwner`, `DataSteward` and `ConsumerRep` write operation.
7. **Domain version freshness.** `expected_domain_version` equals the current `Domain.version`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_DOMAIN_VERSION` (412). Applies to source-registration creation and steward-assignment creation only.
8. **State reachability.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists, including lockdown of terminal states. The generic violation is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409); where a specialised identifier exists it is used instead — `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_FINDING_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_CURATED`, `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_RULE_HAS_OPEN_FINDINGS` and `ERR_NO_PENDING_NOTIFICATION`. Applies to every action endpoint.
9. **Cross-entity linkage integrity.** A merge proposal's two records must belong to the same domain (`ERR_CROSS_DOMAIN_MERGE`) and must be distinct (`ERR_SELF_MERGE`); a measurement's `rule_code` must name an `ACTIVE` rule of the scored domain (`ERR_UNKNOWN_RULE_CODE`); a hierarchy-restructure payload's `parent_node_key` must differ from its `child_node_key` (`ERR_HIERARCHY_CYCLE`); a `lineage_path` must begin at the domain's `authoritative_source_code` (`ERR_LINEAGE_SOURCE_MISMATCH`), end at the subscription's `reporting_asset_key` (`ERR_LINEAGE_TARGET_MISMATCH`) and repeat no node key (`ERR_LINEAGE_CYCLE`), and lineage documentation requires the domain to hold an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration at all (`ERR_AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE_UNDEFINED`). All are HTTP 422. This step guards merge-proposal creation, scorecard runs, change-request creation and lineage documentation.
10. **Segregation of duties.** A change request's reviewer may not be its requester; a finding's waiver may not be its remediation planner; a merge proposal's decider may not be its proposer. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This step guards taking a change request under review, waiving a finding, and deciding a merge proposal — and note that because it sits at priority 10, a state-reachability failure at priority 8 or a linkage failure at priority 9 surfaces in preference to it.
11. **Governance posture gate.** As specified in §2.6. Violation: `ERR_DOMAIN_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (409). This step guards publishing a master record, deciding a merge proposal, and applying a change request.
12. **Classification and clearance.** A consumer subscription may be created only with a `clearance_level` whose rank is at least the domain's `classification_level` rank, and a classification-uplift change request's `target_classification_level` must rank strictly above the domain's current `classification_level`. Violation: `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (403). This step guards consumer-subscription creation and change-request creation.
13. **Interval and calendar geometry.** Steward-assignment intervals may not overlap for the same domain and accountability role unless the incoming assignment is explicitly superseding (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP`, 409); a supplied `effective_to` must fall strictly after `effective_from` (`ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, 422); a freeze window must clear every other scheduled window on the domain by at least 72 hours (`ERR_FREEZE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409); `scheduled_apply_at` must be at or after the computed `earliest_apply_at` (`ERR_LEAD_TIME_NOT_SATISFIED`, 409); and applying requires the logical clock to have reached `scheduled_apply_at` (`ERR_APPLY_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`, 409). This step guards steward-assignment creation, source-registration creation, change-request scheduling and change-request application.
14. **Capacity, uniqueness and confidence constraints.** This step covers the domain-specific business constraints described throughout §3: `ERR_DUPLICATE_RULE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONSUMER_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOURCE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STEWARD_EMAIL`, `ERR_MATCH_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_MATCH_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`, `ERR_CHANGE_CALENDAR_CAPACITY`, `ERR_RECORD_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_OWNER_NOT_PRINCIPAL`, `ERR_LINEAGE_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_TIER1_ACK_MISSING`, `ERR_NO_QUALITY_RULES`, `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE`, `ERR_DUE_DATE_IN_PAST`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STEWARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `ERR_RESOLUTION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. It guards domain creation, source-system registration, steward registration, quality-rule creation, remediation planning, finding resolution, master-record ingestion, merge-proposal creation, merge rescoring, merge decision, record publication, consumer-subscription creation, change-request decision, change-request scheduling, change-request application, and domain activation.
15. **Scorecard weight budget.** The sum of `weight_points` over the domain's `ACTIVE` quality rules, counting the incoming or updated rule, must not exceed 100. Violation: `ERR_RULE_WEIGHT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). This step guards quality-rule creation and quality-rule modification.

### 2.10. Data Classification and Sensitive-Data Handling

Every domain declares a `classification_level`, one of `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED`. Those four values form a strict rank ordering in which `PUBLIC` is rank 1, `INTERNAL` is rank 2, `CONFIDENTIAL` is rank 3 and `RESTRICTED` is rank 4, and every clearance comparison in the system is a comparison of those ranks. The boolean `sensitive_handling_required` is derived, never supplied: it is true exactly when the domain's `classification_level` is `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED`, and false for `PUBLIC` and `INTERNAL`. It is recomputed whenever the classification changes.

Downstream consumers declare their own `clearance_level` from the same four-value enumeration. A consumer may only be registered against a domain if its clearance rank is greater than or equal to the domain's classification rank; equality is acceptable. When a domain's classification is later uplifted, consumers whose clearance has fallen below the new bar are not silently tolerated — they are suspended, as described in §3.8. Classification also feeds the notification lead-time calculation: a domain classified `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED` adds two extra days of required notice to every impact assessment.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Governed Domain

Everything in this system hangs off a domain definition, and defining one is a governance-office act. A `GovernanceAdmin` supplies the domain's `code` — one of the four master data domains `CUSTOMER`, `PRODUCT`, `VENDOR` or `LOCATION`, and no two domain definitions may share a code, a second attempt being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_CODE` — together with a human-readable `name` of between 1 and 120 characters and the `classification_level` that governs sensitive handling. Two further inputs are optional. `notification_lead_days_floor` sets the minimum notice, in whole days between 0 and 30, that the domain will impose on any change that impacts a downstream consumer; when the caller omits it the floor is five days. `expected_attribute_count` declares how many attribute keys a complete record in this domain is supposed to carry, from 1 to 50, and when omitted it is five; this number is the denominator of every completeness measurement in the domain, so a domain expecting five attributes treats a record carrying three populated keys as sixty percent complete.

A newly defined domain lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `activated_at` still null, and — because it has not yet been given an authoritative source — its derived `governance_posture` is already `FROZEN`. Its `version` starts at 1. Every derived quantity starts empty: `open_finding_weight` at 0, `published_master_record_count` at 0, `scheduled_change_count` at 0, `scorecard_run_seq` at 0, `reference_data_revision` at 0, and `authoritative_source_code`, `quality_score_provisional` and `quality_score_certified` all null. The boolean `sensitive_handling_required` is derived immediately from the supplied classification. Reading a single domain returns every one of those derived governance counters alongside the inputs; listing domains may be narrowed by `status`, by `classification_level` and by `governance_posture`, in addition to the usual `limit` and `offset`.

Contributing systems are registered separately from the domains they feed. Each `SourceSystem` carries a short `code` of 1 to 30 characters that must be unique system-wide — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOURCE_CODE` — a descriptive `name`, and a `trust_tier` between 1 and 5 in which 5 denotes the most trusted system. A freshly registered system begins in `status` `REGISTERED` with `certified_at`, `decertified_at` and `decertification_reason` all null, and source systems may be listed filtered by `status`. Certification is the gate that lets a source become authoritative and contribute curated records: the transition `REGISTERED → CERTIFIED` is available only while the system is still `REGISTERED` (attempting it on an already-certified or `DECERTIFIED` system is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`), it stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock, and — because certification can be the missing ingredient that a domain's posture was waiting on — it also recomputes the `governance_posture` of every domain that already holds an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration on the newly certified system.

Human accountability is modelled separately again. A `Steward` row carries a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across all stewards (`ERR_DUPLICATE_STEWARD_EMAIL` otherwise), and a `certification_level` of `JUNIOR`, `SENIOR` or `PRINCIPAL`. Its `open_finding_load` begins at 0 and is thereafter maintained by the system, never supplied. Stewards may be listed filtered by `certification_level`, and reading a single steward exposes the derived `open_finding_load`.

Binding a steward to a domain is done through an accountability assignment. The governance office supplies the `domain_id`, the `steward_id`, the `steward_role` held — `DATA_OWNER`, `DATA_STEWARD` or `CUSTODIAN` — and a half-open accountability interval: `effective_from` is inclusive and `effective_to`, when supplied, is exclusive, so an assignment covers the clock when `effective_from` is at or before the current instant and either `effective_to` is null or the current instant falls strictly before it. Supplying an `effective_to` that is not strictly later than `effective_from` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Two assignments for the same domain and the same `steward_role` may not have intersecting intervals; an incoming assignment that would intersect an `ACTIVE` incumbent is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` unless the caller explicitly asks to hand over by sending `supersede_incumbent` set to true. A handover drives the incumbent from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, and it is only available when the incumbent is `ACTIVE` with a null `effective_to`, when the incumbent's `domain_id` and `steward_role` both match the incoming assignment's, and when the incumbent's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the incoming assignment's `effective_from`. On handover the incumbent's `effective_to` is set to the incoming assignment's `effective_from`, its `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_at` is stamped from the clock, and the domain's `version` advances by one so that the accountability picture has a fresh concurrency anchor. The incoming assignment itself is created `ACTIVE` with a null `superseded_at`. Assignments may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, by `steward_id`, by `steward_role` and by `status`.

Activation is the moment a domain stops being a sketch and starts governing data, and the governance office may only perform it once four independent readiness conditions all hold. The domain must still be in `DRAFT` — attempting activation on a domain that is already `ACTIVE` is `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_DRAFT`. The domain must have at least one quality rule in `ACTIVE` status, otherwise `ERR_NO_QUALITY_RULES`. The domain must have both an `ACTIVE` `DATA_OWNER` assignment and an `ACTIVE` `DATA_STEWARD` assignment whose intervals cover the clock; a domain with an owner but no steward, or a steward but no owner, is rejected with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_INCOMPLETE`. And the domain must hold an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` source registration whose underlying source system is in `CERTIFIED` status, otherwise `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE`. When all four hold, the `DRAFT → ACTIVE` transition sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `governance_posture`, and advances the domain's `version` by one. Note that activation does not force the posture to `OPEN`: if the domain has already accumulated enough finding weight, it can activate straight into `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`.

### 3.2. Authoritative Source Designation and the Decertification Cascade

Registering a source against a domain is how the governance office declares what a system means to that domain. The registration payload carries the `domain_id`, the `source_system_id`, the designated `role` — `AUTHORITATIVE`, `CONTRIBUTING` or `CONSUMING` — a domain-local `trust_rank` between 1 and 10 in which 1 is the highest precedence, an inclusive `effective_from`, an optional exclusive `effective_to`, and the `expected_domain_version` demanded by §2.5. Registrations are created in `status` `ACTIVE` with `superseded_at` and `revoked_reason` null, and may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `source_system_id`, `role` and `status`.

A domain has at most one authoritative source at a time, and the product resolves competition by supersession rather than by refusal. When an incoming registration declares `role` of `AUTHORITATIVE` for a domain that already holds an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration, the incumbent moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`: its `superseded_at` is stamped from the clock and its `effective_to` is back-filled to the incoming registration's `effective_from`, so the two intervals abut without a gap. The domain's `authoritative_source_code` is then rewritten to the `code` of the incoming registration's source system, and the domain's `governance_posture` is recomputed — which is how designating a certified authoritative source can lift a domain out of `FROZEN` in a single request. Note that `authoritative_source_code` holds a source system's short code, not an identifier, and that it is derived rather than declared: it always reflects whichever registration currently has `role` of `AUTHORITATIVE` and `status` of `ACTIVE` for the domain, and it is null when no such registration exists. A client wanting to see the underlying row rather than the code reads it back by listing the domain's registrations filtered to the authoritative role.

Decertification is the most consequential single operation in the system, and it runs a five-hop cascade in one request. It is available only from `CERTIFIED`, and the caller supplies a `reason` string. **Hop 1** moves the source system `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`, stamps `decertified_at` from the clock, and stores the supplied reason as `decertification_reason`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, drives every `SourceRegistration` on that system whose `status` is `ACTIVE` from `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED`, writing the literal string `SOURCE_DECERTIFIED` into each one's `revoked_reason`. This is the branching fork on the registration lifecycle, and the two branches diverge sharply: `SUPERSEDED` preserves the domain's authoritative pointer because a replacement registration exists, whereas `REVOKED` destroys it. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, visits every domain that is thereby left holding no `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration, sets that domain's `authoritative_source_code` to null, and recomputes `governance_posture` — which necessarily yields `FROZEN`, since absence of an active authoritative registration is itself a freezing condition. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3's freeze, takes every master record of those domains whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` and drives it to `publication_status` `RETRACTED` and `curation_status` `QUARANTINED`, writing the literal string `AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE_LOST` into each record's `quarantine_reason`; because the entire published set is retracted, the domain's `published_master_record_count` lands at 0. **Hop 5**, caused by hop 4's retraction of the published set, takes every consumer subscription of those domains that is still `ACTIVE` and whose `lineage_status` is `DOCUMENTED` and moves it to `lineage_status` `STALE`, on the grounds that a documented flow whose first node no longer exists is no longer a truthful description. Across the whole cascade each affected domain's `version` advances by exactly one, in accordance with §2.5 — not once per hop.

The posture side of the domain's parallel state machine deserves restating in its own terms, because it is driven entirely by the system and never by a client. `OPEN → RESTRICTED` fires when a recompute finds `open_finding_weight` at or above 15.00 but below 40.00, or finds at least one finding in the `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` set whose rule carries `CRITICAL` severity, while an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration still exists; it sets the posture to `RESTRICTED` and advances the domain's `version`. `RESTRICTED → FROZEN` fires when a recompute finds `open_finding_weight` at or above 40.00, or finds that no `AUTHORITATIVE` registration with `ACTIVE` status remains; it sets the posture to `FROZEN` and, exactly as in hops 4 and 5 above, retracts and quarantines the domain's published records with `quarantine_reason` `AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE_LOST`, zeroes `published_master_record_count`, stales every `ACTIVE` subscription whose lineage was `DOCUMENTED`, and advances `version`. `OPEN → FROZEN` fires directly, skipping `RESTRICTED`, when the last `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration disappears; it additionally nulls `authoritative_source_code`, and it carries the same retraction, quarantine, counter-zeroing and lineage-staling side effects and the same single `version` advance. Recovery runs the other way with equal specificity. `FROZEN → RESTRICTED` fires when an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration exists again and a recompute finds `open_finding_weight` at or above 15.00 but below 40.00, or finds that at least one open `CRITICAL`-severity finding remains; it sets the posture to `RESTRICTED` and advances `version`. `RESTRICTED → OPEN` fires on a finding-set recompute triggered by resolving or waiving a finding, and requires both that an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration exists and that the recomputed `open_finding_weight` is below 15.00 with no remaining `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` finding whose rule severity is `CRITICAL`; it sets the posture to `OPEN` and advances `version`. Neither recovery transition un-retracts a record or un-stales a lineage — those must be re-driven explicitly.

### 3.3. Defining Quality Rules and Spending the Weight Budget

A quality rule is what makes a domain measurable. A steward defines one by naming the `domain_id` it scores, the `owner_steward_id` accountable for it and its findings, a `rule_code` of 1 to 20 characters that must be unique within the domain (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RULE_CODE` otherwise), the `dimension` measured — one of `COMPLETENESS`, `VALIDITY`, `UNIQUENESS`, `TIMELINESS` or `CONSISTENCY` — a `weight_points` value between 1 and 100 expressing the rule's share of the scorecard, a `threshold_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 carried to two decimal places, and a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` that determines how heavily a breach of the rule weighs on the domain. Rules are created in `status` `ACTIVE` and enter the scorecard denominator from the next run onward. Only a `PRINCIPAL`-certified steward may own a `CRITICAL`-severity rule: naming an owner whose `certification_level` is `JUNIOR` or `SENIOR` on a `CRITICAL` rule is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_NOT_PRINCIPAL`.

The scorecard is a fixed budget of one hundred points per domain. Summing `weight_points` across all of the domain's `ACTIVE` rules — including the rule being created, or including the new value of a rule being modified — must yield at most 100; exceeding it is `ERR_RULE_WEIGHT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Two facts about the `rule_code` are worth internalising early, because they show up again in §3.4: ascending lexicographic order of `rule_code` is what pins the order in which a scorecard run creates its findings, and because `rule_code` is unique within a domain that ordering is total and needs no tie-break.

Rules can be adjusted in flight. A steward may revise `threshold_pct`, revise `weight_points`, or move `status` to retire the rule, in any combination; a weight revision re-validates the hundred-point budget against the domain's `ACTIVE` rule set exactly as a creation does. Retirement is the transition `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and it is guarded: a rule that still carries any finding in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` status cannot be retired, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RULE_HAS_OPEN_FINDINGS`. A retired rule leaves the scorecard denominator from the next run onward — it never enters either numerator or denominator again — but findings already opened against it keep the `severity_weight` they were pinned with, so retiring a rule does not quietly deflate the domain's quality debt. Rules may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `owner_steward_id`, `status` and `dimension`.

### 3.4. Running the Scorecard

A scorecard run is the periodic act of measuring a domain against its rules. A steward or data owner supplies two things: a `basis` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED`, and a `measurements` array in which each entry names a `rule_code` and the `measured_pct` observed for it. A measurement naming a `rule_code` that is not an `ACTIVE` rule of the scored domain is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_RULE_CODE`. The run produces a `ScorecardRun` audit row and returns it with a 201 status, since a run is a newly created resource.

The two bases are not two ways of saying the same thing; they select genuinely different rule sets, and the choice is the caller's. On a `PROVISIONAL` basis the evaluated set is exactly those `ACTIVE` rules for which the payload supplied a `measured_pct`; an `ACTIVE` rule left unmeasured is excluded from the numerator, excluded from the denominator, and produces no finding. A `PROVISIONAL` run is always permitted, including while the domain holds no certified authoritative source at all. On a `CERTIFIED` basis the evaluated set is *every* `ACTIVE` rule of the domain, and an `ACTIVE` rule left unmeasured is treated as having a `measured_pct` of 0.00 — it stays in the denominator, earns zero points, and produces a finding. A `CERTIFIED` run additionally requires the domain to hold an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration whose source system is in `CERTIFIED` status, and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE`. It follows that for identical measurement payloads the `CERTIFIED` score is less than or equal to the `PROVISIONAL` score whenever any `ACTIVE` rule was left unmeasured.

Scoring proceeds in a fixed sequence. First the evaluated rule set is determined per the chosen basis and ordered by `rule_code` ascending. If that set is empty, the run's `weighted_score` is 0.00 and no findings are created at all. Otherwise each evaluated rule earns points: a rule whose `measured_pct` is greater than or equal to its `threshold_pct` earns its full `weight_points` — exact equality is a pass and earns the full weight — while a rule that falls short earns its `weight_points` multiplied by the ratio of its `measured_pct` to its `threshold_pct`, and that per-rule figure is rounded half up to two decimal places *before* anything is added up. The already-rounded per-rule figures are then summed to give the earned total. The denominator is the plain sum of `weight_points` over the evaluated rules. The run's `weighted_score` is one hundred times the earned total divided by that denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places — a second and final rounding. `rules_evaluated_count` records how many rules entered the denominator and `rules_failed_count` how many of them had a `measured_pct` strictly below their `threshold_pct`.

Findings are then opened, in ascending `rule_code` order, for each evaluated rule that fell short — but with a deduplication rule that matters enormously in practice. If the rule already carries a finding in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` status, no new finding is created; instead that existing finding's `measured_pct` is refreshed to the newly observed value and its `gap_pct` is recomputed as the rule's `threshold_pct` minus the new `measured_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and its `severity_weight` is left strictly untouched. Only where no such open finding exists is a new one created, in `status` `OPEN`, carrying the observed `measured_pct`, the corresponding `gap_pct`, and a freshly pinned `severity_weight`. The count of genuinely new findings is recorded as `findings_created_count`, which is therefore not the same number as `rules_failed_count` whenever a breach was already known.

The weight a new finding contributes is where the domain's own posture feeds back into its quality debt. The base points come from the breached rule's `severity`: a `LOW`-severity rule contributes 1 point, `MEDIUM` contributes 3, `HIGH` contributes 8 and `CRITICAL` contributes 20. Those base points are then multiplied by a posture multiplier: 1.00 when the domain's posture was `OPEN`, 1.25 when it was `RESTRICTED` and 1.50 when it was `FROZEN`. The multiplier is derived from the posture as it stood at the instant the run started, which the run records as `posture_before`; it is computed once and recorded as `posture_multiplier_applied`, and the *same* multiplier is applied to every finding the run creates. Each finding's `severity_weight` is the base points times that multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is frozen on the finding row forever after — nothing recomputes it. Only after every finding of the run exists does the system recompute `open_finding_weight` and the `governance_posture`, exactly once, and the resulting posture is recorded on the run as `posture_after`. A run that begins `FROZEN` and creates three findings therefore weights all three at 1.50, not the first at 1.50 and the later ones at some higher multiplier.

The run also persists its epoch. The domain's `scorecard_run_seq` is incremented by one and the resulting value is copied onto the run as `run_seq`, the run's `measured_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the run's `domain_id` records the domain scored. A `PROVISIONAL` run writes its `weighted_score` to the domain's `quality_score_provisional`; a `CERTIFIED` run writes it to `quality_score_certified`. The other basis's stored score is not touched, so a domain that has only ever run provisionally keeps a null `quality_score_certified`. The domain's `version` advances by one for the request. Runs are immutable once written: they may be read individually and listed filtered by `domain_id` and by `basis`, but never modified, which is why a run carries no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Separately from recording a run, the same scoring logic is available as a pure read that creates nothing. That computed read accepts an optional `basis` query parameter and evaluates the domain against the measurements currently stored on its findings, returning a small projection rather than a resource: `domain_id`, the `basis` used, the `weighted_score`, `rules_evaluated_count`, `rules_failed_count`, the domain's current `open_finding_weight`, and its current `governance_posture`. It creates no `ScorecardRun`, opens no finding, moves no counter and does not touch the domain's `version` or its posture.

### 3.5. Remediating and Closing Findings

A `QualityFinding` is the unit of quality debt. It records the `scorecard_run_id` of the run that opened it and the `quality_rule_id` of the rule it breached — and since the rule knows its own domain, the finding's owning domain is reached through the rule. It carries the `opened_at` instant, the `measured_pct` most recently observed for the breached rule, the derived `gap_pct`, and the pinned `severity_weight`. Findings may be listed filtered by `scorecard_run_id`, `quality_rule_id`, `assigned_steward_id`, `planned_by_steward_id`, `waived_by_steward_id` and `status`.

From `OPEN`, a finding forks two ways, and the two branches have deliberately divergent consequences. The remediation branch charges a named steward's workload; the waiver branch never does and can only be walked by a data owner.

Planning a remediation drives the finding `OPEN → REMEDIATION_PLANNED`. The acting steward supplies the `assigned_steward_id` who will do the work, a `due_at` instant, and an optional `plan_note`. Three preconditions apply. The finding must still be `OPEN`; attempting to plan a finding that is already `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` is rejected with `ERR_FINDING_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The nominated assignee must hold an `ACTIVE` `StewardAssignment` on the finding's domain, with a `steward_role` of `DATA_STEWARD`, `CUSTODIAN` or `DATA_OWNER`, whose interval covers the current clock; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STEWARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. And `due_at` must fall strictly after the current logical clock — a due date equal to the clock is not acceptable — otherwise `ERR_DUE_DATE_IN_PAST`. On success the `assigned_steward_id`, `due_at` and `plan_note` are stored, `planned_by_steward_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the assignee's `open_finding_load` is incremented by one. The domain's `open_finding_weight` does not change: the finding's `severity_weight` was pinned at creation and the finding remains in the open set, so planning merely re-labels the debt rather than reducing it.

Resolving a finding evidences that the breach is gone. The caller supplies a `verified_measured_pct` and an optional `resolution_note`, and that percentage must be greater than or equal to the breached rule's `threshold_pct`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_RESOLUTION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. Resolution is reachable from both open-set states, and the two paths differ in exactly one respect. From `REMEDIATION_PLANNED → RESOLVED`, the finding's `status` becomes `RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` is stamped from the clock, `measured_pct` is overwritten with the supplied `verified_measured_pct`, `gap_pct` is set to 0.00, the `resolution_note` is stored, the previously charged assignee's `open_finding_load` is decremented by one, the domain's `open_finding_weight` is recomputed so that this finding's `severity_weight` leaves the sum, and the `governance_posture` is recomputed. From `OPEN → RESOLVED`, everything is identical — `status` `RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` stamped, `measured_pct` overwritten, `gap_pct` set to 0.00, `open_finding_weight` recomputed and posture recomputed — except that no steward's `open_finding_load` moves, because no assignment was ever charged. Either way the domain's `version` advances by one for the request.

Waiving a finding is the data-owner escape hatch, and it is deliberately harder to reach. Only a `DataOwner` holding an `ACTIVE` `DATA_OWNER` assignment on the finding's domain covering the clock may waive, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `planned_by_steward_id` — the person who planned the remediation may not be the person who excuses it, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The supplied `waiver_justification` must be at least 20 characters long; a shorter string is a schema violation. Waiver is reachable from either open-set state. From `OPEN → WAIVED`, the finding's `status` becomes `WAIVED`, `waived_by_steward_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `resolved_at` is stamped from the clock, no `open_finding_load` moves because none was charged, and both `open_finding_weight` and `governance_posture` are recomputed. From `REMEDIATION_PLANNED → WAIVED`, the same fields are written and the same recomputations run, and additionally the assignee's `open_finding_load` is decremented by one. Attempting to waive an already-closed finding is `ERR_FINDING_ALREADY_CLOSED`.

### 3.6. Ingesting, Curating and Publishing Master Records

A `MasterRecord` is one contributing system's claim about one business entity. Ingesting one requires the `domain_id` it belongs to, the `source_system_id` that contributed it, a `natural_key` of 1 to 60 characters that is the business key of the record within the domain, an `attributes` object holding a flat map from attribute key to string value, and a `last_verified_at` instant recording when the record was last checked against its source. The `natural_key` must be unique for the combination of domain and source system: the same business key arriving from two different systems is the normal duplicate case and is allowed, but the same key arriving twice from the same system is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`. A newly ingested record begins with `curation_status` `CANDIDATE` and `publication_status` `UNPUBLISHED`, and with `superseded_by_master_record_id`, `published_at` and `quarantine_reason` all null. Records may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `source_system_id`, `superseded_by_master_record_id`, `curation_status` and `publication_status`.

Two derived numbers are computed on the record at ingestion, again whenever it is curated, and again when a merge fills its attributes from a loser. The first is `completeness_pct`. The system counts the attribute keys that are physically present in `attributes` with a value that is neither null nor the empty string — an absent key and a null-valued key are both counted as missing, and so is an empty string. That count is divided by the domain's `expected_attribute_count`, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half up to two decimal places; the result is then capped at 100.00, so a record carrying more populated keys than the domain expects reads as exactly complete rather than over-complete.

The second is `survivorship_score`, which expresses how good a candidate this record is to be the survivor of a merge. It is the sum of three components. The **trust component** is the contributing source system's `trust_tier` multiplied by ten, so it ranges from 10 points for a tier-1 system up to 50 points for a tier-5 one. The **completeness component** is the record's `completeness_pct` multiplied by four tenths and rounded half up to two decimal places, so it ranges from 0 up to 40 points. The **recency component** starts from a maximum of 20 points and decays by 4 points for every whole month of staleness, where staleness in months is the number of whole days between the record's `last_verified_at` and the current logical clock, divided by thirty and rounded down to a whole number; the component is floored at zero, so a record more than five whole months stale contributes 0 rather than a negative number. The three components are added and the total rounded half up to two decimal places. In practice a tier-5 record verified today with fully populated attributes scores 50 plus 40 plus 20, which is 110.00, while a tier-1 record ninety days stale with half its attributes populated scores 10 plus 20.00 plus 8, which is 38.00.

Curation is the steward's endorsement that the record is fit to be part of the golden set, and it is reachable from two different states with different requirements. `CANDIDATE → CURATED` requires only that the record's contributing source system is in `CERTIFIED` status — curating a record from a `REGISTERED` or `DECERTIFIED` system is rejected with `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and it accepts an optional `last_verified_at`, which is stored when supplied. `QUARANTINED → CURATED` is the rehabilitation path for a record that was quarantined by a decertification cascade, and it is stricter: the source system must again be `CERTIFIED`, and `last_verified_at` is *not* optional — it must be supplied and must be strictly later than the value already on the record, because the whole point of rehabilitation is fresh verification. On that path the `quarantine_reason` is cleared back to null. Either way the record's `curation_status` becomes `CURATED` and its `survivorship_score` is recomputed against the new verification instant.

Publication is the act of releasing a record downstream, and it is the most heavily gated write on the record. It is reachable from `UNPUBLISHED → PUBLISHED` and, after a retraction, from `RETRACTED → PUBLISHED`; both paths carry the identical set of preconditions and the identical side effects. A record already in `publication_status` `PUBLISHED` cannot be published again and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. The record's `curation_status` must be `CURATED`, otherwise `ERR_RECORD_NOT_CURATED`. The owning domain's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and its `governance_posture` must be `OPEN` — `RESTRICTED` is not good enough for publication — otherwise `ERR_DOMAIN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. And the record's `completeness_pct` must be at least 60.00, with exactly 60.00 acceptable, otherwise `ERR_RECORD_INCOMPLETE`. On success `publication_status` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the domain's `published_master_record_count` is incremented by one, and the domain's `version` advances by one.

The reverse direction, `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED`, is never client-driven. It happens when the record loses a survivorship selection and when a decertification cascade freezes its domain, and in both cases it decrements the domain's `published_master_record_count` by one and advances the domain's `version`. Two further system-driven curation transitions complete the picture. `CURATED → MERGED_AWAY` is applied to the loser of a merge decision, and along with the status change it writes the effective survivor's identifier into the loser's `superseded_by_master_record_id`; if the loser happened to be `PUBLISHED`, its `publication_status` additionally becomes `RETRACTED` and the domain's published count is decremented. `CURATED → QUARANTINED` is hop 4 of the decertification cascade described in §3.2: it applies to a `CURATED` record whose domain has just been frozen because its `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration was revoked, it writes `AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE_LOST` into `quarantine_reason`, and it retracts the record's publication if it was published.

It bears emphasising that a master record runs two state dimensions independently on the same row. Publishing never changes `curation_status`, and curating never changes `publication_status`. The only coupling is one-directional: reaching `MERGED_AWAY` or `QUARANTINED` on the curation dimension forces a retraction on the publication dimension. `MERGED_AWAY` is terminal — a record merged away is never curated, published or merged again.

### 3.7. Duplicate Resolution: Matching, Review and Survivorship

A merge proposal is a steward's hypothesis that two records describe the same real-world entity. The proposer names a `survivor_master_record_id` and a `duplicate_master_record_id`, and supplies the two matching-engine outputs as `name_similarity_pct` and `key_overlap_pct`, each a percentage from 0.00 to 100.00 carried to two decimal places. The proposal's own `domain_id` is not supplied — it is copied from the survivor candidate's domain at creation — and `proposer_steward_id` is set from the acting `X-User-ID`. The two records must belong to the same domain, or the proposal is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_DOMAIN_MERGE`, and they must be distinct rows, a proposal naming the same record twice being rejected with `ERR_SELF_MERGE`. A new proposal is created in `status` `PROPOSED` with `reviewer_steward_id`, `effective_survivor_master_record_id`, `survivorship_variant`, `survivorship_field_map`, `override_justification` and `decided_at` all null, `survivor_swapped` false, and `rework_count` at 0. Proposals may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `survivor_master_record_id`, `duplicate_master_record_id`, `effective_survivor_master_record_id`, `proposer_steward_id`, `reviewer_steward_id` and `status`.

The composite `match_score` is computed server-side and is never supplied by a client. The system first forms a weighted sum of three inputs: five tenths of the `name_similarity_pct`, plus three tenths of the `key_overlap_pct`, plus two tenths of the lesser of the two candidates' `completeness_pct` values as those values stand persisted at the moment of the call. That weighted sum is rounded half up to two decimal places. Then a structural discount applies: intra-source duplicates are inherently less trustworthy, so when the survivor candidate and the duplicate candidate share the same `source_system_id`, the whole rounded composite is multiplied by eight tenths and the product is rounded half up to two decimal places again — the discount is applied *after* the weighted sum has been rounded, not folded into it. When the two candidates come from different source systems no discount applies and the rounded weighted sum stands as the `match_score`. The derived `match_confidence` then reads `HIGH` when `match_score` is at least 85.00, `MEDIUM` when it is at least 60.00 but below 85.00, and `LOW` below 60.00. A proposal whose computed `match_score` falls below 45.00 is not worth a human's attention and is refused outright with `ERR_MATCH_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, both at creation and at rescoring.

Review is a lightweight hand-off. The transition `PROPOSED → UNDER_REVIEW` requires the proposal to be in `PROPOSED` status and requires the acting steward to hold an `ACTIVE` `StewardAssignment` on the proposal's domain covering the clock; it records the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewer_steward_id`.

The decision belongs to a data owner and takes a `decision` field with one of three values. `REJECT` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stamps `decided_at` from the clock, and mutates no record whatsoever: `effective_survivor_master_record_id` stays null and `survivor_swapped` stays false. `REWORK` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → REWORK`, requires a `rework_note` of at least 10 characters, clears `reviewer_steward_id` back to null, and deliberately leaves `decided_at` null because the proposal is not decided — it is bounced. `MERGE` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → MERGED` and is the heavy path. In all three cases the proposal must actually be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, and in all three cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the proposal's `proposer_steward_id`, a self-decision being rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A `MERGE` decision carries four further preconditions beyond those two. The domain's `governance_posture` must be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`; a `FROZEN` domain blocks the merge with `ERR_DOMAIN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Both candidate records must have `curation_status` `CURATED`, otherwise `ERR_RECORD_NOT_CURATED`. The source system behind the effective survivor must be in `CERTIFIED` status, otherwise `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And the confidence bar must be met: a `HIGH`-confidence proposal merges freely, a `MEDIUM`-confidence proposal merges only when the data owner supplies an `override_justification` of at least 20 characters, and a `LOW`-confidence proposal cannot be merged at all — the failures in the medium-without-justification and low cases are both `ERR_MATCH_CONFIDENCE_TOO_LOW`.

Survivorship selection is where the system asserts its own judgement over the proposer's, and the variant is chosen by the data, not by the client. The **source-priority** variant applies when exactly one of the two candidates was contributed by the source system behind the domain's `AUTHORITATIVE` registration that is `ACTIVE` at decision time. In that case the survivor is that single authoritative-source candidate *even if its `survivorship_score` is strictly lower than the other candidate's*; the score plays no part in choosing the survivor and is used only afterwards to fill in the field map. The **score-priority** variant applies when both candidates share the authoritative `source_system_id`, and equally when neither of them carries it — including the case where the authoritative registration has been superseded onto some third source system altogether. In that case the survivor is the candidate with the higher `survivorship_score`, breaking ties first by the later `last_verified_at` and then by the earlier `created_at`, and never by `id`. The consequence to internalise is that for one and the same pair of records the two variants select different survivors whenever the authoritative-source record is not the higher-scoring one. Whichever variant ran is recorded as `survivorship_variant`, the chosen record's identifier is recorded as `effective_survivor_master_record_id`, and `survivor_swapped` is set true exactly when that identifier differs from the `survivor_master_record_id` the proposer originally nominated.

The merge itself is a three-hop cascade. **Hop 1** selects the variant, computes and stores `effective_survivor_master_record_id`, `survivor_swapped`, `survivorship_variant` and `survivorship_field_map`, sets the proposal's `status` to `MERGED`, and stamps `decided_at` from the clock. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, enriches the effective survivor from the loser: for every attribute key appearing on either record, the winning value is the effective survivor's value when that key is present and non-null on the survivor, and otherwise the loser's value — so the survivor's data is never overwritten, only its gaps are filled. For each key the `survivorship_field_map` records the `natural_key` of whichever record supplied the winning value, giving a field-by-field audit of provenance. The survivor's `last_verified_at` is set to the later of the two records' values, and its `completeness_pct` and `survivorship_score` are both recomputed against the enriched attribute set. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2's absorption of the loser, drives the loser's `curation_status` to `MERGED_AWAY` and writes the effective survivor's identifier into the loser's `superseded_by_master_record_id`; if the loser was `PUBLISHED` its `publication_status` becomes `RETRACTED` and the domain's `published_master_record_count` is decremented by one. The domain's `version` advances by one for the request, and its counters are reconciled per §2.8. Note that records whose `curation_status` is `MERGED_AWAY` or `QUARANTINED` are not eligible candidates at all, which is why the curation precondition above is checked before any of this runs.

A bounced proposal can be re-matched. Rescoring drives `REWORK → PROPOSED` and takes fresh `name_similarity_pct` and `key_overlap_pct` values from the matching engine. It requires the proposal to be in `REWORK` — rescoring a proposal that is still `PROPOSED` or already decided is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and it requires both candidate records to still have a `curation_status` of `CANDIDATE` or `CURATED`, since a record that has since been merged away or quarantined can no longer participate. On success `match_score` and `match_confidence` are recomputed from the new percentages by exactly the formula above, including the intra-source discount and the 45.00 floor, the `status` returns to `PROPOSED`, and `rework_count` is incremented by one so the audit trail records how many loops the proposal has been through.

### 3.8. Change Control over Reference Data and Hierarchies

Structural alterations to a governed domain go through change control. A steward raises a request by naming the `domain_id`, a `change_type`, a short `title`, and a `proposed_values` payload. There are three change types and they behave differently throughout. `REFERENCE_VALUE_ADD` adds a reference value and requires the key `reference_value` inside `proposed_values`. `HIERARCHY_RESTRUCTURE` re-parents a node and requires the keys `parent_node_key` and `child_node_key`; naming the same node key for both is a cycle and is rejected with `ERR_HIERARCHY_CYCLE`. `CLASSIFICATION_UPLIFT` raises the domain's data classification and requires the key `target_classification_level`, whose rank must be strictly above the domain's current `classification_level` rank — an uplift to the same or a lower level is rejected with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. The derived boolean `structural` is true for exactly the two non-additive types, `HIERARCHY_RESTRUCTURE` and `CLASSIFICATION_UPLIFT`, and false for `REFERENCE_VALUE_ADD`; it is never supplied by the client. A new request is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `requester_steward_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, `reviewer_steward_id` null, `impact_consumer_count` and `required_lead_days` at 0, `revision_cycle_count` at 0, and `submitted_at`, `earliest_apply_at`, `scheduled_apply_at`, `freeze_window_start`, `freeze_window_end`, `applied_at`, `review_note` and `withdraw_reason` all null. Requests may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `requester_steward_id`, `reviewer_steward_id`, `status` and `change_type`.

Submission is what triggers the downstream impact assessment, and it is reachable from two states. `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` is the first submission; `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` is a resubmission after the reviewer asked for revisions, and it differs in exactly one respect — it increments `revision_cycle_count` by one. Both paths require the owning domain's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and both require the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the request's `requester_steward_id`: only the originator resubmits their own request. Both paths stamp `submitted_at` from the logical clock and then compute `impact_consumer_count`, `required_lead_days` and `earliest_apply_at` from scratch against the *current* consumer set and the *new* `submitted_at` — a resubmission does not reuse the numbers from the first submission.

The impact assessment runs in a pinned sequence. First the impacted consumer set is determined: it is every `ConsumerSubscription` of the request's domain whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and which is either impacted by virtue of the change being structural, or — for a non-structural change — carries a `criticality` of `TIER1`. In other words, a structural change impacts every active consumer regardless of criticality, whereas a plain reference-value addition impacts only the `TIER1` consumers. `SUSPENDED` subscriptions never count. The size of that set is stored as `impact_consumer_count`. Second, a base lead time is taken as the largest `required_lead_days` among the impacted consumers, or zero when the impacted set is empty. Third, a floor lead time is taken as the domain's `notification_lead_days_floor` when at least one consumer is impacted, and zero when none is. Fourth, a breadth surcharge is derived from the *count* rather than from the days: three extra days when at least five consumers are impacted, one extra day when at least three but fewer than five are impacted, and none below three. Fifth, a classification surcharge of two extra days applies when the domain's `classification_level` is `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED`, and none otherwise. Sixth — and this ordering is normative — the greater of the base lead and the floor lead is taken *first*, the two surcharges are added to that, and only then is the result capped at thirty days; the capped figure is stored as `required_lead_days`. Folding the surcharges inside the comparison against the floor produces a smaller and incorrect number whenever the base lead is below the floor. Finally, `earliest_apply_at` is set to `submitted_at` advanced by `required_lead_days` whole days.

Consider a concrete case: a hierarchy restructure on a `CONFIDENTIAL` domain whose floor is five days, with four active consumers requiring 2, 3, 7 and 4 days respectively. The change is structural so all four are impacted, making `impact_consumer_count` four. The base lead is seven, the floor is five, and the greater of the two is seven. Four impacted consumers earns the one-day breadth surcharge, and the confidential classification earns two more, giving ten. Ten is under the cap, so `required_lead_days` is 10 and `earliest_apply_at` is ten days after `submitted_at`.

Review and decision belong to a data owner and enforce segregation of duties on both sides. `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` requires the acting steward to hold an `ACTIVE` `DATA_OWNER` assignment on the domain covering the clock, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `requester_steward_id`; a requester attempting to review their own request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The transition records the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewer_steward_id`. The subsequent decision takes a `decision` field and an optional `review_note`, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the recorded `reviewer_steward_id` — the owner who picked the request up is the owner who must dispose of it. The three branches diverge in what happens to the reviewer and to the impact assessment. `APPROVE` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stores the `review_note`, retains `reviewer_steward_id`, and retains the impact assessment as computed; for a structural change it carries one extra precondition — every `ACTIVE` `TIER1` consumer of the domain must already have `lineage_status` `DOCUMENTED`, and if any does not, the approval is refused with `ERR_LINEAGE_INCOMPLETE`. `REQUEST_CHANGES` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, stores the `review_note`, clears `reviewer_steward_id` back to null, and resets the impact assessment — `impact_consumer_count` back to 0, `required_lead_days` back to 0 and `earliest_apply_at` back to null — because the next submission will recompute all three. `REJECT` drives `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stores the `review_note`, retains `reviewer_steward_id`, and deliberately retains `earliest_apply_at` for the audit trail rather than clearing it.

Scheduling an approved change occupies a window on the domain's change calendar. The data owner supplies `scheduled_apply_at`, which must be at or after the computed `earliest_apply_at`; an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_NOT_SATISFIED`. The freeze window is a closed interval around that instant whose extent depends on whether the change is structural: a structural change reserves 24 hours of lead before the instant and 48 hours of trail after it, while a non-structural change reserves no lead at all and 12 hours of trail. Those become `freeze_window_start` and `freeze_window_end`. Because the window endpoints are inclusive, a mandatory separation buffer of 72 hours is required between windows: for every *other* change request of the same domain currently in `SCHEDULED` status, the new window is acceptable only if its start falls at or after that other window's end plus 72 hours, or its end plus 72 hours falls at or before that other window's start. Exactly 72 hours of separation is accepted; one second less is refused with `ERR_FREEZE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Requests in `APPLIED` or `WITHDRAWN` status have already released their windows — their `freeze_window_start` and `freeze_window_end` are null — and never participate in the separation test. A second, aggregate constraint applies to structural changes only: the system counts the other `SCHEDULED` structural change requests of the domain whose `scheduled_apply_at` falls within thirty days of the proposed instant in either direction, and if two or more such neighbours already exist, the schedule is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_CALENDAR_CAPACITY` — at most two structural changes may be scheduled on a domain within any rolling thirty-day span. On success the `status` becomes `SCHEDULED`, `scheduled_apply_at` and the two window boundaries are stored, the domain's `scheduled_change_count` is incremented by one and its `version` advances by one, and the notification fan-out runs: every consumer subscription in the impacted set — determined by exactly the same filter the impact assessment used — has its `pending_change_request_id` set to this request, its `notified_at` stamped from the clock, and its `acknowledged_at` and `acknowledged_revision` cleared to null so that a stale acknowledgement from an earlier notification cannot be mistaken for a fresh one.

Application is the governance office's act and runs a three-hop cascade. Four preconditions guard it. The request must be in `SCHEDULED` status, otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The logical clock must have reached `scheduled_apply_at`, with equality acceptable; an earlier clock yields `ERR_APPLY_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`. The domain's `governance_posture` must be anything other than `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_DOMAIN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. And every consumer subscription whose `pending_change_request_id` points at this request and whose `criticality` is `TIER1` must have a non-null `acknowledged_at`; an unacknowledged critical consumer blocks the change with `ERR_TIER1_ACK_MISSING`. **Hop 1** sets `status` to `APPLIED`, stamps `applied_at` from the clock, increments the domain's `reference_data_revision` by one, decrements the domain's `scheduled_change_count` by one, releases the freeze window by setting both `freeze_window_start` and `freeze_window_end` to null, and advances the domain's `version` by one; when the `change_type` is `CLASSIFICATION_UPLIFT` it additionally writes the payload's `target_classification_level` into the domain's `classification_level` and recomputes `sensitive_handling_required` from the new level. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1's new classification, takes every `ACTIVE` consumer subscription of the domain whose `clearance_level` rank has fallen below the new `classification_level` rank and drives it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, writing the literal string `CLEARANCE_BELOW_CLASSIFICATION` into its `suspended_reason` and clearing its `pending_change_request_id`, `notified_at` and `acknowledged_at` to null. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2 having shrunk the active consumer set, revisits every other change request of the domain whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and recomputes its `impact_consumer_count`, `required_lead_days` and `earliest_apply_at` against the surviving active consumer set — each such request keeping its own `submitted_at` as the base for the recomputed `earliest_apply_at`. Two further effects complete the apply: every consumer subscription of the domain whose `lineage_status` is `DOCUMENTED` and whose `lineage_revision` is lower than the domain's new `reference_data_revision` is moved to `lineage_status` `STALE`, and every subscription still `ACTIVE` that was notified about this request has its `pending_change_request_id`, `notified_at` and `acknowledged_at` cleared to null now that the change has landed.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to the requester or to any data owner holding an `ACTIVE` `DATA_OWNER` assignment on the domain covering the clock, and it requires a `withdraw_reason`. It is reachable from every non-terminal state — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `SCHEDULED` — and it always sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN` and stores the reason. Beyond that, what it compensates depends on what had already happened. Withdrawing from `SCHEDULED` reverses the schedule: `freeze_window_start` and `freeze_window_end` are set to null, the domain's `scheduled_change_count` is decremented by one, and the domain's `version` advances by one. It also reverses the notification fan-out: every consumer subscription whose `pending_change_request_id` names this request has that field, plus `notified_at`, `acknowledged_at` and `acknowledged_revision`, cleared to null. Crucially the domain's `reference_data_revision` is left unchanged — a withdrawn change never counted as applied, so it never advanced the revision and withdrawal must not roll one back. Withdrawing from `DRAFT`, by contrast, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `requester_steward_id`, and because no freeze window and no notification ever existed, no counter changes at all.

### 3.9. Downstream Consumers, Lineage and Acknowledgement

A `ConsumerSubscription` records one downstream system's dependency on one governed domain. The governance office registers it with the `domain_id` consumed, a `consumer_code` of 1 to 40 characters unique across all subscriptions (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CONSUMER_CODE` on a collision), the `reporting_asset_key` naming the terminal reporting asset the consumer produces, a `clearance_level`, a `criticality` of `TIER1`, `TIER2` or `TIER3`, and the `required_lead_days` — between 1 and 30 — of notice this consumer needs before a structural change. Registration enforces the clearance bar of §2.10: a subscription may only be created when its `clearance_level` rank is greater than or equal to the domain's `classification_level` rank, and a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. A new subscription begins with `status` `ACTIVE` and `lineage_status` `UNDOCUMENTED`, an empty `lineage_path`, and `lineage_revision`, `lineage_documented_at`, `pending_change_request_id`, `notified_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_revision` and `suspended_reason` all null. Subscriptions may be listed filtered by `domain_id`, `pending_change_request_id`, `status`, `lineage_status` and `criticality`.

Documenting lineage is a steward's job and is the answer to the auditor's question *where does this number come from?* The steward supplies a `lineage_path`: an ordered array of node keys running from the authoritative source, through each transformation stage, to the reporting asset. The transition `UNDOCUMENTED → DOCUMENTED` and the re-documentation transition `STALE → DOCUMENTED` share an identical validation set. The path must contain at least three node keys, since a source and a target with no transformation between them is not a documented flow; a shorter array is a schema violation. Its first node must equal the domain's current `authoritative_source_code`, otherwise `ERR_LINEAGE_SOURCE_MISMATCH`. Its last node must equal the subscription's `reporting_asset_key`, otherwise `ERR_LINEAGE_TARGET_MISMATCH`. And no node key may appear twice, because a documented flow must be acyclic; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_LINEAGE_CYCLE`. Underlying all three, the domain must actually hold an `ACTIVE` `AUTHORITATIVE` registration — with no authoritative source there is no valid first node at all, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_AUTHORITATIVE_SOURCE_UNDEFINED`. On success the supplied path replaces whatever was stored, `lineage_status` becomes `DOCUMENTED`, `lineage_documented_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `lineage_revision` captures the domain's `reference_data_revision` as it stands at that instant — which is precisely what makes the documentation datable against later changes.

Lineage goes `DOCUMENTED → STALE` without anyone asking. It happens when the domain's `reference_data_revision` advances past the subscription's recorded `lineage_revision`, which is to say when a change request on the domain is applied, and it happens when the domain's `authoritative_source_code` becomes null, which is hop 5 of the decertification cascade. A stale lineage is not an error condition in itself, but it does have teeth: no structural change request on the domain can be approved while any `ACTIVE` `TIER1` consumer's lineage is anything other than `DOCUMENTED`.

Acknowledgement is the only write a consumer representative may make, and it may only be made against its own subscription. It requires a pending notification: a subscription whose `pending_change_request_id` is null has nothing to acknowledge and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NO_PENDING_NOTIFICATION`. On success `acknowledged_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `acknowledged_revision` captures the domain's `reference_data_revision` at acknowledgement time, giving an auditable record of exactly which revision of the reference data the consumer signed off against. Acknowledgement is what unblocks application for `TIER1` consumers, as described in §3.8.

The subscription row, like the master record, runs two independent dimensions. `status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` when a classification uplift leaves the consumer under-cleared, and `lineage_status` moves independently among `UNDOCUMENTED`, `DOCUMENTED` and `STALE`. Suspension never clears documented lineage, and re-documenting lineage never un-suspends a subscription.
