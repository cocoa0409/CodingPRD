# Continuing Ed Noncredit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university extension school sells learning that never appears on a degree transcript, and that fact shapes everything about how its back office runs. There is no registrar's timetable to hide behind: a non-credit section either fills or it does not, and somebody has to decide — on a published date, in front of a contracted instructor and a room full of prepaid corporate learners — whether the section runs at all. Today that decision is taken in a spreadsheet, the refunds that follow are typed by hand into three unconnected systems, and the sponsoring employer discovers weeks later that the voucher it prepaid was quietly consumed by a class that was cancelled.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. It covers the full commercial and academic arc of the extension school: publishing a catalog of courses with continuing-education-unit values and prerequisites, spinning up term-based and rolling sections of those courses, contracting instructors on a stipend-plus-headcount basis, taking registrations paid either out of the learner's own pocket or against a prepaid corporate voucher, executing the minimum-enrollment cancellation decision at census, awarding CEUs and completion certificates against an attendance gate, unwinding registrations through a policy-driven refund and transfer regime, converting a learner's prior non-credit learning into academic credit through a petition pathway, and keeping a repeat-learner marketing list in sync with all of the above.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **money is conserved**: every cent a sponsor deposits is, at every instant, accounted for as either unspent budget, unallocated voucher value, committed value, or consumed value — and a compensating action (a void, a refund, a cancellation) must return the ledger to exactly the identity it held before, with no rounding drift. Second, **time is explicit**: census dates, refund windows, voucher expiries, instructor schedule conflicts, and CEU recency decay are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock that the operator controls, never against wall-clock time. Implementers who treat either commitment as advisory will produce a system that reconciles on paper and not in fact.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API carries its caller identity in two headers: `X-User-Role` names the acting role, and `X-User-ID` names the acting principal. `X-User-Role` must be present on every request and must name one of exactly six declared roles — `ProgramAdmin`, `Registrar`, `Learner`, `Instructor`, `CorporateSponsor`, or `Anonymous`. `X-User-ID` must also be present on every request except those made as `Anonymous`, which is the sole role permitted to omit it. A request whose `X-User-Role` is missing, or names a string that is not one of those six, or whose `X-User-ID` is missing — or present but empty — for a non-`Anonymous` role, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-formed caller whose role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being addressed is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide the extension school's work as follows. A `ProgramAdmin` staffs the program office: it owns the catalog, creates and publishes sections, takes the census cancellation decision, closes sections after they end, contracts instructors, adjudicates credit-for-prior-learning petitions, opens sponsor accounts, and drives the logical clock. A `Registrar` staffs the student-records office: it registers learners on their behalf, records attendance, revokes issued certificates, and decides and settles refund and transfer requests. A `Learner` is a person taking non-credit courses and acting on their own records. An `Instructor` is contracted teaching staff who signs or declines contracts, records attendance, and awards completions on sections they staff. A `CorporateSponsor` is an employer funding learners through prepaid vouchers. `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated visitor whose only capability is self-service learner signup.

Three of these roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the identifier of a persisted row and ownership checks compare that identifier against a foreign-key field on the resource being addressed. When `X-User-Role` is `Learner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Learner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Learner` compare the resource's learner foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Instructor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Instructor`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Instructor` compare the resource's instructor foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CorporateSponsor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CorporateAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CorporateAccount` compare the resource's sponsor foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — specifically as `assessed_by_user_id` on a `CplPetition` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Registrar`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque identifier that may be omitted entirely, and it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Ownership violations carry their own code. A `Learner` acting on another learner's learner record, registration, or petition; an `Instructor` acting on a contract that is not theirs or on a registration whose section they do not staff; a `CorporateSponsor` acting on another sponsor's account or voucher — all are rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The ownership rule extends to two instructor cases that are not simple foreign-key equality: recording attendance on a registration requires the acting `Instructor` to hold a contract on that registration's section whose status is `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE`, and awarding completion on a registration requires the acting `Instructor` to hold a contract on that section whose status is `ACTIVE`. The ownership check is applied on registration creation, payment, attendance recording, completion award, marketing-preference toggling, single-learner fetch, petition creation, citation creation, petition submission, contract signing, sponsor deposits, voucher issuance, voucher assignment, voucher voiding, and refund/transfer request creation.

One further access rule is not about roles at all but about the same acting identity: the program office practises **segregation of duties** on credit-for-prior-learning adjudication. The `ProgramAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as the petition's assessor may never be the `ProgramAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` decides that petition; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Every collection endpoint is paginated by the same two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, a negative `offset`, or a `limit` or `offset` whose supplied text does not parse as an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise, with ties — which the logical clock makes routine, since it advances only when an operator repositions it — broken by the order in which the rows were created, so equal-stamped rows keep their insertion sequence. This ordering is stable and load-bearing — several rules in this document (waitlist seniority, certificate sequence numbering, citation evaluation order, revenue line ordering) depend on it. For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, and those filters are the only channel through which a client can traverse a relationship backwards. Courses may be filtered by `status`, by `prerequisite_course_id`, and by `delivery_mode`. Offerings may be filtered by `course_id`, `term_code`, `status` and `scheduling_mode`. Vouchers may be filtered by `corporate_account_id`, `assigned_learner_id` and `status`. Registrations may be filtered by `offering_id`, `learner_id`, `voucher_id`, `transferred_to_registration_id`, `enrollment_status` and `payment_status`. Instructor contracts may be filtered by `offering_id`, `instructor_id` and `status`. Completion records may be filtered by `registration_id`, `learner_id` and `status`. Refund and transfer requests may be filtered by `registration_id`, `target_offering_id`, `status` and `request_type`. Credit-for-prior-learning petitions may be filtered by `learner_id`, `target_course_id` and `status`. A learner's marketing memberships may be filtered by `segment` and by `state`. All filters are optional and all combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource the API creates is identified by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer. `version` begins at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation is driven directly by the request or indirectly by a cascade originating elsewhere. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every such mutation. The `version` counter is the substrate of the optimistic-concurrency mechanism described in §2.6, and it is also the reason cascade bookkeeping is specified so exhaustively in §3: an implementation that mutates a parent's counters without bumping its `version` will pass a naive read test and fail every concurrency test.

There is exactly one exception. `CplCitation` rows do not participate in versioning: a citation has no `updated_at` and no `version` field, and neither field appears in its representation, which is exactly `id`, `petition_id`, `completion_record_id`, `weight` and `created_at`. Citations are immutable once written — the petition they belong to is the versioned thing, and creating a citation increments the petition's `version` rather than the citation's own.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, window, overlap and recency rule in this specification reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The server starts with its logical clock positioned at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and must not fall back to real wall-clock time either before or after the clock has been repositioned. A `ProgramAdmin` repositions the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity, fires no cascade, and increments no `version`.

Concretely, the logical clock is what is compared against `census_at` when the census decision is taken, against `end_date` when a section is closed out or when a refund window is tested, against `expires_at` when a voucher is redeemed, and against `issued_at` when the age of a cited completion is measured. It is also the source of `enrolled_at`, `issued_at`, `settled_at` and `recomputed_at`, and of `created_at` and `updated_at` on every resource.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Seat and Sponsor-Budget Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the client's decision is made against a snapshot of a parent row that a competing writer may already have invalidated.

Creating a registration against an offering requires the request to carry `expected_offering_version`, a non-optional input field holding the `version` of the target `Offering` as the client last observed it. Seat availability, waitlist depth and the open-versus-waitlist-only posture of the section are all encoded in the offering row, so a client holding a stale version is deciding to enroll against a seat picture that no longer exists. If `expected_offering_version` does not equal the offering's current stored `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and the client must re-read the offering before retrying.

Issuing a voucher against a corporate account requires the request to carry `expected_corporate_account_version`, a non-optional input field holding the `version` of the target `CorporateAccount` as the client last observed it. Issuance draws `face_value_cents` out of the sponsor's `available_cents`, so a sponsor whose supplied version no longer matches the stored version is sizing the voucher against a budget snapshot that a prior deposit or a prior voucher issue has already invalidated. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection neither require nor accept `expected_offering_version`, `expected_corporate_account_version`, or any other expected-version input.

### 2.7. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value, in a request body, or in a response. Wherever a rule multiplies a cent amount by a percentage or a ratio, the product is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents, and the specification below always states exactly where in the evaluation order that rounding happens, because rounding a sum and summing rounded terms give different answers.

Three non-monetary quantities carry their own precision. Continuing Education Units — the `ceu_units` a course is worth, the `ceu_awarded` on a completion record, and a learner's `lifetime_ceu_total` — are decimal numbers carried to exactly one decimal place, rounded half up. An `attendance_ratio` is carried to exactly four decimal places, rounded half up. A petition's `claimed_ceu_total` is carried to exactly two decimal places, rounded half up, while its `awarded_credit_units` is an exact multiple of one half obtained by flooring and never by rounding. A `weight` on a citation is supplied by the client with at most two decimal places.

### 2.8. Request Rejection Order

When a request would fail more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: rules are evaluated in ascending priority order and the **first** rule that fails determines the response. Implementers must respect this ordering literally — a naive implementation that happens to check business rules before existence rules will return the right status for the wrong reason and fail conformance. The ordering, from first-evaluated to last, is:

1. Authentication — `X-User-Role` present and declared, `X-User-ID` present for every role but `Anonymous`; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list; otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request shape, evaluated before any database lookup — every required input field present, types correct, enum members valid, monetary and count fields non-negative integers, `ceu_units` and `weight` within their declared ranges, `course_code` and `email` uniqueness, `end_date` strictly later than `start_date`, `census_at` no later than `start_date`, `term_code` present exactly when `scheduling_mode` is `TERM`, `max_enrollment` no smaller than `min_enrollment`, `target_offering_id` present exactly when `request_type` is `TRANSFER`, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Path existence — every resource named by a path segment exists, validated left to right; otherwise the not-found code for the first unresolvable segment.
5. Body existence — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, covering `course_id`, `learner_id`, `instructor_id`, `offering_id`, `voucher_id`, `target_offering_id`, `completion_record_id`, `prerequisite_course_id` and `target_course_id`; otherwise the corresponding not-found code.
6. Ownership — the entity-backed checks described in §2.1; otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. Optimistic concurrency — the supplied expected parent version equals the stored parent version; otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
8. Offering openness — the offering backing a registration create or a payment is `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`; otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_OPEN`.
9. From-state legality — the addressed resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
10. Course currency — the referenced course is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`.
11. Segregation of duties on a petition decision; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
12. Prerequisite satisfaction on a registration create; otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`.
13. The attendance gate on a completion award; otherwise `ERR_ATTENDANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.
14. Census timing; otherwise `ERR_CENSUS_TOO_EARLY`.
15. Closeout timing; otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_ENDED`.
16. Refund window; otherwise `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
17. Instructor schedule conflict on a contract acceptance; otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.
18. Learner concurrent CEU load on a registration create; otherwise `ERR_LEARNER_LOAD_EXCEEDED`.
19. Transfer target compatibility; otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INCOMPATIBLE`.
20. Citation eligibility; otherwise `ERR_CITATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
21. Duplicate registration; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`.
22. Duplicate credit-for-prior-learning award; otherwise `ERR_CPL_DUPLICATE_AWARD`.
23. Revision budget; otherwise `ERR_CPL_REVISION_LIMIT`.
24. Waitlist capacity; otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_FULL`.
25. Voucher usability; otherwise `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_USABLE`.
26. Sponsor budget sufficiency; otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT`.
27. Voucher balance sufficiency; otherwise `ERR_VOUCHER_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`.
28. Transfer credit sufficiency; otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`.
29. Credit award floor; otherwise `ERR_CPL_AWARD_ZERO`.

The precise trigger condition attached to each of these rules — and the exact set of operations each one guards — is stated in the workflow narrative of §3 rather than gathered here.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Cross-Entity Cascades

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve determines the error. Each resource type has its own not-found code: `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEARNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLETION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404. The same codes are used when a foreign key supplied in a request body fails to resolve, with the caveat that only the identifier types actually accepted in a body can raise them there: a course, offering, learner, instructor, voucher, or completion-record identifier.

Several operations in this system mutate more than one entity. Where that happens, the workflow narrative in §3 enumerates each hop of the cascade in the order it occurs, and each hop is mandatory: the derived counters on a parent (`active_offering_count`, `confirmed_enrollment_count`, `paid_enrollment_count`, `waitlist_count`, `net_program_revenue_cents`, `active_contract_count`, `lifetime_completed_count`, `lifetime_ceu_total`, `funded_cents`, `available_cents`, `unallocated_voucher_cents`, `committed_cents`, `consumed_cents`) are maintained immediately and synchronously as part of the triggering request. There is no deferred recomputation and no eventual consistency anywhere in this system; a read issued immediately after a write observes every consequence of that write.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledger Quantities

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and conservation is an invariant that must hold after every single mutation, not merely at rest.

The first is corporate voucher value. For an individual voucher, `face_value_cents` always equals `remaining_cents` plus `committed_cents` plus `consumed_cents`. For a sponsor account, `funded_cents` always equals `available_cents` plus `unallocated_voucher_cents` plus `committed_cents` plus `consumed_cents`. Committing voucher value against a registration and later releasing it — through a settled refund, or through the section-cancellation cascade — is a forward-and-compensating pair that must leave `funded_cents`, `available_cents` and the identity above exactly as they were before the commitment.

The second is the learner CEU ledger. A learner's `lifetime_ceu_total` always equals the sum of `ceu_awarded` across that learner's completion records whose `status` is `ISSUED`, rounded half up to one decimal place. Awarding a completion and then revoking it must therefore return `lifetime_ceu_total` to exactly its pre-award value, with zero drift.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Catalog Formation, Prerequisites and Retirement

Everything the extension school sells begins as a **Course** — a catalog entry that describes a body of instruction independently of when or how often it is taught. A `ProgramAdmin` creates one by supplying a `course_code`, a `title`, a `ceu_units` value, a `contact_hours` figure, a `list_price_cents` reference price, a `delivery_mode`, a `cpl_credit_units` figure, and optionally a `prerequisite_course_id`. The `course_code` is the catalog's human-facing key: it must be between 1 and 32 characters long and must be unique across all courses, compared as an exact case-sensitive match, so `MGMT-101` and `mgmt-101` are two different codes and neither collides with the other. A duplicate `course_code` is a malformed request and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The academic and commercial numbers on a course are constrained. `ceu_units` — the continuing-education credit a learner earns by completing the course — must be strictly greater than zero, no greater than 10.0, and carried to exactly one decimal place. `contact_hours`, the instructional hours backing that CEU award, must be strictly greater than zero. `list_price_cents` is the catalog reference price and must be zero or positive; note that it is genuinely a *reference* price, because each section may override it. `cpl_credit_units` is what the course is worth in academic credit when a learner earns it through the credit-for-prior-learning pathway rather than by sitting the course, and it must be strictly greater than zero and carried to one decimal place. `delivery_mode` is one of `IN_PERSON`, `ONLINE_SYNC` or `ONLINE_ASYNC`.

Prerequisites are expressed by pointing one course at another. `prerequisite_course_id` defaults to null, meaning the course has no prerequisite. When supplied, it must name an existing course — an unresolvable identifier yields `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND` — and it must not name the course being created itself, nor may it close a prerequisite cycle of any length; either self-reference or cycle formation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because the prerequisite pointer runs from dependent course to prerequisite course, the only way to discover what depends on a given course is to filter the catalog listing by `prerequisite_course_id`.

A course is created directly in `status` `ACTIVE` with an `active_offering_count` of 0. That counter tracks the number of sections of the course whose own status is `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `WAITLIST_ONLY` or `CENSUS_LOCKED` — in other words, sections that are still in play. Sections that have reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` no longer count, so the census-cancellation cascade of §3.6 and the closeout of §3.8 each decrement the parent course's `active_offering_count` and increment that course's `version` by 1 as part of the same request. Retirement, which a `ProgramAdmin` performs, moves the course `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and increments the course's `version` by 1, but it is permitted only when `active_offering_count` is exactly 0; a retirement attempt while any live section still references the course is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `RETIRED` is terminal. A retired course may not back a newly created section, may not have a draft section published, and may not be the target of a credit-for-prior-learning petition submission; all three are rejected with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`.

### 3.2. Sections, Instructor Contracting and Publication

A **section** — an `Offering` in the data model — is a concrete delivery of a catalog course. A `ProgramAdmin` creates one against a `course_id` and chooses a `scheduling_mode` of either `TERM` or `ROLLING`. Term sections belong to an academic term and must carry a `term_code` such as `2026FA`; rolling sections start on demand and must omit `term_code` or send it as null. Supplying `term_code` on a rolling section, or omitting it on a term section, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The calendar of the section is fixed by three instants supplied as ISO 8601 UTC: `start_date`, when instruction begins; `end_date`, which must be strictly later than `start_date`; and `census_at`, the earliest instant at which the minimum-enrollment cancellation decision may be taken, which must be no later than `start_date`. The section also declares a `session_count` of at least 1 — the number of scheduled class meetings, which becomes the denominator of every attendance ratio — a `min_enrollment` of at least 1, a `max_enrollment` no smaller than `min_enrollment`, and a `tuition_cents` figure of zero or more which overrides the course's `list_price_cents` for pricing purposes.

Newly created sections sit in `status` `DRAFT` with `cancellation_reason` null and all four derived counters at zero: `confirmed_enrollment_count`, `paid_enrollment_count`, `waitlist_count` and `net_program_revenue_cents`. Creating a section also increments the parent course's `active_offering_count` by 1 and increments that course's `version` by 1. Of the counters, `confirmed_enrollment_count` is the number of registrations on the section whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED`; `paid_enrollment_count` is the number whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED` **and** whose `payment_status` is either `PAID` or `VOUCHER_COMMITTED`; and `waitlist_count` is the number whose `enrollment_status` is `WAITLISTED`.

Before a section can be published, somebody has to agree to teach it. A `ProgramAdmin` first records the teaching staff as an `Instructor` with an `email`, a `full_name`, and a `credential_level` of `ADJUNCT`, `SENIOR` or `MASTER`; the email is unique across instructors, compared case-insensitively, and a collision is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The program office then offers a teaching contract — an `InstructorContract` — by naming an `offering_id` and an `instructor_id` and setting the commercial terms: a `base_stipend_cents` guaranteed regardless of headcount, a `per_enrollment_cents` rate paid for each counted enrollment, an `enrollment_cap_for_pay` that bounds how many enrollments earn that rate, and optionally a `pay_basis`. All three money and count fields must be zero or positive. `pay_basis` selects which variant of the settlement formula applies and defaults to `CENSUS_LOCKED` when omitted; the alternative is `FINAL_COMPLETION`. A newly offered contract sits in `status` `OFFERED` with `census_headcount`, `final_headcount`, `settled_pay_cents` and `kill_fee_cents` all null. Offering a contract is gated by nothing but the existence of the two identifiers it names: the section may sit in any `status`, and the offer mutates neither the section nor the instructor, so neither of those rows is versioned by it. When both body identifiers fail to resolve, the offering is reported first, as `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_FOUND`.

The named instructor — and only that instructor, since acting on someone else's contract is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — responds with a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`. Declining moves the contract `OFFERED → DECLINED`, a terminal state, and increments the contract's `version` by 1. Accepting moves it `OFFERED → SIGNED`, increments the instructor's `active_contract_count` by 1, and increments both the contract's `version` and the instructor's `version` by 1 each. `active_contract_count` is the number of that instructor's contracts whose `status` is `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE`. Either decision is permitted only while the contract is still `OFFERED` and only while the section it staffs is in `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY` — the section-state gate binds a `DECLINE` exactly as it binds an `ACCEPT` — and otherwise the response is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Acceptance also runs a double-booking check, and the interval arithmetic here is deliberately specific. Consider the instructor's other contracts whose `status` is `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE`. Each of those staffs a section with its own teaching window, and each window is treated as **half-open**: it includes its `start_date` and excludes its `end_date`. If any such window intersects the half-open window of the section being accepted, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Because the windows are half-open, pure adjacency is not a conflict: an instructor may accept a section beginning at exactly the instant another of their sections ends.

Publication is the `ProgramAdmin`'s act of opening a draft section for business, moving it `DRAFT → OPEN` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Four conditions gate it. The section must currently be `DRAFT`. The backing course must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. At least one contract on the section must be in `status` `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE` — a section with no committed instructor cannot be sold. And the logical clock must still be strictly earlier than the section's `start_date`. A failure of the last two conditions, like a failure of the first, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

One later step in the contract lifecycle belongs here for completeness: once a section has cleared its census decision, a `ProgramAdmin` activates each signed contract, moving it `SIGNED → ACTIVE` and incrementing the contract's `version` by 1. Activation requires the contract to be `SIGNED` and requires the section it staffs to be in `status` `CENSUS_LOCKED`; a cross-entity failure on that second condition is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Activation matters beyond bookkeeping, because only an `ACTIVE` contract entitles an instructor to award completions.

### 3.3. Learner Accounts and Marketing Segmentation

Learner signup is deliberately open: an `Anonymous` visitor may create their own account, and a `Registrar` or `ProgramAdmin` may create one on a learner's behalf. The payload carries an `email`, a `full_name`, and optionally `marketing_opt_in`. The email is unique across all learners, compared case-insensitively, so a second signup differing only in letter case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `marketing_opt_in` defaults to true when omitted, and it is a one-time consent flag captured at signup; the learner's live standing is carried separately in `marketing_status`, which is set to `SUBSCRIBED` at creation when `marketing_opt_in` is true and to `UNSUBSCRIBED` otherwise. New learners begin with `lifetime_completed_count` at 0, `lifetime_ceu_total` at 0.0, and `loyalty_tier` at `NEW`.

Consent is revisited over the learner's life. Either the learner themselves or a `Registrar` may toggle the flag by sending a fresh `marketing_opt_in` value; the stored `marketing_opt_in` field itself is left at the value captured at signup and is never rewritten, the learner's `marketing_status` is rewritten to `SUBSCRIBED` or `UNSUBSCRIBED` accordingly, the segmentation recompute described below runs, and the learner's `version` increments by 1. A learner acting on another learner's consent is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The extension school's marketing list is not a flag on the learner — it is a set of **memberships**, one row per segment the learner has ever belonged to. Each `MarketingMembership` names its `learner_id`, its `segment`, its `state`, and the `recomputed_at` instant of the most recent recompute that touched it. `segment` is one of `PROSPECT`, `FIRST_TIME`, `REPEAT`, `CORPORATE_SPONSORED` or `LAPSED`. `state` is `ACTIVE` while the learner currently belongs to that segment and `REMOVED` once a recompute has moved them out; memberships are never deleted, so the membership listing for a learner returns both `ACTIVE` and `REMOVED` rows and the history of a learner's journey through the segments is fully readable.

The recompute itself runs on six occasions: when a learner is created, during seat resolution when a registration is paid, when a voucher is assigned to a learner, as the final hop of the completion-award cascade, when an issued certificate is revoked, and when marketing consent is toggled. It evaluates three independent axes. The **exclusive axis** admits exactly one active segment at a time and is keyed purely on `lifetime_completed_count`: a count of zero puts the learner in `PROSPECT`, a count of exactly one puts them in `FIRST_TIME`, and a count of two or more puts them in `REPEAT`. The **additive axis** is corporate sponsorship, which is considered present when either the learner holds at least one registration whose `voucher_id` is not null and whose `enrollment_status` is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, or at least one voucher has `assigned_learner_id` equal to this learner and a `status` other than `VOIDED`. The **override axis** is consent.

The three axes combine in a fixed way. If the learner's `marketing_status` is `UNSUBSCRIBED`, every one of that learner's memberships moves to `state` `REMOVED` and a single `LAPSED` membership is left `ACTIVE`, created if it does not already exist. Otherwise — that is, when the learner is `SUBSCRIBED` — any `LAPSED` membership moves to `REMOVED`; the membership whose `segment` matches the exclusive result is made `ACTIVE`, created if absent, while every other membership on the exclusive axis moves to `REMOVED`; and the `CORPORATE_SPONSORED` membership is made `ACTIVE` when corporate sponsorship is present and moved to `REMOVED` when it is not. Every membership the run touches has its `recomputed_at` stamped with the current logical-clock instant and its own `version` incremented by 1, and a run touches every row it evaluates — the `LAPSED` row, the three exclusive rows and the `CORPORATE_SPONSORED` row, each whenever it already exists — even where the evaluation leaves that row's `state` unchanged. A membership row is brought into existence only when the run needs it `ACTIVE`; a segment the learner has never belonged to is not materialised merely to record it as `REMOVED`.

### 3.4. Corporate Sponsorship and the Voucher Ledger

Employers fund their staff's continuing education through prepaid vouchers, and the money moves through a four-bucket ledger that must balance at every instant. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a sponsor account by supplying an `org_name` and a `billing_email`; the account starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with all five ledger aggregates — `funded_cents`, `available_cents`, `unallocated_voucher_cents`, `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` — at zero.

Money enters through deposits. Either the `ProgramAdmin` or the sponsor itself posts an `amount_cents` figure, which is added to `funded_cents` and to `available_cents` and increments the account's `version` by 1. The deposit response returns the updated account precisely so that the caller can read the fresh `version` before attempting the version-guarded voucher issue. `funded_cents` is monotonically non-decreasing: it is the running total of every deposit ever posted and nothing ever reduces it.

Issuing a voucher draws value out of `available_cents` and parks it in voucher form. The sponsor (or the program office) supplies a `face_value_cents` strictly greater than zero, an `expires_at` instant, and the `expected_corporate_account_version` described in §2.6. The account must still be `ACTIVE` — issuing against a `SUSPENDED` account is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, tested after the version guard and before the budget — and the account's `available_cents` must be at least `face_value_cents`, or the issue is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402). On success a new voucher appears in `status` `ISSUED` with `assigned_learner_id` null, `remaining_cents` equal to `face_value_cents`, and `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` both zero; the account's `available_cents` decreases by `face_value_cents` while its `unallocated_voucher_cents` increases by the same amount, and the account's `version` increments by 1. The identity of §2.10 is preserved by construction: value has moved from one bucket to another, not appeared or vanished.

A voucher is bound to a person before it can be spent. The owning sponsor names a `learner_id`, and the voucher moves `ISSUED → ASSIGNED` with `assigned_learner_id` set to that learner and its `version` incremented by 1; the assignment also runs the marketing segmentation recompute for the assigned learner, since the corporate-sponsorship axis has just changed. Assignment requires the acting sponsor to own the voucher's `corporate_account_id`, requires the voucher to be `ISSUED`, requires the sponsor account's own `status` to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the logical clock to be strictly earlier than the voucher's `expires_at`. Ownership is tested first and fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; each of the remaining three conditions is tested in the order just given and every one of them fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An unspent voucher can be withdrawn. Voiding is permitted from either `ISSUED` or `ASSIGNED` and only while both `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` are exactly zero — a voucher any part of which has been spent or reserved cannot be voided, and an attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding sets the voucher's `status` to `VOIDED` and its `remaining_cents` to 0, returns `face_value_cents` to the sponsor's `available_cents`, removes the same amount from `unallocated_voucher_cents`, and increments both the voucher's `version` and the account's `version` by 1 each. `VOIDED` is terminal, and voided vouchers drop out of every account aggregate.

The five account aggregates are always derivable, and an implementation may maintain them incrementally only so long as the derived values agree. Over the account's vouchers whose `status` is not `VOIDED`: `available_cents` is `funded_cents` less the sum of `face_value_cents`; `unallocated_voucher_cents` is the sum of `remaining_cents`; `committed_cents` is the sum of the vouchers' own `committed_cents`; and `consumed_cents` is the sum of the vouchers' own `consumed_cents`. All four are integer sums of whole cents and no rounding occurs anywhere in the ledger.

Sponsor accounts can also be put on ice. A `ProgramAdmin` suspends an `ACTIVE` account, moving it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and incrementing its `version` by 1, and reinstates a `SUSPENDED` account, moving it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and again incrementing `version` by 1; the cycle may be repeated indefinitely and the account has no terminal state. While an account is `SUSPENDED`, none of its vouchers may be issued, assigned or redeemed — a redemption attempt is refused with `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_USABLE` — but value already committed against registrations is left entirely untouched.

### 3.5. Registration, Pricing, Payment and Seat Resolution

A **Registration** is the junction between a learner and a section, and it carries two state axes that advance independently of one another on the same row. `enrollment_status` is the seat axis and takes the values `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, `COMPLETED` and `NO_SHOW`. `payment_status` is the money axis and takes the values `UNPAID`, `VOUCHER_COMMITTED`, `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` and `REFUNDED`. A registration may legitimately sit in combinations such as waitlisted-and-paid, enrolled-and-voucher-committed, or enrolled-and-partially-refunded, and implementers must not collapse the two axes into a single status field. On the seat axis, `WITHDRAWN`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, `COMPLETED` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal; on the money axis, `REFUNDED` is terminal.

Either the learner or a `Registrar` opens a registration against a section, supplying the `learner_id` and the `expected_offering_version` guard of §2.6. The registration is created with `enrollment_status` `PENDING` and `payment_status` `UNPAID`, holding no seat whatsoever — a pending registration is a quotation, not a place in the room — with `voucher_id` and `transferred_to_registration_id` both null, `amount_paid_cents` and `amount_refunded_cents` both zero, `sessions_attended` at zero, and `enrolled_at` null. Two snapshots are taken at this moment: `total_sessions` is copied from the section's `session_count` and becomes the fixed denominator of this registration's attendance ratio forever after, and the price is resolved and frozen. Creating a registration also increments the section's `version` by 1, which is exactly why a client must re-read the section between successive creates.

Five business checks guard the create, and they are evaluated in the priority order of §2.8. First, the section must be `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`; a section still in `DRAFT`, already `CENSUS_LOCKED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` yields `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_OPEN`. Second, if the section's course declares a non-null `prerequisite_course_id`, the learner must have satisfied it — either by holding a completion record with `status` `ISSUED` whose originating registration sat on a section of that prerequisite course, or by holding a credit-for-prior-learning petition with `status` `APPROVED` whose `target_course_id` is that prerequisite course; failing both, the answer is `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`. Third comes the concurrent-load ceiling described in the next paragraph. Fourth, the learner must not already hold another registration on this same section whose `enrollment_status` is `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPLETED`; a repeat attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`. Fifth, when the section is currently `WAITLIST_ONLY`, its `waitlist_count` must be strictly below the waitlist cap of 5, or the create is refused with `ERR_OFFERING_FULL`.

The concurrent-load ceiling protects learners from over-committing. Take the learner's existing registrations whose `enrollment_status` is `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, and keep those whose section's teaching window overlaps the new section's window. Here — unlike the instructor schedule check of §3.2 — the windows are treated as **closed** intervals running from `start_date` to `end_date` inclusive at both ends, so two sections that merely touch at an endpoint *do* overlap. Sum the `ceu_units` of the courses behind those overlapping sections, add the `ceu_units` of the course behind the new section, and the total must be no greater than a concurrent ceiling of 4.0 CEU. Exceeding it yields `ERR_LEARNER_LOAD_EXCEEDED`.

Pricing is an eight-step resolution whose evaluation order is pinned, and getting the order wrong changes the answer. Step one sets `list_tuition_cents` to the section's `tuition_cents` — the course's `list_price_cents` plays no part in the arithmetic. Step two reads the learner's `loyalty_tier` **as stored immediately before this pricing call**; the tier is only ever rewritten by the completion-award and revocation recompute, never by registering or paying, so a learner who registers for three sections in a row is priced at the same tier for all three. Step three computes `loyalty_discount_cents` as `list_tuition_cents` multiplied by the tier's discount rate — zero percent for `NEW`, five percent for `RETURNING`, ten percent for `LOYAL`, and fifteen percent for `CHAMPION` — rounded half up to whole cents. Step four subtracts that discount from `list_tuition_cents` to produce a subtotal. Step five selects the processing-fee rate from **that subtotal, never from the final amount due**, by taking the highest of the three fee bands whose floor the subtotal reaches or exceeds: a subtotal of at least 0 cents but below 25,000 cents draws six percent, a subtotal of at least 25,000 cents but below 75,000 cents draws four percent, and a subtotal of at least 75,000 cents draws two and a half percent. Reading the fee band off the final amount due would be circular, since the amount due already contains the fee, and is explicitly forbidden. Step six multiplies the subtotal by the selected rate and rounds half up to whole cents to produce a raw fee. Step seven clamps that raw fee into `processing_fee_cents` by raising it to a floor of 500 cents if it is lower and lowering it to a ceiling of 4,000 cents if it is higher. Step eight sets `total_due_cents` to the subtotal plus `processing_fee_cents`. The resolution consults exactly one section and one learner; it never aggregates across other registrations.

Payment settles the money axis and, immediately afterwards, the seat axis. The learner or a `Registrar` supplies a `method` of either `SELF_PAY` or `VOUCHER`, plus a `voucher_id` when the method is `VOUCHER`; a `voucher_id` sent alongside `SELF_PAY` is ignored entirely and is never resolved, so it can raise no not-found error. Payment requires the registration's `payment_status` to be `UNPAID` and its `enrollment_status` to be `PENDING`, and it requires the section to still be `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`, failing which the answer is `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_OPEN`. A self-paid registration moves `UNPAID → PAID` with `amount_paid_cents` set to `total_due_cents`, and increments the registration's `version` and the section's `version` by 1 each.

Voucher payment is the richer path. Four usability conditions must all hold: the voucher's `status` must be `ASSIGNED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, its `assigned_learner_id` must equal the registration's `learner_id`, the logical clock must be strictly earlier than its `expires_at`, and its sponsor account's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. Any failure yields `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_USABLE` (HTTP 422). Separately, the voucher's `remaining_cents` must be at least the registration's `total_due_cents`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_VOUCHER_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402). On success the registration's `voucher_id` is set from the payload, its `payment_status` moves `UNPAID → VOUCHER_COMMITTED`, and `amount_paid_cents` is set to `total_due_cents`. The voucher's `remaining_cents` decreases by `total_due_cents` while its `committed_cents` increases by the same amount, and its `status` is re-derived: `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` when some `remaining_cents` survives, `EXHAUSTED` when the commitment consumed the last of it. In parallel the sponsor account's `unallocated_voucher_cents` decreases by `total_due_cents` and its `committed_cents` increases by the same amount. The registration's, the voucher's, the sponsor account's and the section's `version` each increment by 1.

Seat resolution runs immediately after either payment path, driven by the section's capacity. If `confirmed_enrollment_count` is strictly below `max_enrollment`, the registration moves `PENDING → ENROLLED`, its `enrolled_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and the section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` each increase by 1. If that increase brings `confirmed_enrollment_count` to exactly `max_enrollment`, the section itself moves `OPEN → WAITLIST_ONLY` and its `version` increments by 1 — the last seat closes the door behind itself. If instead `confirmed_enrollment_count` already equals `max_enrollment` when payment lands, the registration moves `PENDING → WAITLISTED` and the section's `waitlist_count` increases by 1. Either way the marketing segmentation recompute runs for the learner, and the section's `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed.

Waitlists drain by seniority, and the promotion rule is invoked from several places later in this document, so it is stated once here. Whenever a seat is released on a section — by a settled refund, by a settled transfer, or by the cancellation cascade — the system **first** attempts a promotion: among that section's registrations whose `enrollment_status` is `WAITLISTED` and whose `payment_status` is `PAID` or `VOUCHER_COMMITTED`, the one with the smallest `created_at` moves `WAITLISTED → ENROLLED`, has its `enrolled_at` stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and causes the section's `waitlist_count` to decrease by 1 while `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` each increase by 1. Only when no such registration exists does the section fall back from `WAITLIST_ONLY → OPEN`, incrementing its `version` by 1. That fallback closes the cycle between the open and waitlist-only postures, which a section may traverse any number of times.

### 3.6. The Census Fork: Locking or Cancelling a Section

The minimum-enrollment cancellation decision is the single most consequential act in the extension school's calendar, and it is taken once per section by a `ProgramAdmin`. It is permitted only while the section is `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and only once the logical clock has reached or passed the section's `census_at`; taking it earlier is refused with `ERR_CENSUS_TOO_EARLY` (HTTP 409). From there the decision forks on one comparison: whether the section's `paid_enrollment_count` is at least its `min_enrollment`.

When the paid headcount meets or exceeds the minimum, the section runs. It moves `OPEN → CENSUS_LOCKED` or `WAITLIST_ONLY → CENSUS_LOCKED`, and the two starting states produce an identical set of consequences. Every contract on the section whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE` has its `census_headcount` frozen to the section's `paid_enrollment_count` as of this instant — a number that later withdrawals, no-shows and completions will never move. Every registration on the section whose `payment_status` is `VOUCHER_COMMITTED` moves `VOUCHER_COMMITTED → PAID`, and for each one the backing voucher moves exactly `total_due_cents` out of `committed_cents` and into `consumed_cents`, with the voucher's sponsor account making the mirror-image move from its own `committed_cents` into its own `consumed_cents`. Corporate money that was merely reserved becomes irrevocably spent at census. The section's `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed and its `version` increments by 1.

When the paid headcount falls short of `min_enrollment`, the section is cancelled and a four-hop cascade unwinds everything hanging off it. **Hop one**: the section moves `OPEN → CANCELLED` or `WAITLIST_ONLY → CANCELLED`, again identically from either starting state, and its `cancellation_reason` is set to `MIN_ENROLLMENT_NOT_MET`. (The other admissible reason value, `ADMIN_DISCRETION`, exists for cancellations taken outside the census decision; `cancellation_reason` is null for any section that is not `CANCELLED`.) **Hop two**, caused by hop one: every registration on the section whose `enrollment_status` is `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and where its `payment_status` was `PAID`, `VOUCHER_COMMITTED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, that axis moves to `REFUNDED` with `amount_refunded_cents` set to `amount_paid_cents`. This is a **full** refund including `processing_fee_cents` — the cancellation cascade is the only path in the entire system on which the processing fee comes back. As each seat is unwound by this hop the section's own counters follow it: a registration that was `ENROLLED` immediately before this hop decrements the section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` by 1 each, a registration that was `WAITLISTED` decrements its `waitlist_count` by 1, and a registration that was merely `PENDING` held no seat and decrements nothing. **Hop three**, caused by hop two and applying only to registrations whose `voucher_id` is not null: the backing voucher releases the registration's value, decreasing whichever of `committed_cents` or `consumed_cents` held it by `total_due_cents` and increasing `remaining_cents` by the same amount, after which the voucher's `status` is re-derived as described below. **Hop four**, caused by hop three: the voucher's sponsor account recomputes `unallocated_voucher_cents`, `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` so that the conservation identity of §2.10 — `funded_cents` equal to `available_cents` plus `unallocated_voucher_cents` plus `committed_cents` plus `consumed_cents` — still holds exactly.

Alongside that chain, and caused by hop one rather than being part of it, a fan-out runs over teaching staff: every contract on the cancelled section whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED_NO_FAULT`, a terminal state, and has its `kill_fee_cents` computed. Where the terminated contract was `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE`, the instructor's `active_contract_count` also decreases by 1; a contract still merely `OFFERED` was never counted, so nothing is decremented for it. Finally the section's `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed — it necessarily drops to zero, since every line was refunded in full — and the section's `version` increments by 1.

Voucher status re-derivation on release is a small state machine of its own, and it closes the redemption cycle. A voucher that had reached `EXHAUSTED` returns to `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` when the release leaves its `remaining_cents` above zero while its `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` still sum to more than zero. A voucher returns all the way to `ASSIGNED` — from either `EXHAUSTED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` — when the release leaves both `committed_cents` and `consumed_cents` at exactly zero; in the `EXHAUSTED` case this coincides with `remaining_cents` having been restored to the full `face_value_cents`. The same re-derivation runs whenever value returns to a voucher for any reason, whether from this cancellation cascade or from a settled refund.

The section's `net_program_revenue_cents` deserves its own statement because its rounding order is pinned. Take the registrations on the section whose `payment_status` is `PAID` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` **and** whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`; registrations that are `UNPAID`, `VOUCHER_COMMITTED` or `REFUNDED` on the money axis, or `PENDING`, `WAITLISTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `TRANSFERRED_OUT` on the seat axis, are excluded entirely. Order the surviving lines by `created_at` ascending. For each line, take its gross as `amount_paid_cents` less `amount_refunded_cents`; compute institutional overhead as that gross multiplied by an overhead rate of 18.5 percent, **rounded half up to whole cents at this point, per line**; then subtract that overhead and a flat platform fee of 250 cents per registration from the gross to obtain the line's net. `net_program_revenue_cents` is the plain integer sum of those per-line nets, with no further rounding applied to the total. Summing gross across lines and applying the overhead rate once at the end is forbidden and produces a different number whenever a per-line overhead lands on a fractional cent. The figure is recomputed whenever a registration is paid, whenever the census decision is taken, whenever a section is closed out, and whenever a refund or transfer request is settled.

### 3.7. Attendance, Completion and the CEU Certificate

Attendance is recorded as an absolute figure, not as an increment. Either an `Instructor` or a `Registrar` supplies `sessions_attended`, and the registration's `sessions_attended` field is overwritten with exactly that value. The figure must be a non-negative integer but is not bounded above by `total_sessions`, and neither the registration's own two state axes nor the section's `status` gates the write. Sending the same number twice is idempotent and leaves the registration where it was, apart from the `version` increment of 1 that every mutation carries. An `Instructor` may record attendance only on registrations belonging to a section they staff under a contract whose `status` is `SIGNED` or `ACTIVE`; otherwise the answer is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Awarding completion is reserved to the `Instructor`, and more narrowly to an instructor holding a contract on the section whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — a merely `SIGNED` contract is not enough here, and an instructor without an active contract on the section receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Three further conditions gate the award: the registration's `enrollment_status` must be `ENROLLED`, the section's `status` must be `CENSUS_LOCKED`, and the attendance gate must pass. The gate computes `attendance_ratio` as `sessions_attended` divided by `total_sessions`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and requires it to be at least the minimum attendance ratio of 0.80. The comparison is inclusive: a ratio of exactly `0.8000` passes, and only a ratio strictly below it is rejected with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). The CEU credited is then `ceu_awarded`, taken as the section's course's `ceu_units` rounded half up to one decimal place.

A successful award runs a four-hop cascade. **Hop one**: the registration moves `ENROLLED → COMPLETED`. **Hop two**, caused by hop one: a `CompletionRecord` is created with `status` `ISSUED`, its `registration_id` pointing at the registration and its `learner_id` denormalised from that registration so learner-scoped queries need no join, its `ceu_awarded` and `attendance_ratio` as computed above, its `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `certificate_number` minted. **Hop three**, caused by hop two: the learner's `lifetime_completed_count`, `lifetime_ceu_total` and `loyalty_tier` are recomputed. **Hop four**, caused by hop three: the marketing segmentation recompute runs for the learner, so the previously `ACTIVE` membership among `PROSPECT`, `FIRST_TIME` and `REPEAT` moves to `state` `REMOVED` and the newly matching segment gains an `ACTIVE` membership. Crucially, the section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` are **not** decremented by a completion: a completed seat has been consumed rather than released, so no waitlist promotion fires. The registration's, the learner's and the section's `version` each increment by 1.

The certificate identifier is built to a fixed shape: the literal prefix `CEU`, then the section's `term_code`, then a sequence number, the three parts joined by hyphens. For a rolling section, which has no `term_code`, the literal `ROLL` stands in its place. The sequence is the one-based position of this record among that section's completion records ordered by `created_at` ascending, written with leading zeros to exactly four digits — so the first certificate issued on the autumn 2026 delivery reads `CEU-2026FA-0001` and the twelfth on a rolling section reads `CEU-ROLL-0012`.

The learner's lifetime figures are recomputed from scratch rather than nudged, and this matters for the conservation guarantee of §2.10. Take the learner's completion records whose `status` is `ISSUED`; records that have been revoked are excluded from both aggregates. `lifetime_completed_count` is the number of such records. `lifetime_ceu_total` is the sum of their `ceu_awarded`, rounded half up to one decimal place. `loyalty_tier` follows from the count alone, evaluated from the top down: `CHAMPION` at 8 or more completions, otherwise `LOYAL` at 4 or more, otherwise `RETURNING` at 1 or more, otherwise `NEW`. Because the tier is a step function of a recomputed count, a revocation can and does demote a learner.

Revocation is the compensating action, and it belongs to the `Registrar`. It requires the record's `status` to be `ISSUED`, and it is blocked when any citation pointing at this record belongs to a credit-for-prior-learning petition whose `status` is `APPROVED` — the extension school will not pull the ground out from under credit it has already granted — in which case the answer is `ERR_CITATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). On success the record moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`, the originating registration moves `COMPLETED → NO_SHOW` on its seat axis, and the learner's `lifetime_completed_count`, `lifetime_ceu_total` and `loyalty_tier` are recomputed by the same rule as above; because the record has left the issued input set, `lifetime_ceu_total` returns to exactly its pre-award value with no residual drift. The marketing segmentation recompute then runs for the learner. The completion record's, the registration's and the learner's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.8. Section Closeout, Instructor Settlement and Kill Fees

A section is closed out by a `ProgramAdmin` once its teaching has finished. Closeout requires the section to be `CENSUS_LOCKED` and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the section's `end_date`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_ENDED` (HTTP 409). Two sweeps run over the section's registrations. Any registration still sitting at `enrollment_status` `ENROLLED` — enrolled, paid, but never awarded a completion — moves `ENROLLED → NO_SHOW`; no refund is issued for a no-show, the line remains inside the input set of the program-revenue calculation, and the section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` are left exactly where they were, because a no-show seat was consumed rather than released. Any registration still at `PENDING` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with its `payment_status` set to `REFUNDED` and its `amount_refunded_cents` set to `amount_paid_cents` — a learner who never got off the waitlist gets their money back. Each registration this second sweep unwinds from `WAITLISTED` decrements the section's `waitlist_count` by 1, while one unwound from `PENDING` held no seat and decrements nothing, and no waitlist promotion is attempted at closeout. The section then moves `CENSUS_LOCKED → COMPLETED`, a terminal state, its `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed, and its `version` increments by 1.

Instructor pay settles after closeout. A `ProgramAdmin` settles a contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose section has reached `status` `COMPLETED`; a settle attempt against a section that has not completed is a cross-entity failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Settlement first records `final_headcount` as the number of registrations on the section whose `enrollment_status` is `COMPLETED`, evaluated at this instant. It then computes `settled_pay_cents`, moves the contract `ACTIVE → SETTLED` — terminal — decreases the instructor's `active_contract_count` by 1, and increments both the contract's `version` and the instructor's `version` by 1 each.

The pay formula has one shape and two variants, and the variant is chosen by the contract's own `pay_basis`. In both variants, a headcount is capped by taking the smaller of that headcount and `enrollment_cap_for_pay`; the capped figure is multiplied by `per_enrollment_cents` to give the variable component; the variable component is added to `base_stipend_cents` to give a raw total; and the raw total is multiplied by the instructor's credential multiplier — 1.00 for `ADJUNCT`, 1.15 for `SENIOR`, 1.30 for `MASTER` — and rounded half up, once, to whole cents. What differs is the headcount. Under the default `CENSUS_LOCKED` basis, the headcount is the contract's `census_headcount`: the section's `paid_enrollment_count` frozen at the instant the census decision locked the section, unaffected by everything that happened afterwards. Under the `FINAL_COMPLETION` basis, the headcount is the contract's `final_headcount`: the count of completed registrations measured at settle time. For any section where an enrolled learner withdrew, no-showed, or failed the attendance gate, the final-completion headcount is strictly smaller than the census headcount, so the two bases settle materially different amounts from otherwise identical inputs.

A cancelled section pays a different kind of money. When the census fork of §3.6 terminates a contract without fault, `kill_fee_cents` is computed as `base_stipend_cents` multiplied by a kill-fee rate of 25 percent and then by the same credential multiplier — 1.00 for `ADJUNCT`, 1.15 for `SENIOR`, 1.30 for `MASTER` — with a single half-up rounding to whole cents applied to the fully multiplied product. The kill fee deliberately ignores enrollment entirely: `per_enrollment_cents`, `enrollment_cap_for_pay`, `census_headcount` and `final_headcount` play no part in it, because the section the instructor was going to teach never ran.

### 3.9. Unwinding a Registration: Refunds and Transfers

A learner who changes their mind opens a **RefundTransferRequest** against their registration, and either the learner themselves or a `Registrar` may open it. The payload names the `registration_id`, a `request_type` of `REFUND` or `TRANSFER`, optionally a `refund_basis`, and — for a transfer only — a `target_offering_id` naming the destination section. For a transfer, the destination is vetted for compatibility at this moment as well as again at adjudication: it must not be the source registration's own `offering_id`, must be `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`, must sit on a course whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and must sit on a course whose `ceu_units` equals the source course's `ceu_units`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INCOMPATIBLE`. The two fields are mutually constrained: `target_offering_id` must be supplied when `request_type` is `TRANSFER` and must be absent or null when it is `REFUND`, and a violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `refund_basis` selects how the money is valued and defaults to `POLICY_SCHEDULE` when omitted; the alternative is `PRO_RATA_ATTENDANCE`. Requests are opened against a registration whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED`, and they may be opened only while the logical clock is strictly earlier than the source section's `end_date`; once teaching has ended, no refund or transfer may be opened and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).

Creation records two derived values. `days_before_start` is the number of whole days between the logical clock and the source section's `start_date`, obtained by dividing the interval by 86,400 seconds and taking the floor; it may be negative when the section has already begun. `computed_refund_cents` is filled with a provisional valuation — the refund valuation described below when `request_type` is `REFUND`, and exactly 0 when it is `TRANSFER`, whose cash residual cannot be known before settlement — and the request enters `status` `SUBMITTED` with `settled_at` null. `refund_basis` is recorded on a transfer request as well, defaulting to `POLICY_SCHEDULE` in the same way, even though the transfer arm never reads it.

The valuation itself starts from a **refundable base** that deliberately excludes the fee: the registration's `amount_paid_cents` less its `processing_fee_cents`. The processing fee is never returned on this path; only the section-cancellation cascade of §3.6 gives it back. Under the default `POLICY_SCHEDULE` basis, the refundable base is multiplied by a percentage read off the notice period alone, with attendance ignored entirely: 100 percent when `days_before_start` is 14 or more, 75 percent when it is 7 or more but under 14, 50 percent when it is 1 or more but under 7, and 0 percent otherwise — which includes the day the section starts and every day after. Under the `PRO_RATA_ATTENDANCE` basis, the notice period is ignored entirely and the unused portion of the course is valued instead: the unused ratio is `total_sessions` less `sessions_attended`, divided by `total_sessions`; the effective percentage is the smaller of that ratio and a pro-rata ceiling of 90 percent; and the refundable base is multiplied by it. Either way the product is rounded half up to whole cents to give `computed_refund_cents`. The two bases genuinely diverge on identical inputs — a registration one day before the section starts with no sessions attended is valued at 50 percent of the refundable base under the policy schedule and at 90 percent of it under pro-rata attendance.

Adjudication belongs to the `Registrar`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and may also supply a `refund_basis` to override the one recorded at creation; an override supplied on an approval is written back onto the request's own `refund_basis` field, on the transfer arm as well as the refund arm, while omitting it leaves the recorded basis in place. A request must be in `status` `SUBMITTED` to be decided, and the source registration must still be `ENROLLED`; otherwise the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Denial moves the request `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, leaves `computed_refund_cents` at 0, and leaves the registration entirely untouched. Approval forks by `request_type`, and the two arms are genuinely different operations that rejoin only at settlement.

The refund arm moves the request `SUBMITTED → REFUND_APPROVED` and re-values `computed_refund_cents` from scratch under the effective `refund_basis`, using the **current** logical clock and the **current** `sessions_attended` rather than the values captured at creation — so a request approved a week after it was opened is worth less under the policy schedule than it was on the day it was filed. The transfer arm moves the request `SUBMITTED → TRANSFER_APPROVED` and validates the destination instead of the money. The destination section must not be the source registration's own `offering_id`, must be `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`, must sit on a course whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and must sit on a course whose `ceu_units` equals the source course's `ceu_units`; any failure is `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422). The carried credit must also stretch: the registration's `amount_paid_cents` less a flat transfer fee of 2,500 cents must be at least the `total_due_cents` that the pricing resolution of §3.5 produces for this learner on the destination section, or the approval is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402). Either arm increments the request's `version` by 1.

Settlement, also a `Registrar` action, executes the approved arm and moves the request to `SETTLED`, stamping `settled_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Settling a refund moves the source registration `ENROLLED → WITHDRAWN` on the seat axis and adds `computed_refund_cents` to its `amount_refunded_cents`. On the money axis, a registration that was `PAID` moves `PAID → REFUNDED` when `computed_refund_cents` equals `amount_paid_cents` and `PAID → PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` when the refund is above zero but strictly below `amount_paid_cents`, while a refund valued at exactly zero leaves the money axis standing at `PAID` even though the seat axis still moves to `WITHDRAWN`; a registration still sitting at `VOUCHER_COMMITTED` because the section has not yet cleared census moves `VOUCHER_COMMITTED → REFUNDED` with `amount_refunded_cents` set to `amount_paid_cents`, its voucher moving `amount_paid_cents` out of `committed_cents` and back into `remaining_cents`, its voucher `status` re-derived, and its sponsor account moving the same amount out of `committed_cents` and back into `unallocated_voucher_cents`. Where the registration's `voucher_id` is not null the voucher and sponsor account release value exactly as in hops three and four of the cancellation cascade. The source section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` each decrease by 1, the seat-release rule of §3.5 runs — waitlist promotion first, and only failing that the fall back from `WAITLIST_ONLY → OPEN` — and the section's `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed.

Settling a transfer is a two-registration operation. The destination section's `status` is re-checked at this moment and must still be `OPEN` or `WAITLIST_ONLY`, failing which the settle is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than with the incompatibility code used at creation and adjudication. A brand-new registration is then minted on the destination section for the same learner and priced fresh by the eight-step resolution of §3.5 using the learner's **current** `loyalty_tier` — not the tier that priced the source registration — so a learner who has completed a course in the meantime transfers at their improved rate. The new registration's `amount_paid_cents` is set to its own `total_due_cents`, its `payment_status` is set directly to `PAID`, and seat resolution runs on the destination section exactly as it does after an ordinary payment. The destination registration is written exactly once, so it is returned at `version` 1: the price, the paid amount and the seat that settlement hands it are all part of that single creation and bump its counter no further. The source registration then has its `transferred_to_registration_id` set to the new registration's `id`, moves `ENROLLED → TRANSFERRED_OUT` on the seat axis and to `REFUNDED` on the money axis, and receives a cash residual: the source `amount_paid_cents` less the 2,500-cent transfer fee, less the new registration's `total_due_cents`. That residual is written to the request's `computed_refund_cents` and added to the source registration's `amount_refunded_cents`. The source section's `confirmed_enrollment_count` and `paid_enrollment_count` each decrease by 1 and the seat-release rule runs there as well, and `net_program_revenue_cents` is recomputed on **both** sections.

### 3.10. Credit for Prior Learning

The extension school's most academically fraught pathway converts a learner's accumulated non-credit CEUs into academic credit for a specific course. It runs as a petition with a review loop and a hard segregation-of-duties rule, and it is where the `CplCitation` many-to-many junction earns its keep.

Either the learner or a `Registrar` opens a petition by naming a `learner_id` and a `target_course_id`. The petition is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `assessed_by_user_id` null, `claimed_ceu_total` null, `awarded_credit_units` null and `decision_note` null. The learner — and only the learner, this being one of the two operations reserved to that role — then attaches citations. Each citation names a `completion_record_id` and a `weight`, where `weight` is the fraction of that record's `ceu_awarded` the petition is claiming and must be strictly greater than 0, no greater than 1.0, and carried to at most two decimal places. The citation stores its `petition_id` from the petition it was created against, and its representation is that `petition_id` together with `id`, `completion_record_id`, `weight` and `created_at` — carrying neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`, as noted in §2.4 — while creating one increments the owning petition's `version` by 1 rather than its own. Citations may be attached only while the petition itself is in `status` `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; attaching one to a petition in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A cited completion record must have `status` `ISSUED` and must belong to the petitioning learner, or the citation is refused with `ERR_CITATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Submission moves the petition to `SUBMITTED` and is available from `DRAFT` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED` alike — that second edge is what closes the review cycle. The acting `X-User-ID` must be the petition's `learner_id`. At least one citation must exist, and a petition carrying none is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every cited completion record must still be `ISSUED` and still belong to this learner, or the submission fails with `ERR_CITATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The learner must not already hold another petition for the same `target_course_id` in `status` `APPROVED`, or the submission fails with `ERR_CPL_DUPLICATE_AWARD`. And the target course must be `ACTIVE`, or the submission fails with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. These checks are evaluated in the §2.8 priority order rather than in the order narrated here, so a retired target course is reported ahead of an ineligible citation, which is itself reported ahead of a duplicate award. Submission increments the petition's `version` by 1.

A `ProgramAdmin` takes a submitted petition under review, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamping `assessed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer may send it back, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1, re-stamping `assessed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and optionally recording a `decision_note` explaining what needs fixing; a payload that omits `decision_note` leaves whatever note the petition already carried in place rather than clearing it. The revision budget is two rounds: a revision request is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly below 2, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_CPL_REVISION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). The learner then resubmits, and the cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` may run until the budget is spent. Each of these transitions increments the petition's `version` by 1.

Adjudication is a `ProgramAdmin` action taken from `UNDER_REVIEW` with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `decision_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the petition's `assessed_by_user_id` — the person who assessed the petition may not be the person who decides it — and an attempt by the same operator is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both outcomes record the conversion arithmetic: `claimed_ceu_total` and `awarded_credit_units` are computed and stored **even on a denial**, so a denied petition still shows what it would have been worth. `decision_note` is written from the payload unconditionally — overwriting any note a previous revision request left behind, and becoming null when the payload omits it — the petition moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` or `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` — both terminal — and its `version` increments by 1. An approval has a lasting side effect beyond the petition itself: the petition's `target_course_id` thereafter counts as a satisfied prerequisite for that learner in every later prerequisite check, which is the second of the two ways the prerequisite gate of §3.5 can be cleared.

The conversion arithmetic combines four interacting inputs and must be evaluated in the stated order. Take the petition's citations ordered by `created_at` ascending. For each citation, measure the age of the cited record as the interval between the current logical clock and the record's `issued_at`, divided by 365 days of 86,400 seconds each and floored to a whole number of years. A record aged 5 years or more attracts a recency decay factor of 0.5; a younger record attracts a factor of 1.0. The citation's contribution is the record's `ceu_awarded` multiplied by the citation's `weight` and by that factor, rounded half up to two decimal places — **per citation**, before any summing. `claimed_ceu_total` is the sum of those individually rounded contributions, itself rounded half up to two decimal places. Raw credits are then `claimed_ceu_total` divided by a CEU-to-credit divisor of 3.0. A ceiling is imposed by the target course itself: no petition may award more than 75 percent of the target course's own `cpl_credit_units`, so the capped credits are the smaller of the raw credits and that ceiling. Finally `awarded_credit_units` is quantised **downwards** to a half-credit step: divide the capped credits by 0.5, take the floor, and multiply back by 0.5. Flooring is mandatory here — rounding to the nearest half credit is wrong and will award credit the school has not earned. If the resulting `awarded_credit_units` is zero or less and the decision is `APPROVE`, the approval is refused with `ERR_CPL_AWARD_ZERO` (HTTP 422); a denial with a zero award is perfectly legal and simply records the zero.

Because a citation binds a petition to a completion record, the relationship is navigable from both ends: the citations of a petition can be listed from the petition, and the citations referencing a given completion record can be listed from that record — the latter being exactly what the `Registrar` needs before attempting a revocation.
