# Vehicle Sales Incentive Program Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every month an automaker pushes hundreds of millions of dollars of incentive money through its franchised dealer network, and almost none of that money moves as a single lever. Customer cash rebates ride on top of subvented financing; lease support quietly reshapes residual value; stair-step volume objectives pay dealers for units they were going to sell anyway unless the objective is set honestly. The teams that administer these programs today work from spreadsheets that cannot answer the two questions that matter most: *what have we actually committed*, and *what will we still owe if this month's deals hold*. This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets — a single system of record for incentive program design, dealer objective setting, claim adjudication, disbursement, chargeback recovery and budget accrual.

The service is built around a hard separation between **design** and **money**. A program designer composes a program, describes the vehicles and geographies it admits, declares how it may combine with other live programs, and hands it upward for approval; an incentive controller decides whether the automaker will fund it, and later decides which claims are paid and when. A field sales manager owns the dealer stair-step objectives and the protest process dealers use to contest them. A claims auditor stands between the dealer system and the money, validating that a reported retail delivery really happened before any claim built on it can be approved. The dealer system itself is a first-class API consumer: it reports deliveries, submits claims and files protests, and it may never see or touch another dealer's data.

Two engineering constraints shape the whole specification. First, **all money is exact**: amounts are integer cents and rates are integer basis points, so that a reconciliation run months later reproduces the same numbers to the cent. Second, **all time is a logical clock** the test harness controls; the service never reads real wall-clock time, because incentive administration is full of date-sensitive rules — half-open program windows, closed pay periods, back-dated sale detection, month-start retroactive repricing — and those rules must be reproducible on demand. Every endpoint described here is mounted under the `/incentives` prefix, with the single exception of the clock-driving test endpoint.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called (§4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is checked before authorization, and both are checked before anything else about the request.

The five roles are `ProgramDesigner`, `IncentiveController`, `FieldSalesManager`, `ClaimsAuditor` and `Dealer`.

A `ProgramDesigner` composes incentive programs, appends their eligibility footprint rows and stackability edges, submits programs for approval, revises rejected submissions and applies mid-month repricings. A designer never approves anything. An `IncentiveController` owns the money: approving and closing programs, registering dealers and setting dealer standing, running monthly budget accruals, opening and locking and disbursing and cancelling payment batches, resolving chargebacks, and driving the logical clock during testing. A `FieldSalesManager` assigns dealer stair-step objectives from historical volume and market opportunity, resolves dealer protests and finalizes objectives; this role can neither approve programs nor adjudicate claims. A `ClaimsAuditor` validates, rejects and unwinds reported retail deliveries, adjudicates claims and raises chargebacks. A `Dealer` is the dealer's own system: it reports retail deliveries, submits incentive claims and protests assigned objectives.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the dealer role, and the difference is load-bearing.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDesigner`, `IncentiveController`, `FieldSalesManager` or `ClaimsAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a program, as `resolved_by_user_id` on an objective protest, as `validated_by_user_id` on a retail delivery, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `created_by_user_id` on a claim and its chargebacks — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `Dealer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare the resource's dealer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: reporting a retail delivery requires the payload's `dealer_id` to equal `X-User-ID`, submitting a claim requires the referenced delivery's `dealer_id` to equal `X-User-ID`, and filing a protest requires the target objective's `dealer_id` to equal `X-User-ID`. A `Dealer` caller acting on a resource belonging to another dealer is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Two further separations are enforced on top of role membership, both surfacing as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The `X-User-ID` that submitted a program for approval may not be the `X-User-ID` that approves it. And the `ClaimsAuditor` `X-User-ID` recorded in a delivery's `validated_by_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` that adjudicates a claim built on that delivery. These rules are described in full where the workflows live, in §3.2 and §3.6.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific listing states otherwise; the one stated exception is the protest listing of a single dealer objective, which is ordered by `round_index` ascending. For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics; a program id and a claim id are distinguishable only by which collection they belong to. Clients never supply an `id`.

Every persisted resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). All instants accepted in request payloads and all instants emitted in responses use that same representation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical clock reading on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. This applies to `IncentiveProgram`, `Dealer`, `DealerObjective`, `ObjectiveProtest`, `RetailDelivery`, `Claim`, `PaymentBatch`, `Chargeback`, `BudgetAccrual` and `RepricingEvent`.

Two configuration resources are deliberate exceptions. An eligibility footprint row and a stackability edge are append-only configuration: they are written once while their program is still a draft and are never mutated afterwards. Both therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Appending either one does, however, mutate the owning program, so the program's own `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

### 2.5. Money in Cents, Rates in Basis Points, and the Rounding Contract

No amount in this system is ever a decimal. Money is held as an integer number of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. Rates, shares, uplifts and attainment percentages are held as integer basis points, and every field whose name ends in `_bps` is such an integer; one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 10000 basis points is exactly 100.00 percent and 2500 basis points is exactly 25.00 percent. Because both representations are integral, the effective money precision is two decimal places of a currency unit, and no floating-point residue may appear in any stored or returned value.

Rounding is never left to the implementer's discretion, and the direction is not uniform across the system. Where a calculation calls for **half-up rounding**, the fractional part is rounded away from zero at exactly one half, and the rounding is applied once, to the whole expression named, never separately to each factor. Where a calculation calls for **flooring**, the result is truncated toward negative infinity. Where a calculation calls for **ceiling**, the result is raised to the next whole number. The individual rules in §3 state which direction applies at which step, and those steps are pinned: an attainment percentage floors while a tier's unit threshold ceilings, and swapping either one produces a different and incorrect payout.

Signed quantities are legitimate in two places. A repricing event's `net_adjustment_cents` is a signed sum and may be negative when a program's per-unit amount was cut. A program's `accrued_budget_cents` may likewise go negative, because accrual entries are booked net of a cancellation reserve while reversal entries are booked gross; that negative residue is the program's over-accrual correction and must remain visible rather than being clamped to zero.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

The server maintains a logical clock, and every system-stamped instant reads from it: a retail delivery's `reported_at`, a claim's `paid_at`, a repricing event's `effective_at`, a delivery's `unwound_at`, a chargeback's `recovered_at` and a batch's `disbursed_at`. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Before any call to the clock-setting test endpoint, every one of those stamps reads that default value, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. An `IncentiveController` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the test-driving endpoint, which responds with the resulting `current_utc` and mutates no entity and fires no transition.

Interval inclusivity is specified per interval and never generalized, because three different conventions coexist:

- A program's effective window is the **half-open** interval running from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive. A program starting at exactly another program's `effective_to` does not overlap it, and a delivery whose sale instant equals `effective_to` is outside the window.
- An eligibility row's build-date range is the **closed** interval running from `build_date_from` inclusive to `build_date_to` inclusive, and a payment batch's pay period is the **closed** interval running from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` inclusive.
- A retroactive repricing's re-evaluation window is the **half-open** interval running from the containing month's first instant inclusive to the repricing's `effective_at` exclusive.

### 2.7. Request Screening Order: The Validation Ladder

When a single request could fail several checks, the error the client receives is fixed, not arbitrary. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure short-circuits the request; no side effect of any kind is applied to any entity once a check fails.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present, `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Segregation of duties** — on `POST /incentives/programs/{id}/approve` the caller's `X-User-ID` differs from the program's `submitted_by_user_id`, and on `POST /incentives/claims/{id}/adjudicate` the caller's `X-User-ID` differs from the delivery's `validated_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
4. **Dealer ownership** — when the acting role is `Dealer`, `X-User-ID` equals the `dealer_id` of the resource being created or acted on, which means the delivery's `dealer_id` on `POST /incentives/deliveries`, the claimed delivery's `dealer_id` on `POST /incentives/claims`, and the objective's `dealer_id` on `POST /incentives/objectives/{id}/protests`, else `ERR_DEALER_MISMATCH` (403).
5. **Path existence** — every resource named in the URL path exists, resolved strictly left to right: the program before its eligibility rule, the objective before its protest, the batch before the claims it is asked to schedule. A missing resource yields the not-found code of the segment that failed — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Because resolution is left to right, a request naming a nonexistent program and a nonexistent protest under it reports the program failure.
6. **Payload shape** — on every creating or acting endpoint: all required fields present, every enum value a declared member, every integer inside its declared range, every instant a parseable ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits, `effective_to` strictly greater than `effective_from`, `build_date_to` greater than or equal to `build_date_from`, `period_end` greater than or equal to `period_start`, `scheduled_pay_date` greater than or equal to `period_end`, `cap_amount_cents` non-null exactly when `mode` is `CAPPED_TOTAL` and null in every other mode, and `tier_schedule` strictly ascending in both `tier_index` and `threshold_bps`. Any breach yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. **Body foreign keys** — every foreign key carried in the request body resolves to an existing row: the `delivery_id` and `program_id` of a claim, the `dealer_id` of an objective, the `program_b_id` of a stackability edge, every id inside `claim_ids`, and a program's `supersedes_program_id`. A dangling reference yields the matching 404 code (`ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`).
8. **Global uniqueness** — `program_code`, `dealer_code`, `vin` and `batch_code` are each globally unique, and each uniqueness scope includes rows that have reached a terminal state: a `program_code` is taken even by a `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` program, a `vin` even by a `REJECTED` or `UNWOUND` delivery, and a `batch_code` even by a `CANCELLED` batch. Breaches yield `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEALER_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_CODE` (all HTTP 409).
9. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied expected version matches the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_OBJECTIVE_VERSION` (both HTTP 412). See §2.8.
10. **Transition legality** — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the invalid-state code of that entity: `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_OBJECTIVE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PROTEST_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, `ERR_DELIVERY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED`, `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_CHARGEBACK_INVALID_STATE` (all HTTP 409).
11. **Cross-entity state** — the referenced program is `ACTIVE` when a claim is submitted, when a claim is adjudicated, when an objective is created and when a protest is filed, else `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
12. **Program overlap** — the anti-collision check described in §3.2, yielding `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) on program submission and on program approval.
13. **Pay-period overlap** — yielding `ERR_BATCH_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409) when a new batch is opened.
14. **Settlement buffer** — yielding `ERR_BATCH_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409) when a new batch is opened.
15. **Sale date inside the program window** — yielding `ERR_SALE_DATE_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_WINDOW` (422) on claim submission.
16. **Back-dated sale detection** — yielding `ERR_BACK_DATED_SALE_DETECTED` (422) on delivery validation.
17. **Dealer standing** — yielding `ERR_DEALER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` (403) on delivery reporting, delivery validation and claim submission.
18. **Delivery validated** — yielding `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_VALIDATED` (409) on claim adjudication.
19. **Objective finalized** — yielding `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FINAL` (409) on claim adjudication.
20. **Duplicate claim** — yielding `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409) on claim submission.
21. **Program eligibility** — yielding `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PROGRAM` (422) on claim submission.
22. **Forbidden stacking pair** — yielding `ERR_STACK_COMBINATION_FORBIDDEN` (422) on claim submission.
23. **Stack cap headroom** — yielding `ERR_STACK_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422) on claim submission.
24. **Protest limit** — yielding `ERR_PROTEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) when a protest is filed.
25. **Eligibility footprint conflict** — yielding `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_RULE_CONFLICT` (409) when an eligibility row is appended.
26. **Stack edge sanity** — yielding `ERR_INVALID_STACK_EDGE` (409) when a stackability edge is declared.
27. **Repricing window** — yielding `ERR_REPRICING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422) when a repricing is applied.
28. **Accrual period** — yielding `ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_ALREADY_RUN` (409) when a monthly accrual is run.
29. **Chargeback applicability** — yielding `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_APPLICABLE` (409) when a chargeback is raised.
30. **Disbursement timing** — yielding `ERR_PAY_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (422) when a batch is disbursed.
31. **Budget headroom** — yielding `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (402) when a claim is approved.

The ordering has visible consequences worth stating explicitly. Because dealer ownership (rung 4) precedes path existence (rung 5), a `Dealer` caller reaching for another dealer's objective learns only that it is not theirs. Because transition legality (rung 10) precedes the cross-entity program check (rung 11), adjudicating an already-adjudicated claim reports `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED` even when the claimed program has meanwhile left `ACTIVE`. And because budget headroom (rung 31) is the last rung of all, a claim that would exhaust the program's funding is only reported as such after every structural, ownership and eligibility question has been answered.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Programs and Objectives

Three write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because each of them reasons about a number the client read earlier and which another writer may legitimately have moved in the meantime. In all three cases the expected-version field is a **required** input, not an optional one: omitting it is a payload-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

**Submitting a claim.** A request to `POST /incentives/claims` must carry `expected_program_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `IncentiveProgram` named by `program_id`. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no claim row is created. The rule exists because a repricing event may already have replaced the program's `per_unit_amount_cents` — a change that legitimately re-prices every subsequent claim — so a dealer system working from an older read has a stale view of the payable rate and must re-read the program before it may claim against it.

**Applying a repricing.** A request to `POST /incentives/programs/{id}/repricings` must likewise carry `expected_program_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target program. A mismatch with the program's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no repricing event is recorded. Here the reason is bookkeeping integrity: a repricing event records `previous_per_unit_amount_cents` as the value the program carried immediately before the write, and if an earlier repricing has already moved that value, the second event would archive a baseline the program never actually held.

**Resolving a protest.** A request to `POST /incentives/objectives/{id}/protests/{protest_id}/resolve` must carry `expected_objective_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DealerObjective`. A mismatch with the objective's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OBJECTIVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and neither the protest nor the objective is touched. The guard matters because validating a retail delivery moves the objective's `achieved_units`, `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` and increments the objective's `version`; a `FieldSalesManager` about to uphold a protest against a superseded basis is therefore stopped rather than allowed to write units computed from stale attainment.

No other endpoint in this specification takes an expected-version field, and supplying one where it is not defined is simply ignored as an unknown key.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Ledger Conservation

Many operations here touch more than one entity. The rule for all of them is that a cascade is **complete and immediate**: by the time a response is returned, every derived field on every affected entity reflects the operation, every affected entity's `version` has incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` has been refreshed, and no follow-up call is needed to settle the books. There is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this service. When a cascade recomputes a derived aggregate, the aggregate is recomputed from its full defining input set rather than adjusted incrementally, so repeated cascades cannot accumulate drift.

Three conservation invariants govern the money and must hold after every cascade.

**Money conservation.** Consider a claim that is approved, scheduled, paid, then unwound, and whose resulting chargeback is finally recovered. The program's `committed_cents` returns to exactly the value it held before the claim was approved: the charged-back claim leaves the committed input set, and a recovered chargeback contributes nothing. A chargeback that is instead written off deliberately breaks that symmetry — the amount stays inside `committed_cents`, because written-off money is spend the automaker never gets back.

**Unit conservation.** Validating a retail delivery and then unwinding it returns the affected objective's `achieved_units` to exactly its prior value, and therefore returns `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` to their prior values as well.

**Accrual-exposure conservation.** Every chargeback created by a cascade appends exactly one accrual-ledger row of type `REVERSAL` whose `reversal_cents` equals that chargeback's `amount_cents` — never zero rows, never two rows, and never a different amount. A cascade cannot run twice against the same claim, because the first pass moves the claim out of the payment states from which a chargeback is possible.

The reader should also note the two operations that look like chargeback cascades and are not. A protest upheld against an already-paid objective, and a downward retroactive repricing landing on a claim inside a locked batch, both raise a chargeback that recovers only *part* of what was paid, reduce the claim's `approved_amount_cents`, and deliberately leave the claim's payment state exactly where it was. Their mechanics live in §3.3 and §3.9 respectively.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing a Program: Money Levers, Eligibility Footprint and Stacking Edges

A `ProgramDesigner` begins by creating an incentive program, which is born in `DRAFT`. The creation payload names the program with a `program_code` of 1 to 32 characters and a human-readable `name`, and it declares exactly one `incentive_type` chosen from `CUSTOMER_CASH`, `FINANCE_SUBVENTION`, `LEASE_SUPPORT` and `DEALER_STAIR_STEP` — the lever this program pulls. The program's effective window is given as `effective_from` and `effective_to`, where `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`. `per_unit_amount_cents` carries the cash value in cents the program pays for each eligible retail delivery, and must be zero or greater; for a stair-step program the same field is the base per-unit bonus that sits underneath the tier schedule. `budget_cents` is the total funded budget in cents and must be zero or greater. `stack_group` is a reporting label only — stackability decisions are made exclusively from the stacking edges described later in this subsection and never from this label. `priority_rank` is the waterfall order used when several programs stack on one delivery: it must be at least 1, a lower rank is applied to the deal first, and ties on rank are broken by `created_at` ascending.

Three levers are consulted only by particular incentive types, and each defaults to 0 when the designer omits it. `apr_buydown_bps` states how many basis points of APR the automaker buys down and may not exceed 2000 basis points; it is read only when the program's `incentive_type` is `FINANCE_SUBVENTION`. `residual_support_bps` states how many basis points of residual value the automaker supports and may not exceed 1500 basis points; it is read only when `incentive_type` is `LEASE_SUPPORT`. `projected_take_rate_bps` states the expected share of eligible volume that will take the incentive, expressed in basis points and capped at 10000 basis points, and it is read only by the projection-based accrual variant of §3.9.

Stair-step programs additionally carry a `tier_schedule`, which defaults to an empty list. Each element of the schedule is an object with three keys: `tier_index`, the ordinal of the tier; `threshold_bps`, the attainment expressed in basis points of the dealer's objective at which the tier begins, so that 10000 means the dealer has hit exactly 100.00 percent of the objective; and `per_unit_bonus_cents`, the bonus in cents this tier pays per unit. The schedule must be strictly ascending in `tier_index` and, at the same time, strictly ascending in `threshold_bps`; a schedule that violates either ordering is rejected as a malformed payload. `tier_bonus_mode` selects between the two payout variants and defaults to `MARGINAL`, the alternative being `FULL_VOLUME`; §3.7 explains what each one pays. The schedule is ignored entirely unless `incentive_type` is `DEALER_STAIR_STEP`.

Two further optional inputs round out the creation payload. `accrual_method` selects the monthly accrual variant, defaulting to `UNIT_BASED` with `TAKE_RATE_BASED` as the alternative. `stack_cap_cents` defaults to 0 and expresses a program-level ceiling, in cents, on the total stacked incentive of any single delivery that consumes this program, where the value 0 means this program imposes no ceiling of its own. And `supersedes_program_id` may name an earlier program this one replaces; it defaults to null, and when it is supplied the referenced program must exist. On creation the program's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `version` is 1, and every derived money and unit field is initialized to 0: `committed_cents`, `paid_cents`, `accrued_budget_cents`, `claimed_units`, `per_unit_spend_cents` and `residual_impact_bps`. The audit fields `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` both begin as null.

While the program is still a draft, its designer appends **eligibility footprint** rows. Each row is append-only configuration owned by the program named in the path, which is what the row's `program_id` records, and it describes one admissible slice of the automaker's line-up. `model_code`, 1 to 16 characters, names the vehicle model the row admits by exact match with no wildcard. `trim_code` is optional and defaults to null, and a null trim admits every trim of that model. `region_code`, 1 to 8 characters, names the customer geography the row admits, again by exact match with no wildcard. `build_date_from` and `build_date_to` bound the factory build instants admitted, with `build_date_to` required to be at or after `build_date_from`. `min_msrp_cents` defaults to 0 and is an inclusive sticker-price floor in cents where 0 means no floor; `max_msrp_cents` defaults to null and is an inclusive ceiling where null means no ceiling. `allowed_deal_types` is an array of members drawn from `CASH`, `FINANCE` and `LEASE` and defaults to all three.

Taken together, those seven attributes form the **admission predicate**, the single test that decides whether a footprint row admits a particular retail delivery. A row admits a delivery when all of the following hold at once: the row's `model_code` equals the delivery's `model_code`; the row's `trim_code` is either null or equal to the delivery's `trim_code`; the row's `region_code` equals the delivery's `customer_region_code`; the delivery's `build_date` falls at or after `build_date_from` and at or before `build_date_to`; the delivery's `msrp_cents` is at least `min_msrp_cents`; `max_msrp_cents` is either null or at least the delivery's `msrp_cents`; and the delivery's `deal_type` is a member of `allowed_deal_types`. This predicate is used verbatim by claim eligibility (§3.6), by objective attainment (§3.7) and by monthly accrual (§3.9); wherever those sections say a row *admits* a delivery, this is the test being applied.

Footprint rows may not silently duplicate one another. If the row being appended matches an existing row of the **same** program on all three of `model_code`, `trim_code` and `region_code`, and its closed build-date interval intersects that existing row's closed build-date interval, the append is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_RULE_CONFLICT`. Rows of *different* programs never conflict with each other; overlap between programs is a separate concern handled at the approval gate.

The last piece of draft configuration is the **stacking edge**. A designer declares an edge between the program named in the path, recorded on the edge as `program_a_id`, and another program named in the payload as `program_b_id`. The edge is undirected — at most one row may ever join a given unordered pair, and the listing of a program's edges returns every edge touching it in either direction. `mode` says how the pair may combine on a single delivery and is one of `FORBIDDEN`, `CAPPED_TOTAL` or `ALLOWED`. `cap_amount_cents` carries the combined ceiling in cents for the pair and is required exactly when `mode` is `CAPPED_TOTAL`; in the other two modes it must be null. Declaring an edge whose `program_b_id` equals the path program, or declaring a second edge over a pair that an edge already joins, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STACK_EDGE`. Appending either a footprint row or a stacking edge increments the owning program's `version` by 1.

### 3.2. The Approval Gate: Submission, Revision, Approval, Supersession and Closure

Submitting a draft for approval moves the program `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL` and is the `ProgramDesigner`'s hand-off of the design. The submission is admitted only when the program is in `DRAFT`, when the program has at least one eligibility footprint row, and when `effective_to` is strictly later than `effective_from`. Two type-specific gates apply: a program whose `incentive_type` is `DEALER_STAIR_STEP` must have at least one element in its `tier_schedule`, and a program whose `incentive_type` is `LEASE_SUPPORT` must have a `residual_support_bps` strictly greater than 0. Every one of these admission conditions is a statement about the program's own stored state rather than about a request payload — the submit call carries none of these values — so an unmet one is a transition-legality failure reported as `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE` (409) and never as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: that holds for a submission attempted from any status other than `DRAFT`, for a program carrying no eligibility footprint row, and for either type-specific gate alike. On success the caller's `X-User-ID` is written to `submitted_by_user_id` and the program's `version` increments by 1.

Submission also runs the **program-overlap check**, and so does approval. The check looks for another program that is in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE`, carries the same `incentive_type`, has an effective window that intersects this program's half-open window, and has an eligibility footprint that intersects this program's footprint — meaning some pair of footprint rows shares a `model_code`, has overlapping trim scope, shares a `region_code`, and has intersecting closed build-date intervals. Trim scope overlaps when either row's `trim_code` is null, or when both are non-null and equal. If such a program is found, the operation is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — unless a stacking edge whose `mode` is `ALLOWED` or `CAPPED_TOTAL` joins the two programs, which is the designer's explicit statement that the collision is intentional and priced. Window intersection follows the half-open convention of §2.6, so a program starting at exactly another program's `effective_to` does not collide with it. When approval re-runs this check, any program that the program under approval supersedes is excluded from consideration, since that program is about to leave `ACTIVE` anyway.

Not every submission survives review. Sending a pending program back for rework moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL → DRAFT`, optionally recording a `revision_note`, and is available only to a `ProgramDesigner` and only from `PENDING_APPROVAL`. The revision clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, so that the next submission re-establishes the segregation-of-duties baseline from scratch, and increments `version` by 1. This is the system's one genuine design cycle: a program may be submitted, revised and re-submitted without limit, and each re-submission re-runs the overlap check against the world as it stands at that moment rather than against the world at first submission.

Approval moves the program `PENDING_APPROVAL → ACTIVE` and belongs exclusively to the `IncentiveController`. The program must be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`; approval from any other status is `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the program's recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, and an approver who is the submitter is stopped with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. If the program carries a non-null `supersedes_program_id`, the referenced program must exist and must currently be `ACTIVE`. On success the approver's `X-User-ID` is written to `approved_by_user_id` and the program's `version` increments by 1.

Approving a successor program sets off a three-link cascade on the program it replaces. The first link drives the referenced program `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. The second link, caused by the first rather than by the approval itself, walks every claim of the superseded program whose `validation_status` is `SUBMITTED` and drives each one to `validation_status` `REJECTED`, recording `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE` in its `rejection_error_id`. The third link, caused by the second, follows from those rejections: each rejected claim drops out of the committed input set, so the superseded program's `committed_cents`, `claimed_units` and `per_unit_spend_cents` are all recomputed from their defining input sets as described in §3.7 and §3.9. The superseded program's own `version` also increments by 1. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `CLOSED` are terminal program states, and no transition leads out of either one.

Closing a program moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and is likewise the `IncentiveController`'s decision, optionally accompanied by a `closure_note`. Closure is admitted only from `ACTIVE`, and only when no claim of the program has `payment_status` `SCHEDULED` — money already queued for disbursement must settle before the program's books are shut. Closure runs a conditional cascade over the program's dealer objectives: every objective whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `PROTESTED` or `REVISED` transitions to `VOID`, while objectives already in `FINAL` or `VOID` are left untouched. Each objective that is voided has its `payout_cents` set to 0 and drives every one of its protests whose `status` is `OPEN` to `DENIED` with `resolved_units` left null, and each voided objective's `version` increments by 1. The closing program's own `version` increments by 1.

### 3.3. Dealer Registration, Standing, and Stair-Step Objective Proposal

Dealers are registered by the `IncentiveController`. The registration payload carries a `dealer_code` of 1 to 16 characters that is unique across all dealers, the dealer's legal `name`, and the `region_code` of the geography the dealer belongs to — a value that later decides which regional payment batch may pay this dealer. Registration also captures the two numbers the objective-setting process runs on: `historical_volume_units`, the dealer's trailing-twelve-month retail volume, which must be zero or greater; and `market_opportunity_bps`, the market opportunity uplift over that history expressed in basis points and capped at 20000 basis points, so that a value of 2500 means the field expects this dealer to sell 25.00 percent more than last year's run rate. A newly registered dealer has `status` `ACTIVE`, `paid_cents` of 0 and `open_chargeback_cents` of 0.

Dealer standing is a three-valued lever — `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` — that the controller sets by supplying a `status` and, optionally, an explanatory `note`; the change increments the dealer's `version` by 1. The two non-active values are not interchangeable, and the difference is one of the sharper rules in the system. A `TERMINATED` dealer is cut off at the top of the funnel: it may not report retail deliveries, none of its already-reported deliveries may be validated, and it may not submit claims — all three attempts are refused with `ERR_DEALER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. A `SUSPENDED` dealer, by contrast, is **not** blocked by that rule at all: it keeps reporting deliveries, its deliveries keep being validated, and its claims keep being submitted and adjudicated normally. Suspension bites only at the moment money would leave the building, which is disbursement (§3.8).

A `FieldSalesManager` assigns a stair-step objective by naming a dealer under a program. The payload carries the `dealer_id` of the dealer the objective binds, plus an optional `stretch_bps` — an extra stretch in basis points laid on top of the dealer's market opportunity, capped at 10000 basis points and defaulting to 0. The owning program is the one named in the path, which the objective records as `program_id`, and that program must be `ACTIVE`; assigning an objective under a program in any other status is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The assigned objective is computed, not supplied. First, `basis_units` is set to the dealer's `historical_volume_units` — this is the volume basis the objective actually rests on, and the only field that a later upheld protest rewrites. Second, the total uplift in basis points is the smaller of two quantities: the sum of the dealer's `market_opportunity_bps` and the objective's `stretch_bps`, and a system ceiling of 5000 basis points, that is 50.00 percent; the ceiling means no objective may ever demand more than one and a half times the dealer's basis volume however aggressively the field stretches it. Third, the raw unit target is `basis_units` multiplied by the quantity ten thousand plus that capped uplift, divided by ten thousand. Fourth, `objective_units` is the raw unit target rounded half-up to a whole number, and then raised to 1 if that rounding produced anything smaller — the floor of one unit guarantees that attainment can always be divided by the objective without a division error. The rounding happens exactly once, after the uplift ceiling has been applied and before the one-unit floor has been applied. No other dealer's data participates in any part of this computation.

The freshly proposed objective has `status` `PROPOSED`, `protest_round` of 0 and `paid_payout_cents` of 0. It is not, however, born at zero attainment: creation immediately computes `achieved_units` from the dealer's deliveries that are already `VALIDATED`, and derives `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` from that count by the tier arithmetic of §3.7. A field manager who sets objectives mid-month therefore sees the dealer's month-to-date position on the objective the moment it exists.

### 3.4. The Protest Cycle: Filing, Resolution and Finalization

Objectives are contestable, and the contest is a genuine loop rather than a one-shot appeal. A `Dealer` files a protest against its own objective — the objective's `dealer_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID` — asserting a `claimed_basis_units` figure it believes should have been used in place of its `historical_volume_units`, together with a free-text `reason` of 1 to 500 characters. The protest is admitted only when the objective's `status` is `PROPOSED`, `REVISED` or `FINAL`; filing against an objective already in `PROTESTED`, or against one that has been voided, is refused with `ERR_OBJECTIVE_INVALID_STATE`. The owning program must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the filing is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the objective's `protest_round` must be strictly less than two, the hard cap on protest rounds; a third filing against an objective whose `protest_round` already stands at 2 is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Filing creates a protest row with `status` `OPEN`, an `objective_id` naming the objective from the path, and a `round_index` equal to the objective's `protest_round` plus one — so the first protest is round 1 and the second is round 2. The objective's `protest_round` increments by 1, its own `version` increments by 1, and it moves into `PROTESTED` from whichever of the three admissible states it held: `PROPOSED → PROTESTED` for a first-round protest of a fresh objective, `REVISED → PROTESTED` for a second-round protest of an objective already once revised, and `FINAL → PROTESTED` when a dealer contests an objective that had been frozen. That last edge is the reason `FINAL` is a freeze rather than a grave, and it is the only path by which an objective can be recomputed while `paid_payout_cents` is non-zero — which in turn makes the `OBJECTIVE_REVISED` flavour of chargeback reachable at all. A finalized objective that has been protested must be finalized again afterwards before stair-step bonuses can resume.

Resolution belongs to the `FieldSalesManager`, who supplies a `resolution` of either `UPHELD` or `DENIED` along with the `expected_objective_version` guard of §2.8. The named protest must belong to the objective named in the path — otherwise `ERR_PROTEST_NOT_FOUND` — and must still be `OPEN`; resolving an already-resolved protest is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. The parent objective must be in `PROTESTED`. Whichever way it goes, the resolution records `resolved_by_user_id` as the resolving manager's `X-User-ID` and increments both the protest's `version` and the objective's `version` by 1. What differs between the two branches is everything else, and the asymmetry is deliberate.

**Upholding** the protest moves the protest `OPEN → UPHELD` and the objective `PROTESTED → REVISED`, and runs a four-link cascade. Link one accepts the dealer's figure: the objective's `basis_units` is overwritten with the protest's `claimed_basis_units` and `objective_units` is recomputed from that new basis by the same four-step proposal arithmetic of §3.3 — capped uplift, raw target, half-up rounding, one-unit floor — with the recomputed value also recorded on the protest as `resolved_units`. Link two follows from link one: because the denominator moved, the objective's `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` are all recomputed by the tier arithmetic of §3.7. Link three follows from link two and only fires when the objective has already been paid more than it now earns: if `paid_payout_cents` is strictly greater than the newly computed `payout_cents`, a chargeback is raised against the objective's stair-step bonus claim with `reason` `OBJECTIVE_REVISED` and an `amount_cents` equal to `paid_payout_cents` minus the new `payout_cents`, entering in `status` `PENDING`; that claim's `approved_amount_cents` and the objective's `paid_payout_cents` are **both** reduced to the new `payout_cents`, and the claim deliberately **keeps** its `payment_status` of `PAID`, because this is a partial recovery of an overpayment rather than a full reversal of a payment. Link four follows from link three: the reduced `approved_amount_cents` forces recomputation of the program's `committed_cents`, `paid_cents` and `per_unit_spend_cents`, and appends one accrual-ledger row with `entry_type` `REVERSAL` whose `reversal_cents` equals the chargeback's `amount_cents`, which in turn recomputes the program's `accrued_budget_cents`.

**Denying** the protest moves the protest `OPEN → DENIED`, leaves `resolved_units` null, and runs no cascade whatsoever — the objective's `basis_units`, `objective_units` and `payout_cents` are left byte-identical to their pre-protest values, and no chargeback, accrual row or ledger recomputation occurs. Where the denied objective lands is pinned by the protest's `round_index` and by nothing else — not by wall-clock ordering, not by identifier ordering. Denying a round 1 protest returns the objective `PROTESTED → PROPOSED`, the state it held before that protest was filed. Denying a round 2 protest returns the objective `PROTESTED → REVISED`, which is likewise the state it held before round 2 was filed. Note that `protest_round` is **not** decremented by a denial: a dealer whose first protest was denied still has exactly one round left, and a dealer whose second was denied has none.

Finalizing an objective freezes `objective_units` so that stair-step bonus claims can be adjudicated against a stable denominator. A `FieldSalesManager` may finalize an objective whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `REVISED`, giving the edges `PROPOSED → FINAL` and `REVISED → FINAL`; re-finalizing after a survived protest round is the normal path back into `FINAL`. Finalizing from any other status is refused with `ERR_OBJECTIVE_INVALID_STATE`, and finalization additionally requires that no protest of this objective is still `OPEN`. Finalization increments the objective's `version` by 1. `FINAL` is not terminal: as long as `protest_round` is still below two, a further protest un-freezes the objective. The only terminal state of an objective is `VOID`, reached solely through the closure cascade of §3.2.

### 3.5. Retail Delivery Reporting, Back-Dated Sale Detection and Auditor Validation

Nothing in this system is claimable until a retail delivery has been reported and then vouched for. The dealer system reports a delivery for itself: the payload's `dealer_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a dealer whose `status` is `TERMINATED` may not report at all. Each delivery carries a `vin` of exactly 17 characters that is globally unique across every retail delivery ever recorded — including deliveries that were later rejected and deliveries that were later unwound, so a dealer cannot recycle a VIN after a deal falls apart. The vehicle is described by `model_code`, `trim_code`, `build_date` and `msrp_cents`, where the sticker price must be strictly greater than 0. The deal is described by `sale_date`, the retail delivery instant the dealer asserts; `customer_region_code`, the customer's geography; and `deal_type`, one of `CASH`, `FINANCE` or `LEASE`. Three financing attributes default to 0 and are conditionally required: `term_months` must be strictly greater than 0 when `deal_type` is `FINANCE` or `LEASE`; `contract_apr_bps` carries the customer's contract APR in basis points and is read by the finance-subvention leg of the payable amount; and `base_residual_bps`, the vehicle's unsupported residual expressed in basis points of MSRP, must be strictly greater than 0 when `deal_type` is `LEASE`.

At the moment the delivery is recorded, the server stamps `reported_at` from the logical clock — never from real wall-clock time — sets `status` to `REPORTED`, and runs the **back-dated sale detector** immediately. `reporting_lag_days` is the whole number of days between the sale and the report: the elapsed seconds from `sale_date` to `reported_at` divided by 86400 seconds and floored toward negative infinity. The value is therefore negative whenever a dealer reports a future-dated sale. `back_dated_flag` is set to true when either of two conditions holds: `reporting_lag_days` is **strictly greater** than ten days, or `sale_date` is strictly later than `reported_at`. The strictness matters — a lag of exactly ten days is clean and leaves the flag false. Both derived values are computed once at reporting time and are never recomputed afterwards, so moving the logical clock later does not retroactively taint a delivery that was clean when it arrived. Deliveries can be listed with filters on `dealer_id`, on `status` and on `back_dated_flag`, the last of which is how an auditor finds the suspicious reports.

A `ClaimsAuditor` then vouches for the delivery, moving it `REPORTED → VALIDATED`. The delivery must be in `REPORTED`; validating a delivery in any other status is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_INVALID_STATE`. Its `back_dated_flag` must be false, and a flagged delivery is refused with `ERR_BACK_DATED_SALE_DETECTED`. The reporting dealer must not be `TERMINATED`, and a terminated dealer's delivery is refused with `ERR_DEALER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` even though the delivery itself was reported while the dealer was still in good standing. On success the auditor's `X-User-ID` is written to `validated_by_user_id`, and that recorded value becomes the anchor of the second segregation-of-duties rule: the same auditor id may not later adjudicate a claim built on this delivery.

Validation is also the event that credits dealer stair-step progress. Every dealer objective whose owning program's eligibility footprint admits this delivery and whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `PROTESTED`, `REVISED` or `FINAL` has its `achieved_units` incremented and its `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` recomputed, and each such objective's `version` increments by 1 — which is precisely how a `FieldSalesManager` holding an earlier read of the objective is caught as stale when they try to resolve a protest. `achieved_units` is not maintained by counting increments; it is recomputed as the number of retail deliveries whose `dealer_id` equals the objective's `dealer_id`, whose `status` is `VALIDATED`, whose `sale_date` falls at or after the owning program's `effective_from` and strictly before its `effective_to`, and which at least one eligibility footprint row of that program admits by the predicate of §3.1. Deliveries in `REPORTED`, `REJECTED` or `UNWOUND` never count, and the count is insensitive to the order in which the deliveries were reported. The delivery's own `version` increments by 1.

An auditor who does not believe the report rejects it instead, moving the delivery `REPORTED → REJECTED` and recording a `reason`. Rejection is a pure state change: no objective field and no program ledger field moves, and only the delivery's `version` increments by 1. `REJECTED` is terminal, as is `UNWOUND`; the only non-terminal path out of `VALIDATED` is the unwind of §3.9.

### 3.6. Claim Submission and the Stacking Waterfall

The dealer system submits one claim per program per delivery. The payload names the `delivery_id` being claimed and the `program_id` being claimed against, states the `claimed_amount_cents` the dealer's own system computed (which must be zero or greater and is recorded verbatim so that it can be compared later against what the automaker actually approves), and carries the `expected_program_version` guard of §2.8. The claimed delivery's `dealer_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a dealer whose `status` is `TERMINATED` may not claim.

Submission is screened in a fixed order, and the order is observable. First the referenced delivery and program must exist. Then the program version guard must match. Then the program must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the submission is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Then the delivery's `sale_date` must fall at or after the program's `effective_from` and strictly before its `effective_to`, failing which the submission is refused with `ERR_SALE_DATE_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_WINDOW`. Then the dealer's standing is checked. Then duplication: no other claim on the same pairing of `delivery_id` and `program_id` may exist whose `validation_status` is anything other than `REJECTED` and whose `payment_status` is anything other than `CHARGED_BACK` — a second live claim on the same pairing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, while a pairing whose earlier claim was rejected or charged back is free to be claimed again. Only then do the three stacking questions get asked, in the order eligibility, then forbidden pairing, then cap headroom.

Those three questions are answered by one shared piece of machinery, the **stacking waterfall**, which also backs a read-only projection an auditor, controller or dealer can request for any delivery. The waterfall first assembles the delivery's **candidate set**: every program whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose effective window contains the delivery's `sale_date` under the half-open convention, and at least one of whose eligibility footprint rows admits the delivery by the §3.1 predicate. Candidates are then ranked by `priority_rank` ascending and, where ranks tie, by `created_at` ascending; the programs' identifiers are never consulted as a tie-break. If the claimed program is not in the candidate set — because it is not `ACTIVE`, or because its window does not contain the sale instant, or because no footprint row admits the delivery's model, trim, region, build date, MSRP or deal type — the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PROGRAM`, and the read-only projection returns an empty `eligible_program_ids` list.

The waterfall then walks the ranked candidates against what the delivery has already consumed. The **accepted set** is the set of programs behind this delivery's existing claims whose `validation_status` is not `REJECTED` and whose `payment_status` is not `CHARGED_BACK`. The **running total** is the sum, over those accepted claims, of `approved_amount_cents`, except that a claim not yet adjudicated contributes its gross payable amount as computed by the arithmetic of §3.7. For each candidate in rank order, a candidate already in the accepted set is skipped. Then, for every program in the accepted set, the stacking edge joining the unordered pair — of which at most one exists — is consulted: if such an edge exists and its `mode` is `FORBIDDEN`, the candidate is **blocked** and the walk moves on to the next candidate, and a claim requesting a blocked program is refused with `ERR_STACK_COMBINATION_FORBIDDEN`.

Surviving candidates face three independent ceilings, and the binding one is the smallest. The **pair ceiling** is the smallest `cap_amount_cents` among the edges joining this candidate to an accepted program whose `mode` is `CAPPED_TOTAL`; where no such edge exists there is no pair ceiling. The **own ceiling** is the candidate's `stack_cap_cents` when that value is strictly greater than 0, and no ceiling when it is 0. The **partner ceiling** is the smallest `stack_cap_cents` among the accepted programs whose `stack_cap_cents` is strictly greater than 0, and no ceiling when none of them sets one. The candidate's headroom is the smallest of whichever of those three ceilings exist. The candidate's own gross payable amount for this delivery is then computed by the §3.7 arithmetic, and if the running total plus that gross **strictly exceeds** the headroom the candidate is **capped out**, the walk moves to the next candidate, and a claim requesting a capped-out program is refused with `ERR_STACK_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A stacked total landing exactly on the headroom is accepted, because the comparison is strict. A candidate that clears the ceilings is accepted and its gross is added to the running total, which is what makes the walk a waterfall rather than a set of independent tests. Note that ceilings are enforced as refusals and never as silent trims: an over-ceiling claim is rejected, not quietly reduced.

The read-only projection over one delivery returns that whole picture at once: the `delivery_id` it was asked about, `eligible_program_ids` for the candidates the walk accepted, `blocked_program_ids` for those a `FORBIDDEN` edge excluded, `capped_out_program_ids` for those a ceiling excluded, and `stacked_total_cents` for the running total the walk arrived at.

A claim that survives every check is created with several fields the dealer does not supply. `snapshot_per_unit_cents` freezes the program's `per_unit_amount_cents` exactly as it stands at submission, which is what protects an already-submitted claim from a later prospective repricing. `claim_type` is derived from the program's `incentive_type`: it is `UNIT_INCENTIVE` when the program's type is `CUSTOMER_CASH`, `FINANCE_SUBVENTION` or `LEASE_SUPPORT`, and `STAIR_STEP_BONUS` when the type is `DEALER_STAIR_STEP`. `dealer_id` is copied from the delivery at creation and is never re-derived afterwards, even if the delivery is later touched. The claim's two independent state dimensions begin at `validation_status` `SUBMITTED` and `payment_status` `UNPAID`, its `payment_batch_id` is null, its `rejection_error_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `paid_at` are all null, its `approved_amount_cents` starts at 0 rather than null, because a claim that has not yet been adjudicated has no approved money, and the claimed program's `version` increments by 1. A stair-step bonus claim still names a `delivery_id`, which for that claim type points at the dealer's most recent `VALIDATED` delivery credited to the objective; a claim of type `UNIT_INCENTIVE` may never have a null `delivery_id`. Claims may be listed with filters on `program_id`, `dealer_id`, `delivery_id`, `payment_batch_id`, `validation_status` and `payment_status`.

### 3.7. Adjudication: The Payable Amount, Attainment Tiers and Budget Headroom

Adjudication is where the automaker decides what it will actually pay. A `ClaimsAuditor` supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and on a rejection may supply the `rejection_error_id` to be recorded against the claim. The claim's `validation_status` must be `SUBMITTED`; adjudicating a claim that has already been decided is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. Approval further requires that the claim's delivery is in `VALIDATED` — a delivery still in `REPORTED`, or one rejected or unwound, is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_VALIDATED` — and that the claimed program is still `ACTIVE`, failing which the adjudication is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from the `validated_by_user_id` recorded on that delivery; the auditor who vouched for the delivery may not also approve money against it, and an attempt is stopped with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For a claim whose `claim_type` is `STAIR_STEP_BONUS`, a dealer objective matching the claim's `program_id` and `dealer_id` must exist and must be in `FINAL`; a missing objective, or one in any other status, is refused with `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FINAL`. Either decision writes the auditor's `X-User-ID` to `adjudicated_by_user_id`.

Rejection moves the claim `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` on the validation dimension, sets `approved_amount_cents` to 0, records the supplied or system-derived error identifier in `rejection_error_id`, and leaves the program's `committed_cents` exactly where it was — a claim that was only ever `SUBMITTED` had never been committed in the first place. Only the claim's `version` increments by 1.

Approval moves the claim `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED` on the validation dimension and computes `approved_amount_cents` and `rate_basis` in three pinned steps.

**Step one picks the per-unit rate.** If there exists a repricing event on the claimed program whose `mode` is `RETROACTIVE_TO_MONTH_START` and whose window — from its `month_start` inclusive to its `effective_at` exclusive — contains the delivery's `sale_date`, then the rate is the program's **current** `per_unit_amount_cents` and `rate_basis` is recorded as `REPRICED_CURRENT`. Otherwise the rate is the claim's own frozen `snapshot_per_unit_cents` and `rate_basis` is recorded as `AS_SOLD_SNAPSHOT`. The consequence is worth stating plainly: the same claim, on the same delivery, against the same program, produces two different approved amounts depending on whether a retroactive repricing has reached back over that sale instant.

**Step two computes the incentive-type leg**, and for stair-step bonuses it also ends the calculation. When `claim_type` is `STAIR_STEP_BONUS`, `approved_amount_cents` is the matching objective's `payout_cents` minus its `paid_payout_cents` — the earned bonus not yet paid — and the calculation stops there without adding any per-unit rate or type leg. Otherwise the leg depends on the program's `incentive_type`. For `CUSTOMER_CASH` the leg is 0. For `FINANCE_SUBVENTION`, first take the market benchmark APR of 799 basis points, that is 7.99 percent, and subtract the delivery's `contract_apr_bps`; if that difference is negative, treat it as 0. The effective buy-down in basis points is then the smaller of that non-negative difference and the program's `apr_buydown_bps`. The leg is the delivery's `msrp_cents` multiplied by the effective buy-down in basis points, multiplied by the delivery's `term_months`, divided by 120000, with half-up rounding applied once to that entire expression rather than to any individual factor. For `LEASE_SUPPORT` the leg is the delivery's `msrp_cents` multiplied by the program's `residual_support_bps`, divided by 10000, again with half-up rounding applied once to the whole expression.

**Step three sums them.** `approved_amount_cents` is the per-unit rate from step one plus the leg from step two, and that is the only rounding boundary in the calculation. No clamping happens here: budget and stack ceilings are enforced as refusals elsewhere in the ladder, never as a silent reduction of the payable amount.

The last gate an approval passes is **budget headroom**. If the program's `committed_cents` plus this claim's newly computed `approved_amount_cents` would rise above the program's `budget_cents`, the approval is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 402) and nothing is written. Otherwise the approval commits the money and recomputes the program's ledger fields. `committed_cents` is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over the program's claims whose `validation_status` is `VALIDATED` and whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID`, `SCHEDULED` or `PAID`, plus the sum of `amount_cents` over every chargeback reachable through the program's claims whose `status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`. `paid_cents` on the program is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over the program's claims whose `payment_status` is `PAID`, and `paid_cents` on a dealer is the same sum scoped by that dealer's claims instead of the program's. Neither ledger involves any rounding — both are exact sums of integer cents. Approval also increments the claim's `version` by 1 and the program's `version` by 1, and for a `LEASE_SUPPORT` program it refreshes `residual_impact_bps` by the arithmetic of §3.9.

The **per-unit spend monitor** is recomputed at the same moment, and again whenever a claim is charged back, whenever a program is superseded, and whenever a repricing re-evaluates claims. It rests on the same claim set as the committed ledger: the program's claims whose `validation_status` is `VALIDATED` and whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID`, `SCHEDULED` or `PAID`, so that rejected and charged-back claims leave the set immediately. Within that set, `claimed_units` counts only the claims whose `claim_type` is `UNIT_INCENTIVE`, while the spend being monitored is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over **all** claims in the set, stair-step bonuses included. `per_unit_spend_cents` is that spend divided by `claimed_units` and floored; when `claimed_units` is 0 the result is exactly 0 rather than null and never a division error. The aggregate is insensitive to ordering, so no tie-break is needed, and its window is the program's whole life with no month scoping.

Stair-step payouts have their own arithmetic, recomputed whenever a delivery is validated, whenever a delivery is unwound, and whenever a protest is upheld. `attainment_bps` is the objective's `achieved_units` multiplied by 10000, divided by its `objective_units`, and **floored**; because `objective_units` is at least 1 by construction, this division can never fail. `tier_attained` is the highest `tier_index` among the program's `tier_schedule` entries whose `threshold_bps` is **at or below** `attainment_bps`, and it is 0 when no tier qualifies. When `tier_attained` is 0, `payout_cents` is 0 and no further work is done. Otherwise the tiers are read in ascending `tier_index`, which the submission-time schedule check guarantees is also ascending `threshold_bps`, and each tier's unit threshold is the objective's `objective_units` multiplied by that tier's `threshold_bps`, divided by 10000, and **ceilinged** to a whole number. The two roundings run in opposite directions — attainment floors, unit thresholds ceiling — and neither may be swapped for the other.

Which units earn which bonus then depends on the program's `tier_bonus_mode`. Under `MARGINAL`, `payout_cents` starts at 0 and accumulates one band at a time over the tiers whose `tier_index` is at or below `tier_attained`, taken in ascending `tier_index`: the band's upper unit is one less than the unit threshold of the next tier in the schedule, or unbounded when the tier is the last one; the band's unit count is the smaller of `achieved_units` and that upper unit, minus the tier's own unit threshold, plus one; and the band contributes its unit count multiplied by that tier's `per_unit_bonus_cents`. Every unit therefore earns only the bonus of the band it falls in. Under `FULL_VOLUME`, `payout_cents` is instead every one of the objective's `achieved_units` multiplied by the `per_unit_bonus_cents` of the single tier whose `tier_index` equals `tier_attained` — a retroactive re-rate of the whole volume at the top tier reached, which for identical `achieved_units`, `objective_units` and `tier_schedule` always pays strictly more than the marginal-band variant.

### 3.8. Payment Batches: Scheduling, Locking, Disbursement and Cancellation

Money leaves the automaker in regional batches, each covering one closed pay period. An `IncentiveController` opens a batch with a `batch_code` of 1 to 24 characters that is unique across every batch ever created, including cancelled ones; a `region_code` naming the geography whose dealers this batch pays; `period_start` and `period_end` bounding the pay period as a closed interval, with `period_end` required to be at or after `period_start`; and `scheduled_pay_date`, the instant the batch is intended to disburse, which must be at or after `period_end`. A new batch begins with `status` `OPEN`, `claim_count` of 0, `total_cents` of 0, `held_claim_count` of 0 and a null `disbursed_at`.

A region behaves like a resource that its pay periods occupy, and two checks protect that occupancy. The **period-overlap** check refuses a batch whose closed period intersects the closed period of any other batch of the **same** `region_code` whose `status` is `OPEN`, `LOCKED` or `DISBURSED`, reporting `ERR_BATCH_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; batches of different regions never collide, and a cancelled batch has stopped occupying its interval so a later batch may reuse it. The **settlement-buffer** check goes further: even when the closed periods do not intersect, the gap between the new batch's `period_start` and the `period_end` of the nearest preceding batch of the same region must be strictly greater than one whole day, and back-to-back or exactly-one-day-apart periods are refused with `ERR_BATCH_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The buffer exists so that a late-arriving claim for the earlier period has somewhere to land.

Attaching claims to an open batch moves each claim `UNPAID → SCHEDULED` on the payment dimension. The controller supplies `claim_ids`, the list of claims to attach. Every named claim must exist. The batch itself must then be `OPEN`; scheduling into a locked, disbursed or cancelled batch is refused with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. That check on the batch named in the path runs **before** any named claim is screened for usability, so a call naming an unusable claim against a batch that is not `OPEN` reports the batch's `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` and not the claim's failure. Each named claim must then have `validation_status` `VALIDATED` and must have `payment_status` `UNPAID` — this is the one coupling between the claim's two otherwise independent state dimensions, and the reason it exists is that unadjudicated money may never be queued for payment. A named claim that fails either half of that condition is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED` and **the whole call is a no-op**: no claim in the list is attached, no total is recomputed, and no version moves. Each claim's dealer must sit in the batch's `region_code`, and each claim's delivery `sale_date` must fall inside the batch's closed pay period, at or after `period_start` and at or before `period_end`. On success each attached claim has its `payment_batch_id` set to the batch and its `version` incremented by 1, and the batch's `claim_count` and `total_cents` are recomputed with its own `version` incremented by 1. Those two totals are defined over the claims whose `payment_batch_id` equals this batch and whose `payment_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PAID`: `claim_count` is how many such claims there are, and `total_cents` is the exact sum of their `approved_amount_cents`.

Locking a batch moves it `OPEN → LOCKED` and requires at least one claim already attached, meaning `claim_count` of 1 or more; a lock attempted on a batch in any other status is refused with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. From that point no further claim may be attached, and — importantly for §3.9 — the batch's amounts become immutable to retroactive repricing. Locking increments the batch's `version` by 1.

Disbursement moves the batch `LOCKED → DISBURSED` and is refused with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` from any other status. The logical clock must have reached the batch's `scheduled_pay_date`; a disbursement attempted while the clock is strictly earlier than that instant is refused with `ERR_PAY_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. This is where dealer standing finally matters. Every scheduled claim whose dealer is `ACTIVE` moves `SCHEDULED → PAID` on the payment dimension, has `paid_at` stamped from the logical clock, and has its approved amount added to the program's `paid_cents` and to the dealer's `paid_cents`; for a claim whose `claim_type` is `STAIR_STEP_BONUS`, the matching objective's `paid_payout_cents` is set to that claim's `approved_amount_cents`. Every scheduled claim whose dealer has `status` `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is held instead: it stays in `SCHEDULED`, keeps its `payment_batch_id`, and is counted in the batch's `held_claim_count` as measured at the moment of this disbursement attempt. The batch's `disbursed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `total_cents` is recomputed over the claims that remain attached, and the batch's `version` increments by 1. Each claim that moved has its own `version` incremented by 1.

Cancelling a batch moves it `OPEN → CANCELLED`, optionally recording a `cancel_note`, and is refused with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` from any status other than `OPEN`. Cancellation releases the queue: every claim of this batch in `SCHEDULED` returns to `payment_status` `UNPAID` with its `payment_batch_id` cleared to null, and the batch's `claim_count` and `total_cents` are both set to 0. The batch's period stops occupying the region interval, so a later batch may reuse it. Both `DISBURSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal batch states.

Programs themselves can be listed with filters on `status`, on `incentive_type` and on `supersedes_program_id`, which is how a controller finds the successor chain behind a superseded program. Dealer objectives can be listed either under one program or across the whole system, with filters on `dealer_id`, `program_id` and `status`, and an objective's protests can be listed with a filter on `status`; the chargebacks raised against one claim can likewise be filtered by `status`.

### 3.9. Unwinds, Chargebacks, Accrual and the Program's Books

Deals fall apart. When a customer returns a vehicle or a finance contract collapses, a `ClaimsAuditor` unwinds the delivery, moving it `VALIDATED → UNWOUND` with an optional `reason`; the unwind is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_INVALID_STATE` from any status other than `VALIDATED`. The delivery's `unwound_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and a four-link cascade runs over everything the delivery had earned.

Link one moves every claim on this delivery whose `payment_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PAID` to `payment_status` `CHARGED_BACK` and clears each one's `payment_batch_id` to null, which also forces recomputation of the former batch's `claim_count` and `total_cents`. Link two, caused by link one, creates one chargeback per charged-back claim with `reason` `UNWOUND_DEAL`. Link three, caused by link two, appends for each created chargeback exactly one accrual-ledger row with `entry_type` `REVERSAL` whose `reversal_cents` equals that chargeback's `amount_cents`, and recomputes the program's `accrued_budget_cents`. Link four, caused by link three, recomputes the program's `committed_cents`, `paid_cents`, `claimed_units`, `per_unit_spend_cents` and — for a `LEASE_SUPPORT` program — `residual_impact_bps`, and decrements the matching dealer objective's `achieved_units` with a fresh recomputation of `attainment_bps`, `tier_attained` and `payout_cents` and an increment of that objective's `version`. The delivery's own `version` increments by 1. Because `achieved_units` is recomputed from its defining input set rather than adjusted blindly, validating a delivery and then unwinding it returns the objective to exactly the position it held before.

An auditor may also raise a chargeback directly against a single claim, without unwinding anything — a back-dated sale discovered after the fact, or a duplicate the dealer system submitted twice. The payload carries only a `reason`, drawn from `UNWOUND_DEAL`, `BACK_DATED_SALE`, `DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, `OBJECTIVE_REVISED` and `REPRICING_ADJUSTMENT`, and the chargeback records the `claim_id` of the claim named in the path. The claim's `payment_status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `PAID`; a claim still `UNPAID`, or one already `CHARGED_BACK`, has nothing to recover and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_APPLICABLE`. A directly raised chargeback records the raising auditor's `X-User-ID` in `created_by_user_id`, while a chargeback born from a cascade — an unwind, an upheld protest or a retroactive repricing — leaves `created_by_user_id` null. It enters with `status` `PENDING` and a null `recovered_at`, moves the claim to `payment_status` `CHARGED_BACK` with `payment_batch_id` cleared, appends the accrual `REVERSAL` row, recomputes the program's `committed_cents`, `paid_cents`, `claimed_units` and `per_unit_spend_cents`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

How much is recovered depends entirely on the `reason`, and `amount_cents` is always strictly greater than 0 — a reason whose arithmetic would yield zero or less means the cascade raises no chargeback at all. For `UNWOUND_DEAL`, `BACK_DATED_SALE` and `DUPLICATE_CLAIM` the recovery is **full**: `amount_cents` equals the claim's `approved_amount_cents`. For `OBJECTIVE_REVISED` the recovery is **partial**: `amount_cents` is the objective's `paid_payout_cents` minus its `payout_cents`, evaluated on the objective as it stands *after* the units recomputation of the upheld protest described in §3.4. For `REPRICING_ADJUSTMENT` the recovery is likewise partial: `amount_cents` is the claim's old `approved_amount_cents` minus its newly computed approved amount. The two partial-recovery reasons never move the claim's `payment_status` at all — the claim stays `PAID` in the objective-revision case and `SCHEDULED` in the repricing case — which is exactly what distinguishes them from the full reversals above.

A pending chargeback is resolved by the `IncentiveController` in one of two directions, and the ledger consequences diverge. Recovering it moves the chargeback `PENDING → RECOVERED`, stamps `recovered_at` from the logical clock, subtracts its `amount_cents` from the dealer's `open_chargeback_cents`, and recomputes the program's `committed_cents` — which, because the charged-back claim has already left the committed claim set and a recovered chargeback contributes nothing of its own, returns the program's commitment to exactly its pre-approval value. Writing it off moves the chargeback `PENDING → WRITTEN_OFF`, optionally recording a `write_off_note`, likewise stamps `recovered_at` and likewise subtracts `amount_cents` from the dealer's `open_chargeback_cents` — but it deliberately **leaves** the amount inside the program's `committed_cents`, because a written-off chargeback is money the automaker is never getting back and the program's books must keep carrying it. Either resolution increments the chargeback's `version` by 1, and both `RECOVERED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are terminal. Resolving a chargeback that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_INVALID_STATE`. A dealer's `open_chargeback_cents` is at all times the sum of `amount_cents` over the chargebacks of that dealer's claims whose `status` is `PENDING`.

Monthly accrual is how the automaker's books anticipate the money before it is claimed. An `IncentiveController` runs an accrual for one program and one `period_month`, supplied in `YYYY-MM` form with a four-digit year and a month between `01` and `12`. At most one accrual entry may exist per program and period: a second run for the same pairing is refused with `ERR_ACCRUAL_PERIOD_ALREADY_RUN`. The entry records `program_id` from the path, copies the program's `accrual_method` onto the row so that a later edit of the program cannot rewrite history, and sets `entry_type` to `ACCRUAL`. Its `source_reference` stays null on an accrual row; on a reversal row it carries the identifier of the chargeback that produced the reversal as a plain audit breadcrumb — a string, not a foreign key, never consulted in any business decision.

The accrual is computed line by line, one line per eligibility footprint row of the program, taken in `created_at` ascending order. The month window runs from the first instant of `period_month` inclusive to the first instant of the following month exclusive. A line's unit count is the number of retail deliveries whose `status` is `VALIDATED`, whose `sale_date` falls in that window, which the line's footprint row admits by the §3.1 predicate, and for which that row is the **first** admitting row of this program in `created_at` ascending order — so a delivery admitted by several rows is attributed to exactly one of them and no unit is ever double counted. Deliveries in `REPORTED`, `REJECTED` or `UNWOUND` are excluded entirely, and every footprint row of the program participates, since rows are append-only and can never be disabled.

Each line's amount then follows the program's `accrual_method`. Under `UNIT_BASED`, the line amount is the line's unit count multiplied by the program's `per_unit_amount_cents`, multiplied by the quantity ten thousand minus 275, divided by ten thousand — that is, actual validated units net of a flat cancellation reserve of 2.75 percent, withheld against the deals that will not stick. Under `TAKE_RATE_BASED`, the line amount is the line's unit count multiplied by the program's `per_unit_amount_cents`, multiplied by the program's `projected_take_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand — actual validated units scaled by the projected take rate instead of the reserve, so the same deliveries and the same per-unit amount yield a different accrual under the two methods. In both variants **half-up rounding is applied to each line separately, and only then are the rounded line amounts summed**; summing unrounded line values and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer that may differ by one or more cents. The row's `accrued_cents` is the sum of the rounded line amounts, its `unit_basis` is the sum of the line unit counts, and its `reversal_cents` is 0 — an accrual row never carries a reversal, just as a reversal row always carries an `accrued_cents` of 0.

A program's `accrued_budget_cents` is the sum of `accrued_cents` over all of its accrual-ledger rows minus the sum of `reversal_cents` over all of those rows, across both entry types and all periods, computed exactly with no rounding. Because accrual amounts are booked net of the 2.75 percent cancellation reserve while reversals are booked gross, unwinding every delivery of a month drives `accrued_budget_cents` negative by exactly the reserve that had been withheld. That negative residue is the program's over-accrual correction, and it must remain visible rather than being netted away or clamped. The accrual ledger can be listed for a program with filters on `entry_type` and `period_month`.

Programs get repriced mid-month, and repricing is the sharpest edge in the system. A `ProgramDesigner` applies a repricing event carrying a `mode` of either `PROSPECTIVE` or `RETROACTIVE_TO_MONTH_START`, the `new_per_unit_amount_cents` to be written onto the program (which must be zero or greater), and the `expected_program_version` guard of §2.8. The program must be `ACTIVE` and the logical clock must lie inside the program's half-open effective window, at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`; a repricing of a non-active program, or one attempted outside that window, is refused with `ERR_REPRICING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The event records `previous_per_unit_amount_cents` as the program's `per_unit_amount_cents` immediately before the write, stamps `effective_at` from the logical clock, and computes `month_start` as the first instant of the calendar month containing `effective_at`. The program's `per_unit_amount_cents` is then overwritten with `new_per_unit_amount_cents` and the program's `version` increments by 1.

Under `PROSPECTIVE` mode the work stops there: `affected_claim_count` is 0 and `net_adjustment_cents` is 0, because every already-submitted claim keeps its frozen `snapshot_per_unit_cents` and its `AS_SOLD_SNAPSHOT` rate basis, and only claims submitted after this event see the new rate.

Under `RETROACTIVE_TO_MONTH_START` mode the event re-evaluates the month. The target set is the program's claims whose `claim_type` is `UNIT_INCENTIVE`, whose `validation_status` is `VALIDATED`, whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `SCHEDULED`, and whose delivery's `sale_date` falls in the half-open window from `month_start` inclusive to `effective_at` exclusive. Stair-step bonus claims, claims still `SUBMITTED`, rejected claims, already-paid claims and charged-back claims are all excluded — paid money is untouchable. The target claims are processed in `sale_date` ascending order and, where sale instants tie, in claim `created_at` ascending order; identifiers are never used to order the pass. Both counters start at 0. For each claim, the new approved amount is recomputed by the §3.7 arithmetic with the rate basis forced to `REPRICED_CURRENT`, and the delta is that new amount minus the claim's current `approved_amount_cents`. A delta of exactly zero is skipped entirely and does not count. Otherwise `affected_claim_count` increases by 1 and the signed delta is added to `net_adjustment_cents`.

What happens to the claim itself depends on the batch it sits in. If the claim has no `payment_batch_id`, or if the batch it names is `OPEN`, the claim's `approved_amount_cents` is overwritten with the new amount and its `rate_basis` becomes `REPRICED_CURRENT`; where a batch exists, that batch's `total_cents` and `claim_count` are recomputed. If instead the claim's batch is `LOCKED`, the batch is immutable and the claim's `approved_amount_cents` and `rate_basis` are left untouched. In that locked case a negative delta — the automaker cutting the rate on money already queued — raises a chargeback against the claim with `reason` `REPRICING_ADJUSTMENT` and an `amount_cents` equal to the magnitude of the delta, while the claim deliberately stays in `payment_status` `SCHEDULED`. A positive delta on a locked batch records nothing at all: the uplift is simply claimable in a later batch. When the pass completes, the program's `committed_cents`, `claimed_units` and `per_unit_spend_cents` are all recomputed. Note that `affected_claim_count` and `net_adjustment_cents` count every non-zero delta the pass found, including those on locked batches whose claim amounts were left alone.

Lease support has one further consequence the finance organization tracks: the residual value it implicitly underwrites. A program's `residual_impact_bps` is 0 for every program whose `incentive_type` is not `LEASE_SUPPORT`, and for lease-support programs it is recomputed when a claim is approved and when a delivery is unwound, in exactly five steps whose order is pinned and none of which may consult the output of a later step. Step one gathers the program's claims whose `validation_status` is `VALIDATED` and whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID`, `SCHEDULED` or `PAID`, and sums their `approved_amount_cents` into a gross support figure; rejected and charged-back claims are outside the set, which is why an unwind lowers the residual impact. Step two counts those same claims as the supported unit count, and if that count is 0 then `residual_impact_bps` is 0 and the calculation stops. Step three computes the average capitalized cost as the sum of the raw `msrp_cents` of those claims' deliveries divided by the supported unit count, floored. Raw MSRP is used deliberately: subtracting the customer cash stacked on the same delivery would be circular, because that stacked cash is itself limited by a pair cap evaluated against a stacked total that includes this program's own lease support. Step four computes a support ratio in basis points as the gross support figure multiplied by 10000, divided by the product of the average capitalized cost and the supported unit count, floored. Step five sets `residual_impact_bps` to that support ratio multiplied by a pass-through factor of 6000 basis points, divided by 10000, floored. Every division in this calculation floors — half-up rounding appears nowhere in it — and the floors are applied at steps three, four and five in that order.

Finally, the whole financial picture of a program is available as a single computed read for a `ProgramDesigner`, an `IncentiveController` or a `ClaimsAuditor`. It returns the `program_id` it describes, the program's `claimed_units`, `committed_cents`, `paid_cents`, `accrued_budget_cents`, `per_unit_spend_cents` and `residual_impact_bps`, and one derived figure not stored on the program itself: `budget_remaining_cents`, the program's `budget_cents` minus its `committed_cents`.
