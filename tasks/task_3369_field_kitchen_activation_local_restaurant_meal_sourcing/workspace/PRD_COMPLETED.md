# Field Kitchen Activation & Local Restaurant Meal Sourcing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a disaster strikes, the first forty-eight hours decide whether affected families eat. This API exists to make that window operationally tractable: it carries a rapid needs assessment from the field into a deployment decision, converts that decision into physical feeding capacity — a field kitchen with power, water and cold chain, or a contracted local restaurant with a working stove — and then accounts for every meal and every cent that follows. The domain is deliberately broad because the response itself is broad: an activation that begins as an emergency can run for months, and the same record set must serve a coordinator siting a kitchen in a flood zone on day one and a donor auditor reconciling spend at the end of a quarter.

Every operation in this system belongs to exactly one activation. The `Incident` record is the container: it carries the planning target, the funding ceiling and the currency, and every assessment, kitchen, restaurant, production batch, sourcing order, delivery run and donor commitment hangs beneath it. Two kinds of feeding capacity are modelled side by side. A `FieldKitchen` is the organisation's own production site, gated by utilities, cold chain and food-safety clearance. A `PartnerKitchen` is a local restaurant activated under contract, gated by bank verification, vetting, and a parallel contract/production state machine that keeps a kitchen from being pushed past the volume at which it can still cook well. Sourcing runs the other way: `Supplier` records and `IngredientPurchase` orders push cash into the affected economy rather than pulling goods out of it.

The requirements below describe both the contract — headers, envelopes, pagination, error identifiers, precision rules — and the operational logic: how a deployment priority is scored, how a production batch is priced and settled, how meals are conserved across a delivery run, and how donor money is tracked from pledge to disbursement. Field names, state names and error identifiers are normative; they are the vocabulary the API speaks, and an implementation that renames them is not an implementation of this document.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines system-wide rules, principles, and boilerplate that apply to the entire API. The API is mounted under the `/field-ops` path prefix; every endpoint path in Section 4 is relative to that mount.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or carries nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401); whitespace surrounding a header that does carry a value is stripped before the role and ownership comparisons. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the roles the targeted endpoint declares, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Role checks are evaluated after the request body has been parsed and validated, but before any resource is located; the ordering of all checks is set out in 2.5.

Ten roles are recognised, and their names must be spelled exactly as written here: `PlatformAdmin`, `FieldCoordinator`, `FoodSafetyOfficer`, `PartnershipLead`, `ProductionPlanner`, `SourcingOfficer`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `DonorLiaison`, `KitchenOperator`, and `SiteCoordinator`.

Eight of these roles are operational staff whose identifiers are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `FieldCoordinator`, `FoodSafetyOfficer`, `PartnershipLead`, `ProductionPlanner`, `SourcingOfficer`, `LogisticsCoordinator` or `DonorLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier where a workflow calls for attribution, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two roles are entity-backed, and for those the identifier in `X-User-ID` is a real record key. When `X-User-Role` is `KitchenOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PartnerKitchen`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `PartnerKitchen` compare the resource's `partner_kitchen_id` field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DistributionSite`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DistributionSite` compare the resource's `distribution_site_id` field to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Concretely, this gate applies when a `KitchenOperator` drives a production batch or a partner kitchen, and when a `SiteCoordinator` records a stop outcome: a `KitchenOperator` may only act on a batch whose `partner_kitchen_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and a `SiteCoordinator` may only confirm handover at a stop whose `distribution_site_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`.

The `PlatformAdmin` is the emergency operations director and holds override authority across every activation: it may open an activation, drive any transition endpoint as a fallback approver, advance the logical clock, and read the accountability surface. The other roles own narrower slices. A `FieldCoordinator` runs rapid assessments, sites field kitchens, and drives the activation state machine forward. A `FoodSafetyOfficer` owns inspections, food-safety suspensions and the quality verdict on a batch. A `PartnershipLead` vets and contracts local restaurants. A `ProductionPlanner` places production batches and cancels them. A `SourcingOfficer` registers producers and buys ingredients. A `LogisticsCoordinator` registers receiving sites, plans delivery runs, adds stops and records stop outcomes. A `DonorLiaison` records commitments and owns donor reporting. A `KitchenOperator` drives transitions only on its own kitchen and its own batches — the ownership gate of 2.5 is applied on those transition paths, while placing a batch through the creation endpoint is not ownership-gated. A `SiteCoordinator` confirms handover only at its own site.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Requests that never reach a declared endpoint are answered in the same envelope: a path the API does not declare is reported as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and any other transport-level rejection — an unsupported method on a declared path, or an unexpected server-side failure — is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying that transport's own status code.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter sets the maximum number of items returned, defaults to 10 when omitted, and accepts values from 1 to 100 inclusive; a value outside that range, or one that is not an integer, is a malformed request (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). The `offset` parameter sets how many items to skip, defaults to 0, and may not be negative. Both are read when the page is built — after the authentication and role checks of rules 2 and 3 — and an endpoint's optional filter parameters are matched by exact string equality against the named field, with an absent or empty filter ignored and a value that matches nothing returning an empty page rather than an error.

Unless an endpoint's description in Section 4 states otherwise, list endpoints return resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The one exception is the activation list, which returns activations newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending. Two rows can share a `created_at` instant, because the logical clock only moves when a client sets it, so both orders are made total by a server-side monotonic creation counter: when two rows share a `created_at`, the row created earlier — the one holding the lower counter — comes first in an ascending list and last in a descending one.

Paginated list responses carry four fields inside the `meta` object of the response envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of items the query matches in total, ignoring pagination, and `has_more` is true when further items remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identifiers and the Logical Clock

Every entity in this system — `Incident`, `Assessment`, `FieldKitchen`, `PartnerKitchen`, `MealOrder`, `Supplier`, `IngredientPurchase`, `DistributionSite`, `DistributionRun`, `DistributionStop` and `DonorCommitment` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` is a server-minted UUID string, unique across the whole API and carrying no type prefix or entity marker. Two resources of different types never share an `id`, and an `id` never encodes the entity it belongs to.
- `created_at` is an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was created.
- `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on any change to the resource's own properties. No entity in this system is immutable after creation, so `updated_at` is present on all of them.
- `version` is a system-managed integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource. It is the value clients echo back for optimistic concurrency control (see 2.6).

Clients supply instants too: an assessment's `assessed_at`, a batch's `scheduled_start`, a run's `planned_date`, `window_start` and `window_end`, and the clock's `to_iso8601`. Each is read as an ISO 8601 instant — one that carries no UTC offset is taken to be UTC — and is then serialised through the same formatter as the system-managed properties, so it comes back normalised to UTC in the same three-fractional-digit form whatever form the client wrote it in, and a fractional part finer than a millisecond is truncated rather than rounded. A supplied instant is never stored or returned in the client's own spelling; it is always re-serialised in this canonical form.

A mutation is any change to a resource, including one the client did not ask for directly: when a cascade, a recomputation of a derived field, or a system-driven state change alters a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented. Derived and computed values — `committed_cents`, `spent_cents`, `delivered_meal_total`, `local_sourcing_pct`, `cost_per_meal_cents`, `feeding_priority_score`, `food_safety_score`, `quality_score`, `reliability_score`, `meals_produced_total`, `meals_produced_today`, `runs_today_count`, `accepted_meals`, `subtotal_cents`, `logistics_fee_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `provisional_cost_cents`, `final_cost_cents`, `amount_cents`, `total_cost_cents`, `unallocated_cost_cents`, `delivered_meals`, `returned_meals`, `allocated_cost_cents`, `received_meals_total`, `skipped_stop_count`, `delivered_stop_count`, `cash_flow_received_cents`, `delivered_purchase_count`, `disputed_purchase_count`, `allocated_cents`, `disbursed_cents` and `remaining_cents` — are recomputed on the server whenever their inputs change, and a recomputation that changes a stored value counts as a mutation of the row that holds it. Recomputation is not confined to writes: a response that carries a resource row recomputes that row's derived values before serialising it, so a read can itself refresh `updated_at` and increment `version` when a stored value has gone stale — `runs_today_count` and `meals_produced_today`, for instance, are evaluated against the current UTC calendar day and change on the first read after the day rolls over. The values that are pinned at a single moment — a batch's cost buildup and final settlement, a sourcing order's `amount_cents`, a completed run's `unallocated_cost_cents` and its stops' `allocated_cost_cents`, a field kitchen's `food_safety_score`, a restaurant's `quality_score`, an assessment's `feeding_priority_score`, a producer's `cash_flow_received_cents`, and a commitment's `allocated_cents` and `disbursed_cents` — are not recomputed by a read; they move only when the operation that computes them runs, and every other derived value named above is recomputed by a read of the row that holds it. A recomputation that leaves every stored value unchanged is not a mutation and touches neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Time in this system is driven by a logical clock, not by wall-clock time. Before any clock call, the logical clock reads the fixed default instant `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the machine's real time. A `PlatformAdmin`-only test-driving capability sets the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Every deadline, cooldown expiry, production window and day-bucket evaluation in this document is evaluated against the logical clock as it stands at the moment of the request.

Several rules bucket activity by calendar day. A day bucket is the half-open UTC interval running from 00:00:00Z to 24:00:00Z on a single date; an instant exactly at midnight belongs to the day that is starting, not the one that is ending.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence and Request Rejection Order

Requests are rejected by the first rule that fails, and the rules are evaluated in a fixed global order. When two conditions hold at once, the lower-numbered rule wins and its error identifier is the one returned; a later rule is never consulted once an earlier one has failed. Rules that do not apply to a given request are skipped without affecting the relative order of the rules that do. Where a precondition described later in this document is stated without naming the error it yields, the failure is reported with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` if the request is a state transition that already satisfied rule 8, and with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` if the move's payload omits a field the move requires or carries a value outside its declared range. The ladder is:

1. Request-shape and field-range validation — the request body is a well-formed object, every enum-valued field carries a declared member (so a `to_state` that is not a member of the addressed entity's state set, or a partner-kitchen `dimension` that is neither `CONTRACT` nor `PRODUCTION`, is rejected here rather than by rule 8), every numeric field respects its declared range, and `window_end` is strictly after `window_start` on delivery-run creation. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
2. Authentication header presence — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present. Failure yields `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401).
3. Role authorization — `X-User-Role` is one of the roles the endpoint declares. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).
4. `KitchenOperator` ownership — when the caller is a `KitchenOperator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `partner_kitchen_id` of the addressed `MealOrder` and the `id` of the addressed `PartnerKitchen`. Failure yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Both this rule and rule 5 apply only to the transition paths that address one of those rows by id: a create path never compares `X-User-ID` to an id carried in the body, and when the addressed row does not exist the comparison is skipped so that rule 6 decides the error.
5. `SiteCoordinator` ownership — when the caller is a `SiteCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `distribution_site_id` of the addressed `DistributionStop`. Failure yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
6. Path-identifier existence — every `{id}` in the path resolves to a row of the addressed entity, validated left to right in path order, so the first unresolved identifier in the path decides the error. Failure yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIELD_KITCHEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_KITCHEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEAL_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_STOP_NOT_FOUND` according to the entity addressed, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolved assessment, supplier, ingredient purchase, distribution site or donor commitment (all HTTP 404).
7. Body foreign-key existence — every foreign-key identifier carried in the request body resolves to an existing row of the referenced entity. Failure yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
8. Transition reachability — the entity named in the path is in a state whose outgoing transitions include the requested `to_state`, and for a `PartnerKitchen` the requested `dimension` and `to_state` pair is a declared pair. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
9. Terminal-state guard — consulted only once rule 8 has already failed, and then only when the state the entity named in the path is in declares no outgoing transition at all: the rejection is `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), whereas an undeclared move out of a state that is not terminal keeps the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` of rule 8. A terminal state that still declares one compensating exit is not refused, because that move satisfies rule 8 rather than failing it — `PAID` is terminal for a sourcing order yet may still move to `DISPUTED`. For a `PartnerKitchen` the guard is applied per dimension: the contract dimension is terminal at `TERMINATED`, while the production dimension has no terminal state.
10. Optimistic concurrency — `expected_partner_kitchen_version` equals the current `PartnerKitchen.version`, and `expected_distribution_run_version` equals the current `DistributionRun.version`. Failure yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
11. Contracted kitchen — the `PartnerKitchen` referenced by a `MealOrder` has `contract_status` of `CONTRACTED`. Failure yields `ERR_PARTNER_KITCHEN_NOT_CONTRACTED` (HTTP 409).
12. Active activation — the parent `Incident` has `status` of `ACTIVE`. Only two requests consult this rule: the `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` to `OPERATIONAL` transition of a field kitchen and the `RECEIVED` to `ALLOCATED` transition of a donor commitment. No creation path consults it, and the only other precondition that reads the parent activation's status is the demobilization gate of 3.2. Failure yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
13. Operational origin — the `FieldKitchen` a `DistributionRun` loads from has `site_status` of `OPERATIONAL`. Failure yields `ERR_ORIGIN_KITCHEN_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (HTTP 409).
14. Run in transit — the parent `DistributionRun` has `status` of `DELIVERING` before a stop outcome is recorded. Failure yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_DELIVERING` (HTTP 409).
15. Producer not suspended — the `Supplier` referenced by an `IngredientPurchase` does not have `status` of `SUSPENDED` (`PROSPECT`, `APPROVED` and `PREFERRED` all pass). Only the confirmation move of a sourcing order consults this rule; the later moves of an order already under way do not re-check the producer's standing. Failure yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409).
16. Cooldown elapsed — the logical clock is at or after `cooldown_until` when a production window is opened or closed out of `COOLDOWN`. Failure yields `ERR_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
17. Bank verification — `bank_verified` is true before a kitchen may enter `VETTING`. Failure yields `ERR_BANK_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409).
18. Vetting threshold — `vetting_score` is at least 60 before a kitchen may enter `CONTRACTED`. Failure yields `ERR_VETTING_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 409).
19. Utility presence — `power_source` is not `NONE` and `water_source` is not `NONE` before a site may enter `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED`. This check runs before the score gate, so a site that has no water source is reported as `ERR_KITCHEN_UTILITY_MISSING` (HTTP 422) even though its `food_safety_score` is also below the clearance threshold.
20. Food-safety clearance threshold — `food_safety_score` is at least 70 before a site may enter `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED`. Failure yields `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 422).
21. Access not blocked — the receiving site referenced by a stop, or the assessed catchment behind a field-kitchen siting, has `access_constraint` other than `BLOCKED`. Failure yields `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_BLOCKED` (HTTP 422).
22. Dietary coverage — a batch's `dietary_tags` is a superset of the incident's `population_dietary_requirements`. Failure yields `ERR_DIETARY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 422).
23. Driver briefing — `driver_safety_training_completed` is true before a run is created or leaves `PLANNED`. Failure yields `ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_TRAINING_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).
24. Production-window conflict — no other batch of the same kitchen occupies an intersecting half-open window, and the new `scheduled_start` is not inside a trailing post-rejection buffer. Failure yields `ERR_KITCHEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
25. Kitchen daily run cap — the origin field kitchen has fewer than 3 runs already bucketed to the same UTC calendar day. Failure yields `ERR_KITCHEN_DAILY_RUN_CAP` (HTTP 409).
26. Run load capacity — the sum of `planned_meals` over the run's pending stops stays at or below `loaded_meals`. Failure yields `ERR_RUN_LOAD_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409).
27. Stop-sequence uniqueness — `stop_sequence` is unique among the stops of the same run. Failure yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SEQUENCE` (HTTP 409).
28. Stop-site uniqueness — a `DistributionSite` appears at most once among the stops of the same run. Failure yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SITE` (HTTP 409).
29. Funding ceiling — `spent_cents` plus the `provisional_cost_cents` a new batch would add stays at or below `funding_ceiling_cents`. Failure yields `ERR_FUNDING_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
30. Disbursement cap — `disbursed_cents` plus `disbursement_cents` stays at or below `amount_cents`. Failure yields `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT` (HTTP 422).
31. Single-activation reference — every entity referenced by a request body or by a state transition belongs to the same `incident_id` as the subject of the request. Failure yields `ERR_CROSS_INCIDENT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422).
32. Assessment timing — `assessed_at` is not earlier than the parent incident's `created_at`. Failure yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BEFORE_ACTIVATION` (HTTP 422).

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Kitchen and Run Writes

Two write paths in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and they are the only two. Everywhere else, a request is processed without any version comparison.

Creating a production batch carries a non-optional `expected_partner_kitchen_version` field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `PartnerKitchen` named by `partner_kitchen_id`. The server compares it against that kitchen's current `version`. If the kitchen's version has advanced since the client last read it — because another writer changed its contract or production dimension, or because a cascade touched it — the client's view of the kitchen's remaining capacity is stale and the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The batch is not created.

Adding a stop to a run carries a non-optional `expected_distribution_run_version` field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `DistributionRun` named by `distribution_run_id`. The server compares it against that run's current `version`. If the run's version has advanced since the client last read it — because another writer added a stop or recorded a stop outcome — the client's view of the run's remaining load capacity is stale and the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The stop is not added.

Both rejections use the same error identifier and the same status code, and both are evaluated at position 10 of the validation ladder in 2.5, after state reachability and the terminal-state guard but before every business rule. Endpoints that are not named above do not accept a version field at all — a version field sent to any other endpoint is ignored, as is any other body field the addressed endpoint does not declare.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision, Rounding and Integer Cents

Every monetary quantity in this API is stored and returned as an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency values anywhere in a response body, and no monetary field is ever serialised as a string. Field names ending in `_cents` are integer cents; `per_meal_price_cents` and `unit_price_cents_per_kg` are integer cents per unit; `local_sourcing_pct` is an integer percentage rather than a fraction.

Where a calculation produces a fractional cent, the value is rounded half up — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next integer. Rounding is applied only at the points the calculation names, and each named rounding is a single rounding of the final product of that step; intermediate quantities are not pre-rounded. Where a calculation states that no rounding is required, every term is already an integer and the arithmetic is exact.

All monetary values of an activation are denominated in the currency named by that incident's `currency_code`, an ISO 4217 code that defaults to `USD` when the client omits it at creation. The system does not convert between currencies and does not re-denominate an activation after creation.

### 2.8. Activation Hierarchy and Cascading Reconciliation

The resource graph is flat — no entity is a child of another in the storage sense — but it is not independent. Every assessment, kitchen, restaurant, batch, sourcing order, delivery site, delivery run, stop and commitment belongs to one activation through its `incident_id`, and several operations in this system mutate more than the resource the client addressed. When a parent-level event renders a dependent resource impossible, the server resolves the dependent resource itself, without requiring a client request against it, and the client learns the outcome by reading the affected rows afterwards.

Cascades are deliberately shallow. A single hop covers the direct dependents of the resource being driven: suspending a field kitchen aborts the runs that were still loading from it and skips their pending stops; demobilizing a kitchen aborts its planned runs; terminating a restaurant cancels its placed batches; pushing a kitchen to `AT_CAPACITY` cancels its placed batches; closing an activation closes its receiving sites and its allocated commitments. A second hop exists where the first hop's outcome has a second-order consequence: when a stop is skipped and the site it served now has strictly more skipped stops than delivered ones, that site is placed into `SERVICE_ALERT`; and when a producer's standing is recomputed and rises to `PREFERRED`, that producer's other outstanding orders are auto-confirmed. No cascade runs deeper than two hops.

Every row touched by a cascade, by a recomputation, or by a system-driven state change is mutated like any other: its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant and its `version` is incremented by one. A client holding a version from before a cascade will therefore fail the concurrency guard in 2.6 on its next guarded write, which is the intended way for that client to discover that the world moved.

Some transitions are driven by the system rather than by a caller. Producers move between standing states when their reliability is recomputed; receiving sites move between `REGISTERED`, `ACTIVE` and `SERVICE_ALERT` as their stop outcomes accumulate; commitments close when their activation closes; runs abort when their origin kitchen is suspended. These transitions are not addressable as client requests on the entity itself in the cases where the table in Section 4 does not list a transitions path for it, and they occur as a direct consequence of the triggering operation.

### 2.9. Referential Integrity Across the Activation

Identifiers carried in a request body must resolve. Every foreign-key identifier — `incident_id`, `assessment_id`, `partner_kitchen_id`, `supplier_id`, `field_kitchen_id`, `distribution_run_id`, `distribution_site_id` — is checked against the corresponding entity before any business rule runs, and an unresolved identifier yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Resolution is not enough on its own. Every entity referenced by a request body, and every entity reached by a state transition, must belong to the same activation as the subject of the request. A batch placed against a kitchen from a different activation, a stop added to a run from a different activation, or a dispatch pointed at a run serving a different incident is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_INCIDENT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). The single-activation rule is what keeps an activation's meal counts, spend and sourcing share internally consistent, so it is enforced on every create path and on the dispatch transition that binds a batch to a run.

Paths that address a resource nested beneath another resource are validated from left to right. The first identifier in the path that does not resolve decides the error, so a request against a sub-resource of a nonexistent activation reports the activation as missing rather than the sub-resource.

### 2.10. Food Safety, Access and Driver Safety Gates

Three families of operational gate protect people rather than money, and all three reject with HTTP 422 so that a caller can distinguish a safety refusal from a state conflict.

A field kitchen cannot be cleared for food production without utilities and without a passing score. Both `power_source` and `water_source` must be something other than `NONE`, and the utility check is deliberately evaluated ahead of the score check so that a site with no potable water is reported as `ERR_KITCHEN_UTILITY_MISSING` rather than as a score failure. Once utilities are present, `food_safety_score` must be at least 70 or the transition is rejected with `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_SCORE_TOO_LOW`.

A receiving site or an assessed catchment that is `BLOCKED` cannot be served. Recording a stop against a blocked site, adding a stop for a blocked site, or siting a field kitchen against an assessment whose catchment is blocked is rejected with `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_BLOCKED`.

A volunteer driver who has not completed the security and food-handling briefing cannot carry meals. A run whose `driver_safety_training_completed` is false is rejected at creation and again when it attempts to leave `PLANNED`, with `ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_TRAINING_REQUIRED`. Dietary coverage is enforced at the same layer: a batch whose `dietary_tags` do not cover every tag in the incident's `population_dietary_requirements` is rejected with `ERR_DIETARY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`.

### 2.11. Funding Ceilings and Donor Fund Conservation

Each activation carries a hard `funding_ceiling_cents`, and the system refuses to let planned and settled spend cross it. The measure used for that check is the incident's `spent_cents`, which aggregates three streams: the `provisional_cost_cents` of every `MealOrder` of the incident whose `status` is `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `QUALITY_HOLD`, `READY` or `DISPATCHED`; the `final_cost_cents` of every `MealOrder` whose `status` is `DELIVERED`; and the `amount_cents` of every `IngredientPurchase` whose `status` is `DELIVERED` or `PAID`. Orders in `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. Placing a batch that would push `spent_cents` above the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Donor money is conserved on a second axis. A commitment's `amount_cents` is its face value; `allocated_cents` is the portion assigned to the activation's open obligations; `disbursed_cents` is the portion actually released against settled obligations; and `remaining_cents` is `amount_cents` minus `disbursed_cents`, the conserved balance that must never go negative. A disbursement that would push `disbursed_cents` above `amount_cents` is rejected with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT` (HTTP 422).

The activation's `committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over its commitments whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `ALLOCATED`, `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` or `CLOSED`. Commitments in `PLEDGED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded, which is what makes `committed_cents` a measure of money the organisation can actually plan against rather than money it has been promised.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening an Activation and the Rapid Needs Assessment

An activation begins when a `PlatformAdmin` opens a new record. The record carries a human `name` for the operation, an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 `country_code` written as exactly two uppercase letters, a `disaster_type` drawn from `EARTHQUAKE`, `FLOOD`, `HURRICANE`, `WILDFIRE`, `CONFLICT` or `EPIDEMIC`, a `severity_level` on the 1-to-5 operational scale, a `target_daily_meals` planning figure of at least 1, and a `funding_ceiling_cents` of at least 0 that caps cumulative meal-order and sourcing spend. Two further inputs shape how the response is planned: `currency_code`, an ISO 4217 code that defaults to `USD` — both when the field is omitted and when it is sent as an empty string — and governs every monetary field of the activation, and `population_dietary_requirements`, the list of dietary tags the affected population needs every meal to satisfy, drawn from `HALAL`, `VEGETARIAN`, `VEGAN`, `NUT_FREE`, `GLUTEN_FREE` and `LOW_SODIUM`, which defaults to the empty list when omitted. A new activation opens in `MONITORING` with its `version` at 1.

Nothing can be fed while the activation is only being monitored, so the first real move is the transition into `ACTIVE`, which a `FieldCoordinator` or a `PlatformAdmin` may drive. That transition is gated on evidence: at least one assessment of this activation must already be in `SUBMITTED` status. When it succeeds, `activated_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, every field kitchen belonging to this activation whose `site_status` is `SITED` is moved to `UTILITIES_PENDING` so that site preparation can begin in parallel, and the activation's `spent_cents` is recomputed.

Assessments are how the field tells the operation what it is facing, and they are recorded by a `FieldCoordinator`. Each assessment names its `incident_id` and the `assessed_at` instant at which the field team finished its work; that instant may not be earlier than the activation's `created_at`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BEFORE_ACTIVATION`. The assessment then carries the substance of the field report: `population_affected`, the number of people in the assessed catchment, which may not be negative; `estimated_daily_meal_need`, the field estimate of meals required per day there, which may not be negative; `access_constraint`, one of `OPEN`, `LIMITED`, `RESTRICTED` or `BLOCKED`, describing road and security access; `water_available` and `power_available`, booleans reporting whether potable water and grid or generator power reach the catchment; and `partner_kitchens_identified`, the count of local restaurants the team judged activatable, which may not be negative. A final input, `assessment_mode`, selects the planning posture and defaults to `RAPID`.

An assessment is created directly in `SUBMITTED` and is scored the moment it lands. Its `feeding_priority_score` is an integer between 0 and 100 that tells planners which catchment to serve first, and it is assembled from four components. The need component measures how many meals each affected person requires: it is 0 when `population_affected` is 0, and otherwise it is the estimated daily meal need multiplied by 100 and divided by the affected population, rounded half up, capped at 100. The access component is a fixed weight keyed on `access_constraint`: `OPEN` contributes 10, `LIMITED` contributes 25, `RESTRICTED` contributes 40, and `BLOCKED` contributes 55, so a harder-to-reach catchment scores as more urgent. The infrastructure component starts from a raw penalty of 20 points when `water_available` is false plus 15 points when `power_available` is false, and the partner component starts from a raw penalty of 25 minus 5 points for each restaurant the team identified, floored at 0 — a catchment with no local cooking capacity is the hardest to serve.

The `assessment_mode` then scales those last two components, and this is the only place the two postures differ. Under `RAPID`, the default posture for the first 72 hours of an activation, the infrastructure raw penalty is used at full weight and the partner raw penalty is used at single weight. Under `DURABLE`, the posture adopted once the response shifts toward local capacity, the infrastructure penalty is halved with half-up rounding and the partner penalty is doubled and then capped at 40. The four components are summed and the total is capped at 100 to produce `feeding_priority_score`. Consider a catchment with 50,000 people affected, an estimated daily meal need of 10,000, `OPEN` access, no water available, power available, and 1 activatable restaurant. The need component is 10,000 multiplied by 100 and divided by 50,000, which is 20; the access component is 10; the infrastructure raw penalty is 20; and the partner raw penalty is 25 minus 5, which is 20. Scored as `RAPID` the total is 20 plus 10 plus 20 plus 20, giving a `feeding_priority_score` of 70. Scored as `DURABLE` the same field data gives an infrastructure component of 10 and a partner component of 40, for a score of 80 — the durable posture penalises the absence of local capacity far more heavily. The two postures therefore diverge whenever the raw infrastructure penalty differs from twice the raw partner penalty.

An activation can also step back down. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it from `ACTIVE` to `WINDING_DOWN`, but only when no batch of this activation is in `IN_PRODUCTION` or `QUALITY_HOLD` — the operation does not wind down while food is on the line. That transition stamps `wind_down_started_at` and demobilizes every field kitchen of the activation that is still only `SITED` or `UTILITIES_PENDING`, moving it to `DEMOBILIZED` and stamping `demobilized_at`. If the situation deteriorates again, a `PlatformAdmin` may re-escalate from `WINDING_DOWN` back to `ACTIVE`, provided a fresh assessment exists: at least one assessment of this activation must be in `SUBMITTED` with an `assessed_at` strictly after the stored `wind_down_started_at`. Re-escalation clears `wind_down_started_at` and restores precisely the kitchens that the wind-down demobilized — those whose `site_status` is `DEMOBILIZED` and whose `demobilized_at` equals that wind-down instant — returning them to `SITED` with `demobilized_at` cleared. An activation may oscillate between `ACTIVE` and `WINDING_DOWN` as new assessments land. An assessment is never replaced, retired or superseded — nothing in this API edits or deletes an assessment once it has landed — so every assessment ever recorded remains in `SUBMITTED` and stays available as evidence for these transitions.

Closing is the end of the road. A `PlatformAdmin` may move an activation from `WINDING_DOWN` to `CLOSED` only when every field kitchen of the activation is `DEMOBILIZED` and every delivery run of the activation is `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED`. Closing stamps `closed_at` and cascades: every receiving site of the activation whose `status` is `REGISTERED`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_ALERT` is set to `CLOSED`, and every donor commitment of the activation whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` or `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` is set to `CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal for the activation.

### 3.2. Siting and Clearing a Field Kitchen

A `FieldCoordinator` sites a field kitchen against the assessment that justifies it. The request names the `incident_id`, the `assessment_id`, a `site_name` label for the physical location, the site's `latitude` and `longitude` in decimal degrees — bounded to minus 90 through 90 and minus 180 through 180 respectively — the `power_source` (one of `GRID`, `GENERATOR`, `SOLAR` or `NONE`), the `water_source` (one of `PIPED`, `TRUCKED`, `WELL` or `NONE`), the `refrigeration_hours_available` per day and the `daily_capacity_meals` the site can produce, neither of which may be negative. A new site starts in `SITED`.

The site's `food_safety_score` is computed at creation and recomputed on every inspection; it is an integer between 0 and 100 built from three positive terms and one penalty. Power contributes 40 points for `GRID`, 30 for `GENERATOR`, 20 for `SOLAR` and 0 for `NONE`. Water contributes 35 points for `PIPED`, 25 for `TRUCKED`, 15 for `WELL` and 0 for `NONE`. Cold chain contributes 25 points when `refrigeration_hours_available` is at least 12, 15 points when it is at least 6 but under 12, and 0 otherwise. Against those, a site whose `daily_capacity_meals` is under 500 takes a 10-point capacity penalty, because a very small site is harder to run safely at volume. The four terms are combined as power plus water plus cold minus the penalty, and the result is clamped to the range 0 to 100. A site with generator power, trucked water, 12 hours of refrigeration and a capacity of 800 meals scores 30 plus 25 plus 25, which is 80. The same site with solar power, a well, 5 hours of refrigeration and a capacity of 400 scores 20 plus 15 plus 0 minus 10, which is 25 — below any clearance threshold. The boundaries matter exactly as stated: 12 hours earns the full cold-chain points and 11 does not, 6 hours earns the partial points and 5 does not, and a capacity of 500 escapes the penalty while 499 does not.

A `FoodSafetyOfficer` records inspections of a site. An inspection supplies `power_source`, `water_source`, `refrigeration_hours_available` and `daily_capacity_meals`; it overwrites all four on the site, stamps `last_inspection_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and re-runs the food-safety calculation. Inspections are how a site recovers from a suspension, so they are also the mechanism that produces the evidence the recovery transition checks.

The site's journey to production runs through three gates. Moving from `SITED` to `UTILITIES_PENDING` requires that the assessment named by `assessment_id` has an `access_constraint` other than `BLOCKED`; a catchment the teams cannot reach cannot be served, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_BLOCKED`. Moving from `UTILITIES_PENDING` to `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` requires that `power_source` is not `NONE` and `water_source` is not `NONE`, and that `food_safety_score` is at least 70. The utility check runs first: a site with no water source is reported as `ERR_KITCHEN_UTILITY_MISSING` even though its score is also below the threshold, while a site with utilities but a score of 69 is reported as `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. Both are refusals at HTTP 422. Moving from `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` to `OPERATIONAL` requires that the parent activation is `ACTIVE`, and failure yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; a successful transition stamps `activated_at` with the current logical-clock instant.

A `FoodSafetyOfficer` can suspend an operating site at any time, and no precondition beyond role and state applies. Suspension stamps `suspended_at` and cascades one hop: every delivery run of that kitchen whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `LOADING` is moved to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `ORIGIN_KITCHEN_SUSPENDED`, and each of those runs' pending stops is skipped with `failure_reason` set to `ORIGIN_KITCHEN_SUSPENDED`. Recovery runs from `SUSPENDED` back to `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` and requires that `last_inspection_at` is strictly after `suspended_at` and that the current `food_safety_score` is at least 70; recovery clears `suspended_at`, and the site may then be returned to `OPERATIONAL` through the normal clearance path. The full cycle is `OPERATIONAL` to `SUSPENDED` to `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` to `OPERATIONAL`.

Demobilization is the site's terminal move. A `FieldCoordinator` may move a site from `OPERATIONAL` to `DEMOBILIZED` only when the parent activation is in `WINDING_DOWN` or `CLOSED`. Demobilization stamps `demobilized_at` and aborts every delivery run of that kitchen that is still `PLANNED`, setting `abort_reason` to `KITCHEN_DEMOBILIZED`; those runs' pending stops are skipped under the standard run-abort cascade with `failure_reason` set to `RUN_ABORTED`. `DEMOBILIZED` is terminal for a field kitchen. The only state a site can be demobilized from is `OPERATIONAL`; a site in `SITED`, `UTILITIES_PENDING`, `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` or `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the wind-down cascade above is the only path that demobilizes a site which is not `OPERATIONAL` at the time. A site left in `FOOD_SAFETY_CLEARED` or `SUSPENDED` while its activation is winding down therefore blocks closure — the close transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — until the activation is re-escalated to `ACTIVE` and the site is driven back through `OPERATIONAL`. Planners watch one derived counter on the site: `runs_today_count` is the number of delivery runs belonging to this field kitchen whose `planned_date` falls in the current UTC calendar day, and it is recomputed whenever a run is created.

### 3.3. The Local Restaurant Activation Cycle

Buying meals from restaurants inside the affected area does two jobs at once: it feeds people and it puts cash back into a local economy that has just lost its income. A `PartnershipLead` registers a candidate restaurant with an `incident_id`, its `business_name`, the `owner_name` who will sign the activation agreement, a list of free-form `cuisine_tags` used later for culturally appropriate menu matching, the `daily_capacity_meals` the restaurant can produce without dropping quality (at least 1), the `per_meal_price_cents` it charges per meal (at least 1), and a `bank_verified` boolean reporting whether its payout account passed verification. A new restaurant carries two independent state dimensions: `contract_status` starts at `PROSPECT`, `production_status` starts at `IDLE`, and both `vetting_score` and `quality_score` start at 0.

Contract status is driven by a `PartnershipLead` and moves through a vetting cycle. A restaurant may only move from `PROSPECT` to `VETTING` if `bank_verified` is true; without a verified payout account the attempt is rejected with `ERR_BANK_NOT_VERIFIED`. The transition payload may carry the `vetting_score` — an integer from 0 to 100 — that the partnership team recorded; the field is optional, and when it is supplied it is written to the kitchen on whichever contract transition carries it, so a score can be recorded on the way into `VETTING` and again on the way out. The move from `VETTING` to `CONTRACTED` requires the resulting `vetting_score` to be at least 60, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_VETTING_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. Contracting stamps `contracted_at` with the current logical-clock instant. A kitchen that fails vetting can be sent back: `VETTING` to `PROSPECT` has no precondition beyond role and state, and it resets `vetting_score` to 0 so that a later attempt starts from a clean slate.

Suspension and reinstatement form the second half of the contract cycle. A `PartnershipLead` may move a contracted restaurant to `SUSPENDED` at any time. Suspension has a cross-dimension consequence: if the kitchen's `production_status` is anything other than `IDLE`, the kitchen is forced into `COOLDOWN` and `cooldown_until` is set to the current logical-clock instant plus 8 hours, so a restaurant that was mid-production is given a cooling-off period before it can cook again. Reinstatement runs from `SUSPENDED` back to `CONTRACTED` and requires `vetting_score` of at least 60, so a kitchen cannot be brought back on a stale or failing score.

Termination ends the relationship and is available only from `CONTRACTED`. Before a restaurant can be terminated, every batch it has ever produced must be resolved: every `MealOrder` of that kitchen must be in `DELIVERED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED`. Termination stamps `terminated_at` and cancels everything still queued — every batch of that kitchen whose `status` is `PLACED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `KITCHEN_TERMINATED`. `TERMINATED` is terminal for the contract dimension.

Production status is driven by the restaurant's own account, the `KitchenOperator`, and it cycles through four states. A kitchen may move from `IDLE` to `PRODUCING` only when its `contract_status` is `CONTRACTED` and the current logical-clock instant is at or after `cooldown_until`, or `cooldown_until` is null; a kitchen still inside its cooling-off window is rejected with `ERR_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. Opening a window stamps `production_started_at`. A kitchen moves from `PRODUCING` to `AT_CAPACITY` when `meals_produced_today` has reached `daily_capacity_meals`; this is a real ceiling on quality, not a soft target, and entering it cancels every batch of that kitchen still in `PLACED` with `cancel_reason` set to `KITCHEN_AT_CAPACITY`. The same state is entered automatically, without a client request, when a batch delivery pushes `meals_produced_today` to the kitchen's daily capacity. From `AT_CAPACITY` the kitchen moves to `COOLDOWN`, which sets `cooldown_until` to the current logical-clock instant plus 8 hours; and from `COOLDOWN` it returns to `IDLE` only once the logical clock is at or after `cooldown_until`, at which point `cooldown_until` is cleared. The full production cycle is `IDLE` to `PRODUCING` to `AT_CAPACITY` to `COOLDOWN` to `IDLE`.

Two counters track a restaurant's output and one score tracks its cooking. `meals_produced_total` is the lifetime count of meals the restaurant has reported producing for the activation — each production report adds the full reported quantity, so a batch that is reworked and reported again is counted again — and `meals_produced_today` counts only those reported meals whose report instant falls in the current UTC calendar day. Both are incremented when a batch reports its `produced_meals`. The `quality_score` is a rolling figure between 0 and 100 recomputed whenever one of the restaurant's batches reaches a quality-affecting outcome, and it is described in 3.5.

### 3.4. Production Batches: Window Scheduling and Cost Buildup

A production batch is a commitment by a contracted restaurant to cook a defined number of meals in a defined window. A `ProductionPlanner` places one by naming the `incident_id`, the `partner_kitchen_id` of the restaurant, a `menu_name` label for the culturally appropriate menu the batch follows, the `dietary_tags` the batch satisfies (a subset of `HALAL`, `VEGETARIAN`, `VEGAN`, `NUT_FREE`, `GLUTEN_FREE` and `LOW_SODIUM`), the `ordered_meals` requested (at least 1), the `scheduled_start` instant, and the `production_hours` the window will run for (at least 1). The production window is the half-open interval running from `scheduled_start` up to but not including `scheduled_start` plus `production_hours`. A new batch starts in `PLACED`, is stamped with `placed_at`, and leaves `distribution_run_id` unset until the batch is dispatched.

Three gates stand in front of batch creation. The first is optimistic concurrency: the request must carry `expected_partner_kitchen_version` matching the kitchen's current `version`, and a kitchen whose version has advanced since the client last read it causes the create to be rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The second is contractual: the restaurant must be in `contract_status` `CONTRACTED`, or the create is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_KITCHEN_NOT_CONTRACTED`. The third is dietary: the batch's `dietary_tags` must cover every tag in the incident's `population_dietary_requirements`, and a batch that would leave part of the affected population without a meal it can eat is rejected with `ERR_DIETARY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`.

A restaurant cannot be cooking two things in the same pot at the same time, so batch windows must not collide. Creating a batch is rejected with `ERR_KITCHEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` if another batch of the same kitchen in `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `QUALITY_HOLD`, `READY` or `DISPATCHED` status occupies a half-open window that intersects the new one. The same error applies when the new `scheduled_start` falls inside the 8-hour trailing buffer that follows a batch of that kitchen which entered `REJECTED` — a kitchen that has just had a batch rejected needs time to clean down and re-plan before it starts another. Batches in `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` no longer occupy a window.

Money is committed when the batch is placed, and the price is built in a fixed order that resolves an apparent circularity — the discount is keyed on a subtotal, not on a total that already contains the discount. First, `subtotal_cents` is the `ordered_meals` multiplied by the restaurant's `per_meal_price_cents`. Second, a volume tier is selected from that pre-discount subtotal: `BULK` when `subtotal_cents` is at least 2,000,000; `STANDARD` when it is at least 800,000 but under 2,000,000; and `SMALL` otherwise. Third, `logistics_fee_cents` is 3 percent of `subtotal_cents`, rounded half up. Fourth, the discount base is `subtotal_cents` plus `logistics_fee_cents`. Fifth, `volume_discount_cents` is the discount base multiplied by the tier rate, rounded half up, where the `BULK` rate is 0.08, the `STANDARD` rate is 0.05 and the `SMALL` rate is 0.00. Sixth, the batch total is `subtotal_cents` plus `logistics_fee_cents` minus `volume_discount_cents`. Seventh, that total is pinned as `provisional_cost_cents`. Rounding happens at the third and fifth steps only; the sixth step is exact integer arithmetic. The restaurant's `per_meal_price_cents` is never reduced by the tier — the volume discount is a separate negative line on the batch, not a repricing of the meal.

Two worked examples fix the arithmetic. A planner orders 1,000 meals at 900 cents each: `subtotal_cents` is 900,000, which selects `STANDARD`; `logistics_fee_cents` is 3 percent of 900,000, which is 27,000; the discount base is 927,000; `volume_discount_cents` is 5 percent of 927,000, which is 46,350; and `provisional_cost_cents` is 900,000 plus 27,000 minus 46,350, which is 880,650. A second planner orders 100 meals at 500 cents each: `subtotal_cents` is 50,000, which selects `SMALL`; `logistics_fee_cents` is 1,500; the discount base is 51,500; the `SMALL` rate is 0.00 so `volume_discount_cents` is 0; and `provisional_cost_cents` is 51,500. The tier boundaries are exact: a subtotal of 800,000 selects `STANDARD` while 799,999 selects `SMALL`, and 2,000,000 selects `BULK` while 1,999,999 selects `STANDARD`.

Placing a batch has two side effects beyond the batch itself. The restaurant's `version` is incremented by 1, which is precisely why the next planner to place a batch against that kitchen needs a fresh `expected_partner_kitchen_version`. And the activation's `spent_cents` is recomputed, so the funding ceiling check described in 2.11 sees the new commitment immediately.

### 3.5. Quality Gates, Rework and Final Settlement

A placed batch becomes real when production starts. A `ProductionPlanner`, or a `KitchenOperator` acting on its own batch, moves it from `PLACED` to `IN_PRODUCTION`, and the move requires the linked restaurant to be `CONTRACTED` with a `production_status` of `IDLE` or `PRODUCING` — a kitchen that is at capacity or cooling down cannot take on new work. When the batch leaves the line, a `KitchenOperator` moves it from `IN_PRODUCTION` to `QUALITY_HOLD` and reports `produced_meals`, which must be present and not negative. Reporting production sets `produced_meals` on the batch and adds that quantity to the restaurant's `meals_produced_total` and `meals_produced_today`. The batch's `accepted_meals` is derived rather than supplied: it is `produced_meals` minus `rejected_meals`, and it reads 0 while `produced_meals` is still unset.

The quality verdict belongs to a `FoodSafetyOfficer`. A batch in `QUALITY_HOLD` with a `quality_check_score` of at least 80 is released: it moves to `READY`, `ready_at` is stamped, and `rejected_meals` is taken from the request payload, defaulting to 0 when the field is omitted. The `quality_check_score` itself is mandatory on every quality verdict — a move to `READY`, to `REJECTED` or back to `IN_PRODUCTION` that omits it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the supplied score is written to the batch. `accepted_meals` is then recomputed as produced minus rejected, and the restaurant's rolling `quality_score` is recomputed. The supplied `rejected_meals` is not checked against `produced_meals`, so `accepted_meals` is exactly the difference and is not floored at 0. A score between 60 and 79 inclusive does not release the batch and does not reject it — it sends it back. The batch returns from `QUALITY_HOLD` to `IN_PRODUCTION` for rework, which is permitted only while `rework_count` is below 2 — the counter starts at 0 on every new batch — and only when the payload's `quality_check_score` falls in the 60-to-79 band; the move increments `rework_count` by 1 and stores that score, while deliberately leaving `produced_meals` and `rejected_meals` untouched, so the batch is re-produced from the same line state rather than starting over. The rework cycle is `QUALITY_HOLD` to `IN_PRODUCTION` to `QUALITY_HOLD`, and a batch may make that round trip at most twice. A batch whose `quality_check_score` is 59 or below is rejected outright, and so is any batch whose `rework_count` has already reached 2 regardless of its score. Rejection moves the batch from `QUALITY_HOLD` to `REJECTED`, sets `rejected_meals` equal to `produced_meals`, sets `accepted_meals` to 0, recomputes the restaurant's `quality_score`, and recomputes the activation's `spent_cents` so that the failed batch stops counting against the ceiling.

Getting ready meals onto a vehicle is the logistics step. A `LogisticsCoordinator` moves a batch from `READY` to `DISPATCHED` by supplying a `distribution_run_id`; the run must belong to the same activation as the batch and must be in `LOADING` status, and the transition sets `distribution_run_id` on the batch and increases that run's `loaded_meals` by the batch's `accepted_meals`. The last leg is `DISPATCHED` to `DELIVERED`, again driven by a `LogisticsCoordinator`, and it requires the run named by `distribution_run_id` to be in `DELIVERING` status. Delivery stamps `delivered_at`, pins `final_cost_cents` through the final settlement variant described below, recomputes the restaurant's `quality_score`, and recomputes four activation-level figures: `spent_cents`, `delivered_meal_total`, `local_sourcing_pct` and `cost_per_meal_cents`. It also carries a cascade: if the restaurant's `meals_produced_today` has reached its `daily_capacity_meals`, the restaurant's `production_status` is set to `AT_CAPACITY`, which in turn cancels any of its batches still waiting in `PLACED`.

A batch that has not started can be abandoned. A `ProductionPlanner` may move a batch from `PLACED` to `CANCELLED`, supplying a `cancel_reason`; the field is optional, and a batch cancelled without one keeps a null `cancel_reason`. The move recomputes the activation's `spent_cents` so the reservation is released. Batches also arrive in `CANCELLED` indirectly, when their restaurant is terminated or reaches capacity, in which case the `cancel_reason` is `KITCHEN_TERMINATED` or `KITCHEN_AT_CAPACITY` respectively. `DELIVERED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal for a batch.

Settlement has two variants, and the difference between them is the whole point of the quality regime. The provisional variant is pinned once, when the batch is created, and it is simply the batch total produced by the cost buildup in 3.4: `ordered_meals` priced at the restaurant's rate, plus the 3 percent logistics fee, minus the volume discount. It prices the full ordered volume and ignores quality entirely. The final variant is pinned once, when the batch reaches `DELIVERED`, and it prices only what was actually accepted, at the restaurant's raw per-meal price, adjusted by a quality multiplier. The evaluation order is fixed: the billable meal count is resolved first, the base amount second, the multiplier third, and a single rounding last. The billable count is `accepted_meals`. The base amount is that count multiplied by the restaurant's `per_meal_price_cents`. The multiplier is 1.00 when `quality_check_score` is at least 90, 0.95 when the score is between 75 and 89 inclusive, 0.85 when the score is between 60 and 74 inclusive, and 0.00 when the score is 59 or below. `final_cost_cents` is the base amount multiplied by the multiplier, rounded half up once. No intermediate rounding is performed, and the final variant never contains the logistics fee or the volume discount.

Consider a batch of 1,000 meals at 900 cents that produced 1,000 meals, of which 50 were rejected by the quality check, leaving `accepted_meals` of 950, with a `quality_check_score` of 88. The base amount is 950 multiplied by 900, which is 855,000, and the multiplier for a score of 88 is 0.95, so `final_cost_cents` is 812,250. Had the same batch scored 92, the multiplier would be 1.00 and `final_cost_cents` would be 855,000; had it scored 65, the multiplier would be 0.85 and `final_cost_cents` would be 726,750; and had it scored 45, the multiplier would be 0.00 and the batch would be worth nothing at settlement. Because the final variant prices accepted meals only, a batch with any rejected meals settles strictly below an otherwise identical batch with none, and because it carries no fee or discount, a batch that is fully accepted and scores at least 90 still settles below its provisional figure by exactly the logistics fee minus the volume discount. Only batches in `DELIVERED` status contribute their `final_cost_cents` to the activation; batches in `PLACED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `QUALITY_HOLD`, `READY` or `DISPATCHED` contribute their `provisional_cost_cents`, and batches in `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing to either variant.

The restaurant's rolling `quality_score` is what makes quality visible to the partnership team, and it is recomputed whenever one of its batches reaches `READY`, `REJECTED` or `DELIVERED`. The score is a weighted mean over the restaurant's five most recent batches whose status is `DELIVERED` or `REJECTED`; batches in any other status are excluded from the window. The window is ordered by `created_at` descending, and when two batches share a `created_at` the tie is broken by `scheduled_start` ascending and then by `menu_name` ascending — never by `id`. Weights are then assigned from the most recent row backwards as 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1; a restaurant with a shorter history uses only the leading weights, so two batches are weighted 5 and 4. The weighted sum of the batches' `quality_check_score` values is divided by the sum of the weights used, rounded half up, and clamped to the range 0 to 100. A restaurant whose three most recent qualifying batches scored 90, 80 and 70 receives 90 times 5 plus 80 times 4 plus 70 times 3, which is 980, divided by 5 plus 4 plus 3, which is 12, giving 81.67 and a stored `quality_score` of 82. When the window is empty — a restaurant that has never had a batch delivered or rejected — the `quality_score` is left unchanged rather than set to zero, and when a sixth qualifying batch arrives the oldest row drops out of the window and the weights re-normalise across the five that remain.

### 3.6. Local Ingredient Sourcing and Producer Standing

A `SourcingOfficer` registers a local producer with the activation by supplying an `incident_id`, the `producer_name` of the farm, cooperative or food producer, the `locality` — town or district — in which it operates, the `product_categories` it can deliver such as `GRAIN`, `PRODUCE`, `PROTEIN` or `DAIRY`, and a `payment_terms_days` figure for the contractual gap between delivery and cash payment, which defaults to 7 and may not be negative. A new producer starts in `PROSPECT` standing with `cash_flow_received_cents` at zero, both purchase counters at 0, and `reliability_score` at the 50 that the formula below yields for a producer with no settled orders.

A sourcing order is placed against a producer and delivered into a restaurant kitchen. The `SourcingOfficer` supplies the `incident_id`, the `supplier_id` of the producer being paid, the `partner_kitchen_id` of the restaurant receiving the ingredients, an `item_description` of what is being bought, a `quantity_kg` greater than 0, and a `unit_price_cents_per_kg` of at least 1. The order's `amount_cents` is the quantity in kilograms multiplied by the unit price per kilogram, rounded half up, and it is pinned at creation. A new order starts in `REQUESTED` with `requested_at` stamped, and it becomes spend against the activation immediately, because the `spent_cents` aggregation described in 2.11 counts an order only once it reaches `DELIVERED` or `PAID`.

Confirmation is where the producer's standing first matters. Moving an order from `REQUESTED` to `CONFIRMED` is refused only when the linked producer is `SUSPENDED`, and the attempt is then rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_APPROVED`. A producer in `PROSPECT` standing may therefore take a first order: that is exactly how an unknown producer is trialled, and it is the delivery of that first order — not a manual approval — that promotes the producer to `APPROVED` under the standing rules below. A producer that has already been suspended, having fallen below the reliability floor, may not be given new work until its score recovers. The move from `CONFIRMED` to `DELIVERED` has no precondition beyond role and state: it stamps `delivered_at` with the current logical-clock instant and recomputes the producer's `reliability_score`, `delivered_purchase_count` and standing.

Payment is the point of the whole exercise. Moving an order from `DELIVERED` to `PAID` stamps `paid_at`, increases the producer's `cash_flow_received_cents` by the order's `amount_cents`, and recomputes the producer's `reliability_score` and standing. Payment carries the only second-hop cascade in the sourcing flow: if the recomputation lifts the producer into `PREFERRED`, every other order of that producer still sitting in `REQUESTED` is set to `CONFIRMED` automatically, on the reasoning that a producer the operation trusts enough to prefer does not need each of its orders individually cleared. Payment also recomputes the activation's `spent_cents` and `local_sourcing_pct`.

Disputes exist because deliveries go wrong. A `SourcingOfficer` may move an order from `DELIVERED` to `DISPUTED`, supplying a `dispute_reason`; the move recomputes the producer's `reliability_score`, `disputed_purchase_count` and standing. A dispute can be resolved by moving the order from `DISPUTED` back to `DELIVERED`, which clears `dispute_reason` and recomputes the same three values; a dispute that cannot be resolved ends with the order moving from `DISPUTED` to `CANCELLED`, which also recomputes them. Cash already released can be clawed back: moving an order from `PAID` to `DISPUTED` — the compensating action of the payment — decrements the producer's `cash_flow_received_cents` by exactly the order's `amount_cents`, returning that balance to its pre-payment value, and recomputes the producer's standing together with the activation's `spent_cents` and `local_sourcing_pct`. An order that never left the starting line can simply be abandoned: `REQUESTED` to `CANCELLED` recomputes the producer's `reliability_score` and standing. `PAID` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a sourcing order.

Producer standing is derived, never set by hand, and it is recomputed on every transition into `DELIVERED`, `PAID`, `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED`. Two counts feed it. The delivered count is the number of the producer's orders in `DELIVERED` or `PAID`, surfaced as `delivered_purchase_count`. The disputed count is the number of its orders in `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED`, surfaced as `disputed_purchase_count`. Orders still in `REQUESTED` count toward neither. The `reliability_score` is 50, plus 10 for each delivered order, minus 25 for each disputed order, clamped to the range 0 to 100 — so a producer with three settled deliveries and no disputes scores 80, and a producer with one delivery and one dispute scores 35.

Standing itself is resolved from the score and the cash flow in a fixed order, and the order is what makes the outcome unambiguous when several conditions hold at once. First, the producer is `SUSPENDED` whenever `reliability_score` is below 40 — a low score overrides everything else. Second, if the producer is not suspended, it is `PREFERRED` whenever `cash_flow_received_cents` is at least 500,000 and `reliability_score` is at least 70. Third, if neither of those applies, the producer is `PROSPECT` whenever `delivered_purchase_count` is 0 and `disputed_purchase_count` is 0 — an untouched record. Fourth, in every remaining case the producer is `APPROVED`. The state machine follows the same logic: a producer enters `APPROVED` the first time one of its orders reaches `DELIVERED`, rises to `PREFERRED` once it has received at least 500,000 cents in cash and holds a reliability score of at least 70, drops back from `PREFERRED` to `APPROVED` when cash paid falls below 500,000 cents, is suspended from either `APPROVED` or `PREFERRED` when its score falls below 40, and leaves `SUSPENDED` once its score recovers to at least 40 — at which point the resolution order above decides its destination, normally `APPROVED` and `PREFERRED` if the preferred conditions are also met. `Supplier` has no terminal state; a producer's record persists across the whole activation.

### 3.7. Delivery Runs, Stops and Meal Conservation

Meals reach people through receiving sites, and a `LogisticsCoordinator` registers each one with an `incident_id`, a `site_name`, a `site_type` drawn from `SHELTER`, `HOSPITAL`, `NEIGHBORHOOD`, `SCHOOL` or `COMMUNITY_CENTER`, the `population_served` currently relying on the site and the `daily_meal_request` the site asks for, neither of which may be negative, and the `access_constraint` currently facing delivery vehicles. A new site starts in `REGISTERED`. The site accumulates three derived counters as stops are served: `received_meals_total` is the sum of `delivered_meals` over its stops that reached `DELIVERED`, while `skipped_stop_count` and `delivered_stop_count` count its stops in `SKIPPED` and `DELIVERED` status respectively. The site's own service state follows those counters: a `REGISTERED` site becomes `ACTIVE` as soon as its first stop is delivered, and a site in `REGISTERED` or `ACTIVE` is pushed into `SERVICE_ALERT` when its skipped stops strictly outnumber its delivered ones. `SERVICE_ALERT` is recoverable: a later delivered stop that makes the delivered count strictly exceed the skipped count returns the site to `ACTIVE`. When the parent activation closes, any site still in `REGISTERED`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_ALERT` is set to `CLOSED`, which is terminal.

A delivery run is planned against a field kitchen. The `LogisticsCoordinator` supplies the `incident_id`, the `field_kitchen_id` the run loads from, the `driver_name` and `driver_phone` of the volunteer, a `driver_safety_training_completed` boolean, the `vehicle_type` — `CAR`, `VAN`, `TRUCK` or `MOTORCYCLE` — the `planned_date` whose UTC calendar day becomes the run's day bucket, the `window_start` and `window_end` of the delivery window, and the `fuel_cost_cents` and `handling_cost_cents` the run will consume, neither of which may be negative. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; a window that ends at or before it begins is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The driver must have completed the security and food-handling briefing, so a run created with `driver_safety_training_completed` false is rejected with `ERR_DRIVER_SAFETY_TRAINING_REQUIRED`, and the same gate is re-checked when the run tries to leave `PLANNED`. A single field kitchen can only dispatch so much in a day: if it already has 3 runs whose `planned_date` falls in the same UTC calendar day as the new run, creation is rejected with `ERR_KITCHEN_DAILY_RUN_CAP`. A new run starts in `PLANNED` with `loaded_meals` at 0, and its `total_cost_cents` is immediately `fuel_cost_cents` plus `handling_cost_cents` — that sum is the pool that will later be spread across the run's delivered stops. Creating a run also refreshes the origin kitchen's `runs_today_count`.

Stops are added to a planned run by a `LogisticsCoordinator`. Each stop carries a `distribution_run_id`, a `distribution_site_id`, a 1-based `stop_sequence` that orders it inside the run, and a `planned_meals` figure of at least 0 for the handover. Four rules guard the addition. The request must carry `expected_distribution_run_version` matching the run's current `version`, or the add is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The `stop_sequence` must be unique among the run's stops, or the add is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SEQUENCE`. A receiving site may appear at most once among a run's stops, or the add is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOP_SITE`. And the sum of `planned_meals` over the run's stops that are still `PENDING`, counted after the new stop is added, must stay at or below the run's `loaded_meals`, or the add is rejected with `ERR_RUN_LOAD_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY` — a run cannot promise more meals than it is carrying. A stop for a site whose `access_constraint` is `BLOCKED` is rejected with `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_BLOCKED`. A new stop starts in `PENDING` with its `delivered_meals` at 0, and adding it increments the run's `version` by 1, which is what invalidates the next planner's stale `expected_distribution_run_version`.

The run itself moves through six states. Leaving `PLANNED` for `LOADING` requires the origin field kitchen to be `OPERATIONAL` — failure yields `ERR_ORIGIN_KITCHEN_NOT_OPERATIONAL` — and requires the driver's briefing to be complete. That transition accepts an optional `loaded_meals` value: when the field is supplied it is written to the run, and when it is omitted the stored value is left unchanged, which matters because stops may already have been planned against a load figure. Leaving `LOADING` for `EN_ROUTE` requires the request to supply `loaded_meals`, which is applied as part of the transition, and that supplied value must be greater than 0; a `loaded_meals` of 0 or less is refused at rule 1 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` on any move whose `to_state` is `EN_ROUTE`, including the reload from `DELIVERING`. The move also requires that the sum of `planned_meals` over the run's `PENDING` stops is at or below the supplied `loaded_meals`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_RUN_LOAD_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`. The move from `EN_ROUTE` to `DELIVERING` has no precondition beyond role and state — it marks the vehicle as actively working its stops. A run may return to the depot for reload: `DELIVERING` back to `EN_ROUTE` requires that `returned_meals` is greater than 0 and that at least one stop of the run is still `PENDING`, and the reload cycle is `EN_ROUTE` to `DELIVERING` to `EN_ROUTE`. A run completes by moving from `DELIVERING` to `COMPLETED`, which requires every stop of the run to be in `DELIVERED`, `FAILED` or `SKIPPED`; completion stamps `completed_at`, runs the cost allocation described in 3.8, and pins `returned_meals` through the conservation calculation. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

A run can also be abandoned, and abandonment cascades. A `LogisticsCoordinator` may move a run from `PLANNED` to `ABORTED` or from `LOADING` to `ABORTED`, in both cases supplying an `abort_reason` — the field is optional, and a run aborted without one keeps a null `abort_reason`. Either move sets that reason and skips every stop of the run that is still `PENDING`, stamping `failure_reason` as `RUN_ABORTED`. A run may also be aborted by the system rather than by a caller: when the linked field kitchen enters `SUSPENDED`, every run of that kitchen still in `PLANNED` or `LOADING` is moved to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `ORIGIN_KITCHEN_SUSPENDED`, and its pending stops are skipped with `failure_reason` set to `ORIGIN_KITCHEN_SUSPENDED` rather than the generic reason. `ABORTED` is terminal.

Recording what happened at a stop is the last mile of the operation. A `SiteCoordinator` — but only for a stop whose `distribution_site_id` equals its own `X-User-ID` — or a `LogisticsCoordinator` moves a `PENDING` stop to `DELIVERED` and reports the `delivered_meals` actually handed over. Three conditions must hold: the parent run must be in `DELIVERING` status, or the request is rejected with `ERR_RUN_NOT_DELIVERING`; the receiving site's `access_constraint` must not be `BLOCKED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SITE_ACCESS_BLOCKED`; and the reported `delivered_meals` may not exceed the stop's `planned_meals`. A successful handover stamps `delivered_at`, increases the site's `received_meals_total` by the delivered quantity, promotes the site from `REGISTERED` to `ACTIVE` if it had not yet been served, and recomputes the run's `delivered_meals`.

A stop that could not be served can be failed or skipped. A `LogisticsCoordinator` may move a `PENDING` stop to `FAILED`, supplying a `failure_reason` — optional, and stored as null when omitted; this requires the parent run to be in `DELIVERING` and recomputes the run's `returned_meals`. A `PENDING` stop is moved to `SKIPPED` by the system, never by a caller, when its run enters `ABORTED`; the `failure_reason` is `RUN_ABORTED` for a caller-driven abort and `ORIGIN_KITCHEN_SUSPENDED` when the abort came from a kitchen suspension. Skipping carries a second-hop cascade: if the affected site now has strictly more stops in `SKIPPED` than in `DELIVERED`, the site's status is set to `SERVICE_ALERT`, flagging that the site is being missed more often than it is served. `FAILED` and `SKIPPED` are both terminal, with one exception carved out below.

That exception is the compensating reversal. A stop that was recorded as `DELIVERED` can be reversed to `FAILED`, with a `failure_reason` that is optional and stored as null when omitted, as long as the parent run is in `DELIVERING` or `COMPLETED`. The reversal decrements the site's `received_meals_total` by that stop's `delivered_meals`, sets the stop's `delivered_meals` back to 0, and recomputes the run's `delivered_meals` and `returned_meals`. It refreshes the site's counters but does not re-evaluate the site's status, so a reversal on its own never pushes a site into `SERVICE_ALERT`. This is the compensating action of the handover, and it exists so that a handover entered in error — or a load later found to have been mis-recorded — can be corrected after the run has already closed.

Meals are never created or destroyed in this system, and the run is where that invariant lives. At every instant, a run's `loaded_meals` equals its `delivered_meals` plus its `returned_meals` plus the sum of `planned_meals` over its stops that are still `PENDING`; once no stop is pending, the invariant reduces to the simple statement that everything loaded was either delivered or came back. `delivered_meals` is the sum of `delivered_meals` over the run's `DELIVERED` stops, and `returned_meals` is `loaded_meals` minus `delivered_meals` minus the pending planned meals — meals loaded but brought back to the field kitchen. Each of the three stop outcomes moves the quantities in a defined direction. A forward handover of `d` meals increases the run's `delivered_meals` by `d` and the site's `received_meals_total` by `d`, and leaves `loaded_meals` untouched. The compensating reversal of that handover decreases the run's `delivered_meals` by `d`, decreases the site's `received_meals_total` by `d`, and increases `returned_meals` by `d`, again leaving `loaded_meals` untouched. Failing a pending stop leaves `loaded_meals` untouched and raises `returned_meals` by that stop's `planned_meals`, because the pending planned quantity drops out of the third term of the invariant. `loaded_meals` is the conserved quantity: it must be identical before and after a forward-and-reversal pair, and no stop outcome ever changes it.

### 3.8. Run Cost Allocation, Wind-Down and the Donor Accountability Report

Fuel and handling are spent on the whole run, but donors want them attributed to the sites that were actually served. When a run completes, its `total_cost_cents` — the sum of `fuel_cost_cents` and `handling_cost_cents` fixed at creation — is spread across the run's `DELIVERED` stops in proportion to the meals each one handed over. Stops in `PENDING`, `FAILED` or `SKIPPED` status receive `allocated_cost_cents` of 0 and take no part in the allocation. The allocation proceeds in `stop_sequence` ascending order: each eligible stop's share is the run's total cost multiplied by that stop's `delivered_meals` and divided by the total delivered meals across all eligible stops, rounded half up. Each share is rounded independently and the rounded shares are then summed; the shares are never summed unrounded and rounded once at the end. The difference between the run's total cost and the sum of the rounded shares is the residual, and it is added to the eligible stop with the lowest `stop_sequence`, so the allocated shares always sum exactly to the run's total cost and `unallocated_cost_cents` is 0. There is one boundary case: when the total delivered meals across eligible stops is 0, every eligible stop is allocated 0 and the entire cost pool is left in `unallocated_cost_cents` rather than being divided by zero or discarded.

A worked example makes the residual rule concrete. A run has `fuel_cost_cents` of 6,000 and `handling_cost_cents` of 4,000, so its `total_cost_cents` is 10,000. Three of its stops were delivered, each with `delivered_meals` of 1, at sequences 1, 2 and 3. The first two stops each receive 10,000 multiplied by 1 and divided by 3, which is 3,333.33 and rounds to 3,333; the third receives the same 3,333. The rounded shares sum to 9,999, leaving a residual of 1, which is added to the eligible stop with the lowest `stop_sequence` — sequence 1 — giving it 3,334. The final allocations are 3,334, 3,333 and 3,333, and `unallocated_cost_cents` is 0. A second example shows an uneven split: a run with a total cost of 15,000 whose three delivered stops handed over 200, 300 and 500 meals out of 1,000 receives shares of 3,000, 4,500 and 7,500 with no residual at all.

Wind-down and closure ride on the same accounting. Moving the activation into `WINDING_DOWN` requires that no batch of the activation is in `IN_PRODUCTION` or `QUALITY_HOLD`, and it demobilizes every field kitchen still in `SITED` or `UTILITIES_PENDING`. Closing the activation requires every field kitchen to be `DEMOBILIZED` and every delivery run to be `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED`, and it closes the activation's receiving sites and its allocated or partially disbursed commitments in one hop. Once closed, the activation is frozen and the only remaining activity is reporting.

Donor commitments fund the operation, and a `DonorLiaison` records each one with an `incident_id`, the `donor_name`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and an `earmark` describing the spending restriction attached to the money — `UNRESTRICTED`, `FIELD_KITCHENS`, `RESTAURANT_ACTIVATION`, `INGREDIENT_SOURCING` or `LOGISTICS`. A new commitment starts in `PLEDGED`. A pledge becomes real money when it moves to `RECEIVED`, which stamps `received_at` and recomputes the activation's `committed_cents`; a pledge that never arrives can be moved to `WITHDRAWN` instead, but only while `received_at` is still null, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Received money is allocated, then drawn down. Moving a commitment from `RECEIVED` to `ALLOCATED` requires the parent activation to be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; allocation sets `allocated_cents` equal to the commitment's `amount_cents` and recomputes `committed_cents`. Allocation can be undone while nothing has been spent: moving from `ALLOCATED` back to `RECEIVED` requires `disbursed_cents` to be 0, and it resets `allocated_cents` to 0 and recomputes `committed_cents`, so the `RECEIVED` and `ALLOCATED` pair forms a re-allocation cycle. Drawing money down moves a commitment from `ALLOCATED` to `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED`, supplying a `disbursement_cents` of at least 1 and satisfying the cap that `disbursed_cents` plus `disbursement_cents` does not exceed `amount_cents`; the successful move increases `disbursed_cents` by the disbursed amount. Further drawdowns repeat the same move from `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` to `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` under the same two guards, and an over-draw is rejected with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT` (HTTP 422). A commitment can only be closed once its money is fully out the door: moving from `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` to `CLOSED` requires `disbursed_cents` to equal `amount_cents`. Commitments still `ALLOCATED` or `PARTIALLY_DISBURSED` when the activation closes are closed by the system as part of the closure cascade. Throughout, `remaining_cents` is `amount_cents` minus `disbursed_cents`, the conserved balance that tells a liaison how much of the commitment is still available. `CLOSED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

The accountability report is the read that ties meals to money, and it is available to a `PlatformAdmin`, a `DonorLiaison` or a `FieldCoordinator`. It returns the activation's `incident_id`, its `local_sourcing_pct`, its `cost_per_meal_cents`, its `delivered_meal_total`, its `committed_cents`, its `spent_cents`, and a `donor_report_flag`. Three quantities are recomputed on every read. The local-sourcing numerator is the sum of `amount_cents` over the activation's sourcing orders in `DELIVERED` or `PAID` status; orders still in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and orders in `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED`, are excluded, because money that has not reached a producer does not count as having reached the local economy. The denominator is the sum of `final_cost_cents` over the activation's batches in `DELIVERED` status, and `delivered_meal_total` is the sum of `accepted_meals` over those same batches; batches in any other status are excluded from both. `local_sourcing_pct` is the numerator multiplied by 100 and divided by the denominator, rounded half up, or 0 when the denominator is 0. `cost_per_meal_cents` is the denominator divided by `delivered_meal_total`, rounded half up, or 0 when `delivered_meal_total` is 0. The `donor_report_flag` is `BELOW_LOCAL_SOURCING_TARGET` when `local_sourcing_pct` is below 30, and `ON_TARGET` otherwise; the 30 percent figure is the share of delivered-meal spend the operation expects to be matched by local supplier purchases.

One worked read: an activation has taken delivery of sourcing orders totalling 450,000 cents and has delivered batches whose `final_cost_cents` sum to 1,200,000 cents across 1,400 accepted meals. The numerator is 450,000 and the denominator is 1,200,000, so `local_sourcing_pct` is 450,000 multiplied by 100 and divided by 1,200,000, which is 37.5 and rounds half up to 38 — comfortably `ON_TARGET`. `cost_per_meal_cents` is 1,200,000 divided by 1,400, which is 857.14 and rounds to 857 cents per delivered meal, and `delivered_meal_total` is 1,400. Had the same activation taken delivery of only 300,000 cents of local produce, `local_sourcing_pct` would be 25 and the flag would read `BELOW_LOCAL_SOURCING_TARGET`.
