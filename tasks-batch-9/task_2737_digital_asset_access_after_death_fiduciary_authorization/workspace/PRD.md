# Digital Estate Fiduciary Access API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a person dies, the paperwork that once settled an estate — a death certificate, a court's letters of authority, a bank signature card — no longer reaches most of what they owned. The decedent's financial account portal, their mailbox, the photographs in cloud storage, a dozen recurring subscriptions still billing a dead card, an airline loyalty balance and a social profile are each held by a different provider under a different contract, and each provider asks a different question before it will speak to the executor. Fiduciaries lose months to that fragmentation, and the legal answers are genuinely subtle: an online tool designation the decedent clicked through on the provider's own website outranks the will; the will outranks the state's default statutory rule; and even a directive that plainly grants everything does not, by itself, entitle the fiduciary to read the *content* of communications when the provider's policy demands a court order first.

This document specifies the **Digital Estate Fiduciary Access API**, a back-office service that turns that mess into an auditable record. The service holds the estate file, the appointed fiduciaries and the evidentiary documents; it holds an inventory of every digital account the decedent controlled; it resolves, deterministically, exactly what scope of access each provider owes the fiduciary and on what legal basis; it carries the request package through the provider's own review cycle; it records what the fiduciary elected to do with each account — memorialize it, delete it, or transfer it to a named beneficiary; it chases cancellations and refunds on recurring subscriptions; and it appraises what is actually transferable so the estate tax position and the distribution schedule can be locked before the file is closed.

Three properties dominate the design. First, **authority is derived, never asserted**: no operator may type in "this fiduciary may read the mail" — the scope is computed from the recorded instruments and the provider's policy, frozen at decision time, and revocable. Second, **the record is reconcilable**: every aggregate on the estate is a function of its children, so the value distributed to beneficiaries plus the value still undistributed always equals the appraised gross, before and after any reversal. Third, **duties are segregated**: the court registry verifies and revokes paperwork but never touches money; the provider decides but never elects; the fiduciary elects but cannot countersign its own irreversible deletion. The remainder of this document specifies the contracts that make those three properties enforceable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before the body is parsed and before any path parameter is resolved. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the four recognised roles — `Fiduciary`, `PlatformCompliance`, `CourtRegistrar`, `EstateAdmin` — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401: an unrecognised role is treated as an authentication failure, not as a permission failure. A recognised role that is not listed among the target endpoint's authorized roles is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

A **`Fiduciary`** is the estate's executor, administrator or trustee. This role owns the inventory, the request packages, the elections, the cancellations, the refund pursuit, the valuation and the closure. When `X-User-Role` is `Fiduciary`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Fiduciary` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Fiduciary` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and the acting fiduciary's own `estate_id` must equal the estate that owns the target resource — a fiduciary of one estate acting on another estate's resource is rejected with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ESTATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

A **`PlatformCompliance`** principal is a service provider's death-administration desk. It decides the requests addressed to its own platform, executes, rejects or reverses dispositions on its own platform's accounts, confirms or fails cancellations, and settles refund claims. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformCompliance`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Platform`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the `platform_id` of the target resource — the `platform_id` of the `PlatformRequest` itself, or the `platform_id` of the `DigitalAsset` behind a disposition election, a cancellation order or a refund claim. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PLATFORM_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). A provider never files paperwork on the estate's behalf: every creation endpoint is closed to this role.

A **`CourtRegistrar`** is the probate registry. It appoints fiduciaries, verifies and revokes the documents that confer authority, records will and trust provisions, and may revoke a live authority grant. When `X-User-Role` is `CourtRegistrar`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque registry-operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — as `verified_by_user_id` on the `Document` a registrar verifies — but never compares it to any entity's `id` and never resolves it as a foreign key. This role never touches inventory, valuation or money.

An **`EstateAdmin`** is the operator of this administration service itself. It registers providers, opens estate files, drives the logical clock and runs the grant-expiry sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `EstateAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and never resolved as a foreign key. `EstateAdmin` has no authority over any individual estate's substantive decisions and may not submit any transition action of any kind.

Beyond endpoint-level permission there is a second, finer gate. Every state-changing action is submitted to a transitions endpoint as an `action` value, and each action belongs to exactly one role. `Fiduciary` owns `SUBMIT`, `RESUBMIT` and `WITHDRAW` on a platform request; `COUNTERSIGN` and `SUBMIT` on a disposition election; `REQUEST` on a cancellation order; `ESCALATE` on a refund claim; and `FINALIZE` on a valuation run. `PlatformCompliance` owns `START_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_INFO` and `DECIDE` on a platform request; `EXECUTE`, `REJECT` and `REVERSE` on a disposition election; `CONFIRM` and `FAIL` on a cancellation order; and `START_REVIEW`, `CREDIT`, `DENY` and `CHARGE_BACK` on a refund claim. `CourtRegistrar` owns `VERIFY` and `REVOKE` on a document and `REVOKE` on an authority grant. A caller whose role is authorized for the endpoint but does not own the submitted action — a `Fiduciary` submitting `DECIDE`, or a `PlatformCompliance` desk submitting `COUNTERSIGN` — is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

All read endpoints are open to all four roles with five exceptions, all of which exclude `PlatformCompliance`: the estate collection listing, the authority-directive listings and single reads, the beneficiary-designation listings and single reads, the valuation-run listings and single reads, and the authority-resolution projection. A provider's desk sees the traffic it must act on, not the estate's private disposition plan.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sorting and Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination parameters as query-string values. `limit` controls how many records are returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds — a `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer for either — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, oldest record first. This ordering is not cosmetic: several rules in this specification — the tie-break among competing authority directives, the order in which covered accounts are disclosed, the order in which grants are expired, and the rank order in which leftover allocation cents are handed out — depend on the same oldest-first convention, so a client paging a collection sees records in the exact order the server processes them.

For a list response, the `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective offset actually applied), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond this page). Single-resource responses omit all four.

Collections also accept domain filters, all optional and all combinable with pagination. Platforms may be filtered by `platform_type`, `status` and `refund_policy`. Estates may be filtered by `administration_phase` and `domicile_state`. Fiduciaries may be filtered by `estate_id`, `letters_status` and `appointment_type`. Documents may be filtered by `estate_id`, `fiduciary_id`, `doc_type` and `verification_status`. Authority directives may be filtered by `estate_id`, `platform_id`, `directive_type`, `status` and `asset_category`. Digital assets may be filtered by `estate_id`, `platform_id`, `category`, `access_status`, `disposition_status` and `transferability`. Subscription terms may be filtered by `asset_id`, `state` and `billing_interval`. Platform requests may be filtered by `estate_id`, `platform_id`, `status`, `request_type`, `death_certificate_document_id` and `letters_document_id`. Authority grants may be filtered by `estate_id`, `platform_id`, `platform_request_id`, `status`, `granted_scope` and `basis`. Beneficiary designations may be filtered by `estate_id` and `source`. Disposition elections may be filtered by `asset_id`, `beneficiary_designation_id`, `elected_by_fiduciary_id`, `countersigned_by_fiduciary_id`, `election` and `status`. Cancellation orders may be filtered by `asset_id`, `status` and `refund_policy_snapshot`. Refund claims may be filtered by `cancellation_order_id`, `status` and `method`. Valuation runs may be filtered by `estate_id`, `basis` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Record Versioning

Every persisted resource in this service — estates, fiduciaries, documents, platforms, authority directives, digital assets, subscription terms, platform requests, authority grants, beneficiary designations, disposition elections, cancellation orders, refund claims and valuation runs alike — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never client-supplied. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the record was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, rendered identically, and equals `created_at` on a freshly created record. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. There are no exceptions: no entity in this service omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Because most operations here mutate more than one record, the rule is stated once and applies throughout: whenever a rule in §3 says that an operation changes a field on some record, that record's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant — and this is true of every record the operation touches, not only the one named in the URL. An approval that disclosures four accounts, opens a grant and rewrites the estate's counters advances the `version` of the request, the new grant, all four accounts and the estate.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

This service does not read the host machine's wall clock. A single **logical clock** is the sole source of "now" for every timestamp, every review window, every expiry comparison and every date comparison in this API. A fresh server starts its clock at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the clock never advances on its own; only an `EstateAdmin` moves it, by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock re-stamps nothing retroactively — every `created_at`, `updated_at` and event stamp written after the move uses the new value, and previously written stamps are left alone. Moving the clock also does not by itself expire anything: an authority grant whose term has run out remains `ACTIVE` in the record until an expiry sweep is run against its estate.

Date-only fields (`date_of_death`, `issued_on`, `executed_on`, `term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `effective_date`, `claimed_period_start`, `claimed_period_end`, `alternate_valuation_date`, `cancelled_effective_date`) are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. When one of them is compared against the logical clock, it is the clock's *calendar date* that is compared. Day counts over a date range in this specification are **inclusive of both endpoints**: a range that starts and ends on the same day counts 1 day, and a range from the 1st to the 30th of a 30-day month counts 30 days. Adding a whole number of calendar months to a date advances the month and keeps the day-of-month, except that when the target month has no such day the last day of the target month is used instead — so six months after 31 August is the last day of February.

### 2.6. Flat Addressing and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Every entity in this service is addressed at its own top-level collection. There is no deep URL nesting: a digital asset is read at `/digital-assets/{asset_id}` regardless of which estate owns it, and a refund claim at `/refund-claims/{claim_id}` regardless of which order and term it hangs from. Parent-child relationships travel in foreign-key fields on the record and are navigable through the collection filters listed in §2.3, never through the URL. The only nested paths in the API are creation endpoints, where the parent must be unambiguous — a digital asset is created beneath its estate, a subscription term beneath its asset, a refund claim beneath its cancellation order.

Path parameters are resolved outer-to-inner, left-to-right, and each is resolved before the request body is examined. A request naming a non-existent estate fails with `ERR_ESTATE_NOT_FOUND` before anything in the body is looked at; a request naming a real order but a non-existent claim fails on the claim. Identifiers supplied *inside* the body are resolved only after every path parameter has resolved successfully, and among themselves in a defined order: on a platform-request draft, `death_certificate_document_id` is resolved before `letters_document_id`, so if both are wrong the error names the death certificate's failure.

### 2.7. The Order of Rejection

Several checks can fail on the same request, and clients depend on being told *which* one failed. The server therefore evaluates its validations in one strict, globally ordered ladder and returns on the **first** violation it finds; no later check runs, and no later error can mask an earlier one. The ladder is:

1. **Authentication and request shape.** Header presence (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401); role recognisability (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, 401); endpoint-level role permission (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403); body well-formedness on every write endpoint — parseable JSON, every required field present, no unknown field that would change behaviour, and every field matching its declared type, enum membership and range, including `share_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive, cents fields being non-negative integers, and dates and datetimes being valid ISO 8601 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400); and finally action-level role ownership on every transitions endpoint (`ERR_ACTION_ROLE_MISMATCH`, 403).
2. **Path-parameter existence**, in the fixed sequence estate, fiduciary, document, platform, directive, asset, subscription term, platform request, authority grant, beneficiary designation, disposition election, cancellation order, refund claim, valuation run — raising respectively `ERR_ESTATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIDUCIARY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESIGNATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_VALUATION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.
3. **Body-reference existence**, in this order: a `platform_id` in the body (`ERR_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`), a `fiduciary_id` in the body (`ERR_FIDUCIARY_NOT_FOUND`), then `death_certificate_document_id` and `letters_document_id` in that order (`ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`), then `beneficiary_designation_id` (`ERR_DESIGNATION_NOT_FOUND`) — all HTTP 404.
4. **Tenancy and scope.** The fiduciary-to-estate check (`ERR_FIDUCIARY_ESTATE_MISMATCH`, 403) is evaluated first, then the provider-to-platform check (`ERR_PLATFORM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, 403).
5. **Optimistic concurrency.** The estate-version check (`ERR_STALE_ESTATE_VERSION`, 412) then the asset-version check (`ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION`, 412). These sit above every state check, so a stale version supplied against a closed estate still returns 412 rather than a conflict.
6. **Entity state.** Estate closure first (`ERR_ESTATE_CLOSED`, 409), then whether the target record is in a state from which the submitted action is defined, per entity in this order: document (`ERR_INVALID_DOCUMENT_STATE`), platform request (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE`), authority grant (`ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`), disposition election (`ERR_INVALID_ELECTION_STATE`), cancellation order (`ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`), refund claim (`ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`), valuation run (`ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`) — all HTTP 409 — and lastly the once-per-estate finalization guard (`ERR_VALUATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED`, 409).
7. **Referential consistency**, in order: documents belonging to the same estate as the request that cites them (`ERR_DOCUMENT_ESTATE_MISMATCH`, 409); the two cited documents being of the right types (`ERR_DOCUMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, 400); and the cited provider form code matching the provider's own (`ERR_PLATFORM_FORM_MISMATCH`, 409).
8. **Business safety**, in order: `ERR_DEATH_CERTIFICATE_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_LETTERS_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_PLATFORM_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AUTHORITY`, `ERR_CONTENT_DISCLOSURE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_COURT_ORDER_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ONLINE_TOOL_UNSUPPORTED`, `ERR_COUNTERSIGN_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_TRANSFERABLE`, `ERR_DISPOSITION_UNSUPPORTED`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_MISSING`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_EXISTS`, `ERR_REFUND_NOT_OFFERED`.
9. **Business temporal**, in order: `ERR_DATE_OF_DEATH_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_BEFORE_DEATH`, `ERR_ALTERNATE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_REQUEST_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM`, `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
10. **Fiscal and aggregate**, in order: `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`, `ERR_SHARE_TOTAL_INVALID`, `ERR_VALUATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_ASSETS_PENDING_DISPOSITION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_HANDLE`, `ERR_INFO_CYCLE_LIMIT`, `ERR_OPEN_CANCELLATIONS`, `ERR_OPEN_REFUND_CLAIMS`.

Three consequences of this ladder are worth naming explicitly, because they are the cases where two plausible errors compete. Filing a refund claim against a cancellation order that has not been confirmed returns `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, never `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM`, because the order-state check sits in band 6 and the claimed-period check in band 9 — even when the claimed period is also out of term. A submission that both cites the wrong provider form code and lacks content authority returns `ERR_PLATFORM_FORM_MISMATCH`, never `ERR_CONTENT_DISCLOSURE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, because the form must match before the content-authority question is even asked. And a claim whose period is out of term *and* shorter than the minimum claimable stretch returns `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM`, never `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`.

The estate-closure guard at band 6 deserves its own note because of how broadly it applies. Once an estate's `administration_phase` is `CLOSED`, every write whose target belongs to that estate is rejected with `ERR_ESTATE_CLOSED`: appointing a fiduciary, filing a document, verifying or revoking a document, revoking a grant, recording a directive, adding an asset, re-reviewing transferability, recording a subscription term, drafting or transitioning a platform request, electing or transitioning a disposition, transitioning a cancellation order, filing or transitioning a refund claim, recording a beneficiary designation, creating or finalizing a valuation run, running an expiry sweep, and closing the estate a second time. Only reads survive closure. Provider registration and provider policy updates are not estate-owned and are therefore unaffected.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this service rewrite state that a client must have observed before it can safely act, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the **parent** record's `version`.

A `POST /estates/{estate_id}/valuation-runs` request must carry `expected_estate_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of that estate. If the supplied value does not equal the estate's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ESTATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new run legitimately supersedes the prior computed run and rewrites every mirrored valuation field on the estate as well as every beneficiary's planned allocation; a client working from a stale estate view would otherwise overwrite a roll-up computed from an inventory it never saw.

A `POST /digital-assets/{asset_id}/disposition-elections` request must likewise carry `expected_asset_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of that asset. If it does not equal the asset's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the guard protects an election in flight: creating a new election supersedes the asset's prior non-terminal election, so without the check a client could destroy an election recorded against an asset state — a transferability class, an appraised value, an access posture — that it never observed.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, transitions endpoints, provider policy updates, document filing and closure carry no expected-version input, and supplying one is an unknown field that fails body validation.

### 2.9. Money, Basis Points, Counts and Rounding Discipline

All money in this service is expressed as an **integer number of cents** in a field whose name ends in `_cents`. There are no decimal money values anywhere in the API, no currency codes and no floating-point amounts; a client that wants dollars divides by 100 for display only. Every cents field supplied by a client must be a non-negative integer.

Fractional shares are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — in the `share_bp` field, where 10000 basis points is the whole estate. An individual share must be between 1 and 10000 inclusive, and the shares recorded against one estate must sum to exactly 10000 before the estate can be valued.

Rounding happens in exactly three places, and everywhere else the arithmetic is exact integer addition, subtraction, comparison, minimum, maximum or count. First, when an appraisal applies the alternate-date decay factor, the single multiplication is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Second, when a refund entitlement divides the term fee across claimed days, that single division is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Third, when the distributable pool is split across beneficiaries by basis-point share, each beneficiary's exact share is **floored** to a whole cent and the leftover cents are then distributed one at a time — this is a deliberate departure from half-up rounding and is specified in full in §3.8. Rounding is never applied twice to the same quantity, and an intermediate sum is never rounded before the final step it feeds.

Counters — the estate's inventory and disposition tallies, a run's included-asset count, a term's claimed-day total, a request's information-cycle count and a designation's allocation rank — are non-negative integers with no rounding at all.

### 2.10. Cascades, Derived Counters and Ledger Invariants

The estate record is the reconciliation point of the whole system, and almost nothing on it is client-supplied. Only `decedent_name`, `date_of_death`, `domicile_state`, `court_case_number` and, at closure, `closing_statement` come from a client. Everything else is derived and rewritten by the server whenever a child record changes: `alternate_valuation_date`, `administration_phase`, `inventory_total_count`, `disclosed_asset_count`, `assets_pending_disposition_count`, `assets_disposed_count`, `open_cancellation_count`, `cancelled_subscription_count`, `valuation_basis`, `gross_digital_estate_cents`, `admin_deduction_cents`, `taxable_digital_estate_cents`, `undistributed_value_cents`, `refund_recovered_cents` and `closed_at`.

The derived counters are always recomputed from the child rows rather than incremented blindly. `inventory_total_count` is the number of digital assets on the estate. `disclosed_asset_count` is the number of those whose `access_status` is `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED`. `assets_pending_disposition_count` is the number whose `disposition_status` is `PENDING` or `ELECTED`. `assets_disposed_count` is the number whose `disposition_status` is `EXECUTED` or `PURGED`. `open_cancellation_count` is the number of cancellation orders across the estate's assets whose `status` is `PENDING` or `REQUESTED`. `cancelled_subscription_count` is the number of subscription terms across the estate's assets whose `state` is `CANCELLED`. `refund_recovered_cents` is the sum of `credited_cents` over the estate's refund claims whose `status` is `CREDITED`. Because every asset's disposition posture is either pending-or-elected or disposed, `inventory_total_count` always equals `assets_pending_disposition_count` plus `assets_disposed_count`.

Two ledger invariants must hold after every operation, including after every compensating one. The appraised gross must always split cleanly between what has vested and what has not: `gross_digital_estate_cents` equals `undistributed_value_cents` plus the sum of `received_value_cents` across the estate's beneficiary designations. And the recovered-refund mirror must always match its source: `refund_recovered_cents` equals the sum of `credited_cents` over exactly those claims whose `status` is `CREDITED`. A reversal of a transfer, or a chargeback of a credit, restores both invariants to the exact cent.

Cascades run **transitively and in `created_at` ascending order at every level**. When one operation causes a second record to change and that change in turn causes a third, all three changes belong to the same request and all three records advance their `version`. Where a cascade touches a set of sibling records — the accounts covered by a grant, the grants held by an estate — the set is processed oldest-first. §3 names each cascade at the point in the workflow where it fires, and each one ends the same way: the estate's derived counters are recomputed and its `administration_phase` is re-derived from those counters.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Providers and Opening the Estate File

Before any estate can be administered, the providers it will deal with must exist in the register, and only an `EstateAdmin` may put them there. A **`Platform`** record is the machine-readable form of one provider's death-administration policy. It carries a `name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters) and a `platform_type` describing the provider's primary business line, which is one of `FINANCIAL`, `EMAIL`, `CLOUD_STORAGE`, `SUBSCRIPTION`, `LOYALTY` or `SOCIAL`. Its operational posture is the `status` field, one of `PARTICIPATING`, `LIMITED` or `SUSPENDED`; `status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `PARTICIPATING` when omitted. Five booleans record what the provider is willing to do: `supports_online_tool` (whether it offers an online tool designation capable of overriding a will), `supports_transfer` (whether it will hand an account to a beneficiary), `supports_memorialization`, `supports_deletion`, and `requires_court_order_for_content` (whether reading content, as opposed to the catalog, needs a verified court order). A sixth boolean, `license_terminates_at_death`, records whether the provider's licence grant simply ends when the account holder dies.

The remaining policy fields are operational parameters. `review_window_days` is the length, in whole days, of the review window that a submitted request occupies with this provider, and must be at least 1 and at most 90. `form_code` is the provider's own decedent-request form identifier (non-empty, at most 40 characters), which a fiduciary's request package must cite verbatim. `refund_policy` selects which refund algorithm this provider applies to a cancelled subscription term, and is one of `PRORATA_DAILY`, `UNUSED_PERIOD` or `NONE`. `transfer_fee_cents` is a flat, non-negative fee the provider deducts from a transferred account's appraised value. `request_fee_cents` is a non-negative administrative fee charged per approved request package, which later feeds the estate's tax deduction.

A provider's policy is not frozen. An `EstateAdmin` may update any of `status`, `refund_policy`, `supports_online_tool`, `supports_transfer`, `supports_memorialization`, `supports_deletion`, `requires_court_order_for_content`, `license_terminates_at_death`, `review_window_days`, `transfer_fee_cents`, `request_fee_cents` and `form_code`, supplying only the fields that change. Such an update is deliberately **not** retroactive: it does not rewrite any cancellation order's `refund_policy_snapshot`, does not touch the resolved scope already frozen onto a decided request, and does not reclassify any account that has already been classified. Reclassifying an account after a policy change is a separate, explicit act described in §3.4.

Opening the estate file is also an `EstateAdmin` act. The **`Estate`** is created from four supplied values: `decedent_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), `date_of_death`, `domicile_state` (exactly two uppercase letters, naming the jurisdiction whose default statutory rules apply), and `court_case_number` (non-empty, at most 60 characters, the probate identifier printed on the letters of authority). The date of death may not be later than the logical clock's current calendar date; a date equal to today is accepted, while a date in the future is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OF_DEATH_IN_FUTURE`. On creation the server computes `alternate_valuation_date` as the date of death advanced by exactly six calendar months, using the last-day-of-month clamp described in §2.5; this date gates the alternate valuation basis and is never client-supplied. The new estate opens with `administration_phase` set to `INTAKE`, every counter at 0, every money mirror at 0, `valuation_basis` null, and `closing_statement` and `closed_at` both null.

### 3.2. Appointing Fiduciaries, the Evidentiary Record, and the Administration Phase

Only the `CourtRegistrar` appoints a **`Fiduciary`** to an estate. The appointment records the fiduciary's `full_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), an `appointment_type` of `EXECUTOR`, `ADMINISTRATOR` or `TRUSTEE` — the nature of the appointment, which governs which instrument may supply the fiduciary's authority — and a `court_reference` (non-empty, at most 80 characters) naming the court order or trust article that appoints them. The appointment also carries the `estate_id` of the estate it is made over. An appointment confers nothing on its own: `letters_status` starts at `PENDING` and stays there until paperwork proves otherwise.

Evidentiary documents may be filed by either a `CourtRegistrar` or a `Fiduciary` of the estate. A **`Document`** carries a `doc_type` of `DEATH_CERTIFICATE`, `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY` or `COURT_ORDER`; a `reference_number` (non-empty, at most 60 characters) as issued by the issuing authority; an `issued_on` date; and a `jurisdiction` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the issuing authority's state. A `fiduciary_id` may also be supplied, and its treatment is asymmetric by design: it is **required** when `doc_type` is `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY`, naming the fiduciary the letters appoint, and must be **absent** for both `DEATH_CERTIFICATE` and `COURT_ORDER`. Getting that wrong fails body validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; naming a fiduciary that does not exist fails with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_NOT_FOUND`, and naming one belonging to a different estate fails with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ESTATE_MISMATCH`. Every document is filed with `verification_status` at `PENDING`, and with `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_at` and `revocation_reason` all null.

Verification is the registrar's exclusive act. Verifying a `PENDING` document moves its `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, writes the acting registrar's opaque `X-User-ID` into `verified_by_user_id` for audit, and stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock. Verifying a document that is already `VERIFIED`, or one that has been `REVOKED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DOCUMENT_STATE`. When the document being verified is a `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY`, verification also reaches into the fiduciary it names and moves that fiduciary's `letters_status` from `PENDING` to `ISSUED`; that change in turn causes the estate's `administration_phase` to be re-derived. All three records — the document, the fiduciary and the estate — advance their `version`.

Revocation is the mirror image, and it is where the deepest cascade in this system begins; §3.6 traces it in full. A document may be revoked from either `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`, and `REVOKED` is terminal. Both paths store the supplied `revocation_reason`, but they differ sharply in consequence: revoking a document that was still `PENDING` has no downstream effect at all, because a pending document never conferred anything, and only that document's `version` moves. Revoking a `VERIFIED` `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY` sets the named fiduciary's `letters_status` to `REVOKED` and then propagates. Both verification and revocation are refused on a closed estate with `ERR_ESTATE_CLOSED`.

The estate's `administration_phase` is never written by a client. It is re-derived from the estate's children after any document verification change, any fiduciary letters change, any authority-grant status change, any change to an account's access or disposition posture, any asset creation, and any cancellation confirmation. The derivation walks five clauses and **stops at the first one that matches**. If `closed_at` is not null the phase is `CLOSED`, which makes closure sticky and suppresses every later derivation. Otherwise, if the estate has no `DEATH_CERTIFICATE` document in `VERIFIED` status, or no fiduciary at all with `letters_status` equal to `ISSUED`, the phase is `INTAKE`. Otherwise, if `disclosed_asset_count` is 0, the phase is `AUTHORIZED`. Otherwise, if `inventory_total_count` is 0, or `assets_disposed_count` is strictly less than `inventory_total_count`, the phase is `DISCLOSURE`. Otherwise the phase is `WIND_DOWN`.

Because the clauses are ordered and the inputs are all derived, the phase is not monotone. `INTAKE → AUTHORIZED` happens the moment both a verified death certificate and an issued set of letters exist while nothing has yet been disclosed. `AUTHORIZED → DISCLOSURE` follows the first disclosure, once `disclosed_asset_count` is at least 1 and `assets_disposed_count` is still below `inventory_total_count`. `DISCLOSURE → WIND_DOWN` follows the disposition that makes `assets_disposed_count` equal `inventory_total_count`, with at least one asset in inventory. The reverse edges are equally real: a reversal or rejection that returns an account to `PENDING` drops the estate back `WIND_DOWN → DISCLOSURE`, and an authority collapse walks it back `AUTHORIZED → INTAKE` or `DISCLOSURE → INTAKE` as soon as no fiduciary holds issued letters. Every one of these re-derivations advances the estate's `version`.

### 3.3. Recording Authority Directives and Resolving the Precedence Ladder

The legal question at the heart of this service is *what did the decedent actually authorize, and which instrument wins when two of them disagree*. Both a `CourtRegistrar` and a `Fiduciary` of the estate may record an **`AuthorityDirective`**, which is one instrument's instruction about disclosure. A directive names its `directive_type` — `ONLINE_TOOL` for a designation the decedent made on a provider's own website, `WILL_PROVISION` for an article of the will, or `TRUST_PROVISION` for a section of a trust — and the `scope` that instrument grants, one of `NONE`, `CATALOG_ONLY` or `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT`. It carries an `instrument_reference`, which is required and must be non-empty and at most 80 characters: the online-tool screen name, the will article number, or the trust section that carries the instruction. It carries an `executed_on` date recording when the decedent executed it, and the `estate_id` of the estate whose decedent executed it.

A directive's reach is set by two optional bindings. A `platform_id` binds it to one provider; leaving it null makes the directive instrument-wide across every provider. An `asset_category` — one of `FINANCIAL_ACCOUNT`, `EMAIL`, `CLOUD_STORAGE`, `SUBSCRIPTION`, `LOYALTY_PROGRAM` or `SOCIAL_PROFILE` — limits it to one class of account; leaving it null makes it apply to every category. A directive with both bindings null is a catch-all.

Two server-assigned fields complete the record. `precedence_rank` is never client-supplied: it is 1 for an `ONLINE_TOOL` directive and 2 for both `WILL_PROVISION` and `TRUST_PROVISION`, and a lower rank wins. `status` opens at `RECORDED`. Recording a newer directive that matches an existing one on all four of estate, `directive_type`, `platform_id` and `asset_category` flips the older one to `SUPERSEDED`, so an instrument can be corrected without deleting history; a `SUPERSEDED` directive never participates in resolution again. Recording a directive changes no grant and no account — directives are pure input to the resolution below. One guard applies at recording time: an `ONLINE_TOOL` directive may only be recorded against a specific provider that actually offers an online tool, so a null `platform_id` or a provider whose `supports_online_tool` is false is rejected with `ERR_ONLINE_TOOL_UNSUPPORTED`.

**Resolution** answers the scope question for one estate, one provider and, optionally, one asset category. It runs in four steps and is exposed both as a read-only projection — available to a `Fiduciary`, a `CourtRegistrar` or an `EstateAdmin`, taking a `platform_id` and an optional `asset_category` and echoing back `estate_id`, `platform_id`, `asset_category`, `resolved_scope`, `resolved_basis`, `clamp_reason` and `controlling_directive_id` without changing any state — and as the engine behind a request package's submission gate and its frozen decision, described in §3.5.

Step zero is the provider gate: if the provider's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, resolution stops immediately and returns a `resolved_scope` of `NONE`, a `resolved_basis` of `STATUTORY_DEFAULT`, a `clamp_reason` of `NONE` and a null `controlling_directive_id`. A suspended provider owes nothing regardless of what the decedent wrote.

Step one builds the candidate set. A directive is a candidate when its `status` is `RECORDED` and both of its bindings match. Its platform binding matches when its `platform_id` is null or equals the target provider. Its category binding matches when its `asset_category` is null or equals the target category — and when the caller supplies no category at all, only directives whose `asset_category` is null are candidates. One directive is then thrown out of the candidate set entirely: an `ONLINE_TOOL` directive is dropped whenever the target provider's `supports_online_tool` is false, which lets the will-and-trust layer beneath it take over rather than blocking on an honour the provider does not offer.

Step two picks the winner by sorting the candidates on three keys in strict order. The first key is `precedence_rank` ascending, so rank 1 — the online tool — beats rank 2 even when the will grants a wider scope. The second key is specificity descending, where a directive bound to a specific provider scores 2, a directive with a null `platform_id` but a non-null `asset_category` scores 1, and a catch-all with both null scores 0. The third and final key is `created_at` ascending, so the earliest-recorded directive wins a full tie; the tie is never broken by `id` and never broken randomly. The first directive after that sort is the winner and its `id` is the `controlling_directive_id`.

Step three sets the base scope. When the candidate set is empty there is no winner, and the statutory default applies: the base scope is `CATALOG_ONLY` and the basis is `STATUTORY_DEFAULT`. The statutory default never grants content. When there is a winner, the base scope is that directive's own `scope` — which may be `NONE`, `CATALOG_ONLY` or `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT` — and the basis is `ONLINE_TOOL`, `WILL_PROVISION` or `TRUST_PROVISION` according to the winner's `directive_type`.

Step four applies the court-order clamp. The `clamp_reason` starts at `NONE`. If and only if the base scope came out as `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT` *and* the provider's `requires_court_order_for_content` is true, the server looks for a `COURT_ORDER` document on the estate whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`. If no such document exists, the base scope is narrowed to `CATALOG_ONLY` and the `clamp_reason` becomes `COURT_ORDER_REQUIRED`. If such a document does exist, the scope stands and the reason stays `NONE`. The clamp never widens a scope and never fires on a base scope of `NONE` or `CATALOG_ONLY`.

### 3.4. The Inventory: Two Independent Postures and the Transferability Chain

A `Fiduciary` builds the estate's inventory one account at a time. Each **`DigitalAsset`** is created beneath its estate and names the `platform_id` of the provider hosting it, a `category` — one of `FINANCIAL_ACCOUNT`, `EMAIL`, `CLOUD_STORAGE`, `SUBSCRIPTION`, `LOYALTY_PROGRAM` or `SOCIAL_PROFILE` — and an `account_handle`, the login handle or account number (non-empty, at most 120 characters). The handle must be unique within the estate for that provider: a second account with the same combination of `platform_id` and `account_handle` on the same estate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_HANDLE`, while two different estates may perfectly well list the same handle. The fiduciary also supplies `quoted_value_cents`, the provider's own date-of-death value quote for the account as a non-negative integer, and may optionally supply `loyalty_points` (a non-negative integer that defaults to 0 and is recorded purely for audit — it enters no formula anywhere in this specification) and a free-text `inventory_note`.

An account tracks **two entirely independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the single most common misreading of this domain. `access_status` records how far *disclosure* has progressed and moves `UNVERIFIED → CATALOG_DISCLOSED`, `UNVERIFIED → CONTENT_DISCLOSED`, `CATALOG_DISCLOSED → CONTENT_DISCLOSED` when a later grant widens the scope, and either disclosed state → `ACCESS_REVOKED` when authority collapses. `disposition_status` records how far *disposal* has progressed and moves `PENDING → ELECTED → EXECUTED` or `PENDING → ELECTED → PURGED`, with revert edges `ELECTED → PENDING` and `EXECUTED → PENDING`. Neither dimension is a precondition of the other except where this document says so explicitly, and `PURGED` is the one terminal posture from which nothing returns. A newly inventoried account opens at `access_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `content_clamp_reason` `NONE`, and `disposition_status` `PENDING`, with `disclosed_at`, `disposed_at`, `appraised_value_cents` and `appraised_basis` all null. Its `subscription_billing_state` opens at `ACTIVE` when its `category` is `SUBSCRIPTION` and at `NOT_APPLICABLE` for every other category. Adding an account increments the estate's `inventory_total_count` and `assets_pending_disposition_count` and re-derives the `administration_phase`.

At creation the server also classifies the account, writing `transferability` and `transferability_reason`; any value a client tries to supply for either is ignored. The classification walks an **ordered chain of six rules top-down and stops at the first one that matches**, and the order is normative rather than incidental. Rule one: an account whose `category` is `EMAIL` or `SOCIAL_PROFILE` is `NON_TRANSFERABLE` with reason `CATEGORY_PERSONAL` — these are personal to the decedent and cannot be handed to anyone. Rule two: an account whose `category` is `SUBSCRIPTION` is `LICENSE_TERMINATES_AT_DEATH` with reason `CATEGORY_SUBSCRIPTION_LICENCE`. Rule three: an account whose `category` is `CLOUD_STORAGE` on a provider whose `license_terminates_at_death` is true is `LICENSE_TERMINATES_AT_DEATH` with reason `PROVIDER_LICENCE_TERMINATES`. Rule four: an account whose `category` is `LOYALTY_PROGRAM` on a provider whose `supports_transfer` is false is `LICENSE_TERMINATES_AT_DEATH`, also with reason `PROVIDER_LICENCE_TERMINATES`. Rule five: any remaining account on a provider whose `supports_transfer` is false is `NON_TRANSFERABLE` with reason `PROVIDER_NO_TRANSFER`. Rule six, reached only when nothing above matched: the account is `TRANSFERABLE` with reason `TRANSFERABLE_PROPERTY`.

Two consequences of that ordering are load-bearing and must be reproduced exactly. A `SUBSCRIPTION` account on a provider whose `supports_transfer` is false stops at rule two, so its reason is `CATEGORY_SUBSCRIPTION_LICENCE` and never `PROVIDER_NO_TRANSFER`. Conversely, a `CLOUD_STORAGE` account on a provider whose `license_terminates_at_death` is false but whose `supports_transfer` is also false falls past rule three and stops at rule five, so it is `NON_TRANSFERABLE` with reason `PROVIDER_NO_TRANSFER` rather than a terminating licence.

Because provider policy can change after an account was inventoried, a `Fiduciary` may ask for a **transferability review** of one account, optionally attaching a `review_note`. The review re-runs the same six-rule chain against the provider's *current* flags and rewrites `transferability` and `transferability_reason`. It deliberately does **not** clear an existing `appraised_value_cents`: an account that has just been reclassified out of `TRANSFERABLE` keeps the appraisal it already carries until a fresh valuation run replaces it. The review advances the account's `version`. It is refused on a closed estate with `ERR_ESTATE_CLOSED`, and refused to a fiduciary of another estate with `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ESTATE_MISMATCH`.

Only a `TRANSFERABLE` account can carry value into the estate roll-up, and only a `TRANSFERABLE` account can be elected for transfer to a beneficiary. Both `NON_TRANSFERABLE` and `LICENSE_TERMINATES_AT_DEATH` accounts contribute nothing: a terminating licence simply ends at death, so there is nothing left to hand over.

### 3.5. The Request Package and the Provider's Review Cycle

Nothing is disclosed until a provider says so, and a provider only answers a properly assembled package. A `Fiduciary` drafts a **`PlatformRequest`** beneath the estate, naming the `platform_id` it is addressed to, a `request_type` of `CATALOG_ACCESS`, `CONTENT_ACCESS`, `DISPOSITION` or `CANCELLATION`, and a `requested_scope` of either `CATALOG_ONLY` or `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT` — the scope the fiduciary is asking for, which the resolution in §3.3 may well narrow. The package cites its paperwork by identifier: `death_certificate_document_id` and `letters_document_id`. It also cites `platform_form_code` (non-empty, at most 40 characters), the provider's own decedent-request form code.

Three consistency checks fire at drafting time, in the ladder order of §2.7. Both cited documents must belong to the same estate as the request, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENT_ESTATE_MISMATCH` — cross-estate paperwork can never support a request. Each identifier must point at a document of the right class: `death_certificate_document_id` at a `DEATH_CERTIFICATE` and `letters_document_id` at a `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY`. Swapping the two is a type error, rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH` at HTTP 400, and is deliberately distinguished from a verification failure. And the cited `platform_form_code` must equal the provider's `form_code` character for character, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_PLATFORM_FORM_MISMATCH`. A drafted package opens with `status` at `DRAFT`, `info_cycle_count` at 0, `review_window_start` and `review_window_end` null, and `decision`, `decision_note`, `missing_items`, `remediation_note`, `withdrawal_reason`, `resolved_scope` and `resolved_basis` all null, with `clamp_reason` at `NONE`. The provider's `request_fee_cents` is what this package will cost the estate in administrative fees if it is ever approved, and that fee enters the tax deduction described in §3.8.

**Submission** advances the package `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is a `Fiduciary` action. It is gated by six substantive checks beyond the ladder's generic ones. The cited death certificate must be in `VERIFIED` status, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_DEATH_CERTIFICATE_NOT_VERIFIED`; a pending or revoked certificate cannot support a submission. The cited letters must be `VERIFIED` *and* the fiduciary they name must have `letters_status` equal to `ISSUED`, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_LETTERS_NOT_ISSUED` — revoking letters immediately disqualifies that fiduciary from acting. The provider's `status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_PLATFORM_SUSPENDED` is returned, since a suspended provider accepts no traffic and its resolved scope collapses to `NONE` in any case. The cited form code is re-checked against the provider's current `form_code`, because the provider may have changed it since drafting, and a mismatch again yields `ERR_PLATFORM_FORM_MISMATCH`. When the `request_type` is `CONTENT_ACCESS`, the resolution of §3.3 must come out at `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT` with a `clamp_reason` of `NONE`; if the recorded directives never granted content at all, the submission is rejected with `ERR_CONTENT_DISCLOSURE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, and if they did grant content but the provider's `requires_court_order_for_content` is true and no `VERIFIED` `COURT_ORDER` document exists on the estate, it is rejected with the more specific `ERR_COURT_ORDER_REQUIRED`. Those two codes are not interchangeable: the second is only reachable when the pre-clamp scope was `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT`.

The sixth check is the **review window**, and its interval convention matters. On submission the server sets `review_window_start` to the current logical-clock instant and `review_window_end` to that instant advanced by the provider's `review_window_days` days, and the window is **half-open**: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is outside it. A submission is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when its prospective window would overlap the window of another platform request that shares the same estate, the same `platform_id` and the same `request_type` and is currently in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. Because the interval is half-open, a new window beginning exactly at an existing window's `review_window_end` is adjacent rather than overlapping and is allowed. One provider desk may therefore hold at most one live package per request type per estate at a time.

From `SUBMITTED` the provider's desk takes over. Starting review advances the package `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and changes nothing else. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the desk has three exits. It may ask for more paperwork, advancing `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`, storing the `missing_items` it wants and **clearing both `review_window_start` and `review_window_end`** — while the provider waits on the fiduciary it stops occupying the window, freeing the slot. It may decide the package, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `DENIED` together with an optional `decision_note`. A decision of `DENIED` advances `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, stores the note, creates no grant and leaves every account exactly where it was. A decision of `APPROVED` requires once more that the provider's `status` is not `SUSPENDED` and advances `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, triggering the disclosure cascade of §3.6.

The cycle closes through resubmission. A `Fiduciary` whose package sits in `INFO_REQUESTED` may resubmit it, advancing `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, storing a `remediation_note`, increasing `info_cycle_count` by exactly 1 and re-opening the window from the *current* clock position with a fresh `review_window_start` and `review_window_end`. The full non-overlap test is applied again at the new clock position, so a slot taken in the meantime blocks the resubmission with `ERR_REQUEST_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; the paperwork checks on the death certificate, the letters, the provider's suspension posture and the form code are all re-applied as well. This closes the loop `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and the loop is capped: resubmission is only permitted while `info_cycle_count` is strictly below three, so the third resubmission succeeds and raises the count from 2 to 3 while the fourth is rejected with `ERR_INFO_CYCLE_LIMIT`.

A `Fiduciary` may abandon a package from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, advancing it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` and storing a `withdrawal_reason`. Withdrawal always releases the review window: `review_window_start` and `review_window_end` are cleared so the slot no longer blocks a new submission, which for a package withdrawn from `INFO_REQUESTED` simply means the already-null window stays null. `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal; any action submitted against a package whose current `status` does not define that action — deciding a package still in `SUBMITTED`, or submitting one already `APPROVED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE`.

### 3.6. Approval, the Content Clamp, and the Three Ways Authority Collapses

An approval is the only event in the system that creates an **`AuthorityGrant`**. Grants are never created directly by any caller; they are read and they are ended, but they come into existence only as the first hop of an approval cascade. A grant records the `estate_id` and `platform_id` it covers and the `platform_request_id` that produced it, freezes the `granted_scope` and `basis` that the resolution of §3.3 produced at decision time along with the `clamp_reason` that resolution carried, opens its `status` at `ACTIVE`, sets `effective_from` to the current clock instant and `expires_at` to that instant advanced by 90 days, and leaves `revocation_reason` null. The frozen triple matters: the request record itself also keeps `resolved_scope`, `resolved_basis` and `clamp_reason` as of the decision, so a directive revoked afterwards does not retroactively rewrite what the provider agreed to. A grant's `granted_scope` is one of `NONE`, `CATALOG_ONLY` or `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT`, its `basis` one of `ONLINE_TOOL`, `WILL_OR_TRUST`, `STATUTORY_DEFAULT` or `NONE`.

The second hop of the cascade discloses accounts. Every `DigitalAsset` covered by the new grant — same estate, same `platform_id` — whose `access_status` is either `UNVERIFIED` or `ACCESS_REVOKED` is visited in ascending `created_at` order, and each one is raised to the level the grant supports. That level is decided per account by four rules, evaluated in order, first match winning. If the grant's `granted_scope` is `NONE`, the account is left at `UNVERIFIED` with its `content_clamp_reason` at `NONE`; a scope of `NONE` discloses nothing at all. If the `granted_scope` is `CATALOG_ONLY`, the account moves to `CATALOG_DISCLOSED`, and its `content_clamp_reason` becomes `COURT_ORDER_REQUIRED` when the grant's own `clamp_reason` is `COURT_ORDER_REQUIRED` and `NONE` otherwise — the account carries a machine-readable note explaining why content stopped short. If the `granted_scope` is `CATALOG_AND_CONTENT` but the account's `category` is `FINANCIAL_ACCOUNT` and the grant's `basis` is anything other than `ONLINE_TOOL`, the account moves only to `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` and its `content_clamp_reason` becomes `FINANCIAL_REQUIRES_ONLINE_TOOL`: financial accounts surrender their contents only on the decedent's own online-tool designation, never on a will, a trust or the statutory default, no matter how broadly the provider granted. Otherwise the account moves to `CONTENT_DISCLOSED` with its `content_clamp_reason` at `NONE`. Whenever an account's `access_status` rises above `UNVERIFIED` for the first time, `disclosed_at` is stamped with the clock instant.

That per-account rule interacts with re-approval in a way the implementation must preserve. A second approval that widens an account from `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` to `CONTENT_DISCLOSED` leaves the original `disclosed_at` untouched — the timestamp records first disclosure, not the latest one — and resets `content_clamp_reason` to `NONE`, because the clamp that once held has been lifted. Conversely, because the disclosure hop only visits accounts sitting at `UNVERIFIED` or `ACCESS_REVOKED`, a later `CATALOG_ONLY` approval simply skips an account already at `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED`: disclosure never narrows.

The third hop looks after recurring charges. For every account the second hop has just disclosed whose `category` is `SUBSCRIPTION` and which does not already have a cancellation order, the system creates a **`CancellationOrder`** in `PENDING` status and copies the provider's `refund_policy` into the order's `refund_policy_snapshot` at that moment. The snapshot is taken once and never refreshed, so a provider that changes its refund terms afterwards does not change the terms of orders already opened. The fourth hop recomputes the estate's `disclosed_asset_count` and `open_cancellation_count` and re-derives the administration phase, which is how a first approval carries an estate out of `INTAKE` and into `AUTHORIZED`.

Authority ends in exactly three ways, and each carries its own `revocation_reason`.

The first is judicial. A `CourtRegistrar` may revoke a grant outright, advancing its `status` from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `REVOKED`, storing an optional `reason` and setting `revocation_reason` to `REGISTRAR_ORDER`. The revocation cascades two hops: every account of the covered estate and provider whose `access_status` is `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED` is walked in ascending `created_at` order and dropped to `ACCESS_REVOKED`, and then the estate's counters are recomputed and its phase re-derived. Revoking a grant that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`.

The second is the passage of time. An `EstateAdmin` runs a **grant expiry sweep** over one estate, which takes no input and reports what it did. The sweep visits every `ACTIVE` grant of the estate whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock instant, in ascending `created_at` order, and moves each to the terminal `EXPIRED` with `revocation_reason` set to `TERM_EXPIRED`; the boundary is inclusive, so a grant whose `expires_at` equals the clock exactly does expire. For each expired grant the covered disclosed accounts are dropped to `ACCESS_REVOKED` in ascending `created_at` order, exactly as under a registrar revocation. The same sweep also ages out billing: every `SubscriptionTerm` of the estate in `ACTIVE` state whose `term_end_date` falls strictly before the clock's calendar date and whose `auto_renew` is false moves to the terminal `LAPSED`. Finally the estate's counters are recomputed and its phase re-derived. The sweep reports `swept_at` (the clock instant it ran at), `expired_grant_count`, `revoked_asset_count` and `lapsed_term_count`. It is idempotent with respect to a fixed clock: running it a second time without advancing the clock finds nothing left to do and returns all three counts as 0.

The third is the loss of standing, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. When a `CourtRegistrar` revokes a `LETTERS_OF_AUTHORITY` document, the first hop sets the named fiduciary's `letters_status` to `REVOKED`. The second hop is conditional: it fires only if, after that first hop, the estate has **no remaining fiduciary** whose `letters_status` is `ISSUED`. When it does fire, every `ACTIVE` `AuthorityGrant` of the estate — across all providers — is walked in ascending `created_at` order and moved to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `LETTERS_REVOKED`. The third hop then walks every account of the estate whose `access_status` is `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED`, in ascending `created_at` order, dropping each to `ACCESS_REVOKED` and resetting its `content_clamp_reason` to `NONE`, since with no authority at all there is no longer any particular reason content was withheld. The fourth hop recomputes `disclosed_asset_count` and re-derives the phase, which with no disclosed accounts and no live grants returns the estate to `INTAKE`. If a co-fiduciary still holds `ISSUED` letters, the cascade stops after the first hop: the revoked fiduciary can no longer act, but the estate's grants and disclosures stand untouched. This is the mechanism that makes a two-fiduciary estate resilient and a sole-fiduciary estate fragile.

### 3.7. Stopping the Charges and Chasing the Money Back

A decedent's recurring services keep billing until somebody stops them, so the system tracks the paid term, the cancellation and the refund as three separate records with three separate lifecycles.

A `Fiduciary` records a **`SubscriptionTerm`** beneath a `SUBSCRIPTION`-category account, supplying `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, a `billing_interval` of `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL`, the `period_fee_cents` actually paid for the whole term (zero or greater, at most 100000000), and `auto_renew` to say whether the provider will roll the term over. The term is a **closed** interval: both endpoints are inside it, so `term_days` is the number of days from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` plus one, and a term whose two dates are equal is one day long. `term_end_date` must not fall before `term_start_date`, and a violation is an input error reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An account may carry at most one term, and only an account whose `category` is `SUBSCRIPTION` may carry one at all; both a second term on the same account and a first term on an account of any other category are refused with the single code `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_EXISTS`. A new term opens with `state` at `ACTIVE`, `claimed_days_total` at 0 and `cancelled_effective_date` null. Its `state` is one of `ACTIVE`, `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED`, the latter two terminal; `CANCELLED` is reached only through a confirmed cancellation and `LAPSED` only through the expiry sweep of §3.6.

The **`CancellationOrder`** is never created by a caller either: it appears in `PENDING` with its `refund_policy_snapshot` already filled the moment its account is first disclosed, as the third hop of §3.6. Its `status` runs `PENDING`, `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `FAILED`, with `CONFIRMED` terminal and `FAILED` deliberately not terminal. A `Fiduciary` requests the cancellation, advancing the order to `REQUESTED` and supplying the `effective_date` on which service should stop. Two conditions guard the request: the account's `access_status` must be `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED`, because a fiduciary may not act against an account no provider has disclosed to them, and the supplied `effective_date` must fall on or after the estate's `date_of_death`, with equality accepted and an earlier date rejected as `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_BEFORE_DEATH`. The request stamps `requested_at` with the clock instant.

The provider's desk then confirms or fails the order. Confirming advances `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, stores the provider's `platform_reference` and the `auto_renew_stopped` flag it reports, stamps `confirmed_at`, and cascades three hops: the account's `SubscriptionTerm` moves to `CANCELLED` with its own `auto_renew` forced to false and its `cancelled_effective_date` copied from the order's `effective_date`; the account's `subscription_billing_state` moves to `CANCELLED`; and the estate's `open_cancellation_count` and `cancelled_subscription_count` are recomputed. Failing advances `REQUESTED → FAILED` and stores the provider's `failure_reason`, and it changes nothing else — the term stays `ACTIVE` and its `auto_renew` is left exactly as it was, because a failed cancellation attempt has not stopped anything. A `Fiduciary` may request again from `FAILED`, which returns the order to `REQUESTED`, overwrites `effective_date` with the new one, clears `failure_reason` and re-stamps `requested_at`; the disclosure and date-of-death guards apply to the retry just as they did to the first attempt. Acting on an order whose current `status` does not define the action is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

A **`RefundClaim`** is filed by a `Fiduciary` beneath a cancellation order, and only beneath a `CONFIRMED` one; filing against an order in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, and that check outranks every other check on the operation. The claim supplies `claimed_period_start`, `claimed_period_end` and the `pursuit_cost_cents` the estate spent chasing it (zero or greater, at most 100000000). The claimed period is also a **closed** interval, so `claimed_days` is the day count from `claimed_period_start` to `claimed_period_end` plus one.

Four further conditions are checked in this order. First, the account must actually have a `SubscriptionTerm`, since the entitlement is computed from the term's own numbers; without one the filing is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_MISSING`. Second, the order's `refund_policy_snapshot` must not be `NONE`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_OFFERED` — and because the snapshot was frozen at order creation, a later change to the provider's `refund_policy` can neither open nor close this door. Third, the claimed period must sit inside the term: `claimed_period_start` at or after `term_start_date`, `claimed_period_end` at or before `term_end_date`, and `claimed_period_start` no later than `claimed_period_end`, with any violation reported as `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM`. Fourth, the claimed interval must not overlap the interval of any other claim on the same term that is currently in `FILED`, `PLATFORM_REVIEW`, `ESCALATED` or `CREDITED`. Because these intervals are closed, **touching endpoints are an overlap**: a live claim ending on a given day blocks a new claim starting on that same day, and the collision is reported as `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. This is the exact opposite of the half-open review window of §3.5, and the two conventions must not be conflated. Claims that have reached `DENIED` or `CHARGED_BACK` release their interval and no longer block anything.

Only after those four checks does the length rule apply: `claimed_days` must be at least 7, so a claim covering exactly 7 days is accepted and one covering 6 is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`. The ordering is normative — a six-day claim that also falls outside the term reports `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OUT_OF_TERM`, not the length error.

The claim's `method` is copied from the order's `refund_policy_snapshot`, and it selects one of two entitlement formulas, both of which take the term's `period_fee_cents` as the refundable pool and the term's `term_days` as the divisor. Under `PRORATA_DAILY` the entitlement is `period_fee_cents` multiplied by `claimed_days`, divided by `term_days`, with the quotient rounded half up to a whole cent: every claimed day earns its share. Under `UNUSED_PERIOD` only whole 30-day blocks count. The billable day count is the number of complete 30-day blocks inside `claimed_days` — that is, `claimed_days` divided by 30 with the fractional part discarded — multiplied back by 30; the entitlement is then `period_fee_cents` multiplied by that billable day count, divided by `term_days`, rounded half up. A `UNUSED_PERIOD` claim shorter than 30 days therefore yields an entitlement of exactly 0 even though it passed the seven-day minimum, and a 59-day claim is paid as 30 days, not 59. The result is written to `claimed_cents`. A new claim opens with `status` at `FILED` and `credited_cents` at 0, and the term's `claimed_days_total` — defined as the sum of `claimed_days` across the claims on that term in `FILED`, `PLATFORM_REVIEW`, `ESCALATED` or `CREDITED` — rises by this claim's `claimed_days`.

The claim's `status` runs `FILED`, `PLATFORM_REVIEW`, `ESCALATED`, `CREDITED`, `DENIED` or `CHARGED_BACK`, with `DENIED` and `CHARGED_BACK` terminal. The provider's desk opens the file, advancing `FILED → PLATFORM_REVIEW`. From there the `Fiduciary` may escalate, advancing `PLATFORM_REVIEW → ESCALATED` and storing an `escalation_reason`; escalation is a detour, not an exit, and both `PLATFORM_REVIEW` and `ESCALATED` lead to the same two provider decisions. Crediting advances the claim to `CREDITED`, stores the `credited_cents` the provider actually paid — which must not exceed `claimed_cents`, with an exactly equal credit accepted and a larger one rejected as `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` — and adds that amount to the estate's `refund_recovered_cents`. Denying advances the claim to `DENIED`, stores the `denial_reason` and releases the claimed interval, so the term's `claimed_days_total` is recomputed without this claim and the same days may be claimed again.

A credited claim can still be undone. The provider's desk may charge back a `CREDITED` claim, advancing it to the terminal `CHARGED_BACK`, storing a `chargeback_reason` and compensating exactly: the estate's `refund_recovered_cents` is reduced by the claim's `credited_cents`, and the claimed interval is released just as a denial releases it. Acting on a claim whose current `status` does not define the action — crediting one still in `FILED`, or escalating one already `DENIED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

### 3.8. Putting a Number on the Estate and Dividing It

Before anything can be transferred to anybody, the estate must be valued and the beneficiaries' shares must be planned. Both happen inside a **`ValuationRun`**, an immutable snapshot of the arithmetic at one clock position.

The beneficiaries come first. A `Fiduciary` records a **`BeneficiaryDesignation`** beneath the estate with a `beneficiary_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), a `share_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive, a `source` of `WILL`, `TRUST` or `PLATFORM_DESIGNATION`, and an optional `relationship` (at most 60 characters) that is recorded for the distribution schedule and never used in any computation. The server assigns `allocation_rank` as one more than the number of designations the estate already has, so ranks are dense, 1-based and follow `created_at` ascending order, and it opens `planned_allocation_cents` and `received_value_cents` at 0. The running total of `share_bp` across an estate's designations may never exceed 10000 — a designation that would push it past that is rejected with `ERR_SHARE_TOTAL_INVALID` — and it must equal exactly 10000 before a valuation run may be created, so an estate whose shares total 9999 cannot be valued and reports the same code.

Creating a run is a `Fiduciary` action that names a `basis` of either `DATE_OF_DEATH` or `ALTERNATE` and carries `expected_estate_version` for the concurrency check of §2.8. An `ALTERNATE` run additionally requires the clock's calendar date to have reached the estate's `alternate_valuation_date`; the boundary is inclusive, so a run on exactly that date is accepted while a run one day earlier is rejected with `ERR_ALTERNATE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. No run at all may be created once any run on the estate has reached `FINALIZED`, which is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED`: the finalized number is the estate's answer and re-running would invalidate it. Creating a run moves every one of the estate's existing `COMPUTED` runs to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and opens the new run at `COMPUTED`.

The run's first job is to appraise each account, and the inclusion test is strict. An account enters the roll-up only if its `transferability` is exactly `TRANSFERABLE` **and** its `access_status` is `CATALOG_DISCLOSED` or `CONTENT_DISCLOSED`. Both conditions are required: a transferable account nobody has disclosed contributes nothing, and a fully disclosed account that the provider's terms make non-transferable contributes nothing either. Excluded accounts contribute 0 and are not counted in `included_asset_count`.

For an included account the gross figure depends on the run's basis. Under `DATE_OF_DEATH` the gross is the account's `quoted_value_cents` exactly, with no adjustment. Under `ALTERNATE` the value is decayed for the six months between the date of death and the alternate valuation date: each account `category` carries a monthly decay rate in basis points — `FINANCIAL_ACCOUNT` 0, `EMAIL` 0, `SUBSCRIPTION` 0, `CLOUD_STORAGE` 50, `LOYALTY_PROGRAM` 150, `SOCIAL_PROFILE` 200 — and the total decay is that monthly rate multiplied by 6. The gross is then `quoted_value_cents` multiplied by the quantity 10000 minus the total decay basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. A social profile therefore loses 1200 basis points, twelve percent, of its quoted value under the alternate basis, while a financial account and an email account are worth the same under either basis. From the gross the provider's `transfer_fee_cents` is then subtracted — the fee the provider charges to move the account to a beneficiary — and the result is floored at zero, so an account whose transfer fee exceeds its decayed gross appraises at 0 rather than a negative number. The outcome is written to the account's `appraised_value_cents`, and its `appraised_basis` records which basis produced it.

The run then rolls the accounts up. `gross_value_cents` is the sum of the `appraised_value_cents` of the included accounts and `included_asset_count` is how many there were. The administrative-expense deduction is capped by a tier selected **from the gross**, never from the post-deduction figure: a gross below 500000 selects a `deduction_cap_cents` of 25000, a gross below 5000000 selects 150000, and any larger gross selects 400000. The estate's actual administrative costs are the sum of the `request_fee_cents` of the providers behind every one of the estate's platform requests in `APPROVED` status, plus the sum of the `pursuit_cost_cents` of every refund claim of the estate regardless of that claim's status — the money was spent whether or not the claim succeeded. `admin_deduction_cents` is the smaller of the cap and those actual costs, so a frugal estate deducts only what it spent while a costly one is held to its tier. `taxable_value_cents` is the gross minus the deduction, floored at zero. The three figures are mirrored onto the estate as `gross_digital_estate_cents`, `admin_deduction_cents` and `taxable_digital_estate_cents`, the estate's `valuation_basis` mirrors the basis of its newest `COMPUTED` or `FINALIZED` run, and `undistributed_value_cents` is set to the gross minus the sum of `received_value_cents` across the estate's designations.

Finally the run plans the division, and the algorithm is a floor-then-remainder pass, not independent rounding. The pool is the run's `gross_value_cents`. The designations are taken in `allocation_rank` ascending order, which is `created_at` ascending order. In the first pass each designation's `planned_allocation_cents` is the pool multiplied by that designation's `share_bp`, divided by 10000, with the fractional part **discarded** — always floored, never rounded. The leftover is the pool minus the sum of those floors, and it is recorded on the run as `allocation_remainder_cents`. In the second pass exactly one cent is added to each designation in `allocation_rank` ascending order, one designation at a time, until the leftover is exhausted; a leftover of two cents therefore goes to ranks 1 and 2 and nowhere else. The invariant this protects is that the sum of `planned_allocation_cents` equals the pool exactly, to the cent. Rounding each share half up independently would break it and is explicitly wrong: three equal shares of 3334, 3333 and 3333 basis points over a pool of 100 cents plan as 34, 33 and 33 cents, and the one leftover cent goes to rank 1 because it is first, not because its share is largest.

A `COMPUTED` run may be finalized by a `Fiduciary`, which advances it to the terminal `FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` and stores an optional `finalization_note`. Only a `COMPUTED` run may be finalized — finalizing a `SUPERSEDED` or already-`FINALIZED` run is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE` — and because finalization blocks all further runs, it happens exactly once per estate. Finalization is a prerequisite for closing the estate, and a valuation of some kind is a prerequisite for transferring anything.

### 3.9. Electing a Disposition, Executing It, and Closing the File

The last act of administration is deciding what happens to each account, and the two-fiduciary safeguard on irreversible deletion lives here.

A `Fiduciary` records a **`DispositionElection`** beneath an account, naming the `election` — `MEMORIALIZE`, `DELETE` or `TRANSFER` — and carrying `expected_asset_version`, which is mandatory on this operation because an election is a decision taken against a specific reading of the account. An optional `election_note` records the rationale, and `beneficiary_designation_id` names who receives the account. The server resolves `elected_by_fiduciary_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` header, never from the request body, and leaves `countersigned_by_fiduciary_id`, `attestation`, `platform_confirmation_reference`, `rejection_reason`, `reversal_reason` and `executed_at` null with `distributed_value_cents` at 0.

Four substantive gates guard the election at creation time. There must be an `ACTIVE` `AuthorityGrant` covering the account's estate and provider, and the account itself must be disclosed; without live authority the election is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AUTHORITY`, which is what makes a revoked or expired grant freeze the estate's disposition work. A `TRANSFER` requires the account's `transferability` to be exactly `TRANSFERABLE`, or the election is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_TRANSFERABLE`. The provider must support the elected disposition — its `supports_memorialization`, `supports_deletion` or `supports_transfer` flag, whichever corresponds — or the election is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_UNSUPPORTED`. And the beneficiary linkage must match the election exactly: `beneficiary_designation_id` is required for a `TRANSFER` and must be absent for a `MEMORIALIZE` or a `DELETE`, with either violation reported as `ERR_BENEFICIARY_REQUIRED` at HTTP 400.

The lifecycle forks at creation and joins before submission. A `MEMORIALIZE` or `TRANSFER` election opens at `DRAFT`. A `DELETE` election opens at `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`, because deletion destroys an account permanently and one fiduciary acting alone may not order it. Either way, creating an election supersedes whatever came before it: every earlier election on the same account whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `SUBMITTED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, and if the superseded election had reached `SUBMITTED` — meaning it had already moved the account's `disposition_status` to `ELECTED` — the account is returned to `PENDING` so the new election can drive it. Creation also increases the account's `version` by one, which is why a second election attempt built on a stale reading fails the concurrency check with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION`.

A `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` election is countersigned by a `Fiduciary`, advancing it to `DRAFT` where the two branches join. The countersigner must belong to the account's estate and must hold `letters_status` of `ISSUED`, and the `X-User-ID` they present must differ from the election's `elected_by_fiduciary_id`; a fiduciary countersigning their own deletion is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403, and it is that code rather than a generic permission error because the caller does hold the right role. Countersigning records `countersigned_by_fiduciary_id` and stores the `attestation` text.

Submission advances the election `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is a `Fiduciary` action. For a `DELETE` election `countersigned_by_fiduciary_id` must be non-null, or the submission is refused with `ERR_COUNTERSIGN_REQUIRED` — the branch merge is not an escape hatch, and a `DELETE` cannot reach `SUBMITTED` without two distinct fiduciaries having touched it. The account must still be disclosed and its `disposition_status` must still be `PENDING`. Submission sets the account's `disposition_status` to `ELECTED`, recomputes the estate's disposition counters and re-derives the phase.

Execution is a `PlatformCompliance` action, advancing the election `SUBMITTED → EXECUTED`, and it is where the three dispositions finally diverge. The provider must not be `SUSPENDED` and must still support the elected disposition. Execution stamps `executed_at` and stores the `platform_confirmation_reference`.

A `MEMORIALIZE` execution sets the account's `disposition_status` to `EXECUTED` and stamps its `disposed_at`. No value moves, because memorializing an account leaves it in place as a tribute and vests nothing in anybody; the beneficiary ledger is untouched.

A `DELETE` execution sets the account's `disposition_status` to the terminal `PURGED` and stamps `disposed_at`. The account's `account_handle` is deliberately retained on the record for audit even though the underlying account is gone, so the estate file still shows what was destroyed. No value moves here either.

A `TRANSFER` execution moves money, and it is the only branch with fiscal preconditions. The account's `transferability` must be `TRANSFERABLE` and its `appraised_value_cents` must be non-null with a `COMPUTED` or `FINALIZED` valuation run in existence, or the execution is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_REQUIRED`: nothing may be distributed before the estate has been valued. The account's `appraised_value_cents` must not exceed the estate's `undistributed_value_cents`, with an amount exactly equal to the remaining pool accepted and anything larger refused as `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. And the named designation's `received_value_cents` plus the account's `appraised_value_cents` must not exceed that designation's `planned_allocation_cents`, with exact equality accepted and any excess refused as `ERR_BENEFICIARY_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`, so no beneficiary can be handed more than the plan of §3.8 allotted them. When all three hold, the election's `distributed_value_cents` is set to the account's `appraised_value_cents`, the designation's `received_value_cents` rises by that amount, the estate's `undistributed_value_cents` falls by the same amount, and the account's `disposition_status` becomes `EXECUTED` with `disposed_at` stamped. Every execution branch ends by recomputing the estate's disposition counters and re-deriving the phase.

A provider's desk may instead reject a submitted election, advancing it to the terminal `REJECTED` and storing a `rejection_reason`. Rejection returns the account's `disposition_status` to `PENDING` so a different disposition may be elected, and recomputes the estate's counters.

An executed election may be reversed by the provider's desk, advancing it `EXECUTED → REVERSED`, which is terminal. Reversal requires the account's `disposition_status` to be `EXECUTED`; a `PURGED` account can never be reversed, because a deleted account cannot be brought back, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ELECTION_STATE`. Reversal stores a `reversal_reason` and clears `executed_at`. For a `TRANSFER` the compensation is exact and complete: the designation's `received_value_cents` is reduced by the election's `distributed_value_cents`, the estate's `undistributed_value_cents` is increased by the same amount, and `distributed_value_cents` is reset to 0, leaving the ledger byte-for-byte where it stood before the execution. For a `MEMORIALIZE` there is nothing to compensate. Either way the account's `disposed_at` is cleared and its `disposition_status` returns to `PENDING`, and the estate's counters and phase are recomputed.

When every account has been settled, a `Fiduciary` closes the estate, supplying a `closing_statement` (non-empty, at most 2000 characters). Four gates are checked in this order. The estate's newest non-`SUPERSEDED` valuation run must have `status` `FINALIZED`, or closure is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FINALIZED`: the tax position must be locked first. Every account of the estate must have `disposition_status` of `EXECUTED` or `PURGED`, equivalently `assets_pending_disposition_count` at 0, or closure is refused with `ERR_ASSETS_PENDING_DISPOSITION`. Every cancellation order on the estate's accounts must be `CONFIRMED` or `FAILED`, equivalently `open_cancellation_count` at 0, or closure is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CANCELLATIONS` — note that a `FAILED` order counts as closed for this purpose, since the fiduciary has done what it could. Every refund claim of the estate must be `CREDITED`, `DENIED` or `CHARGED_BACK`, or closure is refused with `ERR_OPEN_REFUND_CLAIMS`. Closure stamps `closed_at`, stores the `closing_statement` and pins `administration_phase` to `CLOSED`, suppressing all further derivation. From that moment every mutating operation anywhere beneath the estate is refused with `ERR_ESTATE_CLOSED`, including a second attempt to close it, while reads continue to work so the file remains auditable forever.

## 4. API Endpoints

Every endpoint below returns the standard envelope of §2.2. Operations that create a new record return HTTP 201 — registering a platform, opening an estate, appointing a fiduciary, filing a document, recording an authority directive, adding a digital asset, recording a subscription term, drafting a platform request, recording a beneficiary designation, electing a disposition, filing a refund claim, and creating a valuation run. Every other operation returns HTTP 200, including all reads, all list endpoints, the platform update, every `transitions` action, the estate closure, the authority-resolution projection, the grant-expiry sweep, the transferability review, and the logical-clock setter. Sub-collection creation endpoints are addressed beneath their parent, but every individual record is thereafter addressed by its own flat path, as described in §2.6.
