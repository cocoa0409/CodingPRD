# Alternative Education Credit Recovery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

School districts operate alternative education centers as the last structured stop before a student disappears from the graduation cohort altogether. The population arriving at such a center is heterogeneous by design: some students are referred for behavior, some are simply too far behind on credits to graduate on the comprehensive campus, and some have been effectively unenrolled for weeks and need a re-engagement pathway back into instruction. Each of those three intake stories carries a different urgency, and centers have a fixed number of funded seats and a contractual cap on how many students one certified teacher may carry. The district needs a system that makes seat allocation defensible, keeps credit award policy honest, and produces a transcript that a college registrar or an NCAA eligibility reviewer will accept.

This document specifies the Alternative Education & Credit Recovery API, the district's system of record for that work. It covers referral and placement screening with pathway-weighted prioritization; the seat-time versus competency-based credit award policy chosen per placement; a course catalog offering online, packet and blended delivery with proctoring requirements; credit recovery restricted to courses the student has previously failed, with the district's grade-replacement flag deciding whether the failing grade is replaced or averaged; attendance, pacing and re-entry readiness gates for returning a student to the home school; graduation plan tracking with cohort accountability roll-up to the sending school; on-site staffing ratios and support services; and alternative-education transcript coding.

Two properties govern almost every rule in this specification and should be read as design premises rather than incidental details. First, the system is an accountability instrument: nearly every consequential action is a state transition guarded by a named precondition and a named error, and the actions that move money-equivalents — seats and credits — are paired with a compensating action that must restore the prior state exactly. Second, every derived figure in this system is recomputed from current persisted state on every read rather than cached, so a seat count, a readiness score, a GPA or a cohort flag can never be observed stale.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across the entire API: authentication, response shape, pagination, common properties, precision, concurrency control, and the fixed order in which a request is validated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. If the header pair is present but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Six roles exist, and their exact spellings are `DistrictAdmin`, `PlacementCoordinator`, `HomeSchoolCounselor`, `AltEdTeacher`, `Registrar`, and `SupportProviderStaff`. A `DistrictAdmin` is a district office operator: it opens centers and home schools, publishes the course catalog, revises staffing, adjudicates academic-integrity findings, approves or denies re-entry to the home school, and drives the logical clock. A `PlacementCoordinator` is the center-side intake and operations lead: it screens referrals, offers or waitlists seats, books proctoring, and exits placements. A `HomeSchoolCounselor` is the sending school's counselor: it refers students, supplies missing intake data, and accepts seat offers on behalf of its students. An `AltEdTeacher` is on-site instructional staff: it enrolls placed students in catalog courses, logs attendance and coursework progress, and may submit completed work for credit award. A `Registrar` owns the transcript: historical course rows, graduation plans, credit awards, grade replacement and voids. A `SupportProviderStaff` is an on-site counselor, social worker or behavior specialist who schedules and delivers support services.

Three of these roles are **scope-bound**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary operator string but the identifier of a persisted entity, and every action is checked against that scope:

- When `X-User-Role` is `PlacementCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Center`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Center` compare the resource's `center_id` — including the `center_id` reached indirectly through a referral, a placement, a proctoring station or a support provider — to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_CENTER_SCOPE`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `HomeSchoolCounselor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HomeSchool`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Student` compare that student's `home_school_id` to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCHOOL_SCOPE`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SupportProviderStaff`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SupportProvider`; ownership checks on a support session compare its `support_provider_id` to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_PROVIDER_SCOPE`.

The other three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `DistrictAdmin`, `Registrar` or `AltEdTeacher`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands it — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a course enrollment when work is submitted for award, and as `re_entry_requested_by_user_id` on a placement when re-entry is requested — and it compares those recorded values against the acting `X-User-ID` at the paired downstream step, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

Collection reads behave differently from single-resource reads for the three scope-bound roles: a collection listing returns only the rows inside the caller's own scope rather than raising a scope error, while a single-resource read and every action endpoint apply the scope check described above and reject out-of-scope targets.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many rows to skip before the returned window begins; when omitted it defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every business resource in this system — centers, home schools, students, courses, prior course records, referrals, placements, course enrollments, credit awards, graduation plans, proctoring stations, proctored exam sessions, support providers and support sessions — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): a system-managed counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

A cascade that mutates a second entity as a side effect mutates it fully: that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if it had been written directly. The specific version bumps each transition produces are stated with the transition in §3.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before any clock-setting request has been made every date- or time-dependent rule reads that default; the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `DistrictAdmin` may advance the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the clock may only move forward — a supplied `current_utc` earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_BACKWARD`, while a value equal to the present value is accepted. The logical clock date is what stamps `enrolled_on` and `posted_on`, what sets `offer_expires_on`, and what drives the elapsed-day arithmetic behind every pacing figure; advancing the clock therefore changes pacing indices, pacing flags and re-entry readiness scores without any other request being made.

### 2.5. Precision, Rounding and Credit Granularity

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, and each figure is rounded to the number of decimal places named for it:

- Ratios and rates that express a fraction of one — attendance rate, pacing and readiness components, the readiness score, the referral priority score, the adjusted graduation rate — are carried to 4 decimal places.
- The pacing index is carried to 3 decimal places, and its input fractions to 4.
- The student-to-teacher ratio, mastery percent, credit totals, expected credits by grade level, and GPA are carried to 2 decimal places.
- Per-row quality points inside the GPA computation are carried to 3 decimal places before they are summed.
- Seat margins and staffing headroom are integers obtained by rounding down (floor), never by rounding half up.

Credits have their own granularity rule that overrides ordinary rounding: **a credit award is floored down to a multiple of 0.25**, and that floor is applied to each award individually, never to a sum of awards. Two enrollments that each compute to 0.375 of a credit therefore post 0.25 and 0.25, for a total of 0.50 credits — not 0.75. Aggregates such as a graduation plan's `credits_earned` sum the already-floored per-award values and never re-floor the sum or floor a summed fraction.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Seat, Placement and Plan Writes

Four write operations carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, because each of them is a perfectly legitimate operation to repeat and the only thing distinguishing a correct repetition from a mistaken one is whether the client is working from the parent record it actually observed. For each of these, the named version field is a **required** input, not an optional one, and its value must equal the parent entity's current `version` at the moment the request is processed.

- `POST /course-enrollments` requires `expected_placement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Placement`. Because a placement's version advances on attendance postings, re-entry requests and re-entry denials, a value that no longer matches the placement's current `version` means the caller is adding coursework to a placement it no longer understands; the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/award-credit` requires `expected_graduation_plan_version`, carrying the registrar's last observed `version` of the student's `GraduationPlan`. Every posted award advances the plan's version through the credit cascade, so a mismatch means a second award would be posted on top of a transcript picture that no longer holds; the award is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/offer` requires `expected_center_version`, carrying the coordinator's last observed `version` of the receiving `Center`. Offers, withdrawals, acceptances, exits, re-entry approvals and staffing changes all advance the center's version, so a mismatch means the seat picture the coordinator reasoned from is gone; the offer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `PATCH /centers/{center_id}/staffing` requires `expected_center_version` as well, carrying the last observed `version` of the `Center` being edited. Staffing edits are otherwise legitimate at any time, and the derived `staffing_headroom` and `intake_posture` depend on the roster the editor believed was current; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.7. Funded Seats, Staffing Ratio and the Intake Posture Gate

A center is opened with a `name`, a funded `seat_capacity` of at least 1, a `certified_teacher_fte` greater than zero carried to 2 decimal places, a `support_staff_fte` of zero or more carried to 2 decimal places, and a contractual `max_students_per_teacher` of at least 1 expressing how many students one full teacher FTE may carry. Everything else the center reports is derived from those inputs and the center's live roster, recomputed on every read and re-derived at the end of any request that changes the number of active students, the number of reserved seats, or the certified teaching FTE:

- `restricted_seat_margin` is ten percent of the funded seat capacity, rounded down to a whole seat.
- `active_student_count` is the number of placements at this center whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `RE_ENTRY_PENDING`. Placements in `RE_ENTERED` or `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL` have released their seat and are not counted.
- `seats_reserved` is the number of referrals at this center whose `status` is `PLACEMENT_OFFERED`. A referral sitting in `WAITLISTED` reserves nothing.
- `seats_committed` is the active student count plus the reserved seats.
- `seats_available` is the funded seat capacity minus the committed seats.
- `student_teacher_ratio` is the active student count divided by the certified teacher FTE, rounded to 2 decimal places.
- `staffing_headroom` is the certified teacher FTE multiplied by the contractual students-per-teacher cap, rounded down to a whole number, minus the active student count.

From those figures the center's `intake_posture` is derived, and the three branches are evaluated **in a fixed order with the first match winning**. If either the available seats are at or below zero or the staffing headroom is at or below zero, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if either the available seats are at or below the restricted seat margin or the staffing headroom is at or below 1, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. No endpoint sets the posture directly; it is always a derivation.

The posture is a gate with two different strengths. A `FROZEN` center accepts no new seat offer and no new course enrollment, and both refusals carry `ERR_INTAKE_FROZEN`. A `RESTRICTED` center still accepts new course enrollments for students already placed there, but it accepts a new seat offer only for a referral whose `pathway` is `BEHAVIOR` or whose `priority_score` is at least 0.5000; an offer that meets neither condition is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_RESTRICTED`. An `OPEN` center accepts both.

Two conserved quantities hold at all times for every center, and they are asserted after every seat-moving transition — offering a seat, withdrawing an offer, accepting an offer, exiting a placement, approving a re-entry, and a system waitlist promotion. First, available seats plus committed seats always equals the funded seat capacity. Second, committed seats always equals the active student count plus the reserved seats. Seats are integers and no rounding is involved. These invariants have a compensating consequence: withdrawing an outstanding offer must return `seats_available`, `seats_committed` and `seats_reserved` to exactly the values they held immediately before that offer was made, with zero drift; and accepting an offer moves one unit from `seats_reserved` into `active_student_count`, so `seats_committed` does not change at all across an acceptance.

Whenever a re-derivation moves a center's posture **into** `OPEN` — whether from `RESTRICTED` or from `FROZEN` — the system immediately attempts a waitlist promotion. It selects at most **one** referral whose `status` is `WAITLISTED` and whose `center_id` is this center, ranking candidates by `priority_score` in descending order and breaking ties by `created_at` in ascending order, and moves that single winner from `WAITLISTED` to `PLACEMENT_OFFERED`. When the candidate set is empty, no promotion occurs and no error is raised. Because a promotion adds one to `seats_reserved`, the posture is then re-derived a second time inside the same request and may settle back to `RESTRICTED` or even `FROZEN`. A re-derivation that moves a center from `FROZEN` to `RESTRICTED` — seats or staffing recovered, but not enough to clear the cushion — performs **no** promotion; promotion fires only on the transition into `OPEN`. Likewise, transitions from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED`, from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` and from `RESTRICTED` to `FROZEN` tighten the gate and never promote.

### 2.8. Derived Values, Cascade Depth and Hierarchical Consistency

Every field this document describes as derived, computed or aggregated is **recomputed on read** from current persisted state and the logical clock each time the owning resource is serialized into a response. No derived value is cached and none can go stale. The only exception in spirit — not in mechanism — is the small set of **accumulators**, which are advanced by an explicit request rather than recomputed: a placement's `days_present` and `days_enrolled`, and an enrollment's `modules_passed` and `seat_hours_logged`. Each of those starts at 0 and is advanced by the deltas an attendance or progress report supplies; every figure computed *from* them is still derived on read.

Because entity parentage in this system is expressed by identifier fields rather than by nested URLs, consistency is maintained by explicit cascades rather than by containment. The system's two deepest cascades are the credit award posting and its compensating void, each of which propagates through four hops: from the enrollment and its new award, to the backing historical transcript row and the student's credit and GPA totals, to the student's graduation plan ledger and plan status, and finally to the sending home school's cohort accountability figures. Every entity touched at every hop is mutated in full, with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented. A void must restore the values the ledger held immediately before the award posted, with zero drift; §3.8 states this obligation precisely.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

The district operates two paired approvals under segregation of duties, and in both the rule is the same: the operator who performed the upstream step may not perform the downstream one. The `X-User-ID` recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` when an enrollment was submitted for award may not be the `X-User-ID` that awards credit for it, nor the one that denies the award. The `X-User-ID` recorded as `re_entry_requested_by_user_id` when re-entry was requested may not be the `X-User-ID` that approves that re-entry. All three violations are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note the asymmetry: denying a re-entry is *not* covered by this rule, because a denial returns the placement to the state it came from and creates no self-approval hazard.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The district's audit posture requires a deterministic answer in that case, so the checks below are numbered and **the response always carries the error of the lowest-numbered rung that fails**; no later rung may mask an earlier one. Within the business rules the district's declared order of concerns is: academic integrity and safety first, then referential and catalog eligibility, then temporal resource conflicts, then academic-policy gates, and capacity and staffing last.

1. Both the `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers are present and non-empty. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized role list. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Every identifier embedded in the request path exists, validated left to right. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter. Failure is the 404 belonging to the first missing identifier — one of `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOME_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SUPPORT_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`.
4. A scope-bound role acts only inside its own scope, as described in §2.1. Applies to every endpoint acted on by `PlacementCoordinator`, `HomeSchoolCounselor` or `SupportProviderStaff`. Failure: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_CENTER_SCOPE`, `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCHOOL_SCOPE` or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_PROVIDER_SCOPE` (403).
5. The request body is well formed: required inputs are present, enum values fall inside their declared domain, numerics fall inside their declared range, dates are ISO 8601 and booleans are booleans. Applies to every endpoint that takes a request body. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
6. Every identifier supplied inside the request body resolves to an existing row — `student_id`, `center_id`, `course_id`, `prior_course_record_id`, `placement_id`, `proctor_station_id`, `support_provider_id` and `prior_referral_id`. Applies to `POST /referrals`, `POST /prior-course-records`, `POST /students`, `POST /graduation-plans`, `POST /course-enrollments`, `POST /proctor-sessions`, `POST /support-sessions`, `POST /proctor-stations`, `POST /support-providers` and `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/resubmit`. Failure is the matching 404 from the set `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`.
7. An interval payload is strictly ordered: a proctored exam session's `scheduled_end` must be later than its `scheduled_start`, and a support session's `end_at` must be later than its `start_at`. Equal endpoints are not accepted. Applies to `POST /proctor-sessions` and `POST /support-sessions`. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422).
8. Segregation of duties, as defined in §2.9. Applies to `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/award-credit`, `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/deny-award` and `POST /placements/{placement_id}/approve-re-entry`. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. The optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.6: the supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version`. Applies to `POST /course-enrollments`, `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/award-credit`, `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/offer` and `PATCH /centers/{center_id}/staffing`. Failure: `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CENTER_VERSION` (412).
10. The target resource — and every resource whose state the action reads as a precondition — is in a state that permits the requested transition. Applies to every action endpoint. Failure: `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_SUPPORT_SESSION_STATE_INVALID` (409).
11. Uniqueness. A student's `state_student_id` is globally unique (`ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_STUDENT_ID`); a course's `code` is globally unique (`ERR_DUPLICATE_COURSE_CODE`); a student has at most one graduation plan (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN`); a student has at most one referral in a non-terminal status, meaning `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `PLACEMENT_OFFERED` or `WAITLISTED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_REFERRAL`); a placement holds at most one enrollment per course whose `progress_state` is anything other than `AWARD_DENIED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`); and a historical transcript row backs at most one in-flight credit-recovery enrollment, meaning one whose `progress_state` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` (`ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_ALREADY_ENROLLED`). Once that attempt reaches `AWARDED` the historical row is instead guarded by the replacement rule at rung 13. Applies to `POST /students`, `POST /courses`, `POST /graduation-plans`, `POST /referrals` and `POST /course-enrollments`. All six are 409.
12. Academic-integrity gates. An enrollment whose `integrity_state` is `FLAGGED` accepts no progress report, no award submission and no award (`ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`); a course whose `proctoring_required` is true cannot be submitted for award while the enrollment has zero `COMPLETED` proctored exam sessions (`ERR_PROCTORED_EXAM_MISSING`); and a proctored exam session may only be booked for an enrollment whose course has `proctoring_required` true (`ERR_PROCTORING_POLICY_VIOLATION`). Applies to `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/progress`, `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/submit-for-award`, `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/award-credit` and `POST /proctor-sessions`. All three are 409.
13. Catalog and recovery eligibility. A credit-recovery enrollment requires a `prior_course_record_id` (`ERR_MISSING_PRIOR_RECORD`); that record's `outcome` must be `FAILED` (`ERR_COURSE_NOT_PREVIOUSLY_FAILED`); its `student_id` and `course_id` must match the placement's student and the requested course (`ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_MISMATCH`); its `replacement_status` must be `NOT_REPLACED` (`ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_ALREADY_REPLACED`); a proctored exam session's station must sit in the same center as the enrollment's placement (`ERR_STATION_CENTER_MISMATCH`); and a support session's provider must serve the same center as its placement (`ERR_PROVIDER_CENTER_MISMATCH`). Applies to `POST /course-enrollments`, `POST /proctor-sessions` and `POST /support-sessions`. All six are 409.
14. Resource interval conflicts. A proctoring station admits one session at a time, compared as a half-open interval extended by the station's turnaround buffer (`ERR_STATION_CONFLICT`); a support provider admits at most its concurrent-caseload cap of intersecting sessions, compared as closed intervals (`ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Applies to `POST /proctor-sessions` and `POST /support-sessions`. Both are 409.
15. Re-entry to the home school requires the student's graduation plan `posture` to be `ON_TRACK` or `AT_RISK`; an `OFF_TRACK` plan blocks the request. Applies to `POST /placements/{placement_id}/request-re-entry`. Failure: `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_BLOCKS_RE_ENTRY` (409).
16. Re-entry requires an `attendance_rate` of at least 0.9000; an attendance rate that is null because no enrolled days have been logged fails this check. Applies to `POST /placements/{placement_id}/request-re-entry`. Failure: `ERR_ATTENDANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (409).
17. Re-entry requires a `readiness_score` of at least 0.7500. Applies to `POST /placements/{placement_id}/request-re-entry`. Failure: `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (409).
18. Credit-award academic gates, themselves evaluated in this internal order: first the enrollment's `final_percent` must be at least the course's `min_passing_percent` (`ERR_FINAL_PERCENT_BELOW_PASSING`); then, under the competency policy, `mastery_pct` must be at least 70.00 (`ERR_MASTERY_NOT_MET`); then, under the seat-time policy, the computed credits must be greater than 0.00 (`ERR_SEAT_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`). Applies to `POST /course-enrollments/{course_enrollment_id}/award-credit`. All three are 409.
19. Center capacity and staffing. A center whose `intake_posture` is `FROZEN` accepts no new offer and no new enrollment (`ERR_INTAKE_FROZEN`); a `RESTRICTED` center accepts a new offer only for a referral on the `BEHAVIOR` pathway or with a `priority_score` of at least 0.5000 (`ERR_INTAKE_RESTRICTED`); and a staffing change that would leave `staffing_headroom` below zero against the center's existing `ACTIVE` and `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` placements is rejected (`ERR_STAFFING_RATIO_EXCEEDED`). Applies to `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/offer`, `POST /course-enrollments` and `PATCH /centers/{center_id}/staffing`. All three are 409.
20. The logical clock may only move forward: the supplied `current_utc` must be at or later than the clock's present value. Applies to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. Failure: `ERR_CLOCK_BACKWARD` (409).

### 2.11. Path Identifier Resolution

Because every business collection in this API is mounted at the root rather than nested inside a parent, most paths carry a single identifier: `home_school_id`, `center_id`, `course_id`, `student_id`, `prior_course_record_id`, `graduation_plan_id`, `referral_id`, `placement_id`, `course_enrollment_id`, `credit_award_id`, `proctor_station_id`, `proctor_session_id`, `support_provider_id` or `support_session_id`. Where a path carries more than one identifier, they are resolved strictly left to right and the request fails on the **first** one that does not resolve, with that identifier's own 404 code, before any later identifier is looked at. Identifier resolution always precedes scope checking, which in turn precedes body validation, as the ladder in §2.10 fixes.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the District: Schools, Centers, Catalog and Rosters

Before a single student can be referred, a `DistrictAdmin` must register the sending schools. A home school is created with a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a state reporting `district_code` of 1 to 20 characters, and an `accountability_cohort_year` between 2000 and 2100 identifying the four-year graduation cohort currently under accountability review. Everything the school reports about that cohort is derived. `cohort_size` counts the students whose `home_school_id` is this school and whose `cohort_year` equals the school's accountability cohort year, in any enrollment status. `graduates_count` counts that same cohort narrowed to students whose `status` is `GRADUATED`. `alt_ed_placed_count` counts the distinct students in that cohort who have at least one placement, in any placement status. `adjusted_grad_rate` is the graduate count divided by the cohort size, rounded to 4 decimal places. From that rate comes `cohort_flag`: `COMPLIANT` when the rate is at least 0.8500, otherwise `WATCH` when it is at least 0.7000, otherwise `PRIORITY`. A school with an empty cohort is a special case with its own answer — when the cohort size is zero, the adjusted rate is null and the flag reads `NOT_RATED`, which is exactly what a freshly registered school reports: counts of 0, 0 and 0, a null rate, and `NOT_RATED`.

Opening a center is likewise a `DistrictAdmin` act, supplying the `name`, `seat_capacity`, `certified_teacher_fte`, `support_staff_fte` and `max_students_per_teacher` values whose derivations §2.7 lays out; at creation the center immediately reports its `restricted_seat_margin`, `seats_available`, `staffing_headroom` and `intake_posture` computed from those inputs against an empty roster. Staffing is not frozen afterwards. A `DistrictAdmin` may revise any of `certified_teacher_fte`, `support_staff_fte` and `max_students_per_teacher` — each independently optional in the revision, so a caller may send one, two or all three — under the stale-version guard of §2.6. A revision that would leave `staffing_headroom` below zero when measured against the center's existing `ACTIVE` and `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` placements is refused outright with `ERR_STAFFING_RATIO_EXCEEDED`; the district will not let a paper edit create a ratio violation for students already sitting in the building. A revision that is accepted re-derives `student_teacher_ratio`, `staffing_headroom` and `intake_posture`, and — because raising the certified FTE can lift a center out of `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED` — may trigger the single waitlist promotion described in §2.7.

The course catalog is also district-owned. Each course is published with a `code` of 2 to 16 characters that is unique across the catalog and doubles as the transcript course code, a `title` of 1 to 120 characters, a `subject_area` drawn from `ELA`, `MATH`, `SCIENCE`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES` and `ELECTIVE` that determines which graduation-plan subject bucket the credit lands in, a `credit_value` that is a multiple of 0.25 between 0.25 and 2.00 representing full credit for complete mastery, and a `modality` of `ONLINE`, `PACKET` or `BLENDED`. Instructional structure is captured by `total_modules`, the number of gradable modules, which must be at least 1; by `required_seat_hours`, the seat hours required for full credit under the seat-time policy, which must be greater than zero and is carried to 2 decimal places; and by `pacing_days`, the calendar days allotted to finish the course at expected pace, which must be at least 1. Assessment policy is captured by `proctoring_required`, which must be true for `ONLINE` and `BLENDED` courses and marks a course whose proctored exam must be completed before award submission; by `proctored_exam_weight`, the share the proctored exam takes inside the mastery computation, accepted between 0.00 and 0.60 at 2 decimal places and defaulting to 0.40 when omitted; by `ncaa_approved`, marking a course on the district's NCAA approved-core list; and by `min_passing_percent`, the lowest final percent that may be submitted for credit, accepted between 0 and 100 and defaulting to 60 when omitted.

Students are registered by either a `DistrictAdmin` or a `Registrar`, with a `home_school_id` naming the school of record, a `state_student_id` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all students, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `cohort_year` between 2000 and 2100, and a `grade_level` that must be exactly 9, 10, 11 or 12. A new student's `status` begins at `ENROLLED_HOME`; the status advances to `PLACED_ALT` when a seat offer is accepted, to `RE_ENTERED` when a re-entry is approved, to `EXITED_ALT` when a placement is exited without re-entry, and to `GRADUATED` when a graduation plan completes. At creation the derived transcript totals `credits_earned_total`, `alt_ed_credits` and `ncaa_acceptable_credits` all read 0.00 and `gpa` reads null.

Historical transcript rows are the `Registrar`'s exclusive province, and they are the raw material of credit recovery. Each row records the `student_id` whose transcript it belongs to, the `course_id` originally attempted, a `term_code` of 1 to 16 characters such as a spring-semester label, the `original_percent` earned on that attempt between 0 and 100 at 2 decimal places, the `credits_attempted` the original attempt was worth as a multiple of 0.25 between 0.25 and 2.00, and the `outcome`, which is `FAILED`, `PASSED` or `INCOMPLETE`. From the original percent the system derives `original_grade_code` by letter bands: `A` at 90 or above, `B` at 80 or above, `C` at 70 or above, `D` at 60 or above, and `F` below 60 — except that a row whose outcome is `INCOMPLETE` carries the literal code `I` regardless of percent. Every new row starts with `replacement_status` set to `NOT_REPLACED`, and posting one re-derives the student's `credits_earned_total` and `gpa` immediately. Only rows whose outcome is `FAILED` are eligible for credit recovery; that restriction is the whole point of the row.

Two kinds of on-site resource complete the setup. Proctoring stations are registered by a `DistrictAdmin` or a `PlacementCoordinator` against the `center_id` the station physically sits in, with a room-or-seat `label` of 1 to 32 characters and a `station_type` of `COMPUTER` or `PAPER`. A new station reports a `turnaround_buffer_minutes` of 15 — a fixed sanitation and reset window appended to the end of every booked session before the next booking may begin — and a `scheduled_session_count` of 0, which counts the sessions on that station whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. Support providers are assigned by a `DistrictAdmin` to a `center_id` with a `role_type` of `COUNSELOR`, `SOCIAL_WORKER` or `BEHAVIOR_SPECIALIST`, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and an optional `max_concurrent_students` between 1 and 8 expressing how many students the provider may hold in one overlapping time band; when omitted it defaults to 4. A new provider's `completed_session_count`, which counts its support sessions whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, reads 0.

### 3.2. Graduation Plans, the Credit Ledger and What "On Track" Means

A student cannot receive alternative-education credit without a graduation plan, because the plan is where the district's grade-replacement policy is pinned and where progress is judged. A `Registrar` opens exactly one plan per student — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN` — supplying the `student_id` of the owner, a `required_credits_total` that is a multiple of 0.25 between 0.25 and 40.00 reflecting that student's possibly modified graduation requirements, a `grade_replacement_policy` of either `REPLACE_ON_RECOVERY` or `AVERAGE_ON_RECOVERY`, and an optional `projected_completion_term` of 1 to 16 characters. The plan opens in `DRAFT`.

A `DRAFT` plan is inert: credit may not be awarded against it. The `Registrar` activates it, moving `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, and may revise `projected_completion_term` in the same act. Activation is only legal from `DRAFT`; attempting it on a plan that has already moved on is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, and that same code is what refuses a credit award aimed at a plan still sitting in `DRAFT`. An award attempted for a student who has no plan at all fails earlier and differently, with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

The plan reports a four-figure credit ledger, all of it derived on read. `credits_earned` mirrors the owner's `credits_earned_total`, which is the sum of `credits_attempted` across the student's historical rows whose `outcome` is `PASSED`, plus the sum of `credits_awarded` across the student's credit awards whose `status` is `POSTED`, the total rounded to 2 decimal places. `credits_in_progress` is the sum of `projected_credits` across the student's course enrollments whose `progress_state` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD`, rounded to 2 decimal places — work that is under way but not yet banked. `credits_remaining` is the required total minus the earned total, floored at zero so it never reports a negative remainder, rounded to 2 decimal places. Alongside those, the student's own record separates the alternative-education slice of the ledger: `alt_ed_credits` sums `credits_awarded` across `POSTED` awards, and `ncaa_acceptable_credits` sums the same but only over awards whose `ncaa_acceptable` flag is true, each rounded to 2 decimal places.

Whether a student is on track is a grade-level question, not an absolute one. The plan derives `expected_credits_by_grade` as the required credit total multiplied by a grade-progress factor, rounded to 2 decimal places, where a ninth grader's factor is 0.25, a tenth grader's is 0.50, an eleventh grader's is 0.75 and a twelfth grader's is 1.00 — a senior is expected to have everything. From that expectation comes `posture`, evaluated in a fixed order. A plan whose `status` is `COMPLETED` reads `ON_TRACK` unconditionally, regardless of the grade-level expectation. Otherwise, if the earned credits are at least ninety-five percent of the expected credits by grade — that threshold itself rounded to 2 decimal places before the comparison is made — the posture is `ON_TRACK`. Otherwise, if the earned credits are at least eighty percent of that same expectation, again rounded to 2 decimal places before comparing, the posture is `AT_RISK`. Otherwise the posture is `OFF_TRACK`. Posture matters beyond reporting: an `OFF_TRACK` plan blocks its student's re-entry request outright, as §3.9 details.

One invariant is asserted across the whole credit ledger after every award posting and every award void: the plan's `credits_earned`, the student's `credits_earned_total`, and the rounded sum of passed historical credits plus posted award credits must all be equal. Historical rows stamped `REPLACED` and awards moved to `VOIDED` contribute nothing to that sum. This is the district's named conserved quantity, and §3.8 states the zero-drift obligation it imposes on a void.

### 3.3. Referral Intake, Pathways and Priority Scoring

A referral is authored by the sending school's `HomeSchoolCounselor`, never by the center. The counselor names the `student_id` being referred and the `center_id` receiving the referral, declares a `pathway` of `BEHAVIOR`, `CREDIT_DEFICIENCY` or `RE_ENGAGEMENT`, and supplies the three intake counters that quantify the case: `deficiency_credits`, the credits the student is behind at referral time, which must be zero or greater and a multiple of 0.25; `behavior_incident_count`, office discipline referrals in the current year, zero or greater; and `days_disengaged`, consecutive school days absent or unenrolled before this referral, also zero or greater. A `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters carries the counselor's account. A referral may also cite a `prior_referral_id`, a nullable reference to an earlier referral this one re-opens; when omitted it is null. A student may hold only one referral in flight at a time — that is, one whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `PLACEMENT_OFFERED` or `WAITLISTED` — and a second concurrent attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_REFERRAL`. A new referral's `status` begins at `SUBMITTED`.

The `priority_score` decides who gets the next seat, and it is computed at referral time from the three counters, capped so that no single dimension can dominate without limit. The deficiency component is the deficiency credits divided by a cap of 8.00 credits, held at a maximum of 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places. The behavior component is the behavior incident count divided by a cap of 5 incidents, likewise held at a maximum of 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places. The disengagement component is the days disengaged divided by a cap of 30 days, again held at 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places. A student four credits behind and a student twelve credits behind therefore differ, but a student sixteen credits behind scores no higher than one eight credits behind.

The declared pathway then selects which of the three components carries the most weight. On the `CREDIT_DEFICIENCY` pathway the weights are 0.60 for deficiency, 0.20 for behavior and 0.20 for disengagement. On the `BEHAVIOR` pathway they are 0.20, 0.60 and 0.20 in that same order. On the `RE_ENGAGEMENT` pathway they are 0.20, 0.20 and 0.60. Each weighted term is rounded to 4 decimal places **before** the three are summed, and the sum is rounded to 4 decimal places as well — the rounding order is pinned and produces a different result from summing first. That figure is the base score. When the referral cites a `prior_referral_id`, a re-referral bump of 0.0500 is added to the base and the result is held at a maximum of 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places; when no prior referral is cited the base is the score unchanged. The bump applies whenever a prior referral is cited, regardless of what status that earlier referral is in.

Screening is the coordinator's work. A `PlacementCoordinator` at the receiving center takes a referral from `SUBMITTED` to `SCREENING`, optionally recording a `screening_note`. From `SCREENING` the coordinator has four onward moves — requesting more information, offering a seat, waitlisting, or denying — and the seat-related three belong to §3.4. Requesting more information moves the referral `SCREENING → INFO_REQUESTED` and records the required `missing_items` describing what the home school must supply. Denial moves the referral `SCREENING → DENIED`, a terminal status, recording the required `denial_reason`. Every one of these coordinator transitions requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equal the referral's `center_id`, and each increments the referral's `version` by 1. A transition attempted from the wrong status — screening something already screened, denying something already denied — is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`.

Intake can loop. From `INFO_REQUESTED` the sending school's `HomeSchoolCounselor` resubmits, moving the referral `INFO_REQUESTED → SCREENING`, clearing `missing_items`, replacing `narrative` and overwriting whichever of `deficiency_credits`, `behavior_incident_count` and `days_disengaged` are supplied — each optional in the resubmission. Because the counters may have changed, the resubmission **recomputes `priority_score` from the revised inputs** using exactly the same weighting and rounding rules described above, including the re-referral bump if a prior referral is cited. Resubmission requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equal the `home_school_id` of the referred student, and it increments the referral's `version` by 1. A referral may travel the `SCREENING → INFO_REQUESTED → SCREENING` loop any number of times; there is no cap and no penalty.

### 3.4. Seating Decisions: Offers, the Waitlist, Withdrawal and Acceptance

Offering a seat is the moment a referral becomes a capacity commitment, so it is the most heavily guarded transition in the intake flow. A `PlacementCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the referral's `center_id` moves a referral from `SCREENING` to `PLACEMENT_OFFERED`, supplying `expected_center_version` per §2.6 and optionally `offer_valid_days`. The offer is permitted when the center's `intake_posture` is `OPEN`, and also when the posture is `RESTRICTED` provided the referral's `pathway` is `BEHAVIOR` or its `priority_score` is at least 0.5000 — a behavior placement is never held up by a thin seat cushion. A `FROZEN` posture refuses the offer with `ERR_INTAKE_FROZEN`; a `RESTRICTED` posture with a referral that qualifies for neither exemption refuses it with `ERR_INTAKE_RESTRICTED`. On success the system sets `offer_expires_on` to the logical clock date plus `offer_valid_days`, defaulting to 10 days when the parameter is omitted, leaves `promoted_from_waitlist` at false, increases the center's `seats_reserved` by one and decreases `seats_available` by one, re-derives the center's `intake_posture`, and increments both the center's `version` and the referral's `version` by 1. The expiry date is informational only: no sweep job ever acts on it, and an offer past its expiry remains outstanding until a human withdraws it.

Waitlisting is the coordinator's answer when the case is sound but the seat is not there. It moves the referral from `SCREENING` to `WAITLISTED` with a required `waitlist_reason`, reserves no seat whatsoever, and increments the referral's `version` by 1. Because a waitlisted referral holds no seat, waitlisting has no effect on the center's posture. What it does buy the student is a place in the promotion queue: as §2.7 specifies, when the center's posture is re-derived into `OPEN`, exactly one waitlisted referral of that center — the one with the highest `priority_score`, ties broken by the earliest `created_at` — is transitioned `WAITLISTED → PLACEMENT_OFFERED` by the system itself. A system promotion is distinguishable from a coordinator offer by two fields: `promoted_from_waitlist` is set to true, and `offer_expires_on` is set to the logical clock date plus 5 days rather than to the coordinator's chosen window. Like a manual offer, a promotion raises the center's `seats_reserved` by one, re-derives the posture a second time within the same request, and increments the referral's `version` by 1.

An outstanding offer is not irrevocable. The coordinator may withdraw it, moving the referral from `PLACEMENT_OFFERED` back to `SCREENING` and optionally recording a fresh `screening_note`. Withdrawal is the exact compensating action for the offer: `offer_expires_on` is cleared to null, `promoted_from_waitlist` is reset to false, the center's `seats_reserved` decreases by one and `seats_available` increases by one, restoring the seat ledger to precisely the values it held before the offer was made, and the center's `intake_posture` is re-derived. Because releasing a reserved seat can lift the posture into `OPEN`, a withdrawal may itself trigger the single waitlist promotion — meaning a coordinator who withdraws one offer may see a different student's referral offered in the same response cycle. Both the center's `version` and the referral's `version` increment by 1.

Acceptance is the sending school's decision, not the center's. A `HomeSchoolCounselor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the referred student's `home_school_id` moves the referral from `PLACEMENT_OFFERED` to `ACCEPTED`, a terminal status, supplying the two inputs that define the placement: `credit_policy`, which is either `SEAT_TIME` or `COMPETENCY` and is fixed here for the whole placement, and `start_date`, the first day of attendance at the center as an ISO date. Acceptance creates a placement whose `referral_id`, `student_id` and `center_id` are copied from the referral and whose `status` begins at `ACTIVE`, and it sets the student's `status` to `PLACED_ALT`. The seat accounting is a conversion rather than a new commitment: the center's `seats_reserved` falls by one while `active_student_count` rises by one, so `seats_committed` is unchanged across the acceptance, and `student_teacher_ratio`, `staffing_headroom` and `intake_posture` are all re-derived. The center's `version` and the referral's `version` each increment by 1. Choosing `credit_policy` at this moment is consequential and irreversible: it decides which of the two credit-award variants in §3.8 will govern every course the student takes under this placement.

### 3.5. Coursework at the Center: Enrollment, Recovery Eligibility, Attendance and Pacing

Enrolling a placed student in a catalog course is instructional work, performed by an `AltEdTeacher`. The enrollment names the `placement_id` the seat work is done under, the `course_id` from the catalog, an `enrollment_type` of either `CREDIT_RECOVERY` or `NEW_CREDIT`, and — for recovery only — the `prior_course_record_id` of the historical row being recovered. `expected_placement_version` accompanies the request under the stale-version guard of §2.6, and a successful create increments the placement's `version` by 1. A center whose `intake_posture` is `FROZEN` accepts no new enrollment at all and refuses it with `ERR_INTAKE_FROZEN`; a `RESTRICTED` posture, by contrast, still admits enrollments, because the student is already occupying a seat and adding coursework consumes none. A placement may hold only one enrollment per course whose `progress_state` is anything other than `AWARD_DENIED`, so a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` — while a course whose earlier attempt was denied may be re-attempted.

Credit recovery is deliberately narrow: **only a course the student has previously failed may be recovered.** A `CREDIT_RECOVERY` enrollment that omits `prior_course_record_id` is refused with `ERR_MISSING_PRIOR_RECORD`. A cited record whose `outcome` is `PASSED` or `INCOMPLETE` rather than `FAILED` is refused with `ERR_COURSE_NOT_PREVIOUSLY_FAILED` — the course was never failed, so there is nothing to recover. A cited record whose `student_id` is not the placement's student, or whose `course_id` is not the course being enrolled, is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_MISMATCH`. A record already stamped `REPLACED` by an earlier successful recovery is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_ALREADY_REPLACED`. And a record that already backs an in-flight recovery attempt — one whose `progress_state` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` — is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_ALREADY_ENROLLED`, so a student cannot run two simultaneous recoveries of the same failure.

A new enrollment is stamped with `enrolled_on`, the logical clock date at creation, which becomes its pacing baseline. Its `progress_state` begins at `IN_PROGRESS` and its `integrity_state` begins at `CLEAR`; these are two genuinely independent axes on the same row, and §3.6 explains how the integrity axis moves without disturbing the progress axis. Its accumulators `modules_passed` and `seat_hours_logged` begin at 0, its `module_avg_percent`, `proctored_exam_percent`, `final_percent` and `awarded_credits` all begin as null, and on the enrollment day itself its `pace_index` is null with `pacing_flag` reading `NOT_YET_RATED`, because no instructional day has yet elapsed.

An `AltEdTeacher` reports coursework progress against an in-progress enrollment, supplying any combination of three optional inputs: `modules_passed_delta`, which advances `modules_passed` but is capped so the accumulator never exceeds the course's `total_modules`; `seat_hours_delta`, which advances `seat_hours_logged`; and `module_avg_percent`, which does not accumulate but **replaces** the previously reported running module average outright. A progress report on an enrollment whose `integrity_state` is `FLAGGED` is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. A successful report re-derives the enrollment's `mastery_pct`, `pace_index`, `pacing_flag` and `projected_credits`, and it reaches upward to re-derive the owning placement's `pacing_component`, `readiness_base_score`, `engagement_bonus` and `readiness_score`.

Pacing answers a single question: is the student getting through the material as fast as the course expects? Let the elapsed days be the number of days from the enrollment's `enrolled_on` date to the current logical clock date, which is zero on the enrollment day itself. When the elapsed days are zero, `pace_index` is null and `pacing_flag` is `NOT_YET_RATED`. Otherwise the expected fraction is the elapsed days divided by the course's `pacing_days`, rounded to 4 decimal places and held at a maximum of 1.0000; the actual fraction is `modules_passed`, itself held at a maximum of the course's `total_modules`, divided by `total_modules` and rounded to 4 decimal places; and `pace_index` is the actual fraction divided by the expected fraction, rounded to 3 decimal places. The resulting `pacing_flag` is `ON_PACE` when the index is at least 0.900, otherwise `BEHIND` when it is at least 0.700, otherwise `CRITICAL`. Because the expected fraction is capped at 1.0000, a student who finishes every module still reports an index of 1.000 rather than an inflated one, and a student who finishes early reports an index above 1.000 only while the course window is still open.

Attendance is logged separately, also by an `AltEdTeacher`, against a placement rather than an enrollment. The report supplies `days_present_delta` and `days_enrolled_delta`, both required, which advance the placement's `days_present` and `days_enrolled` accumulators. From those, `attendance_rate` is derived as days present divided by days enrolled, rounded to 4 decimal places, and it reads null while `days_enrolled` is still zero. A successful attendance posting re-derives `attendance_rate`, `attendance_component`, `readiness_base_score`, `engagement_bonus`, `readiness_score` and `re_entry_eligible`. Attendance may only be logged against a placement in a status that permits it; a posting against a `RE_ENTERED` or `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL` placement is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.6. Proctored Examinations and the Academic-Integrity Axis

Online and blended coursework only carries weight with a postsecondary reviewer if the summative exam was supervised, which is why every `ONLINE` and `BLENDED` course must be published with `proctoring_required` true. A `PlacementCoordinator` books the exam slot, naming the `course_enrollment_id` whose proctored exam the session delivers, the `proctor_station_id` that will be occupied, and the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` instants as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. The end must be strictly later than the start; equal instants are refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Booking a session for an enrollment whose course has `proctoring_required` false is refused with `ERR_PROCTORING_POLICY_VIOLATION` — an unproctored course has no exam to supervise. Booking on a station whose `center_id` differs from the center of the enrollment's placement is refused with `ERR_STATION_CENTER_MISMATCH`; a student cannot sit an exam in another center's building.

Station occupancy is exclusive and buffered. A station admits one session at a time, and the occupancy test is a **half-open** interval comparison — a session ending exactly when another begins does not conflict on its own — but each existing session's end is extended by the station's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` of 15 before the comparison is made, so the next booking may only begin a quarter of an hour after the previous session's scheduled end. Both `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` sessions occupy their station for this purpose; a session marked as a no-show does not. An overlap is refused with `ERR_STATION_CONFLICT`. A successful booking starts the session's `status` at `SCHEDULED` and increases the station's `scheduled_session_count` by 1.

A session ends one of two ways, and only from `SCHEDULED` — attempting either move on a session that has already ended is refused with `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`. The coordinator records the result by completing the session, moving `status` from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED` with a required `score_percent` between 0 and 100 at 2 decimal places and an optional `integrity_incident` boolean stating whether the proctor observed a violation. Completion makes this session's score visible to the enrollment as `proctored_exam_percent` — defined as the `score_percent` of the latest `COMPLETED` session for that enrollment when ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the most recently created completed session wins, and null while no session for the enrollment has completed — and therefore re-derives the enrollment's `mastery_pct` and `projected_credits`. If `integrity_incident` is true, completion additionally drives the enrollment's `integrity_state` from `CLEAR` to `FLAGGED` and stamps its `integrity_incident_code` with the literal value `PROCTOR_INCIDENT`. The session's `version` increments by 1. Alternatively, the coordinator marks a no-show, moving `status` from `SCHEDULED` to `NO_SHOW` with an optional `no_show_note`; this releases the station interval so that an overlapping booking now succeeds, decreases the station's `scheduled_session_count` by 1, and increments the session's `version` by 1. Both `COMPLETED` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal.

Mastery blends coursework with the supervised exam. When the enrollment's `module_avg_percent` is still null, `mastery_pct` is null and no mastery judgement can be made. When the course has `proctoring_required` true **and** a `proctored_exam_percent` is available, `mastery_pct` is the module average multiplied by one minus the course's `proctored_exam_weight`, plus the proctored exam percent multiplied by that same weight, the result rounded to 2 decimal places — so with the default weight of 0.40 the coursework carries sixty percent and the exam forty. In every other case, meaning an unproctored course or a proctored course with no completed exam yet, `mastery_pct` is simply the module average rounded to 2 decimal places.

Academic integrity travels on its own axis, and it moves independently of the award axis: `integrity_state` runs `CLEAR → FLAGGED → CLEARED` or `CLEAR → FLAGGED → VOIDED_FOR_INTEGRITY`, while `progress_state` continues to run `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD → AWARDED` or `→ AWARD_DENIED`. Neither axis constrains which value the other holds; the only place the award axis reads the integrity axis is the credit-award precondition. Raising a flag moves `integrity_state` from `CLEAR` to `FLAGGED` and is available both to a `PlacementCoordinator` and to a `DistrictAdmin`, who supply the required `integrity_incident_code`; the same transition is also driven by the system when a proctored session completes with an incident, in which case the code is the literal `PROCTOR_INCIDENT`. Flagging does not change `progress_state`, but it does have two immediate consequences: the enrollment's placement reports `re_entry_eligible` as false for as long as any of its enrollments is `FLAGGED`, and the enrollment itself accepts no progress report, no award submission and no award, each refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. The enrollment's `version` increments by 1.

Only a `DistrictAdmin` may resolve a flag, and only from `FLAGGED` — a resolution attempted against an unflagged enrollment is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The resolution request carries a required `resolution` naming which terminal integrity value to adopt, plus an optional `integrity_resolution_note`. The two branches are deliberately asymmetric in consequence and must both be implemented in full. When `resolution` is `CLEARED`, `integrity_state` moves `FLAGGED → CLEARED`, the note is recorded, `progress_state` is left exactly as it was, and progress and award activity resume — because the award precondition accepts `CLEARED` just as it accepts `CLEAR`. When `resolution` is `VOIDED_FOR_INTEGRITY`, `integrity_state` moves `FLAGGED → VOIDED_FOR_INTEGRITY`, the note is recorded, and additionally, if this enrollment already has a credit award in `POSTED` status, that award is voided with `void_reason` set to the literal `INTEGRITY_VOID` and the entire void compensation cascade of §3.8 runs. Either branch increments the enrollment's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. On-Site Support Services

Support services are the part of the model that recognizes credit recovery is not only an academic problem. A support session is scheduled either by a `PlacementCoordinator` or by the delivering `SupportProviderStaff`, naming the `support_provider_id` who will deliver the service, the `placement_id` receiving it, the `start_at` and `end_at` instants as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps, and a `service_code` drawn from `ACADEMIC_COUNSELING`, `BEHAVIOR_COACHING`, `SOCIAL_WORK` and `RE_ENGAGEMENT_CHECKIN`. The end must be strictly later than the start, and an equal or inverted pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A provider whose `center_id` differs from the center of the session's placement is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CENTER_MISMATCH`.

Provider capacity is a group-session cap rather than an exclusivity rule, and it is compared differently from station occupancy. A provider may hold up to `max_concurrent_students` sessions in one overlapping time band, and the intersection test is a **closed** interval comparison — sessions that merely touch at an endpoint are treated as intersecting, which is the opposite of the station rule in §3.6 and is intentional, since a counselor cannot hand off one student at the exact instant they receive another. When the count of intersecting sessions in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status, plus the session being scheduled, would exceed the provider's cap, the booking is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful booking starts the session's `status` at `SCHEDULED`.

Delivery is recorded by the provider itself and nobody else: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the session's `support_provider_id`, and completion moves `status` from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED`, which is terminal. The completing request supplies a required `minutes_delivered` between 1 and 480 recording the minutes actually delivered, and an optional `completion_note`. Completing a session that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_SUPPORT_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`. Completion increases the delivering provider's `completed_session_count` by 1, increases the receiving placement's `support_sessions_completed` by 1 — that figure being the count of the placement's support sessions in `COMPLETED` status — and, through that count, can raise the placement's `engagement_bonus` and therefore its `readiness_score`, as §3.9 details. The session's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Awarding Credit: Seat Time, Competency, the Transcript Row, and the Void That Reverses It

Submitting completed work for award is a shared responsibility: either an `AltEdTeacher` or a `Registrar` may do it, supplying the required `final_percent` — the final course percent of record. The submission moves `progress_state` from `IN_PROGRESS` to `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` and is legal only while the owning placement's `status` is `ACTIVE`; a submission against a placement that has moved to `RE_ENTRY_PENDING`, `RE_ENTERED` or `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL` is refused. A course whose `proctoring_required` is true may not be submitted while the enrollment has zero `COMPLETED` proctored exam sessions, which is refused with `ERR_PROCTORED_EXAM_MISSING`; an unproctored course carries no such requirement. An enrollment whose `integrity_state` is `FLAGGED` may not be submitted, which is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. On success the system records `final_percent` and captures the acting `X-User-ID` into `submitted_by_user_id`, which is the comparand the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.9 will use at award time, and increments the enrollment's `version` by 1.

Awarding credit belongs to the `Registrar` alone, and it is the most heavily preconditioned action in the system. The enrollment's `progress_state` must be `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, or the award is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The supplied `expected_graduation_plan_version` must match the student's plan `version` per §2.6. The plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the award is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. The recorded `final_percent` must be at least the course's `min_passing_percent`, or the award is refused with `ERR_FINAL_PERCENT_BELOW_PASSING`. The enrollment's `integrity_state` must be either `CLEAR` or `CLEARED` — those two and no others — or the award is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. The computed credit figure must be greater than zero, and the refusal here depends on which policy governs: under `COMPETENCY` it is `ERR_MASTERY_NOT_MET`, under `SEAT_TIME` it is `ERR_SEAT_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`. Finally, when `enrollment_type` is `CREDIT_RECOVERY`, the backing historical row's `replacement_status` must still be `NOT_REPLACED`, or the award is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_RECORD_ALREADY_REPLACED`.

How many credits get posted depends entirely on the `credit_policy` fixed on the placement at acceptance, and the two variants are genuinely different accounting philosophies that must both be implemented. Under `SEAT_TIME` the student is paid for time in the seat: the seat fraction is `seat_hours_logged` divided by the course's `required_seat_hours`, rounded to 4 decimal places and held at a maximum of 1.0000, and the credits are the course's `credit_value` multiplied by that seat fraction, then floored down to a multiple of 0.25. Mastery is ignored entirely in this variant — a student with the hours banks the credit, and a student whose seat-hour computation floors all the way to 0.00 is refused with `ERR_SEAT_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`. Under `COMPETENCY` the student is paid for demonstrated mastery, in three bands: when `mastery_pct` is at least 80.00 the award is the course's full `credit_value` regardless of seat hours or unfinished modules; when `mastery_pct` is at least 70.00 but below 80.00 the award is the credit value multiplied by the module fraction — `modules_passed`, held at a maximum of the course's `total_modules`, divided by `total_modules`, rounded to 4 decimal places — then floored down to a multiple of 0.25; and when `mastery_pct` is below 70.00 the award computes to 0.00 and is refused with `ERR_MASTERY_NOT_MET`. In both variants the quarter-credit floor applies to this award alone, per §2.5. The same computation, run against whatever progress data exists at the moment, is what an enrollment continuously exposes as `projected_credits` — what the award *would* post right now.

A successful award posts a transcript row and then propagates through four hops. **Hop 1** moves `progress_state` from `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` to `AWARDED` and creates a credit award in `POSTED` status. That row carries the `course_enrollment_id` it was awarded for, the `student_id` denormalized from the enrollment's placement so the transcript can be queried by student, the `prior_course_record_id` it replaces or averages against when there is one, the computed `credits_awarded`, an `award_basis` copied verbatim from the placement's `credit_policy`, and a `posted_on` stamp holding the logical clock date at posting. It also carries the district's transcript coding: `modality_code` is `OL` for an `ONLINE` course, `PK` for `PACKET` and `BL` for `BLENDED`; `transcript_code` is the course's `code`, then a hyphen, then that modality code, then the literal suffix `-AE`; `alt_ed_flag` is always true, because every award posted through this system is alternative-education coded; `transcript_grade_code` is derived from the enrollment's `final_percent` by band — `A` at 90 or above, `B` at 80 or above, `C` at 70 or above, and otherwise `D`, there being no `F` band since a sub-passing percent cannot be posted at all; and `ncaa_acceptable` is true only when all four of the following hold together — the course's `ncaa_approved` is true, the course's `proctoring_required` is true, a `COMPLETED` proctored session with `integrity_incident` false exists for the enrollment, and `credits_awarded` equals the course's full `credit_value`. Partial credit is never NCAA-acceptable. The row's `grade_replacement_mode` is `NONE` when `enrollment_type` is `NEW_CREDIT`; otherwise it is `FULL_REPLACEMENT` when the student's plan carries `grade_replacement_policy` of `REPLACE_ON_RECOVERY`, and `AVERAGED` when the plan carries `AVERAGE_ON_RECOVERY`. Hop 1 also sets the enrollment's `awarded_credits` to the posted figure.

**Hop 2** reaches the historical transcript row. When `grade_replacement_mode` is `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, the backing record's `replacement_status` is stamped `REPLACED`, which drops that row out of the GPA input set entirely; when the mode is `AVERAGED` or `NONE`, the historical row is left as it stands. Either way the student's `credits_earned_total`, `alt_ed_credits`, `ncaa_acceptable_credits` and `gpa` all move. **Hop 3** carries that change into the graduation plan: `credits_earned`, `credits_remaining` and `posture` are recomputed, and when the earned credits reach or pass `required_credits_total` the plan transitions `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and the student's `status` becomes `GRADUATED`. **Hop 4** follows the graduating student back to the sending school, where `graduates_count`, `adjusted_grad_rate` and `cohort_flag` are all recomputed — which is precisely the cohort-accountability consequence the district cares about, since a student who graduates from the alternative center still counts in the home school's four-year rate. The award increments the enrollment's `version` by 1 and the graduation plan's `version` by 1.

Denial is the `Registrar`'s alternative, and it is guarded by the same segregation-of-duties comparand: the operator recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` may not deny the award either, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Denial moves `progress_state` from `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` to `AWARD_DENIED`, which is terminal on the progress axis, and records the required `denial_reason`. The recorded `final_percent` is deliberately retained for audit. No credit award row is created and the backing historical record keeps `replacement_status` of `NOT_REPLACED`. Because a denied enrollment leaves the owning placement's pacing input set, denial also re-derives that placement's `pacing_component` and `readiness_score`. The enrollment's `version` increments by 1.

GPA is where the district's grade-replacement flag shows its teeth. The transcript GPA is derived on every student read from two kinds of row. The first kind is historical: every prior course record of the student whose `replacement_status` is `NOT_REPLACED` and whose `outcome` is either `FAILED` or `PASSED` contributes its `original_grade_code` and its `credits_attempted`; a row whose outcome is `INCOMPLETE` never counts. The second kind is alternative-education: every credit award of the student whose `status` is `POSTED` and whose `credits_awarded` is greater than zero contributes its `transcript_grade_code` and its `credits_awarded`. Grade points are `A` worth 4.0, `B` worth 3.0, `C` worth 2.0, `D` worth 1.0 and `F` worth 0.0. The rounding order is pinned: each row's quality points — its grade points multiplied by its credits — are rounded to 3 decimal places **first**, those rounded per-row figures are summed, the credits of all rows are summed separately, and the GPA is the quality-point sum divided by the credit sum, rounded to 2 decimal places last. When the credit sum is zero the GPA is null. Rows are unordered, since addition is the only combining operation.

That mechanism produces two materially different transcripts from identical academic work. Under `REPLACE_ON_RECOVERY`, a recovery award posts with `grade_replacement_mode` of `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, the failed historical row is stamped `REPLACED` and leaves the input set entirely, so only the recovery grade counts and the denominator holds the recovery credits alone. Under `AVERAGE_ON_RECOVERY`, a recovery award posts with `grade_replacement_mode` of `AVERAGED`, the failed historical row stays `NOT_REPLACED`, and both rows count — the original `F` contributing 0.000 quality points against its full `credits_attempted` while the recovery contributes its own, with the denominator holding both attempts. The same student, the same work, a materially lower GPA.

A posted award can be undone, and the reversal must be exact. A `Registrar` voids an award, supplying a required `void_reason`, moving `status` from `POSTED` to `VOIDED`, which is terminal; voiding an award that is already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`. The same void is driven by the system, with `void_reason` set to the literal `INTEGRITY_VOID`, when a flagged enrollment is resolved as `VOIDED_FOR_INTEGRITY` per §3.6. The void runs the award cascade in reverse across the same four hops. **Hop 1** takes the row out of every `POSTED` aggregate, clears the enrollment's `awarded_credits` back to null — the value an enrollment reports whenever it has no `POSTED` award — and returns the enrollment's `progress_state` from `AWARDED` to `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD`. **Hop 2** returns the backing historical row's `replacement_status` to `NOT_REPLACED` when the voided award had `grade_replacement_mode` of `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, so that row re-enters the GPA input set, and restores the student's `credits_earned_total`, `alt_ed_credits`, `ncaa_acceptable_credits` and `gpa` to their pre-award values. **Hop 3** recomputes the plan's `credits_earned`, `credits_remaining` and `posture`, and a plan sitting in `COMPLETED` whose earned credits now fall below `required_credits_total` transitions `COMPLETED → ACTIVE`; the student's `status` is then re-derived from that student's placements, in this order — `RE_ENTERED` if the latest placement is `RE_ENTERED`, otherwise `PLACED_ALT` if any placement is `ACTIVE` or `RE_ENTRY_PENDING`, otherwise `EXITED_ALT` if the latest placement is `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`, otherwise `ENROLLED_HOME` — so the student leaves `GRADUATED`. **Hop 4** restores the sending school's `graduates_count`, `adjusted_grad_rate` and `cohort_flag` accordingly. The void increments the credit award's `version` by 1 and the graduation plan's `version` by 1. The obligation this cascade carries is stated as a zero-drift requirement: after voiding an award, the student's `credits_earned_total`, `alt_ed_credits`, `ncaa_acceptable_credits`, `gpa` and the backing row's `replacement_status` must equal exactly the values they held immediately before that award was posted.

### 3.9. Returning to the Home School, or Exiting Without Re-Entry

The point of an alternative education placement is to end. A placement ends in one of exactly two terminal statuses — `RE_ENTERED`, meaning the student went back to the comprehensive campus, or `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`, meaning the placement closed without that return — and the district's whole re-entry policy is encoded in a single readiness figure that decides which conversation is even allowed to start.

**Readiness** is a weighted blend of three components, all carried to 4 decimal places. The `attendance_component` is simply the placement's `attendance_rate`, or 0.0000 when that rate is null because no enrolled days have been logged. The `pacing_component` is computed over the placement's *rated* enrollments — those whose `progress_state` is `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` or `AWARDED`, with `AWARD_DENIED` enrollments excluded from the input set entirely. When the placement has no rated enrollments at all, the pacing component is 0.0000. Otherwise each rated enrollment contributes its `pace_index` held at a maximum of 1.0000, and an enrollment whose `pace_index` is still null because no instructional day has elapsed contributes a full 1.0000 rather than zero — a brand-new enrollment is given the benefit of the doubt. The pacing component is the sum of those per-enrollment terms divided by the number of rated enrollments, rounded to 4 decimal places. The `credit_component` measures recovery against the referral's declared shortfall: it is the sum of `credits_awarded` across the `POSTED` credit awards reachable through this placement's enrollments, divided by the `deficiency_credits` recorded on the placement's referral — with that denominator floored at 0.25 so a referral declaring no deficiency does not produce a division by zero — held at a maximum of 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places.

Those three feed `readiness_base_score`, which is the attendance component weighted at 0.50, plus the pacing component weighted at 0.30, plus the credit component weighted at 0.20, the sum rounded to 4 decimal places. On top of the base sits `engagement_bonus`, a two-tier reward for actually using the support services on site. The tier is selected on the **rounded base score**, in fixed order: when the base is at least 0.8000 and `support_sessions_completed` is at least 2, the bonus is 0.0500; otherwise, when the base is at least 0.6500 and `support_sessions_completed` is at least 1, the bonus is 0.0300; otherwise the bonus is 0.0000. Then `readiness_score` is the base plus the bonus, held at a maximum of 1.0000 and rounded to 4 decimal places. This ordering is pinned deliberately, because the definition looks circular — the score is base plus bonus while the bonus tier is keyed on the base. It is not circular in practice: components are computed, the base is rounded, the tier is looked up against that rounded base, and the readiness score is produced once. The bonus is never re-tiered against the readiness score, and no fixed-point iteration is performed.

A placement also publishes `re_entry_eligible` as a convenience flag, true only when all four of these hold simultaneously: `attendance_rate` is non-null and at least 0.9000; `readiness_score` is at least 0.7500; no enrollment of this placement has `integrity_state` of `FLAGGED`; and the student's graduation plan — in any of `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — has a `posture` other than `OFF_TRACK`.

Requesting re-entry is open to either a `PlacementCoordinator` or a `DistrictAdmin`, moves the placement from `ACTIVE` to `RE_ENTRY_PENDING`, and requires a `target_re_entry_date` naming the requested home-school re-entry date as an ISO date. The same four gates that drive the eligibility flag are enforced as hard preconditions here, and they are evaluated in this order: first the graduation plan's `posture`, where an `OFF_TRACK` plan refuses the request with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_BLOCKS_RE_ENTRY`; then attendance, where a null or below-0.9000 `attendance_rate` refuses it with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM`; then readiness, where a `readiness_score` below 0.7500 refuses it with `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; then integrity, where any enrollment of this placement sitting in `FLAGGED` refuses it with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD`. A request against a placement that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. On success the placement records `target_re_entry_date` and captures the acting `X-User-ID` into `re_entry_requested_by_user_id`, and its `version` increments by 1. The seat is **not** released at this point: a placement in `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` still counts inside the center's `active_student_count`, so nothing about the center's capacity picture changes yet.

Adjudication belongs to a `DistrictAdmin` and can go either way, any number of times. Denial moves the placement from `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` back to `ACTIVE`, records the required `re_entry_denial_reason`, and clears both `target_re_entry_date` and `re_entry_requested_by_user_id` back to null so a later request starts clean; the seat is held throughout and the placement's `version` increments by 1. The home school may bounce a re-entry request back this way repeatedly; the `ACTIVE → RE_ENTRY_PENDING → ACTIVE` loop has no cap. Approval moves the placement from `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` to `RE_ENTERED`, which is terminal, and records the required `actual_re_entry_date` confirmed by the home school. Approval is subject to segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `re_entry_requested_by_user_id`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the administrator who asked for the return may not be the one who grants it. Approval sets the student's `status` to `RE_ENTERED` and releases the seat: the center's `active_student_count` falls by one, `seats_committed` falls by one, `seats_available` rises by one, `student_teacher_ratio`, `staffing_headroom` and `intake_posture` are all re-derived, and — because releasing a committed seat can lift the posture into `OPEN` — a waitlisted referral may be promoted in the same request per §2.7. The center's `version` and the placement's `version` each increment by 1.

Exiting is the unsuccessful ending, and it belongs to the `PlacementCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the placement's `center_id`. It is reachable from **both** `ACTIVE` and `RE_ENTRY_PENDING` — a pending re-entry does not shield a placement from being closed out — and in both cases the destination is `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`, which is terminal, with a required `exit_reason` recorded and an identical side-effect set. The student's `status` becomes `EXITED_ALT`. Unfinished coursework is closed out conditionally: every enrollment of this placement whose `progress_state` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED_FOR_AWARD` moves to `AWARD_DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to the literal `PLACEMENT_EXITED`, each such enrollment's `version` incrementing by 1, while enrollments already sitting in `AWARDED` or `AWARD_DENIED` are left completely untouched — an exit never claws back credit already banked. Every proctored exam session in `SCHEDULED` status belonging to those enrollments and every support session in `SCHEDULED` status belonging to this placement are cancelled, releasing their reserved station and provider intervals so other students can book them. Finally the seat is released exactly as on an approved re-entry: `active_student_count` falls by one, `seats_committed` falls by one, `seats_available` rises by one, the `intake_posture` is re-derived, and one waitlisted referral may be promoted, which itself re-derives the posture a second time. The center's `version` and the placement's `version` each increment by 1.

One consequence of all this ties back to §2.6 and is easy to miss. A placement's `version` advances on attendance postings, on a re-entry request, and on a re-entry denial — none of which are obviously "structural" events. That is exactly why creating a course enrollment must carry `expected_placement_version`: a teacher who read the placement, then had a colleague log a day of attendance, is no longer looking at the placement they think they are.
