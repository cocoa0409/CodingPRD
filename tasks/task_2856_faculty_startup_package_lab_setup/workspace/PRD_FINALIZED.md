# Faculty Startup Package API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Recruiting a research-active professor is one of the most expensive commitments a university makes, and almost none of that commitment is a salary line. A dean's office negotiates a *startup package*: a headline dollar figure broken into equipment, personnel, renovation and discretionary money, scheduled across several fiscal years, and paid for jointly by the hiring department, the college and the dean's own discretionary pool. Today that negotiation lives in spreadsheets and email. Nobody can answer, on demand, how much of a five-year commitment has actually been spent, which laboratory rooms are double-promised, whether a construction overrun is the college's problem or the package's, or how much unspent money the institution gets back when the professor leaves for a competing offer two years in.

This API is the system of record for that whole arc. It models the negotiation itself as a state machine with a genuine counteroffer loop and an enforced separation of duties, so that the person who drafts a package is never the person who endorses it and never the person who approves it. It models money as an append-only ledger of cent-exact transactions rather than as mutable balance fields, so every encumbrance, release, disbursement, expiration, carryforward and recapture is individually attributable to one of the three funding units that paid for it. And it models the physical consequences of a startup package — the rooms assigned, the walls demolished, the instruments delivered and the graduate students hired — as first-class resources whose lifecycles are gated on the package's spend authority.

Two design constraints shape everything that follows. First, the system is **deterministic**: it reads time from a logical clock that a client sets explicitly, because fiscal-year boundaries, tranche expirations, vendor lead times and separation effective dates must be reproducible rather than dependent on wall-clock time. Second, the system is **conservative with money**: no operation ever encumbers a partial amount, no rounding step ever creates or destroys a cent, and every settlement of a prior commitment mirrors the exact per-funder split of the commitment it settles. Where a business rule and a convenience feature conflict, the business rule wins and the request is rejected with a specific, machine-readable error identifier.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply to every endpoint in the API. All collections are mounted as flat peers at the API root — `/funding-units`, `/faculty`, `/startup-packages`, `/commitment-tranches`, `/lab-spaces`, `/space-assignments`, `/renovation-projects`, `/equipment-orders`, `/staff-appointments` — with nesting used only for read-only sub-resource lists and for phase-level actions.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before any other check in the system. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `DeanOffice`, `DepartmentChair`, `Dean`, `FacilitiesManager`, and `Faculty`. Their spellings are exact and case-sensitive.

- **`DeanOffice`** — dean's-office staff who draft packages, schedule commitment tranches, propose laboratory space, place purchase orders, activate and end staff appointments, and settle departures. This role holds the requester and operator end of the negotiation chain but is never permitted to approve.
- **`DepartmentChair`** — the chair of the hiring department, who endorses a submitted package on behalf of the unit carrying the departmental cost share.
- **`Dean`** — the college dean, who counters, approves and closes out packages, awards construction contracts, and may run the fiscal-year rollover. A `Dean` can neither draft nor endorse.
- **`FacilitiesManager`** — the campus facilities operator, who maintains the room inventory, upgrades utility envelopes, activates and releases room assignments, scopes and sequences construction, and receives and installs equipment.
- **`Faculty`** — the recruited or retained faculty member, who may request equipment and propose staff against their own package and read their own records, but may never place an order, activate an appointment, or touch any negotiation step.

Two of the roles are backed by persisted entities, and for those roles `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary token but a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against.

When `X-User-Role` is `DepartmentChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FundingUnit` — specifically the department unit; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FundingUnit` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which for a startup package means its `department_unit_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Dean`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `FundingUnit`, this time the college unit, and the comparison is made against the package's `college_unit_id`. For funding-unit-scoped reads, either role may address its own unit or one of that unit's descendants in the parent-child hierarchy. A mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `Faculty`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Faculty` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Faculty` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — the package's `faculty_id`, the room assignment's `faculty_id`, or, for an equipment order, a staff appointment or a renovation project, the `faculty_id` of the package that owns it. A `Faculty` actor reading or writing another faculty member's resource is rejected with `ERR_FACULTY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `DeanOffice`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a state transition demands a signature — as `proposed_by_user_id` on submit, and as `approved_by_user_id` where the approving actor is recorded — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilitiesManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier that the server neither compares to any entity's `id` nor persists.

Segregation of duties is enforced across the three negotiation signatures. The user who endorses a package must differ from the `proposed_by_user_id` recorded at submit, and the user who approves it must differ from both `proposed_by_user_id` and `endorsed_by_user_id`. A violation of either constraint yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that these three signatures are held by three different roles as well as three different user identifiers, and that the `Dean` who approves records `approved_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a list of resources accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` states how many items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order. The package's ledger listing follows this same rule, which is what makes "creation order" the canonical reading order of the transaction log.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2. Every filter query parameter offered by a list endpoint is optional, and supplying several of them narrows the result set conjunctively.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Ledger Immutability

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`, and no endpoint accepts one in a request body.

Nine of the ten entity types — `FundingUnit`, `Faculty`, `StartupPackage`, `CommitmentTranche`, `LabSpace`, `SpaceAssignment`, `RenovationProject`, `RenovationPhase`, `EquipmentOrder` and `StaffAppointment` — additionally carry the full audit triple:

- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, stamped when the resource is first persisted.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource. Recomputation of a derived field — a rollup total, an allocated share, an installed utility load — counts as a mutation of the resource that owns the field and therefore both refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

The one exception is `SpendTransaction`. Ledger rows are append-only and never edited after they are written, so a `SpendTransaction` carries only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. There is no endpoint that updates or deletes a ledger row, and no cascade ever rewrites one; a correction is expressed by writing a new, compensating row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Calendar Conventions

The server reads "now" from a logical UTC clock rather than from wall-clock time. On startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time before the first client call. Any of the five roles may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; every subsequent clock read returns that instant until the next such call. Setting the clock mutates no entity and re-evaluates no tranche on its own — window-dependent state changes are computed when the next business call touches the affected resource.

Three interval conventions coexist, and they deliberately differ:

- **Commitment tranche availability windows are half-open.** A tranche is spendable when the clock is at or after `available_on` and strictly before `expires_on`. Reaching `expires_on` exactly means the tranche is no longer spendable. `expires_on` must be strictly later than `available_on`.
- **Room occupancy windows are half-open.** An assignment covers the clock when the clock is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`; a null `effective_to` means the window is open-ended and extends indefinitely into the future. Two assignments that merely touch — where one's `effective_to` equals the other's `effective_from` — do not overlap, so a room can be handed from one occupant to the next on the same instant. When `effective_to` is supplied it must be strictly later than `effective_from`.
- **Construction phase windows are closed.** A phase occupies the room for the whole of `planned_start` through `planned_end` inclusive, because a crew works the last day of its window. Two phases whose windows share even a single endpoint therefore *do* overlap. `planned_end` must be at or after `planned_start`.

Fiscal years are labelled by their ending calendar year. **Fiscal year N runs from `N-1`-07-01T00:00:00Z inclusive to `N`-07-01T00:00:00Z exclusive** — so fiscal year 2027 opens on 2026-07-01 and closes the instant 2027-07-01 arrives. A `fiscal_year` label must be between 2020 and 2100 inclusive.

Fields typed as calendar dates — `planned_start_date`, `departure_effective_on`, `spend_deadline_on`, `start_date` and `end_date` — are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. Fields typed as instants are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps.

### 2.6. Cents, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no decimal currency amounts anywhere in a request body or a response body, and no floating-point currency arithmetic is permitted. A percentage field is an integer number of percentage points.

Wherever a percentage is applied to a cent amount, or a fractional full-time-equivalent factor is applied to a monthly cost, the intermediate value is rounded **half up to whole cents**. Wherever a cap is applied on top of a percentage, the rounding happens first and the cap is applied to the rounded figure, never the other way round.

Two non-currency quantities have their own precision rules. `spend_down_ratio` is a number rounded half up to **four** decimal places, and every threshold comparison against it is made at that precision. Power is expressed in kilowatts as a number carried to **two** decimal places with half-up rounding on running sums — `power_kw_capacity`, `required_power_kw` and `installed_power_kw` all follow that rule. Fume-hood counts (`fume_hood_count`, `required_fume_hoods`, `installed_fume_hoods`) and floor loads (`floor_load_psf`, `required_floor_load_psf`) are plain integers, and `net_sq_ft` is a positive integer of assignable net square feet.

### 2.7. Cost Sharing Across the Three Funder Units

Every startup package names three funding units that jointly pay for it, and every single cent that moves on its ledger is attributed to those three units at the moment the ledger row is written. The department's percentage is `dept_share_pct`, the college's is `college_share_pct`, and the dean's-office discretionary share is `dean_share_pct`. All three are integers, each in the range 0 through 100, and their sum must be exactly 100; a package creation request whose three percentages do not sum to exactly 100 is rejected with `ERR_SHARE_PCT_SUM_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The three percentages are supplied only at package creation and can never be edited afterwards, so a ledger row written in year four splits on exactly the percentages negotiated in year one.

The split of a single transaction amount works as follows. Take the transaction's positive `amount_cents`. For each of the three funders, form the exact rational product of that amount and the funder's percentage divided by 100, without rounding, and take its floor as that funder's base allocation. The remainder is the transaction amount minus the sum of the three base allocations, and it is always 0, 1 or 2 cents. Distribute the remainder by the **largest fractional part first** rule: sort the three funders by the discarded fractional part of their exact product in descending order, breaking any tie by the fixed funder ordering department, then college, then dean's office — never by identifier and never by creation timestamp — and give one extra cent to each of the first *remainder* funders in that order. The results are stored on the ledger row as `dept_share_cents`, `college_share_cents` and `dean_share_cents`, and by construction they sum to the transaction amount exactly.

The order in which splitting and summing occur is normative and is a common source of one-cent drift. **Each transaction is split individually and the whole-cent results are then accumulated per funder.** Amounts are never accumulated first and the accumulated total split afterwards; the two orders can differ by as much as one cent per funder per transaction.

Settlement rows are a deliberate exception to re-running the split. A `RELEASE` or `DISBURSEMENT` row that settles the *whole* of an open encumbrance copies, verbatim, the stored `dept_share_cents`, `college_share_cents` and `dean_share_cents` of the `ENCUMBRANCE` row it names in `reverses_transaction_id`. A *partial* settlement — an appointment ended early, a renovation finishing under its estimate — runs the split above on the settled portion to build the `DISBURSEMENT` row, and then derives the paired `RELEASE` row's three shares as the per-funder arithmetic remainder: the stored encumbrance share minus the disbursed share. Deriving the release by re-running the split on the released portion instead would drift by up to two cents per funder and would break the conservation property that makes the ledger reconcilable.

### 2.8. Funding Unit Pool Accounting

A `FundingUnit` has a `name`, a `unit_type` that is one of `COLLEGE`, `DEPARTMENT` or `DEAN_OFFICE`, and a self-referential `parent_unit_id`. A `DEPARTMENT` or `DEAN_OFFICE` unit must supply a `parent_unit_id` naming a unit whose `unit_type` is `COLLEGE`; a `COLLEGE` unit must leave `parent_unit_id` null. Each unit is seeded with a `cost_share_pool_cents` figure, at least 0, that bounds how much it may reserve across all startup packages at once.

Five further amounts on a unit are derived, never written directly, and are recomputed from the stored per-row splits every time the ledger moves:

- **`reserved_cents`** — the sum of this unit's largest-remainder share of `total_committed_cents` over every startup package with `negotiation_status` of `APPROVED`, `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED_OUT` that names this unit, minus this unit's share of every `EXPIRY` and every `RECAPTURE` ledger row. Expired and recaptured money therefore returns to the pool automatically.
- **`committed_cents`** — the sum of this unit's stored share over ledger rows of kind `ENCUMBRANCE`, minus its stored share over rows of kind `RELEASE` and `DISBURSEMENT`. This is the unit's exposure to encumbrances that are still open.
- **`disbursed_cents`** — the sum of this unit's stored share over ledger rows of kind `DISBURSEMENT`.
- **`refunded_cents`** — the sum of this unit's stored share over ledger rows of kind `EXPIRY` and `RECAPTURE`.
- **`available_pool_cents`** — `cost_share_pool_cents` less `reserved_cents`. This identity holds for every unit at every instant.

On creation a unit's `reserved_cents`, `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents` and `refunded_cents` are all 0, and `available_pool_cents` equals `cost_share_pool_cents`.

### 2.9. The Tranche Balance Identity and the Meaning of a Ledger Row

The ledger is the only place cents move, and a `CommitmentTranche` is the only thing they move on. Every tranche satisfies a single conserved identity at all times, in every status, with zero tolerance:

> the tranche's originally scheduled `amount_cents` plus its `carried_in_cents` equals the sum of its `available_balance_cents`, `encumbered_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `carried_out_cents`, `expired_cents` and `recaptured_cents`.

Equivalently, `available_balance_cents` is `amount_cents` plus `carried_in_cents`, less `encumbered_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `carried_out_cents`, `expired_cents` and `recaptured_cents`.

Each of the seven ledger row kinds moves cents between exactly two terms of that identity, and `amount_cents` on a row is always a positive magnitude — the direction is implied by the `kind`, never by a sign:

- **`ENCUMBRANCE`** moves cents from available to encumbered.
- **`RELEASE`** moves cents from encumbered back to available.
- **`DISBURSEMENT`** moves cents from encumbered to disbursed.
- **`EXPIRY`** moves cents from available to expired.
- **`CARRYFORWARD_OUT`** moves cents from available to carried-out on the source tranche.
- **`CARRYFORWARD_IN`** raises both `carried_in_cents` and the available balance on the destination tranche by the same amount.
- **`RECAPTURE`** moves cents from available to recaptured.

Each row also records the `package_id` whose ledger it belongs to, the `tranche_id` it moves cents on, a `source_kind` naming the workflow that produced it — one of `EQUIPMENT_ORDER`, `STAFF_APPOINTMENT`, `RENOVATION_PROJECT`, `ROLLOVER` or `SETTLEMENT` — a polymorphic `source_id`, a `reverses_transaction_id`, and a human-readable `memo` explaining the reason. The `source_id` resolves according to `source_kind`: it is the identifier of the `EquipmentOrder`, `StaffAppointment` or `RenovationProject` that caused the row, and it is null for `ROLLOVER` and `SETTLEMENT` rows. It is deliberately not a canonical foreign key with its own reverse index; rows belonging to a given source are discovered by listing the owning package's transactions and filtering on `source_id`. Similarly, `reverses_transaction_id` is set only on `RELEASE` and `DISBURSEMENT` rows, where it names the `ENCUMBRANCE` row being settled, and is null on every other kind; the self-reference is resolved through the same package's transaction list.

A package's spend-summary read exposes the rollups defined in §3.4 together with `total_carried_out_cents` and `total_carried_in_cents` (the sums of `carried_out_cents` and `carried_in_cents` across the package's tranches), the three per-funder totals `dept_share_cents`, `college_share_cents` and `dean_share_cents` accumulated over the package's whole ledger, `spend_down_ratio`, and a boolean `balance_identity_holds` that reports whether the identity above is satisfied for every tranche of the package.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write endpoints are guarded by the version of a *parent* entity rather than by their own state, because in both cases the operation is individually legitimate and no state precondition can catch a writer working from a stale picture. On these two endpoints the version field is a required, non-optional input; on every other endpoint in this API no version field is accepted at all.

`POST /space-assignments` must carry `expected_space_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `LabSpace` named by `space_id`. If that value does not equal the room's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SPACE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two assignments whose share percentages both fit inside the room's 100-percent envelope are each legitimate on their own, so a writer whose view of the room's occupancy has gone stale is not caught by any share check; the version guard is what stops it from allocating share against a room whose occupancy picture it no longer knows.

`POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place` must carry `expected_package_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `StartupPackage` that funds the order. If that value does not equal the package's current `version`, the placement is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Placing two different orders against the same package is legitimate whenever the tranche waterfall covers both, so the second placement is not blocked by any state precondition; the version guard is what forces a buyer to re-read the package — and therefore its tranche balances — after every ledger-moving event.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Existence Checking Order

When a path contains more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the request fails on the *first* one that does not name an existing resource. A request against `/renovation-projects/{id}/phases/{phase_id}/start` therefore reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown project without ever looking for the phase; and a `phase_id` that exists but belongs to a different project is reported the same way, because a phase is only addressable through its own project. The same principle applies to `/startup-packages/{id}/tranches/{tranche_id}`, where an unknown package `id` is reported before the `tranche_id` is examined, and to a `tranche_id` that exists but is not a tranche of the package in the path.

Identifiers arriving in a request body or as a query filter are checked with the same rigour as path identifiers, and the not-found code is specific to the entity type being resolved: `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_FOUND` for a faculty member, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` for a package, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND` for a commitment tranche, `ERR_SPACE_NOT_FOUND` for a laboratory room, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` for a renovation project or one of its phases, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for an equipment order, a staff appointment or a room assignment, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for a funding unit, a room assignment referenced as a plain resource, or a renovation phase. `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` is also the code used when a referenced funding unit exists but its `unit_type` does not match the slot it was used in — a `department_unit_id` that names a `COLLEGE`, a `dean_unit_id` that names a `DEPARTMENT`, and so on.

### 2.12. Cascading Consistency and Server-Initiated Transitions

Many operations in this system change more than one resource. A departure notice freezes a package and cancels its not-yet-committed orders and appointments. A separation releases encumbrances, drains tranches, unwinds three funder pools, releases rooms and stamps a disposition on every installed instrument. Substantial completion of a renovation settles a ledger, reclassifies tranches, re-derives three units' balances and promotes room assignments. In all such cases the server performs the dependent transitions itself, without a client request naming the dependent resource, and each dependent resource that changes has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented independently. Where a cascade writes ledger rows, the rollups on the owning package and the balances on the three funding units are recomputed as part of the same request, so a client that reads any of them immediately afterwards sees a fully settled picture. Consistency is immediate; there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation anywhere in this API.

Several states are reachable only through such a cascade and have no endpoint of their own. `SUPERSEDED` on a package, `WITHDRAWN` on a room assignment, `DECOMMISSIONED` on a laboratory room, `RECAPTURED` and `OBLIGATED` on a tranche, and `CANCELLED` on a renovation project are all server-initiated outcomes described in §3.

### 2.13. Validation Precedence

A single malformed or ill-timed request can violate several rules at once. When it does, the rule with the **lowest priority number** in the ordered ladder below is the one reported, and no later rule is evaluated. The ladder is global: it spans authentication, payload shape, existence, scope, concurrency, state and finally the fiscal checks that require reading the ledger. Implementations must evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient, because several pairs of rules are genuinely reachable together and the tests distinguish them.

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list. Applies to every endpoint.
3. `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) — the body parses; every required input field is present and non-null; every enum value is a member of its declared set; integer cent fields are at least 0; `fte` is greater than 0 and at most 1.00; share percentages are within 1 through 100; dates are ISO 8601; and every interval is correctly ordered, meaning `planned_start` is at or before `planned_end`, `effective_from` is strictly before `effective_to`, `available_on` is strictly before `expires_on`, and `start_date` is at or before `end_date`. Applies to every write endpoint.
4. `ERR_SHARE_PCT_SUM_INVALID` (422) — `dept_share_pct` plus `college_share_pct` plus `dean_share_pct` equals exactly 100. Applies to `POST /startup-packages`.
5. `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `FundingUnit` named by any unit-identifier path segment or body field exists, and its `unit_type` matches the slot it is used in: `department_unit_id` must be a `DEPARTMENT`, `college_unit_id` a `COLLEGE`, `dean_unit_id` a `DEAN_OFFICE`. Applies to `POST /funding-units`, `POST /faculty`, `POST /startup-packages`, `GET /funding-units/{id}` and `GET /funding-units/{id}/faculty`.
6. `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `Faculty` named by the path `id` or by `faculty_id` exists. Applies to every `/faculty/{id}/...` endpoint, `POST /startup-packages` and `POST /space-assignments`.
7. `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `StartupPackage` named by the path `id`, by `package_id`, or by `superseded_package_id` exists. Applies to every `/startup-packages/{id}/...` endpoint, `POST /renovation-projects`, `POST /equipment-orders` and `POST /staff-appointments`.
8. `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `CommitmentTranche` named by the path identifier exists and belongs to the package in the path when both appear. Applies to `GET /commitment-tranches/{id}` and `GET /commitment-tranches/{id}/spend-transactions`.
9. `ERR_SPACE_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `LabSpace` named by the path `id`, by `space_id`, or by `install_space_id` exists. Applies to `GET /lab-spaces/{id}`, `POST /space-assignments`, `POST /renovation-projects` and `POST /equipment-orders`.
10. `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the `RenovationProject` named by the path `id` exists, and the `RenovationPhase` named by `phase_id` belongs to it. Applies to every `/renovation-projects/{id}/...` endpoint.

Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/endorse`, `POST /startup-packages/{id}/counter`, `POST /startup-packages/{id}/approve`, `POST /startup-packages/{id}/year-end-rollover`, `GET /funding-units/{id}` and `GET /funding-units/{id}/faculty`.

Applies to `GET /startup-packages/{id}`, `GET /startup-packages/{id}/spend-summary`, `GET /startup-packages/{id}/tranches`, `GET /startup-packages/{id}/staff-appointments`, `GET /startup-packages/{id}/spend-transactions`, `POST /equipment-orders` and `POST /staff-appointments`.

Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/endorse` and `POST /startup-packages/{id}/approve`.
15. `ERR_STALE_SPACE_VERSION` (412) — `expected_space_version` equals the current `version` of the target `LabSpace`. Applies to `POST /space-assignments`.
16. `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION` (412) — `expected_package_version` equals the current `version` of the target `StartupPackage`. Applies to `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`.
17. `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the package's `negotiation_status` permits the requested transition. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/tranches`, `DELETE /startup-packages/{id}/tranches/{tranche_id}`, and the submit, endorse, counter, revise, approve, activate, year-end-rollover and close-out actions on `/startup-packages/{id}`.
18. `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN` (409) — the package's `spend_status` is `SPENDING`, and not `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE`, `FROZEN` or `SETTLED`, before any money-moving operation. Applies to `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`, `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`, `POST /staff-appointments/{id}/activate` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/substantial-completion`.

Applies to `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`, `/receive`, `/install` and `/cancel`; `POST /staff-appointments/{id}/activate` and `/end`; `POST /space-assignments/{id}/activate` and `/release`.
20. `ERR_RENOVATION_STATE_INVALID` (409) — the `RenovationProject` (or, for a phase action, the phase and its predecessors) is in a status from which the requested action is declared. Applies to `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/phases`, `/solicit-bids`, `/rescope`, `/award`, `/phases/{phase_id}/start`, `/phases/{phase_id}/complete` and `/substantial-completion`.

Applies to `POST /space-assignments`, `POST /space-assignments/{id}/activate`, `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`, `POST /staff-appointments/{id}/activate` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`. This rule is the only reachable faculty gate on `POST /space-assignments`, which carries no spend-authority precondition and is therefore never shadowed by priority 18. It deliberately does **not** apply to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/activate`, whose own faculty gate is stated in §3.4.

Applies to `POST /space-assignments` and `POST /space-assignments/{id}/activate`.
26. `ERR_PHASE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (422) — no two phases of the same project overlap as closed intervals from `planned_start` through `planned_end`. Applies to `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/phases` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`.
27. `ERR_PHASE_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (422) — consecutive phases, ordered by `sequence_no`, are separated by at least two calendar days between the earlier phase's `planned_end` and the later phase's `planned_start`. Applies to `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/phases` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`.

31. `ERR_NO_TRANCHES_DEFINED` (422) — the package has at least one commitment tranche. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/submit`.
32. `ERR_TRANCHE_SUM_MISMATCH` (422) — the sum of the package's tranche `amount_cents` equals `proposed_total_cents` exactly. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/submit`.
33. `ERR_ROLLOVER_FISCAL_YEAR_NOT_ELAPSED` (409) — the requested fiscal year's window has closed, meaning the clock is at or after the July 1st instant of the requested `fiscal_year`. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/year-end-rollover`.
34. `ERR_ROLLOVER_ALREADY_RUN` (409) — no tranche of the requested fiscal year is already in status `CARRIED_FORWARD` or `EXPIRED`; the rollover for a year runs at most once. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/year-end-rollover`.

Applies to `POST /faculty/{id}/departure-notice`.

Applies to `POST /startup-packages` when `package_kind` is `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER`.
38. `ERR_PREDECESSOR_HAS_OPEN_ENCUMBRANCES` (409) — no tranche of the superseded package is in status `OBLIGATED` and its `total_encumbered_cents` is 0. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/activate` when the package supersedes another.
39. `ERR_OPEN_ENCUMBRANCES_BLOCK_CLOSEOUT` (409) — no tranche of the package is `OBLIGATED` and no equipment order, staff appointment or renovation project of the package still holds an encumbrance. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/close-out`, and deliberately not to `POST /faculty/{id}/separate`, which releases the open encumbrances itself.

41. `ERR_UNIT_POOL_EXCEEDED` (422) — each of the three funder units has an `available_pool_cents` at least equal to its share of `proposed_total_cents`. Applies to `POST /startup-packages/{id}/approve`.

Applies to `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`, `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`, `POST /staff-appointments/{id}/activate` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/substantial-completion`.
43. `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRANCHE_BALANCE` (422) — the summed `available_balance_cents` of the eligible tranches is at least the amount to be encumbered. Applies to `POST /equipment-orders/{id}/place`, `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/award`, `POST /staff-appointments/{id}/activate` and `POST /renovation-projects/{id}/substantial-completion`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out. Because the two concurrency guards sit at priorities 15 and 16, above every state and fiscal check, a stale version is reported even when the operation would also have failed for a frozen package or an empty tranche set. And because the existence checks at priorities 5 through 11 sit above the scope checks at 12 and 13, a scoped actor addressing a resource that does not exist receives the not-found code rather than a permission error.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Funding Hierarchy and Recruiting a Candidate

Before any money can be promised, the dean's office registers the units that will pay. Registration supplies a `name`, a `unit_type` and, for anything other than a college, a `parent_unit_id`; it also seeds the unit's `cost_share_pool_cents`. The hierarchy is intentionally shallow and self-referential: a `COLLEGE` unit sits at the top with no parent, and both `DEPARTMENT` units and the college's `DEAN_OFFICE` discretionary pool hang directly off it. Attempting to register a `DEPARTMENT` whose declared parent is itself a department, or a `COLLEGE` that names any parent at all, fails the unit-type check described in §2.13. Because the parent link points inward rather than outward, the reverse direction is served by a filterable list: units can be listed by `parent_unit_id` or by `unit_type` to walk the tree downward.

A recruitment begins with a faculty record. The dean's office supplies the candidate's `full_name`, the `rank` at which the appointment is offered — one of `ASSISTANT`, `ASSOCIATE` or `FULL` — the `funding_unit_id` of the home department, which must be a unit whose `unit_type` is `DEPARTMENT`, and the `planned_start_date` on which the appointment is expected to begin. The record is created in `appointment_status` `CANDIDATE` with `retention_round` at 0, and `activated_on`, `departure_intent`, `departure_effective_on`, `target_funding_unit_id` and `separated_on` all null.

When the candidate actually arrives on campus, the dean's office records the arrival and the record advances `CANDIDATE → ACTIVE`, stamping `activated_on` from the logical clock. This is the only route out of `CANDIDATE`; a candidate who never arrives simply stays a candidate. `ACTIVE` is the status that unlocks every money-moving operation on that person's package, and the two terminal statuses, `DEPARTED` and `TRANSFERRED_INTERNAL`, are reached only through the separation workflow of §3.9. The middle status, `DEPARTURE_NOTICE`, is the one place this state machine becomes cyclic: a faculty member who has given notice because of an outside offer can be brought back to `ACTIVE` by a successful retention counteroffer.

Faculty records are listed with optional filters on `funding_unit_id`, on `target_funding_unit_id` — which is how the receiving department discovers who is transferring in — and on `appointment_status`. A funding unit also offers a list of the faculty whose home unit it is, accepting the same `appointment_status` filter.

### 3.2. Drafting the Commitment: Packages and Their Tranche Schedule

Drafting a package is the act of writing down a negotiation, not of moving money. The dean's office supplies the `faculty_id` of the person being recruited or retained, a `package_kind` of either `INITIAL` or `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER`, the `dean_unit_id` of the dean's-office pool that will fund the discretionary share (which must be a unit whose `unit_type` is `DEAN_OFFICE`), the three cost-share percentages, a `proposed_total_cents` figure that must be strictly greater than 0, and a `spend_deadline_on` calendar date after which no tranche of this package may be spent. A counteroffer additionally names the `superseded_package_id` of the package it recommits; an `INITIAL` package must leave that field null, and a `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER` must supply it.

Two of the package's funding references are derived rather than supplied. The server copies `department_unit_id` from the faculty member's `funding_unit_id`, and copies `college_unit_id` from that department unit's `parent_unit_id`. A new package is created with `negotiation_status` `DRAFT`, `spend_status` `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE`, `negotiation_round` 1, `counter_total_cents` null, all three signature fields null, `settlement_variant` null, and `recaptured_unspent_cents`, `transferred_balance_cents` and `college_holdback_cents` all 0.

**Multi-year commitment tranches** are what turn a headline figure into a schedule. Each tranche pins one `category` — `EQUIPMENT`, `PERSONNEL`, `RENOVATION` or `DISCRETIONARY` — to one `fiscal_year`, with an `amount_cents` strictly greater than 0 and a half-open spending window running from `available_on` up to but excluding `expires_on`. A tranche is created in status `SCHEDULED` with `available_balance_cents` equal to its `amount_cents`, `carried_in_cents`, `carried_out_cents`, `encumbered_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `expired_cents` and `recaptured_cents` all 0, and `opened_on` null. Every tranche create re-derives the owning package's rollups.

Each tranche also declares what happens to its unencumbered money at year-end via `carryforward_policy`: `NONE` means nothing carries, `FULL` means the whole remaining balance may carry, and `CAPPED` means only a percentage may. That percentage is `carryforward_cap_pct`, an integer between 0 and 100 that is required when `carryforward_policy` is `CAPPED`; a `NONE` or `FULL` tranche must not supply one and the server stores 0 for it. Critically, the cap is a percentage of the tranche's **original** `amount_cents` and never of `amount_cents` plus `carried_in_cents`, and never of the tranche's current available balance — a tranche that received a large carry-in from the prior year can therefore carry out far less than it holds.

While the package is still in `DRAFT`, a scheduled tranche can be withdrawn from it, which is how a countered package is re-submitted at a genuinely lower total. Withdrawal is permitted only while `negotiation_status` is `DRAFT` — otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the tranche's own status is `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_SPEND_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. A tranche that has ever been `OPEN` carries ledger rows and can never be withdrawn. Withdrawal hard-deletes the tranche row and re-derives the package rollups; it returns no body.

Tranches are readable individually with their full balance breakdown, and as a filterable list under their package accepting `category`, `fiscal_year` and `status`.

### 3.3. The Negotiation Cycle: Three Signatures and a Counteroffer Loop

The negotiation axis of a package is a genuine cycle, not a ladder: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DEAN_REVIEW → COUNTERED → DRAFT` can repeat as often as the parties need, with `negotiation_round` counting the laps. `CLOSED_OUT` and `SUPERSEDED` are its two terminal states. Each of the transitions below increments the package's `version` by one.

**Submission** moves a package `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is the point at which the schedule must reconcile with the headline number. Two conditions are checked, in this order: the package must have at least one commitment tranche, or the submission fails with `ERR_NO_TRANCHES_DEFINED`; and the sum of every tranche's `amount_cents` must equal `proposed_total_cents` exactly, with zero tolerance, or the submission fails with `ERR_TRANCHE_SUM_MISMATCH`. On success the acting user's identifier is recorded as `proposed_by_user_id` — this is the first of the three segregated signatures.

**Endorsement** by the department chair moves the package `SUBMITTED → DEAN_REVIEW` and records `endorsed_by_user_id`. Three gates apply and they are evaluated in the global order of §2.13, so the unit-scope check at priority 12 fires before the segregation check at priority 14, which fires before the state check at priority 17: the acting chair's identifier must equal the package's `department_unit_id` or the call fails with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; the acting identifier must differ from `proposed_by_user_id` or the call fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the package must actually be in `SUBMITTED`.

**A counter** by the dean moves the package `DEAN_REVIEW → COUNTERED`. The dean supplies `counter_total_cents`, which is stored on the package as the most recent counteroffer total, and the acting identifier must equal the package's `college_unit_id` or the call is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Countering has one non-obvious secondary effect: it **voids the endorsement** by clearing `endorsed_by_user_id` back to null. The department chair must therefore endorse again on the next lap, and — because the segregation rule compares against whatever `endorsed_by_user_id` currently holds — a package that has been countered can legitimately be endorsed by a different chair user than before.

**A revision** by the dean's office closes the loop, moving the package `COUNTERED → DRAFT`. The office supplies `revised_total_cents`, which replaces `proposed_total_cents` outright; `negotiation_round` increases by one; and `proposed_by_user_id` is cleared to null so the next submission re-establishes the first signature. Note that the revision does not touch the tranche schedule, which is why lowering a total in earnest usually means withdrawing tranches as described in §3.2 — otherwise the next submission will fail the sum check.

**Approval** by the dean moves the package `DEAN_REVIEW → APPROVED` and is where the institution first puts money at risk. Four conditions must hold: the package is in `DEAN_REVIEW`; the acting identifier equals the package's `college_unit_id`, else `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; the acting identifier is neither `proposed_by_user_id` nor `endorsed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and, for each of the three funder units, that unit's `available_pool_cents` is at least its share of the package's `total_committed_cents` under the split of §2.7, else `ERR_UNIT_POOL_EXCEEDED`. On success the acting identifier is stored as `approved_by_user_id`, and each funder unit's `reserved_cents` rises by exactly its computed share of `total_committed_cents`, with `available_pool_cents` re-derived accordingly. Because submission already enforced that the tranche amounts sum to `proposed_total_cents` exactly, and revision returns the package to `DRAFT` before it can be re-submitted, `total_committed_cents` and `proposed_total_cents` are necessarily equal at the moment of approval — the pool check and the reservation may be read against either figure. The reservation is against the pool only; nothing has been encumbered on any tranche yet, and no ledger row is written.

Packages are listed with filters on `faculty_id`, `superseded_package_id`, `department_unit_id`, `college_unit_id`, `dean_unit_id`, `negotiation_status` and `spend_status`, which together make every one of the package's inbound references reverse-navigable.

### 3.4. Activation, Spend Authority and the Encumbrance Waterfall

A package carries **two independent state axes on the same row**, and conflating them is the single most common misreading of this domain. `negotiation_status` records where the negotiation stands. `spend_status` records whether money may actually move, and takes one of `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE`, `SPENDING`, `FROZEN` or `SETTLED`. The two evolve separately: a package can reach `negotiation_status` `ACTIVE` while `spend_status` is still `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE` for a moment, and `spend_status` can become `FROZEN` while `negotiation_status` remains `ACTIVE` indefinitely. **The negotiation axis alone never authorises spending.** Every money-moving operation — placing a purchase order, awarding a construction contract, activating a staff appointment, and reconciling a project at substantial completion — requires `spend_status` to be exactly `SPENDING`, and reports `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN` when it is `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE`, `FROZEN` or `SETTLED`.

Activation by the dean's office is the one call that advances both axes at once: `negotiation_status` `APPROVED → ACTIVE` and, in the same request, `spend_status` `NOT_YET_AVAILABLE → SPENDING`. Its faculty gate depends on `package_kind`. For an `INITIAL` package the faculty member must already be in `appointment_status` `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_ACTIVE` — the money follows the arrival, not the offer letter. For a `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER` the faculty member is necessarily in `DEPARTURE_NOTICE`, since that is precisely what made the predecessor package eligible to be superseded, and this very call is what returns them to `ACTIVE`; so for that kind the gate is `DEPARTURE_NOTICE` rather than `ACTIVE`. This asymmetry is why the general faculty-status rule at priority 21 deliberately excludes package activation from its scope.

Activation also opens the schedule. Every tranche of the package in status `SCHEDULED` whose availability window contains the clock — that is, whose `available_on` is at or before the clock and whose `expires_on` is strictly after it — advances `SCHEDULED → OPEN` and has `opened_on` stamped from the clock. Tranches whose window has not opened yet stay `SCHEDULED`; tranches whose window has already closed also stay `SCHEDULED` and can only ever be expired or recaptured later. The package's `activated_on` is stamped from the clock, and its `version` increments.

Six rollups are re-derived on the package after every ledger batch and after every tranche create. `total_committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every tranche of the package regardless of status. `total_encumbered_cents`, `total_disbursed_cents`, `total_expired_cents` and `total_recaptured_cents` are the sums of the corresponding per-tranche amounts, again over every tranche regardless of status. `total_available_cents`, by contrast, is the sum of `available_balance_cents` over only those tranches in status `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` — an `OBLIGATED`, `CARRIED_FORWARD`, `EXPIRED` or `RECAPTURED` balance is not available. Finally, `spend_down_ratio` is `total_disbursed_cents` plus `total_encumbered_cents`, divided by `total_committed_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and defined as 0.0 when `total_committed_cents` is 0. Those six rollups together with `spend_down_ratio` are the ledger totals the package's own representation carries; the two carryforward totals `total_carried_out_cents` and `total_carried_in_cents` are not fields of the package and are exposed only on the spend-summary read described in §2.9.

**The encumbrance waterfall** is the single mechanism by which any commitment reaches the ledger, and it behaves identically for equipment placement, renovation award, staff activation and the overrun pass at substantial completion. Given a required amount and a required category, the candidate set is every tranche of that package whose `category` matches, whose `status` is exactly `OPEN`, and whose half-open availability window contains the clock; `SCHEDULED`, `OBLIGATED`, `CARRIED_FORWARD`, `EXPIRED` and `RECAPTURED` tranches never participate. If the candidate set is empty the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_OPEN_TRANCHE`. Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `expires_on` ascending, ties broken by `fiscal_year` ascending, further ties broken by `created_at` ascending — never by identifier — so that the soonest-expiring money is always consumed first. The capacity of the set is the sum of the candidates' `available_balance_cents`; if that capacity is strictly less than the required amount the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRANCHE_BALANCE` and **no partial encumbrance is written at all**, because the waterfall is all-or-nothing. Capacity exactly equal to the required amount is accepted: the last tranche is drained to an `available_balance_cents` of exactly 0 and stays `OPEN`.

When the capacity check passes, the waterfall walks the ordered candidates taking from each the lesser of the amount still outstanding and that tranche's `available_balance_cents`, writing one `ENCUMBRANCE` row per tranche actually consumed and stopping as soon as the outstanding amount reaches 0. Each row's per-funder split is computed on **that row's own amount**, not on the total required — this is the per-transaction rounding order pinned in §2.7. Every funder unit's `committed_cents` then rises by its stored share of each row written.

Ledger rows are readable per package, filtered by `kind` and `source_kind`, and per tranche, filtered by `kind`.

### 3.5. Laboratory Space: Inventory, Occupancy Shares and Utility Envelopes

Facilities owns the room inventory. Registering a room supplies its `building_code` and `room_number`, its `net_sq_ft` of assignable area, a `space_class` of `WET`, `DRY` or `COMPUTATIONAL`, and the four figures that make up its **utility envelope**: `power_kw_capacity` in kilowatts, a `chilled_water_available` flag stating whether process chilled water is plumbed to the room, a `fume_hood_count` of certified hoods, and a `floor_load_psf` rating in pounds per square foot. Power capacity, net area and floor load must all be strictly greater than 0; the hood count may be 0. A room is created in status `AVAILABLE` with `allocated_share_pct` at 0, `installed_power_kw` at 0.00 and `installed_fume_hoods` at 0. The pair of `building_code` and `room_number` must be unique across the whole inventory; a duplicate registration is rejected with `ERR_SPACE_ALREADY_REGISTERED`.

After an infrastructure project raises what a room can support, facilities can overwrite the envelope in place. The upgrade supplies all four envelope figures — `power_kw_capacity`, `fume_hood_count`, `chilled_water_available` and `floor_load_psf` — and each supplied capacity must be at least the room's currently installed load: the new `power_kw_capacity` must be at least `installed_power_kw`, and the new `fume_hood_count` at least `installed_fume_hoods`. Chilled water may not be withdrawn while any installed instrument in the room requires it. Violating any of these three conditions yields `ERR_VALIDATION`. A successful upgrade overwrites the four envelope fields, leaves `installed_power_kw` and `installed_fume_hoods` untouched, does **not** change the room's `status`, and increments the room's `version`. That version bump is exactly what invalidates a stale room read held by a client about to propose an assignment.

**Occupancy is fractional and time-bounded.** The dean's office proposes a share of a room to a faculty member by supplying the `space_id`, the `faculty_id`, a `share_pct` between 1 and 100, an `effective_from` instant, an optional `effective_to` instant (null meaning open-ended), and the `expected_space_version` required by §2.10. The faculty member must be in `appointment_status` `ACTIVE` or `CANDIDATE` — a room may legitimately be promised before arrival — and anything else yields `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_ACTIVE`. The assignment is created in status `PROPOSED` with `released_on` null.

The share arithmetic that guards a proposal deserves care, because the same calculation serves two different purposes with two different filters. For the **overlap check**, the peers of a candidate assignment are the other assignments on the same room whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` and whose half-open window overlaps the candidate's window; a `RELEASED` or `WITHDRAWN` assignment holds no share and never counts, while a merely `PROPOSED` one does, because a proposal already reserves capacity. Two windows overlap when each one's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the other's `effective_to`, treating a null `effective_to` as unbounded — so windows that merely touch do not overlap. If the candidate's `share_pct` plus the sum of the peers' `share_pct` exceeds 100, the request is rejected with `ERR_SPACE_SHARE_EXCEEDED`; a sum of exactly 100 is accepted and 101 is not. For the **room's reported `allocated_share_pct`**, the filter is different: it is the sum of `share_pct` over the room's `PROPOSED` and `ACTIVE` assignments whose half-open window contains the *current clock instant*, not the peak over all future instants and not the total ever granted. A future-dated assignment therefore passes the overlap check — which compares windows to each other, not to the clock — without raising `allocated_share_pct` until the clock enters its window.

Facilities activates a proposal, moving it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, provided the faculty member is still `ACTIVE` or `CANDIDATE`, the room is not `DECOMMISSIONED`, and the aggregate share still satisfies the same 100-percent envelope — the check is re-run at activation, because peers may have been added since the proposal, and a failure here is again `ERR_SPACE_SHARE_EXCEEDED`. When the activating assignment's window contains the clock, the room itself advances `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED`; either way `allocated_share_pct` is re-derived and both resources' `version` values increment.

Releasing an active assignment moves it `ACTIVE → RELEASED`. Facilities supplies `released_on`, which must be at or after the assignment's `effective_from`; the server stores it and also closes the window by setting `effective_to` to the same instant, exclusive. The room's `allocated_share_pct` is re-derived, and if it falls to 0 the room moves `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`. The fourth assignment status, `WITHDRAWN`, is never requested by a client: a `PROPOSED` assignment is withdrawn by the server when the separation cascade of §3.9 runs or when a room is decommissioned, and the room's `allocated_share_pct` is re-derived at that point too.

A room has four statuses in all. Beyond `AVAILABLE` and `ASSIGNED`, a room moves to `UNDER_RENOVATION` when a construction contract on it is awarded — from either `AVAILABLE` or `ASSIGNED` — and returns to `ASSIGNED` at substantial completion. While a room is `UNDER_RENOVATION`, equipment installation into it is refused with `ERR_SPACE_NOT_READY_FOR_INSTALL`. The fourth status, `DECOMMISSIONED`, has no endpoint at all: it is reached only by the separation cascade, and only in the narrow case where the departing faculty member held the entire 100 percent of the room and a renovation project on that room was cancelled by the same cascade. Decommissioning also moves every remaining assignment on that room to `WITHDRAWN`.

Rooms are listed with filters on `building_code`, `space_class` and `status`; assignments are listed with filters on `space_id`, `faculty_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. Renovation: Scoping, Construction Sequencing and Cost Reconciliation

A renovation project is opened by facilities against a package and a room: the request supplies the `package_id` whose `RENOVATION` tranches will fund the work, the `space_id` of the room, a `scope_tier` of `COSMETIC`, `MODERATE` or `GUT`, and an `estimate_cents` figure strictly greater than 0. The project starts in status `SCOPING` with `rescope_count` at 0 and every derived cost field — `awarded_cents`, `subsidy_estimate_cents`, `package_charge_estimate_cents`, `actual_total_cents`, `subsidy_final_cents`, `package_charge_final_cents`, `cost_variance_cents`, `awarded_on` and `completed_on` — null.

**Construction phases** are added one at a time, each carrying its `project_id` implicitly from the path, a `sequence_no` of at least 1, a human-readable `name` such as demolition or mechanical rough-in, a `planned_start` and `planned_end` bounding its closed occupancy window, and a `budget_cents` strictly greater than 0. A phase is created in status `PENDING` with `actual_cost_cents`, `actual_start` and `actual_end` null. Every phase addition re-validates the project's whole sequence plan, and so does the award. Five conditions make up that plan validation:

1. The `sequence_no` values across the project's phases must be exactly the contiguous run from 1 up to the number of phases, with no gap and no duplicate, or the request fails with `ERR_PHASE_SEQUENCE_GAP`.
2. Each phase's `planned_start` must be at or before its `planned_end`, or the request fails with `ERR_VALIDATION`.
3. No two phases may overlap as **closed** intervals: they overlap whenever one phase's `planned_start` is at or before the other's `planned_end` *and* the other's `planned_start` is at or before the first's `planned_end`. Because the intervals are closed, two phases sharing a single day do overlap. A violation is `ERR_PHASE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
4. Consecutive phases, taken in `sequence_no` order, need a changeover gap: the later phase's `planned_start` less the earlier phase's `planned_end` must be at least two calendar days. Exactly two days is accepted; one day is not. A violation is `ERR_PHASE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
5. At award time, the sum of the phases' `budget_cents` must equal the project's `awarded_cents`, or the award fails with `ERR_NO_PHASES_DEFINED`.

A project with zero phases cannot go out to bid and cannot be awarded, both reported as `ERR_NO_PHASES_DEFINED`; and it cannot reach substantial completion while any phase is not `COMPLETE`, reported as `ERR_PHASES_INCOMPLETE`. Cancelled projects are not re-validated.

**Scoping cycles.** Facilities sends a scoped project out to bid, moving it `SCOPING → BID`, which requires at least one phase to exist. If the bids come back wrong, facilities can rescope, moving the project `BID → SCOPING` while supplying a `revised_estimate_cents` that replaces `estimate_cents` and a `scope_tier` that replaces the previous tier; `rescope_count` increases by one each time. This is the construction-scoping cycle `SCOPING → BID → SCOPING`, and it may repeat.

**The award** is performed by the dean and is the project's first money-moving act. It moves the project `BID → AWARDED`, stores the supplied `awarded_cents`, and stamps `awarded_on` from the clock. Four preconditions apply: the project is in `BID`; the funding package's `spend_status` is `SPENDING`, else `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN`; the phase sequence plan validates as above; and the `RENOVATION` tranche candidates cover the amount to be encumbered, failing with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRANCHE_BALANCE` or, when the candidate set is empty, `ERR_NO_OPEN_TRANCHE`. The room moves to `UNDER_RENOVATION`, and the project's `version` increments.

The amount encumbered at award is not the award itself, because the college subsidises part of every renovation according to how invasive it is. The **scope-estimate allocation** works like this: the subsidy percentage is 0 for a `COSMETIC` project, 25 for a `MODERATE` one and 40 for a `GUT` one. Multiply `awarded_cents` by that percentage, divide by 100, and round half up to whole cents; then take the lesser of that figure and the standing subsidy ceiling of 20,000,000 cents — two hundred thousand dollars — and store the result as `subsidy_estimate_cents`. The rounding happens before the ceiling is applied, never after. `package_charge_estimate_cents` is then `awarded_cents` less `subsidy_estimate_cents`, and that is the amount driven through the encumbrance waterfall of §3.4 against category `RENOVATION`, one `ENCUMBRANCE` row per tranche consumed, with each funder unit's `committed_cents` rising by its stored share of each row.

**Phase execution** follows the sequence strictly. Facilities starts a `PENDING` phase, moving it `PENDING → ACTIVE` and stamping `actual_start` from the clock, provided the project is in `AWARDED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and **every phase of the project with a lower `sequence_no` is already `COMPLETE`**; starting out of sequence is rejected with `ERR_RENOVATION_STATE_INVALID`. Starting the phase whose `sequence_no` is 1 also advances the project `AWARDED → IN_PROGRESS`. Completing an active phase moves it `ACTIVE → COMPLETE`, stores the supplied `actual_cost_cents`, and stamps `actual_end` from the clock. No ledger row is written per phase; the ledger settles exactly once, at substantial completion.

**Substantial completion** moves the project `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_on`, and requires every phase to be `COMPLETE` with a recorded `actual_cost_cents`, else `ERR_PHASES_INCOMPLETE`. It also requires the package's `spend_status` to be `SPENDING`, because it moves money. The **final-actual allocation** differs from the scope estimate on both its base and its ceiling, and the divergence is deliberate. First, `actual_total_cents` is the sum of `actual_cost_cents` over the project's `COMPLETE` phases. Second, the tolerated overrun is `awarded_cents` multiplied by 10 percent, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents; the **subsidisable** base is the lesser of `actual_total_cents` and `awarded_cents` plus that tolerated overrun, so the college subsidises a modest overrun but not a cent beyond it. Third, the subsidy percentage for the project's `scope_tier` is applied to the subsidisable base, rounded half up to whole cents, and then clamped to the same 20,000,000-cent ceiling; the result is `subsidy_final_cents`. Fourth — and this is the asymmetry — `package_charge_final_cents` is `actual_total_cents` less `subsidy_final_cents`: the package is charged the **whole** actual cost, not the subsidisable portion. Finally, `cost_variance_cents` is `package_charge_final_cents` less `package_charge_estimate_cents`, and a positive variance means the package absorbs more than it encumbered.

A project that finishes exactly on budget therefore has a `cost_variance_cents` of 0. An overrun inside the tolerance splits between college and package in the tier ratio. An overrun beyond the tolerance loads the whole excess onto the package. And once the subsidy computation exceeds the ceiling — which `GUT` projects reach readily — both allocations clamp to the same figure and the variance becomes the full actual-minus-awarded delta with no subsidy relief at all.

Reconciliation then settles the ledger in three linked steps. First, the award's `ENCUMBRANCE` rows are settled: per tranche, a `DISBURSEMENT` row for the lesser of `package_charge_final_cents` and `package_charge_estimate_cents`, and a `RELEASE` row for any unspent remainder, each mirroring the stored split of the row it reverses under the settlement rule of §2.7; and when `cost_variance_cents` is strictly greater than 0, a fresh waterfall pass encumbers the overrun and is immediately followed by a `DISBURSEMENT` of the same amount. When `cost_variance_cents` is exactly 0 no reconciliation row is written at all, and when it is negative the surplus is released back to the tranches that carried the estimate. Second, each affected tranche's `encumbered_cents`, `disbursed_cents` and `available_balance_cents` are re-derived, and any tranche sitting in `OBLIGATED` whose `encumbered_cents` reaches 0 auto-transitions to `EXPIRED`, expiring its residual available balance through an `EXPIRY` row. Third, each funding unit's `committed_cents` and `disbursed_cents` — and, where the second step produced an `EXPIRY`, its `reserved_cents` and `refunded_cents` — are re-derived from the stored per-row splits. The room finally moves `UNDER_RENOVATION → ASSIGNED`, and every `PROPOSED` assignment on that room whose half-open window contains the clock is promoted to `ACTIVE`, with `allocated_share_pct` re-derived.

Projects are listed with filters on `package_id`, `space_id`, `status` and `scope_tier`; a project's phases are listed in sequence order with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.7. Equipment Procurement and the Installation Fork

Either the dean's office or the faculty member themselves may request a piece of equipment. The request supplies the `package_id` that funds it, the `install_space_id` of the room the instrument will live in, the `vendor_name`, an `item_description`, the `quoted_cents` figure strictly greater than 0, a `lead_time_days` count of whole days at least 0, and the four utility demands the installation will make on the room: `required_power_kw` in kilowatts (at least 0), a `requires_chilled_water` flag, a `required_fume_hoods` count (at least 0), and a `required_floor_load_psf` rating strictly greater than 0. A `Faculty` actor may only request against their own package. The order is created in status `REQUESTED` with no ledger movement whatsoever, `encumbered_cents` and `disbursed_cents` at 0, and `placed_on`, `expected_delivery_on`, `delivered_on`, `installed_on`, `utility_block_reason` and `transfer_disposition` all null.

**Placement** is a dean's-office act and is where the money is committed. It moves the order `REQUESTED → ORDERED`, stamps `placed_on` from the clock, and derives `expected_delivery_on` as the clock advanced by `lead_time_days` whole days. Four preconditions apply, in the global order of §2.13: the order is in `REQUESTED`; the package's `spend_status` is `SPENDING`, else `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN`; the supplied `expected_package_version` equals the package's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PACKAGE_VERSION`; and the `EQUIPMENT` tranche candidates cover `quoted_cents`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRANCHE_BALANCE`, or `ERR_NO_OPEN_TRANCHE` when the candidate set is empty. Because the concurrency guard sits at priority 16 and the tranche checks at priorities 42 and 43, a stale version is reported in preference to an insufficient balance. The waterfall then encumbers `quoted_cents` across `EQUIPMENT` tranches, one `ENCUMBRANCE` row per tranche consumed with its own per-funder split, each funder unit's `committed_cents` rises by its stored share, and **both** the order's `version` and the package's `version` increment — the latter being what forces the next buyer to re-read.

While an order sits in `ORDERED`, `DELIVERED` or `UTILITY_BLOCKED`, its `encumbered_cents` reads as its `quoted_cents`; while it is `REQUESTED`, `INSTALLED` or `CANCELLED`, its `encumbered_cents` reads as 0. Its `disbursed_cents` is `quoted_cents` once the order is `INSTALLED` and 0 in every other status.

Facilities records receipt of the shipment, moving the order `ORDERED → DELIVERED` and stamping `delivered_on`. Receipt moves no cents at all: the encumbrance stays open until the instrument is actually installed and working.

**Installation forks.** Attempting an installation is a single operation with two legitimate outcomes, both of which return HTTP 200 with the order body — the block is a branch, not an error. The shared preconditions are: the order is in `DELIVERED` or `UTILITY_BLOCKED`; the package's `spend_status` is `SPENDING`, else `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN`; the target room is covered by an assignment in status `ACTIVE` whose `faculty_id` equals the package's `faculty_id` and whose half-open window contains the clock, else `ERR_SPACE_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_FACULTY`; and the room's own `status` is `ASSIGNED`, else `ERR_SPACE_NOT_READY_FOR_INSTALL`.

The fork itself is decided by a **four-check utility feasibility test** run in a pinned order, so that the reported reason is deterministic even when a room fails several checks at once. The room's power headroom is its `power_kw_capacity` less its `installed_power_kw`; its hood headroom is its `fume_hood_count` less its `installed_fume_hoods`. The checks are, in order: first, `required_power_kw` must be at most the power headroom, otherwise the reason is `POWER`; second, either the order does not require chilled water or the room's `chilled_water_available` is true, otherwise the reason is `CHILLED_WATER`; third, `required_fume_hoods` must be at most the hood headroom, otherwise the reason is `FUME_HOOD`; fourth, `required_floor_load_psf` must be at most the room's `floor_load_psf`, otherwise the reason is `FLOOR_LOAD`. All four comparisons are inclusive, so an instrument that consumes exactly the remaining headroom installs successfully and leaves the headroom at 0, while asking for one unit more blocks. Only the **first** failing check is reported.

On success the order moves `DELIVERED → INSTALLED` (or `UTILITY_BLOCKED → INSTALLED`, which is the join point of the fork and behaves identically), `installed_on` is stamped from the clock, and `utility_block_reason` is set to null. Three cascade steps follow. The order's open `ENCUMBRANCE` rows are settled by `DISBURSEMENT` rows of the same amounts on the same tranches, each mirroring the stored split of the row it reverses. Each affected tranche's `encumbered_cents` falls and its `disbursed_cents` rises; a tranche sitting in `OBLIGATED` whose `encumbered_cents` reaches 0 auto-transitions to `EXPIRED`, expiring any residual `available_balance_cents` through an `EXPIRY` row. And each funding unit's `committed_cents` falls while its `disbursed_cents` rises by the stored shares — plus, where an `EXPIRY` was produced, its `reserved_cents` falls and its `refunded_cents` rises by that row's shares. The room's `installed_power_kw` and `installed_fume_hoods` are re-derived as the running sums over that room's `INSTALLED` orders only.

On failure the order moves `DELIVERED → UTILITY_BLOCKED` with `utility_block_reason` set to the first failing check, and the divergent side-effect set is precisely that **nothing else happens**: no ledger row is written, nothing is disbursed, the encumbrance stays open with the money still committed, and the room's `installed_power_kw` and `installed_fume_hoods` are unchanged. This is the state in which facilities upgrades the room, or another order in the room is cancelled to free headroom, after which the installation can simply be retried and will take the `UTILITY_BLOCKED → INSTALLED` edge.

**Cancellation** by the dean's office is available from `REQUESTED`, `ORDERED`, `DELIVERED` and `UTILITY_BLOCKED`, and always lands the order in `CANCELLED`. From `REQUESTED` there is nothing to compensate, since no cents were ever encumbered. From the other three statuses the compensation is identical in all cases: one `RELEASE` row per open `ENCUMBRANCE` row of that order, each copying the exact stored per-funder split of the row it reverses so that no funder's cent drifts. Each affected tranche's `encumbered_cents` falls and its `available_balance_cents` rises — except against an `OBLIGATED` tranche, where the released cents are immediately expired through an `EXPIRY` row of the same amount, because cents released from an obligated tranche can never be re-spent. Each funding unit's `committed_cents` falls by the stored shares.

Orders are listed with filters on `package_id`, `install_space_id`, `status` and `utility_block_reason`.

### 3.8. Lab Staff and Graduate Appointments

Personnel money buys people. Either the dean's office or the faculty member proposes an appointment against the package's `PERSONNEL` tranches, supplying the `package_id`, an `appointment_type` of `LAB_MANAGER`, `POSTDOC`, `GRAD_RA` or `TECHNICIAN`, an `fte` fraction strictly greater than 0 and at most 1.0, a `monthly_cost_cents` figure strictly greater than 0 representing the full-time monthly cost *before* the full-time-equivalent factor is applied, a `start_date` naming the first committed month and an `end_date` naming the last, with `end_date` at or after `start_date`. A `Faculty` actor may only propose against their own package. The appointment is created in status `PROPOSED` with `encumbered_cents` and `disbursed_cents` at 0 and `ended_on` null.

`committed_months` is derived as an **inclusive month span**: the difference in calendar years multiplied by 12, plus the difference in calendar months, plus one. An appointment running from March to May of the same year therefore commits three months, and one running from November of one year to February of the next commits four.

The nested rounding order used to price an appointment is normative. **The full-time-equivalent-weighted monthly charge is rounded half up to whole cents first, and only then multiplied by the month count.** Concretely, `monthly_cost_cents` multiplied by `fte`, rounded half up to whole cents, gives the monthly charge; the encumbrance total is that monthly charge multiplied by `committed_months`. Rounding the product of `monthly_cost_cents`, `fte` and `committed_months` instead is explicitly not the rule here, and produces a different total whenever the weighted monthly cost has a fractional cent.

Activation by the dean's office moves the appointment `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and encumbers that total. Four preconditions apply: the appointment is in `PROPOSED`; the package's `spend_status` is `SPENDING`, else `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN`; the faculty member is in `appointment_status` `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the `PERSONNEL` tranche candidates cover the encumbrance total, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRANCHE_BALANCE`, or `ERR_NO_OPEN_TRANCHE` when the candidate set is empty. The waterfall then writes one `ENCUMBRANCE` row per `PERSONNEL` tranche consumed, and each funding unit's `committed_cents` rises by its stored share. While the appointment is `ACTIVE`, its `encumbered_cents` reads as the encumbrance total less whatever has already been disbursed against it; while it is `PROPOSED`, `CANCELLED` or `ENDED`, its `encumbered_cents` reads as 0.

Ending an appointment — early or exactly on schedule — moves it `ACTIVE → ENDED` and stamps `ended_on` from the clock. The dean's office supplies an `end_date` which must be at or after the appointment's `start_date`. The months actually served are computed with the same inclusive month-span arithmetic against the supplied `end_date`, then clamped so they never exceed `committed_months`. That supplied `end_date` is an input to the arithmetic only and is never written back onto the appointment: the stored `start_date`, `end_date` and `committed_months` keep the values recorded at creation, which is what leaves the clamp something to clamp against. The amount disbursed is the monthly charge multiplied by the months served, and the amount released is the encumbrance total less the amount disbursed. `DISBURSEMENT` rows for the served part and `RELEASE` rows for the unserved remainder are written against the same tranches that carried the encumbrance, both mirroring the stored splits of the `ENCUMBRANCE` rows they settle: the disbursed part is split on its own amount, and each paired release share is derived as the per-funder arithmetic remainder, exactly as §2.7 requires. Each affected tranche's `encumbered_cents` falls, its `disbursed_cents` rises by the served part, and its `available_balance_cents` rises by the released part — unless the tranche is `OBLIGATED`, in which case the released cents are immediately expired. Each funding unit's `committed_cents` falls and its `disbursed_cents` rises by the stored shares.

The fourth appointment status, `CANCELLED`, is never requested directly. It is reached only by the freeze cascade of §3.9, which cancels appointments still sitting in `PROPOSED`; `ended_on` is stamped and, because nothing was ever encumbered, no ledger row is written.

Appointments are readable individually with their committed months and encumbrance, and as a filterable list under their package accepting `status` and `appointment_type`.

### 3.9. Fiscal Year-End, Departure, Retention and Final Settlement

**Closing a fiscal year on a package** is a single deliberate operation, available to the dean's office and to the dean, that takes a `fiscal_year` label and reclassifies every tranche of that year. A `Dean` running it must be scoped to the package's `college_unit_id`. Two temporal guards apply: the requested year's window must have closed, meaning the clock is at or after that year's July 1st boundary, else `ERR_ROLLOVER_FISCAL_YEAR_NOT_ELAPSED`; and no tranche of that year may already be `CARRIED_FORWARD` or `EXPIRED`, because the rollover for a year runs at most once, else `ERR_ROLLOVER_ALREADY_RUN`.

The rollover then executes six steps in a pinned order. Reordering any pair changes the outcome, so the order is normative.

1. **Read the eligibility ratio before writing anything.** Take the package's `spend_down_ratio` as computed from the pre-rollover ledger, before this call writes a single row. Carryforward is permitted only when that ratio is **at or above 0.5000** — a package that has committed or spent half of its total commitment has earned the right to roll money forward. At the boundary the ratio is eligible; 0.4999 is not. A department that has barely started spending loses its unspent money instead of banking it.
2. **Select the subjects.** The subjects are the package's tranches whose `fiscal_year` equals the requested year and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN` or `OBLIGATED`. Tranches of that year already sitting in `CARRIED_FORWARD`, `EXPIRED` or `RECAPTURED` are already settled and are skipped entirely.
3. **Obligate what still carries open commitments.** Any subject whose `encumbered_cents` is greater than 0 moves to status `OBLIGATED`. Its `available_balance_cents` is nevertheless still processed by steps 4 through 6 exactly like any other subject — it carries forward and/or expires, ending at 0. What an `OBLIGATED` tranche keeps is its `encumbered_cents`, which survives the rollover and settles later by `DISBURSEMENT`, or by a `RELEASE` immediately followed by an `EXPIRY` row of the same amount. The `OBLIGATED` label outranks the `CARRIED_FORWARD` and `EXPIRED` labels that step 6 would otherwise assign: the status stays `OBLIGATED` until the last encumbrance on it settles, at which point the tranche auto-transitions `OBLIGATED → EXPIRED`. An `OBLIGATED` tranche accepts `DISBURSEMENT` and `RELEASE` rows against its open encumbrances but is no longer a waterfall candidate, so its cents can never be committed to something new.
4. **Compute each subject's carry-eligible amount.** If step 1 found the package ineligible, the carry-eligible amount is 0. Otherwise it depends on `carryforward_policy`: for `FULL` it is the whole `available_balance_cents`; for `CAPPED` it is the lesser of `available_balance_cents` and the tranche's `amount_cents` multiplied by `carryforward_cap_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents; for `NONE` it is 0. The `CAPPED` ceiling is a percentage of the tranche's original `amount_cents` — never of `amount_cents` plus `carried_in_cents`, and never of the current available balance.
5. **Find the destination and move the money.** The destination is the tranche of the **same package** with the **same `category`** and a `fiscal_year` exactly one greater than the requested year, selected by `expires_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. When no such tranche exists, the carry-eligible amount collapses to 0 for that subject: money expires rather than being conjured into a year that was never scheduled. Otherwise a `CARRYFORWARD_OUT` row for the carry-eligible amount is written against the subject, raising its `carried_out_cents`, and a `CARRYFORWARD_IN` row of the same amount is written against the destination, raising its `carried_in_cents` and its available balance together. Carryforward itself moves no funder money: no unit's `reserved_cents` or `refunded_cents` changes on account of a carry.
6. **Expire the residue and label the subject.** Whatever `available_balance_cents` remains after step 5 is the leftover; when it is greater than 0 an `EXPIRY` row is written for it. The subject's final status is `CARRIED_FORWARD` when its carry-eligible amount was greater than 0, otherwise `EXPIRED` when there was a leftover to expire, and otherwise still `EXPIRED` with no row written at all — a fully drained `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` tranche still closes as `EXPIRED`. The one exception is the subjects that step 3 marked `OBLIGATED`, whose status stays `OBLIGATED`. For every `EXPIRY` row written, each funding unit's `reserved_cents` falls and its `refunded_cents` rises by that row's stored share, returning the money to the pool.

`CARRIED_FORWARD` is a terminal status precisely because steps 5 and 6 together always drive the subject's `available_balance_cents` to exactly 0: the carried-out amount plus the expired residue consume it entirely, so no later transition can move cents on it. The rollover also opens next-year tranches that are now inside their window, advancing them `SCHEDULED → OPEN` and stamping `opened_on`.

**Closing out a package** is the dean's act of ending it cleanly. It requires `negotiation_status` `ACTIVE` and a `total_encumbered_cents` of exactly 0; any open encumbrance held by an equipment order, a staff appointment or a renovation project, or any tranche still `OBLIGATED`, blocks it with `ERR_OPEN_ENCUMBRANCES_BLOCK_CLOSEOUT`. On success, every tranche in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, `OBLIGATED` or `CARRIED_FORWARD` with an `available_balance_cents` greater than 0 moves to `EXPIRED` with an `EXPIRY` row for its residual; each funding unit's `reserved_cents` falls and its `refunded_cents` rises by its share of every such row; `spend_status` moves to `SETTLED`; `negotiation_status` moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED_OUT`; and `closed_out_on` is stamped from the clock.

**A departure notice** is how the institution learns that a commitment may not run its course. The dean's office records a `departure_intent` of `DEPARTURE` (a plain resignation), `INTERNAL_TRANSFER` (a move to another department in the same college) or `OUTSIDE_OFFER` (a competing offer that opens a retention negotiation), together with a `departure_effective_on` calendar date from which separation may be executed. An `INTERNAL_TRANSFER` must also name a `target_funding_unit_id`: an existing unit whose `unit_type` is `DEPARTMENT`, different from the faculty member's current `funding_unit_id`, and sharing the same parent college. A missing or invalid target — or a target supplied for a non-transfer intent — is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_REQUIRED`. The faculty member must be `ACTIVE`, and moves `ACTIVE → DEPARTURE_NOTICE`; the three notice fields are stored, with `target_funding_unit_id` left null for anything but a transfer.

The notice is also the root of the **freeze cascade**. The faculty member's package with `negotiation_status` `ACTIVE` moves `spend_status` `SPENDING → FROZEN`, which in turn cancels the not-yet-committed downstream work: every `StaffAppointment` of that package still in `PROPOSED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and every `EquipmentOrder` still in `REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Items that already carry money — orders in `ORDERED`, `DELIVERED` or `UTILITY_BLOCKED`, appointments in `ACTIVE`, projects in `AWARDED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — are deliberately left untouched until separation, because the institution is still contractually on the hook for them. From this point on, every money-moving operation on that package fails with `ERR_PACKAGE_SPEND_FROZEN`.

**A retention counteroffer** is the escape hatch from `DEPARTURE_NOTICE`, and it is the mechanism that makes both the package's negotiation axis and the faculty member's appointment axis cyclic. A counteroffer is drafted as an ordinary package with `package_kind` `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER` and a `superseded_package_id` naming the predecessor, and it then runs the whole negotiation of §3.3 from `DRAFT` again. Three additional checks apply at creation. The predecessor must be in `negotiation_status` `ACTIVE`, belong to the same faculty member, and that faculty member must be in `appointment_status` `DEPARTURE_NOTICE` with a `departure_intent` of exactly `OUTSIDE_OFFER`, else `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — an internal transfer or a plain resignation cannot be countered. The **recommitment floor** must be met: sum the `available_balance_cents` of the predecessor's tranches in status `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` to get the predecessor's unspent balance, add 2,500,000 cents of genuinely new money — twenty-five thousand dollars — and the counteroffer's `proposed_total_cents` must be at least that floor, else `ERR_RECOMMITMENT_BELOW_FLOOR`. A counteroffer that rolls the entire unspent balance forward and adds exactly the minimum new money sits at the floor and is accepted. Finally, the faculty member's `retention_round` becomes the predecessor's round count plus one; the field is defined as the count of that faculty member's `RETENTION_COUNTEROFFER` packages whose `negotiation_status` is `ACTIVE`, `CLOSED_OUT` or `SUPERSEDED`.

Activating the counteroffer fires the **recommitment cascade** on the predecessor. It is blocked with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_HAS_OPEN_ENCUMBRANCES` if any predecessor tranche is `OBLIGATED` or its `total_encumbered_cents` is not 0. Otherwise, for each `category` in turn, the sum of `available_balance_cents` over the predecessor's tranches in status `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN` or `CARRIED_FORWARD` is removed by `RECAPTURE` rows that drive those tranches to `RECAPTURED`, and the same amount is added as a `CARRYFORWARD_IN` row on the successor's earliest tranche of that same category — earliest by `expires_on` ascending, ties broken by `fiscal_year` ascending, further ties by `created_at` ascending. Where the successor has no tranche in that category, the amount simply stays recaptured and is refunded to the funder units. Each funding unit's `reserved_cents` falls and its `refunded_cents` rises by its share of every `RECAPTURE` row. The predecessor's `spend_status` becomes `SETTLED` and its `negotiation_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`; the successor, which was never frozen, spends under `SPENDING`. And the faculty member moves `DEPARTURE_NOTICE → ACTIVE` with `departure_intent`, `departure_effective_on` and `target_funding_unit_id` all cleared to null and `retention_round` re-derived, closing the retention cycle `ACTIVE → DEPARTURE_NOTICE → ACTIVE`. Both packages have their `version` incremented.

**Separation** is the terminal settlement and the deepest cascade in the system. The dean's office executes it once the clock is at or after `departure_effective_on`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEPARATION_NOT_DUE`, and the faculty member must be in `DEPARTURE_NOTICE`. A `departure_intent` of `DEPARTURE` or `OUTSIDE_OFFER` moves the faculty member `DEPARTURE_NOTICE → DEPARTED`; an intent of `INTERNAL_TRANSFER` moves them `DEPARTURE_NOTICE → TRANSFERRED_INTERNAL` and additionally rewrites their `funding_unit_id` to the recorded `target_funding_unit_id`. Either way `separated_on` is stamped from the clock. Note that separation is explicitly exempt from the close-out encumbrance guard, because releasing those encumbrances is part of its own work.

The cascade then runs in four linked stages. First, the committed work is wound down: every `StaffAppointment` in `ACTIVE` moves to `ENDED` with an effective end date of the clock's own calendar date, running exactly the same served-months and remainder arithmetic described in §3.8 — and, exactly as there, that effective date is an arithmetic input only and is not written onto the appointment, whose stored `end_date` and `committed_months` are left as recorded while `ended_on` is stamped from the clock; every `EquipmentOrder` in `ORDERED`, `DELIVERED` or `UTILITY_BLOCKED` moves to `CANCELLED`, releasing its open encumbrance; every `RenovationProject` in `SCOPING` or `BID` moves to `CANCELLED` with no ledger movement at all, while `AWARDED` and `IN_PROGRESS` projects keep their encumbrance and have it released the same way an equipment order's is. Second, each `RELEASE` row lowers its tranche's `encumbered_cents` and raises its `available_balance_cents`, and a tranche in `OBLIGATED` whose `encumbered_cents` reaches 0 auto-transitions to `EXPIRED` — so cents released back onto an obligated tranche are expired rather than becoming available for settlement. Third, the settlement calculation runs on whatever unspent balance remains. Fourth, every `EquipmentOrder` in status `INSTALLED` receives a `transfer_disposition`: `RETAINED_BY_UNIT` when the intent was `DEPARTURE`, `TRANSFERRED_WITH_FACULTY` when it was `INTERNAL_TRANSFER`, and `SURPLUSED` when it was `OUTSIDE_OFFER` — the instrument was bought for a programme that left for a competing institution, so it returns to the college's shared inventory rather than to the department. The package finally moves `negotiation_status` `ACTIVE → CLOSED_OUT` with `closed_out_on` stamped, and `spend_status` `FROZEN → SETTLED`.

The settlement itself has **two variants that agree on the money recovered and disagree on where it lands**, and `settlement_variant` records which one ran. In both variants the unspent balance is the sum of `available_balance_cents` over the package's tranches in status `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` only — measured after the release stage above, so `encumbered_cents` is 0 everywhere by then, and `CARRIED_FORWARD`, `EXPIRED` and `RECAPTURED` tranches have no available balance left to settle. In both variants `RECAPTURE` rows drain each such tranche to 0 and move it to `RECAPTURED`, split across the three original funders by the ordinary rule of §2.7, so the department, the college and the dean's office each recover exactly their own money. That identical unwind is the conserved quantity that makes the settlement zero-sum rather than a duplication of funds.

Under the `DEPARTURE_RECAPTURE` variant — chosen when `departure_intent` is `DEPARTURE` or `OUTSIDE_OFFER`, meaning the faculty member leaves the university — all of it stays in the original pools: `recaptured_unspent_cents` is set to the whole unspent balance, and both `transferred_balance_cents` and `college_holdback_cents` are 0.

Under the `INTERNAL_TRANSFER` variant — chosen when `departure_intent` is `INTERNAL_TRANSFER` and a `target_funding_unit_id` is set — the same recapture happens against the same three funders with the same splits, and then the money moves onward. `college_holdback_cents` is the unspent balance multiplied by 15 percent, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents; `transferred_balance_cents` is the unspent balance less that holdback, so the two always sum to the unspent balance exactly; and `recaptured_unspent_cents` is set to the holdback alone, not to the whole balance. The receiving department is then credited: its `cost_share_pool_cents` rises by `transferred_balance_cents`. Fifteen percent of the residue therefore stays with the college as the price of moving a startup commitment between departments, and eighty-five percent follows the faculty member. Summed across all four units the pool deltas are zero in both variants — the receiving unit's gain is exactly the funders' recovery less the holdback.

Space is released as part of the same request: every `SpaceAssignment` of the faculty member in status `ACTIVE` moves to `RELEASED`, every one still in `PROPOSED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and each affected room whose `allocated_share_pct` falls to 0 moves `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE`. In the narrow case where the departing faculty member held the full 100 percent of a room and a renovation project on that room was cancelled by this same cascade, the room instead moves `ASSIGNED → DECOMMISSIONED`.
