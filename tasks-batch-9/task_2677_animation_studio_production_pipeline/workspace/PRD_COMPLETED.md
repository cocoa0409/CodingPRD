# Animation Studio Production Pipeline API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An animation studio runs several shows at once, and each show is a long chain of dependent commitments: a script has to be signed off before a storyboard is worth drawing, a character model has to be published before it can be rigged and reused, a shot cannot be lit before it has been animated, and none of it can be mastered before a client has sat in a calibrated screening room and said yes. Today those commitments live in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and a render-farm scheduler that nobody reconciles against the budget. The consequence is familiar to every production manager: the farm bills for frames the show cannot afford, an outsourcing vendor delivers work that quietly fails a security requirement, and a shot that was reported complete on Friday is reopened on Monday by a director note that nobody tracked.

This document specifies the **Studio Production Pipeline API**, the system of record that replaces those spreadsheets. It is mounted under `/studio` and it owns the whole span of a production: pre-production approval gates, asset construction with a reuse library, shot production across five review-gated stages, review sessions whose director notes reach down into individual shot tasks, render farm capacity with priority scheduling and per-frame cost, outsourcing packages with quality and security standards, and final delivery with color management and deliverable versioning. Every number a producer reads — committed spend, earned value, cost and schedule performance indices, retake counts, partner quality — is derived by this service from primitive events, never typed in by hand.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **nothing is advisory**: if the system says a production has 12 shots and 12 are complete, the phase advance is allowed, and if one is not, the advance is refused with a specific error rather than a warning. Second, **the studio's ledgers and rollups are always reconciled at the moment a request returns** — there is no background job, no eventual consistency window, and no report that is stale relative to the write that just succeeded. Where a single operation has consequences on three entities, all three are updated before the response is emitted.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Studio Roles and Request Identity

Every request to this API, without exception, carries two headers: `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` declares the seat they are sitting in. If either header is missing, empty or composed only of whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not listed as an authorized role for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before the server looks at the URL's identifiers or the request body.

The six roles are `ProductionManager`, `Artist`, `Supervisor`, `Director`, `OutsourceCoordinator` and `PartnerRepresentative`. They are peers with deliberately non-overlapping write surfaces rather than a hierarchy — no role is a superset of another, and a `ProductionManager` cannot decide a stage review any more than a `Supervisor` can open a show.

A `ProductionManager` runs the show: opening productions, submitting pre-production gate packages, creating shots, assigning stages to artists, registering screening rooms and render pools, reserving and settling render capacity, advancing production phases, and creating, submitting and shipping deliverables. An `Artist` builds assets and executes shot stages, acting only on the tasks assigned to them. A `Supervisor` is the quality authority: stage review decisions, reuse-library promotions, and QC sign-off on both outsourced packages and deliverables. A `Director` is the creative authority: pre-production gate decisions, running review sessions, and raising and resolving director notes. An `OutsourceCoordinator` owns the vendor surface: onboarding, approving and suspending partners, and drafting and issuing outsourcing packages. A `PartnerRepresentative` is the vendor itself, acting on its own packages.

Identity semantics differ between the five internal roles and the vendor role, and the difference matters for every ownership check in this specification.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProductionManager`, `Artist`, `Supervisor`, `Director` or `OutsourceCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands a trace — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a pre-production gate, as `library_submitted_by_user_id` and `library_decided_by_user_id` on an asset, as `assigned_artist_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a shot stage task, as `raised_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a director note — but it never compares that value against any entity's `id`. Comparisons involving these roles are always between two recorded operator identifiers, never between an operator identifier and a primary key. In particular, an `Artist` may act on a stage task only when `X-User-ID` equals the task's `assigned_artist_user_id`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_NOT_TASK_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `OutsourcePartner` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `OutsourcePartner` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: accepting or delivering an outsourcing package requires `X-User-ID` to equal that package's `partner_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PACKAGE_PARTNER` (HTTP 403). A `PartnerRepresentative` may also log hours on and submit a stage task, but only when the task's `outsource_package_id` names a package belonging to that same partner; otherwise the rejection is `ERR_NOT_TASK_OWNER`. The vendor's read surface is deliberately narrow: it is authorized only on the stage-task and outsourcing-package read endpoints, so a partner probing productions, shots, assets, sessions, notes, pools, jobs or deliverables receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. On those two reads no ownership filter is applied at all: a `PartnerRepresentative` may read and list every stage task and every outsourcing package in the studio, not merely those belonging to its own partner row.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical studio clock of §2.4 rather than from the host's wall clock — so two responses returned without an intervening clock move carry the same timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds, or one that is not parseable as an integer, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Paginated responses carry four additional keys inside `meta`: the effective `limit`, the effective `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). The `response_type` for a collection read is `"array"` and `data` holds the page of rows.

Unless a collection states otherwise, rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest row appears first. Because the logical clock of §2.4 only moves when a client moves it, many rows legitimately share a `created_at`; wherever two rows tie on a collection's stated ordering key, the tie is broken by the order in which the rows were created, oldest first. Filter query parameters are matched literally against the stored field and are not themselves validated against that field's enum, so a filter value no row carries yields an empty page rather than `ERR_VALIDATION`, and a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent. Exactly four collections use a domain-natural ordering instead: pre-production gates are ordered by `sequence_index` ascending, shots by `shot_code` ascending, shot stage tasks by `stage_order` ascending, and review sessions by `window_start` ascending. Productions, assets, director notes, screening rooms, render pools, render jobs, outsourcing partners, outsourcing packages and deliverables all use `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Studio Clock

Every resource in this system — all thirteen entity types — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, both taken from the logical clock reading at the moment of the write that produced them. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row, including mutations reached through a cascade from another entity's operation. A single accepted request advances a given row's `version` by at most 1 in total, however many steps of that request's cascade happen to touch that same row — so the gate that clears the last approval, which is both approved and locked by one call, ends that call one version higher and not two. No entity in this domain is exempt: there is no resource that carries only `id` and `created_at`. Whenever a rule in this document says a field is recomputed **as part of a write**, the owning row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances with it. A field that is instead derived at read time, as described in §2.7, is not a mutation of the row and moves neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProductionManager` may move it with the administrative test-driving endpoint `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`, which is the only key `data` carries. The target may be any value that parses as an ISO 8601 instant, including one earlier than the current reading, so the clock may be driven backwards as freely as forwards; a supplied timestamp that carries no zone offset is read as UTC, and one that does not parse at all is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Setting the clock mutates no business row, but every derived quantity that reads the clock changes with it: a screening room's `calibration_status`, a shot's schedule-based planned value, a partner's quality decay windows, and the rejection of reservation windows that start in the past. Every timestamp the system writes itself — `decided_at`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `opened_at`, `closed_at`, `last_calibrated_at`, `qc_decided_at`, `delivered_at` — is taken from the logical clock at the moment of the write.

### 2.5. Production Phase Gating: What Is Writable When

A production moves through four phases in strict order — `PRE_PRODUCTION`, `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`, `DELIVERY` and finally `WRAPPED` — and the current phase decides which parts of the show are writable. This is the single most pervasive precondition in the system, and every violation of it reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

While a production is in `PRE_PRODUCTION`, gate submissions and gate review decisions are the productive work; shots and assets may be created, but no stage work may begin. Once the production is `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`, gate work is closed and the shot floor opens: stage assignment, hour logging, stage submission and stage review decisions, render reservations, review session bookings, director notes and package issuance all require this phase. In `DELIVERY`, new shots may no longer be created for the production, but deliverable work continues and review sessions may still run. Shipping a deliverable requires `DELIVERY` specifically. Deliverable creation, deliverable submission and the deliverable QC decision are each legal in exactly two phases: `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or `DELIVERY`. Once a production is `WRAPPED`, nothing under it is writable at all.

Asset and shot creation therefore require the owning production's `phase` to be either `PRE_PRODUCTION` or `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`; asset build advancement carries the same two-phase allowance. The three remaining asset operations — library nomination, library decision and source relinking — are legal in any phase other than `WRAPPED`, that is in `PRE_PRODUCTION`, `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or `DELIVERY`. On the phase-advance operation itself, the phase check fires only when the requested target is not the legal successor of the current phase and the current phase is not `PRE_PRODUCTION` — a production still in `PRE_PRODUCTION` gets a more specific gate-oriented rejection instead, described in §3.1.

### 2.6. Segregation of Duties Across Submit and Decide

Three artifacts in this pipeline are submitted by one person and decided by another: a pre-production gate, a shot stage task, and an asset nominated to the reuse library. In all three the studio's audit policy is identical — **the operator identifier that submitted an artifact may never be the operator identifier that decides it**. On a gate review decision the deciding `X-User-ID` is compared against the gate's `submitted_by_user_id`; on a stage review decision it is compared against the task's `submitted_by_user_id`; on a library decision it is compared against the asset's `library_submitted_by_user_id`. If the two are equal, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and no counter, timestamp or state moves.

The rule is about identifiers, not roles. Because the gate submitter is a `ProductionManager` and the gate decider is a `Director`, the roles already differ; the check still runs, because nothing prevents one human from holding both seats. Deliverable and package QC decisions are not covered by this rule — their integrity comes from the phase and state preconditions instead.

### 2.7. Rollup Reconciliation and Cascade Ordering

A large part of this specification is bookkeeping that the caller never writes directly. Counters on a `Shot` are derived from its five stage tasks; counters on a `Production` are derived from its shots, its packages and its render jobs. All of it is reconciled synchronously: by the time a mutating request returns, every ancestor figure it disturbed has been recomputed. A recomputed row has its `version` advanced and its `updated_at` refreshed only when at least one of its stored derived fields actually changed value; a recomputation that lands back on the figures the row already held is not a mutation and moves neither.

Some of these derived fields are, in addition, **recomputed on every read** rather than only on write, because their value depends on something outside the read row's own stored events — on the logical clock, or on the current state of another row. They include a screening room's `calibration_status`, the schedule-based planned value on a shot and on a production — and, because it is derived from that planned value, the `spi` of each, which is likewise never stored — both partner quality scores, and an asset's `reuse_depth`, `reuse_discount_pct` and `effective_complexity_points`. Reading such a resource twice at two different clock positions, or before and after a write to a row it derives from, can legitimately return two different values without any write to the resource itself in between — and therefore without its `version` or `updated_at` moving.

When one operation touches many rows, the order is normative because counters and flags are order-sensitive. Wherever a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows, the set is processed in `created_at` ascending order, with ties broken by creation order exactly as in §2.3: the director notes of a closing session, the outsourcing packages of a partner being suspended, and the reuse-chain descendants of an asset being retired are all walked oldest-first.

Two rollup chains recur often enough to name here. On the shot side, `retake_count` is the sum of `revision_count` over the shot's five stage tasks, `escalation_flag` is true when any of those tasks has `escalated` true, and `status` is derived from the tasks' statuses and the production's phase — never set by a client. On the production side, `shot_count`, `completed_shot_count`, `at_risk_shot_count` and `total_retake_count` are counts and sums over the production's shots, and `render_committed_cents` and `render_actual_cents` are the sums of the shots' `render_cost_committed_cents` and `render_cost_actual_cents` respectively.

### 2.8. Money, Rounding and the Production Budget Position

All money in this system is an **integer count of cents**. Wherever a monetary value has to be derived from a non-integer intermediate, the intermediate is rounded to a whole cent using round-half-up (a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero). Non-monetary derived numbers carry fixed precision too: `contingency_pct`, `difficulty_factor`, `base_complexity_points`, `effective_complexity_points`, `quality_min_score`, both partner quality scores, `budgeted_hours`, `actual_hours`, `committed_core_hours` and `reuse_discount_pct` all carry two decimal places with round-half-up; the cost and schedule performance indices `cpi` and `spi` carry four decimal places with round-half-up. Where a client supplies a value for one of those two-decimal fields, the value is quantized to two decimals with round-half-up on ingest and stored in that form, rather than being rejected for carrying more precision than the field declares.

The production's budget position is recomputed in a fixed four-step order. **Step one** produces the pre-overhead direct figure. The system sums `fee_committed_cents` over every outsourcing package of the production whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, `DELIVERED`, `QC_PASSED` or `QC_FAILED`; packages in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `RECALLED` contribute nothing. To that it adds labor: for every stage task of every shot of the production, the task's `actual_hours` multiplied by the studio labor rate of 9,000 cents per hour and rounded to a whole cent, with those per-task amounts then summed. Finally it adds the production's `render_actual_cents`. The result is stored as `direct_committed_cents`.

**Step two** applies studio overhead, and the tier is keyed on the pre-overhead direct figure only — never on the grand total. When `direct_committed_cents` is strictly below 5,000,000 cents (50,000 dollars) the overhead rate is 15 percent; when it is strictly below 20,000,000 cents (200,000 dollars) the rate is 12 percent; at or above that second ceiling the rate is 10 percent. `overhead_fee_cents` is `direct_committed_cents` multiplied by the selected rate, rounded to a whole cent. **Step three** sets `total_committed_cents` to the sum of `direct_committed_cents` and `overhead_fee_cents`, computes the spending ceiling as the production's `budget_cents` multiplied by one plus its `contingency_pct` and rounded to a whole cent, and sets `available_cents` to that ceiling minus `total_committed_cents`. Because the tier is keyed on the direct figure, a direct commitment of 4,900,000 cents takes the 15 percent rate and yields a total of 5,635,000 cents; keying the tier on the resulting total, which lands above the first ceiling, would produce the wrong 5,488,000 and is not what this system does.

**Step four** is the prospective form of the same computation, used as a gate rather than as a report: before an outsourcing package is issued, steps one through three are re-run with the candidate package's `fee_cents` added to the package-fee sum, and if the resulting `total_committed_cents` would exceed the spending ceiling the issue is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_WOULD_EXCEED` (HTTP 402). The position is recomputed for real on package acceptance, on package QC decisions, on package recall, on render job settlement and on stage-task hour logging. `available_cents` may go negative only as the result of a cascade that commits money without passing the prospective gate — it is never driven negative by an operation the gate could have refused.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Pools and Productions

Two write endpoints in this system take an explicit version echo, because in both cases a second sequential request is legitimate product behaviour and therefore cannot be refused by any state precondition — only a stale view of the parent can be detected.

`POST /studio/render-jobs` requires the non-optional field `expected_render_pool_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `RenderPool` as the client last read it. Every accepted reservation, settlement, cancellation and preemption on that pool advances `RenderPool.version` by 1, so if any of those has happened since the client's read, the value no longer matches and the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RENDER_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) without touching the pool's `committed_core_hours` or `reserved_job_count`. A shot may legitimately hold several reservations over the same window for the same stage task, which is exactly why no `RenderJob` state precondition would catch the duplicate: only the pool version does.

`POST /studio/deliverables` requires the non-optional field `expected_production_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Production` as the client last read it. Each accepted deliverable creation advances `Production.version` by 1, so a create carrying an older value is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no `version_index` is consumed by the rejected attempt. This keeps supersession intentional: because creating a new version legitimately supersedes the `QC_PASSED` version already in the same group, a client that has not observed the current state of the production must re-read before it can supersede anything.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version echo. Requests that supply one where it is not specified simply have it ignored, in line with the general rule that unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.10. Request Evaluation Order

When a single request could fail several checks at once, the studio needs a deterministic answer to "which error does the caller see?". The thirty-five checks below are evaluated **strictly in the order listed**, and the first one that fails is the one reported; later checks never run. This ordering is normative, not descriptive — a request that is both unauthorized and referencing a missing row reports the authorization failure, and a request that would both exceed capacity and exceed the render allowance reports the capacity failure.

1. **Headers.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The declared role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the request path resolves to a stored row, validated **left to right** so that the leftmost unresolvable identifier is the one reported. The error names the target type: `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RENDER_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying an identifier in its path.
4. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key in the request body resolves to a stored row, validated in the order the fields are declared in that endpoint's input list; an array of foreign keys such as `stage_task_ids` is validated element by element in array order, so the first unresolvable element is the one reported. The same family of 404 not-found identifiers is used. Applies to every write carrying foreign keys in its body. A body field of foreign-key shape that is absent, or present but not a non-empty string, is not examined at this step at all: it falls through to the schema check and is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`.
5. **Schema.** Required fields are present, types are correct, enum members are legal, and numeric ranges are respected. Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. A field present with an explicit JSON `null` counts as absent: a required field so supplied is a schema failure, and an optional one falls back to its default. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an action endpoint with no required fields accepts a call with no body at all, while a body that is not parseable JSON or that parses to something other than a JSON object is itself a schema failure. A failure is `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). Applies to every write.
6. **Task ownership.** On stage-task submission and hour logging, `X-User-ID` equals the task's `assigned_artist_user_id`, or the caller is the `PartnerRepresentative` whose identifier equals the `partner_id` of the partner behind the task's `outsource_package_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_TASK_OWNER` (403). Applies to exactly two operations: `POST /studio/stage-tasks/{id}/submit` and `POST /studio/stage-tasks/{id}/log-hours`.
7. **Package ownership.** On package acceptance and delivery, `X-User-ID` equals the package's `partner_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_PACKAGE_PARTNER` (403). Applies to exactly two operations: `POST /studio/outsource-packages/{id}/accept` and `POST /studio/outsource-packages/{id}/deliver`.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The deciding `X-User-ID` differs from the submitter recorded on the artifact — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to exactly three operations: `POST /studio/pre-production-gates/{id}/review-decision` (against `submitted_by_user_id`), `POST /studio/stage-tasks/{id}/review-decision` (against `submitted_by_user_id`) and `POST /studio/assets/{id}/library-decision` (against `library_submitted_by_user_id`).
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied version echo matches the parent's stored `version` — otherwise HTTP 412 with `ERR_STALE_RENDER_POOL_VERSION` on `POST /studio/render-jobs` and `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` on `POST /studio/deliverables`. Applies to exactly those two endpoints.
10. **Production phase.** The owning production's `phase` permits the operation as described in §2.5 — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID` (409). Applies to every write under a production.
11. **Target state.** The target row's own state field permits the requested transition. The error names the target: `ERR_GATE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSET_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSET_LIBRARY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RENDER_JOB_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PARTNER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint except `POST /studio/stage-tasks/{id}/log-hours`, which carries no precondition on its target's own status at all, and also to `POST /studio/render-jobs`, where the referenced stage task must be in `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `RETAKE`.
12. **Partner not suspended.** The outsourcing partner behind the operation is in good standing: on package issue and package accept its `status` must be exactly `APPROVED`, so a partner still in `ONBOARDING` is refused here as well, while on package deliver it is enough that the partner is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to exactly three operations: package issue, package accept and package deliver.
13. **Stage predecessor.** For a stage task whose `stage_order` is greater than 1, the sibling task of the same shot whose `stage_order` is one lower has status `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_PREVIOUS_STAGE_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/stage-tasks/{id}/assign` only.
14. **Security tier.** The partner's `security_certification_level` is at least the package's `security_tier` on the ordered scale where `NONE` is lowest, then `TIER1`, then `TIER2`, then `TIER3` — otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to package issue.
15. **Security obligations.** Partner-level security obligations are met: `security_certification_level` is not `NONE` when a partner is approved; `watermark_applied` is true on delivery when the partner's `watermark_required` is true; and the partner's `mfa_enforced` is true when the package's `security_tier` is `TIER2` or `TIER3` — otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (422). Applies to two operations: `POST /studio/outsource-partners/{id}/status` and package deliver.
16. **Partner quality.** The partner's `quality_score_rolling` is at least the package's `quality_min_score` at issue time — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_QUALITY_BELOW_STANDARD` (422). Applies to package issue.
17. **Color pipeline.** A deliverable's `color_space` is a member of the production's `approved_color_spaces`, and equals the production's `master_color_space` when `deliverable_type` is `MASTER_EXR`; and a `CLIENT_REVIEW` session's room `room_color_space` is likewise a member of the production's `approved_color_spaces` — otherwise `ERR_COLOR_PIPELINE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /studio/deliverables` and `POST /studio/review-sessions`.
18. **Room calibrated at least once.** The screening room's `last_calibrated_at` is not null before a session may be booked in it — otherwise `ERR_ROOM_NOT_CALIBRATED` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/review-sessions`.
19. **Calibration still valid.** At the moment a session is opened, the room's read-time `calibration_status` is exactly `CALIBRATED`, that is, the age of the calibration in whole days as derived in §3.4 is at most `calibration_valid_days` — otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/review-sessions/{id}/start`.
20. **No blocking notes.** No director note with `severity` `BLOCKING` and `status` either `OPEN` or `RETAKE_ISSUED` exists in the deliverable's scope — the single shot when `shot_id` is set, otherwise every shot of the production — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_NOTES_OPEN` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/deliverables/{id}/submit`.
21. **Single-production references.** Cross-aggregate references stay inside one production: a director note's target task belongs to a shot of the session's production, a package's `stage_task_ids` all belong to shots of the package's production, an asset's `derived_from_asset_id` belongs to the same production, and a deliverable's `shot_id` belongs to its production — otherwise `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE` (422). Applies to five endpoints: `POST /studio/director-notes`, `POST /studio/outsource-packages/{id}/issue`, `POST /studio/assets`, `POST /studio/assets/{id}/relink-source` and `POST /studio/deliverables`. A drafted package may legally hold a foreign scope; the check fires at issue time.
22. **Acyclic reuse chain.** The `derived_from_asset_id` chain stays acyclic and no longer than eight edges — a cycle is `ERR_ASSET_REUSE_CYCLE` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/assets` and `POST /studio/assets/{id}/relink-source`.
23. **Client review before mastering.** A `MASTER_EXR` deliverable may be submitted only after at least one review session of the production whose `session_type` is `CLIENT_REVIEW` has reached status `CLOSED`; a cancelled client review does not count — otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/deliverables/{id}/submit`.
24. **Window sanity.** `window_start` is strictly earlier than `window_end` and is not earlier than the logical clock now; and a shot's `planned_start_at` is strictly earlier than its `planned_finish_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). Applies to three endpoints: `POST /studio/review-sessions`, `POST /studio/render-jobs` and `POST /studio/shots`.
25. **Room turnover.** The requested session window, expanded by the room's `turnover_buffer_minutes` on both sides, does not intersect the stored unexpanded window of any `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` session in the same room — otherwise `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/review-sessions`.
26. **Seat capacity.** `attendee_count` is at most the room's `seat_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_ROOM_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /studio/review-sessions`.
27. **Code uniqueness.** `production_code` is unique across all productions; `shot_code` and `asset_code` are unique within their production; `package_code` is unique within its production — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to four creates: productions, shots, assets and outsourcing packages.
28. **Gate sequence.** Every gate of the same production with a smaller `sequence_index` has status `APPROVED` before this gate may be submitted — otherwise `ERR_GATE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/pre-production-gates/{id}/submit`.
29. **Gates outstanding.** A production still in `PRE_PRODUCTION` cannot be pushed forward by hand — otherwise `ERR_GATES_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/productions/{id}/advance-phase`.
30. **Shots complete.** `completed_shot_count` equals `shot_count` before a production may enter `DELIVERY`; the check passes vacuously when the production has no shots at all — otherwise `ERR_SHOTS_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to the phase-advance endpoint.
31. **Deliverables complete.** At least one deliverable of the production has status `DELIVERED` and none is left in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `QC_PASSED` before the production may be `WRAPPED` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to the phase-advance endpoint.
32. **One in-flight version.** No deliverable in the target version group is already in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLE_VERSION_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/deliverables`.
33. **Farm capacity.** The render pool admits the requested cores over the requested window, after critical-priority preemption where applicable — otherwise `ERR_RENDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /studio/render-jobs`.
34. **Render allowance.** The production's `render_committed_cents` plus its `render_actual_cents` plus this job's `estimated_cost_cents` is at most the production's `render_allowance_cents` — otherwise `ERR_RENDER_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to `POST /studio/render-jobs`.
35. **Budget headroom.** The prospective `total_committed_cents`, including this package's fee and the overhead recomputed on top of it, stays within `budget_cents` multiplied by one plus `contingency_pct` — otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_WOULD_EXCEED` (402). Applies to `POST /studio/outsource-packages/{id}/issue`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because farm capacity (33) precedes the render allowance (34), a reservation that is both too large for the pool and too expensive for the show reports the capacity error. And because the security-tier check (14) precedes the quality check (16) which precedes the budget check (35), issuing a package to an under-certified, low-scoring partner with no budget headroom reports the security-tier error alone.

### 2.11. Color Management from Room to Master

Color is a first-class constraint in this pipeline rather than metadata. Four color spaces exist in the system and they are spelled exactly `ACEScg`, `LogC`, `Rec709` and `sRGB`. A production declares one of them as its `master_color_space` and carries a list of `approved_color_spaces` that any of its deliverables may use; that list defaults to the single-element list containing the production's `master_color_space`, must be non-empty, and must contain `master_color_space` even when the client supplies it explicitly.

Every deliverable's `color_space` must be a member of the owning production's `approved_color_spaces`. A deliverable whose `deliverable_type` is `MASTER_EXR` is held to a stricter standard: its `color_space` must be exactly the production's `master_color_space`, not merely an approved one. Screening rooms participate in the same pipeline through their `room_color_space`, which is the projector's calibration target: booking a session whose `session_type` is `CLIENT_REVIEW` into a room whose `room_color_space` is not among the production's `approved_color_spaces` is refused. All three of these failures report `ERR_COLOR_PIPELINE_MISMATCH`. Sessions of type `DAILIES` and `DIRECTOR_REVIEW` are not subject to the room color check.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Show and Clearing the Pre-Production Gates

A production manager opens a show by declaring what it is and what it may cost. The payload carries a `title` of between 1 and 200 characters, a `production_code` that must be between 2 and 16 uppercase alphanumeric characters and must not already be in use by any other production in the studio, a `format` that is one of `FEATURE`, `SERIES_EPISODE` or `SHORT`, a `budget_cents` that may not be negative, a `render_allowance_cents` that may not be negative, and a `master_color_space`. Three fields are optional. `contingency_pct` is the fraction of the budget released as a contingency reserve; it defaults to 0.10, carries two decimal places, and must be between 0 and 0.50 inclusive. `approved_color_spaces` defaults to the single-element list holding the declared `master_color_space`. `gate_types` names the pre-production approval gates the show will run; it defaults to all four gates and must be a non-empty subset of `SCRIPT`, `STORYBOARD`, `ANIMATIC` and `DESIGN`, stored in the canonical order in which those four names are listed here; a name repeated in the supplied array is collapsed rather than rejected, so the stored list holds each named gate exactly once. A duplicate `production_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

The new production starts in `PRE_PRODUCTION`. All of its computed figures start at zero — `shot_count`, `completed_shot_count`, `at_risk_shot_count`, `total_retake_count`, `direct_committed_cents`, `overhead_fee_cents`, `total_committed_cents`, `render_committed_cents`, `render_actual_cents`, `bcwp_cents`, `bcws_cents` and `acwp_cents` — while `cpi` and `spi` are null and `available_cents` opens at the full spending ceiling, that is, `budget_cents` multiplied by one plus `contingency_pct` and rounded to a whole cent.

Creating the production also **materializes its gate set immediately**, one gate row per element of `gate_types`. Each gate is stamped with the owning `production_id`, its `gate_type`, and a `sequence_index` assigned by walking the canonical order and numbering consecutively from 1 — so for the default full set `SCRIPT` is 1, `STORYBOARD` is 2, `ANIMATIC` is 3 and `DESIGN` is 4. A given `gate_type` occurs at most once per `production_id`, so a production never holds two `SCRIPT` gates. Every gate starts with `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_count` 0, and null `package_url`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`. These rows appear in **no** creation response: the only way to discover them is the gate collection read, filtered by `production_id`, and optionally narrowed by `gate_type` or `status`.

Gate approval is a two-person loop. The production manager submits a gate's artifact bundle by supplying its `package_url` — any non-empty string, with no URL syntax enforced — which drives the gate from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`, stores the URL, and records the submitter in `submitted_by_user_id`. Submission is legal only while the production is in `PRE_PRODUCTION`, and only when every gate of the same production with a smaller `sequence_index` has already reached `APPROVED` — an out-of-order submission is refused with `ERR_GATE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`. Submitting a gate that is not in `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is refused: a gate already `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` — both of which a production can hold while it is still in `PRE_PRODUCTION` — is refused with `ERR_GATE_STATE_INVALID`. A `LOCKED` gate is a different matter, because a gate only reaches `LOCKED` at the instant its production leaves `PRE_PRODUCTION`: the production-phase check of §2.10 (check 10) therefore fails before the gate's own state is ever examined (check 11), and an otherwise well-formed submission aimed at a `LOCKED` gate reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_GATE_STATE_INVALID`.

The director then decides the gate, supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` together with an optional `note`, which must be a string when supplied but is not persisted anywhere — the gate row carries no note field. Both outcomes record `decided_by_user_id` and stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock, and both require the gate to be `IN_REVIEW` (otherwise `ERR_GATE_STATE_INVALID`) and require the deciding identifier to differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. A `CHANGES_REQUESTED` decision moves the gate `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and adds 1 to its `revision_count`; the production manager then resubmits with a fresh `package_url`, moving it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` and overwriting `submitted_by_user_id`. This is a genuine loop: `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` may run any number of times, and `revision_count` is **never** reset, so it is a lifetime tally of how many times the director sent the artifact back.

Approving the production's last outstanding gate is the event that starts the show. The moment every gate row of the production has `status` `APPROVED`, the system — with no separate request from anyone — moves the production `PRE_PRODUCTION → PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`, drives every one of its gates `APPROVED → LOCKED` (a locked gate is immutable; because its production has by then left `PRE_PRODUCTION`, the production-phase check runs ahead of the gate's own state check on any further submission attempt, so an otherwise well-formed attempt returns `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID`), recomputes every shot of the production from `BLOCKED_PREPROD` to `READY`, and adds 1 to the production's `version`. The shots' schedule-based planned value and schedule performance index become meaningful from this point and are recomputed on the next read of each shot.

Beyond that, phase advancement is manual and takes a single `to_phase` field. Only `DELIVERY` and `WRAPPED` may be requested; asking for `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` is a schema violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`, precisely because that phase is reached by clearing gates and never by request. Asking to advance at all while the production is still in `PRE_PRODUCTION` is refused with `ERR_GATES_INCOMPLETE`, which is the caller's cue that gates remain outstanding. Moving `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE → DELIVERY` requires `completed_shot_count` to equal `shot_count`, and that check passes vacuously for a production that has no shots at all; otherwise the advance is refused with `ERR_SHOTS_INCOMPLETE`. Moving `DELIVERY → WRAPPED` requires at least one deliverable of the production to be `DELIVERED` and no deliverable to remain in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `QC_PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE`. Each accepted advance adds 1 to the production's `version`. Once the production is in `DELIVERY`, creating further shots for it is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID`; once it is `WRAPPED`, every write on every child of the production is refused the same way. The production collection read may be filtered by `phase`.

### 3.2. Asset Construction and the Reuse Library

Assets are the reusable furniture of a show, and they carry **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `build_status` tracks construction; `library_status` tracks whether the asset has been promoted into the studio's reuse library. Neither dimension constrains the other except where this section says so explicitly, and an operation on one dimension never silently moves the other.

An asset is briefed with the owning `production_id`, an `asset_code` unique within that production — any non-empty string, with no length ceiling or character class enforced — an `asset_type` chosen from `CHARACTER_MODEL`, `PROP`, `ENVIRONMENT`, `RIG` and `SURFACING_PACKAGE`, and a `base_complexity_points` estimate that must be strictly greater than zero and carries two decimal places. Optionally the payload names a `derived_from_asset_id`, the reuse-library asset this one is built from; leaving it out marks the asset as an original build. A repeated `asset_code` inside a production is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, and a `derived_from_asset_id` pointing at an asset of a different production is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE`. New assets open at `build_status` `BRIEFED` and `library_status` `PRIVATE`, with `reuse_source_stale` false — unconditionally so, even when an asset further up the declared chain is already `LIBRARY_DEPRECATED`, because the flag is only ever derived from a chain at relink time — and null `library_submitted_by_user_id` and `library_decided_by_user_id`, and their `reuse_depth`, `reuse_discount_pct` and `effective_complexity_points` are computed immediately.

Construction advances one step at a time. An artist supplies a `to_build_status`, and the value must be **exactly the immediate successor** of the current one along `BRIEFED → MODELING → RIGGING → SURFACING → PUBLISHED`; skipping a step, or going backwards, is refused with `ERR_ASSET_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted step adds 1 to the asset's `version`. Reaching `PUBLISHED` is what makes an asset eligible for library nomination. A published asset can also be taken out of service by advancing it `PUBLISHED → RETIRED`; retiring an asset that has not reached `PUBLISHED` is likewise `ERR_ASSET_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. Build advancement requires the owning production's phase to be `PRE_PRODUCTION` or `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`.

Library promotion is the second dimension and it is its own submit-and-decide loop. An artist nominates a published asset, which moves `library_status` `PRIVATE → LIBRARY_CANDIDATE` and records the nominator in `library_submitted_by_user_id`; the nomination requires `build_status` to be `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_ASSET_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`) and `library_status` to be `PRIVATE` (otherwise `ERR_ASSET_LIBRARY_STATE_INVALID`), and it leaves `build_status` completely untouched. A supervisor then decides the candidate with a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`. Both branches require `library_status` to be `LIBRARY_CANDIDATE` and both require the deciding identifier to differ from `library_submitted_by_user_id`, and both record `library_decided_by_user_id`. `APPROVED` moves the asset `LIBRARY_CANDIDATE → LIBRARY_APPROVED`, which is what makes it a legal reuse source that actually grants a discount to assets derived from it. `REJECTED` moves it `LIBRARY_CANDIDATE → PRIVATE`, from where it may be nominated again — the library dimension is a loop, not a one-shot. A rejection leaves `library_submitted_by_user_id` holding the previous nominator rather than clearing it; a fresh nomination overwrites the value.

The reuse discount is computed by walking the `derived_from_asset_id` chain upward from the asset toward the root of its chain. `reuse_depth` is the number of edges on that walk, so an asset with no `derived_from_asset_id` has depth 0. The discount is zero — `reuse_discount_pct` of 0.00 — in any of three cases: the depth is 0; the asset's **immediate** source has a `library_status` other than `LIBRARY_APPROVED`; or the asset's `reuse_source_stale` flag is true. Otherwise the discount depends on depth alone: 0.40 at depth 1, 0.25 at depth 2, and 0.10 at depth 3 or deeper. `effective_complexity_points` is then `base_complexity_points` multiplied by the quantity one minus `reuse_discount_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The discount therefore shrinks as reuse gets more distant: a base of 200.00 yields 120.00 at depth 1, 150.00 at depth 2 and 180.00 at depth 3, and depth is never averaged over the chain.

These three figures are derived by walking the chain **on every read** of the asset rather than served from a stored snapshot, so a change on an ancestor reaches its descendants with no write to their rows at all: a library decision taken on an ancestor, or a repointing of an ancestor's own source, changes what a descendant's very next read reports for `reuse_depth`, `reuse_discount_pct` and `effective_complexity_points`, while that descendant's `version` and `updated_at` stay exactly where they were. The one ancestor change that does write to descendant rows is retirement, described below, because it writes the stored `reuse_source_stale` flag.

Because the chain is walked rather than cached, two guards keep the walk finite. A create or a repointing whose resulting chain would revisit an asset already on the walk is refused with `ERR_ASSET_REUSE_CYCLE`, and a chain longer than eight edges is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Repointing is a production manager's operation: it supplies a new `derived_from_asset_id`, which is mandatory on this call, and recomputes `reuse_depth`, `reuse_discount_pct`, `effective_complexity_points` and `reuse_source_stale` from the new chain, writing no row but the repointed one. `reuse_source_stale` becomes true exactly when some asset on the new ancestor chain carries `library_status` `LIBRARY_DEPRECATED`, and false otherwise, so a repointing away from a deprecated ancestor clears the flag as readily as a repointing toward one sets it. The new source need not itself be `LIBRARY_APPROVED` — an unapproved source is accepted and simply grants a discount of 0.00.

Retirement is where the two dimensions meet, and it is the widest cascade in the asset aggregate. When an asset advances `PUBLISHED → RETIRED` and its `library_status` is `LIBRARY_APPROVED` at that moment, the library dimension follows it down: `library_status` moves `LIBRARY_APPROVED → LIBRARY_DEPRECATED`. That deprecation then sets `reuse_source_stale` to true on **every** asset whose `derived_from_asset_id` chain contains the retired asset — not merely its immediate children — and those descendants are walked in `created_at` ascending order. A descendant whose `reuse_source_stale` was already true is left exactly as it stands, because the flag has nothing to flip. Each descendant on which the flag does flip recomputes its reuse credit under the rule above: because `reuse_source_stale` is now true, its `reuse_discount_pct` collapses to 0.00 and its `effective_complexity_points` returns to its full `base_complexity_points`. The retired asset and every descendant whose flag actually flipped each have their `version` advanced by 1 and their `updated_at` refreshed; a descendant that was already stale has neither. The asset collection read can be filtered by `production_id`, by `derived_from_asset_id`, or by either state dimension independently using `build_status` and `library_status`.

### 3.3. Shots, the Five Stages, and the Retake Loop

A shot is the unit the studio schedules, budgets and measures. Creating one takes the owning `production_id`, a `shot_code` unique within that production, a `sequence_code` grouping label such as `010` — any non-empty string, with no numeric or length format enforced — a `frame_count` of at least 1, a `budget_cents` that may not be negative and is the shot's budget at completion, and the planned schedule pair `planned_start_at` and `planned_finish_at`, where the finish must be strictly after the start. Two fields are optional: `difficulty_factor`, a planning multiplier that defaults to 1.0, carries two decimal places and must lie between 0.5 and 3.0 inclusive; and `stage_budget_hours`, a map of planned hours keyed by stage. `difficulty_factor` is recorded for planning purposes and does not enter any of the derived cost or earned-value figures specified in this document.

Creating a shot **materializes its five stage tasks immediately**, one per stage, in the fixed order `LAYOUT`, `ANIMATION`, `EFFECTS`, `LIGHTING`, `COMP`. Each task is stamped with the owning `shot_id`, its `stage` — which occurs exactly once per `shot_id` — a `stage_order` of 1 through 5 following that same order, and its `stage_weight_pct` — 0.10 for `LAYOUT`, 0.40 for `ANIMATION`, 0.15 for `EFFECTS`, 0.15 for `LIGHTING` and 0.20 for `COMP`, which together sum to exactly 1.00. Every task opens at `status` `NOT_STARTED`, with `actual_hours` 0.00, `revision_count` 0, `escalated` false, a null `outsource_package_id`, and null `assigned_artist_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `approved_at`. Each task's `budgeted_hours` is taken from the supplied `stage_budget_hours` entry for its stage, and is 10.0 when no entry is supplied; the value must be strictly greater than zero and carries two decimal places. `stage_budget_hours`, when supplied, must be a JSON object or the create is `ERR_VALIDATION`; keys that do not name one of the five stages are ignored exactly as any other unknown field is, and a stage whose entry is an explicit `null` counts as not supplied and takes the 10.0 default. Like the pre-production gates, these five rows appear in no creation response: they are discoverable only through the stage-task collection read, filtered by `shot_id` and optionally by `stage`, `status` or `outsource_package_id`.

A newly created shot has `status` `BLOCKED_PREPROD` while its production is still in `PRE_PRODUCTION`, and `READY` if the production has already reached `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`. The shot's `status` is never written by a client. The derivation runs as an ordered cascade. It is `BLOCKED_PREPROD` whenever the owning production is still in `PRE_PRODUCTION`, and that phase test is taken **first** and overrides everything the tasks say, so a shot of a pre-production show reads `BLOCKED_PREPROD` whatever its five tasks hold. Otherwise it is `COMPLETE` at the instant all five tasks are `APPROVED`; failing that it is `IN_PRODUCTION` as soon as any one of the five has left `NOT_STARTED` — which includes a task sitting in `APPROVED` while a later sibling has not yet been assigned, as well as the `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` and `RETAKE` cases, and reaching any of those requires the production to be in `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or `DELIVERY`; and it is `READY` only while all five are still `NOT_STARTED`. It drops back `COMPLETE → IN_PRODUCTION` the moment any approved task is driven back to `RETAKE`. A shot therefore has **no terminal state** — a director note raised against an approved compositing task can reopen a shot that was reported finished. Each of these four recomputations adds 1 to the shot's `version`, and reaching or leaving `COMPLETE` recomputes the production's `completed_shot_count` upward or downward respectively. Alongside `status`, `current_stage` always names the stage of the lowest-`stage_order` task of the shot whose status is not `APPROVED`, and is null once the shot is `COMPLETE`. Creating a shot recomputes the production's `shot_count`; the shot collection read may be filtered by `production_id` and by `status`.

Stage work begins when a production manager assigns an artist, supplying `assigned_artist_user_id` — any non-empty string, never checked against any stored row — and optionally a replacement `budgeted_hours` that must itself be strictly greater than zero, carries two decimal places, and overwrites the seeded value. Assignment drives the task `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS`, sets the shot's `current_stage` to this task's stage, and recomputes the shot `READY → IN_PRODUCTION`, adding 1 to the task's `version`. It requires the production to be `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`, it requires the task to still be `NOT_STARTED` (otherwise `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`), and for any task whose `stage_order` is above 1 it requires the sibling task one order lower to be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PREVIOUS_STAGE_NOT_APPROVED`. Stages of a shot are thus worked strictly in order.

While a task is in flight, the assigned artist — or the vendor representative when the task is inside an outsourcing package — records worked time by supplying `hours`, a number strictly greater than zero that is quantized to two decimals with round-half-up on ingest and then added to the task's `actual_hours`. Hour logging is the one action endpoint with **no** precondition on its target's own status: ownership and the `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` phase are its only gates, so hours may legitimately be logged against a task that is `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `RETAKE` or even already `APPROVED`, and no `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` is ever raised here. A `NOT_STARTED` task is nevertheless unreachable in practice, because it carries no `assigned_artist_user_id` and no package link and therefore fails the ownership check with `ERR_NOT_TASK_OWNER`. Logging hours immediately recomputes the shot's `labor_cost_cents` and `acwp_cents` and the production's `direct_committed_cents`, `overhead_fee_cents`, `total_committed_cents` and `available_cents`.

Handing work in drives the task `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED`, records `submitted_by_user_id` and stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock; an optional `hours` field on the same call — again strictly greater than zero and quantized to two decimals — is added to `actual_hours` before the transition is recorded. The same operation also closes the retake loop, moving a task `RETAKE → SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` overwritten and `submitted_at` refreshed. Neither form resets `revision_count`, and `escalated` stays true once it has been set. Submission requires the production to be `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` and the caller to own the task, and when the task carries an `outsource_package_id` it additionally requires that package to be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`. Only `IN_PROGRESS` and `RETAKE` may be submitted: a task that is `NOT_STARTED`, already `SUBMITTED`, already `IN_REVIEW` or already `APPROVED` is `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`. The task's own state is examined before the package's, so a `NOT_STARTED` task linked to a package that is not `IN_PROGRESS` reports `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`.

A supervisor closes the loop with a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `RETAKE` plus an optional `note`, which must be a string when supplied but is not persisted anywhere — the stage-task row carries no note field. Either branch requires the task to be `SUBMITTED` or `IN_REVIEW` — anything else is `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` — and either branch requires the deciding identifier to differ from the task's `submitted_by_user_id`. `APPROVED` drives the task to `APPROVED` from either of those two states, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock, records `reviewed_by_user_id`, and recomputes the shot's `bcwp_cents` upward because this stage's weight is now earned, along with the shot's `cpi`, `spi` and `status` and the production's rollups; if all five tasks are now `APPROVED` the shot becomes `COMPLETE` and the production's `completed_shot_count` is recomputed. Approving a task does **not** resolve the director notes standing against it — they remain `OPEN` or `RETAKE_ISSUED` until someone resolves them explicitly. `RETAKE` drives the task to `RETAKE` from either `SUBMITTED` or `IN_REVIEW`, and the two source states behave identically; it records `reviewed_by_user_id` exactly as an approval does — the deciding supervisor is stamped on both branches — but it stamps no `approved_at` and instead clears any `approved_at` the task was carrying.

Every entry into `RETAKE`, from whatever source, runs the same escalation arithmetic. The task's `revision_count` is increased by 1, and `escalated` is set to true when `revision_count` has reached **three or more** — the comparison is inclusive, so the third retake escalates the task and the second does not. Once true, `escalated` latches: a later approval never clears it, and `revision_count` never resets. When `escalated` flips to true, the owning shot's `escalation_flag` becomes true and the production's `at_risk_shot_count` is recomputed. Independently of escalation, every retake recomputes the shot's `retake_count` as the sum of `revision_count` over its five tasks, and the production's `total_retake_count` as the sum of `retake_count` over its shots. Tasks belonging to other shots never influence this shot's flag or counters.

Two paths into `RETAKE` are driven by the system rather than by a supervisor, and both can reach a task that was already `APPROVED`. A major or blocking director note issued when its review session closes, and an outsourcing package that fails QC or is recalled, each drive their target task `APPROVED → RETAKE`. That transition clears `approved_at`, adds 1 to `revision_count`, recomputes the shot's `bcwp_cents` **downward** because the stage has lost its earned credit, and recomputes the shot `COMPLETE → IN_PRODUCTION` and the production's `completed_shot_count` downward. This is what makes `APPROVED` a non-terminal state for a stage task. One more system-driven edge exists: creating a director note against a task that is `SUBMITTED` moves it `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW` without changing any counter, while a target in any other status — `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `RETAKE`, already `IN_REVIEW` or already `APPROVED` — keeps the status it holds when notes are raised against it, and no status precondition on the target task guards note creation at all.

### 3.4. Screening Rooms, Review Sessions and Director Notes

Screening rooms are studio configuration, registered by a production manager with a `name`, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1, and a `room_color_space`. Two optional fields tune the room's behaviour: `turnover_buffer_minutes`, the mandatory idle time between two sessions in the room, which defaults to 30 minutes and may not be negative; and `calibration_valid_days`, the number of days a projector calibration stays good for, which defaults to 30 and must be at least 1. A freshly registered room has a null `last_calibrated_at` and therefore a `calibration_status` of `UNCALIBRATED`, and it cannot host anything until it is calibrated.

Calibration is a bare action with no payload: it stamps `last_calibrated_at` with the logical clock now and restarts the validity window, and it always advances the room's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, even when a repeat calibration taken at an unmoved clock rewrites the very same instant. It carries no phase gate and no state precondition of any kind, so a room may be recalibrated any number of times. `calibration_status` itself is never stored as an independent decision — it is derived on every read from three things: it is `UNCALIBRATED` when `last_calibrated_at` is null; it is `EXPIRED` when the age of the calibration in whole days is strictly greater than `calibration_valid_days`; and it is `CALIBRATED` otherwise. That age is the elapsed time from `last_calibrated_at` to the logical clock now with any fractional day discarded, so a room whose age lands exactly on `calibration_valid_days` still reads `CALIBRATED` and only tips to `EXPIRED` a full day later; and when the clock has been driven back to an instant before `last_calibrated_at` the age is negative, which is likewise not greater than `calibration_valid_days`, so the room reads `CALIBRATED`. Because the derivation reads the logical clock, the same room can read `CALIBRATED` before the clock is moved and `EXPIRED` afterwards with no write in between. The room collection read may be filtered by `calibration_status`.

Booking a review session is the director's call. The payload names the `production_id` under review, the `screening_room_id` to occupy, a `session_type` of `DAILIES`, `DIRECTOR_REVIEW` or `CLIENT_REVIEW`, the occupation interval `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive, and strictly after the start), and an `attendee_count` of at least 1 which defaults to 1. Booking requires the production to be `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE`, and four further checks stand between the request and a `SCHEDULED` session — evaluated in the §2.10 order, which is the `CLIENT_REVIEW` color check first, then the calibration check, then the window check, then the room-turnover check of the next paragraph, and the seat-capacity check last, rather than in the order they happen to be listed here. The window must be sane and must not start before the logical clock now, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The room must have been calibrated at least once, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_NOT_CALIBRATED`. A `CLIENT_REVIEW` booking additionally requires the room's `room_color_space` to be among the production's `approved_color_spaces`, otherwise `ERR_COLOR_PIPELINE_MISMATCH`. And `attendee_count` may not exceed the room's `seat_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A booked session opens at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `note_count` and `retake_issued_count` both 0 and null `opened_at` and `closed_at`.

Room turnover is enforced asymmetrically and the asymmetry is deliberate. The **requested** window is expanded by `turnover_buffer_minutes` at both ends, and that expanded interval is tested against the **stored, unexpanded** windows of every `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` session already booked in the same room. All intervals are half-open, so a session that ends exactly when another begins does not overlap. Consequently a gap between two sessions exactly equal to `turnover_buffer_minutes` is legal, and any smaller gap conflicts and is refused with `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Sessions that are `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored entirely by this test, which is why cancelling a session frees the room. The session collection read may be filtered by `production_id`, `screening_room_id`, `session_type` and `status`.

Opening a session moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `opened_at`. It requires the session to be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`), the production to be `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or `DELIVERY`, and — evaluated at the moment of the call, not at booking time — the room's `calibration_status` to be exactly `CALIBRATED`, otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`. A room can therefore pass the booking check in the morning and fail the opening check in the afternoon.

While a session is `IN_PROGRESS`, and only then, the director raises notes against it. A note names its `review_session_id`, the `shot_stage_task_id` it is tracked against, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `BLOCKING`, and a `note_text` of between 1 and 2000 characters. Raising a note against a session that is not `IN_PROGRESS` is `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`, and naming a target task that belongs to a shot of a different production than the session's is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE`. A new note opens at `status` `OPEN` with `raised_by_user_id` set to the director and `resolved_by_user_id` null, recomputes the session's `note_count` — which is the count of every note ever raised against that session whatever its current status, so resolving or waiving a note never brings it back down — and — as noted above — moves a `SUBMITTED` target task to `IN_REVIEW`. The note collection read may be filtered by `review_session_id`, `shot_stage_task_id`, `severity` and `status`.

**Closing a session is where notes become work.** The close is legal while the session's production is in any phase other than `WRAPPED`, requires the session to be `IN_PROGRESS` (otherwise `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`), moves it `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED`, and stamps `closed_at`. It then processes the session's notes in `created_at` ascending order. Each note that is still `OPEN` and whose `severity` is `MAJOR` or `BLOCKING` moves `OPEN → RETAKE_ISSUED`; each such note whose target stage task is in `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` additionally drives that task to `RETAKE` with `revision_count` increased by 1. Notes whose `severity` is `MINOR` stay `OPEN` and issue no retake at all. Notes whose target task is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or already `RETAKE` also stay `OPEN` and issue no retake — there is nothing to send back. The session's `retake_issued_count` is set to the number of notes actually moved to `RETAKE_ISSUED`. Every shot touched by those retakes recomputes its `retake_count`, `escalation_flag` and `status`, and the production recomputes `total_retake_count`, `at_risk_shot_count` and `completed_shot_count`.

Cancelling a session is the compensating alternative and behaves identically from `SCHEDULED` and from `IN_PROGRESS`. It too is legal in any phase other than `WRAPPED`. It accepts an optional `reason`, which must be a string when supplied but is not persisted anywhere, moves the session to `CANCELLED`, stamps `closed_at`, and moves **every** note of the session that is still `OPEN` to `WAIVED` — regardless of severity, and issuing no retake whatsoever. A note that had already reached `RETAKE_ISSUED` is left exactly as it stands, and the session's `retake_issued_count` is not disturbed. Notes waived this way have a null `resolved_by_user_id`, because no operator decided them individually. The cancelled session's window stops participating in room overlap checks, freeing the room for other bookings. Cancelling a session that is already `CLOSED` is `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`.

Notes are finally cleared one at a time with a `resolution` of `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED`. The call is legal while the production behind the note's session is in any phase other than `WRAPPED`. Both branches require the note to be in `OPEN` or `RETAKE_ISSUED`; a note that is already `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_NOTE_STATE_INVALID`. `RESOLVED` is the stricter branch: it is permitted only when the note's target stage task has reached `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`, and it records `resolved_by_user_id`. `WAIVED` is permitted from either state without any condition on the target task, records `resolved_by_user_id`, and — importantly — **does not revert a retake that has already been issued**: waiving the note does not restore the task, and no counter on the shot or the production changes. When the note being cleared was the last `BLOCKING` note of its scope still in `OPEN` or `RETAKE_ISSUED`, deliverable submission for that scope becomes possible again.

### 3.5. Render Farm Capacity, Priority Scheduling and Per-Frame Cost

A render pool is registered by a production manager with a `name`, a `total_cores` count of at least 1, and a `cost_per_core_hour_cents` billing rate of at least 1 cent. A new pool opens with `committed_core_hours` of 0.00, a `reserved_job_count` of 0, and a `version` of 1. Both computed figures are derived from the pool's reservations: `committed_core_hours` is the sum of `committed_core_hours` over the pool's jobs whose `status` is `RESERVED`, carried to two decimal places, and `reserved_job_count` is the count of those same jobs. The pool read is what a client uses to learn the `version` it must echo back as `expected_render_pool_version` when reserving.

Reserving capacity names the target `render_pool_id`, the `shot_stage_task_id` whose frames are to be rendered, a `priority` of `LOW`, `NORMAL`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` which defaults to `NORMAL`, a `requested_cores` count of at least 1 that is held for the whole window, the half-open reservation interval `window_start` and `window_end` (the end strictly after the start, the start not before the logical clock now), a `frame_count` of at least 1, a `seconds_per_frame` estimate strictly greater than zero carried to two decimal places — quantized to that precision with round-half-up on ingest rather than refused for carrying more, exactly as `actual_seconds_per_frame` is at settlement — and the mandatory `expected_render_pool_version`, which must arrive as a JSON integer. The referenced stage task must be in `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `RETAKE`, otherwise `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`; a task that is `NOT_STARTED` or `APPROVED` cannot hold farm capacity.

Admission is decided in three steps and is **all or nothing** — a request is never partially satisfied. First the pool's usable ceiling is established, and the headroom the studio keeps free is deliberately **not** linear in priority: the reserved headroom fraction is 0.20 for a `LOW` request, 0.10 for `NORMAL`, and 0.00 for both `HIGH` and `CRITICAL`. The headroom core count is `total_cores` multiplied by that fraction and rounded **up** to a whole core — always up, never rounded to nearest and never down — and the usable ceiling is `total_cores` minus that headroom. A hundred-core pool therefore keeps 10 cores free from a `NORMAL` request and 20 from a `LOW` one, while a `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` request may use all 100.

Second, the peak concurrent commitment over the requested window is measured. The candidate set is every job of the same pool whose `status` is `RESERVED` and whose window genuinely intersects the request: the stored job's `window_start` is strictly before the request's `window_end` **and** the stored job's `window_end` is strictly after the request's `window_start`. A job that merely touches the request — one whose `window_end` equals the request's `window_start` — does not intersect, and jobs that are `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED` or `PREEMPTED` are excluded entirely. The instants examined are the request's own `window_start` together with the `window_start` of each candidate job that falls at or after the request's `window_start`. At each such instant the system sums the `requested_cores` of every candidate job whose interval covers that instant — that is, whose `window_start` is at or before the instant and whose `window_end` is strictly after it — and the peak usage is the largest of those sums. When no candidate job intersects at all, peak usage is 0.

Third, available capacity is the usable ceiling minus that peak usage. If `requested_cores` is at most the available capacity, the job is admitted. If not, and the request's `priority` is anything other than `CRITICAL`, the reservation is refused with `ERR_RENDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Only a `CRITICAL` request gets a second chance through preemption.

The preemption selector is fully pinned so that two runs on the same data always choose the same victims, and no row identifier is ever consulted. Eligible victims are the pool's jobs that are `RESERVED`, carry a `priority` of `LOW` or `NORMAL`, and intersect the requested window under the same half-open test. They are ranked by `priority` ascending on the scale where `LOW` comes before `NORMAL`, then by `requested_cores` descending, then by `created_at` ascending. The system walks that ranked list, preempting one job at a time and recomputing peak usage after each, and stops as soon as `requested_cores` fits within the available capacity. If the eligible set is empty, or if the request still does not fit after the entire list has been walked, then **nothing is preempted at all** and the reservation is refused with `ERR_RENDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the admission is atomic, and there is no such thing as a partial preemption that leaves victims dead and the request rejected.

A preempted job moves `RESERVED → PREEMPTED` and records the admitting critical job in its `preempted_by_render_job_id`. Its commitment is released, so the pool's `committed_core_hours` and `reserved_job_count` are recomputed downward; its `estimated_cost_cents` stops counting toward the shot's `render_cost_committed_cents`, so that field and the production's `render_committed_cents` both decrease; and its `final_cost_cents`, its `frames_completed` and its `actual_seconds_per_frame` all stay null, because a preempted job is never billed, while the `cost_per_frame_cents` it was quoted at reservation time is left in place rather than cleared. A victim belonging to a different shot, or even to a different production, is reconciled through the same chain. The job collection read can be filtered by `render_pool_id`, `shot_stage_task_id`, `preempted_by_render_job_id`, `priority` and `status`, which is how a caller discovers what a critical admission displaced.

Cost is computed in a **normative step order**, and getting the order wrong produces an observably different answer. For a given job, the core-seconds per frame is the job's `requested_cores` multiplied by the applicable seconds-per-frame figure. The raw per-frame cost in cents is the pool's `cost_per_core_hour_cents`, multiplied by those core-seconds per frame, divided by the 3,600 seconds in an hour, and then multiplied by the priority multiplier — 0.80 for `LOW`, 1.00 for `NORMAL`, 1.35 for `HIGH` and 1.75 for `CRITICAL`. **Step one** rounds that raw per-frame value half-up to a whole cent and stores it as `cost_per_frame_cents`. **Step two** multiplies that already-rounded per-frame figure by the applicable frame count. Summing unrounded per-frame values and rounding only the total is wrong: three cores at 175 cents per core-hour, 50 seconds per frame at `NORMAL` priority over 120 frames yields 7 cents per frame and therefore 840 cents in total, not the 875 that rounding at the end would produce.

At reservation time the seconds figure is the supplied `seconds_per_frame` and the frame count is `frame_count`, and the product is stored as `estimated_cost_cents`, fixed at that moment and never re-estimated. The job's `committed_core_hours` is its `requested_cores` multiplied by the length of its window expressed in hours, carried to two decimal places. Accepting the reservation sets `status` to `RESERVED`, adds 1 to the pool's `version`, recomputes the pool's `committed_core_hours` and `reserved_job_count`, and then recomputes the shot's `render_cost_committed_cents` followed by the production's `render_committed_cents` — in that order, so the ledger above always agrees with the ledger below. When a critical admission requires preemption, the selected victims are released **before** the incoming job is admitted, and the pool's `version` still advances by exactly 1 for the request as a whole rather than once per released victim and once again for the admission. `actual_seconds_per_frame`, `frames_completed` and `final_cost_cents` all stay null while the job is `RESERVED`.

Settling a reservation is legal while the production behind the job's stage task is in any phase other than `WRAPPED`, requires the job to still be `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_RENDER_JOB_STATE_INVALID`, and takes three fields: an `outcome` of `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`, the observed `actual_seconds_per_frame`, and the `frames_completed` count. The outcome and the frame count must agree: `COMPLETED` requires `frames_completed` to equal the job's `frame_count`, and `FAILED` requires it to be strictly less; a mismatch either way is `ERR_VALIDATION`. That cross-field agreement is evaluated **after** the phase and job-state checks rather than with the rest of the schema — as are the package QC score agreement of §3.6 and the failed-deliverable note requirement of §3.8 — so a job that is no longer `RESERVED` reports `ERR_RENDER_JOB_STATE_INVALID` even when its `frames_completed` also contradicts its `outcome`. Settlement stores `actual_seconds_per_frame`, recomputes `cost_per_frame_cents` from the actual seconds under exactly the same two-step rounding rule, and sets `final_cost_cents` to that per-frame figure multiplied by `frames_completed` — so a failed job is billed for the frames it actually produced, a partial settlement rather than a write-off. Both outcomes release the committed core hours from the pool, add 1 to the pool's `version`, and reconcile the same ledger chain: the shot's `render_cost_actual_cents`, `acwp_cents` and `cpi`, and the production's `render_actual_cents`, `acwp_cents` and `cpi`. Only `COMPLETED` and `FAILED` jobs contribute to `render_cost_actual_cents`; `RESERVED` jobs sit in `render_cost_committed_cents` and never touch actual cost.

Releasing a reservation before it runs is the compensating operation, takes no payload, and is legal only from `RESERVED` and, like settlement, only while the production is in some phase other than `WRAPPED`. It moves the job `RESERVED → CANCELLED`, leaves `final_cost_cents` null and sets `frames_completed` to 0 — the one place the two release paths differ, since a preempted job's `frames_completed` stays null — adds 1 to the pool's `version`, and restores the pool's `committed_core_hours` and `reserved_job_count`, the shot's `render_cost_committed_cents` and the production's `render_committed_cents` to **exactly** their pre-reservation values. Cancelled and preempted jobs are never priced: neither ever carries a `final_cost_cents` or an `actual_seconds_per_frame`, though each keeps the `cost_per_frame_cents` it was quoted at reservation time.

Finally, every reservation is checked against the show's render allowance before it is accepted: the production's `render_committed_cents` plus its `render_actual_cents` plus this job's `estimated_cost_cents` must be at most the production's `render_allowance_cents`, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_RENDER_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Because that check runs after admission has been *planned* but before anything at all is written, a `CRITICAL` request that would only have fitted through preemption and is then refused here leaves every candidate victim untouched and still `RESERVED`: no job is ever preempted by a reservation that does not itself succeed.

### 3.6. Outsourcing Partners, Packages and Vendor QC

Vendors are onboarded by the outsourcing coordinator with a `legal_name` — any non-empty string, with no length ceiling or character class enforced — and a `security_certification_level` of `NONE`, `TIER1`, `TIER2` or `TIER3` — an ordered scale on which `NONE` is the lowest and `TIER3` the highest — which defaults to `NONE`. Two security flags are optional: `mfa_enforced`, which declares that the partner enforces multi-factor authentication across its pipeline and defaults to false, and `watermark_required`, which declares that deliveries from this partner must carry a forensic watermark and defaults to **true**. A new partner opens at `status` `ONBOARDING`, with `qc_decided_package_count` at 0 and both quality figures — `quality_score_rolling` and `quality_score_lifetime` — seeded at the neutral default of 50.00. The partner collection read may be filtered by `status`.

Partner lifecycle changes are requested by supplying the target `status`, and exactly three transitions exist. `ONBOARDING → APPROVED` requires the partner's `security_certification_level` to be something other than `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`; once approved, packages may be issued to the partner. `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` is the enforcement action. `SUSPENDED → APPROVED` is reinstatement. Any other requested move — for example `ONBOARDING → SUSPENDED`, or approving a partner that is already `APPROVED` — is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_STATE_INVALID`.

Suspension is the widest cascade in the vendor aggregate, and reinstatement is deliberately **not** its inverse. On suspension, every package of that partner whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `DELIVERED` is processed in `created_at` ascending order and moved to `RECALLED`. Each recalled package has its `fee_committed_cents` reset to 0, which recomputes the owning production's `direct_committed_cents`, `overhead_fee_cents`, `total_committed_cents` and `available_cents` — the show drops back to exactly the position it held before the partner accepted the work. Every stage task whose `outsource_package_id` names one of those recalled packages has that link cleared, and those tasks whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` are additionally driven to `RETAKE` with `revision_count` increased by 1; tasks in any other status — `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or already `RETAKE` — are left in place and are not retaken, though their `outsource_package_id` is still cleared and that clearing alone advances the task's `version` by 1. Affected shots recompute `retake_count`, `escalation_flag` and `status`, and the production recomputes `total_retake_count`, `at_risk_shot_count` and `completed_shot_count`. Reinstating the partner afterwards restores none of it: recalled packages stay `RECALLED`, issued retakes are not reverted, and the only effect is that new packages may be issued again.

A package is drafted with the `production_id` it draws work from, the `partner_id` it will be issued to, a `package_code` unique within that production — any non-empty string, with no length ceiling or character class enforced — a `security_tier` of `TIER1`, `TIER2` or `TIER3`, a `fee_cents` amount that may not be negative, and a `stage_task_ids` array naming the stage tasks in scope, which defaults to an empty array. Optionally it carries `quality_min_score`, the minimum rolling partner quality required at issue time, which defaults to 70.00, carries two decimal places, and must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive. A drafted package opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `fee_committed_cents` 0, `watermark_applied` false, `delivery_count` 0, and null `qc_score` and `qc_decided_at`. The stage tasks are **not** linked at draft time, and a draft may legally hold a scope that crosses production boundaries — that particular sin is caught later. Drafting itself is legal while the production is in any phase other than `WRAPPED`, and the partner's own `status` is not examined at all, so a package may be drafted for a partner still in `ONBOARDING` or already `SUSPENDED`. The package collection read may be filtered by `production_id`, `partner_id` and `status`.

Issuing the package moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED` and is where all the vendor gates fire. The partner must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. The partner's `security_certification_level` must be at least the package's `security_tier` on the ordered scale, otherwise `ERR_SECURITY_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. The partner's `quality_score_rolling` must be at least the package's `quality_min_score`, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_QUALITY_BELOW_STANDARD`. Every element of `stage_task_ids` must belong to a shot of this package's `production_id`, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE`. And the prospective budget position — the show's committed total recomputed with this package's `fee_cents` added and its overhead re-derived — must stay within the spending ceiling, otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_WOULD_EXCEED`. When all five pass, every task in `stage_task_ids` has its `outsource_package_id` set to this package — overwriting whatever link that task already carried, since nothing forbids two packages from naming the same stage task — and **no money is committed yet**: `fee_committed_cents` stays at 0 until the vendor accepts. Issuing a package that is not `DRAFT` is `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`.

Acceptance is the vendor's own action and moves the package `ISSUED → IN_PROGRESS`. It is legal while the production is in any phase other than `WRAPPED` — the show may already have advanced to `DELIVERY` since the package was issued — and it requires the caller's identifier to equal the package's `partner_id` and the partner to still be `APPROVED`. Acceptance is the money event: `fee_committed_cents` is set to `fee_cents`, and the production's `direct_committed_cents`, `overhead_fee_cents`, `total_committed_cents` and `available_cents` are recomputed.

Delivery is likewise the vendor's action and takes a single mandatory `watermark_applied` assertion — omitting it, or supplying anything that is not a JSON boolean, is `ERR_VALIDATION`, and whatever is asserted is stored on the package, true or false alike. Delivery is legal while the production is in any phase other than `WRAPPED`, and from two states: `IN_PROGRESS → DELIVERED` for a first delivery, and `QC_FAILED → DELIVERED` for a redelivery after a failed QC; from anywhere else it is `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`. Both forms require the caller's identifier to equal `partner_id`, require `watermark_applied` to be true whenever the partner's `watermark_required` is true, and require the partner's `mfa_enforced` to be true whenever the package's `security_tier` is `TIER2` or `TIER3`; any of those three failing is `ERR_SECURITY_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`. Both forms add 1 to `delivery_count`. They differ in what they do to the scoped work: a first delivery moves every scoped stage task whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` to `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` set to the vendor's identifier, while a redelivery moves every scoped stage task whose `status` is `RETAKE` to `SUBMITTED`. Both forms stamp `submitted_at` on each moved task from the logical clock, but only a first delivery writes `submitted_by_user_id`: a redelivery leaves that field holding whoever submitted the task last, which is what the subsequent stage review decision compares itself against. On a redelivery the package's `qc_score` and `qc_decided_at` retain their failed values until the next QC decision replaces them.

Vendor QC is a supervisor's fork, legal while the production is in any phase other than `WRAPPED` and taken on a `DELIVERED` package (anything else is `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`) with a `decision` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and a `qc_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive. Unlike `quality_min_score`, `qc_score` carries no decimal places at all: it must arrive as a JSON integer, so a body carrying `90.00` where it means `90` is a type violation and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. It is stored and reported as that same integer. The score and the decision must agree: `PASS` requires `qc_score` to be at least the package's `quality_min_score`, and `FAIL` requires it to be strictly below, and a contradiction either way is `ERR_VALIDATION` — an agreement evaluated after the phase and package-state checks rather than with the rest of the schema, so a package that is not `DELIVERED` reports `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` even when its score also contradicts its decision. Both branches store `qc_score` and stamp `qc_decided_at` from the logical clock, add 1 to the partner's `qc_decided_package_count`, recompute both `quality_score_rolling` and `quality_score_lifetime`, and leave `fee_committed_cents` at `fee_cents` — a failed package is still paid for, and only the vendor's quality is debited. From there the two branches are **disjoint**. `PASS` moves the package `DELIVERED → QC_PASSED` and drives every scoped stage task whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `IN_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`, stamping each one's `approved_at` from the logical clock and recording the deciding supervisor in its `reviewed_by_user_id`, which lifts the shot's `bcwp_cents`, recomputes the shot's `status`, and recomputes the production's rollups. `FAIL` moves the package `DELIVERED → QC_FAILED` and drives every scoped stage task whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` **or** `APPROVED` to `RETAKE` with `revision_count` increased by 1, which recomputes the shot's `retake_count`, `escalation_flag` and `status` and the production's `total_retake_count`.

Partner quality is not a stored opinion; it is recomputed from the vendor's QC history on every read, and in two variants that can legitimately disagree. The input set is the partner's packages whose `status` is `QC_PASSED` or `QC_FAILED` and whose `qc_decided_at` is not null. Packages in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `DELIVERED` or `RECALLED` never contribute, which means a recall silently removes a package from the score base. Each contributing package has an age in whole days, obtained by taking the elapsed time from its `qc_decided_at` to the logical clock now and discarding any fractional day. Each also has a tier weight taken from its `security_tier`: 1.0 for `TIER1`, 1.5 for `TIER2` and 2.0 for `TIER3` — harder work counts for more.

The `ROLLING_90D` variant, stored in `quality_score_rolling`, applies a step decay on top of the tier weight: the full factor of 1.00 when the age is at most 30 days, 0.75 when the age is above 30 and at most 60 days, 0.50 when the age is above 60 and at most 90 days, and exclusion from the input set altogether once the age exceeds 90 days. Each package's weight is its tier weight multiplied by its decay factor. The score is the sum over the input set of each package's `qc_score` multiplied by its weight, divided by the sum of the weights, with the division performed once on the accumulated sums and the result rounded half-up to two decimal places — per-term rounding is not used, so nothing accumulates. When the input set is empty the score is the neutral default of 50.00. The `LIFETIME` variant, stored in `quality_score_lifetime`, is the same computation with **no** decay factor and **no** age cut-off: each package's weight is its tier weight alone, so every QC decision the vendor has ever received counts at full strength forever. For any partner with a decision older than 30 days the two variants therefore return different numbers, and it is the rolling variant — never the lifetime one — that the issue-time quality gate consults.

### 3.7. Earned Value at the Shot Level

Producers judge a show by three numbers per shot — what the plan said should have been spent by now, what has actually been earned, and what has actually been paid — and by the two ratios between them. This system derives all five from primitive events, recomputes them whenever a stage task is approved, retaken or logs hours, or whenever a render job of the shot settles, and additionally exposes them through a dedicated computed read on the shot that reports `shot_id`, the `variant` used, `bcws_cents`, `bcwp_cents`, `acwp_cents`, `cpi`, `spi` and a `computed_at` stamp taken from the logical clock. That read accepts an optional `variant` query parameter; omitting it, or supplying it as an empty string, selects the default variant described below, while a `variant` naming neither `STAGE_WEIGHT` nor `HOURS_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — unlike the filter parameters of §2.3, this one is validated against its enum rather than matched literally. The eight keys listed above are the whole of that read's `data`, and it reports no other field of the shot.

The **planned value**, `bcws_cents`, is purely schedule-driven and identical under both variants. The shot's planned span is the elapsed time from its `planned_start_at` to its `planned_finish_at`, which is guaranteed positive because the create rejects a non-positive span. The elapsed portion is the time from `planned_start_at` to the logical clock now. The schedule fraction is the elapsed portion divided by the span, then clamped so that it is never below 0.0 and never above 1.0 — a shot whose planned window has not opened yet has fraction 0.0, and one whose planned window has closed has fraction 1.0. `bcws_cents` is the shot's `budget_cents` multiplied by that clamped fraction, rounded half-up to a whole cent. Because it reads the clock, it is recomputed on every read of the shot.

The **actual cost**, `acwp_cents`, is also identical under both variants. The shot's logged hours are the sum of `actual_hours` over its five stage tasks, regardless of each task's status. Those hours are multiplied by the studio labor rate of 9,000 cents per hour and the product rounded half-up to a whole cent, which is exactly the value stored as the shot's `labor_cost_cents`. `acwp_cents` is that labor figure plus the shot's `render_cost_actual_cents`. Render jobs reach actual cost through that field alone, which is to say only through jobs that have reached `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`; a `RESERVED` job sits in `render_cost_committed_cents` and contributes nothing to `acwp_cents`. Note that the shot's labor figure rounds the summed hours once, whereas the production-level ledger of §2.8 rounds each task's labor separately before summing — the two are computed for different purposes and the difference is intentional.

The **earned value**, `bcwp_cents`, is the one figure that differs between the two variants, and for identical data the two return different `bcwp_cents`, `cpi` and `spi`.

The `STAGE_WEIGHT` variant is the default, and it is the variant persisted into the shot's own `bcwp_cents`, `cpi` and `spi` fields and rolled up into the production. Under it, credit is earned only in discrete stage-sized jumps: the system sums `stage_weight_pct` over exactly those stage tasks of the shot whose `status` is `APPROVED`, multiplies the shot's `budget_cents` by that sum, and rounds half-up to a whole cent. A stage that is nine-tenths worked but not yet approved earns nothing at all, and a stage driven back to `RETAKE` loses its credit immediately.

The `HOURS_PROGRESS` variant is requested explicitly through the `variant` parameter and is the supervisor's in-flight view. Under it, the system divides the sum of `actual_hours` over the shot's five tasks by the sum of `budgeted_hours` over the same five tasks, clamps that ratio so it is never below 0.0 and never above 1.0, multiplies the shot's `budget_cents` by it, and rounds half-up to a whole cent. This variant credits partial work on unapproved stages, which is precisely why it is never persisted and never rolled up.

Both indices follow the same rule under either variant. The cost performance index `cpi` is `bcwp_cents` divided by `acwp_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and is **null** when `acwp_cents` is 0. The schedule performance index `spi` is `bcwp_cents` divided by `bcws_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and is **null** when `bcws_cents` is 0. At the production level the same five figures are simple aggregates over the show's shots: `bcwp_cents`, `bcws_cents` and `acwp_cents` are the sums of the shots' corresponding fields — always the persisted `STAGE_WEIGHT` figures — and the production's `cpi` and `spi` are derived from those sums under exactly the same four-decimal rounding and the same null-on-zero-denominator rules. The production's `bcws_cents`, like the shot's, is recomputed on every read because it moves with the clock.

### 3.8. Final Delivery: Versioning, Color and Wrap

A deliverable is a piece of media the studio hands over, and it may be scoped either to the whole show or to a single shot. Creating one names the owning `production_id`, a `deliverable_type` of `MASTER_EXR`, `PROXY_MP4`, `AUDIO_STEM` or `SHOT_PLATE`, the media's `color_space`, its `resolution` as pixel dimensions of the form three-to-five digits, the lowercase letter x, then three-to-five digits — `3840x2160`, for example, while an uppercase `3840X2160` is `ERR_VALIDATION` — and the mandatory `expected_production_version`, which must arrive as a JSON integer. Three fields are optional: `shot_id`, which scopes the deliverable to one shot and whose absence makes the deliverable production-wide; `frame_rate`, which defaults to 24.0 and must be one of 23.976, 24.0, 25.0, 29.97 or 30.0; and `bit_depth`, the bits per channel, which defaults to 16 and must be one of 8, 10, 12 or 16. Creation requires the production to be in `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or `DELIVERY`, and a `shot_id` naming a shot of a different production is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCTION_REFERENCE`.

Versioning is per **group**, and the grouping key is the combination of `production_id`, `deliverable_type` and `shot_id`, where a null `shot_id` matches only other rows with a null `shot_id`. A production-wide master and a shot-scoped master of the same show are therefore two separate groups with independent numbering. A new deliverable's `version_index` is one more than the number of rows already in its group, and **every** row counts toward that number regardless of status — including rows that are `SUPERSEDED` or `QC_FAILED` — so the index never decreases and never reuses a value. `version_label` is the letter `v` followed by the index padded with leading zeros to at least two digits: the first deliverable of an empty group is `v01`, the second is `v02`, and the hundredth is `v100`. It is never `v1` and never `v00`.

Only one version of a group may be in flight at a time. If any row of the target group is already in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, the create is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_VERSION_CONFLICT`. Otherwise the new row is stored at `status` `DRAFT` with a null `qc_note` and a null `delivered_at`, every row of the group that is `QC_PASSED` is moved `QC_PASSED → SUPERSEDED`, and the production's `version` is advanced by 1. A superseded deliverable can never be delivered, and it no longer blocks the show from being wrapped.

Submitting a drafted deliverable moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and adds 1 to its `version`; submitting anything that is not `DRAFT` is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. Two creative gates guard the submission. First, no director note with `severity` `BLOCKING` may still be `OPEN` or `RETAKE_ISSUED` within the deliverable's scope — that scope is the single shot when `shot_id` is set, and every shot of the production otherwise — or the submission is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_NOTES_OPEN`. Second, a `MASTER_EXR` submission additionally requires that at least one review session of the production with `session_type` `CLIENT_REVIEW` has reached `status` `CLOSED`; a client review that was cancelled does not count, and the absence of a closed one is `ERR_CLIENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

Deliverable QC is a supervisor's fork on a `SUBMITTED` row, taken with a `decision` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and an optional `qc_note`; a decision on a row in any other status is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. `PASS` moves the deliverable `SUBMITTED → QC_PASSED` and stores the `qc_note` when one was supplied. `FAIL` moves it `SUBMITTED → QC_FAILED` and **requires** `qc_note` to be present and not blank — a note of whitespace alone is refused exactly as an absent one is — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`, a requirement evaluated after the phase and deliverable-state checks rather than with the rest of the schema; because a failed row is no longer in flight, the group is freed and a fresh version may be created.

Shipping the deliverable moves it `QC_PASSED → DELIVERED` and stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock, after which the row is immutable. It is legal only from `QC_PASSED` — anything else is `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the production has reached `DELIVERY`; attempting it while the show is still `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` is `ERR_PRODUCTION_PHASE_INVALID`. Once at least one deliverable of the show is `DELIVERED` and none remains in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `QC_PASSED`, the production manager may take the show `DELIVERY → WRAPPED`, after which nothing under it is writable. The deliverable collection read may be filtered by `production_id`, `shot_id`, `deliverable_type` and `status`.
