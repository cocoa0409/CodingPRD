# Open Access Publishing Fee & Repository Deposit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university library's scholarly communications office sits at the intersection of three budgets that were never designed to talk to each other: the negotiated read-and-publish agreements that the library signs with commercial publishers, the institutional article processing charge (APC) subsidy funds that pay for everything the agreements do not cover, and the research grants whose funders impose open-access mandates with their own embargo and licensing terms. Today that reconciliation happens in spreadsheets. A corresponding author whose manuscript has just been accepted cannot find out whether the venue is covered by an agreement, whether the remaining quota on that agreement has already been spent, or whether the office will subsidise the balance — and by the time an invoice arrives from the publisher three months later, nobody remembers which discount was negotiated or whether it was actually applied.

This document specifies the API for a system that makes each of those decisions explicit, dated and auditable. Every accepted manuscript is recorded, run through a coverage determination that picks exactly one funding route, and — where institutional money is involved — carried through a reviewed request that draws on APC funds in a fixed waterfall order subject to per-author annual caps and per-fund per-article ceilings. Publisher invoices are re-priced independently by the institution and either validated or disputed. Accepted manuscripts are deposited into the institutional repository, curated, assigned a persistent identifier, and linked to the author's ORCID profile, which is what ultimately satisfies (or breaches) the funder's mandate. On top of the transactional record sits the reporting the office needs at renewal time: what was actually spent, what the agreements saved, and which publisher holds the most leverage.

Two technical constraints shape the whole surface. First, the topology is deliberately flat: every entity collection is addressed as a top-level path segment mounted directly under the API root, and the handful of nested read paths (a publisher's journals, an article's deposits) are read views only — ownership is expressed by identifiers in request bodies, never by URL nesting. Second, the system is driven by a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any request is made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Every date comparison, every `created_at` and `updated_at` value, and every event stamp such as `activated_at`, `submitted_at`, `ingested_at` or `last_screened_at` is taken from that clock, which an operator may advance explicitly. Correctness of the fiscal, embargo and deadline arithmetic therefore depends only on the clock the caller has set.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is evaluated before authorisation, and both are evaluated before anything else about the request is inspected.

The five roles are `AgreementAdmin`, `ScholCommLibrarian`, `FundManager`, `RepositoryCurator` and `CorrespondingAuthor`.

- An `AgreementAdmin` is the negotiator in the scholarly communications office. This role maintains the reference data the rest of the system depends on: publishers, journals, transformative agreements, agreement coverage lines, authors, funders and grants.
- A `ScholCommLibrarian` screens publishers for predatory-publishing signals, records acceptances and incoming publisher invoices, reviews fund requests before a funding decision is taken, publishes the version of record, and runs the periodic compliance sweep.
- A `FundManager` is the budget officer. This role opens and freezes APC funds, decides fund requests by running the allocation waterfall, and moves money to publishers by approving invoices for payment and then paying them.
- A `RepositoryCurator` works the institutional repository queue, ingesting or returning submitted manuscript deposits and minting persistent identifiers.
- A `CorrespondingAuthor` acts on their own scholarly output: recording an acceptance, requesting institutional funds, and depositing the accepted manuscript.

When `X-User-Role` is `AgreementAdmin`, `ScholCommLibrarian`, `FundManager` or `RepositoryCurator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `reviewed_by_user_id` when a `ScholCommLibrarian` takes a fund request into review, as `decided_by_user_id` when a `FundManager` decides one, or as `payment_approved_by_user_id` when a `FundManager` approves an invoice for payment — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `CorrespondingAuthor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Author`. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `Author` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: for an article, `corresponding_author_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; for a fund request, the `corresponding_author_id` of the article the request belongs to must equal `X-User-ID`; for a repository deposit, the `corresponding_author_id` of the article the deposit belongs to must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check applies when a `CorrespondingAuthor` records an accepted manuscript (where the supplied `corresponding_author_id` must be the caller's own identifier), runs an eligibility check on an article, creates a fund request, resubmits one, withdraws one, creates a repository deposit, or submits a deposit for curation. The other four roles are never subject to the ownership check.

The logical-clock endpoint is deliberately open to all five roles, because every role needs to drive time forward when exercising the system.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative states otherwise; rows created earlier appear earlier in the page. When a list endpoint responds, the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination was applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Most resources in this system are long-lived, mutable records that accumulate history, and they all carry the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix; a publisher identifier and an article identifier are indistinguishable by shape.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment the row was inserted.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row.

`Publisher`, `Journal`, `TransformativeAgreement`, `Author`, `Funder`, `Grant`, `Article`, `APCFund`, `FundRequest`, `FundAllocation`, `PublisherInvoice` and `RepositoryDeposit` all carry the full set. Two entities are append-only ledger rows that are never revised after insertion, and they therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an `AgreementCoverage` and from an `EligibilityDetermination`. A coverage line, once added to an agreement, is a fixed statement of what that agreement pays for that journal over that window; a determination, once written, is a permanent record of what the office decided on a particular date, and it is superseded by writing a further determination rather than by editing the existing one.

The logical clock is the single source of "now". It is initialised to exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and is moved only by an explicit request carrying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity — no sweep runs implicitly, no state changes — but every subsequent request observes the new instant. Where this document speaks of the "clock date", it means the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC.

### 2.5. Identifier Resolution Order and Referential Integrity

Requests are checked for the existence of everything they reference before any business rule is evaluated. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the rejection. Once every path identifier has resolved, foreign-key identifiers carried in the request body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and again the first unresolvable one determines the rejection. Identifiers supplied as list filters are resolved on the same terms.

Each entity has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_PUBLISHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOURNAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUND_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`. Because resolution precedes business logic, a request that names a non-existent article *and* violates a state rule is reported as a 404, not as a conflict.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Publishers and Articles

Two create operations in this system extend a parent record whose state may legitimately have moved since the client last looked at it, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`. In both cases the version field is a required, non-optional part of the request body — omitting it is a malformed request.

Drafting a transformative agreement requires `expected_publisher_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Publisher` named by `publisher_id`. If that value does not equal the publisher's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Nothing about a publisher's state forbids a second agreement — a new agreement legitimately extends or supersedes the negotiated position — so a repeat attempt against unchanged state would itself succeed. The guard exists because the publisher's screening verdict or its agreement portfolio may have moved under the negotiator's feet, and a negotiator must not draft against a stale picture.

Creating a repository deposit requires `expected_article_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Article` named by `article_id`. If that value does not equal the article's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again, no state precondition blocks the create once a prior deposit has reached `INGESTED` — a new deposit legitimately supersedes the previously ingested version — so the rejection is purely about the depositing client's view of the article's version lineage being out of date.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Money, Rounding and the Institutional Fiscal Year

All monetary quantities in this system are integer counts of US cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere: no allocation is split below a cent, and no remainder is redistributed after a waterfall completes. Discounts, VAT rates and the negotiation leverage ratio are the only fractional values, and they are expressed as fractions of one rather than as percentages — a fifteen percent discount is `0.15`.

Wherever a fractional multiplication must be reduced to cents, the result is rounded half up: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. The number of rounding operations and their position in an expression are pinned per calculation and are stated in the narrative that defines each calculation, because rounding each component and then summing can differ from summing and then rounding. Invoice pricing in particular rounds twice — once for the net amount and once for the tax amount — and never rounds the sum of the two.

The institution's fiscal year is labelled by the calendar year in which it ends, and it starts on 1 July. Fiscal year N therefore runs from 1 July of year N−1 through 30 June of year N, inclusive at both ends. An `APCFund` carries its own `fiscal_year` label alongside an explicit `period_from` and `period_to` pair, and both the label and the explicit period participate in fund eligibility. Invoices are attributed to a fiscal year by their `invoice_date`; allocations are attributed by the `fiscal_year` of the fund they draw on.

### 2.8. Encumbrance and Cash: the Two Utilisation Bases

The same ledger of allocation lines answers two different questions, and the system computes both from one rule with two variants. A `FundAllocation` line is `ENCUMBERED` while the money is promised, `DISBURSED` once it has actually left the institution, and `RELEASED` if the promise was cancelled. Released lines never count towards utilisation under either basis.

The rule collects allocation lines whose fund has the requested `fiscal_year`. When the rule is scoped to a single author, it keeps only lines whose fund request belongs to an article whose `corresponding_author_id` is that author; when it is scoped to the whole institution, it keeps every line for the year. Under the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` variant, utilisation is the sum of `amount_cents` over lines whose status is `ENCUMBERED` or `DISBURSED`, so money promised but not yet paid already consumes the author's allowance. Under the `CASH_BASIS` variant, utilisation is the sum of `amount_cents` over lines whose status is `DISBURSED` only, so the identical ledger reports a strictly smaller — and frequently zero — figure. In author scope, the remaining allowance is the author's `annual_cap_cents` less the utilisation, floored at zero so that it is never negative. Both sums are order-insensitive and are expressed in whole cents.

`Author.apc_committed_cents` is the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` figure for that author in the current fiscal year, and `Author.apc_disbursed_cents` is the `CASH_BASIS` figure for the same scope. Cap enforcement at funding-decision time always uses the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` variant. The annual spend report exposes the choice to the caller through its `basis` query parameter, where the value `ENCUMBRANCE` selects the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` variant and is the default when the parameter is omitted, and the value `CASH` selects the `CASH_BASIS` variant.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties in the Payment Path

Money leaves the institution in two steps performed by two different people. A `FundManager` first approves a validated invoice for payment, and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded on the invoice as `payment_approved_by_user_id`. A `FundManager` then pays the approved invoice — but the paying operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `payment_approved_by_user_id`. An attempt by the same operator to both approve and pay is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

A second, structural separation runs alongside it: review of a fund request is a `ScholCommLibrarian` responsibility and the funding decision is a `FundManager` responsibility, so `reviewed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` are necessarily different people by role construction rather than by an explicit comparison.

### 2.10. Derived Counters, Cascades and Version Bumps

A substantial share of this specification is bookkeeping that the server maintains rather than the client supplies. Whenever a fact changes, every counter derived from that fact is recomputed in the same request, and every row whose stored values change has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one. The recomputations are always full recomputations from the underlying rows, never incremental adjustments, so a derived counter can be checked against first principles at any time.

- `Publisher.active_agreement_count` is the number of that publisher's transformative agreements whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Whenever an agreement of a publisher changes `status`, this count is recomputed and `Publisher.version` is incremented by one.
- `TransformativeAgreement.articles_used` is the number of articles whose effective eligibility determination names this agreement and whose determination route is either `TA_FULL` or `TA_CAPPED`. It is recomputed on both the previously selected and the newly selected agreement whenever a determination is written.
- `APCFund.committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that fund's `ENCUMBERED` allocation lines; `APCFund.spent_cents` is the same sum over its `DISBURSED` lines; `APCFund.available_cents` is `total_budget_cents` less `committed_cents` less `spent_cents`.
- `FundRequest.approved_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that request's allocation lines whose status is `ENCUMBERED` or `DISBURSED`; `FundRequest.author_share_cents` is `requested_cents` less `approved_cents` as at decision time; `FundRequest.fully_funded` is true exactly when `approved_cents` equals `requested_cents`.
- `Article.determination_count` is the number of determinations written for that article and equals the highest `sequence_no` among them; `Article.latest_deposit_ordinal` is the highest `version_ordinal` among that article's deposits.
- `Grant.output_count` is the number of articles referencing the grant; `Grant.compliant_output_count` is the number of those whose `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT`.
- `Author.linked_output_count` is the number of that author's articles' deposits whose `status` is `INGESTED`, counted across deposits whose article has this author as corresponding author.

Cascades that span several entities are described where the triggering operation is described, and they always run to completion within the single request that triggered them. A cascade never partially applies.

### 2.11. Validation Order and Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the reported error is determined by a fixed global precedence. The rules are listed here in evaluation order, lowest number first; the first rule that fails produces the response, and no later rule is evaluated. This ordering matters: a request that both lacks permission and names a frozen fund reports the permission failure, and a request whose parent version is stale reports the stale-version failure rather than any downstream state conflict.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Existence.** Path identifiers resolve left to right, then body foreign keys resolve in the order the endpoint declares its inputs — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.5 (404). Applies to every endpoint that carries a path identifier or a foreign-key body field.
4. **Ownership.** When `X-User-Role` is `CorrespondingAuthor`, `X-User-ID` equals the target article's `corresponding_author_id`, reached directly, or through the article of a fund request, or through the article of a deposit; on article creation it must equal the supplied `corresponding_author_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to seven operations: `POST /articles`, `POST /articles/{id}/eligibility-checks`, `POST /fund-requests`, `POST /fund-requests/{id}/resubmissions`, `POST /fund-requests/{id}/withdrawal`, `POST /repository-deposits` and `POST /repository-deposits/{id}/submission`.
5. **Request shape.** Required fields are present; enum members are recognised; integer amounts are not negative; discount and VAT fractions lie inside their declared ranges; `currency` is `USD`; `capped_amount_cents` is present when and only when `coverage_type` is `CAPPED_APC`; the pagination `limit` lies in 1 through 100 and `offset` is not negative; `effective_until` is strictly after `effective_from`; `covered_until` is not before `covered_from`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Parent version.** `expected_publisher_version` equals the publisher's current `version` on `POST /transformative-agreements`, and `expected_article_version` equals the article's current `version` on `POST /repository-deposits` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to those two endpoints only.
7. **Agreement activatable.** Activation requires the agreement's `status` to be `DRAFT` and requires at least one coverage line to exist for it — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVATABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /transformative-agreements/{id}/activation`.
8. **Fund request reviewable.** Starting review requires `status` `SUBMITTED`; raising an information request and taking a decision each require `status` `UNDER_REVIEW`; withdrawal requires `status` to be one of `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (409). Applies to four operations: `POST /fund-requests/{id}/start-review`, `POST /fund-requests/{id}/info-requests`, `POST /fund-requests/{id}/decision` and `POST /fund-requests/{id}/withdrawal`.
9. **Fund request resubmittable.** Resubmission requires `status` `INFO_REQUESTED` — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /fund-requests/{id}/resubmissions`.
10. **Deposit state.** Submission requires the deposit's `status` to be `DRAFT`; a curation decision requires it to be `SUBMITTED`. Otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /repository-deposits/{id}/submission` and `POST /repository-deposits/{id}/curation-decision`.
11. **Invoice state.** Validation requires the invoice's `status` to be `RECEIVED`; payment approval requires `VALIDATED`; payment requires `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`. Otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /publisher-invoices/{id}/validation`, `POST /publisher-invoices/{id}/payment-approval` and `POST /publisher-invoices/{id}/payment`; in particular, every payment path out of `DISPUTED` fails here.
12. **Not already published.** Publication requires the article's `publication_date` to be absent — otherwise `ERR_ARTICLE_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` (409). Applies to `POST /articles/{id}/publication`.
13. **Fund open.** A manual freeze requires the fund's `status` to be `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_FUND_FROZEN` (409). Applies to `POST /apc-funds/{id}/freeze`.
14. **Segregation of duties.** The paying `FundManager`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the invoice's `payment_approved_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /publisher-invoices/{id}/payment`.
15. **Publisher not blocked.** The publisher of the article's journal has a `screening_status` other than `BLOCKED` — otherwise `ERR_PUBLISHER_BLOCKED` (422). Applies to two operations: `POST /articles/{id}/eligibility-checks` and `POST /fund-requests`.
16. **Deposit licence permitted.** The deposit's `license` satisfies the funder's `license_required`: a requirement of `CC_BY` admits only `CC_BY`; a requirement of `CC_BY_OR_NC` admits `CC_BY` or `CC_BY_NC`; a requirement of `ANY` admits every licence value. Otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). Applies to `POST /repository-deposits`.
17. **ORCID present for ingest.** Ingest requires the article's corresponding author to have a non-null `orcid`, because ingest links the deposit to the author profile — otherwise `ERR_ORCID_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /repository-deposits/{id}/curation-decision` when the decision is `INGEST`.
18. **Grant active.** When the article references a grant, that grant's `status` is `ACTIVE`; additionally, on article creation the `accepted_on` date must fall inside the closed window from `award_from` through `award_until`. Otherwise `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to three operations: `POST /articles`, `POST /articles/{id}/eligibility-checks` and `POST /fund-requests`.
19. **Eligibility determined.** An effective eligibility determination exists for the article; additionally, on fund-request creation, that determination's `eligible_request_cents` must be greater than zero. Otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED` (409). Applies to `POST /fund-requests` and `POST /publisher-invoices`. Note the asymmetry: an invoice may legitimately be raised for an author-pays article whose `eligible_request_cents` is zero, but a fund request may not.
20. **Coverage journal belongs to the agreement's publisher.** The journal named by a coverage line belongs to the same publisher as the agreement — otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_JOURNAL_PUBLISHER_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /transformative-agreements/{id}/coverages`.
21. **Article funded.** Payment approval requires the article's `funding_status` to be one of `FUNDED`, `PARTIALLY_FUNDED` or `SELF_FUNDED` — otherwise `ERR_ARTICLE_NOT_FUNDED` (409). Applies to `POST /publisher-invoices/{id}/payment-approval`.
22. **Appointment window.** `accepted_on` falls inside the corresponding author's closed appointment window, from `appointment_from` through `appointment_until` inclusive — otherwise `ERR_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /articles`.
23. **Agreement window overlap.** No other transformative agreement of the same publisher whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` has a half-open window from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive that intersects the new agreement's half-open window. Windows that merely touch — where the new agreement's `effective_from` equals another's `effective_until` — are permitted. Otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /transformative-agreements`.
24. **Coverage window conflict.** No other coverage line for the same `journal_id` has a closed window that is within one day of the new closed window. Concretely, the new line is rejected when its `covered_from` is on or before the other line's `covered_until` plus one day *and* its `covered_until` is on or after the other line's `covered_from` minus one day — so back-to-back coverage of the same journal on consecutive days is a conflict, not an adjacency. Otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /transformative-agreements/{id}/coverages`.
25. **One open deposit per article.** At most one deposit per article may have a `status` of `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, and `version_ordinal` is strictly increasing per article. Otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_VERSION_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /repository-deposits`.
26. **ISSN unique.** `issn` is unique across journals — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ISSN` (409). Applies to `POST /journals`.
27. **Agreement code unique.** `agreement_code` is unique across transformative agreements — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_CODE` (409). Applies to `POST /transformative-agreements`.
28. **ORCID unique.** `orcid` is unique among authors that have one; authors without an ORCID iD do not collide with each other. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORCID` (409). Applies to `POST /authors`.
29. **Invoice number unique per publisher.** `invoice_number` is unique within a `publisher_id`; two different publishers may use the same invoice number. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVOICE_NUMBER` (409). Applies to `POST /publisher-invoices`.
30. **Fund priority unique per year.** The pair of `fiscal_year` and `priority_rank` is unique across APC funds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_FUND_PRIORITY` (409). Applies to `POST /apc-funds`.
31. **Codes unique.** `publisher_code`, `funder_code`, `fund_code` and `grant_number` are each unique within their own entity — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). Applies to `POST /publishers`, `POST /funders`, `POST /apc-funds` and `POST /grants`.
32. **Request within eligibility.** `requested_cents` is not greater than the effective determination's `eligible_request_cents`, checked both when the request is created and when it is resubmitted — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_ELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to `POST /fund-requests` and `POST /fund-requests/{id}/resubmissions`.
33. **Author cap not exhausted.** The author's remaining annual allowance on the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` basis is greater than zero at decision time — otherwise `ERR_AUTHOR_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to `POST /fund-requests/{id}/decision` when the decision is to approve.
34. **Some fund capacity.** The allocation waterfall yields an approved amount greater than zero across the eligible open funds — otherwise `ERR_NO_FUND_CAPACITY` (409). Applies to `POST /fund-requests/{id}/decision` when the decision is to approve.

Note the relative position of rules 33 and 34: the author's cap is tested before the waterfall runs, so an author whose allowance is fully consumed is reported as a cap exhaustion even when no fund has capacity either.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Publisher Landscape and Predatory-Venue Screening

Everything begins with the counterparties. An `AgreementAdmin` registers a publisher by supplying its legal `name`, which must be between 1 and 200 characters, a short `publisher_code` of between 2 and 16 uppercase alphanumeric characters that must be unique across all publishers and that later appears inside minted DOIs and in negotiation reporting, and a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters giving the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 country of incorporation. A freshly registered publisher has not been screened: its `screening_status` is `UNSCREENED`, its `predatory_score` is absent, its `active_agreement_count` is zero, and the signal snapshot fields and `author_guidance` and `last_screened_at` are all absent.

Screening is the office's defence against predatory venues, and it is a `ScholCommLibrarian` responsibility. A screening run supplies six signals about the publisher, all of which overwrite whatever the previous run recorded — earlier runs are never averaged in. The signals are `doaj_indexed`, indicating whether the publisher's titles are indexed in the Directory of Open Access Journals; `cope_member`, indicating membership of the Committee on Publication Ethics; `editorial_board_verified`, indicating that the editorial board was confirmed to exist and to consist of real, contactable scholars; `retraction_rate_per_1000`, the number of retractions per thousand published articles; `peer_review_days_median`, the median number of days from submission to first decision; and `apc_transparency_score`, a score from 0 to 100 rating how clearly the publisher discloses its charges.

The verdict is a weighted score built from those six signals, starting at zero. Twenty-five points are added when the publisher is not indexed in DOAJ. Fifteen points are added when it is not a COPE member. Twenty points are added when the editorial board could not be verified. The retraction signal contributes the retraction rate per thousand multiplied by five and rounded half up to a whole number, but capped so that it can never contribute more than twenty-five points. Fifteen points are added when the median peer-review turnaround is strictly less than fourteen days, on the reasoning that a two-week median is implausibly fast for genuine review; a median of exactly fourteen days adds nothing. Finally, a transparency credit is subtracted: one tenth of the `apc_transparency_score`, rounded half up to a whole number. The running total is then clamped into the range from 0 to 100 inclusive — a negative total becomes zero and a total above one hundred becomes one hundred — and the clamped value is stored as `predatory_score`.

The score maps onto `screening_status` through three inclusive thresholds. A score of 85 or more is `BLOCKED`. A score of at least 60 but below 85 is `PREDATORY_FLAGGED`. A score of at least 35 but below 60 is `WATCHLIST`. Anything below 35 is `CLEARED`. Note that `UNSCREENED` is only ever the pre-screening value; a completed run always produces one of the other four. Each status pins an exact `author_guidance` string that the office shows to authors. For `CLEARED` the guidance reads "Venue cleared: institutional APC funds and agreement coverage may be used." For `WATCHLIST` it reads "Venue on watchlist: confirm indexing and peer-review claims before submitting." For `PREDATORY_FLAGGED` it reads "Venue flagged as predatory: no institutional funds; deposit the accepted manuscript instead." For `BLOCKED` it reads "Venue blocked: eligibility checks are refused; contact the scholarly communications office." The run also stamps `last_screened_at` from the logical clock and increments the publisher's `version` by one.

The two severe verdicts have teeth elsewhere in the system. A `BLOCKED` publisher refuses to be assessed at all: an eligibility check or a fund request touching one of its journals is rejected with `ERR_PUBLISHER_BLOCKED`. A `PREDATORY_FLAGGED` publisher is assessed, but the assessment always concludes that the author pays — as described in §3.4. `WATCHLIST` and `CLEARED` place no restriction on funding.

An `AgreementAdmin` registers each journal under its owner by supplying the `publisher_id`, the `issn` in the four-digit, hyphen, three-digit, final-character form (`NNNN-NNNX`) which must be unique across all journals, the `title`, the `oa_model` — one of `FULLY_OA`, `HYBRID` or `SUBSCRIPTION` — the publisher's `list_apc_cents`, which is the list article processing charge in cents and may not be negative, and optionally `required_embargo_months`, the publisher-imposed embargo on the accepted manuscript expressed in whole months, which must be between 0 and 48 inclusive and which defaults to twelve months when omitted. Read views exist to list a publisher's journals, optionally narrowed by `publisher_id` and by `oa_model`; to list a publisher's agreements, optionally narrowed by `publisher_id` and by `status`; and to list every coverage line touching a given journal across all agreements, optionally narrowed by `journal_id`.

### 3.2. Read-and-Publish Agreements, Coverage Lines and the Quota Cycle

A transformative agreement is the negotiated instrument that lets institutional authors publish open access at a covered venue without paying the list charge. An `AgreementAdmin` drafts one against a publisher by supplying `publisher_id`, a negotiated `agreement_code` that must be unique across all agreements, an `effective_from` date on which cover begins, an `effective_until` date on which cover ends, an `article_quota` of at least one giving the number of articles the agreement covers over its whole window, a `discount_pct` between 0 and 0.95 inclusive expressed with at most four decimal places, a non-empty `covered_oa_models` list drawn from `FULLY_OA` and `HYBRID` naming which journal business models the agreement may cover, and the `expected_publisher_version` that guards the write as described in §2.6. The agreement's window is half-open: `effective_from` is included and `effective_until` is excluded, and `effective_until` must be strictly after `effective_from`. A newly drafted agreement has `status` `DRAFT`, `articles_used` of zero and no `activated_at`; drafting it also increments the publisher's `version` by one. Because two agreements with the same publisher must not be in force at once, a draft whose half-open window intersects the half-open window of any other `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` agreement of that publisher is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; a draft that begins exactly on the day another agreement's window ends is a permitted hand-over rather than an overlap.

Which journals an agreement actually pays for is expressed by coverage lines added by an `AgreementAdmin`. A coverage line names a `journal_id`, a `coverage_type` of `FULL_APC`, `CAPPED_APC` or `READ_ONLY`, an optional `capped_amount_cents`, and a `covered_from` and `covered_until` pair whose window is closed at both ends. `FULL_APC` means the agreement pays the whole discounted charge for that journal. `CAPPED_APC` means the agreement pays up to a per-article ceiling, and for this type `capped_amount_cents` is required and must be greater than zero; for the other two types it must be absent. `READ_ONLY` means the agreement buys reading access only and never funds publication. The coverage window must satisfy `covered_until` on or after `covered_from`, and it must lie entirely inside the agreement's own window — on or after the agreement's `effective_from` and strictly before its `effective_until`. The journal named must belong to the same publisher as the agreement, or the line is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_JOURNAL_PUBLISHER_MISMATCH`. Coverage lines for the same journal must additionally be separated by more than one day, as spelled out in rule 24 of §2.11; a violation yields `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Activation by an `AgreementAdmin` moves the agreement from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`. It requires the agreement still to be in `DRAFT` and to have at least one coverage line; failing either condition yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the publisher's `active_agreement_count` — which rises by one, since the agreement has just become active — and increments the publisher's `version` by one.

Quota is the scarce resource, and it circulates. Each eligibility determination that selects an agreement holds one quota token on it, and the agreement's `articles_used` counts the tokens currently held. When a determination is written and the recomputed `articles_used` reaches or exceeds `article_quota`, the agreement moves itself from `ACTIVE` to `EXHAUSTED`, which decreases the publisher's `active_agreement_count` by one and increments the publisher's `version` by one. When a token is released — because a later determination for that article stopped the earlier one being effective — the recomputed `articles_used` may fall back below `article_quota`, and if the clock date is still strictly before the agreement's `effective_until` the agreement moves itself from `EXHAUSTED` back to `ACTIVE`, increasing the publisher's `active_agreement_count` by one and again incrementing the publisher's `version` by one. This `ACTIVE` → `EXHAUSTED` → `ACTIVE` cycle is the only compensating path for agreement quota; there is no manual way to hand a token back.

Expiry closes the story. During the compliance sweep, any agreement whose `effective_until` is on or before the clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, and `EXPIRED` is terminal. An agreement expiring out of `ACTIVE` decreases the publisher's `active_agreement_count` by one and increments the publisher's `version` by one. An agreement expiring out of `EXHAUSTED` changes no count, because an exhausted agreement was already not being counted as active; an agreement expiring out of `DRAFT` likewise changes no count, because a draft was never counted.

### 3.3. People, Mandates, Awards and the APC Funds

An `AgreementAdmin` registers each corresponding author with the attributes that fund eligibility depends on: a `full_name`, an institutional `email`, an optional `orcid` iD in the four-groups-of-four form (`NNNN-NNNN-NNNN-NNNX`) which must be unique across all authors that have one, a `department_code` used for recharge, an `affiliation_status` drawn from `ELIGIBLE_FACULTY`, `ELIGIBLE_POSTDOC`, `ELIGIBLE_GRAD`, `VISITING`, `EMERITUS` and `UNAFFILIATED`, an `appointment_from` and `appointment_until` pair delimiting the current appointment as a closed window, and an optional `annual_cap_cents` giving the author's own annual subsidy ceiling, which may not be negative and which defaults to five thousand dollars — that is, 500000 cents — when omitted. A newly registered author has zero `apc_committed_cents`, zero `apc_disbursed_cents` and zero `linked_output_count`. The ORCID iD is optional at registration but becomes mandatory later: without it, no deposit of the author's work can be ingested, because ingest is what links the deposited output to the author profile.

Funders are registered with their open-access mandate parameters, and those parameters drive both the funding route and the compliance clock. A funder carries a `name`, a unique `funder_code`, a `mandate_type` of `NONE`, `GREEN_OA`, `GOLD_OA` or `IMMEDIATE_CC_BY`, a `max_embargo_months` between 0 and 24 inclusive giving the longest embargo the funder tolerates on a deposited manuscript, a `deposit_deadline_days` between 1 and 365 inclusive giving how many days after acceptance the manuscript must be ingested by, a `license_required` of `ANY`, `CC_BY` or `CC_BY_OR_NC`, and an `apc_allowable` flag saying whether the funder permits institutional or grant money to pay a gold open-access charge at all.

An award is recorded as a grant naming its `funder_id`, its `pi_author_id` — the principal investigator, who is an author in the system — a unique `grant_number`, an `award_from` and `award_until` pair delimiting the award period as a closed window, and an `apc_budget_cents` figure which may not be negative and which is carried for reporting only: it never constrains an allocation. A new grant starts with `status` `ACTIVE` and `mandate_status` `IN_PROGRESS`, with `output_count` and `compliant_output_count` at zero. The compliance sweep closes a grant once the clock date is strictly after `award_until`, moving `status` from `ACTIVE` to `CLOSED`; the practical consequence is that articles referencing a closed grant can no longer qualify for the institutional-fund route, because that route requires an active grant. Grants may be listed by the author who is their principal investigator, narrowed by `pi_author_id` and `status`, and by their funder, narrowed by `funder_id` and `status`.

Institutional money lives in APC funds opened by a `FundManager`. A fund carries a unique `fund_code`, a `fiscal_year` label between 2020 and 2100, an explicit `period_from` and `period_to` pair, a `total_budget_cents` that must be greater than zero, a `priority_rank` of at least one that determines its place in the allocation waterfall and that must be unique among funds sharing a fiscal year, a non-empty `eligible_affiliation_statuses` list drawn from the author affiliation values naming which classes of author the fund may pay for, and a `per_article_cap_cents` greater than zero limiting what the fund will contribute to any single article. A lower `priority_rank` is drawn first. On opening, `status` is `OPEN`, `committed_cents` and `spent_cents` are zero, `available_cents` equals `total_budget_cents`, and `freeze_reason` is absent.

Freezing removes a fund from the waterfall permanently — `FROZEN` is terminal, and there is no path back to `OPEN`. A `FundManager` may freeze a fund manually by supplying a `reason`, which is recorded verbatim as `freeze_reason`; the fund must currently be `OPEN`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FUND_FROZEN`. The system also freezes funds by itself. Whenever an allocation is encumbered or disbursed against a fund, the fund is re-examined: if `committed_cents` plus `spent_cents` reaches or exceeds ninety-eight percent of `total_budget_cents` — computed as `total_budget_cents` multiplied by 0.98 and rounded half up to whole cents — the fund moves from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` with `freeze_reason` set to the literal value `AUTO_THRESHOLD`. Leaving two percent unspent is deliberate: it reserves headroom for the reconciliation adjustments that always arrive at year end. Fund ledgers may be read directly, and the allocation lines drawn on a fund may be listed with optional `fund_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.4. Recording an Acceptance and Determining Coverage

An accepted manuscript may be recorded either by its `CorrespondingAuthor` or by a `ScholCommLibrarian` on the author's behalf. The payload names the `journal_id` of the venue that accepted the work, the `corresponding_author_id`, optionally a `grant_id` naming the primary funding award — absent for unfunded work — a `manuscript_title`, an `accepted_on` acceptance date, and optionally an `apc_list_cents` figure giving the charge the publisher actually quoted for this specific article. Two temporal gates apply at this moment. The `accepted_on` date must fall inside the corresponding author's closed appointment window; an acceptance dated before `appointment_from` or after `appointment_until` is rejected with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. And when a grant is named, that grant must be `ACTIVE` and `accepted_on` must fall inside the closed award window from `award_from` through `award_until`; otherwise the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

An article is unusual in carrying **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The funding dimension, `funding_status`, tracks who is paying and moves through `DRAFT`, `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`, `FUNDING_REQUESTED`, `FUNDED`, `PARTIALLY_FUNDED`, `SELF_FUNDED` and `DECLINED`, with `DECLINED` terminal. The compliance dimension, `compliance_status`, tracks the funder mandate and moves through `PENDING_DEPOSIT`, `EMBARGOED`, `COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT` and `EXEMPT`, with `COMPLIANT`, `NON_COMPLIANT` and `EXEMPT` terminal. The two dimensions never read or write each other: no funding transition inspects `compliance_status`, and no compliance transition inspects `funding_status`. A newly recorded article always starts with `funding_status` `DRAFT`, while its `compliance_status` is set at creation to either `PENDING_DEPOSIT` or `EXEMPT` by the mandate rule described in §3.8, which also sets `deposit_deadline_on` and `embargo_until`. When a `grant_id` is supplied, the grant's `output_count` and `mandate_status` are recomputed as part of the same request. Articles may be listed with optional `journal_id`, `corresponding_author_id`, `grant_id`, `funding_status` and `compliance_status` filters, and read individually to see both dimensions together with the coverage amounts.

Coverage is decided by running an **eligibility check**, which either a `ScholCommLibrarian` or the article's own `CorrespondingAuthor` may request, and which appends a new immutable `EligibilityDetermination` row rather than editing an existing one. The check takes an optional `basis` naming which variant to run — `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — and defaults to `PROVISIONAL` when omitted. The row that is written carries the `article_id`, a per-article one-based `sequence_no` equal to the article's `determination_count` plus one, the `basis` used, the `reference_date` the windows were tested against, the `route` chosen, the `agreement_id` and `coverage_id` of whatever was matched, the `list_base_cents` the pricing worked from, the `discount_pct_applied`, the `covered_amount_cents`, the `author_share_cents`, the `eligible_request_cents`, a pinned `determination_reason` code, and a `quota_position`. **The effective determination of an article is the row with the highest `sequence_no`**; every earlier row remains readable but stops being effective. Determinations may be listed with optional `article_id`, `agreement_id`, `coverage_id` and `basis` filters, and read individually.

Before anything is computed, the publisher of the article's journal must not be `BLOCKED`, or the check is refused with `ERR_PUBLISHER_BLOCKED`; and if the article names a grant, that grant must be `ACTIVE`, or the check is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The determination itself proceeds in a fixed order. First, the `reference_date` is fixed: under the `PROVISIONAL` variant it is the article's `accepted_on`; under the `FINAL` variant it is the article's `publication_date` when one has been set, and otherwise the current clock date. Second, the base charge `list_base_cents` is fixed: under the `FINAL` variant, if the article carries an `apc_list_cents` value it is used, and otherwise the journal's `list_apc_cents` is used; under the `PROVISIONAL` variant the journal's `list_apc_cents` is always used, even when the article carries a quoted figure.

Third comes the screening gate. If the publisher's `screening_status` is `PREDATORY_FLAGGED`, the determination stops immediately with the author paying: the `route` is `AUTHOR_PAYS`, no agreement or coverage line is recorded, `discount_pct_applied` is 0, `covered_amount_cents` is 0, `author_share_cents` equals `list_base_cents` in full, `eligible_request_cents` is 0 so no institutional subsidy may be requested, `quota_position` is absent, and `determination_reason` is `PREDATORY_FLAGGED_NO_INSTITUTIONAL_FUNDS`.

Fourth, the candidate agreements are assembled. An agreement is a candidate when all of the following hold: it belongs to the publisher of the article's journal; its `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, which means `DRAFT` and `EXPIRED` agreements are never candidates; the `reference_date` falls inside its half-open window, being on or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_until`; the journal's `oa_model` appears in the agreement's `covered_oa_models`; the agreement passes its quota gate; and the agreement has a coverage line for exactly this journal whose `coverage_type` is `FULL_APC` or `CAPPED_APC` — a `READ_ONLY` line never funds anything — and whose closed window contains the `reference_date`, being on or after `covered_from` and on or before `covered_until`.

The quota gate is where the two variants diverge most sharply, and the divergence is deliberate. Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant the gate is **forward-looking**: it counts every article whose effective determination names this agreement with a route of `TA_FULL` or `TA_CAPPED`, regardless of that determination's basis, and requires the count to be strictly less than `article_quota`. Provisional holds therefore consume quota as if they were commitments, which is what stops the office over-promising cover. Under the `FINAL` variant the gate is **chronological**: it counts only those articles whose effective determination names this agreement with a route of `TA_FULL` or `TA_CAPPED` *and* whose basis is `FINAL`, again requiring the count to be strictly less than `article_quota`. An agreement whose tokens are all held by provisional determinations can therefore still cover a final determination — the committed tokens are the only ones that block at publication time.

Where more than one candidate survives, they are ranked by `discount_pct` descending, then by `effective_until` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending as the tie-break of last resort; the identifier is never used as a tie-break. In practice the candidate set holds at most one agreement, because rule 23 of §2.11 forbids overlapping windows for the same publisher, but the ranking is pinned so that the outcome is deterministic regardless.

Fifth, the route is chosen. When at least one candidate exists, the highest-ranked agreement is selected together with its single matching coverage line for this journal on the `reference_date`. The `discount_pct_applied` is that agreement's `discount_pct`, and the discounted price is `list_base_cents` multiplied by one minus the discount, rounded half up to whole cents — this is the only rounding step in the whole determination. The `quota_position` is the number of tokens already held on that agreement plus one, making it a one-based index of where this article sits in the agreement's quota. If the matched coverage line's `coverage_type` is `FULL_APC`, the `route` is `TA_FULL`, `covered_amount_cents` is the whole discounted price, `author_share_cents` is 0, `eligible_request_cents` is 0 because there is nothing left to subsidise, and `determination_reason` is `TA_FULL_COVERAGE`. If instead the line's `coverage_type` is `CAPPED_APC`, the `route` is `TA_CAPPED`, `covered_amount_cents` is the smaller of the line's `capped_amount_cents` and the discounted price, `author_share_cents` is the discounted price less that covered amount, `eligible_request_cents` equals `author_share_cents` when the article qualifies for the institutional-fund route and is 0 otherwise, and `determination_reason` is `TA_CAPPED_COVERAGE`.

When no candidate exists, `discount_pct_applied` is 0 and `quota_position` is absent, and the choice turns entirely on whether the article qualifies for the institutional-fund route. It qualifies when, and only when, all four of these hold: the article names a grant; that grant's `status` is `ACTIVE`; the grant's funder has a `mandate_type` of either `GOLD_OA` or `IMMEDIATE_CC_BY`; the funder's `apc_allowable` is true; and the journal's `oa_model` is `FULLY_OA`. A hybrid journal therefore never qualifies for the fund route, and neither does a `GREEN_OA` or `NONE` mandate. If it qualifies, the `route` is `FUND_APC`, `covered_amount_cents` is 0, `author_share_cents` is the whole `list_base_cents`, `eligible_request_cents` is likewise the whole `list_base_cents`, and `determination_reason` is `NO_COVERAGE_FUND_ELIGIBLE`. If it does not qualify, the `route` is `AUTHOR_PAYS`, `covered_amount_cents` is 0, `author_share_cents` is the whole `list_base_cents`, `eligible_request_cents` is 0, and `determination_reason` is `NO_COVERAGE_AUTHOR_PAYS`.

Finally the results are written back. The determination is inserted, `Article.determination_count` rises by one, and the article's own `eligibility_route`, `covered_amount_cents` and `author_share_cents` are refreshed from the newly effective row. The funding dimension then follows the route: a `TA_FULL` route sets `funding_status` to `FUNDED`; a `TA_CAPPED` route sets it to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED` when `eligible_request_cents` is greater than zero and to `SELF_FUNDED` when `eligible_request_cents` is zero; a `FUND_APC` route sets it to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`; an `AUTHOR_PAYS` route sets it to `SELF_FUNDED`. Two situations leave `funding_status` untouched no matter what the route says: when it is already `DECLINED`, and when any fund request of this article has a `status` of `APPROVED` or `PAID`. Lastly, `articles_used` is recomputed on both the previously selected agreement and the newly selected agreement — this single step is what releases the old quota token and consumes the new one — and any resulting agreement status change drives the publisher's `active_agreement_count` and increments the publisher's `version` by one.

Read in state-machine terms, a first check on a fresh article moves it from `DRAFT` to `FUNDED` when the route is `TA_FULL`, from `DRAFT` to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED` when the route is `FUND_APC` or is `TA_CAPPED` with a positive `eligible_request_cents`, and from `DRAFT` to `SELF_FUNDED` when the route is `AUTHOR_PAYS` or is `TA_CAPPED` with `eligible_request_cents` of zero. Re-running the check is expressly allowed and is how the office reacts to an agreement that has just been activated or exhausted, but every re-run requires that no fund request of the article sits in `APPROVED` or `PAID`. A re-run that lands on the same conclusion cycles `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED` back to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`, appending a further determination that displaces the previous one and recomputing `articles_used` on both the previously and the newly selected agreements. A re-run that now finds full agreement cover moves `SELF_FUNDED` to `FUNDED`, recomputing `articles_used` on the newly selected agreement and possibly driving that agreement from `ACTIVE` to `EXHAUSTED` along with the publisher's `active_agreement_count` and `version`. A re-run that now finds the fund route, or capped cover with something left to request, moves `SELF_FUNDED` to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`. And a re-run that now finds nothing at all moves `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED` to `SELF_FUNDED`, recomputing `articles_used` on the agreement that was previously selected so that its token is handed back.

### 3.5. Requesting, Reviewing and Deciding Institutional Subsidy

Where a determination leaves an author with something to pay and the article qualifies to ask, the `CorrespondingAuthor` opens a fund request naming the `article_id`, a `requested_cents` amount that must be greater than zero, and a `justification` explaining the case. The request is admitted only when an effective determination exists for the article and its `eligible_request_cents` is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED` — and only when `requested_cents` does not exceed that `eligible_request_cents`, an excess being rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_ELIGIBLE`. The publisher must not be `BLOCKED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PUBLISHER_BLOCKED`, and any named grant must be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A new request has `status` `SUBMITTED` and `revision_round` of zero, with `info_needed`, `response_note`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason` all absent, `approved_cents` and `author_share_cents` at zero and `fully_funded` false. Creating it advances the article's funding dimension from `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED` to `FUNDING_REQUESTED`.

Review is a separate hand. A `ScholCommLibrarian` takes a submitted request into review, moving it from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`. If something is unclear, the same librarian raises an information request, moving `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED` and recording the question as `info_needed`. The author answers by resubmitting, which requires the request to be in `INFO_REQUESTED` — any other state yields `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE` — and which supplies a fresh `requested_cents` together with a `response_note`. Resubmission replaces `requested_cents` outright, records the `response_note`, increases `revision_round` by one, and returns the request to `SUBMITTED`. The new amount is re-checked against the effective determination's `eligible_request_cents` exactly as at creation, so an author cannot escalate the ask through the revision loop; an excess is again `ERR_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_ELIGIBLE`. The `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED` cycle may repeat without limit, and `revision_round` is simply the count of completed cycles.

The funding decision belongs to a `FundManager`, never to the reviewer. The decision payload carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `reason`, and in both branches the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` and the supplied reason as `decision_reason`. The request must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`; from any other state the decision is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REVIEWABLE`.

A rejection is the simpler branch and its effects are deliberately narrow: the request moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REJECTED`, the `decision_reason` is recorded, no allocation line is created, and no fund ledger anywhere changes. The article's funding dimension moves from `FUNDING_REQUESTED` to `DECLINED`. `DECLINED` is terminal for the funding dimension, which is why a later eligibility check on the same article leaves `funding_status` alone even when it discovers new agreement cover.

An approval is where the money actually moves, and it runs the **allocation waterfall**. The author's remaining annual allowance is computed first, as the author's `annual_cap_cents` less that author's utilisation for the fiscal year on the `ENCUMBRANCE_INCLUSIVE` basis, floored at zero. If the remaining allowance is zero, the approval is refused with `ERR_AUTHOR_CAP_EXHAUSTED` and nothing is written. Otherwise a request ceiling is set to the smallest of three independent limits: the request's own `requested_cents`, the effective determination's `eligible_request_cents`, and the author's remaining allowance. The amount still to be filled starts at that ceiling.

The candidate funds are those whose `status` is `OPEN` — frozen funds are excluded outright — whose `fiscal_year` equals the fiscal year of the current clock date, whose period contains the clock date so that `period_from` is on or before it and `period_to` is on or after it, and whose `eligible_affiliation_statuses` list contains the author's `affiliation_status`. Candidates are drawn in ascending `priority_rank`, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break of last resort; ties cannot actually arise because a fiscal year admits each rank only once. A running sequence counter starts at zero. For each candidate in turn, if nothing remains to fill the waterfall stops. Otherwise the fund's own availability is computed as `total_budget_cents` less `committed_cents` less `spent_cents`, and the amount taken from this fund is the smallest of three quantities: the amount still to be filled, that availability, and the fund's `per_article_cap_cents`. If the amount taken is zero or negative, this fund is **skipped and the waterfall continues to the next fund** — a fund with no headroom never aborts the allocation. Otherwise the sequence counter increases by one and an allocation line is written carrying the `fund_request_id`, the `fund_id`, the `amount_cents` taken, the `sequence_index` from the counter, and a `status` of `ENCUMBERED` with no `released_reason`. The fund's `committed_cents` rises by the amount taken and its `available_cents` is recomputed; the fund is then tested against the ninety-eight percent auto-freeze threshold described in §3.3 and may move to `FROZEN` immediately, in which case later requests will not see it. Finally the amount still to be filled is reduced by the amount taken, and the waterfall moves on.

The approved total is the request ceiling less whatever remained unfilled. If it is zero — no eligible open fund could contribute a single cent — the approval is refused with `ERR_NO_FUND_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409) and nothing is written. Otherwise `FundRequest.approved_cents` is set to that total, `FundRequest.author_share_cents` becomes `requested_cents` less the approved total, and `FundRequest.fully_funded` is true exactly when the approved total equals `requested_cents`. The request moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`. The article's funding dimension moves from `FUNDING_REQUESTED` to `FUNDED` when the request was fully funded and to `PARTIALLY_FUNDED` when it was not. Every touched fund's `committed_cents` and `available_cents` are refreshed, and the author's `apc_committed_cents` is recomputed. All arithmetic here is in whole cents: no fund contributes a fraction of a cent, and no remainder is redistributed after the last candidate has been considered.

Withdrawal is the author's escape hatch and it exists in two shapes with quite different consequences. Withdrawing from `SUBMITTED` or from `INFO_REQUESTED` simply moves the request to `WITHDRAWN` and returns the article's funding dimension to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`; no allocation exists yet, so no ledger moves. Withdrawing from `APPROVED` is a compensating transaction: every allocation line of the request whose `status` is `ENCUMBERED` becomes `RELEASED` with its `released_reason` set to the literal value `REQUEST_WITHDRAWN`, each affected fund's `committed_cents` and `available_cents` are recomputed so that the encumbered total returns to exactly its pre-approval value, the author's `apc_committed_cents` is recomputed, and the article's funding dimension returns to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`. Withdrawal from any state other than `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REVIEWABLE` — which is also what happens once the article's invoice has been paid, because payment moves the request out of `APPROVED` into `PAID`. A withdrawal request carries a `reason`. Released lines are permanent: freezing a fund and releasing an encumbrance are both one-way, and a released line never counts towards utilisation on either basis.

The allocation lines of a request may be listed in waterfall order, optionally narrowed by `fund_request_id` and `status`, and the request itself may be read to see its `approved_cents` and `revision_round`. The remaining transition on a request — from `APPROVED` to `PAID` — is not driven by the author or the reviewer at all; it fires as a consequence of the publisher invoice being paid, as described in §3.7.

### 3.6. Publishing the Version of Record

Publication is a `ScholCommLibrarian` action and it does four things at once. The payload supplies the `publication_date`. The article must not already have a `publication_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ARTICLE_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`. On success the `publication_date` is stored and a `doi` is minted in the form of the literal prefix `10.5555/`, then the publisher's `publisher_code`, then a full stop, then the publication sequence number — a server-maintained, institution-wide counter that begins at 1 and increases by one for every article published. A second effect is that the article's `embargo_until` is recomputed, because the embargo clock is measured from the publication date once one exists rather than from acceptance.

The third effect is the one with the widest reach: publication automatically runs a further coverage determination on the `FINAL` basis, exactly as described in §3.4 but with `reference_date` taken from the `publication_date` just set, with `list_base_cents` preferring the article's own `apc_list_cents` when it has one, and with the chronological quota gate that counts only committed tokens. That determination is appended with the next `sequence_no`, `determination_count` rises by one, and it becomes the effective determination — which means the price the invoice will later be checked against is the price as at publication, not the price as at acceptance.

The fourth effect follows from the third. Because a new determination has become effective, `articles_used` is recomputed on the agreement the previous determination had selected, releasing its quota token, and on whichever agreement the final determination selects. An agreement sitting in `EXHAUSTED` whose recomputed `articles_used` has fallen below its `article_quota` moves back to `ACTIVE`, provided the clock date is still strictly before its `effective_until`; the publisher's `active_agreement_count` is recomputed and the publisher's `version` is incremented by one whenever an agreement's status changes this way.

Where the final determination reaches a different conclusion from the provisional one, the article's funding dimension follows it. An article that was `FUNDED` on provisional agreement cover but whose final determination routes to `FUND_APC` moves from `FUNDED` back to `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`, so the author may now open a subsidy request. An article that was `FUNDED` but whose final determination routes to `AUTHOR_PAYS` moves from `FUNDED` to `SELF_FUNDED`. Both of these re-routings require that the article had no `publication_date` before the call and that no fund request of the article sits in `APPROVED` or `PAID`; where a fund request has already been approved or paid, the funding dimension is left exactly as it stands, because the money has already been committed against the earlier decision. Publication returns the updated article rather than the determination.

### 3.7. Publisher Invoices: Independent Re-pricing, Disputes and Payment

Publishers bill after the fact, and the office's first job is to work out what the bill *should* say. A `ScholCommLibrarian` records an incoming invoice by supplying the `publisher_id` of the issuer, the `article_id` the charge relates to, the publisher's own `invoice_number`, the gross `invoice_amount_cents` billed, the `currency`, the `claimed_discount_pct` the publisher says it applied, the `vat_pct` fraction of value-added or sales tax applied to the net amount, and the `invoice_date` of issue. Only `USD` is accepted as `currency`; any other value is a malformed request. The `claimed_discount_pct` must lie between 0 and 0.95 inclusive and the `vat_pct` between 0 and 0.30 inclusive. The `invoice_number` must not already have been used by the same publisher, though two different publishers may reuse a number freely; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVOICE_NUMBER`. The invoiced article must have an effective eligibility determination, or the invoice is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_DETERMINED` — but unlike a fund request, an invoice is perfectly legitimate for an article whose `eligible_request_cents` is zero, because an author-pays article still generates a bill. A recorded invoice starts in `status` `RECEIVED` with no `dispute_reason`, no `payment_approved_by_user_id` and no `payment_reference`, and the expected amount is priced immediately at creation.

The re-pricing works from the article's effective determination. The base is that determination's `list_base_cents`. The `entitled_discount_pct` — the discount the institution is actually entitled to — depends on the route: when the determination's route is `TA_FULL` or `TA_CAPPED`, it is that determination's `discount_pct_applied`, because the agreement fixes the discount; when the route is `FUND_APC` or `AUTHOR_PAYS`, there is no agreement and the entitlement comes instead from the publisher's volume tier described below. **The order of rounding is pinned and is not negotiable**: each component is rounded to whole cents and only then are the components added. First, `net_cents` is the base multiplied by one minus the `entitled_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, `vat_cents` is that already-rounded `net_cents` multiplied by the invoice's `vat_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — the tax is computed on the rounded net, not on the unrounded net. Third, `expected_amount_cents` is `net_cents` plus `vat_cents` with no further rounding applied to the sum. Summing first and rounding once is explicitly *not* the rule and can differ by up to one cent. Finally, `variance_cents` is the invoice's `invoice_amount_cents` less the `expected_amount_cents`, and may be negative when the publisher under-billed.

The verdict, stored as `validation_outcome`, is decided by four tests evaluated strictly in this order, the first match winning. If the `claimed_discount_pct` is less than the `entitled_discount_pct` by more than one ten-thousandth — that is, if the claimed discount falls below the entitled discount minus 0.0001 — the outcome is `DISCOUNT_SHORTFALL`, because the publisher under-applied the negotiated discount; the small epsilon exists so that floating-point noise in a claimed fraction is not read as a shortfall. Otherwise, if `variance_cents` is exactly zero the outcome is `MATCH`. Otherwise, if the absolute value of `variance_cents` is no more than one hundred cents — one US dollar — the outcome is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`. Otherwise the outcome is `AMOUNT_VARIANCE`. Note that the discount test runs before the amount tests, so an invoice that under-applies the discount but happens to arrive at the right total is still a `DISCOUNT_SHORTFALL`.

The volume tier itself is a small piece of tiered pricing with a circular-looking dependency that is resolved by a pinned evaluation order. The tier depends on cumulative net spend with the publisher, while this invoice's own `net_cents` depends on the tier. The order is: prior paid net spend is computed **from other invoices only, and only from those whose `status` is `PAID`** — the invoice being priced is excluded, and so are invoices sitting in `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` or `DISPUTED`; then the tier is selected from that prior figure; then this invoice's `net_cents` is computed from the selected tier and is never fed back into the prior figure. The prior figure is the sum of `net_cents` over the same publisher's paid invoices whose `invoice_date` falls in the same institutional fiscal year as this invoice's `invoice_date`. Tier boundaries are inclusive: prior paid net spend of fifty thousand dollars or more (5000000 cents) earns a twenty percent entitlement, at least twenty thousand dollars (2000000 cents) earns fifteen percent, at least five thousand dollars (500000 cents) earns ten percent, and anything below five thousand dollars earns nothing at all.

Validating an invoice is a `ScholCommLibrarian` action that requires the invoice to be in `RECEIVED` and that forks the lifecycle. It first recomputes the whole pricing — refreshing `entitled_discount_pct`, `net_cents`, `vat_cents`, `expected_amount_cents` and `variance_cents`, since a determination may have changed since receipt — and then branches on the recomputed `validation_outcome`. A `MATCH` or a `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` moves the invoice from `RECEIVED` to `VALIDATED` and records no dispute. A `DISCOUNT_SHORTFALL` or an `AMOUNT_VARIANCE` moves the invoice from `RECEIVED` to `DISPUTED` and sets `dispute_reason` to that same outcome value. `DISPUTED` is terminal and has no payment path whatsoever: to be paid, the publisher must issue a fresh invoice under a new `invoice_number`.

Payment then proceeds in the two-person sequence of §2.9. A `FundManager` approves a `VALIDATED` invoice for payment, which requires the invoiced article's `funding_status` to be one of `FUNDED`, `PARTIALLY_FUNDED` or `SELF_FUNDED` — an article still in `DRAFT`, `ELIGIBILITY_CHECKED`, `FUNDING_REQUESTED` or `DECLINED` yields `ERR_ARTICLE_NOT_FUNDED` — and which moves the invoice from `VALIDATED` to `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` while recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `payment_approved_by_user_id`. A different `FundManager` then pays it, supplying the bank `payment_reference`, which moves the invoice from `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` to `PAID`.

Payment is the largest cascade in the system and it runs three further hops beyond the invoice itself. First, the article's fund request whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves to `PAID`. Second, every allocation line of that request whose `status` is `ENCUMBERED` becomes `DISBURSED`. Third, for each of those lines, the owning fund moves the line's `amount_cents` out of `committed_cents` and into `spent_cents`, leaving `available_cents` unchanged in total but converting a promise into a payment. The author's `apc_committed_cents` and `apc_disbursed_cents` are then both recomputed, the first falling and the second rising. When the article has no approved fund request at all — the self-funded case, where the author or their grant paid the publisher directly — the invoice still reaches `PAID`, but none of the three hops fire and no ledger moves. Invoices may be listed with optional `publisher_id`, `article_id` and `status` filters, and read individually to inspect the validation verdict.

### 3.8. Depositing the Manuscript, Curation and Mandate Compliance

The compliance dimension of an article is governed by a single rule that is evaluated at four different moments: when the article is first recorded, when the version of record is published, when a deposit is ingested, and during the two relevant phases of the compliance sweep. The rule begins with an exemption test. If the article names no grant, or if the grant's funder has a `mandate_type` of `NONE`, the article is `EXEMPT` outright with no `deposit_deadline_on` and no `embargo_until`; an exempt article never enters the sweep and counts as satisfying its grant for roll-up purposes. Otherwise the deadline and the embargo are computed. The `deposit_deadline_on` is the article's `accepted_on` advanced by the funder's `deposit_deadline_days` in whole calendar days. The embargo length in months is zero when the funder's `mandate_type` is `IMMEDIATE_CC_BY`, and otherwise the smaller of the funder's `max_embargo_months` and the journal's `required_embargo_months` — the institution never applies a longer embargo than either party requires. The embargo is measured from the article's `publication_date` when one exists and from its `accepted_on` when it does not, and `embargo_until` is that base date advanced by the embargo length in whole months, keeping the day of month and clamping to the last day of the target month where the original day does not exist there. All of this arithmetic is in whole calendar days and whole months; there is no partial-day component anywhere.

At ingest, the verdict is decided by two comparisons whose boundary behaviour differs deliberately. If the ingest date — the clock date at the moment of ingest — is **strictly after** `deposit_deadline_on`, the article is `NON_COMPLIANT`; depositing exactly on the deadline is on time. Otherwise, if the clock date is **on or after** `embargo_until`, the article is `COMPLIANT`; the embargo releases on its stated date rather than the day after. Otherwise the article is `EMBARGOED`, meaning the manuscript is safely in the repository but may not yet be made public.

Deposits are created by the `CorrespondingAuthor`. The payload names the `article_id`, a `version_label` of either `ACCEPTED_MANUSCRIPT` or `VERSION_OF_RECORD` saying which manuscript version the file represents, a `file_checksum` that must be a SHA-256 digest rendered as exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, a `license` of `CC_BY`, `CC_BY_NC`, `CC_BY_NC_ND` or `ALL_RIGHTS_RESERVED`, and the `expected_article_version` that guards the write per §2.6. The chosen `license` must satisfy the funder's `license_required` as spelled out in rule 16 of §2.11, or the deposit is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_PERMITTED`. At most one deposit per article may be open at a time — that is, sitting in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` — so a second open deposit is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_VERSION_CONFLICT`. A created deposit takes a `version_ordinal` of the article's `latest_deposit_ordinal` plus one, which makes ordinals monotonic and strictly increasing within an article, and it starts in `status` `DRAFT` with no `persistent_identifier`, no `submitted_at`, no `ingested_at`, no `curation_note`, no `embargo_until` and a `returned_count` of zero. Creating it also increments the article's `version` by one and updates the article's `latest_deposit_ordinal`.

The author submits the draft for curation, which requires the deposit to be in `DRAFT` and which moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` while stamping `submitted_at` from the logical clock. A `RepositoryCurator` then decides, supplying a `decision` of either `RETURN` or `INGEST` together with an optional `curation_note`. The deposit must be in `SUBMITTED`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE`.

Returning the deposit moves it from `SUBMITTED` back to `DRAFT`, increases `returned_count` by one and records the `curation_note`. A return has **no compliance side effects at all**: the article's `compliance_status`, `embargo_until` and `latest_deposit_ordinal` are untouched, no grant counter moves, and no persistent identifier is minted. The `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `DRAFT` cycle may repeat without limit, and `returned_count` records how often curation sent the file back.

Ingesting the deposit is the moment the institution takes custody, and it first requires author profile linkage: the article's corresponding author must have a non-null `orcid`, or the ingest is refused with `ERR_ORCID_REQUIRED`. On success the deposit moves from `SUBMITTED` to `INGESTED`, `ingested_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and a `persistent_identifier` is minted as the literal prefix `hdl:2027/ir.` followed by the institutional ingest counter rendered as six digits with leading zeros — the counter begins at 1, so the first ingest in the system produces `hdl:2027/ir.000001`. The deposit's own `embargo_until` is copied from the article as at ingest. Three cascade hops then follow. First, any other deposit of the same article that was in `INGESTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`; a superseded row **retains its `persistent_identifier`** so that citations of the older version keep resolving, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. Second, the article's `latest_deposit_ordinal`, `embargo_until` and `compliance_status` are recomputed by the mandate rule above, moving the compliance dimension from `PENDING_DEPOSIT` to `EMBARGOED` when the embargo has not yet elapsed, to `COMPLIANT` when it has, and to `NON_COMPLIANT` when the ingest came after the deadline. Third, the grant's `compliant_output_count` and `mandate_status` are recomputed. The author's `linked_output_count` is recomputed as well, since it counts ingested deposits of that author's articles.

The grant-level verdict is a compound state derived entirely from the grant's articles, and it is recomputed whenever any of them changes compliance. `Grant.output_count` is the number of articles referencing the grant and `Grant.compliant_output_count` is how many of those are `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT`. If **any** article of the grant is `NON_COMPLIANT`, the `mandate_status` is `BREACHED`. Otherwise, if the grant has at least one article and every one of them is `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT`, the `mandate_status` is `SATISFIED`. In every other case it is `IN_PROGRESS`. In state-machine terms the grant's mandate dimension moves from `IN_PROGRESS` to `SATISFIED` once an article reaching `COMPLIANT` or `EXEMPT` completes the set, from `IN_PROGRESS` to `BREACHED` as soon as one article reaches `NON_COMPLIANT`, and from `SATISFIED` to `BREACHED` when a further article later reaches `NON_COMPLIANT`. `BREACHED` dominates `SATISFIED` and is terminal for the mandate dimension; a breach is never repaired by subsequent compliant deposits. An article's deposit versions may be listed with optional `article_id` and `status` filters, and a deposit may be read individually to see its `persistent_identifier`.

### 3.9. The Compliance Sweep and Negotiation Analytics

Time does not pass by itself in this system, so the office runs a **compliance sweep** as an operational checkpoint. It is a `ScholCommLibrarian` action, it takes no input, and it runs four phases in a fixed order, each phase processing the rows it touches in ascending `created_at` order so that the outcome is fully deterministic.

Phase one expires agreements: every transformative agreement in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` whose `effective_until` is on or before the clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, with the publisher's `active_agreement_count` recomputed and the publisher's `version` incremented for those that were `ACTIVE`. Phase two closes grants: every `ACTIVE` grant whose `award_until` is strictly before the clock date moves to `CLOSED`. Phase three catches missed deposit deadlines: every article whose `compliance_status` is still `PENDING_DEPOSIT` and whose `deposit_deadline_on` is strictly before the clock date moves to `NON_COMPLIANT`, and the owning grant's `mandate_status` moves to `BREACHED`. Phase four releases elapsed embargoes: every article whose `compliance_status` is `EMBARGOED` and whose `embargo_until` is on or before the clock date moves to `COMPLIANT`, with the grant's `compliant_output_count` and `mandate_status` recomputed. Ordering matters between phases three and four: a grant that gains a breach in phase three stays `BREACHED` even if phase four then promotes another of its articles, because a breach dominates.

The sweep reports what it did rather than returning an entity. Its response carries `swept_at_utc` as the logical-clock instant of the run, `agreements_expired_count`, `grants_closed_count`, `articles_marked_non_compliant_count` and `articles_promoted_compliant_count`, each being the number of rows the corresponding phase actually changed.

The annual spend report is the other reporting surface, available to a `FundManager` or a `ScholCommLibrarian`. It requires a `fiscal_year` and accepts an optional `basis` — `ENCUMBRANCE`, the default, or `CASH` — and an optional `publisher_id` to narrow the invoice-derived figures to a single counterparty. The fiscal-year window runs from 1 July of the year before the requested `fiscal_year` through 30 June of the requested `fiscal_year`, inclusive at both ends.

Three separate populations feed the report. From the allocation ledger, the report takes every allocation line whose fund carries the requested `fiscal_year`: `total_committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over lines whose status is `ENCUMBERED`, and `total_disbursed_cents` is the same sum over lines whose status is `DISBURSED`; released lines are excluded from both. The headline `total_allocated_cents` then depends on the `basis`: on the encumbrance basis it is `total_committed_cents` plus `total_disbursed_cents`, and on the cash basis it is `total_disbursed_cents` alone. From the invoice ledger, the report takes every invoice whose `status` is `PAID` and whose `invoice_date` falls inside the fiscal-year window, further narrowed to the requested `publisher_id` when one is supplied: `paid_invoice_count` is how many such invoices there are, and `avg_paid_net_cents` is the sum of their `net_cents` divided by that count and rounded half up to whole cents — or exactly zero when there are no such invoices, since division by zero is never attempted. From the determination ledger, the report takes the effective determination of every article whose `accepted_on` falls inside the fiscal-year window: `ta_articles_covered` counts those whose route is `TA_FULL` or `TA_CAPPED`, and `ta_savings_cents` sums, over exactly that same subset, the `list_base_cents` less the `covered_amount_cents` less the `author_share_cents` of each determination — the portion of list price that the negotiated discount removed altogether. Across the whole determination population, `hybrid_article_count` counts those whose journal has an `oa_model` of `HYBRID` and `fully_oa_article_count` counts those whose journal has an `oa_model` of `FULLY_OA`.

Two derived figures close the report. The `top_publisher_code` names the publisher with the largest paid net spend: the eligible pool is the publishers appearing among the paid invoices above, they are ranked by the sum of their invoices' `net_cents` descending with `publisher_code` ascending as the tie-break, and the winner's `publisher_code` is reported — or nothing at all when the pool is empty. The `negotiation_leverage_ratio` expresses savings against cash actually paid: it is `ta_savings_cents` divided by `total_disbursed_cents`, except that the divisor is floored at 1 so that a year with no disbursement yields the savings figure rather than an undefined value, and the quotient is rounded half up to four decimal places. Both the `fiscal_year` requested and the `basis` used are echoed back in the response alongside these figures.
