# Beverage Program & Wine List Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multi-unit restaurant group runs its wine program as a single commercial machine with many moving parts: a beverage director who owns the catalogue and signs off on every printed list, sommeliers who build those lists venue by venue, distributor representatives who release scarce allocated bottles to the group as a whole, a compliance officer who decides whether a given wholesaler may legally ship, and a financial controller who has the last word on any loss the cellar absorbs. Today that machine runs on spreadsheets, printed bin cards and email threads. Allocation offers are divided by hand and re-divided when a venue drops out; a vintage change is discovered only when a guest points at a list that no longer matches the bottle; by-the-glass bottles die on the backbar with nobody accountable for the pours that were never sold; and the gap between what the cellar actually gave up and what the recorded sales should have cost — the pour-cost variance that every beverage director is judged on — is reconstructed weeks later, if at all.

This document specifies the Beverage Program & Wine List Management API, the system of record that replaces those spreadsheets. All business resources are mounted under the `/beverage-program` prefix. The API models the full chain: venue and cellar geography, distributor relationships and licence standing, allocation offers and the pro-rata waterfall that divides them, cellar lots with bin locations and par levels, an append-only depletion ledger that is the single source of truth for cost of goods and revenue, list versions that move through review and publication, by-the-glass preservation slots, spoilage write-offs, wine-dinner event pricing, corkage quoting, and the pour-cost variance runs that gate the whole program.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **nothing is ever edited away**: inventory that moves out of the cellar and money that is recognised are both recorded as ledger entries, and undoing either means writing a compensating entry, never rewriting history. Second, **the numbers are the specification**: every price, every awarded bottle, every percentage in this document has exactly one correct value derivable from the inputs, with the order of rounding steps pinned. Where two plausible readings of an arithmetic rule exist, this document names the one the system implements.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts: authentication, response shape, pagination, temporal semantics, precision, concurrency guards, and the cross-cutting principles that govern inventory conservation and fiscal posture.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles the system recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the header pair is well formed but the declared role is not authorised for the target operation, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `BeverageDirector`, `VenueSommelier`, `DistributorRep`, `Controller`, and `ComplianceOfficer`.

A `BeverageDirector` is the group's beverage director. The role owns master data — venues, distributors and wines — reviews and publishes list versions, runs the pour-cost job, and confirms wine dinners; its scope is group-wide and it is not bound to any single venue. When `X-User-Role` is `BeverageDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands an actor identity — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `published_by_user_id` on a list version — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `VenueSommelier` is the sommelier of exactly one venue. When `X-User-Role` is `VenueSommelier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Venue`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Venue` compare the resource's venue reference to `X-User-ID`. The check is satisfied not only by a `venue_id` in the path or the body but also by the venue reached transitively — through a list version to its owning wine list, through a list entry to its version and then that version's wine list, or through a lot, a by-the-glass slot, a write-off, an event or an allocation claim. A sommelier acting on anything that resolves to a different venue is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and that rejection is evaluated before any existence check on foreign keys carried in the request body.

A `DistributorRep` is the sales representative of one distributor. When `X-User-Role` is `DistributorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; posting an allocation offer or filtering the offer collection for a `distributor_id` other than `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A distributor representative appears on no write endpoint under cellar bins, inventory lots, list versions, by-the-glass slots, spoilage write-offs or wine dinner events; those attempts return `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

A `Controller` is the group financial controller: the sole approver, rejecter and reverser of spoilage write-offs, the sole settler and voider of wine dinners, and the sole handler of pour-cost variance investigations. A controller never builds or publishes lists. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the alcohol-licensing compliance officer and the only authority over distributor licence clearance and holds; it is read-only everywhere else, and its reviews move only the compliance dimension of an offer. For both of these roles, as for `BeverageDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a spoilage write-off) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

Two operations in this system deliberately compare `X-User-ID` against a stored actor rather than against a role. Publishing a list version and approving or rejecting a spoilage write-off are both refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the identifier recorded as the submitter on the object being acted on. Because submitting a list version is shared between the director and the sommelier while publishing is director-exclusive, a director who submits a version cannot publish it. Because proposing a write-off is shared between the sommelier and the controller while approval is controller-exclusive, a controller who proposes a write-off cannot approve or reject it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific collection states otherwise in Section 3, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections carry a domain-specific primary ordering with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break: inventory lots are returned in `received_at` ascending order (which is the same order in which they are consumed), depletion ledger entries in `occurred_at` ascending order, list entries in `sort_rank` ascending order, by-the-glass slots in `slot_index` ascending order, and wine dinner events in `starts_at` ascending order. Records are never ordered by `id`.

For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records beyond the current page remain.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

No business rule in this system reads the wall clock. Instead the server maintains a single logical UTC clock, which starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the process starts and moves only when an operator sets it explicitly through the test-driving clock endpoint, supplying the new instant as `to_iso8601`. That endpoint is available to all five roles and returns HTTP 204 with no body. Every deadline comparison, every preservation expiry, every occupancy interval, and every timestamp the server mints — `opened_at`, `published_at`, `printed_at`, `allocated_at`, `resolved_at`, `last_pour_cost_run_at`, `occurred_at` — is taken from this logical clock.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Append-Only Ledger

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was created.

All resources except the depletion ledger additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. The `version` integer is what the concurrency guards in Section 2.7 compare against, and it is distinct from a list version's `version_no`, which is the human-facing sequence number of a printed list.

`DepletionEntry` is the exception. Ledger entries are immutable events, not resources: a depletion entry carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is never modified after it is written. Correcting a depletion is done by appending a further entry whose signed values negate the original, and both entries remain readable forever.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents, and every field carrying money is named with a `_cents` suffix. Percentages and other decimal quantities are carried to two decimal places unless a rule states otherwise. Wherever a rule rounds, it rounds half up — a value exactly on the midpoint moves away from zero — and never banker's-rounds.

Three qualifications matter because several formulas in Section 3 chain rounding steps. First, when a rule states that a value is raised to the next multiple of some unit, that step is a ceiling, not a rounding: a bottle price is lifted to the next whole dollar even when it is one cent below it, and an amount already sitting exactly on the multiple is left alone. Second, the order of a rounding chain is part of the specification: where a rule says to round a per-unit figure first and then multiply by a count, computing the product first and rounding once at the end is wrong. Third, one field is carried to four decimal places for audit purposes: `raw_share` on an allocation claim, which records a claim's unrounded proportional share of an offer, is persisted rounded half up to four decimals while the awarding arithmetic itself uses the exact rational value.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Offers and Draft List Versions

Two write operations in this system require the client to prove it is working from a current view of a parent record, because in both cases the conflicting operation is perfectly legitimate on its own and therefore cannot be caught by any state precondition.

Filing an allocation claim — `POST /beverage-program/allocation-offers/{offer_id}/claims` — requires the non-optional body field `expected_offer_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AllocationOffer`. If that value does not equal the offer's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second venue filing a claim against the same offer is normal product behaviour — the offer stays `OPEN` and the new claim would succeed — so the guard exists to stop a claimant from sizing its request against an offer whose claim population it has not observed.

Adding an entry to a draft list version — `POST /beverage-program/list-versions/{list_version_id}/entries` — requires the non-optional body field `expected_list_version_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ListVersion`. If that value does not equal the version's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adding a second entry to the same `DRAFT` version is likewise legitimate on its own, so the stale-version check is the only deterministic guard against a writer building entries on an outdated view of the version's contents.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where a client needs the current value, single-resource reads of an allocation offer and of a list version both expose `version` for exactly this purpose.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Validation

Path identifiers are resolved left to right. A request that names a venue and then a bin within it fails on the venue first if neither exists, and the error names the leftmost unresolved segment. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after all path identifiers, in the order the body fields are listed for that operation. The not-found catalogue is per resource type: `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPLETION_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINE_LIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND` — each HTTP 404. A missing wine behind `predecessor_wine_id` reports `ERR_WINE_NOT_FOUND`; a list entry named by a pour-plan line that does not exist reports `ERR_LIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`; a `source_claim_id` filter naming no claim reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

Because many operations in this system can fail several ways at once, the order in which checks run is itself part of the contract. Checks are evaluated strictly in the following sequence, and the first failure is the one reported:

1. Both headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The role is authorised for this operation — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. For every `VenueSommelier` operation, `X-User-ID` matches the venue the request resolves to — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
5. For posting and for listing allocation offers, a `DistributorRep`'s `X-User-ID` matches the `distributor_id` in the body or query — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
6. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found error.
7. Every foreign key in the body resolves, in declared body order — otherwise the matching not-found error.
8. Required body fields are present and carry the declared JSON type — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
9. Enum-typed fields carry a declared member value — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422).
10. Numeric fields respect their declared ranges — otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). This covers `offered_bottles` of at least 1, `seat_count` between 1 and 400 inclusive, `priority_rank` between 1 and 5 inclusive, `slot_index` between 1 and 24 inclusive, `pour_size_ml` between 25 and 500 inclusive, every cost-of-goods target percentage between 5.0 and 60.0 inclusive, and the requirement that `cogs_target_pct_by_category` carry exactly the six wine-category keys.
11. `limit` and `offset` are valid on list endpoints — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
12. A list entry marked for the by-the-glass rotation supplies a pour size — otherwise `ERR_POUR_SIZE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400).
13. An event's window opens strictly before it closes — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). A reporting period likewise starts strictly before it ends — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (HTTP 422).
14. Uniqueness: `venue_code` across all venues (`ERR_VENUE_CODE_TAKEN`), `bin_code` within its venue (`ERR_BIN_CODE_TAKEN`), one claim per venue per offer (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`), one entry per wine per list version (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LIST_ENTRY_WINE`) — each HTTP 409.
15. The version guards of Section 2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
16. State preconditions on the target record, in the order they are introduced in Section 3: list-version state, offer fulfilment state, claim state, lot state, slot state, write-off state, event state, investigation state, the revision-round ceiling, and the non-empty-version requirement.
17. Segregation of duties — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
18. Compliance safety: distributor hold, offer block, prohibited by-your-own-bottle category.
19. Temporal safety: offer deadline, receipt not in the future, slot occupancy, event-window conflict.
20. Referential consistency between the records a request joins: bin and lot venue agreement, slot wine agreement, by-the-glass eligibility, pour-plan membership, slot write-off shape, pinned-version freshness.
21. Remaining value constraints: reserve-zone eligibility, bin capacity, request against offered pool, eligible claims, minimum saleable pours, pours against bottle, by-your-own-bottle limit.
22. Fiscal gates: frozen posture, restricted posture against price band, sufficient bottles, and the requirement that a reporting period contain revenue.

Section 3 states the error identifier for each of these checks alongside the workflow it guards. The relative order above is what disambiguates a request that violates two rules at once: a `VenueSommelier` reaching into another venue's cellar hears about scope before it hears that the bin identifier does not exist, and a stale version is reported before any state precondition is examined.

### 2.9. Conservation of Bottles and of Money

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and the invariants over them are the reason the ledger is append-only.

The first is bottles. A lot's `bottles_received` is written once at receipt and never changes. Its `bottles_on_hand` is always `bottles_received` reduced by the signed sum of ledger movements against that lot, and it never goes negative. Its `bottles_committed` counts the by-the-glass slots holding an opened bottle from that lot, so the bottles a lot can actually give up are `bottles_on_hand` less `bottles_committed`. Every operation that removes bottles has a compensating counterpart that returns exactly the same bottles to exactly the same lot: reversing an approved write-off restores its bottles, and voiding a settlement returns every drawn bottle to the lot it came from.

The second is money. A venue carries two signed running sums, `cogs_ledger_cents` and `revenue_ledger_cents`, each the sum of the corresponding signed field over every depletion entry booked against that venue, across every reason. Both start at 0 when the venue is created. A compensating entry carries the exact negation of the entry it offsets, so a write-off approval followed by its reversal, or a settlement followed by its void, leaves both ledgers at their original values.

A single operation in this system frequently moves several records at once, and the whole cascade is part of that operation. When a lot runs dry, its bin's stored count, its bin's utilisation and possibly its bin's state all change, and the list entries that depended on that wine may flip out of stock, which in turn changes counters on the list versions those entries belong to. Section 3 states each of these cascades where the operation that triggers it is described. Whenever a resource changes as part of such a cascade, its own `updated_at` is refreshed and its own `version` increments by 1 — a parent whose counters move because a child was written is itself a mutation.

### 2.10. Fiscal Posture

Each venue carries a derived gate called `program_posture`, taking one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, and initialised to `OPEN` when the venue is registered. Posture is recomputed from two inputs: the venue's `last_variance_pct`, which is the pour-cost spread produced by its most recent variance run and starts at 0.00, and whether any variance investigation for that venue currently sits in the `ESCALATED` state.

The posture is `FROZEN` when `last_variance_pct` is strictly greater than 6.00 percentage points, or when at least one variance investigation for the venue is `ESCALATED`. It is `RESTRICTED` when `last_variance_pct` is strictly greater than 3.00 and at most 6.00 and no investigation is escalated. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. The boundaries belong to the lower band: a spread of exactly 3.00 leaves the venue `OPEN`, and a spread of exactly 6.00 leaves it `RESTRICTED`.

Posture gates three things. A `FROZEN` venue cannot have a list version published and cannot have a wine dinner confirmed; both are refused with `ERR_VENUE_PROGRAM_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A venue that is either `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` cannot accept a new list entry whose computed price band lands in the top two bands; that refusal is `ERR_VENUE_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) and it is evaluated only after the entry's price has been computed, because the band is an output of the pricing rule rather than an input to it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Venues, Cellar Geography and the Wine Catalogue

Before anything can be listed, poured or costed, a `BeverageDirector` registers the venue. Registration takes the restaurant's `name` (1 to 120 characters) and a short operator code `venue_code` (1 to 12 characters) that must be unique across the whole group; a collision is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CODE_TAKEN`. The commercially significant part of registration is `cogs_target_pct_by_category`, an object whose keys must be exactly the six wine categories the group trades in — `SPARKLING`, `WHITE`, `ROSE`, `RED`, `DESSERT` and `FORTIFIED` — and whose values are each a percentage between 5.0 and 60.0 inclusive. These targets are the anchor of every list price the venue prints. A separate `event_cogs_target_pct`, defaulting to 30.0 and likewise bounded to 5.0 through 60.0, is used for wine-dinner ticket pricing instead of the per-category targets.

Registration also fixes how the venue accounts for the cost of a bottle leaving the cellar. `costing_method` defaults to `FIFO` and may instead be `WEIGHTED_AVERAGE`; Section 3.7 explains what each choice does to the numbers. Four fields configure the venue's corkage policy and are quoted, never charged, by the corkage capability described in Section 3.9: `corkage_fee_cents` is the per-bottle fee before any waiver or discount and defaults to 3500 (that is, thirty-five dollars) with 0 permitted; `corkage_waiver_purchase_ratio` is how many bottles a party must buy off the list to earn one corkage waiver and defaults to 2, with a minimum of 1; `byo_bottle_limit_per_party` caps how many outside bottles one party may bring and defaults to 2, with 0 permitted; and `byo_prohibited_categories` is a possibly empty array — the default — of wine categories the venue simply refuses. Finally `priority_rank`, an integer from 1 to 5 defaulting to 3 where a lower number means higher priority, is the venue's standing in the allocation queue and appears again in Section 3.3. On creation the venue's `program_posture` starts at `OPEN`, its `last_variance_pct` at 0.00, its `last_pour_cost_run_at` at null, and both `cogs_ledger_cents` and `revenue_ledger_cents` at 0. The venue collection may be filtered by `program_posture` and by `costing_method`.

Within a venue, physical storage is modelled as cellar bins, which a `VenueSommelier` or a `BeverageDirector` may create. A bin carries a `bin_code` of 1 to 12 characters that must be unique within its venue — a repeat is `ERR_BIN_CODE_TAKEN` — a `zone` of `CELLAR`, `BACKBAR`, `RESERVE` or `OFFSITE`, a hard `capacity_bottles` of at least 1, and a reporting flag `temperature_controlled` that defaults to true. Three computed fields describe occupancy: `bottles_stored` is the sum of `bottles_on_hand` across the lots sitting in this bin whose state is `ON_HAND` and starts at 0; `utilization_pct` is `bottles_stored` multiplied by 100, divided by `capacity_bottles`, rounded half up to two decimals, and starts at 0.00; and `bin_state` is `FULL` when `bottles_stored` equals `capacity_bottles` exactly and `ACTIVE` in every other case, starting at `ACTIVE`. The `RESERVE` zone is reserved in the literal sense: only a wine flagged as distributor-allocated may be stored there, and routing a non-allocated wine into a reserve bin is refused with `ERR_BIN_ZONE_MISMATCH`. Bins may be listed filtered by `venue_id`, by `zone` and by `bin_state`.

The catalogue itself is group-wide and director-owned. A wine record carries a `producer` and a `cuvee` (each 1 to 120 characters), a `vintage_year` between 1900 and 2100, a `category` drawn from the six above, a `bottle_size_ml` between 187 and 6000 defaulting to 750, a `reference_cost_cents` of at least 1, a `case_pack` between 1 and 24 defaulting to 6, and a boolean `is_allocated` defaulting to false. Two points about these fields are easy to get wrong and matter everywhere downstream. `reference_cost_cents` is the catalogue replacement cost of a bottle and is the *only* cost that feeds list pricing — the landed cost recorded on a physical lot never influences a printed price. And a vintage change is never an edit: a new vintage of an existing wine is a new catalogue row that names the previous row in `predecessor_wine_id`, which is otherwise null. Each row also carries `successor_count`, starting at 0 and counting how many catalogue rows name this one as their predecessor. Section 3.6 covers what happens to live lists when that count moves. The catalogue may be filtered by `category`, by `is_allocated` and by `predecessor_wine_id`, the last of which exposes a vintage lineage.

Distributors are registered by the director too, with a legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `license_number` of 1 to 32 characters, an `allocation_tier` of `A`, `B` or `C` defaulting to `B`, and a scorecard figure `on_time_delivery_pct` between 0.0 and 100.0 defaulting to 95.0. The tier and the scorecard are relationship-management reporting only and have no effect on allocation arithmetic. What does matter is `compliance_state`, which is initialised to `PENDING_REVIEW` and is not settable at registration, and `last_review_note`, which is null until the first review. Distributors may be listed filtered by `allocation_tier` and by `compliance_state`.

### 3.2. Licence Standing and the Two Independent Dimensions of an Allocation Offer

Licence clearance belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` alone. A review supplies a `decision` of either `CLEARED` or `HOLD` together with an optional `note`, which is stored as the distributor's `last_review_note`, and returns the updated distributor with HTTP 200. A decision of `CLEARED` moves a distributor `PENDING_REVIEW → CLEARED` or `HOLD → CLEARED`; a decision of `HOLD` moves it `PENDING_REVIEW → HOLD` or `CLEARED → HOLD`. Because this machine cycles, a distributor may be held and cleared repeatedly over its lifetime and has no terminal state.

An allocation offer is posted by the `DistributorRep` of the offering house and names its own `distributor_id`, the `wine_id` on offer, an `offered_bottles` pool of at least 1, a landed `unit_cost_cents` of at least 1 that will be stamped on every lot created from this offer, and a `response_deadline` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. Posting an offer for a distributor whose licence standing is `HOLD` is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 422). On creation, `unallocated_bottles` is set to the full `offered_bottles`, `claim_count` to 0, and `allocated_at` to null.

An offer is the one record in this system that carries two state dimensions at once, and they never move each other. `fulfillment_state` tracks the commercial lifecycle and is initialised to `OPEN`: claims may be filed while it is `OPEN`, it becomes `ALLOCATED` once the waterfall has run, and it becomes `CLOSED` once every granted claim has been received or withdrawn. `compliance_state` tracks the licence dimension and is initialised to `PENDING_LICENSE_CHECK`, with one exception — when the offering distributor's standing is already `CLEARED` at the moment the offer is posted, the offer's `compliance_state` is set to `CLEARED` at creation. Thereafter the compliance dimension is moved only by distributor reviews, and a review moves it regardless of where the fulfilment dimension currently sits.

The cascade from a review is precise. When a review leaves the distributor in `HOLD`, every offer of that distributor whose compliance dimension reads `PENDING_LICENSE_CHECK` moves `PENDING_LICENSE_CHECK → BLOCKED` and every offer reading `CLEARED` moves `CLEARED → BLOCKED`; each such offer's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, and no offer's `fulfillment_state` changes — an offer that has already allocated its bottles stays `ALLOCATED` while becoming `BLOCKED`. When a review leaves the distributor in `CLEARED`, every offer of that distributor whose compliance dimension reads `BLOCKED` moves `BLOCKED → CLEARED`, and every offer reading `PENDING_LICENSE_CHECK` moves `PENDING_LICENSE_CHECK → CLEARED`, again with `version` incremented by 1 and again without touching the fulfilment dimension. Offers may be listed filtered by `distributor_id`, by `wine_id`, by `fulfillment_state` and by `compliance_state`, and the two state filters are independent of each other. The single-offer read exposes both dimensions together with the offer's `version`, which a claimant needs for the guard in Section 2.7.

### 3.3. Competing for Allocation, Awarding the Pool and Receiving Bottles

Consider a scarce Burgundy released to the group in a pool of nineteen bottles while four venues each want ten. That is the situation the claim-and-waterfall workflow exists to resolve. A `VenueSommelier` files a claim by naming its own `venue_id`, the `requested_bottles` it wants (at least 1) and the `expected_offer_version` guard described in Section 2.7. A claim is refused when the offer's fulfilment dimension is anything other than `OPEN` (`ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`, HTTP 409); when this venue already holds a claim on this offer in *any* state, including a withdrawn or rejected one (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, HTTP 409); when `requested_bottles` exceeds the offer's `offered_bottles` (`ERR_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_OFFER`, HTTP 422); when the logical clock has reached or passed the offer's `response_deadline`, so that only an instant strictly earlier than the deadline is accepted (`ERR_OFFER_DEADLINE_PASSED`, HTTP 422); and when the distributor behind the offer stands in `HOLD` (`ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, HTTP 422). A successful claim is created in state `REQUESTED` with `granted_bottles` 0, `raw_share` 0.0000 and `remainder_awarded` false, and it increments the parent offer's `claim_count` by 1 and the offer's `version` by 1 — which is precisely why the next claimant's guard value must be re-read.

Awarding is a director-only act, takes no request body, and drives the offer from `OPEN` to `ALLOCATED`. It is refused when the fulfilment dimension is not `OPEN` (`ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`), when the offer's compliance dimension reads `BLOCKED` (`ERR_OFFER_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`, HTTP 422), and when no claim on the offer sits in `REQUESTED` (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLAIMS`, HTTP 422). Only claims in `REQUESTED` enter the division; claims already withdrawn, rejected, granted, partially granted or received never re-enter a waterfall.

The division itself works as follows. Let the pool be the offer's `offered_bottles` and the total demand be the sum of `requested_bottles` over the participating claims. When total demand is less than or equal to the pool, every claim is granted exactly what it asked for and the offer's `unallocated_bottles` becomes the pool less the total demand. When total demand exceeds the pool, each claim's proportional share is the pool multiplied by that claim's `requested_bottles` and divided by the total demand, evaluated as an exact rational number; that share is persisted as `raw_share`, rounded half up to four decimal places, for audit only, while the arithmetic below uses the exact value. Each claim is first given the whole-number part of its share, discarding the fraction — the floor is taken *before* anything is summed. The bottles left over, being the pool less the sum of those whole-number parts, are then handed out one bottle at a time to claims ordered by the discarded fraction descending, then by the claiming venue's `priority_rank` ascending so that rank 1 outranks rank 5, then by the claim's `created_at` ascending; identifiers are never used as a tie-break. A claim that receives one of those bottles has `remainder_awarded` set to true. No claim may end up with more than it asked for, so any award is capped at that claim's `requested_bottles`, and bottles freed by such a cap are handed out again by repeating the same ordered pass over the claims that still hold less than they requested. When the dust settles, the offer's `unallocated_bottles` is the pool less the sum of all awards. Rounding each proportional share half up and then summing the results is explicitly **not** the rule; it produces a different award vector whenever a discarded fraction is exactly one half.

Each participating claim then lands in one of three states according to its award: `REQUESTED → GRANTED` when the award equals `requested_bottles`, `REQUESTED → PARTIALLY_GRANTED` when the award is greater than zero and less than `requested_bottles`, and `REQUESTED → REJECTED` when the award is zero, in which case `granted_bottles` is left at 0. Alongside the claim writes, the offer records `allocated_at` as the logical clock, recomputes `unallocated_bottles`, and increments its own `version` by 1. The compliance dimension is untouched by awarding.

A venue may drop out. Withdrawing is available to the claiming `VenueSommelier` and only while the claim is in `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`; anything else is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE` (HTTP 409). A withdrawal from `REQUESTED` simply moves `REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN`. A withdrawal from `GRANTED` or from `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` first returns the claim's `granted_bottles` to the offer's `unallocated_bottles` and then zeroes `granted_bottles`, so the offered pool is conserved exactly. In all three cases the offer's `claim_count` decrements by 1 and its `version` increments by 1. When a withdrawal leaves an `ALLOCATED` offer with no claim at all in `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, the offer moves `ALLOCATED → CLOSED` and its `version` increments again for that transition.

Taking delivery is the point at which a paper award becomes physical stock. The claiming sommelier supplies the `bin_id` the bottles are going into and the `received_at` instant of the delivery, and the operation returns the newly created lot with HTTP 201. Six checks guard it: the claim must be in `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED` (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_GRANTED`, HTTP 409); the offer's compliance dimension must not be `BLOCKED` (`ERR_OFFER_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`); the distributor must not stand in `HOLD` (`ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`); `received_at` must not be later than the logical clock (`ERR_RECEIVED_AT_IN_FUTURE`, HTTP 422); the nominated bin must belong to the claiming venue (`ERR_LOT_VENUE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422); the bin's zone must not be `RESERVE` unless the wine is flagged allocated (`ERR_BIN_ZONE_MISMATCH`); and the bin's `bottles_stored` plus the incoming bottles must not exceed its `capacity_bottles` (`ERR_BIN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409). On success the claim moves `GRANTED → RECEIVED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED → RECEIVED` — the two branches are identical in both their guards and their effects — and a new lot appears carrying the claim's venue, the offer's wine, `source_claim_id` set to the claim, `bottles_received` and `bottles_on_hand` both equal to `granted_bottles`, `bottles_committed` 0, `unit_cost_cents` copied from the offer, and state `ON_HAND`. The receiving bin's `bottles_stored` rises by that count, its `utilization_pct` is recomputed, and it becomes `FULL` if stored bottles now equal capacity. Every list entry for that venue-and-wine pair has its `availability_state` recomputed, and each owning list version's `out_of_stock_entry_count` is rewritten to match. And if this receipt leaves the offer with no claim in `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, the offer moves `ALLOCATED → CLOSED`.

Not every bottle arrives through an allocation. A sommelier may also record a direct purchase receipt, naming the `venue_id`, the `wine_id`, the destination `bin_id`, the `bottles_received` count of at least 1, the landed `unit_cost_cents` of at least 1 and the `received_at` instant. The same future-receipt, venue-agreement, reserve-zone and capacity checks apply and carry the same error identifiers. The resulting lot has `bottles_on_hand` equal to `bottles_received`, `bottles_committed` 0, state `ON_HAND` and `source_claim_id` left null, which is what distinguishes a direct buy from an allocation receipt. A direct receipt also has the pleasant side effect of restocking the list: every list entry for that venue-and-wine pair whose `availability_state` reads `OUT_OF_STOCK` returns to `AVAILABLE`, decrementing its version's `out_of_stock_entry_count`.

Claims may be listed filtered by `offer_id`, by `venue_id` and by `state`; the single-claim read exposes `granted_bottles` and `raw_share`. Lots may be listed filtered by `venue_id`, `wine_id`, `bin_id`, `source_claim_id` and `state`, and they come back in `received_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, which is the order in which they will be consumed.

Every movement of stock out of a lot writes one immutable ledger entry, and Section 3.7 onwards refers to these constantly. A ledger entry records the `venue_id` whose ledgers it moves, the `wine_id` depleted, the `lot_id` the bottles came out of, and an `occurred_at` instant taken from the logical clock which is the field every reporting period filters on. It carries a `reason` from the set `SALE_BTG`, `SALE_BOTTLE`, `EVENT`, `SPOILAGE`, `STAFF_EDUCATION` and `REVERSAL`; a signed whole-bottle `bottles` movement, negative on a reversal; a signed `pours` count that is 0 for whole-bottle movements; and the signed `cogs_cents` and `revenue_cents` the movement recognises. Four nullable references say what caused the entry: `list_entry_id` is set for by-the-glass and bottle sales, `btg_slot_id` for slot closures, `event_id` for event settlements and their voids, and `write_off_id` for write-off approvals and reversals. Entries may be read individually or listed filtered by `venue_id`, `wine_id`, `lot_id`, `list_entry_id`, `btg_slot_id`, `event_id`, `write_off_id` and `reason`, ordered by `occurred_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. Reversals always appear as additional negative-value entries; an existing entry is never edited or deleted.

### 3.4. Composing a Wine List: Drafts, Entries and the Pricing Rules

A wine list is a container, not a document. Either a `BeverageDirector` or a `VenueSommelier` creates one inside a venue with nothing more than a `name` of 1 to 80 characters — "Dinner List", "Reserve Book" — and the container then owns an ordered chain of versions. Two counters live on the container: `next_version_no`, initialised to 1, which is the sequence number the next version created under this list will take; and `published_version_no`, initialised to null, which holds the `version_no` of whichever version is currently live. At most one version per list is live at any moment, meaning at most one sits in `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING`. Lists may be filtered by `venue_id`.

Opening a new draft is likewise available to the sommelier and the director. The only body field is the optional `supersedes_version_id`, naming the live version this draft is intended to replace; when supplied it must belong to the same wine list, and the reference is merely recorded — the predecessor is not superseded until this draft is actually published. The new version takes its `version_no` from the container's `next_version_no` and then advances that counter by 1. It starts in state `DRAFT` with `entry_count`, `stale_entry_count`, `out_of_stock_entry_count` and `revision_round` all 0, `reprint_required` false, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `published_by_user_id`, `published_at` and `printed_at` all null. Versions may be listed filtered by `wine_list_id`, by `supersedes_version_id` — which exposes the supersession chain — and by `state`. The single-version read exposes the version's `version` integer, which an entry writer needs for the guard in Section 2.7.

Adding a wine to a draft is a sommelier's job and is the single richest computation in the system. The body names the `wine_id`, a `sort_rank` of at least 1 giving the print order of the entry within its category block, and the `expected_list_version_version` guard. Four optional fields shape the listing: `by_the_glass`, defaulting to false, says whether the wine joins the by-the-glass rotation; `pour_size_ml`, between 25 and 500, is the glass pour and is mandatory whenever `by_the_glass` is true and otherwise null; `par_bottles`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, is the target bottle count on hand for this listing; `tasting_note`, up to 500 characters and permitted to be empty, is the staff-education note printed on the list; and `education_verified`, defaulting to false, records whether floor staff have been signed off on the wine. Entries may be added only while the owning version is in `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409) — and a wine may appear at most once per version, a repeat being `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIST_ENTRY_WINE` (HTTP 409). Marking an entry for the glass without a pour size is `ERR_POUR_SIZE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400).

The bottle price is derived from the wine's catalogue cost in a fixed sequence, and it is frozen onto the entry as `bottle_price_cents` at creation. Start from the wine's `reference_cost_cents`. Select a markup multiplier from the cost: 3.50 when the cost is at most 2000 cents, 3.00 when it is above 2000 and at most 6000, 2.60 when it is above 6000 and at most 15000, and 2.20 when it is above 15000 — every break belongs to the band below it, so a cost of exactly 6000 cents takes the 3.00 multiplier. Multiply the cost by that multiplier and round half up to whole cents; call this the markup candidate. Independently, take the venue's target percentage for this wine's category out of `cogs_target_pct_by_category`, multiply the cost by 100, divide by that percentage and round half up to whole cents; call this the target candidate. Take the larger of the two candidates. Multiply the winner by 1.15 when the wine is flagged allocated and by 1.00 when it is not, then round half up to whole cents. Finally raise the result to the next whole multiple of 100 cents — that is, to the next whole dollar, leaving an amount already exactly on a dollar untouched. The outcome is `bottle_price_cents`.

That price then fixes the entry's `price_band`: `VALUE` at 6000 cents or below, `CORE` above 6000 and at most 12000, `PREMIUM` above 12000 and at most 25000, and `RESERVE` above 25000. Once the band is known, the fiscal gate of Section 2.10 applies — a venue whose posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` refuses a new entry landing in `PREMIUM` or `RESERVE` with `ERR_VENUE_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`. The ordering here is deliberate: the price is computed first and the gate is applied to its output.

When the entry joins the by-the-glass rotation, two further fields are computed. Divide the wine's `bottle_size_ml` by the entry's `pour_size_ml` and discard any fraction to get the gross pour count, then subtract one pour as the spoilage allowance every open bottle concedes; the remainder is `effective_pours`. A pour size so large that this leaves fewer than one saleable glass is refused with `ERR_POUR_SIZE_TOO_LARGE` (HTTP 422). The glass price then follows from the same category target: multiply the wine's `reference_cost_cents` by 100, divide by the venue's category target percentage, divide again by `effective_pours`, round that half up to whole cents, and finally raise the result to the next whole multiple of 25 cents. The order is pinned — round to the cent first, then lift to the quarter — and the outcome is `glass_price_cents`. When `by_the_glass` is false, both `effective_pours` and `glass_price_cents` are null.

Three more fields are settled at creation. `availability_state` reads `AVAILABLE` when at least one lot of the version's venue and this wine sits in state `ON_HAND` with bottles still on hand, and `OUT_OF_STOCK` otherwise; the default is `OUT_OF_STOCK`, and an entry created out of stock increments its version's `out_of_stock_entry_count`. `vintage_stale` is set to true when the wine already has a successor vintage in the catalogue — that is, when its `successor_count` is greater than 0 — and each such entry increments its version's `stale_entry_count`. And `contest_points` is computed from the ledger, which for a brand-new entry means a score of 0.00 unless staff education has already been signed off. Adding an entry increments the parent version's `entry_count` by 1 and its `version` by 1.

The sales-contest score is worth stating in full, because it is recomputed every single time a ledger entry naming this listing is written. Gather every ledger entry whose `list_entry_id` is this entry, taking the signed values so that reversals subtract. Add up the `pours` on entries whose reason is `SALE_BTG` to get pours sold, and the `bottles` on entries whose reason is `SALE_BOTTLE` to get bottles sold. Add up `cogs_cents` across all of the entries and `revenue_cents` across all of them. When total revenue is greater than zero, express realised cost of goods as a percentage — total cost multiplied by 100, divided by total revenue, rounded half up to two decimals — then take the venue's category target for this wine, subtract the realised percentage from it, divide the difference by that same target, clamp the result to no less than 0.0 and no more than 1.0, and round half up to four decimals; that clamped figure is the margin factor. When total revenue is zero or negative, the margin factor is 0.0. The score is then one point per pour sold, plus three points per bottle sold, plus five points when `education_verified` is true and nothing when it is false, plus ten points multiplied by the margin factor, with the total rounded half up to two decimals.

Editing an entry is permitted only while its version is still `DRAFT`; otherwise `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. The editable fields are `by_the_glass`, `pour_size_ml`, `tasting_note`, `education_verified`, `par_bottles` and `sort_rank`. Changing either of the first two re-runs the glass-price computation — including the mandatory-pour-size and minimum-saleable-pours checks, which carry the same two error identifiers as at creation — and when `by_the_glass` becomes false both `effective_pours` and `glass_price_cents` are cleared to null. Changing `education_verified` re-runs the contest score. What editing never does is re-price the bottle: `bottle_price_cents` and `price_band` are frozen at creation and no patch moves them. Entries may be listed filtered by `list_version_id`, `wine_id`, `price_band`, `by_the_glass` and `availability_state`.

### 3.5. Review, Publication and the Supersession Chain

A list version travels `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → PUBLISHED` in the happy case, and the machine is deliberately cyclic in two places. Its terminal states are `SUPERSEDED` and `ARCHIVED`; a version in either accepts nothing further.

Submitting a draft for review is shared between the `VenueSommelier` and the `BeverageDirector`. It requires the version to be in `DRAFT` (`ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`) and to carry at least one entry, an empty version being refused with `ERR_EMPTY_LIST_VERSION` (HTTP 422). On success the version moves `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, `submitted_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, overwriting whatever a previous submission recorded, and the version's own `version` increments by 1.

The director may bounce it back. Requesting changes is director-exclusive, carries a mandatory `note`, requires the version to be in `IN_REVIEW` (`ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, HTTP 409), and requires `revision_round` to be strictly less than three. A fourth round is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The version moves `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, `revision_round` increments by 1, and `submitted_by_user_id` is cleared back to null — which is what makes the segregation-of-duties check on the next publication attempt meaningful. This `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` loop is the first of the two cycles in this machine, and entries become editable again for as long as it lasts.

Publication is director-exclusive and carries no body. Three preconditions guard it, and their order matters. The version must be in `IN_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — a director who both submits and tries to publish is blocked by identity, not by role. And the venue behind this version, reached through the version's wine list, must not be in `FROZEN` posture, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_PROGRAM_FROZEN`.

Publication does five things. The version moves `IN_REVIEW → PUBLISHED`. It stamps `published_at` and `printed_at` with the logical clock and `published_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. The owning container's `published_version_no` is set to this version's `version_no`. Every entry on this version has its `availability_state` recomputed against current lot data, and the version's `out_of_stock_entry_count` is rewritten from that recount. And when this draft named a `supersedes_version_id`, the version it named moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` if it was live, or `REPRINT_PENDING → SUPERSEDED` if it was awaiting a reprint; either transition sets `pinned_version_stale` to true on every wine dinner event in state `PLANNED`, `PRICED` or `CONFIRMED` that pins the superseded version, which is what eventually blocks that event's settlement.

A draft that will never see print is abandoned rather than deleted. Archiving is director-exclusive, requires the version to be in `DRAFT`, moves it `DRAFT → ARCHIVED`, and increments the version's `version` by 1. An archived version is terminal and accepts no further entry writes.

### 3.6. Vintage Changes, Staleness and Reprints

A vintage change begins in the catalogue, not on the list. When the director registers a new wine naming an existing one in `predecessor_wine_id`, that predecessor's `successor_count` increments by 1 — and then the consequences fan out. Every list entry referencing the predecessor wine, on any version whose state is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING`, has `vintage_stale` set to true. Each affected version recounts its `stale_entry_count` and rewrites `reprint_required`, which is true exactly when the stale count has reached two entries and false below that. A version that is currently `PUBLISHED` and whose `reprint_required` has just become true moves `PUBLISHED → REPRINT_PENDING`, and that transition sets `pinned_version_stale` to true on every wine dinner event in state `PLANNED`, `PRICED` or `CONFIRMED` that pins it. One superseded vintage on a live list is therefore tolerated as an errata; the second forces a reprint.

A version sitting in `REPRINT_PENDING` is still live for every purpose except the freshness of its printed paper: it still counts as the container's published version, slots and events may still be created against it, and it may still be superseded. What it needs is an acknowledgement that the new paper has been printed, which either the sommelier or the director may give. That acknowledgement requires the version to be in `REPRINT_PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_REPRINT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) — and moves it `REPRINT_PENDING → PUBLISHED`. It stamps `printed_at` with the logical clock, clears `vintage_stale` to false on every entry of the version, resets `stale_entry_count` to 0 and sets `reprint_required` back to false. This `PUBLISHED → REPRINT_PENDING → PUBLISHED` loop is the second cycle in the list-version machine, and a list may go round it as often as the catalogue changes.

One thing a reprint acknowledgement deliberately does **not** do is un-stale the events. Any wine dinner whose `pinned_version_stale` was already set to true keeps that flag, and its settlement stays blocked, even after the list itself returns to `PUBLISHED`. The pin recorded a promise about a specific printed list at a specific moment; reprinting the list does not restore that promise.

### 3.7. The By-the-Glass Rail: Opening, Preserving and Closing a Bottle

Every by-the-glass listing is poured from a physical bottle standing in a numbered position on a preservation rail, and the system models that position as a slot. A `VenueSommelier` opens one by naming the `venue_id`, the `list_entry_id` being poured, the `lot_id` the bottle is drawn from, the `slot_index` between 1 and 24 identifying the rail position, and the `preservation_method`, which is one of `ARGON`, `VACUUM`, `COOLER` or `NONE`.

Five referential and safety checks guard the open. The list version behind the entry must be live, meaning in `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING`; anything else is `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). The nominated lot must be `ON_HAND`; a depleted or written-off lot is `ERR_LOT_NOT_ON_HAND` (HTTP 409). That lot must hold the same wine as the list entry and belong to the same venue as the slot, or the request is `ERR_SLOT_WINE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The list entry must actually be in the rotation, since pouring a bottle-only listing by the glass makes no sense: `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_BY_THE_GLASS` (HTTP 422). And the lot must have a bottle to give — its `bottles_on_hand` less its `bottles_committed` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOTTLES` (HTTP 409).

Rail positions are exclusive, and the geometry of that exclusivity is specific. A slot occupies its position from its `opened_at` instant through its `preservation_expires_at` instant, with **both endpoints inside** the occupied interval and no buffer on either side. When a new slot is requested, the system looks at the slots on the same venue and the same `slot_index` whose state is `OPEN` or `EXPIRED`. If any of them has an `opened_at` at or before the current clock instant and a `preservation_expires_at` at or after that same instant, the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OCCUPIED` (HTTP 409). Adjacency therefore collides: a request at an instant exactly equal to an occupant's expiry is rejected, while one millisecond later is accepted, and all comparisons are made at whole-millisecond granularity. When no occupant covers the current instant, any occupant still sitting in `OPEN` whose expiry the clock has passed is swept `OPEN → EXPIRED` as part of accepting the new slot; that sweep writes no ledger entry and leaves its stale bottle still committed to the abandoned slot, where it stays until a write-off resolves it.

Preservation life is a function of the grape and the gadget. Each wine category has a base window measured in hours: 24 for `SPARKLING`, 72 for `WHITE`, 72 for `ROSE`, 96 for `RED`, 168 for `DESSERT` and 336 for `FORTIFIED`. Each preservation method multiplies that base: argon blanketing doubles it at a factor of 2.0, vacuum stoppering leaves it unchanged at 1.0, refrigeration extends it by half at 1.5, and leaving the bottle unprotected halves it at 0.5. Multiply base by factor, discard any fraction of an hour, and cap the result at 720 hours — thirty days — whichever is smaller. `opened_at` is the logical clock at creation and `preservation_expires_at` is `opened_at` advanced by that many hours.

Opening a slot also freezes what the bottle is expected to yield: the slot's own `effective_pours` is copied from the list entry's `effective_pours` at open time, so a later edit to the entry's pour size cannot move the target of a bottle already standing on the rail. The slot starts in state `OPEN` with `pours_sold`, `staff_education_pours` and `spoiled_pours` all 0. On the inventory side the lot's `bottles_committed` rises by 1 while its `bottles_on_hand` is deliberately left alone — the bottle is spoken for but has not yet left the cellar's books. Slots may be listed filtered by `venue_id`, `list_entry_id`, `lot_id` and `state`.

Closing the slot is where the money lands. The sommelier submits `pours_sold` and, optionally, `staff_education_pours`. The slot must be in `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409), and the two submitted pour counts together must not exceed the slot's frozen `effective_pours`, otherwise `ERR_POURS_EXCEED_BOTTLE` (HTTP 422). Which branch the close takes is decided by the clock, not by the caller. When the logical clock is at or before `preservation_expires_at`, the slot moves `OPEN → CLOSED` and the bottle is accounted for. When the clock is strictly after `preservation_expires_at`, the slot instead moves `OPEN → EXPIRED`: the submitted pour counts are recorded as submitted, `spoiled_pours` is set to the slot's full `effective_pours`, and — this is the important divergence — **no ledger entry is written, no revenue is recognised, and neither venue ledger moves.** The lot's `bottles_committed` is also left untouched, so the bottle remains reserved against that slot until somebody writes it off.

On the `OPEN → CLOSED` branch, `spoiled_pours` is set to the slot's `effective_pours` less `pours_sold` less `staff_education_pours`, and the cost of the bottle is split three ways. First, price exactly one bottle out of the slot's lot using the venue's costing rule described below; call that the bottle cost. Divide the bottle cost by the slot's `effective_pours` and round half up to whole cents to get a cost per pour. The sale bucket takes the cost per pour multiplied by `pours_sold`; the education bucket takes the cost per pour multiplied by `staff_education_pours`; and the spoilage bucket takes whatever is left, being the bottle cost less the sale bucket less the education bucket. Rounding per pour happens first and the multiplication by the pour counts second, and the spoilage bucket absorbs the rounding drift, so the three bucket costs always sum to exactly the bottle cost. Revenue is `pours_sold` multiplied by the list entry's `glass_price_cents`.

The closure then appends one ledger entry per bucket, in the pinned order sale, then education, then spoilage, and a bucket whose pour count is zero produces no entry at all. The sale entry carries reason `SALE_BTG`, the whole-bottle movement of 1 in its `bottles`, `pours` equal to `pours_sold`, the sale bucket's cost and the computed revenue. The education entry carries reason `STAFF_EDUCATION`, a `bottles` of 0, `pours` equal to `staff_education_pours`, the education bucket's cost and zero revenue. The spoilage entry carries reason `SPOILAGE`, a `bottles` of 0, `pours` equal to `spoiled_pours`, the spoilage bucket's cost and zero revenue. All of them name the slot in `btg_slot_id` and the listing in `list_entry_id`. The lot's `bottles_committed` falls by 1 and its `bottles_on_hand` falls by 1; a lot that reaches zero on hand moves `ON_HAND → DEPLETED`, with the bin and availability cascade of Section 3.8. Every created entry's `cogs_cents` is added to the venue's `cogs_ledger_cents` and every `revenue_cents` to its `revenue_ledger_cents`. Finally the listing's `contest_points` is recomputed, because new ledger entries now name it.

How a bottle is costed depends on the venue's `costing_method`, and this is the one place where two venues with identical cellars and identical sales report different numbers. In both variants the candidate lots are those for the venue-and-wine pair whose state is `ON_HAND` and whose `bottles_on_hand` less `bottles_committed` is greater than zero; lots that are `DEPLETED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` are excluded from both. The candidates are ordered by `received_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and never by identifier. Under `FIFO`, walk that order taking from each lot the smaller of what it has available and what remains to be drawn, and the cost of goods is the sum over the lots touched of bottles taken from that lot multiplied by that lot's `unit_cost_cents`; no rounding is needed. Under `WEIGHTED_AVERAGE`, first compute a blended unit cost over the whole candidate set *before* any bottle is drawn — the sum over candidates of available bottles multiplied by that lot's `unit_cost_cents`, divided by the total available bottles, rounded half up to whole cents — and then charge the number of bottles consumed at that single blended unit. The physical draw still follows the same `received_at` ascending order in both variants; only the costing differs, so whenever the candidate lots disagree on `unit_cost_cents` the two methods produce different figures for the same draw. A slot closure and a write-off are both pinned to one explicit lot, so the candidate ordering only actually chooses lots when an event settlement draws across several.

### 3.8. Spoilage Write-Offs, the Lot Lifecycle and Compensating Reversals

A corked bottle, a broken case, a rail bottle that died overnight: all of them are losses, and no loss is booked without a controller's signature. Proposing a write-off is shared between the `VenueSommelier` and the `Controller`, which is deliberate — it is the only arrangement in which a controller can both propose and then attempt to approve, and therefore the only way the segregation-of-duties rule on this path is reachable within a single role. A proposal names the `venue_id` absorbing the loss, the `lot_id` being written down, the `bottles` count of at least 1, a `reason_code` from `CORKED`, `OXIDIZED`, `BREAKAGE`, `PRESERVATION_EXPIRY` and `TEMPERATURE`, a `narrative` of 1 to 500 characters retained for audit, and optionally a `btg_slot_id` when the loss is an expired preservation slot.

At proposal time the venue named must be the lot's own venue (`ERR_LOT_VENUE_MISMATCH`), the lot must be `ON_HAND` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_ON_HAND`), and the bottles proposed must not exceed the lot's `bottles_on_hand` less its `bottles_committed` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOTTLES`). When a `btg_slot_id` is supplied, all three of the following must hold or the proposal is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WRITE_OFF_INVALID` (HTTP 422): the named slot is in state `EXPIRED`, its `lot_id` is the same lot named in the body, and `bottles` is exactly 1. The write-off is created in state `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `cogs_cents` initialised to 0, and `approved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note` both null. Nothing moves in inventory or in either ledger until a controller acts. Write-offs may be listed filtered by `venue_id`, `lot_id`, `btg_slot_id` and `state`.

Approval is controller-exclusive and is the transitive cascade entry point of the whole system — money, inventory, bins, list availability and rail slots all move on one call. Four preconditions guard it, in this order: the write-off is in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409); the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the lot is `ON_HAND`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_ON_HAND`; and the bottles fit within what the lot can give up, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOTTLES`. That last check has a subtlety: the available count is the lot's `bottles_on_hand` less its `bottles_committed`, but when the write-off names a `btg_slot_id` one bottle is added back to that allowance, because the bottle being written off is precisely the one the expired slot is holding.

On approval the write-off moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and computes `cogs_cents` by pricing the written-off bottles out of that one nominated lot under the venue's costing method. One ledger entry is appended with reason `SPOILAGE`, a positive `bottles` equal to the write-off's bottle count, a positive `cogs_cents` equal to the figure just computed, zero `revenue_cents`, and `write_off_id` naming the write-off. Then the cascade: the lot's `bottles_on_hand` falls by that many bottles; the bin's `bottles_stored` falls by the same amount, its `utilization_pct` is recomputed and a bin that was `FULL` returns to `ACTIVE`; and the venue's `cogs_ledger_cents` rises by the recognised cost.

If the lot reaches zero bottles on hand it moves `ON_HAND → DEPLETED`, and depletion has its own consequences. The bin recomputes `bottles_stored`, `utilization_pct` and `bin_state`. And when no lot at all for that venue-and-wine pair is left in `ON_HAND` with bottles on hand, every list entry for that venue and wine on a version that is `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING` moves `availability_state` from `AVAILABLE` to `OUT_OF_STOCK`, which in turn increments each owning version's `out_of_stock_entry_count`. A depleted lot goes one step further and becomes terminally `WRITTEN_OFF` when the approval that emptied it carried a `reason_code` of `PRESERVATION_EXPIRY` or `TEMPERATURE`; the lot is then frozen and no further rail slot may reference it, an attempt being `ERR_LOT_NOT_ON_HAND`. Finally, when the approved write-off named a `btg_slot_id`, that slot moves `EXPIRED → WRITTEN_OFF` and the bottle it was holding is released, decrementing the lot's `bottles_committed` by 1.

Rejection is the quiet branch. It is controller-exclusive, requires the write-off to be in `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_SUBMITTED`) and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the submitter (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), carries a mandatory `note` that is stored as the write-off's `resolution_note`, and moves the record `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Every bottle count and both ledgers are left exactly as they were.

Reversal is the compensating branch, and it exists because the ledger is append-only. It is controller-exclusive, carries a mandatory `note` stored as `resolution_note`, and requires the write-off to be in `APPROVED` (`ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_APPROVED`, HTTP 409) and the lot to be either `ON_HAND` or `DEPLETED` — a lot that has already gone terminally `WRITTEN_OFF` is frozen and the reversal is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_ON_HAND`. On success the write-off moves `APPROVED → REVERSED` and one further ledger entry is appended with reason `REVERSAL`, a negative `bottles` equal to the negation of the written-off count, a negative `cogs_cents` equal to the negation of the recognised cost, zero `revenue_cents` and the same `write_off_id`, so that the approval entry and the reversal entry sum to exactly zero. The lot's `bottles_on_hand` rises by the written-off count, and a lot that had gone `DEPLETED` returns `DEPLETED → ON_HAND`; the bin's `bottles_stored` rises by the same amount and its `utilization_pct` is recomputed. The venue's `cogs_ledger_cents` falls by the recognised cost, returning to its pre-approval value. List entries that had been flipped to `OUT_OF_STOCK` by the original approval return to `AVAILABLE`, decrementing their versions' `out_of_stock_entry_count`. And when the write-off named a `btg_slot_id`, that slot returns `WRITTEN_OFF → EXPIRED` and the lot's `bottles_committed` rises by 1 again. Throughout all of this the original approval entry is never mutated and never deleted.

### 3.9. Wine Dinner Events: From Plan to Settlement and Back

A wine dinner is a ticketed event poured from a live list, and it moves `PLANNED → PRICED → CONFIRMED → SETTLED` with cancellation available along the way. A `VenueSommelier` schedules one by naming the `venue_id`, the `list_version_id` the pour plan is drawn from, an event `name` of 1 to 120 characters, the window bounds `starts_at` and `ends_at`, a `seat_count` between 1 and 400, and the `pour_plan` itself.

The pour plan is an array of between 1 and 12 lines, each an object carrying a `list_entry_id` and a `pours_per_seat` between 1 and 6. Every line's entry must belong to the event's own list version; a line naming an entry from another version, or a line whose `pours_per_seat` is below 1, is refused with `ERR_POUR_PLAN_ENTRY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The referenced version must itself be live, in `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The window must open strictly before it closes, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_WINDOW`.

A venue cannot run two dinners on top of each other, and needs turnover time between them. The occupancy geometry here is deliberately the opposite of the preservation rail's. Event windows are half-open — the start instant belongs to the event and the end instant does not — and each existing window is widened symmetrically by a two-hour turnover buffer, that is by 120 minutes on each side. A new event conflicts with an existing one when the new event's `starts_at` falls strictly before that event's `ends_at` advanced by the buffer **and** that event's `starts_at` falls strictly before the new event's `ends_at` advanced by the buffer. Any conflict against an event of the same venue in state `PLANNED`, `PRICED`, `CONFIRMED`, `SETTLED` or `SETTLEMENT_VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409); cancelled events do not occupy their windows, and cancelling an event releases its window for future bookings. Because both comparisons are strict, adjacency is permitted: an event starting exactly 120 minutes after another one ends is accepted, while one minute earlier is refused.

A new event starts in state `PLANNED` with `pinned_version_no` set to the referenced version's `version_no`, `pinned_version_stale` false, and all five pricing fields — `beverage_cost_per_seat_cents`, `provisional_gross_cents`, `tier_discount_pct`, `final_ticket_price_cents` and `projected_pour_cost_pct` — null. Events may be listed filtered by `venue_id`, `list_version_id` and `state`.

Pricing is a sommelier act with no request body, and it is a single pass with a pinned evaluation order. **Step one**, the per-seat beverage cost. For each pour-plan line, resolve the list entry and through it the wine, then compute how many pours a bottle yields at the standard event pour of 100 millilitres: divide the wine's `bottle_size_ml` by 100, discard any fraction, and subtract one pour for the allowance. Divide the wine's `reference_cost_cents` by that pour count and round half up to whole cents to get a cost per pour, then multiply by that line's `pours_per_seat` and round half up again to get the line cost. Sum the line costs across the plan; the total is `beverage_cost_per_seat_cents`. Rounding happens per line and the summation comes afterwards — summing raw figures and rounding once at the end is not the rule. **Step two**, the provisional per-seat gross: multiply `beverage_cost_per_seat_cents` by 100, divide by the venue's `event_cogs_target_pct`, round half up to whole cents, then raise the result to the next whole multiple of 500 cents; that is `provisional_gross_cents`. **Step three**, the volume tier, keyed on the provisional gross revenue, being `provisional_gross_cents` multiplied by `seat_count`. When that provisional revenue is below 250000 cents the `tier_discount_pct` is 0.0; when it is at least 250000 and below 500000 the discount is 4.0; when it is at least 500000 the discount is 7.0. Both breaks belong to the band above them, because the comparison is inclusive on the lower bound. **Step four**, the final ticket price: take `provisional_gross_cents`, multiply by 100 less the discount percentage, divide by 100, round half up to whole cents, then raise the result to the next whole multiple of 100 cents; that is `final_ticket_price_cents`. **Step five and six**, the projection: multiply `beverage_cost_per_seat_cents` by `seat_count` to get the event's total beverage cost, then express it as a percentage of gross ticket revenue — total beverage cost multiplied by 100, divided by `final_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by `seat_count`, rounded half up to two decimals; that is `projected_pour_cost_pct`. The apparent circularity here is resolved by fiat: the tier is read once from the provisional revenue in step three and is **never** re-derived from the final ticket price multiplied by the seat count. There is no second pass and no fixed-point iteration. Neither `bottle_price_cents` nor `glass_price_cents` is an input to any of this.

Pricing moves the event `PLANNED → PRICED`, and it may be repeated: a `PRICED` event re-priced stays `PRICED → PRICED`, recomputing all five fields against current entry data. Because the tier is keyed on the provisional gross, re-pricing unchanged inputs is idempotent. Repricing is permitted only before confirmation; the pour-plan membership check applies on every pricing attempt, and an event in any state other than `PLANNED` or `PRICED` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Confirmation is director-exclusive. It requires the event to be in `PRICED` (`ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`), the `final_ticket_price_cents` to be present rather than null, the pinned list version to be in `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING` (`ERR_LIST_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`), and the venue's posture not to be `FROZEN` (`ERR_VENUE_PROGRAM_FROZEN`). On success the event moves `PRICED → CONFIRMED`, `pinned_version_no` is set to that version's `version_no` — this is the version-pinning-on-approval moment — and `pinned_version_stale` is set to false. From here the priced ticket and the pinned list are frozen.

Settlement is controller-exclusive and requires the event to be in `CONFIRMED` (`ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`) with `pinned_version_stale` false; a pinned version that has left `PUBLISHED` since confirmation — because it went to `REPRINT_PENDING`, was superseded or was archived — blocks settlement with `ERR_PINNED_LIST_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 409). The draw itself walks the pour-plan lines in array order. For each line, resolve the entry and its wine, recompute the event pour yield exactly as in pricing (the wine's `bottle_size_ml` divided by 100, fraction discarded, less one pour), then compute the bottles the line needs as `seat_count` multiplied by that line's `pours_per_seat`, divided by the pour yield and **rounded up** to a whole bottle, because a partial bottle still opens a whole bottle. The bottles available for that line are the sum over the venue's `ON_HAND` lots of that wine of `bottles_on_hand` less `bottles_committed`; if any line's availability falls short of what it needs, settlement is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOTTLES`. Otherwise each line's bottles are drawn from the lots in `received_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and priced under the venue's costing method as described in Section 3.7.

The event then moves `CONFIRMED → SETTLED`. `settled_cogs_cents` is the sum of the priced draws and `settled_revenue_cents` is `final_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by `seat_count`. One ledger entry is appended per consumed lot with reason `EVENT`, carrying that lot's bottle count and that lot's share of the cost, and the whole settled revenue is booked on the **first** such entry only, with `revenue_cents` of 0 on all the rest. Every lot drained to zero moves `ON_HAND → DEPLETED` with the full bin and list-availability cascade of Section 3.8, and the bins release the stored bottles. The entries' costs and revenues are added to the venue's `cogs_ledger_cents` and `revenue_ledger_cents`.

Voiding a settlement is the compensating counterpart and is likewise controller-exclusive, carrying a mandatory `note`. It requires the event to be in `SETTLED` (`ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`) and moves it `SETTLED → SETTLEMENT_VOIDED`. For each entry the settlement wrote, one further entry is appended with reason `REVERSAL` carrying the exact negation of that entry's `bottles`, `cogs_cents` and `revenue_cents`. Every bottle drawn at settlement returns to the exact lot it came from, lots that had gone `DEPLETED` return `DEPLETED → ON_HAND`, and the bins re-absorb the bottles. Both venue ledgers return to their pre-settlement values, and `settled_cogs_cents` and `settled_revenue_cents` are reset to null.

Cancellation is available to the sommelier and the director together, carries an optional `note`, and reaches `CANCELLED` from `PLANNED`, from `PRICED`, from `CONFIRMED` or from `SETTLEMENT_VOIDED`; every other origin state is `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`, which means a settled event must be voided before it can be cancelled. Cancelling from `PLANNED` or from `PRICED` simply releases the venue window. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` or from `SETTLEMENT_VOIDED` additionally clears `pinned_version_no` back to null. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.10. Cost Governance: Pour-Cost Variance, Investigations, Par Levels and Corkage

The number the whole program is judged on is the gap between two percentages, and computing it is a director-only job over an explicit period. The run supplies `period_start` and `period_end`, which must be strictly ordered or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`. The window is half-open on the ledger's `occurred_at`: an entry counts when its `occurred_at` is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`.

Two populations are drawn from that window. The **actual** population is every ledger entry for the venue in the window, whatever its reason, taken with its signed values — so spoilage, staff education and reversal entries all count, the last of them negatively. The **theoretical** population is the same window restricted to entries whose reason is `SALE_BTG`, `SALE_BOTTLE` or `EVENT`, which is what the recorded sales should have cost. If the total `revenue_cents` of the actual population is zero, or if the total `revenue_cents` of the theoretical population is zero, no percentage is defined and the run is refused with `ERR_NO_REVENUE_IN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the actual pour cost is the actual population's total `cogs_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by its total `revenue_cents`, rounded half up to two decimals; the theoretical pour cost is the same arithmetic over the theoretical population. The variance is then the theoretical percentage subtracted from the actual percentage and rounded half up to two decimals again — computed from the two **already-rounded** percentages, not from the raw ratios.

A completed run writes the variance to the venue's `last_variance_pct`, stamps `last_pour_cost_run_at` with the logical clock, and recomputes `program_posture` against the boundaries of Section 2.10: `OPEN` at or below 3.00 percentage points, `RESTRICTED` strictly above 3.00 and at or below 6.00, and `FROZEN` strictly above 6.00 or whenever an escalated investigation exists for the venue. When the recomputed posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — equivalently, whenever the variance exceeds 3.00 — the run opens a variance investigation in state `OPEN`, carrying `venue_id`, `triggered_variance_pct` set to the variance just computed, and `period_start` and `period_end` copied from the run; it does so only if the venue does not already have an investigation in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`. The run returns the updated venue.

The same arithmetic is available as a read-only report to the director and the controller, which recomputes on demand from `period_start` and `period_end` and never touches posture, never opens an investigation and never writes anything. It returns `period_start`, `period_end`, `actual_pour_cost_pct`, `theoretical_pour_cost_pct`, `variance_pct`, `actual_cogs_cents`, `actual_revenue_cents`, `theoretical_cogs_cents`, `theoretical_revenue_cents` and `entry_count`, the last being how many ledger entries fell inside the window. The same period ordering and revenue preconditions apply, with the same two error identifiers.

An investigation is the controller's file on an unexplained gap, and it carries `escalation_note`, `resolution_note` and `resolved_at`, all null while it is `OPEN`. Escalating it is controller-exclusive, carries a mandatory `note` stored as `escalation_note`, and requires the investigation to be in `OPEN`; an already-escalated or already-resolved file is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (HTTP 409). Escalation moves the file `OPEN → ESCALATED` and recomputes the venue's posture, which necessarily becomes `FROZEN` because an escalated investigation now exists — and that freeze is what stops list publication and event confirmation at the venue until the file is closed.

Resolving is also controller-exclusive and takes a `resolution` of either `EXPLAINED` or `ADJUSTED` together with a mandatory `resolution_note`. It may be applied to a file in `OPEN` or in `ESCALATED`, giving four transitions — `OPEN → RESOLVED_EXPLAINED`, `ESCALATED → RESOLVED_EXPLAINED`, `OPEN → RESOLVED_ADJUSTED` and `ESCALATED → RESOLVED_ADJUSTED` — and both resolved states are terminal. Every resolution stores the note and stamps `resolved_at` with the logical clock. The two resolutions then differ in exactly one respect, and it is the difference the controller is really choosing between. An `EXPLAINED` resolution accepts the variance as real: the venue's `last_variance_pct` is left as it is and the posture is recomputed from that figure alone, so a venue frozen only because this file was escalated thaws to whatever its variance alone implies once no other escalated file remains — `RESTRICTED` if the variance is above 3.00 and at most 6.00, `OPEN` if it is at or below 3.00, and still `FROZEN` if the variance itself exceeds 6.00. An `ADJUSTED` resolution declares the variance an accounting artefact: `last_variance_pct` is reset to 0.00 and the posture is recomputed, which returns the venue to `OPEN` provided no other escalated investigation for that venue remains. Investigations are readable and listable by the director and the controller only, filtered by `venue_id` and by `state`.

Replenishment is served by a par-level report available to the director and the sommelier, which reads the single live list version of the venue — the one in `PUBLISHED` or `REPRINT_PENDING` — and walks its entries. For each entry, the bottles on hand are the sum of `bottles_on_hand` across the venue's lots of that wine whose state is `ON_HAND`. The bottles on order are the sum of `granted_bottles` across the venue's allocation claims for that wine whose state is `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`; claims already `RECEIVED` are excluded, because those bottles have become lots and would otherwise be counted twice. The deficit is the entry's `par_bottles` less the on-hand count less the on-order count. When the deficit is zero or negative, nothing is reordered; otherwise the reorder quantity is the deficit rounded up to a whole number of cases, that is, the deficit divided by the wine's `case_pack`, rounded up to a whole number of cases, and multiplied back by `case_pack`. A line is flagged urgent when the on-hand count is strictly below half the par level rounded up to a whole bottle, so an on-hand count exactly equal to that half-par ceiling is **not** urgent. Lines come back sorted by the urgency flag descending, then by reorder quantity descending, then by the entry's `sort_rank` ascending. The response carries `venue_id`, a `generated_at` instant, `line_count`, `urgent_line_count` and the `lines` array, and each line carries `list_entry_id`, `wine_id`, `par_bottles`, `bottles_on_hand`, `bottles_on_order`, `deficit_bottles`, `reorder_bottles` and `is_urgent`. The optional `urgent_only` flag restricts the array to urgent lines, and `limit` and `offset` page it.

The last capability in this section persists nothing at all. A sommelier quoting corkage for a party that has brought its own bottles supplies `byo_bottle_count`, `bottles_purchased_from_list`, `party_size` and `byo_categories`, the array of wine categories the party has brought. Two policy checks run first: a count above the venue's `byo_bottle_limit_per_party` is refused with `ERR_BYO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and any category present in the venue's `byo_prohibited_categories` is refused with `ERR_BYO_CATEGORY_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). The quote then works as follows. Divide `bottles_purchased_from_list` by the venue's `corkage_waiver_purchase_ratio`, discarding any remainder, to get how many waivers the party has earned; the waived bottles are the smaller of that figure and `byo_bottle_count`. Because the division discards the remainder, one waiver is earned at exactly the ratio and not before. The chargeable bottles are `byo_bottle_count` less the waived bottles, and the total is the chargeable bottles multiplied by the venue's `corkage_fee_cents`. Finally, a large party earns a break: when `party_size` is at least 8, the total is reduced by 25.0 percent — multiplied by 100 less 25.0, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents — and a party of exactly 8 qualifies. The response reports `waived_bottles`, `chargeable_bottles`, `corkage_total_cents` and `large_party_discount_applied`, the last being true exactly when the large-party reduction was applied.
