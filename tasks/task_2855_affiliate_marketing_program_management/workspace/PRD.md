# Affiliate Program Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A retail affiliate program is a marketplace of trust in which the retailer pays third-party publishers for demand it believes they created. The commercial problem is that the payment claim and the evidence for it arrive from different systems at different times: a click is recorded by a tracking pixel, an order is posted by the commerce platform hours or days later, a return lands weeks after that, and the affiliate network issues a fee statement at the end of the month. Between those events sit judgement calls the program owner cannot avoid — whether a coupon site genuinely produced an incremental sale, whether a partner bought ads on the retailer's own brand terms, whether a click was forced by a browser extension, and whether a commission that has already been paid must now be recovered.

This document specifies the Affiliate Program Management API, the system of record for that entire chain. It governs partner admission and site-quality screening, the category-scoped commission schedules that price each order, tracking implementation and its cookie window, last-click attribution with cross-device matching and deduplication against non-affiliate marketing channels, the incrementality policy that reduces last-click credit for coupon and loyalty partners, trademark-bidding and traffic-quality monitoring with the violation cases it opens, fraud-driven suspension, commission validation against returns and cancellations with clawback, and finally payout batch preparation, tax withholding, network fee reconciliation and settlement.

Two design commitments run through every section and should be read as non-negotiable. First, money is append-only: no commission movement is ever edited or deleted, and every correction is a new signed ledger entry, so the partner balance a payout releases can always be reconstructed from the movements behind it. Second, the program separates who observes from who decides: the analyst who screens an application cannot decide it, the analyst who opens a violation case cannot clear it, and the finance user who prepares a payout batch cannot approve it. Both commitments constrain the endpoint contract as much as the business narrative, and the rest of this document is written on the assumption that neither can be traded away for convenience.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API. Business resources are exposed as top-level collections — there is no product-wide URL prefix, so a partner lives at `/affiliates`, an order at `/conversions`, a settlement run at `/payout-batches`, and so on. Where a collection appears one segment below another (a partner's tracking links, for instance) that nested path is a read or write channel onto a peer collection, never a separate identity for the child; the child remains addressable by its own top-level collection and its own `id`.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller in two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity that user is acting in. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, or supplies one that names no known user, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The single exception is partner self-registration, which is deliberately reachable without credentials; every other route in the API requires authentication.

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `PartnerManager`, `ComplianceAnalyst`, `FinanceAnalyst`, `FinanceController`, `AffiliatePartner`, `TrackingIngestor`, and `Anonymous`. `ProgramAdmin` owns program configuration — networks and rate cards — and holds the reinstatement and termination levers plus a read path into every collection, but never screens, decides, prepares or approves. `PartnerManager` owns the partner relationship: it decides screened applications and mints tracking links, and it touches no money. `ComplianceAnalyst` runs screening and the monitoring programme, may suspend a partner but never reinstate one. `FinanceAnalyst` prepares and reconciles payout batches but cannot approve or pay them. `FinanceController` approves, pays and voids batches, releases holds and verifies tax forms, but cannot prepare or reconcile one. `AffiliatePartner` is the publisher itself. `TrackingIngestor` is the tracking pixel and order feed. `Anonymous` is the unauthenticated caller.

Authorization is checked against the role list of the addressed operation, and that check runs **before** any existence lookup, so a caller holding the wrong role learns nothing about which identifiers exist. A caller whose `X-User-Role` is absent from the operation's role list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). `Anonymous` reaches exactly one operation, partner self-registration; every other route answers `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` for an unauthenticated caller.

Role identity is resolved as follows. When `X-User-Role` is `AffiliatePartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Affiliate`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Affiliate` compare the resource's partner reference to `X-User-ID`, and an `AffiliatePartner` that addresses a resource resolving to a different partner is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That ownership check runs after existence, so the caller receives an accurate 403 rather than a misleading 404. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `PartnerManager`, `ComplianceAnalyst`, `FinanceAnalyst`, `FinanceController`, `TrackingIngestor` or `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever an audit trail or a duty-separation check needs it — as `screened_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an application, `opened_by_user_id` on a violation case, `verified_by_user_id` on a tax document, `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a payout batch, and `released_by_user_id` on a payout line — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paged with two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it defaults to 10 when omitted, and an explicitly supplied value must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and an explicitly supplied value must be an integer of 0 or greater. A `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101, and an `offset` of -1 are all rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400); omitting both parameters entirely applies the defaults of 10 and 0 rather than erroring.

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before paging is applied and `has_more` states whether any row remains beyond the current page.

Pagination validation is in force on all thirteen list channels: the partner list, the application list, the rate-card list, the order list, the violation-case list, the ledger-entry list, the payout-batch list, the payout-line list, a partner's monitoring-scan list, a partner's tax-document list, a partner's tracking-link list, a tracking link's click list, and an order's attribution-decision list.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Mutation Counters

Every resource in the system is minted with an `id` that is a server-generated UUID carrying no type prefix — clients never supply it and must not infer the entity type from its shape. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the row was first written.

Most resources are mutable and additionally carry two system-managed fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, in the same format, refreshed on every change to the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a balance, a counter or a score. The mutable resources are the network, the partner, the application, the commission policy, the tracking link, the order, the violation case, the tax document, the payout batch and the payout line.

Four resources are append-only records of something that happened and are never edited after creation: the click event, the attribution decision, the monitoring scan, and the commission ledger entry. These four carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and no endpoint exists to update them. The single exception to their immutability is administrative rather than semantic: a ledger entry's batch reference may be stamped when a payout batch claims it and un-stamped back to null when that batch is voided or when the batch is paid while the entry's line is still held. Those two stampings are the only mutations the system performs on a ledger entry, and they do not give it a `version`.

### 2.5. Path Resolution Order for Partner-Scoped and Order-Scoped Collections

Several collections are reachable one segment below their owner, and requests against them resolve identifiers strictly left to right. The leftmost identifier is looked up first, and the first identifier that names nothing terminates the request with that identifier's own 404 code. A request for a partner's tracking links or tax documents or monitoring scans under a partner id that does not exist therefore answers `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_FOUND`, never a tracking-link, tax-document or scan error. A click ingested through a tracking link id that does not exist answers `ERR_TRACKING_LINK_NOT_FOUND`. A request for an order's attribution decisions under an unknown order id answers `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_FOUND`.

The same discipline extends to owner references that arrive in the request body rather than the path. A partner registration naming an unknown network fails with `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, and one naming an unknown `parent_affiliate_id` fails with `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_FOUND`. A monitoring scan or a manually filed violation case naming an unknown `affiliate_id` fails with `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_FOUND`, while a manually filed case citing a `source_scan_id` that names no scan fails with `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. A payout batch naming an unknown network fails with `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`. Existence resolution always precedes ownership, state and business checks, but always follows the authentication and authorization checks of §2.1.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a request body, a response body or a stored field, and any field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Commission rates, fee rates, withholding rates, credit shares, variance measures and incrementality scores are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — so a 4% commission is 400 basis points and a full share of credit is 10000 basis points. Any field whose name ends in `_bps` is an integer count of basis points.

Wherever a computation produces a non-integer intermediate value, the result is rounded **half up** — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Two stored quantities are decimal numbers rather than integers: the site-quality score, the brand-suitability score and the trailing fraud score are each numbers rounded half up to exactly one decimal place. Everything else that is rounded is rounded half up to a whole cent or a whole basis point.

The **order** in which roundings are applied is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, and two places in this document pin it explicitly. When a commission is derived from an order total and then reduced by a credit share, the gross figure is rounded to whole cents first and the reduced figure is rounded from that already-rounded gross; deriving the reduced amount in a single expression from the order total, the rate and the credit share is wrong and will produce a different answer. When a payout batch aggregates its partner lines, each line is rounded to whole cents first and the batch totals are plain integer sums of those already-rounded per-line values; summing unrounded per-line products and rounding once at the batch level is wrong and produces a different answer. Both divergences are typically a single cent, which is precisely why they must be specified rather than left to taste.

### 2.7. Stale-View Rejection on Version-Guarded Writes

Four operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent resource's `version`. On each of them the guard field is a **required** input — omitting it is a malformed request, not an opt-out — and it must carry the `version` value the client last observed on the named parent. If that value differs from the parent's current `version`, the write is refused and nothing is persisted.

- Deciding an application requires `expected_affiliate_version` matching the applicant partner's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AFFILIATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a partner's record can legitimately advance without its status changing: filing a tax document for that partner bumps the partner's `version` and moves its `tax_document_status` to `PENDING`. The status precondition still reads `PENDING_APPLICATION` and no other rule would object, so the stale version is the only thing standing between the reviewer and a decision taken on a view of the applicant that is out of date.
- Running attribution on an order requires `expected_conversion_version` matching that order's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONVERSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The bumper here is click ingestion: a late click that falls inside the cookie window of an order still awaiting attribution refreshes that order's `candidate_click_count` and increments its `version`, so a caller whose candidate set was read before that click would otherwise resolve attribution against a click set the server has already superseded.
- Approving a payout batch requires `expected_batch_version` matching that batch's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The bumper is hold release: releasing a hold on any line of a reconciled batch recomputes the batch totals and increments the batch's `version` while deliberately leaving the batch in `VALIDATED`, so without the guard the approver would release a payable total they never saw.
- Activating a rate card requires `expected_policy_version` matching that policy's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — a schedule that has been edited since the activator read it cannot be activated on the stale view.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Three paired steps in this program must be performed by two different people, and the separation is enforced on `X-User-ID`, never on `X-User-Role` — two users holding the same role satisfy it, and one user holding two roles does not. The user who screened an application may not decide it. The user who prepared a payout batch may not approve it. The analyst who opened a violation case may not verify the remediation submitted against it. Any of these three collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.9. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification is evaluated against a single logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the program's time-dependent behaviour is reproducible. A test-only control operation pins that clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Until such a call is made, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Setting the clock performs no business writes whatsoever and triggers no sweep of its own; the sweeps described in §3 run only when their own operations are invoked.

The rules that read the logical clock are the cookie window that bounds attribution candidates, the thirty-day hold before a commission may be validated, the ninety-day trailing window and per-day decay of the fraud score, tax document expiry, and the twenty-four-hour cooldown between monitoring scans.

### 2.10. Rule Evaluation Order

When more than one rule could reject a request, the outcome is decided by a single global ordering. Rules are evaluated in strictly ascending order of the numbers below, and the **first** rule whose condition fails decides the response; no later rule is evaluated and nothing is persisted. This ordering is observable and is part of the contract — a request that violates three rules at once must answer with the lowest-numbered one.

1. `X-User-ID` is present and names a known user — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every route except partner self-registration, which is the only anonymous route.
2. The caller's role appears in the addressed operation's role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every route, and because it precedes every existence lookup a wrong-role caller cannot probe which identifiers exist.
3. A partner identifier resolves to an existing partner — otherwise `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-partner read, the status-change operation, the partner's monitoring-scan list, the partner's tax-document list and filing, the partner's application filing, and the partner's tracking-link minting and listing. It also covers a `parent_affiliate_id` naming no partner on registration and an `affiliate_id` naming no partner when a monitoring scan or a manually filed violation case is created.
4. An application identifier resolves to an existing application — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-application read, screening, deciding and resubmission.
5. A rate-card identifier resolves to an existing commission policy — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-policy read and activation.
6. A tracking-link identifier resolves to an existing link — otherwise `ERR_TRACKING_LINK_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-link read, link disabling, click ingestion and the link's click list.
7. An order identifier resolves to an existing conversion — otherwise `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-order read, attribution, order adjustment and the order's attribution-decision list.
8. A case identifier resolves to an existing violation case — otherwise `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-case read and case transitions, and additionally to a manually filed case whose `source_scan_id` names no monitoring scan.
9. A batch identifier resolves to an existing payout batch — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-batch read, validation, approval, payment and voiding.
10. A line identifier resolves to an existing payout line — otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-line read and hold release.
11. A `network_id` resolves to an existing network — otherwise `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the single-network read, partner registration, and payout-batch creation.
12. A tax-document identifier resolves to an existing document — otherwise `ERR_TAX_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to tax-document verification.
13. When the caller's role is `AffiliatePartner`, the addressed resource resolves to the caller's own partner row — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to seven operations: the single-partner read, the partner's tracking-link list, the partner's tax-document list, tax-document filing, application filing, application resubmission, and case transitions.
14. Every required body field is present with its declared type, every enum value is a declared member of its enumeration, every integer respects its declared range, and every instant is a well-formed ISO 8601 value — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every route. This covers missing required fields, unknown enum members, negative money, out-of-range scores, malformed instants, and a declared-promotional-methods value that is not an array. Unknown extra keys in a request body are ignored, not rejected.
15. `limit` is an integer from 1 to 100 and `offset` is an integer of 0 or greater — otherwise `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (400). Applies to the thirteen list channels named in §2.3.
16. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user who performed the paired earlier step — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to deciding an application, approving a payout batch, and verifying a case's remediation.
17. The partner whose resource is being written is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `TERMINATED` — otherwise `ERR_AFFILIATE_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to minting a tracking link, filing an application, ingesting a click, and resubmitting an application.
18. The application's `review_state` is not one of the terminal values `APPROVED`, `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `REJECTED` — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to screening, deciding and resubmission, and doubles as the idempotency guard on a repeated decision.
19. The application's `review_state` is `SUBMITTED` when screening, `SCREENED` when deciding, and `CHANGES_REQUESTED` when resubmitting — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SCREENED` (409). Applies to screening, deciding and resubmission, and catches for example an attempt to decide a `SUBMITTED` application or to screen one already `SCREENED`.
20. The order's `attribution_status` is `UNATTRIBUTED` — otherwise `ERR_CONVERSION_ALREADY_ATTRIBUTED` (409). Applies to attribution. Note that an attribution run over an empty candidate set leaves the order `UNATTRIBUTED`, so that order remains re-runnable.
21. The order's `attribution_status` is not `UNATTRIBUTED`, and its `validation_status` is neither `REVERSED` nor `CLAWED_BACK` — otherwise `ERR_CONVERSION_NOT_ATTRIBUTED` (409). Applies to order adjustment: there is no commission to unwind on an unattributed order, and an order already fully reversed or clawed back is terminal on the money dimension.
22. The case's `state` is neither `VERIFIED_CLEARED` nor `UPHELD` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to case transitions.
23. The requested case action is declared for the case's current state, and an escalation additionally requires at least two failed verification rounds — otherwise `ERR_CASE_INVALID_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to case transitions, and catches verifying from `OPEN`, submitting remediation from `OPEN`, and escalating too early.
24. The batch's `state` is neither `PAID` nor `VOIDED` — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to batch validation, approval, payment and voiding, and doubles as the idempotency guard on a repeated payment or void.
25. The batch's `state` is `DRAFT` when validating, `VALIDATED` when approving, and `APPROVED` when paying — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_VALIDATED` (409). Applies to batch validation, approval and payment, and catches for example approving a `DRAFT` batch or paying a `VALIDATED` one.
26. The line's `state` is one of `HELD_TAX_DOC`, `HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` or `HELD_FRAUD`, and the owning batch is in `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` — otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_HELD` (409). Applies to hold release. Releasing an `INCLUDED`, `PAID` or `VOIDED` line, or any line on an `APPROVED` or terminal batch, fails here. `HELD_FRAUD` deliberately **satisfies** this check so that a fraud-held line falls through to rule 34 rather than being misreported as merely not held.
27. The applicant partner's `status` is `PENDING_APPLICATION` and no violation case for that partner is `UPHELD` — otherwise `ERR_AFFILIATE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409). Applies to deciding an application: a decision cannot activate a partner that compliance has already suspended, terminated, or upheld a violation against.
28. The tracking link's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_TRACKING_LINK_DISABLED` (409). Applies to click ingestion and to link disabling, so a disabled link can neither receive clicks nor be disabled a second time.
29. `expected_affiliate_version` equals the applicant partner's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AFFILIATE_VERSION` (412). Applies to deciding an application.
30. `expected_conversion_version` equals the order's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONVERSION_VERSION` (412). Applies to attribution.
31. `expected_batch_version` equals the batch's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` (412). Applies to batch approval.
32. `expected_policy_version` equals the policy's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (412). Applies to rate-card activation.
33. The application's `brand_suitability_score` is at least 70.0 — otherwise `ERR_BRAND_SUITABILITY_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to deciding an application, and acts as a brand-safety veto: an application below that floor can never be approved, not even with conditions.
34. The line's `state` is not `HELD_FRAUD` — otherwise `ERR_FRAUD_HOLD_NOT_CLEARABLE` (409). Applies to hold release. A fraud hold is never releasable through the finance channel; the partner must first be reinstated, after which a later batch regenerates the line. This rule outranks the tax-document check on the same call.
35. When reinstating a partner, that partner's `open_violation_case_count` is 0 — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_VIOLATION_CASES` (409). Applies to the status-change operation.
36. When reinstating a partner, that partner's `fraud_risk_score_trailing` is strictly below 25.0 — otherwise `ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH` (409). Applies to the status-change operation. The boundary is strict: a trailing score of exactly 25.0 keeps the partner suspended.
37. The policy's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than its `effective_to` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` (422). Applies to rate-card activation only; window ordering is never checked at creation, so a draft may legitimately hold an inverted window. This rule outranks the overlap check.
38. The requested scan window neither overlaps nor sits within twenty-four hours of another monitoring scan for the same partner and the same engine — otherwise `ERR_SCAN_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to monitoring-scan creation.
39. No active rate card with the same product category and an intersecting partner-type scope has a window overlapping the activating policy's window, unless the two windows are exactly equal — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to rate-card activation.
40. The partner has no application in a non-terminal review state — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION` (409). Applies to application filing: one live application per partner, though a rejected history does not block a fresh filing.
41. `order_ref` is unique across all orders — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_REF` (409). Applies to order creation, and deduplicates double-fired conversion pixels on the retailer side.
42. The prospective sub-affiliate depth is at most 1 — otherwise `ERR_SUB_AFFILIATE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to partner registration.
43. A supplied `parent_affiliate_id` names a partner whose `partner_type` is `SUB_NETWORK`, and is not the row being created — otherwise `ERR_CIRCULAR_SUB_AFFILIATE` (422). Applies to partner registration.
44. The application's `revision_round_count` is strictly below 2 — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (422). Applies to deciding an application and to resubmission.
45. The requested decision is compatible with the application's `screening_outcome` — otherwise `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_PASSED` (422). Applies to deciding an application.
46. The order's accumulated returned amount plus the amount on this request is at most the order total — otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_ORDER` (422). Applies to order adjustment.
47. An active rate card covers the order's product category, the winning partner's partner type, and the order's placement instant — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COMMISSION_POLICY` (422). Applies to attribution.
48. The partner has a tax document whose `status` is `VALID` and whose `expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_TAX_DOCUMENT_INVALID` (422). Applies to hold release, and is deliberately ranked below the fraud-hold check so that a fraud-held line on a partner with an expired form reports the fraud hold.
49. The batch's `net_payable_cents` is at most 5,000,000 cents — otherwise `ERR_APPROVAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to batch approval, and is ranked below the state check so that approving an over-limit `DRAFT` batch reports the state error.
50. The network statement reconciles against the retailer's own computation — otherwise `ERR_NETWORK_FEE_VARIANCE` (422). Applies to batch validation.
51. At least one unstamped ledger entry qualifies for the requested network and period — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PAYOUT_LINES` (422). Applies to payout-batch creation, and creates nothing at all, so a retry after the validation sweep catches up can still succeed.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

A single request in this system routinely mutates several resources. Whenever it does, every touched row is a full mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by exactly 1, even when the only change is a recomputed counter, balance, score or total. A client that reads a partner, then triggers an operation that touches that partner indirectly, must expect the partner's `version` to have moved.

Cascades are described in this document as ordered hops, and the ordering matters because later hops consume the output of earlier ones. Suspending a partner, for example, is a four-hop operation: the partner itself moves to `SUSPENDED`; then every `ACTIVE` partner whose `parent_affiliate_id` names it is suspended too; then, for every partner newly suspended anywhere in that fan-out, its `ACTIVE` tracking links are disabled and its `INCLUDED` payout lines on non-terminal batches are moved to a fraud hold; and finally every batch touched by those holds has its fee, withholding and payable totals recomputed. A cascade never re-enters a hop it has already completed — the parent-propagation hop does **not** re-evaluate the sub-partner's own fraud score, for instance; it is a pure propagation.

The server is expected to perform these secondary transitions on its own, without a client request naming the affected resource. That applies equally to the automatic suspension a rising fraud score triggers, to the supersession of a rate card by an identically windowed successor, to the disabling of tracking links behind a partner status change, to the hold states a payout line falls into, to the expiry of a tax document noticed during a payout sweep, and to the validation of a conversion whose hold period has elapsed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Networks, Partner Registration and Sub-Networks

Before any partner can exist, a `ProgramAdmin` registers the affiliate **network** the retailer transacts through. A network is created with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique across all networks, a `statement_currency` of `USD`, `EUR` or `GBP`, and a fee model describing how the network charges its override. The `fee_model` is one of `PERCENT_OF_COMMISSION`, `FLAT_PER_CONVERSION` or `HYBRID`, and it constrains the two fee components that accompany it: `fee_bps` is the percentage component in basis points, from 0 to 3000, and must be 0 when the fee model is `FLAT_PER_CONVERSION`; `flat_fee_cents` is the per-conversion flat component, from 0 to 1000 cents, and must be 0 when the fee model is `PERCENT_OF_COMMISSION`. A `HYBRID` network may carry both components as non-zero.

Publisher partners register themselves, and this is the one place in the API where an unauthenticated caller is served — a `ProgramAdmin` or `PartnerManager` may also register a partner on the publisher's behalf. Registration supplies a `legal_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `contact_email` that must contain an `@` character and must be unique across all partners, a `payout_currency` of `USD`, `EUR` or `GBP`, the `network_id` of the network the partner is contracted through, and a `partner_type` describing the promotional model: `CONTENT`, `INFLUENCER`, `PAID_SEARCH`, `COUPON`, `LOYALTY_CASHBACK` or `SUB_NETWORK`.

Registration may optionally supply `parent_affiliate_id`, which nests the new partner beneath an existing sub-network. That reference defaults to null, and a partner with a null parent is a top-level partner. Only a partner whose own `partner_type` is `SUB_NETWORK` may parent another, and a partner may never parent itself; violating either is rejected as a circular sub-affiliate. The system derives `sub_affiliate_depth` from the parent chain: it is 0 when no parent is supplied, and otherwise one greater than the parent's own depth. Because the program allows exactly one level of nesting, a depth above 1 is rejected — a sub-affiliate of a sub-affiliate does not exist in this program.

A freshly registered partner starts life in `status` `PENDING_APPLICATION` with `suspension_cause` `NONE`. Every counter and balance starts at zero: `attributed_conversion_count`, `new_customer_attributed_count`, `coupon_assisted_conversion_count`, `accrued_balance_cents`, `reserved_balance_cents`, `lifetime_paid_cents`, `open_violation_case_count` and `tracking_link_count` are all 0, `fraud_risk_score` is 0, and `fraud_risk_score_trailing` is 0.0. Its `tax_document_status` is `NONE`, meaning no tax form is on file at all. Registration also computes `incrementality_score_bps` and `incrementality_tier` from those zeroed counters using the incrementality formula of §3.5 — which, because the counters are empty, means the tier a partner starts with is determined purely by its partner type.

Partners are listable with optional filters on `status`, `partner_type`, `network_id`, `parent_affiliate_id` and `incrementality_tier`. The single-partner read exposes the partner's scores, tier, counters and balances, and is the channel through which an `AffiliatePartner` inspects its own money — it can read every figure but move none of them.

### 3.2. Application Screening and the Decision Fork

Admission to the program is a review cycle recorded as an **application**. A partner files one by supplying the `site_url` it will promote from, which must begin with `https://`; a `primary_traffic_source` naming the dominant source of its traffic, from `ORGANIC_SEARCH`, `CONTENT_NEWSLETTER`, `SOCIAL_ORGANIC`, `PAID_SEARCH`, `COUPON_DIRECTORY`, `CASHBACK_PORTAL` or `INCENTIVIZED_TRAFFIC`; a `monthly_traffic_estimate` of self-reported sessions that must be 0 or greater; a non-empty `declared_promo_methods` array drawn from `EDITORIAL_CONTENT`, `EMAIL`, `SOCIAL_POST`, `COUPON_FEED`, `TRADEMARK_PAID_SEARCH`, `BROWSER_EXTENSION` and `TOOLBAR`; and a non-empty `product_categories` array drawn from `APPAREL`, `ELECTRONICS`, `HOME`, `BEAUTY` and `GROCERY`. The application is written with `review_state` `SUBMITTED`, `screening_outcome` `NOT_SCREENED` and `revision_round_count` 0, and both score fields — `site_quality_score` and `brand_suitability_score` — start as null, since nothing has been measured yet. A partner may hold only one live application at a time; a partner whose earlier application was rejected may file again. Filing an application also requires the partner to be neither suspended nor terminated, and the application records the applicant on its `affiliate_id`.

Screening is exclusive to a `ComplianceAnalyst` and moves the application `SUBMITTED → SCREENED`. The analyst submits five measurements: `domain_authority` as an integer from 0 to 100, `content_originality_pct` as an integer from 0 to 100, `broken_link_ratio_bps` as an integer from 0 to 10000, `brand_safety_flag_count` as an integer from 0 to 20, and `restricted_vertical_overlap_bps` as an integer from 0 to 10000. Screening requires the application to be `SUBMITTED` and the applicant partner to still be `PENDING_APPLICATION`. It stamps `screened_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, writes both scores and the derived `screening_outcome`, and increments the application's `version`.

**Site quality** is a weighted composite. The base figure is the sum of three terms: forty-five hundredths of `domain_authority`, thirty-five hundredths of `content_originality_pct`, and twenty hundredths of a link-health term. The link-health term is 100 minus one hundredth of `broken_link_ratio_bps`, floored at 0 so that a pathological link ratio contributes nothing rather than a negative amount. That base is then multiplied by a source multiplier chosen by `primary_traffic_source`: 1.00 for `ORGANIC_SEARCH`, 0.95 for `CONTENT_NEWSLETTER`, 0.90 for `SOCIAL_ORGANIC`, 0.75 for `PAID_SEARCH`, 0.65 for `COUPON_DIRECTORY`, 0.60 for `CASHBACK_PORTAL` and 0.40 for `INCENTIVIZED_TRAFFIC`. A penalty is then subtracted, computed as 10 points per brand-safety flag and capped as described below. The result is clamped into the range 0 to 100 and rounded half up to one decimal place, and that rounding happens exactly once, at the very end — the intermediate product of base and multiplier is never rounded.

The penalty cap and one extra multiplier depend on what the applicant declared it will do. Where `declared_promo_methods` contains neither `TRADEMARK_PAID_SEARCH` nor `COUPON_FEED`, the standard screen applies: the penalty is capped at 30 points and no further multiplier is used. Where `declared_promo_methods` contains `TRADEMARK_PAID_SEARCH` or `COUPON_FEED`, the restricted screen applies instead: the penalty cap rises to 45 points, and the product of base and source multiplier is additionally multiplied by 0.90 before the penalty is subtracted. In both screens the penalty is the smaller of the cap and 10 points per flag, the clamp to 0–100 is applied after the subtraction, and the single half-up rounding to one decimal place still happens only at the end.

**Brand suitability** is simpler and shares one input with site quality. It starts at 100, subtracts one hundredth of `restricted_vertical_overlap_bps`, and subtracts a further 12 points for each brand-safety flag counted in `brand_safety_flag_count`. The result is clamped into the range 0 to 100 and rounded half up to one decimal place.

The two scores together produce `screening_outcome`, which takes one of `NOT_SCREENED`, `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL` or `FAIL`. The outcome is `PASS` when `site_quality_score` is at least 60.0 and `brand_suitability_score` is at least 70.0. It is `CONDITIONAL` when `brand_suitability_score` is at least 70.0 and `site_quality_score` is at least 45.0 but strictly below 60.0. It is `FAIL` when `site_quality_score` is strictly below 45.0, or when `brand_suitability_score` is strictly below 70.0 regardless of site quality. Both lower bounds are inclusive: a site-quality score of exactly 45.0 is `CONDITIONAL`, and exactly 60.0 is `PASS`.

A screened application is then decided by a `PartnerManager`, who is never the analyst that screened it. The decision carries a `decision` naming one of four branches, a `decision_notes` free-text rationale that is recorded on the application, an `expected_affiliate_version` guard as described in §2.7, and — on one branch only — a `conditional_requirements` array. Every branch requires the application to be `SCREENED`, the applicant to still be `PENDING_APPLICATION`, and no violation case against the applicant to be `UPHELD`; every branch stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the deciding `X-User-ID` and increments the application's `version`. Beyond that the four branches diverge, and the divergence is deliberate.

- `APPROVE` moves the application `SCREENED → APPROVED` and requires the `screening_outcome` to be exactly `PASS`. It cascades the applicant partner `PENDING_APPLICATION → ACTIVE`.
- `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` moves the application `SCREENED → APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` and accepts a `screening_outcome` of either `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL`. Unlike the plain approval it additionally requires `conditional_requirements` to be a non-empty array, whose contents are the obligations attached to the admission and are stored on the application. It cascades the applicant partner `PENDING_APPLICATION → ACTIVE` exactly as `APPROVE` does.
- `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the application `SCREENED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and requires `revision_round_count` to be strictly below 2. It accepts any `screening_outcome`, including `FAIL`. Unlike the other three branches it performs **no** partner cascade at all: the applicant's `status` is untouched and remains `PENDING_APPLICATION`.
- `REJECT` moves the application `SCREENED → REJECTED` and accepts any `screening_outcome`. It cascades the applicant partner `PENDING_APPLICATION → TERMINATED`.

A decision whose branch is incompatible with the screening outcome is rejected — an `APPROVE` on anything other than `PASS`, an `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` on a `FAIL` outcome, and an `APPROVE_WITH_CONDITIONS` carrying an empty `conditional_requirements` array all answer `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_PASSED`. A `FAIL` outcome therefore admits only `REJECT` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`. Independently of the outcome gate, any approval branch on an application whose `brand_suitability_score` is below 70.0 is refused with `ERR_BRAND_SUITABILITY_VIOLATION`; brand safety is a veto that conditions cannot buy off.

Activation of a partner is more than a status flip. The partner moves to `ACTIVE`, its `suspension_cause` is set back to `NONE`, its `incrementality_score_bps` and `incrementality_tier` are recomputed from its current counters, and its `version` increments. Termination through a rejection is likewise a cascade: the partner moves to `TERMINATED`, every one of its tracking links that is still `ACTIVE` is disabled with a `disabled_reason` of `APPLICATION_REJECTED`, and the partner's `version` increments.

An application sent back for changes can be revised, and this is the one loop in the review lifecycle. The publisher — acting as `AffiliatePartner`, where its own `X-User-ID` must equal the application's applicant reference — resubmits an application that is `CHANGES_REQUESTED` while the partner is still `PENDING_APPLICATION` and `revision_round_count` remains strictly below 2. Resubmission overwrites `site_url`, `primary_traffic_source` and `declared_promo_methods` with the values supplied on the resubmission, increments `revision_round_count` by 1, clears `site_quality_score`, `brand_suitability_score` and `screened_by_user_id` back to null, resets `screening_outcome` to `NOT_SCREENED`, moves the application `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and increments the application's `version`. The application must then be screened again by a `ComplianceAnalyst`, and the loop can run at most twice: once `revision_round_count` reaches 2 both a further `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision and a further resubmission are refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, forcing the reviewer to approve or reject outright.

Applications are listable with optional filters on `affiliate_id`, `review_state` and `screening_outcome`, and each application is individually readable including its two scores.

### 3.3. Commission Rate Cards and Their Effective Windows

Commissions are priced by **rate cards** — category-scoped schedules that a `ProgramAdmin` drafts and later activates. A draft is created with a `policy_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique across all rate cards, a `product_category` from `APPAREL`, `ELECTRONICS`, `HOME`, `BEAUTY` and `GROCERY`, a non-empty `partner_type_scope` array listing the partner types the schedule applies to, and the two rates that make new-versus-returning differentiation possible: `new_customer_rate_bps`, applied when the buyer was classified as new, and `returning_customer_rate_bps`, applied when the buyer was not. Both rates are integers in basis points from 0 to 2000. The schedule's applicability is bounded by `effective_from`, an inclusive lower bound, and `effective_to`, an exclusive upper bound, both ISO 8601 UTC instants — the window is half-open, so an order placed exactly at `effective_from` is covered and one placed exactly at `effective_to` is not. A new rate card is written with `status` `DRAFT` and a null `superseded_by_policy_id`.

Creation performs no window arithmetic at all. An inverted window and a window overlapping an existing schedule are both accepted at draft time, deliberately, so that a program owner can stage a rate change before deciding when it takes effect. Both conditions are enforced only at activation, and in a fixed order: the ordering check comes first, so a card whose `effective_to` is at or before its `effective_from` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` before any overlap is even considered.

Activation moves the card `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and increments its `version`. It compares the activating window against every card that is currently `ACTIVE`, carries the same `product_category`, and shares at least one partner type with the activating card's `partner_type_scope`. Two windows conflict when the activating card starts strictly before the incumbent's end **and** the incumbent starts strictly before the activating card's end. A window that begins exactly where another ends is therefore adjacent rather than overlapping and is allowed. A conflicting window is refused with `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — except in one case. When the incumbent's window is **exactly equal** to the activating card's window, both bounds matching, the activation succeeds and supersedes the incumbent: the older card moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_policy_id` is set to the activating card's identifier, and its `version` increments. Exactly-equal windows supersede; partially overlapping windows are rejected.

Rate cards are individually readable and listable with optional filters on `status`, `product_category` and `superseded_by_policy_id`, which makes the supersession chain traversable after the fact.

### 3.4. Tracking Implementation and Click Ingestion

A partner promotes through **tracking links**, minted for it by a `ProgramAdmin` or `PartnerManager` while the partner is neither suspended nor terminated. Each link is created with a `landing_url` that must begin with `https://`, and a partner-chosen `sub_id` of 1 to 40 characters used for the partner's own reporting, which must be unique among that partner's links but may repeat across partners. Two attribution controls live on the link rather than on the program: `cookie_window_days` is the length of the attribution lookback in days, an integer from 1 to 90 that defaults to 30 when omitted, and `device_graph_enabled` is a boolean defaulting to false which, when true, permits cross-device matching on the household identifier as well as the device identifier. A new link is written with `status` `ACTIVE` and a null `disabled_reason`, and minting it increments the owning partner's `tracking_link_count` and `version`.

Links are disabled deliberately or as a consequence. A `ProgramAdmin`, `PartnerManager` or `ComplianceAnalyst` may disable an `ACTIVE` link by supplying a `reason`, which moves it `ACTIVE → DISABLED`, records that text as the link's `disabled_reason`, and increments the link's `version`; clicks already recorded through that link are retained for audit but immediately stop being eligible attribution candidates. Disabling a link that is already `DISABLED` is refused, as is ingesting a click through one. Links are also disabled by the server when their owning partner leaves good standing, and the `disabled_reason` then records which event caused it: `AFFILIATE_SUSPENDED` when the partner was suspended, `AFFILIATE_TERMINATED` when the partner was terminated, and `APPLICATION_REJECTED` when the partner was terminated by a rejected application. Each of those system disablings increments the link's `version` too.

Clicks arrive from the `TrackingIngestor` (a `ProgramAdmin` may also post them) against an `ACTIVE` link belonging to a partner in good standing. A click carries `occurred_at`, the client-asserted click instant as an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits; a `device_id` of 1 to 64 characters identifying the device fingerprint the click was seen on; a `household_id` of 1 to 64 characters used for device-graph matching; a `click_type` classifying the click at ingestion as `ORGANIC_CLICK`, `FORCED_CLICK` or `IFRAME_IMPRESSION`; a `referrer_domain` of 1 to 120 characters naming the referring domain; and, when the click came from paid search, the `paid_search_keyword` it was bought on, defaulting to null, together with `is_trademark_term`, a boolean defaulting to false that marks the keyword as containing a protected brand term. The click also records the `tracking_link_id` of the link it arrived through, taken from the addressed link rather than from the request body. Clicks are immutable once written and no operation updates one, so like monitoring scans they carry only an `id` and a `created_at`.

Ingesting a click has one consequence beyond the click row itself, and it is the reason attribution is version-guarded. For every order that is still `UNATTRIBUTED` and whose cookie window the new click falls inside, the server recomputes that order's `candidate_click_count` and increments that order's `version` by 1. A caller that read an order's candidate set and then ran attribution without re-reading it will find its `expected_conversion_version` stale, which is exactly the intent described in §2.7.

A link's clicks are listable with an optional `click_type` filter, and each link is individually readable.

### 3.5. Attribution, Channel Deduplication and Commission Booking

An **order** is posted to the program by the `TrackingIngestor` (or a `ProgramAdmin`) as a conversion candidate. It carries an `order_ref`, the retailer's own order identifier of 1 to 40 characters, unique across all orders so that a double-fired pixel is caught; an `order_total_cents` of at least 1, being the subtotal eligible for commission; a `product_category` from the five retail categories; a `customer_ref` of 1 to 64 characters as a pseudonymous buyer identifier; an `is_new_customer` boolean recording the retailer's own new-versus-returning classification; a `placed_at` instant, ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, which anchors the cookie window; a `device_id` naming the device the order was placed on; and an optional `household_id` naming that device's household. Three further fields describe competing influences on the sale: `coupon_code_used` records the coupon redeemed on the order and defaults to null; `other_channel_touch` records the last non-affiliate marketing touch the retailer saw, one of `NONE`, `PAID_SOCIAL`, `EMAIL`, `BRAND_PAID_SEARCH` or `DIRECT`, defaulting to `NONE`; and `other_channel_touch_at` is the instant of that touch, defaulting to null and required whenever `other_channel_touch` is anything other than `NONE`.

Each order carries two independent state dimensions on the same row, and they advance on different triggers. `attribution_status` is the credit dimension and takes `UNATTRIBUTED`, `AFFILIATE_ATTRIBUTED`, `CREDIT_REDUCED`, `DEDUPED_TO_OTHER_CHANNEL` or `DETACHED`. `validation_status` is the money dimension and takes `PENDING_VALIDATION`, `VALIDATED`, `REVERSED` or `CLAWED_BACK`. A new order begins at `UNATTRIBUTED` on the first and `PENDING_VALIDATION` on the second, with `gross_commission_cents`, `payable_commission_cents`, `clawed_back_cents` and `returned_amount_cents` all zeroed, and with `attributed_affiliate_id`, `attributed_click_id` and `commission_policy_id` all null. Order creation also computes `candidate_click_count` from the clicks already on file, without selecting a winner and without booking anything. Orders are individually readable and listable with optional filters on `attributed_affiliate_id`, `attributed_click_id`, `commission_policy_id`, `attribution_status` and `validation_status`.

Attribution is then resolved in a single run, guarded by `expected_conversion_version`, and only while the order is still `UNATTRIBUTED` — a second run on an order whose credit dimension has already moved is refused with `ERR_CONVERSION_ALREADY_ATTRIBUTED`. The run proceeds in five steps whose order is fixed.

**Step one builds the eligible click set.** A click qualifies only when all of the following hold: its `click_type` is `ORGANIC_CLICK`, so a `FORCED_CLICK` and an `IFRAME_IMPRESSION` never attribute at all; the tracking link that produced it is currently `ACTIVE`; the click's `occurred_at` is at or after the instant obtained by subtracting the link's own `cookie_window_days`, converted to days of 86,400 seconds each, from the order's `placed_at`; the click's `occurred_at` is strictly earlier than the order's `placed_at`, so the window is half-open with an inclusive lower bound and a strict upper bound; and the click matches the order's device. Device matching succeeds either when the click's `device_id` equals the order's `device_id`, or — only when the click's link has `device_graph_enabled` set to true — when the click's `household_id` is non-null and equals the order's `household_id`. The size of the resulting set is recorded as the order's `candidate_click_count`.

**Step two picks the winner.** The eligible set is ranked by `occurred_at` descending, and ties on that instant are broken by `created_at` descending, so the most recently recorded of two simultaneous clicks wins. The `id` field is never used as a tie-break. If the eligible set is empty the run still succeeds with 200: `candidate_click_count` is 0, the order stays `UNATTRIBUTED`, `gross_commission_cents` and `payable_commission_cents` are 0, no ledger entry is appended, and — because the order remains `UNATTRIBUTED` — a later run after a fresh click arrives can still win it. Two audit rows are written even in this case, one per algorithm variant, each with `candidate_click_count` 0, `credit_share_bps` 0, `computed_commission_cents` 0 and a `dedup_reason` of `NO_ELIGIBLE_CLICK`, and the order's `version` increments by 1.

**Step three deduplicates against other channels.** When the order's `other_channel_touch` is not `NONE` and its `other_channel_touch_at` is strictly later than the winning click's `occurred_at`, the affiliate loses last click. The order moves `UNATTRIBUTED → DEDUPED_TO_OTHER_CHANNEL`, its `attributed_affiliate_id` is left null while `attributed_click_id` still records the winning click, both `gross_commission_cents` and `payable_commission_cents` are 0, and the two audit rows are written with `credit_share_bps` 0, `computed_commission_cents` 0 and a `dedup_reason` of `OTHER_CHANNEL_LATER_TOUCH`, with the raw last-click variant marked as the selected one. No ledger entry is appended and every counter and balance on every partner is left exactly as it was; only the order's `version` increments. A tie does **not** dedupe: an `other_channel_touch_at` exactly equal to the winning click's `occurred_at` leaves the credit with the affiliate.

**Step four prices the order.** The applicable rate card is the one selected as described below. The rate used is `new_customer_rate_bps` when the order's `is_new_customer` is true and `returning_customer_rate_bps` when it is false. The raw commission — stored as `gross_commission_cents` — is the order's `order_total_cents` multiplied by that rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent.

**Step five applies the credit share.** The payable commission — stored as `payable_commission_cents` — is `gross_commission_cents` multiplied by the variant's `credit_share_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The two roundings are applied strictly in this order, gross first and payable second, each half up. Computing the payable figure in one expression from the order total, the rate and the credit share, dividing by one hundred million, is forbidden and yields a different, wrong answer.

Which variant supplies that credit share is the incrementality policy at the heart of this program, and both variants are **always** materialised so the divergence between them stays observable. The raw last-click variant, named `LAST_CLICK_RAW`, applies when the winning partner's `incrementality_tier` — read from the counters as they stand *before* this order is counted — is `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and it is not the case that the partner's `partner_type` is `COUPON` or `LOYALTY_CASHBACK` with a non-null `coupon_code_used` on the order. Under it `credit_share_bps` is 10000, so the payable commission equals the gross commission exactly, and the order moves `UNATTRIBUTED → AFFILIATE_ATTRIBUTED`. The adjusted variant, named `INCREMENTALITY_ADJUSTED`, applies when the winning partner's pre-state `incrementality_tier` is `LOW`, **or** when the partner's `partner_type` is `COUPON` or `LOYALTY_CASHBACK` and the order's `coupon_code_used` is non-null. Under it `credit_share_bps` is 3000, so the payable commission is thirty per cent of the gross rounded half up to a whole cent, the order moves `UNATTRIBUTED → CREDIT_REDUCED`, and the selected audit row carries a `dedup_reason` of `LOW_INCREMENTALITY_PARTNER`. On a reduced order `gross_commission_cents` keeps the unreduced figure, so the two money fields deliberately diverge on the same row and the size of the reduction stays auditable. The coupon-partner condition is an override in its own right: a `COUPON` or `LOYALTY_CASHBACK` partner sitting comfortably in the `HIGH` tier still has its credit reduced whenever the order redeemed a coupon.

Both crediting branches share the same preconditions beyond the version guard: the order must be `UNATTRIBUTED`, the eligible click set must be non-empty, the winning click's tracking link must be `ACTIVE`, the winning partner must be `ACTIVE`, an active rate card must cover the order's category and the partner's type at the order's `placed_at`, and either the order has no other-channel touch or that touch is at or before the winning click. Both branches then perform the same sequence of writes. The order records `attribution_status`, `attributed_affiliate_id`, `attributed_click_id`, `commission_policy_id`, `candidate_click_count`, `gross_commission_cents` and `payable_commission_cents`. Two audit rows are written, one per variant, with exactly one marked selected. An `ACCRUAL` ledger entry is appended for the partner in the amount of the payable commission, positive in sign. That ledger append raises the partner's `accrued_balance_cents` by the same amount. The partner's `attributed_conversion_count` increments, along with `new_customer_attributed_count` when the order was flagged as a new customer and `coupon_assisted_conversion_count` when the order redeemed a coupon, after which the partner's `incrementality_score_bps` and `incrementality_tier` are recomputed to their post-state values. Finally both the order's `version` and the partner's `version` increment by 1.

**Selecting the rate card.** The candidate set is every rate card whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `product_category` equals the order's `product_category`, whose `partner_type_scope` contains the winning partner's `partner_type`, and whose half-open window contains the order's `placed_at` — that is, `effective_from` is at or before `placed_at` and `effective_to` is strictly after it. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, and ties on that instant are broken by `created_at` ascending, so the most recently effective card wins and, among equally effective cards, the older row wins. The winner supplies the two rates and is stamped onto the order's `commission_policy_id`. When the candidate set is empty the whole run fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COMMISSION_POLICY` and **nothing** is written: no audit rows, no ledger entry, no version bump. That asymmetry against the empty-click-set case is intentional — an empty click set is a normal commercial outcome, whereas a missing rate card is a configuration error the program owner must fix.

**The audit rows.** Every attribution run writes exactly one row per declared variant against the order's `conversion_id`, so both algorithms remain inspectable even though only one drives the ledger. Each row records its `algorithm_variant` as `LAST_CLICK_RAW` or `INCREMENTALITY_ADJUSTED`; a `selected` boolean that is true on exactly one row per run; the `candidate_click_count` as the eligible set stood at evaluation; the `winning_click_occurred_at` instant of the click that won last-click ranking, left null when the candidate set was empty; a `device_match_mode` of `NONE`, `DEVICE_EXACT` when the winner matched on the device identifier, or `HOUSEHOLD_GRAPH` when it matched only through the household graph; the `credit_share_bps` that variant would grant, from 0 to 10000; the `computed_commission_cents` that variant would book; a `dedup_reason` of `NONE`, `OTHER_CHANNEL_LATER_TOUCH`, `LOW_INCREMENTALITY_PARTNER` or `NO_ELIGIBLE_CLICK` explaining why credit was withheld or reduced; and an `evaluated_at` instant read from the logical clock. These rows are append-only and are listable per order with an optional `algorithm_variant` filter.

**Measuring incrementality.** A partner's incrementality is recomputed from its own counters at three moments: at the end of an attribution run, at the end of an order adjustment, and when a partner is activated on admission. Two shares are derived first, both in basis points and neither rounded on its own. The coupon-assisted share is `coupon_assisted_conversion_count` multiplied by 10,000 and divided by `attributed_conversion_count`, where the divisor is treated as 1 whenever the count is 0 so a fresh partner is well defined. The new-customer share is `new_customer_attributed_count` multiplied by 10,000 and divided by that same guarded divisor. The score is then fifty-five hundredths of the new-customer share, plus forty-five hundredths of the amount by which the coupon-assisted share falls short of 10,000, minus a fixed discount chosen by `partner_type`: 0 for `CONTENT`, 0 for `INFLUENCER`, 500 for `PAID_SEARCH`, 500 for `SUB_NETWORK`, 1500 for `COUPON` and 2000 for `LOYALTY_CASHBACK`. That figure is rounded half up to a whole basis point and clamped into the range 0 to 10000, and the result is `incrementality_score_bps`. The tier follows: `LOW` when the score is 3500 or below, `MEDIUM` when it is from 3501 to 6000 inclusive, and `HIGH` when it is 6001 or above. A brand-new partner with all three counters at zero therefore scores 4500 less its type discount, which places `CONTENT` and `INFLUENCER` partners at 4500 in `MEDIUM`, `PAID_SEARCH` and `SUB_NETWORK` partners at 4000 in `MEDIUM`, `COUPON` partners at 3000 in `LOW`, and `LOYALTY_CASHBACK` partners at 2500 in `LOW`.

The evaluation order inside an attribution run is pinned and must not be rearranged, because the score depends on the counters and the counters depend on the attribution. First the winning click and the rate card are resolved. Second, the `incrementality_tier` is read from the counters as they stand before this order — the pre-state — and the variant is chosen from that reading. Third, the gross and payable commissions are computed. Fourth, and only then, `attributed_conversion_count` increments, together with `new_customer_attributed_count` when the buyer was new and `coupon_assisted_conversion_count` when a coupon was redeemed. Fifth, `incrementality_score_bps` and `incrementality_tier` are recomputed to their post-state values and persisted. The consequence is a rule worth stating plainly: an order never reduces its own credit share. Only the next order sees the effect of this one.

### 3.6. The Commission Ledger, Validation and Clawback

Every movement of money in this program is a **ledger entry**, and entries are appended, never edited. Each entry names the partner whose pool moves and, except for batch-level adjustments where it is null, the order that caused the movement. Its `entry_type` is one of `ACCRUAL`, `REVERSAL`, `CLAWBACK`, `ADJUSTMENT` or `SETTLEMENT`. Its `amount_cents` is signed: positive for an `ACCRUAL`, and negative for a `REVERSAL`, a `CLAWBACK`, a `SETTLEMENT`, and for an `ADJUSTMENT` raised after settlement. Its `memo` is a short machine-generated description of what moved and why. Its `payout_batch_id` is null while the entry is unbatched and carries a batch reference once a settlement run has claimed it. Entries are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `affiliate_id`, `conversion_id`, `payout_batch_id` and `entry_type`, and an `AffiliatePartner` may list its own.

Two partner balances summarise the ledger. `accrued_balance_cents` is the unreserved commission pool: the sum of every ledger entry for that partner that is not reserved by an approved batch. It may legitimately go **negative** after a clawback, and no rule prevents that — a partner who returns more than it has since earned owes the program money. `reserved_balance_cents` is the amount moved out of the accrued pool by a batch approval and not yet paid or released. `lifetime_paid_cents` is the cumulative sum of the net amounts across paid lines and only ever grows.

A commission is not final when it is booked. An order sits at `PENDING_VALIDATION` until the retailer's return window has closed, and the sweep that closes it runs at the start of payout-batch generation. That sweep moves an order `PENDING_VALIDATION → VALIDATED` when the order's `attribution_status` is `AFFILIATE_ATTRIBUTED` or `CREDIT_REDUCED` and thirty days have elapsed since its `placed_at` as measured against the logical clock. The boundary is inclusive: an order whose `placed_at` is exactly thirty days before the current clock validates in that sweep. Each order the sweep moves has its `version` incremented.

Cancellations and returns are recorded as **order adjustments** by the `TrackingIngestor`, a `ProgramAdmin` or a `FinanceAnalyst`. An adjustment supplies an `adjustment_type` of `FULL_CANCELLATION`, `FULL_RETURN` or `PARTIAL_RETURN`, the `returned_amount_cents` of merchandise coming back on this event, and the `occurred_at` instant of the event. Adjustments are refused on an order that is still `UNATTRIBUTED`, and on one whose `validation_status` is already `REVERSED` or `CLAWED_BACK`. Before anything else the cumulative arithmetic is checked: the order's accumulated `returned_amount_cents` plus the amount on this request must not exceed the order's `order_total_cents`, which also has the effect of requiring a `FULL_RETURN` or `FULL_CANCELLATION` to supply exactly the outstanding remainder.

**Adjustments inside the unvalidated window.** While the order's `validation_status` is `PENDING_VALIDATION`, the entire accrual is undone regardless of how much of the order came back — the retailer has not yet accepted the sale, so the program does not pro-rate it. The magnitude reversed is the order's `payable_commission_cents`, appended as a `REVERSAL` entry against that order in the negative of that amount. That append lowers the partner's `accrued_balance_cents` by the same magnitude. The detachment then decrements the partner's `attributed_conversion_count`, and `new_customer_attributed_count` and `coupon_assisted_conversion_count` wherever they had been incremented for this order, and recomputes the partner's `incrementality_score_bps` and `incrementality_tier`. Both the order's `version` and the partner's `version` increment. A `FULL_CANCELLATION` in this window, permitted from either `AFFILIATE_ATTRIBUTED` or `CREDIT_REDUCED` and requiring the accumulated return to reach the full `order_total_cents`, drives the order to `DETACHED` on the credit dimension and `REVERSED` on the money dimension, clears `attributed_affiliate_id` back to null, sets `clawed_back_cents` to the reversed `payable_commission_cents` and `returned_amount_cents` to the full `order_total_cents`. A `FULL_RETURN` arriving while the order is still `PENDING_VALIDATION` behaves identically: `validation_status` becomes `REVERSED`, `attribution_status` becomes `DETACHED`, and a `REVERSAL` entry for the full payable commission is appended with the same balance, counter and incrementality consequences. For an order that had its credit reduced, the reversal magnitude is the **reduced** `payable_commission_cents`, never the raw `gross_commission_cents` — the program only ever unwinds what it actually booked.

**Adjustments after validation.** Once the order is `VALIDATED` the clawback is pro-rata on what came back, and the arithmetic is expressed as a target rather than an increment. The target total is the order's `payable_commission_cents` multiplied by the cumulative returned amount, divided by the order's `order_total_cents`, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The magnitude of this adjustment is that target total minus the `clawed_back_cents` already recorded on the order, and `clawed_back_cents` accumulates to the new target while `returned_amount_cents` accumulates the merchandise value. Computing each increment independently rather than differencing against the running total lets two partial returns drift by a cent, so the target-total form is authoritative. The entry raised is a `CLAWBACK`, except that it is an `ADJUSTMENT` of the same magnitude when the order's original `ACCRUAL` entry has already been stamped to a batch that is `PAID`. The order's `validation_status` becomes `CLAWED_BACK` when the cumulative return has reached the full `order_total_cents`, and otherwise stays `VALIDATED` so that further partial returns can follow. Crucially, `attribution_status` is **not** changed and the partner's attribution counters are **not** decremented in this window: the conversion really happened, and its incrementality signal is retained even though its money is being recovered. The append lowers the partner's `accrued_balance_cents` by the magnitude, and both the order's and the partner's `version` increment.

Where the new entry lands depends on the batch its accrual belongs to. When the original `ACCRUAL` is stamped to a batch that is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED`, the new entry is stamped to that same batch, which triggers a recompute of that partner's line in it and of the batch totals; if the recomputed net amount on that line falls below the 2,500-cent minimum, the line moves `INCLUDED → HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` and the batch totals are recomputed again without it. When the accrual's batch is already `PAID`, the `ADJUSTMENT` entry is deliberately left unstamped so that a later settlement run claims it, and the paid batch's totals are never rewritten.

### 3.7. Compliance Monitoring, Violation Cases and Fraud Scoring

Compliance watches four surfaces — `GOOGLE_ADS`, `BING_ADS`, `SHOPPING_FEED` and `EXTENSION_STORE` — and records what it sees as a **monitoring scan** against one partner over one closed observation window. A scan carries the partner, the `engine` observed, a `window_start` and a `window_end` that are both inclusive bounds, and four measured signals: `trademark_hits`, the count of ad impressions bought on protected brand terms, which may not be negative; `forced_click_ratio_bps`, the share of the partner's clicks classified as forced, in basis points from 0 to 10000; `cookie_stuffing_index`, a composite stuffing indicator on a 0-to-100 scale; and `unauthorized_coupon_hits`, the count of unauthorized coupon placements observed, which may not be negative. The row also records `opened_case_count`, the number of violation cases this scan created. Scans are append-only observations and are never edited, so like clicks they carry only an `id` and a `created_at` and have neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `ComplianceAnalyst` records them, and either may list the scans recorded against a partner, optionally filtered by `engine`.

Monitoring is rate-limited per surface. A new scan is refused when its window overlaps another scan's window for the same partner and the same `engine`, when the two windows merely touch at an endpoint, or when the new window's nearer edge sits within twenty-four hours of the existing window — the cooldown interval is closed, so a gap of exactly twenty-four hours is still a collision. A different `engine` for the same partner, or the same `engine` for a different partner, is never a collision.

Recording a scan is the program's only automated source of violation cases. The four signals are examined in a **fixed order**, and each condition that holds opens at most one case for this scan: `trademark_hits` greater than zero opens a `TRADEMARK_BIDDING` case; `forced_click_ratio_bps` of 1500 or more opens a `FORCED_CLICK` case; `cookie_stuffing_index` of 60 or more opens a `COOKIE_STUFFING` case; and `unauthorized_coupon_hits` greater than zero opens an `UNAUTHORIZED_COUPON` case. All four may fire from one scan, giving `opened_case_count` of 4. Each case opened this way records the scan in its `source_scan_id` and leaves `opened_by_user_id` null, because no human filed it. The partner's `open_violation_case_count` rises by the number of cases opened and both fraud-score variants are recomputed.

A `ComplianceAnalyst` or `ProgramAdmin` may also file a case by hand, supplying the partner, the `violation_type`, an `evidence_ref` of one to 120 characters pointing at the evidence artifact, and the `detected_at` instant at which the violation was observed — that instant, not the filing time, anchors the trailing decay. A hand-filed case may optionally cite a `source_scan_id`, and if it names no scan on file the request is refused as an unknown case reference. A hand-filed case records the filer in `opened_by_user_id`. However it was opened, a case starts at `OPEN` with `failed_verification_count` of zero and a `severity_weight` copied from the fixed severity table at creation: 40 for `COOKIE_STUFFING`, 30 for `FORCED_CLICK`, 25 for `UNAUTHORIZED_COUPON` and 20 for `TRADEMARK_BIDDING`. Cases are readable individually and listable with filters on `affiliate_id`, `source_scan_id`, `state` and `violation_type`.

A case moves through a **cyclic** remediation lifecycle, driven by a single transition operation that takes an `action`, a `note` recorded into `last_note`, and — depending on the action — a `remediation_evidence_ref` or a `verify_outcome`. From `OPEN` a `ComplianceAnalyst` requests remediation, moving the case to `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED`, which is refused if the partner is already `TERMINATED`. From `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` the partner itself submits remediation, moving the case to `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and storing the `remediation_evidence_ref`, which must be present; the caller must be the partner the case is against and that partner must not be `TERMINATED`. From `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` a `ComplianceAnalyst` verifies. A verification whose outcome is cleared moves the case to the terminal `VERIFIED_CLEARED`, lowers the partner's `open_violation_case_count` by one and recomputes both fraud variants. A verification whose outcome is failed sends the case **back to `OPEN`** and raises its `failed_verification_count` by one; both variants are recomputed, though the point-in-time figure does not move because `OPEN` and `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` both count at full weight. That loop may repeat any number of times. Either verification requires that the acting user is not the user who filed the case. From `OPEN` a `ComplianceAnalyst` may escalate to `ESCALATED`, but only once `failed_verification_count` has reached two — an escalation attempted earlier is refused as an invalid transition. From `ESCALATED` only a `ProgramAdmin` may verify with an upheld outcome, moving the case to the terminal `UPHELD` and recomputing both variants. Every transition increments the case's `version`.

Two fraud figures are maintained on every partner, recomputed and persisted together on every trigger — the creation of a case by a scan, the manual filing of a case, and every case transition — and neither is ever derived from the other.

The **point-in-time** figure lives in `fraud_risk_score`. It counts only cases whose `state` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, `ESCALATED` or `UPHELD`, sums their `severity_weight` values, and caps the result at 100. It applies no decay at all and ignores `detected_at` entirely, so a three-year-old `OPEN` case still counts at its full weight, and a `VERIFIED_CLEARED` case contributes exactly zero. It is an integer from 0 to 100.

The **trailing** figure lives in `fraud_risk_score_trailing`. It counts cases in **any** state, `VERIFIED_CLEARED` included, whose `detected_at` is at or after ninety days before the current logical clock; a case older than that drops out of the figure entirely. Each surviving case contributes its `severity_weight` multiplied by an age decay and by a state factor. The age in days is the whole number of complete days between the case's `detected_at` and the clock, discarding any partial day. The decay is 1.00 minus 0.01 for each whole day of age, with a floor of 0.20 that it never falls below. The state factor is 0.25 for a `VERIFIED_CLEARED` case and 1.00 for every other state. The contributions are summed, capped at 100, and rounded half up to one decimal place. Because the two figures select different case sets, the trailing figure can be strictly lower than the point-in-time figure for the same cases when they are old, and strictly higher when the only cases on file are recent and cleared.

**Auto-suspension.** Whenever a recompute leaves an `ACTIVE` partner's point-in-time `fraud_risk_score` at 70 or above, the program suspends that partner in the same request, setting `status` to `SUSPENDED` and `suspension_cause` to `FRAUD_SCORE_BREACH`, and the suspension cascade described in §2.11 runs in full: every `ACTIVE` partner whose `parent_affiliate_id` is this partner is suspended with `suspension_cause` `PARENT_SUSPENSION`; for every partner newly suspended in the request, its `ACTIVE` tracking links are disabled with `disabled_reason` `AFFILIATE_SUSPENDED` and its `INCLUDED` payout lines on batches that are `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` move to `HELD_FRAUD` with `hold_reason` `AFFILIATE_SUSPENDED`; and the `network_fee_cents`, `withholding_cents` and `net_payable_cents` totals of every touched batch are recomputed. Sub-affiliate suspension is a pure parent-propagation hop: a child's own fraud score is **not** re-evaluated and its `suspension_cause` records the parent, not a breach of its own. Every partner touched has its `version` incremented.

Compliance may also suspend by hand through the partner status-change operation, which takes a `target_status` of `SUSPENDED`, `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATED` together with a `reason` for the change. A `target_status` of `SUSPENDED` is available to a `ComplianceAnalyst` from either `PENDING_APPLICATION` or `ACTIVE`, sets `suspension_cause` to `COMPLIANCE_ACTION` and runs the same cascade. Freezing an applicant mid-review leaves its application at `SCREENED` but that application can no longer be approved. Reinstatement is a `ProgramAdmin` action on a `SUSPENDED` partner and carries three gates: the partner's `open_violation_case_count` must be zero, its `fraud_risk_score_trailing` must be strictly below 25.0, and if it has a `parent_affiliate_id` that parent must itself be `ACTIVE`. Reinstatement restores `status` to `ACTIVE` and `suspension_cause` to `NONE` and nothing else: tracking links are **not** re-enabled, so the partner must be minted new ones, and payout-line holds are **not** released, so a `FinanceController` must release each line explicitly. Termination is available to a `ProgramAdmin` from `PENDING_APPLICATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, disables the partner's `ACTIVE` tracking links with `disabled_reason` `AFFILIATE_TERMINATED`, and is final — no transition leaves `TERMINATED`.

### 3.8. Tax Documentation

Payment requires a tax form on file. A partner's own user, a `ProgramAdmin` or a `FinanceController` files one, giving the `form_type` — `W9` for a domestic filer or `W8BEN` for a foreign one — the `tax_id_last4`, which is exactly four characters of the tax identifier, a `treaty_withholding_bps` from 0 to 3000 claiming a treaty rate, and the `expires_on` instant after which the document is no longer valid. A `W9` filing must declare a `treaty_withholding_bps` of zero, since there is no treaty to claim. Filing creates the document at `PENDING`, sets the partner's mirrored `tax_document_status` to `PENDING`, and increments the partner's `version` while leaving the partner's own `status` untouched. A partner may hold many documents over time and may list its own, filtered by `status`.

Only a `FinanceController` verifies a filed document, declaring a `verification_outcome` of either `VALID` to accept it or `REJECTED` to reject it. Accepting requires the document to be `PENDING` and its `expires_on` to be strictly after the current logical clock, and moves it to `VALID` recording the verifier in `verified_by_user_id`. Rejecting requires only that the document is `PENDING` and moves it to the terminal `REJECTED`. Either outcome is mirrored onto the partner's `tax_document_status`, but **only when the verified document is that partner's most recent one ordered by `created_at` descending** — verifying an older document leaves the mirrored status alone, because the mirror always reflects the newest filing. Both the document's and the partner's `version` increment.

A `VALID` document does not stay valid forever. A tax-status refresh runs at the start of payout-batch generation and again at the start of batch validation: every `VALID` document whose `expires_on` is at or before the current logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, and where that document is its partner's most recent one the partner's `tax_document_status` is mirrored to `EXPIRED`, with both `version` values incremented. The expiry boundary is therefore inclusive on the expired side, while the boundary for accepting a document at verification is strict — a document expiring at exactly the current instant can no longer be accepted and, once accepted earlier, expires at that instant.

### 3.9. Settlement: Payout Batches, Lines and Network Reconciliation

Money leaves the program in **payout batches**. A batch is one settlement run for one network over one period, created by a `FinanceAnalyst` who names the `network_id`, a `period_start` that is an inclusive lower bound and a `period_end` that is an **exclusive** upper bound. The creator is recorded in `prepared_by_user_id` and the batch opens at `DRAFT`.

Generation happens in a fixed sequence inside that one request. First the validation sweep of §3.6 runs, moving every qualifying order from `PENDING_VALIDATION` to `VALIDATED`. Then the tax-status refresh of §3.8 runs, expiring stale documents and mirroring the result onto their partners. Then the batch claims every ledger entry that is still unstamped — `payout_batch_id` null — whose order is `VALIDATED` or `CLAWED_BACK` and whose order's `placed_at` falls inside the period, at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. Each claimed entry has this batch stamped onto its `payout_batch_id`, and one **payout line** is created per distinct partner among them. If no entry qualifies, the request fails with the no-eligible-lines error and **no batch row is created at all**, so a retry after the validation sweep has caught up can still succeed.

Each line settles one partner's position inside the batch, and is computed over the entries stamped to this batch for that partner. Its `gross_cents` is the sum of the `amount_cents` of the `ACCRUAL` entries. Its `clawback_cents` is the absolute value of the sum of the `amount_cents` of the `REVERSAL`, `CLAWBACK` and `ADJUSTMENT` entries. Its `net_before_fees_cents` is `gross_cents` less `clawback_cents`. Its `conversion_count` is the number of distinct orders behind the line's `ACCRUAL` entries. Its `network_fee_cents` is the network's percentage component — `net_before_fees_cents` multiplied by the network's `fee_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to a whole cent — plus the network's `flat_fee_cents` multiplied by the line's `conversion_count`; a network configured for one fee model contributes nothing from the other, because the unused field is pinned to zero at registration. The withholding base is `net_before_fees_cents` less `network_fee_cents`. The line's `withholding_bps` comes from the partner's most recent tax document ordered by `created_at` descending: when that document's `status` is `VALID` the line uses its `treaty_withholding_bps`, and in every other case — no document at all, `PENDING`, `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED` — the line uses the default backup withholding rate of 2400 basis points. The `withholding_cents` is the withholding base multiplied by `withholding_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. The line's `net_cents` is the withholding base less `withholding_cents`.

**The rounding order is pinned and observable.** Each line is rounded to whole cents first, and the batch totals are plain integer sums of the already-rounded per-line values: `gross_commission_cents` sums the lines' `gross_cents`, `clawback_cents` sums the lines' `clawback_cents`, `network_fee_cents` sums the lines' `network_fee_cents`, `withholding_cents` sums the lines' `withholding_cents`, and `net_payable_cents` sums the lines' `net_cents`. Summing the unrounded per-line products and rounding once at the batch level is forbidden and produces a different, wrong answer, typically differing by a cent per line. Which lines contribute also differs by total: `gross_commission_cents` and `clawback_cents` include **every** line whatever its state, except that a `VOIDED` line contributes to neither, so that the reconciliation check sees the full statement-side gross; while `network_fee_cents`, `withholding_cents` and `net_payable_cents` include only lines that are `INCLUDED` or `PAID`. The batch's `line_count` is the number of lines generated for it.

A line opens at `INCLUDED` and can be held for three distinct reasons, each recording its own `hold_reason`. A line whose recomputed `net_cents` falls below the 2,500-cent minimum payout moves to `HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` with `hold_reason` `BELOW_MINIMUM_PAYOUT`; the boundary is inclusive on the pay side, so exactly 2500 stays `INCLUDED` and 2499 holds. That check runs at generation, again at validation, and again after a clawback recompute. A line whose partner's `tax_document_status` is anything other than `VALID` at validation time moves to `HELD_TAX_DOC` with `hold_reason` `TAX_DOCUMENT_NOT_VALID`. A line whose partner is not `ACTIVE` at validation time, or whose partner is suspended later while the batch is still `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED`, moves to `HELD_FRAUD` with `hold_reason` `AFFILIATE_SUSPENDED`. A held line is excluded from the batch's payable totals, which are recomputed when it leaves them. Every line movement increments the line's `version`.

**Validation and reconciliation.** A `FinanceAnalyst` validates a `DRAFT` batch, supplying the `statement_gross_cents` and `statement_fee_cents` that the network reported for the period. The gross check runs first and is exact: if `statement_gross_cents` differs at all from the batch's own recomputed `gross_commission_cents`, the request fails with the network-fee-variance error and nothing whatsoever is written. Then the fee variance is measured. The `network_fee_variance_cents` is `statement_fee_cents` less the batch's `network_fee_cents`, and it keeps its sign, so a statement that undercharges records a negative value. The `network_fee_variance_bps` is the absolute value of that variance multiplied by 10000 and divided by the batch's `network_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole basis point; when the batch's own fee is zero the divisor is treated as 1 rather than dividing by zero. A variance of exactly zero cents sets `reconciliation_status` to `MATCHED`. Otherwise a `network_fee_variance_bps` of 50 or less sets `reconciliation_status` to `ACCEPTED_WITH_VARIANCE` — the boundary is inclusive — and anything larger fails with the network-fee-variance error. Accepting a variance **annotates only**: it records the two variance fields and the status, and adjusts neither `network_fee_cents`, nor `net_payable_cents`, nor any ledger entry. The retailer's own computation stays authoritative and the network's statement difference is merely noted. A successful validation refreshes tax-document expiry, applies the tax and fraud holds, recomputes every surviving line and the batch totals, records the reconciliation fields, moves the batch to `VALIDATED` and increments its `version`.

A `FinanceController` releases a hold on one line, giving a `reason`. Release is available only while the owning batch is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED`, and only for a `HELD_TAX_DOC` or `HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` line — a `HELD_FRAUD` line is never releasable through this channel and is refused with its own dedicated error, which outranks the tax check so that a fraud-held line on a partner with an expired form still reports the fraud hold. Release additionally requires the partner to hold a tax document that is `VALID` and whose `expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock. On success the line returns to `INCLUDED`, its `hold_reason` clears to null, `released_by_user_id` records the controller, the line is recomputed, and the batch's `network_fee_cents`, `withholding_cents` and `net_payable_cents` are recomputed with the batch's `version` incremented while its `state` stays exactly as it was. Releasing a below-minimum hold waives the minimum-payout floor for that line only.

**Approval, payment and voiding.** A `FinanceController` approves a `VALIDATED` batch, supplying the expected batch version. Approval requires the acting user to differ from the batch's `prepared_by_user_id`, and requires the batch's `net_payable_cents` to be at most 5,000,000 cents. On success the batch moves to `APPROVED` with `approved_by_user_id` recorded, and for every `INCLUDED` line the line's `net_before_fees_cents` moves out of the partner's `accrued_balance_cents` and into its `reserved_balance_cents`, with each reserved partner's `version` incremented. Note carefully that the amount reserved is the pre-fee figure, not the line's `net_cents`.

A `FinanceController` pays an `APPROVED` batch, supplying a `payment_reference`, which must be present. The batch moves to `PAID`, records the reference, and every `INCLUDED` line moves to `PAID`. For each paid line a `SETTLEMENT` ledger entry is appended for the negative of that line's `net_before_fees_cents`, stamped to this batch; the partner's `reserved_balance_cents` falls by that same `net_before_fees_cents` and its `lifetime_paid_cents` rises by the line's `net_cents` — the difference between the two is the network's fee and the tax withheld, which the partner never receives. Lines still sitting in any held state are left held and their ledger entries are **un-stamped** back to a null `payout_batch_id` so a later batch can claim them. The batch's `version` and every paid partner's `version` increment.

A `FinanceController` may void a batch that is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `APPROVED`, supplying a reason recorded in `void_reason`. Every line moves to `VOIDED` whatever state it was in, every ledger entry stamped to this batch is un-stamped back to null, and **no ledger entry is created or deleted** — the audit trail of what was earned is untouched by the failure to pay it. For a batch that had reached `APPROVED`, each reserved line's `net_before_fees_cents` moves back out of the partner's `reserved_balance_cents` and into its `accrued_balance_cents`, with each released partner's `version` incremented; for a `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` batch there is no balance movement at all, because neither state has reserved anything. Voiding is therefore an exact compensating action for approval: approving and then voiding leaves the sum of `accrued_balance_cents` and `reserved_balance_cents` and the entire ledger row set exactly as they were beforehand. Both `PAID` and `VOIDED` are terminal — a paid or voided batch accepts no further transition, which is also what makes a repeated payment or a repeated void answer with the terminal-batch error rather than acting twice.
