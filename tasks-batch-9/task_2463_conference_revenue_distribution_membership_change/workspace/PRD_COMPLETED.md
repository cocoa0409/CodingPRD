# Conference Revenue Distribution & Membership Change API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A collegiate athletic conference office is, financially speaking, a pooling arrangement wrapped in a governance charter. Media rights money, postseason payouts and championship gate receipts arrive at the conference rather than at its member institutions, and the office must then divide that money according to formulas the members themselves voted on — formulas that weigh media appearances, postseason performance, validated postseason units and the phase-in status of recently admitted schools. At the same time, the membership itself is not static. Institutions petition to join and pay entry fees over several instalments; incumbents file withdrawal notices and owe exit settlements scaled by how much notice they gave; grant-of-rights obligations mean a departing school's future distributions are held back against what it owes. Every one of these facts changes the arithmetic of the next distribution.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that office. It covers eight cooperating concerns: the conference record and its reserve policy, the member roster and its admission and departure lifecycle, revenue pools and the distribution runs computed against them, postseason appearances and the units they mint, weighted governance voting over membership actions and annual budgets, the competitive schedule with its protected rivalries and travel-partner pairings, championship event hosting and settlement, and the audit reporting that attests every posted distribution back to the membership. The service is deliberately conservative: money-moving operations are split across roles so that no single user both computes and posts a distribution, every cent of a pool is accounted for by a reconciliation identity that must balance exactly, and any restatement of revenue invalidates the allocations computed from the stale figure rather than silently leaving them in place.

Two engineering constraints shape the whole design. First, all money is held as integer cents and all rates as integer basis points, so that no floating-point rounding is ever observable in a response — where rounding is required, the direction is pinned per formula. Second, the parts of the system that several actors touch concurrently — a revenue pool being allocated, a membership action being balloted, the conference reserve, a member's profile — are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, so a client working from a stale read is rejected rather than allowed to overwrite state it never observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system. Once the caller is identified, the role is matched against the set of roles authorised for the addressed endpoint (the authorisation column of the endpoint table in §4); a role outside that set is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authorisation is evaluated immediately after authentication and before any resource is loaded, so an unauthorised caller learns nothing about whether the addressed record exists.

There are exactly eight roles. A **`Commissioner`** runs the conference office: seating charter members, amending member profiles, proposing, withdrawing, closing and executing membership actions, filing and rescinding withdrawal notices, cancelling games and championship events, awarding championship hosting, voiding bowl appearances, and recording the membership's acceptance of an issued audit report. A **`FinanceOfficer`** owns the money-shaping side: creating revenue pools and driving their lifecycle, computing distribution runs, settling championship events, drafting budget proposals and opening and closing budget voting, and advancing a member's distribution phase; a `FinanceOfficer` may never post a run, because posting belongs to the `Treasurer` and additionally requires a different user than the one who computed it. A **`Treasurer`** moves cash: posting and reversing distribution runs, recording entry-fee instalments, and contributing to or withdrawing from the conference reserve; a `Treasurer` may not post a run whose `computed_by_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`. An **`Auditor`** attests runs to the membership by drafting and issuing audit reports, and may not issue a report on a run it computed itself. A **`ComplianceOfficer`** reviews postseason appearances — beginning review, requesting revision, resubmitting, approving and voiding — closes voting on membership actions, and marks games played; a `ComplianceOfficer` may not close voting on an action it proposed. A **`ScheduleCoordinator`** builds the competitive calendar: registering venues, creating protected rivalries, creating, playing and cancelling games, and awarding and cancelling championship events. A **`PlatformAdmin`** bootstraps a deployment: setting the logical clock, initialising the conference, registering venues, creating and amending members, and reading everything. A **`MemberRepresentative`** acts on behalf of one member institution.

Seven of the eight roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Commissioner`, `FinanceOfficer`, `Treasurer`, `Auditor`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ScheduleCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `computed_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` on a distribution run, as `proposed_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a membership action, as `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a budget proposal, and as `issued_by_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on an audit report — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. `MemberRepresentative` is the exception: when `X-User-Role` is `MemberRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member institution, and ownership checks compare it against the member the request targets. Concretely, for a member profile amendment, an entry-fee payment, a bowl-appearance submission, a bowl-appearance resubmission, and a ballot cast on either a membership action or a budget proposal, `X-User-ID` must equal the `Member` `id` named in the path or carried in the request body as `member_id`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_MEMBER_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A bowl-appearance resubmission is the one case where the request carries no member identifier of its own, so the check reads the `member_id` of the appearance named in the path instead; if that appearance identifier does not resolve there is nothing to compare against, the ownership check is skipped, and the request falls through to `ERR_BOWL_APPEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`. Likewise a ballot whose body omits `member_id`, or carries it as something other than a string, has nothing to compare and is left to the request-shape rung to refuse. That ownership check is the third check in the ladder — it runs after authentication and role authorisation, and before the conference-initialisation check, path resolution and every business rule. A ballot is also stamped with `cast_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of the representative who cast it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and the smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400), as is a `limit` or `offset` supplied as anything that is not a base-ten integer; an empty value for either is treated as though the parameter had been omitted. Unless a specific endpoint states another ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest record appears first and pagination over an unchanging data set is stable. For every list response, the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before the window was applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current window. All other filters offered by a list endpoint are additive: supplying several narrows the result to records matching all of them. A filter is applied only when it is present and non-empty, and an unparseable filter value is never an error — an integer filter such as `fiscal_year` or `phase_year` given a non-numeric value, or a boolean filter such as `media_designated` given something outside `true`/`false`/`1`/`0`, simply matches no record and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0.

### 2.4. The Conference Singleton and the Logical Clock

The service holds exactly one conference office record, and it must exist before anything else can be written. Initialising it is a `PlatformAdmin` capability, and a second attempt to initialise while a conference record already exists is rejected with `ERR_CONFERENCE_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). Conversely, every write in the system other than conference initialisation itself and the logical-clock capability is rejected with `ERR_CONFERENCE_NOT_INITIALIZED` (HTTP 409) while no conference exists; read endpoints report the same code for the same reason. In the validation ladder, the conference-existence check runs after the three access-control checks and before path resolution, request-shape validation and all business rules. Because there is only ever one conference, the `conference_id` a request body carries is validated only as a non-empty string: it is never resolved against the singleton's `id`, so it raises no 404 of its own, but it is stored verbatim and it is what scopes the membership — institution-code uniqueness, rivalry uniqueness, the roster a ballot's eligible weight is summed over, and the allocation basis of a distribution run all compare against it. A body that names an identifier other than the real conference's therefore creates a record in a scope of its own rather than being rejected.

Time is deterministic. The server never reads the host wall clock: instead it reads a logical clock, which a `PlatformAdmin` may set to an explicit instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, and the capability returns the newly established instant as `current_utc`. Until it is set, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Every server-side reference to "now" — the instant a withdrawal notice is filed, the instant a distribution run is posted, resource creation and update timestamps — resolves against this logical clock.

The conference record itself carries the office's `name`, the `fiscal_year_start_month` in which its fiscal year opens (a calendar month from 1 through 12, defaulting to 7), the `current_fiscal_year` it is operating in (defaulting to 2027), the cash `unit_value_cents` of one validated postseason unit (defaulting to 34,000,000 cents, that is 340,000 dollars), and `phase_schedule_years`, the number of phasing years a petitioning institution serves before reaching full share (at least 1, defaulting to 3). Its `governance_status` is `FORMING` while charter membership is still being seated and `OPERATING` once the first budget proposal has been approved; those are the only two values it takes, and it starts at `FORMING`. Eight further conference-level figures are maintained by the system rather than supplied: `approved_operating_expense_cents` and `approved_governance_expense_cents` (both starting at 0), `reserve_target_cents` and `reserve_balance_cents` (both starting at 0), `reserve_posture` (starting at `OPEN`), `funded_ratio_bps` (starting at 10000), `escrow_balance_cents` (starting at 0), `governance_fee_revenue_cents` (starting at 0), and `validated_postseason_unit_count` (starting at 0). A freshly initialised conference is at `version` 1.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID, with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Most resources also carry three housekeeping properties: `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with three fractional digits; `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that record; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation. Three resource types are deliberately lighter: a distribution line, a postseason unit and a ballot carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have neither an `updated_at` nor a `version` field, because they are never independently updated — their state changes only as a consequence of a transition on their parent, and their status field is the only thing that moves.

When an operation mutates several records, each mutated record's own `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed. A cascade therefore leaves a version trail across the whole affected graph, and that trail is exactly what the optimistic concurrency rules in §2.6 detect.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Four write operations require the client to prove it is working from a current read, by echoing back the `version` it last observed on a parent record. Each of these fields is a required input, not an optional one, and each mismatch has its own error identifier.

Computing a distribution run on a revenue pool requires `expected_pool_version` to equal that pool's current `version`; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because creating a run legitimately supersedes any earlier computed run on the same pool, and because a championship settlement or a book reopening may have restated the pool's `gross_revenue_cents` or cleared its `audited_expense_cents`; a writer holding a stale view of the pool would otherwise supersede an allocation it never saw and compute against figures that no longer apply.

Casting a ballot on a membership action requires `expected_action_version` to equal that action's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_ACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An amendment loop voids every ballot already cast and advances the action's version, so a ballot echoing an older version would be counted against proposal terms the representative never read.

A reserve transfer requires `expected_conference_version` to equal the conference record's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_CONFERENCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Posting a distribution run, approving a budget and approving a bowl appearance all move `reserve_balance_cents`, `reserve_target_cents` or `escrow_balance_cents`, so a transfer sized against a stale balance could drive `reserve_posture` to an unintended value.

Amending a member profile requires `expected_member_version` to equal that member's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Phase advances, withdrawal notices, rescissions and any schedule-compliance recomputation that actually changes the stored score all advance a member's version, so a stale profile write could reinstate a travel partner or a home venue that newer state has already invalidated.

No other write in the system takes an expected-version field. The four version checks sit at one point in the validation ladder: after access control, conference existence, path resolution, request-shape validation, foreign-key resolution, and the uniqueness and pairing checks, but before every state-machine precondition and every business rule. A stale version is therefore reported even when the addressed record is in a status that would also have refused the operation.

### 2.7. Integer Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer cents and all rates and weights are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent. No amount is ever stored or returned as a decimal fraction, and no computation is performed in floating point, so the results in this document are exactly reproducible. Where a formula divides, the rounding direction is pinned by that formula and is never left to the implementation's default: the reserve sweep, the provisional withholding, the bowl reimbursement ceiling, the exit-settlement base assessment, the exit-settlement surcharge and the championship host share all round **up** to the next whole cent; per-line distribution shares, the grant-of-rights cap, the approval ratio, the funded ratio and the phase multiplier all round **down** to the next whole cent or basis point. Threshold comparisons that would otherwise require division are performed by cross-multiplication — the quorum test and the reserve-posture bands both work this way — so that no intermediate rounding can shift a boundary. Wherever a threshold appears in this document, whether the boundary itself passes or fails is stated explicitly; there are no approximations anywhere in the specification.

### 2.8. Validation Order, Path Resolution and Foreign Keys

The service evaluates checks in a fixed order, and the first failure determines the response. The full ladder is: authentication (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), role authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), member ownership for a `MemberRepresentative` (`ERR_NOT_MEMBER_OWNER`), conference existence (`ERR_CONFERENCE_NOT_INITIALIZED`, or `ERR_CONFERENCE_ALREADY_EXISTS` on initialisation), path-identifier resolution (the 404 family), request-shape validation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), pagination bounds (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`), body foreign-key resolution (the 404 family again), institution-code uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_INSTITUTION_CODE`), member-pair distinctness (`ERR_MEMBER_PAIR_INVALID`), rivalry uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RIVALRY`), the optimistic-concurrency version checks (the 412 family), the charter-window check (`ERR_CHARTER_WINDOW_CLOSED`), the per-entity status checks (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_APPEARANCE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_GAME_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`, in that relative order), the amendment budget (`ERR_AMEND_LIMIT_REACHED`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), the already-distributed guard on voiding an appearance (`ERR_UNITS_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`), the temporal checks (`ERR_MEMBER_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_YEAR`), the remaining shape constraints (`ERR_VENUE_NOT_HOME_VENUE`, then `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_WINDOW`, then `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_TO_VOTE`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_BALLOT`), the referential preconditions (`ERR_NO_APPROVED_BUDGET`, then `ERR_BOWL_REVIEW_PENDING`, then `ERR_AUDIT_REPORT_NOT_ISSUED`, then `ERR_HOST_COMPLIANCE_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_POOL_CLOSED_TO_REVENUE`, then `ERR_RECONCILIATION_IMBALANCE`), and finally the fiscal checks (`ERR_RESERVE_POSTURE_FROZEN`, then `ERR_AUDITED_EXPENSE_MISSING`, then `ERR_EMPTY_ALLOCATION_BASIS`, then `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING`, then `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OVERPAYMENT`, then `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`).

Identifiers embedded in a URL are resolved left to right, and the first one that does not resolve produces its own 404 code: `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVENUE_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOWL_APPEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTECTED_RIVALRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_GAME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHAMPIONSHIP_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AUDIT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Identifiers carried in a request body are resolved the same way and produce the same codes; body-level resolution covers member creation, revenue-pool creation, membership-action creation, budget-proposal creation, protected-rivalry creation, schedule-game creation, championship-event creation, audit-report creation, both ballot-casting operations, and member profile amendment, and the codes it can raise there are `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVENUE_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PROTECTED_RIVALRY_NOT_FOUND`.

Request-shape validation is uniform across the API and raises `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400): every required field must be present, every enumerated value must be one the specification declares, every amount field named in cents and every count must be a non-negative integer, and every instant must parse as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp. A client-supplied instant is retained as the instant it denotes rather than as the literal text received — any UTC offset it carries is normalised to UTC — and on read-back it is rendered in exactly the ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits that §2.5 pins for `created_at` and `updated_at`. That rendering is uniform across every instant the API returns, a client-supplied `game_date`, `window_start` or `window_end` no less than a server-stamped `withdrawal_notice_at` or `posted_at`, so a client that writes `2026-09-08T19:00:00Z` reads the value back as `2026-09-08T19:00:00.000Z`. A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON is treated as an empty object, so it fails only on whichever required field it is missing; a body that parses as valid JSON but is not an object — an array, a string, a number — is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The free-text accompaniments §3 mentions are, without exception, optional: a `reason` on a pool reopening, a run reversal, a game cancellation, an event cancellation, an appearance voiding, a proposal withdrawal or a withdrawal notice, a `note` on a budget proposal or a revision request, a `memo` on a reserve transfer or a computed run, and a `reference` on an entry-fee instalment may all be omitted without error, and a supplied value is checked to be a string (an empty string is acceptable, a non-string is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) and then dropped — none of those fields appears on any resource representation the API returns, so a client cannot read back what it wrote. The two free-text fields that do survive are a membership action's `rationale` and an audit report's `note`, both of which are stored and returned, and both of which are optional too, defaulting to null.

### 2.9. Cascades, Compensation and Conserved Quantities

Several operations reach well beyond the record they address, and the propagation is specified rather than incidental. A championship settlement is the deepest chain: it computes the event's revenue split, adds the participant pool to the linked revenue pool's `gross_revenue_cents`, drives that pool back to `DRAFT` and clears its `audited_expense_cents`, marks every distribution run still in `COMPUTED` on that pool as `SUPERSEDED`, and voids every `ACTIVE` line of those runs. Executing a forced withdrawal is nearly as deep: it departs the member, cancels its scheduled games, lapses the protected rivalries those games were satisfying, recomputes the compliance score of every counterpart member, and flips any scheduled championship event whose host now scores too low into an at-risk state.

Two pairs of operations are designed as exact compensations. Voiding an approved bowl appearance reverses everything approving it did: the residual it credited to conference escrow is debited again, its validated units return to a voided state, and the conference-wide validated unit count returns to its pre-approval value. Reversing a posted distribution run likewise restores every conference-level counter that posting moved — the reserve balance, the escrow balance, the retained governance fee revenue and each member's paid exit settlement — so that the reconciliation identity of §3.9 holds again with the pool back at its pre-posting totals. Where a compensation exists, the specification's intent is that the conserved quantity is exactly conserved; an implementation that reverses only some of the counters is wrong even if no error is raised.

### 2.10. Reserve Policy and the Funding Posture Ladder

The conference maintains a reserve, and its funding level gates discretionary activity. The reserve target is 15 percent of the approved operating expense: `reserve_target_cents` is `approved_operating_expense_cents` multiplied by 1500 basis points and divided by 10000, rounded up to the next whole cent. The approved operating expense figure comes only from the most recently approved budget proposal, so before any budget is approved the target is 0.

From the target and the balance the system derives two figures. The funded ratio, `funded_ratio_bps`, is 10000 when the target is 0; otherwise it is `reserve_balance_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `reserve_target_cents`, rounded down to a whole basis point. The posture, `reserve_posture`, is one of `OPEN`, `RESERVE_RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, and is derived by comparing the balance against the target without dividing: when the target is 0 the posture is `OPEN`; otherwise, if the balance multiplied by 10000 is less than the target multiplied by 2500 basis points the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if the balance multiplied by 10000 is less than the target multiplied by 6000 basis points the posture is `RESERVE_RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Both boundaries fall on the better-funded side: a reserve funded to exactly 6000 basis points of target is `OPEN`, and one funded to exactly 2500 basis points is `RESERVE_RESTRICTED`, not `FROZEN`. The derivation runs whenever a reserve transfer is recorded, whenever a budget proposal reaches approval, and whenever a distribution run is posted or reversed.

The posture has teeth in two places. While the posture is `FROZEN`, computing a distribution run and awarding a championship event are both refused with `ERR_RESERVE_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) — those are the only two operations the freeze blocks. And while the posture is `RESERVE_RESTRICTED`, the reserve sweep taken out of each new distribution run triples, from 500 basis points of the net pool to 1500, as described in §3.7.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Three steps in the system may not be performed by the user who performed the step before them, and all three raise the same code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Posting a distribution run requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from that run's `computed_by_user_id`. Closing the voting on a membership action requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from that action's `proposed_by_user_id`. Issuing an audit report requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the `computed_by_user_id` of the run the report attests. These are checks on the user identifier, not merely on the role: a `Treasurer` and a `FinanceOfficer` are different roles, but if the same operator identifier appears in both requests the second one fails. In each case the check runs after the addressed record's status check and before the operation's remaining referential and fiscal preconditions.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Seating the Membership: Charter Seats, Petitions and the Phased Ladder

A conference is born empty and in `FORMING`. During that window, and only during it, the `Commissioner` (or a `PlatformAdmin`) may seat founding institutions directly. Creating a member institution takes a payload naming the owning conference as `conference_id`, the institution's display `name`, its short `institution_code`, its `admission_path`, the `home_venue_id` at which it will host, and the `grant_of_rights_through_fiscal_year` that bounds its media-rights commitment; three further values are optional — an `entry_fee_cents` assessment, a `travel_partner_member_id`, and a `primary_contact_email` for the athletics business office. The institution code must be 2 to 8 characters drawn from the uppercase ASCII letters and the digits 0 through 9 — so `TXAM` and `USC2` are both acceptable while a lowercase letter, a space, a punctuation mark, a one-character code or a nine-character one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it must be unique across the conference; a collision with an existing member's code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INSTITUTION_CODE`. A travel partner reference that points at the member being created is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_PAIR_INVALID`, since a school cannot be its own travel partner.

The `admission_path` value decides everything about what the new row looks like. `CHARTER` seats the institution immediately at full share: the member is created in status `FULL_SHARE` with `distribution_phase_bps` of 10000, `voting_weight_bps` of 10000, `phase_year` of 4 and `entry_fee_cents` of 0. Charter seating is atomic with creation, so the row is never observable in `PETITIONED`. Because the charter window closes when the conference graduates to `OPERATING`, a charter creation attempted after the first budget has been approved is refused with `ERR_CHARTER_WINDOW_CLOSED`. `PETITIONED` is the other path: the institution is created in status `PETITIONED` with `distribution_phase_bps` of 0, `voting_weight_bps` of 0, `phase_year` of 0 and `entry_fee_paid_cents` of 0. A petitioner is an applicant, not a member — it earns nothing and votes on nothing until the membership admits it. Over its life a member moves through six possible statuses: `PETITIONED`, `ADMISSION_DENIED`, `ADMITTED_PHASING`, `FULL_SHARE`, `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` and `DEPARTED`, of which `ADMISSION_DENIED` and `DEPARTED` are final.

Entry fees arrive in instalments. A `Treasurer`, or the institution's own `MemberRepresentative`, records a payment as an `amount_cents` figure with an optional `reference` string, and the amount is added to the member's `entry_fee_paid_cents`. Instalments are only accepted while the member is still `PETITIONED`; against any other status the payment is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. Overpayment is refused rather than absorbed: if the existing `entry_fee_paid_cents` plus the new `amount_cents` would exceed the assessed `entry_fee_cents`, the payment fails with `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OVERPAYMENT` (HTTP 422), so the sum of instalments can equal the assessment exactly but never surpass it.

Once the membership has voted a petitioner in, the institution climbs a **phased distribution ladder** rather than jumping straight to a full share. Each advance is a distinct step taken by a `Commissioner` or a `FinanceOfficer`, requires the member to be `ADMITTED_PHASING`, and requires the entry fee to be fully settled — that is, `entry_fee_paid_cents` at least equal to `entry_fee_cents`; a member still owing any part of its fee is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 402). An advance increments `phase_year` by 1 and then re-derives the share multiplier. The multiplier depends only on status and the ladder length. For a member in `PETITIONED`, `ADMISSION_DENIED` or `DEPARTED`, `distribution_phase_bps` is 0. For a member in `FULL_SHARE` or `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` it is 10000. For a member in `ADMITTED_PHASING` it is 10000 multiplied by the member's `phase_year`, divided by one more than the conference's `phase_schedule_years`, rounded down to a whole basis point, and then capped at 10000. With the default of three phasing years the ladder is therefore: `phase_year` 1 gives 2500 basis points, 2 gives 5000, 3 gives 7500, and 4 gives 10000. The advance that carries the member to 10000 — equivalently, the advance whose resulting `phase_year` reaches one more than `phase_schedule_years` — is also the advance that promotes it out of `ADMITTED_PHASING` into `FULL_SHARE`, with `distribution_phase_bps` and `voting_weight_bps` both set to 10000. Advances taken before that point leave the member in `ADMITTED_PHASING`, which is why the ladder step is a self-loop on that status. Voting weight tracks the ladder but is not identical to it: `voting_weight_bps` is 0 for a member in `PETITIONED`, `ADMISSION_DENIED`, `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` or `DEPARTED`, is 10000 for a member in `FULL_SHARE`, and otherwise equals `distribution_phase_bps`. Every advance also increments the member's `version` by 1.

Three profile attributes stay mutable: the `home_venue_id`, the `travel_partner_member_id` and the `grant_of_rights_through_fiscal_year`. Amending them is available to a `Commissioner`, a `PlatformAdmin`, or the institution's own `MemberRepresentative`, and the request must carry `expected_member_version` alongside whichever of the three fields it wishes to change. Only the supplied fields are rewritten; omitted fields are left exactly as they were, and the member's `version` advances by 1 even when the request supplies none of the three and so changes nothing. For the two reference fields an explicit null is a value rather than an omission: sending `home_venue_id` or `travel_partner_member_id` as null clears the reference, which is the only way to detach a travel partner or to leave a member without a home venue. `grant_of_rights_through_fiscal_year` behaves differently, because it must be an integer whenever it is mentioned at all — an explicit null there is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A member in `DEPARTED` or `ADMISSION_DENIED` may not be amended at all, which surfaces as `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`, and a travel-partner value equal to the member's own identifier is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_PAIR_INVALID`. The roster can be read whole or filtered — by `conference_id`, by `home_venue_id`, by `travel_partner_member_id`, or by `status` — and each member's record also exposes the computed values other subsections describe: `withdrawal_notice_at`, `withdrawal_effective_fiscal_year`, `notice_days`, `last_full_annual_distribution_cents`, `exit_settlement_total_cents`, `exit_settlement_paid_cents` and `schedule_compliance_score`.

### 3.2. Governing by Weighted Ballot: Membership Actions and Their Negotiation Loop

Admissions and forced departures are not administrative acts; they are votes. A `Commissioner` opens the process by proposing a **membership action** naming the `conference_id`, the subject `member_id`, the `action_type` — either `ADMIT` or `WITHDRAW` — and the `effective_fiscal_year` in which the change takes effect, optionally with an `entry_fee_cents` assessment to be levied on an admitted institution and a free-text `rationale` circulated to the membership with the ballot. The effective year must be strictly later than the conference's `current_fiscal_year`; an equal or earlier year is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_YEAR` (HTTP 422). An `ADMIT` proposal additionally requires the conference to have an approved budget in place for its `current_fiscal_year`; without one the proposal fails with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_BUDGET`, because the office may not commit to a new member's phased shares before the membership has adopted a budget. The subject member must be in a status compatible with the action, and that compatibility test does not consult `action_type` at all: exactly two statuses are incompatible with either kind of proposal, `DEPARTED` and `ADMISSION_DENIED`, and a subject in one of them yields `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. Any other status may be proposed against, so an `ADMIT` may be raised over an institution already in `FULL_SHARE` and a `WITHDRAW` over a mere petitioner; it is execution rather than proposal that insists the subject actually hold the status its action type expects. A new action is created in `PROPOSED` with `amend_count` of 0, `quorum_weight_bps` of 6000, `proposed_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `version` 1; `required_approval_bps` and `closed_by_user_id` are both null until the ballot opens and closes respectively, `eligible_weight_bps`, `cast_weight_bps`, `for_weight_bps`, `against_weight_bps` and `approval_ratio_bps` are all 0, and `quorum_met` is false. Its lifecycle runs `PROPOSED` → `VOTING` → either `APPROVED` → `EXECUTED` or `REJECTED`, with `WITHDRAWN` available as an early exit from `PROPOSED`.

Opening the ballot moves the action `PROPOSED` → `VOTING` and is refused with `ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID` from any other status. It requires at least one member of the action's own conference to carry a positive `voting_weight_bps` — a conference with nobody eligible to vote cannot hold a vote, and one where nobody qualifies is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_TO_VOTE`, the same code an ineligible ballot draws — and it fixes the approval threshold: `required_approval_bps` becomes 7500 for an `ADMIT` and 6000 for a `WITHDRAW`, reflecting that admitting a new school is a higher bar than releasing one. Abandoning a proposal before the ballot opens is also possible: withdrawing it with a `reason` moves it `PROPOSED` → `WITHDRAWN` and voids every ballot attached to it.

Each institution votes through its own `MemberRepresentative`. A ballot payload names the `member_id` it is cast for, the `choice` — `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN` — and `expected_action_version`. On acceptance the ballot is created in status `CAST` with its `weight_bps` snapshotted from the member's `voting_weight_bps` at that moment, so later changes to the member's weight do not retroactively re-weight a vote already cast. Casting a ballot writes nothing onto the parent action: the action record is not mutated, so its `version` does not advance and none of its tally fields are touched — the weights are not accumulated as ballots arrive but are summed from the surviving `CAST` ballots only at the moment voting closes, and the same is true of a budget-proposal ballot in §3.3. `expected_action_version` is therefore invalidated only by a transition on the action itself, such as an amendment, and never by another representative's ballot. Ballots are only accepted while the action is `VOTING` (`ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID` otherwise), the voting member must carry a positive `voting_weight_bps` (`ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_TO_VOTE` otherwise), and a member that already holds a `CAST` ballot on the same question is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BALLOT`. A ballot itself has only two statuses, `CAST` and `VOIDED`, and it can be listed per action or per budget proposal — filtered by `member_id` and `status` — or per member, filtered by `status`. Membership actions themselves can be read individually or listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `member_id`, `action_type` and `status`.

Negotiation is explicit in this design. While an action is under vote, its terms may be amended — a new `effective_fiscal_year`, a revised `entry_fee_cents`, a rewritten `rationale`, in any combination. All three amendable fields are optional and only those actually supplied are rewritten, so an amendment may carry an empty body and still consume one of the two permitted amendments; an explicit null `rationale` clears the circulated text, whereas an explicit null `effective_fiscal_year` or `entry_fee_cents` is read as an omission and leaves the existing value standing rather than raising `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Amending carries no expected-version field of its own. Amending returns the action `VOTING` → `PROPOSED`, transitions every `CAST` ballot on it to `VOIDED`, resets `cast_weight_bps`, `for_weight_bps`, `against_weight_bps` and `approval_ratio_bps` to 0, increments `amend_count` by 1 and advances the action's `version`. The `required_approval_bps` fixed when the ballot opened is deliberately left in place rather than cleared, and `eligible_weight_bps` and `quorum_met` are left alone as well, since only a close ever writes those two. The membership can therefore be re-canvassed on revised terms, but not indefinitely: the loop terminates because an amendment is only accepted while `amend_count` is below the amendment limit of 2, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_AMEND_LIMIT_REACHED`. A revised effective year is validated exactly as it was at proposal time, so an amendment that would set the effective year to the current fiscal year or earlier is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_YEAR`.

Closing the ballot is where the weighted arithmetic happens, and it is performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `Commissioner` other than the one who proposed the action — the same user who proposed it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The tally counts only ballots still in `CAST`; ballots voided by an amendment are excluded entirely. The eligible weight, `eligible_weight_bps`, is the sum of `voting_weight_bps` across the conference's members whose weight is positive, snapshotted at the moment voting closes — so a member that filed a withdrawal notice mid-vote drops out of both the numerator and the denominator. The cast weight, `cast_weight_bps`, is the sum of `weight_bps` over the counted ballots and includes abstentions. The `for_weight_bps` and `against_weight_bps` figures are the same sums restricted to ballots whose choice is `FOR` and `AGAINST` respectively. The decisive weight is `for_weight_bps` plus `against_weight_bps`, which deliberately excludes abstentions, and `approval_ratio_bps` is `for_weight_bps` multiplied by 10000 and divided by that decisive weight, rounded down to a whole basis point; when no decisive weight was cast at all the ratio is 0. Quorum is tested without division: `quorum_met` is true when `cast_weight_bps` multiplied by 10000 is greater than or equal to `eligible_weight_bps` multiplied by the quorum threshold of 6000 basis points. The outcome is `APPROVED` when quorum was met and `approval_ratio_bps` is greater than or equal to `required_approval_bps`, and `REJECTED` otherwise; both comparisons are inclusive, so an `ADMIT` whose ratio lands on exactly 7500 basis points passes, and a `WITHDRAW` at exactly 6000 passes. Whichever branch is taken, the tally fields — `eligible_weight_bps`, `cast_weight_bps`, `for_weight_bps`, `against_weight_bps`, `quorum_met`, `approval_ratio_bps` and `required_approval_bps` — are written onto the action, `closed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the action's `version` advances. The two branches differ in their consequences: an approval leaves the subject member untouched and merely unlocks execution, while a rejection of an `ADMIT` also drives the subject institution `PETITIONED` → `ADMISSION_DENIED` with `voting_weight_bps` set to 0 and its `version` advanced. A rejection of a `WITHDRAW` leaves the subject member exactly where it was, under notice.

Executing an approved action is the `Commissioner`'s final step, and it is refused with `ERR_ACTION_STATE_INVALID` unless the action is `APPROVED` and the subject member is in the status the action type expects — `PETITIONED` for an `ADMIT`, `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` for a `WITHDRAW`. Execution sets the action to `EXECUTED` and advances its `version`. For an `ADMIT`, the subject member moves `PETITIONED` → `ADMITTED_PHASING` with `phase_year` set to 1, the phase multiplier re-derived from that (2500 basis points under the default three-year ladder), `voting_weight_bps` set equal to `distribution_phase_bps`, and the action's `entry_fee_cents` copied onto the member as its assessment; an execution attempted while the institution still owes an assessed entry fee is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING`. That test is measured against the assessment the member already carries, not against the one the action is about to levy: it compares the member's `entry_fee_paid_cents` with the member's current `entry_fee_cents`, and only once it passes is the action's `entry_fee_cents` written onto the member. A petitioner created with no assessment therefore executes cleanly even when the action levies a fee, and that newly levied fee is then outstanding, which — because instalments are only accepted while a member is still `PETITIONED` — leaves the phase ladder of §3.1 blocked with `ERR_ENTRY_FEE_OUTSTANDING`. An assessment intended to be collectable must therefore be recorded on the member at creation, not only on the action. For a `WITHDRAW`, the consequences fan out across five hops. The subject member moves `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` → `DEPARTED` with `distribution_phase_bps` and `voting_weight_bps` both set to 0. Every game still `SCHEDULED` with that member as either the home or the away participant becomes `CANCELLED`. Every `ACTIVE` protected rivalry involving that member which is left with no non-cancelled meeting in the conference's `current_fiscal_year` moves `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED`, and so does any `ACTIVE` rivalry that one of the games just cancelled was carrying as its `protected_rivalry_id` and that is now left unmet, even where the departing member is not one of that rivalry's two parties. The `schedule_compliance_score` of every counterpart member of a lapsed rivalry is recomputed and persisted; the participants of the cancelled games are not otherwise rewritten, because §3.4 recomputes a score on every read in any case. And finally the hosting oscillation of §3.5 is applied to the departing member, to every participant of a cancelled game and to every counterpart just recomputed, so that any championship event still `SCHEDULED` whose host member's recomputed score has fallen below the hosting minimum of 90 moves `SCHEDULED` → `HOST_AT_RISK` and, in the other direction, an event already `HOST_AT_RISK` whose host now scores 90 or better returns to `SCHEDULED`. All five hops are part of the same operation; none of them is optional.

### 3.3. The Annual Budget, the Reserve and the Governance Fee

The membership adopts a budget each year, and adopting the first one is what turns a `FORMING` conference into an `OPERATING` one. A `FinanceOfficer` drafts the proposal against a `conference_id` and a `fiscal_year`, stating the `proposed_operating_expense_cents` that will run the office and the `proposed_governance_expense_cents` covering the compliance and governance services billed to members; both must be non-negative integers, and an explanatory `note` may accompany them, though the proposal record does not retain it. The draft is created in `DRAFT` with `version` 1, `required_approval_bps` of 6670 fixed from the outset rather than at the opening of the ballot, `approved_by_user_id` null, every tally figure 0 and `quorum_met` false, and `prepared_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`. A proposal's lifecycle is short — `DRAFT` → `VOTING` → either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, both of which are final — and any operation addressed to a proposal in the wrong status is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`. Proposals can be read individually or listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `fiscal_year` and `status`.

Putting a draft to the membership moves it `DRAFT` → `VOTING`, requires at least one member of the proposal's conference to carry a positive `voting_weight_bps` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_TO_VOTE`, and sets `required_approval_bps` to the budget threshold of 6670 basis points, a two-thirds-plus bar that is deliberately higher than the 6000 needed to release a member. Budget ballots work exactly like membership-action ballots — a `MemberRepresentative` submits a `member_id` and a `choice` of `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN`, the ballot is created `CAST` with `weight_bps` snapshotted from the member, zero-weight members are refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE_TO_VOTE`, and a second ballot from the same institution is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BALLOT` — with one contract difference: a budget ballot carries no expected-version field, because a budget proposal has no amendment loop that could invalidate ballots already cast. A ballot may reference a membership action through `membership_action_id` or a budget proposal through `budget_proposal_id`, and exactly one of the two is populated on any given ballot; the other is null.

Closing the budget ballot is the `FinanceOfficer`'s act and uses the same tally arithmetic as a membership action: only `CAST` ballots count, `eligible_weight_bps` is snapshotted at close, abstentions raise `cast_weight_bps` but stay out of the decisive denominator, `approval_ratio_bps` is the for-weight multiplied by 10000 divided by the decisive weight rounded down, and `quorum_met` is true when `cast_weight_bps` multiplied by 10000 is at least `eligible_weight_bps` multiplied by 6000. The proposal is `APPROVED` when quorum was met and the ratio is greater than or equal to 6670 basis points, and `REJECTED` otherwise; in both cases the tally fields `eligible_weight_bps`, `cast_weight_bps`, `for_weight_bps`, `against_weight_bps`, `quorum_met` and `approval_ratio_bps` are written and the proposal's `version` advances. The branches then diverge sharply. A rejection changes nothing on the conference record at all. An approval, by contrast, records `approved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, copies `proposed_operating_expense_cents` into the conference's `approved_operating_expense_cents` and `proposed_governance_expense_cents` into its `approved_governance_expense_cents`, recomputes `reserve_target_cents` as 15 percent of the newly approved operating expense rounded up to the next cent, re-derives `reserve_posture` and `funded_ratio_bps` from that new target, moves `governance_status` `FORMING` → `OPERATING` if the conference was still forming, and advances the conference's `version` by 1 in addition to the proposal's. Graduating to `OPERATING` is one-way and it is what closes the charter-seating window described in §3.1.

The reserve itself is moved by the `Treasurer`, one transfer at a time. A transfer names its `transfer_type` — `CONTRIBUTION` adds to `reserve_balance_cents`, `WITHDRAWAL` subtracts from it — an `amount_cents`, the `expected_conference_version` the client last read, and an optional `memo`. A withdrawal larger than the current `reserve_balance_cents` is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422); the reserve may be emptied exactly but never overdrawn. Every accepted transfer re-runs the posture derivation of §2.10, rewriting `reserve_posture` and `funded_ratio_bps`, and advances the conference's `version` by 1 — which is precisely why the next transfer needs a fresh read. Two other flows move reserve money without a transfer request: the sweep taken from each posted distribution run adds to the balance, and reversing that run takes it back out. The conference also accumulates `governance_fee_revenue_cents`, the running total of the flat governance and compliance service fees retained from posted distribution lines, and `escrow_balance_cents`, which holds provisional distribution withholdings together with approved bowl-reimbursement residuals.

### 3.4. Building the Competitive Schedule: Rivalries, Travel Partners and Rest

Venues are registered before anything can be played in them. A `ScheduleCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin` registers one with a `name`, a `city`, a `seating_capacity` that must be strictly greater than 0, and a `concurrent_event_capacity` that defaults to 1 and must be at least 1 — the latter governs how many championship occupancy windows may overlap on that venue at once, and is used in §3.5 rather than in ordinary game scheduling.

Rivalries come next. Protecting one records the `conference_id`, the two parties as `member_a_id` and `member_b_id`, the `required_annual_meetings` the pair must schedule each fiscal year (defaulting to 1, minimum 1), and an optional `label` naming the trophy or series. The two parties must be different institutions, and an attempt to protect a rivalry between a member and itself is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_PAIR_INVALID`. The pair is treated as unordered: if any protected rivalry already links the same two institutions in this conference — whether it is still `ACTIVE` or has already gone `LAPSED` — the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RIVALRY`, so the same fixture cannot be protected twice under two labels. A rivalry is created `ACTIVE` and has exactly one other status, `LAPSED`, which is terminal.

Scheduling a game is a `ScheduleCoordinator` action and takes the `conference_id`, the `home_member_id`, the `away_member_id`, the `venue_id`, the `game_date` kickoff instant, the `fiscal_year` the game counts toward, and optionally `media_designated` (defaulting to false, and the flag that decides whether the game counts as a media appearance in the distribution weighting) and `protected_rivalry_id` when the game is meant to satisfy a protected series. A supplied `protected_rivalry_id` need only resolve to an existing rivalry, on pain of `ERR_PROTECTED_RIVALRY_NOT_FOUND`; the service does not require that rivalry's two parties to be the game's two participants, nor that it still be `ACTIVE`, so a mislinked game counts toward whichever rivalry it names. Several checks guard it, and their relative order matters because they carry different codes. A game between a member and itself is refused first with `ERR_MEMBER_PAIR_INVALID`. A participant in `DEPARTED` or `ADMISSION_DENIED` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. Then comes the **member rest buffer**: neither participant may already have a game in status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED` whose `game_date` is fewer than six days away from the requested `game_date`, in either direction. The separation is the absolute difference between the two kickoff instants measured against a span of exactly six times twenty-four hours rather than a count of calendar dates, so two kickoffs six days apart to the minute are accepted and anything tighter is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`; the scan reaches every existing game in those two statuses regardless of its `fiscal_year` or its venue. Only after the rest buffer passes does the home-venue check run: the supplied `venue_id` must be the home member's own `home_venue_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_VENUE_NOT_HOME_VENUE` (HTTP 422). A game is created in `SCHEDULED` and immediately triggers a recomputation of `schedule_compliance_score` for both participants.

A scheduled game has two possible outcomes. Marking it played, which a `ScheduleCoordinator` or `ComplianceOfficer` may do, moves it `SCHEDULED` → `PLAYED`, requires both participants to be in `ADMITTED_PHASING`, `FULL_SHARE` or `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED`, and recomputes both participants' compliance scores; `PLAYED` is what promotes a media-designated game into the final audited appearance count of §3.7. Cancelling it, which requires a `reason` and is available to a `ScheduleCoordinator` or `Commissioner`, moves it `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED` and starts a chain: if the game carried a `protected_rivalry_id` and no meeting for that rivalry in the conference's `current_fiscal_year` remains in status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED`, that rivalry moves `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED`; the `schedule_compliance_score` of both participants and of every counterpart of a rivalry that just lapsed is recomputed; and any championship event still `SCHEDULED` whose host member's recomputed score has dropped below 90 moves `SCHEDULED` → `HOST_AT_RISK`. Either transition advances the game's `version` by 1. Any game-level operation addressed to a game that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_GAME_STATE_INVALID`.

The compliance score itself is the conference's measure of whether a member is scheduling in good faith, and it starts at 100 for a brand-new member. It is computed for the conference's `current_fiscal_year` only, and it counts only games in status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED` — cancelled games are excluded from every count, which is exactly why a cancellation lowers a score. Three penalties are subtracted from a base of 100. The first is 15 points for each **unmet protected rivalry** involving the member, where a rivalry counts as unmet if it is `LAPSED`, or if it is `ACTIVE` but the number of games referencing it in the current fiscal year with status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED` is fewer than its `required_annual_meetings`. The second is 8 points for each **travel-partner gap**. If the member's `travel_partner_member_id` is null the gap count is 0 outright; otherwise it is the number of the member's own away games in the current fiscal year (status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED`) for which no away game of its travel partner exists at the same `venue_id` in the same fiscal year, in status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED`, whose `game_date` is within three days of the member's game in either direction — a separation of exactly three days still counts as paired. The third is 5 points for each unit of **home-schedule imbalance**, measured as the member's `home_game_count` minus its `away_game_count` minus 1, treated as 0 when that difference is negative or zero; both counts cover the current fiscal year in status `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED`. The resulting `schedule_compliance_score` is clamped at 0, so no combination of penalties can push it negative. The score is recomputed when a game is created, played or cancelled, when a rivalry lapses, when it is read directly, and as part of the withdrawal cascade in §3.2. Only the write triggers persist it, and each of them persists it only when the freshly computed value actually differs from the stored one, so a recomputation that changes nothing leaves the member's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they were. A read never writes it back: `schedule_compliance_score` is recomputed at serialisation time whenever a member record is returned, so it is always current in a member representation even though the stored value may lag, and neither reading the member nor reading the breakdown advances the member's `version`. Reading the breakdown returns the whole of it: the `member_id`, the `unmet_rivalry_count`, the `travel_partner_gap_count`, the `home_game_count`, the `away_game_count` and the `schedule_compliance_score`. A score of at least 90 is what qualifies a member to host a championship, and the games and rivalries behind it can be listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `home_member_id`, `away_member_id`, `venue_id`, `protected_rivalry_id`, `fiscal_year`, `media_designated` and `status` for games, and by `conference_id`, `member_a_id`, `member_b_id` and `status` for rivalries.

### 3.5. Championship Hosting, Venue Occupancy and Event Settlement

Championship events are awarded, hosted and then settled, and they are the one place where money flows back into a revenue pool that has already been booked. Awarding an event — a `Commissioner` or `ScheduleCoordinator` capability — names the `conference_id`, the `venue_id` to be occupied, the `host_member_id` that will host and receive the host share, the `revenue_pool_id` that will be credited at settlement, the `sport` contested, the `fiscal_year` the event settles into, and the occupancy window as `window_start` and `window_end`. The window is half-open: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant.

Five guards apply, in a fixed order. A host institution in a status that cannot host — `DEPARTED` or `ADMISSION_DENIED` — is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. Then venue occupancy is checked: the number of events on the same venue in status `SCHEDULED`, `HOST_AT_RISK` or `HOSTED` whose half-open window intersects the requested half-open window must be strictly less than that venue's `concurrent_event_capacity`, and otherwise the award is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because both windows are half-open, a requested window that begins exactly at another event's `window_end` does not intersect it and is therefore permitted. Next, `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; an inverted or zero-length window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Then the host must be scheduling in good faith: its `schedule_compliance_score` must be at least 90, and a host below that minimum is refused with `ERR_HOST_COMPLIANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Finally, an award is discretionary spending, so it is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_POSTURE_FROZEN` while the conference's `reserve_posture` is `FROZEN`. A successful award creates the event in `SCHEDULED` and snapshots the host's score at that moment as `host_compliance_score_at_award`.

Host eligibility is not frozen at award time; it oscillates. Whenever a host member's `schedule_compliance_score` is recomputed and falls below 90, its still-`SCHEDULED` events move `SCHEDULED` → `HOST_AT_RISK`; whenever the recomputed score returns to 90 or above, they move `HOST_AT_RISK` → `SCHEDULED` again. Both directions advance the event's `version` by 1. An at-risk event is not cancelled — the conference may still stage it — but the condition is flagged to the membership in the audit reporting of §3.9.

Confirming that an event was hosted moves it from either `SCHEDULED` or `HOST_AT_RISK` into `HOSTED`, and confirmation is deliberately permitted from the at-risk state so that a lapse in the host's scheduling compliance does not strand an event that has already been played. Cancelling an event, which takes a `reason`, is likewise available from either `SCHEDULED` or `HOST_AT_RISK` and moves the event to `CANCELLED`, which releases its venue window so that later awards no longer count it in the overlap test. Any event operation addressed to an event in an incompatible status — settling one that was never hosted, for instance — is refused with `ERR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Settlement is a `FinanceOfficer` action, is only available from `HOSTED`, and supplies three figures: `ticket_revenue_cents`, `sponsorship_revenue_cents` and `event_expense_cents`. From them the system derives `net_event_revenue_cents` as ticket receipts plus sponsorship receipts minus event expense. The `host_share_cents` is that net figure multiplied by the host share of 2000 basis points and divided by 10000, rounded up to the next whole cent — but only when the net figure is strictly positive; when the net is zero or negative the host share is 0. The `participant_pool_cents` is then `net_event_revenue_cents` minus `host_share_cents`, which means that a loss-making event passes its entire negative net through to the participant pool while the host is shielded from it. The event moves `HOSTED` → `SETTLED` and its `version` advances.

What follows is the deepest cascade in the system, and every hop is mandatory. The `participant_pool_cents` is added to the linked revenue pool's `gross_revenue_cents`. Because that gross figure has now been restated, a pool sitting in `OPEN` or `BOOKS_CLOSED` is driven back to `DRAFT`, and if it was `BOOKS_CLOSED` its `audited_expense_cents` is cleared to null — the audited figure was an assertion about a gross that no longer holds. Because the pool has reverted, every distribution run on that pool still in `COMPUTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, whatever its `variant`, and each such run's `superseded_by_run_id` stays null: no new run is displacing it, so there is nothing to point at. And because those runs are superseded, every one of their lines still `ACTIVE` moves to `VOIDED`. A superseded run's own `version` advances, its lines have no `version` to advance, and no member or conference counter moves, because a `COMPUTED` run had never paid anything out. The pool's `version` advances by 1, which is what makes any client holding an older `expected_pool_version` fail its next attempt to compute a run. Settlement is only accepted while the linked pool can still absorb restated revenue, meaning its status is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `BOOKS_CLOSED`; against a `CLOSED` pool the settlement is refused with `ERR_POOL_CLOSED_TO_REVENUE`. Events can be listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `venue_id`, `host_member_id`, `revenue_pool_id`, `fiscal_year` and `status`.

### 3.6. Postseason Appearances, Expense Reimbursement and Postseason Units

Each bowl or postseason-tournament appearance is submitted by the institution that played in it — through its own `MemberRepresentative`, or by a `Commissioner` or `ComplianceOfficer` on its behalf. The submission states the `fiscal_year` of the appearance, the `event_name`, the `event_tier` as either `BOWL` or `TOURNAMENT`, the `payout_cents` gross payout received, the `submitted_expense_cents` the institution claims for reimbursement, the one-way `travel_miles` travelled, and the `units_claimed` number of postseason units the appearance claims. All four numeric values must be non-negative integers. The appearance is created in `SUBMITTED`, and submitting it mints `units_claimed` postseason units in status `PROVISIONAL`, each carrying its parent's identifier as `bowl_appearance_id`, the credited `member_id`, a `fiscal_year` copied from the parent appearance, and a 1-based `unit_index` giving its ordinal within the appearance. A member in `DEPARTED` or `ADMISSION_DENIED` may not submit at all (`ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`).

Review is a `ComplianceOfficer` workflow with a loop in it. Taking a `SUBMITTED` appearance into review moves it to `UNDER_REVIEW`. From there the officer may send it back for correction with an explanatory `note`, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` and incrementing its `revision_round` by 1. The institution then resubmits with restated figures — a new `submitted_expense_cents`, new `travel_miles`, and optionally a new `units_claimed` — which returns the appearance `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `UNDER_REVIEW`, rewrites those three fields with the supplied values, and discards and re-mints the appearance's postseason units so that exactly `units_claimed` units exist again, all in `PROVISIONAL`. Discarding here is deletion, not transition: the previous units are removed from the store outright rather than moved to `VOIDED`, so they vanish from every unit listing and leave no trace, and the replacements carry fresh identifiers, fresh `created_at` instants and `unit_index` values running from 1 again. An omitted `units_claimed` leaves the claimed count as it was, but the units are re-minted regardless. A resubmission is confined to the owning institution's representative or a `ComplianceOfficer`, and the revision loop may be taken any number of times before approval — there is no cap on revision rounds. Every one of these transitions advances the appearance's `version` by 1, and any of them addressed to an appearance in the wrong status is refused with `ERR_APPEARANCE_STATE_INVALID`: review may only begin from `SUBMITTED`, revision may only be requested from `UNDER_REVIEW`, resubmission only from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, approval only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and voiding from any status that is not already `VOIDED`.

Approval is where the reimbursement arithmetic runs, and it interlocks four inputs through two independent caps and one payout-driven band, so the outcome is not a linear function of any single input. First, `allowable_expense_cents` is the smaller of the claimed `submitted_expense_cents` and the allowable-expense cap of 8,000,000 cents (80,000 dollars). Second, `travel_allowance_cents` is the smaller of `travel_miles` multiplied by the travel rate of 95 cents per mile and the travel-allowance cap of 2,500,000 cents (25,000 dollars). Third, the reimbursement band is selected by the payout alone — `event_tier` is descriptive and plays no part in the arithmetic: a `payout_cents` of at least 10,000,000 cents (100,000 dollars) selects the high-payout band of 6000 basis points, and anything below that selects the low-payout band of 8500 basis points. The `reimbursement_ceiling_cents` is `payout_cents` multiplied by the selected band and divided by 10000, rounded up to the next whole cent. Then `reimbursed_cents` is the smaller of the sum of `allowable_expense_cents` and `travel_allowance_cents` and that ceiling, and `residual_to_pool_cents` is `payout_cents` minus `reimbursed_cents`. Because the band steps down as the payout rises past the threshold, a larger payout can actually lower the reimbursement — a deliberate property of the policy, not an artefact.

Approving therefore moves the appearance `UNDER_REVIEW` → `APPROVED`, writes `allowable_expense_cents`, `travel_allowance_cents`, `reimbursement_ceiling_cents`, `reimbursed_cents` and `residual_to_pool_cents`, adds `residual_to_pool_cents` to the conference's `escrow_balance_cents`, transitions every postseason unit of the appearance `PROVISIONAL` → `VALIDATED`, advances the conference's `validated_postseason_unit_count` by `units_claimed`, and increments the conference's `version` by 1. A validated unit is the only kind that counts toward a unit-based allocation or a valuation.

Voiding an appearance takes a `reason` and is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or `Commissioner`. From `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` it simply moves the appearance to `VOIDED` and transitions each of its still-`PROVISIONAL` units to `VOIDED`, permanently excluding them from every valuation; the appearance's `version` advances by 1. From `APPROVED` it is a true compensation and reverses the approval exactly: `residual_to_pool_cents` is subtracted from the conference's `escrow_balance_cents`, every unit moves `VALIDATED` → `VOIDED`, `validated_postseason_unit_count` is reduced by `units_claimed`, and the conference's `version` advances — leaving both conference counters back at their pre-approval values. That compensation is only safe while the money has not yet gone out, so voiding an approved appearance is refused with `ERR_UNITS_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED` when any of its postseason units has already been paid out on a posted run. A distribution line carries no unit-level foreign key, so that reference is read through the allocation that consumes validated units: the refusal fires when some distribution line credited to the appearance's own `member_id` belongs to a run in status `POSTED` whose pool has `allocation_method` `UNIT_BASED` and a `fiscal_year` equal to the appearance's. The line's own status is not consulted, so a line since voided still blocks the void; a posted run over an `EQUAL_SHARE` or `APPEARANCE_PERFORMANCE_WEIGHTED` pool never blocks it, however much money it moved; and the guard is applied only when the appearance being voided is `APPROVED`, since from the three pre-approval statuses nothing has been distributed to begin with. A postseason unit thus has exactly three statuses — `PROVISIONAL`, `VALIDATED` and `VOIDED`. Units can be listed for a single appearance, filtered by `status`, or for a member across appearances, filtered by `fiscal_year` and `status`. An appearance can likewise be read on its own or listed for a member and filtered by `fiscal_year` and `status`.

The cash entitlement a member has built up from its units is reported separately, and it has two variants keyed on the member's own status. `CURRENT_YEAR_ONLY` is keyed on exactly one status and `ROLLING_SIX_YEAR` covers all five others, `PETITIONED` and `ADMISSION_DENIED` included, even though neither can have accumulated a unit. For a member in `ADMITTED_PHASING`, `FULL_SHARE` or `DEPARTED` — or in either of those two — the `ROLLING_SIX_YEAR` variant applies: the window covers the six fiscal years ending at the conference's `current_fiscal_year`, so `window_start_fiscal_year` is `current_fiscal_year` minus 5 and `window_end_fiscal_year` is `current_fiscal_year`. For a member in `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED` the `CURRENT_YEAR_ONLY` variant applies: both window bounds are the `current_fiscal_year` itself, so a school under notice loses its historical units from the entitlement entirely. Both bounds are inclusive. Within the window, `validated_unit_count` is the number of that member's postseason units in status `VALIDATED` whose `fiscal_year` falls inside the window — provisional and voided units never count — and `valuation_cents` is that count multiplied by the conference's `unit_value_cents`, an exact multiplication with no rounding. The valuation response names the `member_id`, the `variant` applied, both window bounds, the count and the valuation.

### 3.7. Revenue Pools, Distribution Runs and Posting Money to the Membership

A revenue pool books one category of conference revenue for one fiscal year. A `FinanceOfficer` creates it with the `conference_id`, the `fiscal_year` whose revenue it books, the `pool_type` as one of `MEDIA_RIGHTS`, `POSTSEASON_UNIT` or `CHAMPIONSHIP`, the `allocation_method` as one of `APPEARANCE_PERFORMANCE_WEIGHTED`, `EQUAL_SHARE` or `UNIT_BASED`, the `gross_revenue_cents` receipts booked into it, and the `estimated_expense_cents` pre-audit expense estimate. Both money figures must be non-negative. The pool is created in `DRAFT` with `audited_expense_cents` null and `version` 1. Pools can be listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `fiscal_year`, `pool_type` and `status`, and each pool is read individually so that a client can capture the `version` a distribution run must echo.

The pool moves through four statuses. Opening a `DRAFT` pool, which additionally requires the conference to be `OPERATING` — a conference still `FORMING` is refused with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`, the only code this transition declares, rather than with a conference-specific code — sets it `OPEN` and makes it eligible for provisional runs. Closing the books on an `OPEN` pool supplies the `audited_expense_cents` and sets the pool `BOOKS_CLOSED`, which is the only status from which a final audited run may be computed; before that is allowed, every bowl appearance whose `fiscal_year` equals the pool's `fiscal_year` must have reached `APPROVED` or `VOIDED`, and a single appearance still `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` refuses the operation with `ERR_BOWL_REVIEW_PENDING`. Reopening a `BOOKS_CLOSED` pool takes a `reason`, returns it to `OPEN`, clears `audited_expense_cents` back to null and supersedes every run on the pool still in `COMPUTED` — voiding those runs' `ACTIVE` lines and leaving their `superseded_by_run_id` null, exactly as the settlement cascade of §3.5 does, since no new run is displacing them; it is only permitted while no run on the pool is currently `POSTED`, and a pool carrying one is refused with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`. Both that test and its mirror below read the run's present status rather than its history, so a run that has since been reversed no longer blocks a reopening and no longer counts as the posted run a closure demands. Closing a `BOOKS_CLOSED` pool permanently sets it `CLOSED`, and requires that at least one run on the pool be in `POSTED` at that moment — a pool with no such run is likewise refused with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`, and no audit report on that run need have been issued or accepted for the closure to be permitted. Every one of these four transitions advances the pool's `version` by 1, and an operation addressed to a pool in the wrong status is refused with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`. Beyond these deliberate transitions the pool can also be driven backwards without anyone asking: a championship settlement that restates `gross_revenue_cents` returns an `OPEN` or `BOOKS_CLOSED` pool to `DRAFT` as described in §3.5, so this machine is a cycle rather than a pipeline and a client must never assume that a pool it opened is still open.

Computing a run is the `FinanceOfficer`'s central act. It supplies the `variant` as either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL_AUDITED`, the `expected_pool_version` for optimistic concurrency, and optionally a `memo`. A stale `expected_pool_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION`, which is precisely how a client that missed an intervening championship restatement is stopped. The variant and the pool status are paired: a `PROVISIONAL` run is only accepted while the pool is `OPEN`, and a `FINAL_AUDITED` run only while it is `BOOKS_CLOSED`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`. A `FINAL_AUDITED` run additionally requires `audited_expense_cents` to be set, and is refused with `ERR_AUDITED_EXPENSE_MISSING` if it is null. Computing a run is discretionary spending, so it is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_POSTURE_FROZEN` while the conference's `reserve_posture` is `FROZEN`.

The eligible member set is fixed: every member of the pool's conference whose `status` is `ADMITTED_PHASING`, `FULL_SHARE` or `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED`, enumerated in `created_at` ascending order. Members in `PETITIONED`, `ADMISSION_DENIED` or `DEPARTED` are excluded outright — a school under withdrawal notice still shares in the pool, but a departed one does not.

The allocation runs in four steps and its rounding is pinned at every point.

**Step one — the net pool.** The expense basis depends on the variant: a `PROVISIONAL` run uses `estimated_expense_cents` and a `FINAL_AUDITED` run uses `audited_expense_cents`. The `net_pool_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` minus that basis, and it may be negative if expenses exceed receipts.

**Step two — the reserve sweep.** The sweep rate is 1500 basis points while the conference's `reserve_posture` is `RESERVE_RESTRICTED` and 500 basis points otherwise. The reserve shortfall is `reserve_target_cents` minus `reserve_balance_cents`, treated as 0 when the balance already meets or exceeds the target. The `reserve_draw_cents` is the smaller of `net_pool_cents` multiplied by the sweep rate divided by 10000 and rounded up to the next whole cent, and that shortfall — so a fully funded reserve draws nothing at all no matter what the rate says. The `distributable_cents` is `net_pool_cents` minus `reserve_draw_cents`. Both halves of that comparison are taken literally when the net pool is negative: the swept figure is then negative too, and being the smaller of the two it becomes the draw, so `distributable_cents` comes out above `net_pool_cents` and posting the run moves the reserve balance down rather than up. Rounding up a negative quotient means rounding toward zero, as ordinary ceiling division does. The draw is computed here but does not touch `reserve_balance_cents` until the run is posted.

**Step three — the per-member weight.** A raw weight in basis points is derived per eligible member, branching on the pool's `allocation_method`. Under `EQUAL_SHARE` the raw weight is the base weight of 10000 basis points for everyone. Under `UNIT_BASED` it is 10000 multiplied by the member's count of postseason units in status `VALIDATED` whose `fiscal_year` equals the pool's `fiscal_year` — so a member with no validated units for that year has a raw weight of exactly 0. Under `APPEARANCE_PERFORMANCE_WEIGHTED` it is 10000, plus 1500 for each media appearance, plus 2500 for each postseason berth. A media appearance is a schedule game in the pool's `fiscal_year` with `media_designated` true in which the member appears as either `home_member_id` or `away_member_id`; for a `PROVISIONAL` run such a game counts while its status is `SCHEDULED` or `PLAYED`, while for a `FINAL_AUDITED` run only `PLAYED` games count. A postseason berth is a bowl appearance of that member in the pool's `fiscal_year`; for a `PROVISIONAL` run it counts while its status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, while for a `FINAL_AUDITED` run only `APPROVED` appearances count. The raw weight is then scaled by the member's ladder position: `weight_bps` is the raw weight multiplied by the member's `distribution_phase_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded down. A member whose `weight_bps` lands on exactly 0 — a fully phased-in school in a unit-based pool with no validated units, or a first-year school whose raw weight is small enough that the phase fraction floors it away — receives no distribution line at all, and therefore is never charged the governance service fee either. The `total_weight_bps` recorded on the run is the sum of `weight_bps` over the members that do receive a line, and if that total is 0 the whole request is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_ALLOCATION_BASIS`.

**Step four — the pinned rounding pass.** Each line's `gross_share_cents` is first `distributable_cents` multiplied by that line's `weight_bps` and divided by `total_weight_bps`, rounded down. The leftover, `distributable_cents` minus the sum of those floored shares, is then handed out one cent at a time. The receiving order is the line members sorted by `weight_bps` descending and, among equal weights, by the member's `created_at` ascending; the cents are dealt out along that order cyclically, so if there are more leftover cents than lines the order wraps around and a line may be topped up more than once. Each time a line receives a cent its `gross_share_cents` rises by 1 and its `remainder_cent_awarded` rises by 1. Rounding each line half-up and summing is explicitly **not** the rule and produces a different, non-conserving answer; the floor-then-deal pass exists so that not one cent of `distributable_cents` disappears.

Immediately after the shares are set, each line's offsets are computed. The `withheld_cents` is `gross_share_cents` multiplied by the provisional withholding of 1000 basis points and divided by 10000, rounded up, on a `PROVISIONAL` run, and exactly 0 on a `FINAL_AUDITED` run. The `governance_fee_cents` is the flat governance and compliance service fee of 1,250,000 cents (12,500 dollars), charged identically on every line regardless of its size, which means a small line can be driven negative by it. The available cents are `gross_share_cents` minus `withheld_cents` minus `governance_fee_cents`. The grant-of-rights offset then applies only when all three of the following hold: the member's `status` is `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED`, its `grant_of_rights_through_fiscal_year` is greater than or equal to the pool's `fiscal_year`, and its outstanding exit balance — `exit_settlement_total_cents` minus `exit_settlement_paid_cents` — is strictly positive. When it applies, the cap is `exit_settlement_total_cents` multiplied by 5000 basis points divided by 10000, rounded down, and `gor_offset_cents` is the smallest of that cap, the outstanding balance, and the available cents treated as 0 when the available cents are negative. When any of the three conditions fails, `gor_offset_cents` is 0. Finally `net_payable_cents` is the available cents minus `gor_offset_cents`, and it may be negative when the flat fee exceeds the share. The `exit_settlement_paid_cents` figure used here is read as of run creation and is only actually advanced when the run is posted.

A successful computation creates the run in `COMPUTED` with one `ACTIVE` line per line member, records the acting user as `computed_by_user_id`, writes `net_pool_cents`, `reserve_draw_cents`, `distributable_cents` and `total_weight_bps`, sets any earlier run on the same pool that is still `COMPUTED` — whatever that older run's `variant`, so a `FINAL_AUDITED` computation displaces a lingering `PROVISIONAL` run just as readily as another final one — to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_run_id` pointing at the new run and every one of that older run's `ACTIVE` lines moved to `VOIDED`, and advances the pool's `version` by 1. The new run itself is created at `version` 1 with `posted_at`, `posted_by_user_id` and `superseded_by_run_id` all null, and the `memo` is not part of its representation. Runs can be listed globally, filtered by `revenue_pool_id`, `superseded_by_run_id`, `variant` and `status`, or per pool, where the listing is already scoped to that pool and accepts only the other three filters, ignoring a `revenue_pool_id` parameter entirely; a run's lines are listed in the members' `created_at` ascending order and can be filtered by `member_id` and `status`; and every line credited to a single member across all runs can be listed and filtered by `status`.

Posting is a `Treasurer` action and is where the money actually moves. It is only available from `COMPUTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `computed_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, a `FINAL_AUDITED` run additionally requires an audit report on that run to be in status `ISSUED` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_AUDIT_REPORT_NOT_ISSUED`, and the parent pool must still be `OPEN` or `BOOKS_CLOSED`. That last condition is tested first rather than last, because §2.8 places `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID` ahead of `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` and both ahead of segregation of duties: a `COMPUTED` run whose pool has been driven back to `DRAFT` by a championship settlement, or carried on to `CLOSED`, therefore reports `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID` even when the acting user is also the one who computed it. Posting sets the run `POSTED`, stamps `posted_at` from the logical clock and `posted_by_user_id` from the acting user, increases the conference's `reserve_balance_cents` by the run's `reserve_draw_cents` and re-derives `reserve_posture` and `funded_ratio_bps` from the new balance, increases `escrow_balance_cents` by the sum of the lines' `withheld_cents`, increases `governance_fee_revenue_cents` by the sum of the lines' `governance_fee_cents`, advances each affected member's `exit_settlement_paid_cents` by that member's line's `gor_offset_cents` where it is positive, and — only when the run's `variant` is `FINAL_AUDITED` and the pool's `pool_type` is `MEDIA_RIGHTS` — writes each line's `gross_share_cents` into that member's `last_full_annual_distribution_cents`, which is the figure every future exit settlement is assessed against. The conference's `version` advances by 1.

Reversing a posted run takes an optional `reason`, is a `Treasurer` action, is available only from `POSTED`, and requires the parent pool not to be `CLOSED` — again the pool is examined before the run, so a `CLOSED` pool refuses the reversal with `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID` whatever state the run is in, and no segregation-of-duties test applies, so the same `Treasurer` who posted a run may reverse it. It is an exact compensation: every `ACTIVE` line moves to `VOIDED`, `reserve_balance_cents` falls by `reserve_draw_cents` and `reserve_posture` is re-derived, `escrow_balance_cents` falls by the sum of `withheld_cents`, `governance_fee_revenue_cents` falls by the sum of `governance_fee_cents`, each member's `exit_settlement_paid_cents` is rolled back by that line's `gor_offset_cents`, and the conference's `version` advances by 1. Reversal leaves every conference-level counter exactly where it stood before the post. Two things are deliberately not undone: `last_full_annual_distribution_cents`, once written onto a member by a final audited media-rights posting, stays where that posting put it, so an exit assessment already keyed on it is not disturbed; and the run keeps the `posted_at` and `posted_by_user_id` it was stamped with, as the record of a payment that really happened. A member's own `updated_at` and `version` move on a post or a reversal only if that member's figures actually changed — a line with no grant-of-rights offset on a run that is not a final audited media-rights run touches nothing. `REVERSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal — a reversed run is never re-posted, and a new run must be computed instead.

### 3.8. Departure: Withdrawal Notice, the Exit Assessment and Rescission

Filing a withdrawal notice is a `Commissioner` action against a member in `FULL_SHARE`; any other status is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. It supplies the `withdrawal_effective_fiscal_year` in which the departure takes effect, optionally the `notice_days` given, and optionally a `reason`. The effective year must be strictly greater than the conference's `current_fiscal_year`, and a notice filed for the current year or an earlier one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_YEAR` (HTTP 422). Filing sets the member's `status` to `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED`, stamps `withdrawal_notice_at` from the logical clock, drops its `voting_weight_bps` to 0 so it can no longer influence any ballot, switches its postseason-unit valuation to the `CURRENT_YEAR_ONLY` variant of §3.6, and advances the member's `version` by 1.

The exit assessment then runs, and its six steps must be taken in exactly this order because step four is deliberately keyed on the base rather than on the total. Step one establishes `notice_days`, and the client has the first word: a `notice_days` supplied in the request is taken at face value and used verbatim as the key to step two, however far it sits from the calendar. Only when the field is omitted does the server derive it, as the whole number of days from `withdrawal_notice_at` to the first day of the `withdrawal_effective_fiscal_year` — that first day being day 1 of the conference's `fiscal_year_start_month` in the calendar year that labels the effective fiscal year, so under the default July start a notice stamped `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` against effective year 2028 derives 700 days and one against effective year 2027 derives 334. The whole number is the elapsed difference truncated toward the past, and it is negative when that opening day has already gone by, which places the notice in the lowest band. A `notice_days` supplied as a negative integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, since it is a count. Step two selects the `notice_multiplier_bps`: 10000 basis points when `notice_days` is 730 or more, 20000 when it is at least 365 but fewer than 730, and 30000 when it is fewer than 365 — so a school that gives less than a year's notice pays three times its annual distribution rather than one. Step three computes `base_assessment_cents` as the member's `last_full_annual_distribution_cents` multiplied by `notice_multiplier_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded up; a member that has never appeared on a posted final audited media-rights run has a `last_full_annual_distribution_cents` of 0 and therefore a base assessment of 0. Step four selects the `surcharge_bracket_bps` from `base_assessment_cents` alone: 200 basis points when the base is below 100,000,000 cents, 350 when it is at least 100,000,000 but below 300,000,000, and 500 when it is 300,000,000 or more. Step five computes `surcharge_cents` as `base_assessment_cents` multiplied by `surcharge_bracket_bps` divided by 10000, rounded up. Step six sets `exit_settlement_total_cents` to `base_assessment_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`. Step four must read the step-three base and must never re-read the step-six total: bracketing on the total would be circular, since the total depends on the surcharge, the surcharge on the bracket, and the bracket on the total. Fixing the base as the bracket key is what breaks that loop, and it means a base sitting just under a bracket ceiling stays in the lower bracket even though its total crosses the ceiling.

Rescinding a notice is also a `Commissioner` action and is only available while the member is `WITHDRAWAL_NOTICED`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`. It is permitted only while the departure has not been ratified and no money has changed hands on it: no membership action of type `WITHDRAW` naming this member may be in `VOTING`, `APPROVED` or `EXECUTED`, and the member's `exit_settlement_paid_cents` must be 0. Both of those extra preconditions report `ERR_MEMBER_STATE_INVALID`, the same code the status guard uses, so the three refusals are indistinguishable by `error_id`. Rescission returns the member's `status` to `FULL_SHARE`, restores its `voting_weight_bps` to 10000, clears `withdrawal_notice_at`, `notice_days` and `withdrawal_effective_fiscal_year` back to null, zeroes `notice_multiplier_bps`, `base_assessment_cents`, `surcharge_bracket_bps`, `surcharge_cents`, `exit_settlement_total_cents` and `exit_settlement_paid_cents`, restores the `ROLLING_SIX_YEAR` valuation variant so the member's whole six-year unit history counts again, and advances the member's `version` by 1. Because the rescission returns the member to a status it already held, the member machine is a cycle rather than a one-way path, and a member may file, rescind and re-file a notice.

The assessment can be read at any time, by a `Commissioner`, `FinanceOfficer`, `Treasurer`, `Auditor`, `PlatformAdmin` or `MemberRepresentative`, and the read shows the working rather than just the answer: the `member_id`, the `notice_days`, the `notice_multiplier_bps`, the `base_assessment_cents`, the `surcharge_bracket_bps` keyed on that base, the `surcharge_cents`, the `exit_settlement_total_cents`, the `exit_settlement_paid_cents` accumulated so far from grant-of-rights offsets, and the `remaining_balance_cents` still owed, which is the total minus the paid amount. The read is available for any member, whatever its status: for one that has never filed a notice it succeeds with `notice_days` null and every assessed figure — multiplier, base, bracket, surcharge, total, paid and remaining balance — at 0, rather than refusing.

### 3.9. Audit Reporting and the Cent-Conservation Identity

An `Auditor` drafts a report attesting a computed run, naming the `conference_id`, the `distribution_run_id` being attested, the `fiscal_year` covered, the `opinion` as one of `UNQUALIFIED`, `QUALIFIED` or `ADVERSE`, and optionally the narrative `note` that accompanies the opinion to the membership. The attested run must be in `COMPUTED`, and drafting against a run in any other status is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The report is created in `DRAFT` with `reconciliation_delta_cents` 0, and reports can be listed and filtered by `conference_id`, `distribution_run_id`, `fiscal_year` and `status`, or read individually.

Issuing the report is what unlocks the posting of a final audited run, and it will not happen unless the run's arithmetic conserves every cent. The identity considers only the run's lines still in status `ACTIVE`, enumerated in the members' `created_at` ascending order; voided lines are excluded entirely, which is why a run whose lines have all been voided reconciles to its `net_pool_cents` less its `reserve_draw_cents`: the reserve draw stays in the identity whatever becomes of the lines, so an all-voided run whose pool drew nothing reconciles to its whole net pool and one that drew a sweep reconciles to the remainder. Summing those active lines gives `total_net_payable_cents`, `total_withheld_cents`, `total_governance_fee_cents` and `total_gor_offset_cents`, and the `reconciliation_delta_cents` is the run's `net_pool_cents` minus the sum of its `reserve_draw_cents` and those four totals. For a run whose lines are all active the delta must be exactly 0 — that is the invariant the pinned remainder pass of §3.7 exists to guarantee, and it is the reason reversing a posted run must restore every conference counter it moved. Issuing is refused with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_IMBALANCE` (HTTP 422) when the delta is anything other than 0, is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` unless the report is in `DRAFT`, and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the attested run's `computed_by_user_id` — an auditor may not attest a run they computed themselves. The attested run's status is checked only when the report is drafted and never again at issue time, so a run that has since been superseded or reversed may still be issued against, provided the identity still balances over its remaining `ACTIVE` lines. A successful issue recomputes and writes `reconciliation_delta_cents`, sets the status to `ISSUED`, records `issued_by_user_id` from the acting user, and advances the report's `version` by 1.

The same identity can be read directly for any run by a `Commissioner`, `FinanceOfficer`, `Treasurer`, `Auditor` or `PlatformAdmin`, and the read returns the `distribution_run_id`, the `net_pool_cents`, the `reserve_draw_cents`, `total_net_payable_cents`, `total_withheld_cents`, `total_governance_fee_cents`, `total_gor_offset_cents` and the `reconciliation_delta_cents`. A `MemberRepresentative` is not admitted to this view.

Acceptance closes the loop. A `Commissioner` records the membership's acceptance of an `ISSUED` report, which requires the attested run to be in `POSTED` at that moment — the membership accepts a report about money that has actually been distributed, not about a proposal, and a run short of `POSTED`, or one already reversed, refuses the acceptance with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, the only refusal this step declares and the same code an out-of-status report draws. Acceptance sets the status to `ACCEPTED`, records `accepted_by_user_id` from the acting user, and advances the report's `version` by 1; `ACCEPTED` is terminal. Acceptance is the governance step the conference relies on before permanently closing the pool, but it is not enforced as one: the pool-closing rule of §3.7 asks only for a posted run and never inspects any report.
