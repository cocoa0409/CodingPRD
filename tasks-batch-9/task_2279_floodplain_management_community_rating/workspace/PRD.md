# Floodplain Management & Community Rating API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal floodplain administrator carries an unusual double burden. On one side sits the regulatory floor: every structure built or substantially rebuilt inside a mapped special flood hazard area must be elevated above the base flood elevation, every elevation must be documented by a licensed surveyor, and every one of those documents must survive an audit years later. On the other side sits the community's own insurance rating: the higher standards a town adopts voluntarily — extra freeboard, cumulative substantial-improvement tracking, repetitive-loss outreach — translate into Community Rating System credit points that reduce flood insurance premiums for every policyholder in the jurisdiction, but only if the documentation is filed, verified, and recertified on schedule. The two obligations interfere with each other constantly: an unresolved enforcement case can retrograde a rating the community spent a decade earning, and a single flood map revision can invalidate a folder full of accepted surveys.

This service is the system of record that keeps both books straight. It is mounted under `/floodplain` and models the full administrative chain: the flood map inventory and its revisions, the parcels and structures those maps govern, development permit review with base flood elevation determination and freeboard arithmetic, elevation certificate collection and review, the cumulative improvement and damage ledger that drives substantial improvement and substantial damage determinations, the appeals that can undo them, post-disaster inspection sweeps, enforcement through violations and remediation orders with the community sanction risk they accumulate, repetitive loss area analysis and owner outreach, and the annual recertification cycle in which the state verifies claimed credit and sets the community's class.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **snapshots are binding**: when a permit is submitted the governing zone, base flood elevation, and adopted freeboard are frozen onto that permit, and later amendments to the community's standards never rewrite them. Second, **derived state is always recomputed, never patched**: sanction risk, credit totals, cumulative ratios, and repetitive-loss classifications are recalculated from their inputs on every triggering write, so that a reversal is genuinely a reversal rather than a second entry that happens to cancel the first.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. Exactly four roles exist, and their spellings are fixed: `StateNfipCoordinator`, `FloodplainAdministrator`, `PropertyOwner`, and `AppealsBoardMember`. If either header is missing, blank, or `X-User-Role` is not one of those four strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Every endpoint listed in §4 is readable by all four roles when it is a `GET`; write authority is narrower and is stated per endpoint in that table.

The `StateNfipCoordinator` is the state-level NFIP and CRS authority. It registers communities, owns the flood map inventory, verifies higher-standards documentation, and makes recertification decisions. When `X-User-Role` is `StateNfipCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The `FloodplainAdministrator` is the municipal official who does the day-to-day work: permit review, issuance and closeout, certificate review, determinations, ledger maintenance, enforcement, repetitive-loss analysis and outreach, credit submissions, and recertification filing. When `X-User-Role` is `FloodplainAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it where a transition says so — a determination stores the acting administrator's identifier in the case's `determined_by_user_id` — but it never resolves that value against an entity's `id`.

The `AppealsBoardMember` sits on the local floodplain appeals board and decides substantial improvement and substantial damage appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealsBoardMember`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `appeal_decided_by_user_id` on the case it decides but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. It is, however, compared to `determined_by_user_id`: this is the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.8 and §3.7.

The `PropertyOwner` is the owner of record acting for a single parcel, and this role alone is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Property`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Property` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a permit is owned by the owner whose identifier equals the permit's `property_id`; an elevation certificate is owned by the owner whose identifier equals the `property_id` of the permit the certificate was filed against; a determination case is owned by the owner whose identifier equals the `property_id` of the case's structure. When a `PropertyOwner` acts on a resource that does not belong to it, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of items skipped before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` appear in the `meta` object of every list response, where `total_count` counts the matching rows before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields, and the Immutable Ledger

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID, with no type prefix and no domain-flavored formatting. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources — communities, FIRM panels, properties, structures, permits, elevation certificates, determination cases, post-disaster inspections, violations, remediation orders, repetitive loss areas, higher-standards submissions, and recertification cycles — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that touch only computed fields such as a recomputed `sanction_risk_points` or a recomputed `cumulative_improvement_pct`.

The one exception is the improvement ledger. An `ImprovementRecord` is append-only and immutable in the eyes of clients: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. A record is never edited; when its effect must be undone, a compensating reversal record is appended instead, and the only flag the server ever flips on the original is the derived `reversed` marker described in §3.6.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Derived Deadlines, and Bounded Loops

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical UTC clock whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every timestamp it stamps, every deadline it computes, and every window comparison it makes reads that logical clock. A test-driving capability lets a `StateNfipCoordinator` or a `FloodplainAdministrator` move the clock forward by supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced. The clock is monotonic: a target instant earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Advancing the clock mutates no entity and fires no deferred action — nothing expires merely because time passed; expiry is evaluated by the write that reads it.

Five derived deadlines are stamped from the clock at the moment of the write that creates them, and none of them is ever recomputed afterwards. A permit's `expires_at` is its `issued_at` plus 365 days. An accepted certificate's `retention_review_due_at` is its `accepted_at` plus 5 calendar years — this is the record-retention audit date that supports CRS Activity 310 credit. A determination's `appeal_deadline_at` is its `determined_at` plus 30 days. A verified standard's `doc_expires_at` is its `verified_at` plus 5 calendar years. An inspection's `deadline_at` is the sweep's `disaster_event_date` plus the sweep's `inspection_window_days`. Day arithmetic is exact; calendar-year arithmetic clamps 29 February to 28 February when the target year has no leap day.

Two loops in the system are bounded, and both bounds are exclusive ceilings on a counter that has not yet been incremented. A permit may re-enter review only while its `revision_round` is strictly less than 3; a remediation order may be extended only while its `extension_count` is strictly less than 2. Unless a rule explicitly says "strictly after", every comparison of the logical clock against a stored deadline is inclusive — acting exactly at the deadline instant is acting on time.

### 2.6. Elevation Arithmetic, Datum Discipline, and Rounding

Elevations in this system are feet above the NAVD88 vertical datum, and every elevation the server computes or stores as a derived value is rounded to exactly 1 decimal place using round-half-up. This applies to a permit's `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`, to a certificate's `normalized_lowest_floor_ft` and `freeboard_margin_ft`, and to a structure's `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` (which is copied from an accepted certificate and therefore inherits its precision). Rounding is applied once at each stated step of a formula, never partway through an addition — where §3 describes an intermediate value as rounded, that rounding is normative and re-ordering it produces a different answer.

Ratios and percentages — a structure's `cumulative_improvement_pct` and `single_event_improvement_pct`, a case's `computed_ratio_pct` and `threshold_pct`, an inspection's `damage_ratio_pct`, and a loss area's `coverage_pct` — are rounded to exactly 2 decimal places using round-half-up. Currency values, including every `cost_usd`, `market_value_usd`, `claim_paid_amount_usd`, `damage_cost_usd`, and the accumulators `cumulative_counted_cost_usd` and `cumulative_claim_paid_usd`, are exact to the cent and are summed without intermediate rounding. Credit points and class numbers are integers throughout; where a formula multiplies a points rate by a fractional quantity, the product is truncated toward zero to a whole number of points as described in §3.9.

All threshold comparisons in this domain are inclusive on the passing side unless stated otherwise: a ratio that lands exactly on 50 percent is substantial, a coverage figure that lands exactly on 75 percent earns partial credit, and a freeboard margin of exactly 0.0 feet is acceptable.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Structures and Communities

Two write operations carry an optimistic-concurrency token, because both of them supersede an existing record and would otherwise let a client discard work it never observed. In both cases the token is a required, non-optional field of the request body; omitting it is a schema violation.

A `POST /floodplain/determination-cases` request must carry `expected_structure_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Structure`. Opening a case legitimately withdraws any prior case of that structure that is still `OPEN` or `DETERMINED` and reverts the structure's `substantial_status`, so if the structure's `version` has advanced since the client read it — because a ledger record landed, because the assessed value was corrected, or because another case was determined — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STRUCTURE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than silently discarding a determination made against newer ledger data.

A `POST /floodplain/higher-standards` request must carry `expected_community_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Community`. Drafting a submission for an activity and cycle year that already has a draft or rejected submission supersedes that submission, and the points being claimed are only meaningful against the freeboard and cumulative standards recorded on the community at the version the client observed. If `expected_community_version` does not equal the community's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMMUNITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Endpoints not named above take no version input at all, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Order of Request Validation

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. The server evaluates in the following sequence and returns on the first failure.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the four declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list in §4, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Path existence** — each identifier in the URL is resolved left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the error: `communityId` raises `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `panelId` raises `ERR_FIRM_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`, `propertyId` raises `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `structureId` raises `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_FOUND`, `permitId` raises `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `certificateId` raises `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `recordId` raises `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `caseId` raises `ERR_DETERMINATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `inspectionId` raises `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `violationId` raises `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `orderId` raises `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `areaId` raises `ERR_LOSS_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `standardId` raises `ERR_HIGHER_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, and `cycleId` raises `ERR_CRS_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404.
4. **Property-owner ownership** — when the acting role is `PropertyOwner`, the owning property must match `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER`. This check guards exactly five operations: permit creation, permit submit or resubmit, elevation certificate filing, certificate amendment, and appeal filing.
5. **Request schema** — required body fields present, enum members valid, numbers inside their declared constraints, `limit` inside 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, `market_value_usd` greater than 0, `claimed_points` at least 1, `cost_usd` at least 0 and present for any non-reversal ledger record, `cycle_end_date` strictly after `cycle_start_date`, and `state_code` exactly two uppercase letters. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. **Body foreign-key existence** — every identifier supplied in the body must resolve, raising that entity's own 404: `community_id`, `firm_panel_id`, `property_id`, `structure_id`, `permit_id`, `violation_id`, and `crs_cycle_id` raise their respective not-found errors, and a `reverses_record_id` that does not resolve raises `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Referential consistency of resolved references** (HTTP 422) — a permit's `structure_id` must belong to the supplied `property_id`, else `ERR_STRUCTURE_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`; a certificate's `structure_id` must equal its permit's `structure_id`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STRUCTURE_MISMATCH`; a `reverses_record_id` must target a record of the same structure and must be present for a reversal and absent otherwise, else `ERR_REVERSAL_TARGET_INVALID`; and a `crs_cycle_id` supplied at outreach launch must belong to the same community as the area, else `ERR_CYCLE_COMMUNITY_MISMATCH`.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_structure_version` and `expected_community_version` as described in §2.7.
9. **Own-state transition gate** — the target resource must be in a state that admits the requested transition, each endpoint raising its own HTTP 409: `ERR_PANEL_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_DEFICIENT`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_DETERMINED`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_ASSESSED`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_LOSS_AREA_NOT_DESIGNATED`, `ERR_OUTREACH_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_OUTREACH_ALREADY_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`. One deliberate asymmetry lives here: issuing a remediation order admits a violation that is already `ORDER_ISSUED` at this step, so that the duplicate-order rule at step 18 is the check that actually rejects a second active order.
10. **Cross-entity state guard** (HTTP 422) — creating a property or submitting a permit requires the governing FIRM panel's `status` to be `EFFECTIVE`, else `ERR_PANEL_NOT_EFFECTIVE`; launching outreach requires the referenced cycle's `status` to be `OPEN`, else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`; and both designation and outreach launch require the area's `analysis_status` to be `ANALYZED`, else `ERR_LOSS_AREA_ANALYSIS_STALE`.
11. **Elevation determinability** — a permit submission requires a governing panel that supports an elevation determination. A panel whose `flood_zone` is `X` raises `ERR_OUTSIDE_FLOOD_HAZARD_AREA`; a panel whose `base_flood_elevation_ft` is null raises `ERR_BFE_UNDETERMINED`.
12. **Elevation adequacy** — on approval, `proposed_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` must be at least `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`; on certificate acceptance, `freeboard_margin_ft` must be at least 0.0. Either failure is `ERR_ELEVATION_BELOW_REQUIRED`.
13. **Substantial-improvement gate on approval** — `ERR_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED`, as described in §3.4.
14. **Closeout documentation** — `ERR_FINISHED_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`, as described in §3.4.
15. **Community sanction gates** — suspension first (`ERR_COMMUNITY_SUSPENDED`), then probation (`ERR_COMMUNITY_ON_PROBATION`), both HTTP 403 and both evaluated only at permit issuance.
16. **Temporal windows** — the appeal window (`ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`), then the inspection assessment window (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`), then the remaining clock comparisons: recertification submitted past due raises `ERR_CYCLE_PAST_DUE`, a lapse recorded while the clock is at or before the deadline raises `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_PAST_DUE`, a default recorded while the clock is at or before the order deadline raises `ERR_ORDER_DEADLINE_NOT_PASSED`, a ledger record dated after the clock raises `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, an order deadline that is not strictly in the future raises `ERR_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE`, and a backwards clock target raises `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`.
17. **Segregation of duties** — an appeal decision by the same identifier recorded in `determined_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
18. **Counters, interval geometry, uniqueness, and single-active constraints** — in this order: the revision ceiling (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), the extension ceiling (`ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), panel effective-date geometry (`ERR_PANEL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP`), recertification-cycle window geometry (`ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), inspection-sweep window geometry (`ERR_SWEEP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), the uniqueness family (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CID_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARCEL_ID`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_YEAR`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DECLARATION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_REVISION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_ACTIVITY`), and finally the single-active constraints `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_ACTIVE` and `ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_REVERSED`.
19. **Credit and certification readiness** — verification of a standard whose recomputed ceiling is zero raises `ERR_STANDARD_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`; certification with an unresolved submission outstanding raises `ERR_UNRESOLVED_SUBMISSIONS` and certification without a required visit raises `ERR_VERIFICATION_VISIT_REQUIRED`.
20. **Documentation completeness and outreach arithmetic** — a finished-construction certificate filed or amended without `surveyor_license_no` raises `ERR_SURVEYOR_LICENSE_REQUIRED`, and a certificate filed against a permit that is not `ISSUED` raises `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`; an outreach launch whose target is below the analyzed count raises `ERR_OUTREACH_TARGET_BELOW_ANALYZED` and a delivery that would overshoot the target raises `ERR_DELIVERY_EXCEEDS_TARGET`.

### 2.9. Flat Addressing and Referential Integrity

Every collection in this service is a peer directly beneath `/floodplain`; there are no nested resource paths. Ownership is expressed with foreign keys carried in request bodies — `community_id`, `firm_panel_id`, `property_id`, `structure_id`, `permit_id`, `inspection_id`, `violation_id`, `reverses_record_id`, `crs_cycle_id` — rather than with URL hierarchy. The left-to-right path-validation discipline of §2.8 step 3 therefore usually resolves a single identifier, but where a path carries more than one segment identifier the leftmost is resolved first and its 404 wins.

Filter parameters on list endpoints follow the same naming. Panels can be filtered by `community_id`, `supersedes_panel_id`, `panel_number`, and `status`; properties by `community_id`, `firm_panel_id`, `repetitive_loss_area_id`, and `repetitive_loss_status`; structures by `property_id`, `anchor_record_id`, `compliance_status`, and `substantial_status`; permits by `property_id`, `structure_id`, `si_determination_case_id`, and `status`; certificates by `permit_id`, `structure_id`, `status`, and `certificate_type`; ledger records by `structure_id`, `permit_id`, `inspection_id`, `reverses_record_id`, `record_type`, and `counted_in_cumulative`; determination cases by `structure_id`, `anchor_record_id`, `status`, and `method`; inspections by `structure_id`, `declaration_code`, and `status`; violations by `property_id`, `structure_id`, `permit_id`, `status`, and `severity`; remediation orders by `violation_id` and `status`; repetitive loss areas by `community_id`, `outreach_crs_cycle_id`, `status`, `analysis_status`, and `outreach_status`; higher-standards submissions by `community_id`, `activity_code`, `documentation_status`, and `documentation_cycle_year`; and recertification cycles by `community_id`, `cycle_year`, and `status`. Every filter is optional and combines conjunctively with the others and with `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Three named cascades run inside the request that triggers them, and all of their effects are visible in the same response. There is no deferred or eventual processing anywhere in this service.

**The post-disaster cascade** fires when an inspection is finalized. It appends a damage record to the structure's ledger; the appended record forces recomputation of the structure's anchor and both ratio methods and of the owning property's repetitive-loss figures; that recomputation may open and immediately determine a substantial-damage case; that determination forces every live permit of the structure to recompute its required elevation and may push approved permits back into revision; and, on a separate branch of the same recomputation, a change to the property's `repetitive_loss_status` marks its assigned repetitive loss area's `analysis_status` as `STALE`.

**The enforcement cascade** fires when a remediation order is recorded as defaulted. The order becomes `DEFAULTED`, its violation escalates, the community's sanction points and posture are re-derived with the defaulted order's penalty now counted, any approved or issued permit named by that violation is revoked, and — if and only if the re-derived posture is `SUSPENDED` — every recertification cycle of the community that is still `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, or `UNDER_VERIFICATION` is retrograded and the community's class is reset to the worst value.

**The map-revision cascade** fires when a filed FIRM panel is activated. The incumbent panel is superseded, every property pointing at the incumbent is repointed to the new panel, permits of those properties whose frozen zone or base flood elevation no longer match the new panel are pushed back into revision with fresh snapshots and a recomputed requirement, and the accepted elevation certificates of those permits' structures are superseded — clearing the structures' recorded lowest floor and returning them to a pending-certificate compliance state.

Wherever a cascade iterates over a collection, it processes rows in ascending `created_at` order. Every row a cascade touches is a mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if a client had written it directly.

### 2.11. Interval Geometry

Three families of records occupy time intervals, and each family uses a different, deliberately specified geometry.

FIRM panel effective dates are ordered rather than overlapping. A panel may be filed or activated only when no other panel sharing its `community_id` and `panel_number` is currently `EFFECTIVE` with an `effective_date` at or after the candidate's `effective_date`; a candidate whose date ties or precedes the incumbent's is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP`. Zero-length coverage — a successor sharing the incumbent's exact effective date — is therefore forbidden.

Recertification cycle coverage intervals are closed on both ends, and a shared endpoint counts as an overlap. A candidate interval running from `cycle_start_date` through `cycle_end_date` inclusive may not intersect the closed interval of any cycle of the same community whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_VERIFICATION`, `CERTIFIED`, or `RETROGRADED`; only `LAPSED` cycles are ignored. An intersection, including one that touches at a single instant, is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

Inspection sweep windows are compared after buffer expansion. A candidate sweep's window is expanded to run from 7 days before its event date to 7 days after its event date plus its `inspection_window_days`, and that expanded window may not intersect the closed window running from any existing sweep's `disaster_event_date` through that sweep's `deadline_at`. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_SWEEP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Community Registration and the Adopted Standards

Everything in the system hangs off a `Community`. A `StateNfipCoordinator` registers one by supplying the municipality's `name`, its two-letter `state_code`, and its NFIP `cid_number`, which must be unique across every community in the system — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CID_NUMBER`. Two adopted standards may be supplied at registration and both have defaults: `freeboard_ft`, the vertical margin the community requires above the base flood elevation, defaults to 1.0 and must lie between 0.0 and 6.0 inclusive with at most two decimal places; `cumulative_period_years`, the look-back window in whole years used by the cumulative substantial-improvement method, defaults to 10 and must lie between 0 and 25 inclusive, where 0 disables cumulative tracking entirely. A freshly registered community starts with `sanction_risk_points` at 0, `compliance_posture` at `GOOD_STANDING`, `crs_credit_points_claimed` and `crs_credit_points_verified` at 0, `crs_class` at 10, `standards_adopted_at` set to the current logical clock, and `version` at 1.

Amending the adopted standards is the floodplain administrator's prerogative, not the state's: a `FloodplainAdministrator` may revise `freeboard_ft`, `cumulative_period_years`, or both. The amendment restamps `standards_adopted_at` to the current logical clock and increments the community's `version`. Lowering a standard has teeth. Every `VERIFIED` higher-standards submission of that community is re-evaluated against the new configuration, and any whose recomputed `max_points_allowed` now falls below its recorded `verified_points` moves to `EXPIRED` with `verified_points` reset to 0 — processed in ascending `created_at` order — after which `crs_credit_points_verified` and `crs_class` are recomputed. What the amendment deliberately does *not* touch is existing permits: each permit keeps the `freeboard_applied_ft` it snapshotted at submit, and no permit requirement is recomputed by a standards amendment.

A computed compliance summary is available to the `StateNfipCoordinator` and the `FloodplainAdministrator` and reports the community's enforcement and rating posture in one payload: `community_id`, the current `sanction_risk_points` and `compliance_posture`, the `counted_violation_count` of violations currently inside the counted set, the `defaulted_order_count` of defaulted orders currently carrying a penalty, the `a510_credit_applied` value actually subtracted by the probation credit rule, and the rating figures `crs_class`, `crs_credit_points_claimed`, `crs_credit_points_verified`, `freeboard_ft`, and `cumulative_period_years`.

### 3.2. The Flood Map Inventory and Map Revision Coordination

Flood maps enter the system as `FirmPanel` records filed by the `StateNfipCoordinator`. A filing names the `community_id`, the `panel_number` printed on the map (for example `12345C0100`), the revision `suffix` letter (for example `H`), the `flood_zone`, and the `effective_date` at which the panel is intended to become the governing map; `base_flood_elevation_ft` is optional at filing. The `flood_zone` is one of `A`, `AE`, `AO`, `VE`, or `X`. For zones `A`, `AE`, and `VE`, `base_flood_elevation_ft` is an elevation in feet NAVD88; for zone `AO` the same field carries a flood *depth* in feet rather than an elevation, which matters materially to the requirement arithmetic in §3.4; and a null value is tolerated only for unnumbered zone `A` panels. A filed panel begins with `status` at `PENDING_REVISION`, `supersedes_panel_id` null, and `version` at 1. At most one `PENDING_REVISION` panel may exist for a given `community_id` and `panel_number` pair; a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_REVISION`.

Activating a panel is what makes it the governing map, and it is the coordinator's most consequential single action. Activation moves the panel from `PENDING_REVISION` to `EFFECTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` with the current logical clock, and records in `supersedes_panel_id` the identifier of the panel that was `EFFECTIVE` for the same `community_id` and `panel_number` at that moment — null when this is the community's first panel for that number. Activation is refused with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_PENDING` when the panel is not `PENDING_REVISION`, and with `ERR_PANEL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP` when an `EFFECTIVE` sibling panel already holds an `effective_date` at or after the candidate's, per §2.11.

The map-revision cascade then runs in four ordered hops. First, the superseded incumbent moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — an effective panel is retired only by the activation of its successor, and a `PENDING_REVISION` panel is never superseded directly. Second, caused by the first hop, every `Property` whose `firm_panel_id` pointed at the superseded panel is repointed to the newly effective panel. Third, caused by the second hop, every `DevelopmentPermit` belonging to one of those repointed properties whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, and whose frozen `determined_bfe_ft` differs from the new panel's `base_flood_elevation_ft` or whose frozen `determined_zone` differs from the new panel's `flood_zone`, moves to `REVISION_REQUIRED` with `determined_zone` and `determined_bfe_ft` re-snapshotted from the new panel and `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` recomputed. Fourth, caused by the third hop, for each permit that the third hop moved to `REVISION_REQUIRED`, every `ElevationCertificate` whose `structure_id` equals that permit's `structure_id` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — the survey no longer reflects the governing map — and that structure's `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` is cleared to null with `compliance_status` returning to `PENDING_CERTIFICATE`, unless it is `PRE_FIRM_EXISTING`, which is never disturbed. Within each affected collection, hops two through four process rows in ascending `created_at` order.

### 3.3. Parcels, Structures, and Two Independent Status Axes

A `FloodplainAdministrator` records each parcel as a `Property` naming its `community_id`, the `firm_panel_id` of the governing panel — which must be `EFFECTIVE` at creation, else `ERR_PANEL_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — the assessor's `parcel_id`, the `mapped_area_code` printed on the panel for the parcel's flood-hazard sub-area, and the owner's `owner_name` and `owner_email`. The `parcel_id` must be unique within its community; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARCEL_ID`. A new property starts with `repetitive_loss_area_id` null, `qualifying_claim_count` 0, `max_window_claim_count` 0, `cumulative_claim_paid_usd` 0, `repetitive_loss_status` `NONE`, and `version` 1. The `owner_email` is the contact of record for repetitive-loss outreach, and `mapped_area_code` is the key that repetitive loss area analysis matches on. A property's identifier is also its owner's credential: as §2.1 states, a `PropertyOwner` authenticates with the `Property` `id`.

On a parcel sit one or more `Structure` records, each created by the administrator with the parent `property_id`, a `structure_use` of `RESIDENTIAL`, `NON_RESIDENTIAL`, or `ACCESSORY`, a `pre_firm` flag that is true when construction predates the community's first FIRM, the `construction_year` of original construction, the `market_value_usd` of the structure excluding land — which must be strictly greater than 0 — and `highest_adjacent_grade_ft`, the highest adjacent grade in feet NAVD88 that serves as the datum for zone `AO` requirements. A new structure has `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` null, `substantial_status` `NONE`, `anchor_record_id` null, `cumulative_counted_cost_usd`, `cumulative_improvement_pct` and `single_event_improvement_pct` all 0, and `version` 1.

A structure carries two status dimensions that advance independently and never read each other. The **compliance axis** is `compliance_status`, one of `PRE_FIRM_EXISTING`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATE`, `COMPLIANT`, or `NON_COMPLIANT`, and it is driven only by elevation-certificate acceptance and by enforcement. At creation the axis starts at `PRE_FIRM_EXISTING` when `pre_firm` is true and at `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` otherwise. Acceptance of a finished-construction certificate moves `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` to `COMPLIANT` provided the structure carries no violation in `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED`. An elevation-deficiency violation naming the structure moves either `COMPLIANT` or `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` to `NON_COMPLIANT`. Remediating that violation, when no other violation of the structure remains in `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED`, moves `NON_COMPLIANT` back to `COMPLIANT` when `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` is not null and back to `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` when it is null. A map revision that supersedes the structure's accepted certificate moves `COMPLIANT` to `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` and clears the recorded elevation. The **determination axis** is `substantial_status`, one of `NONE`, `SUBSTANTIAL_IMPROVEMENT`, or `SUBSTANTIAL_DAMAGE`, and it is driven only by determination case outcomes: a substantial outcome moves `NONE` to the case's `determination_type`, and a sustained appeal, a withdrawn case, or a not-substantial determination moves either substantial value back to `NONE`. Every movement on the determination axis forces the structure's permits to recompute their required elevation.

An administrator may correct a structure's assessed `market_value_usd` — typically after a sustained appeal. The correction replaces the value and increments the structure's `version`, and it re-runs the ledger aggregates against the existing records. Crucially, the per-record `market_value_at_event_usd` snapshots on past ledger rows are *not* rewritten, so a value correction changes which ratio a *future* record produces without retroactively rewriting the denominator of a past one.

A computed improvement summary is available for any structure and reports `structure_id`, the current `anchor_record_id`, the `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` of the closed anchored cumulative window — both null when no counted record exists — the `counted_record_count` of counted, non-reversed records lying inside that window, and the three aggregates `cumulative_counted_cost_usd`, `cumulative_improvement_pct`, and `single_event_improvement_pct`.

### 3.4. The Development Permit Lifecycle

A `PropertyOwner` opens a `DevelopmentPermit` in `DRAFT` by naming the `property_id` it owns, the `structure_id` the work targets — which must belong to that property, else `ERR_STRUCTURE_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` — a `project_type` of `NEW_CONSTRUCTION`, `IMPROVEMENT`, or `REPAIR`, the `declared_project_cost_usd` of the work (at least 0), and the `proposed_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` the applicant intends to build to. An optional `floodproofing_requested` flag, defaulting to false, records a request for dry-floodproofing credit in place of physical elevation. A draft permit has `revision_round` 0, every determination snapshot null, and `version` 1. The permit's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUIRED`, `APPROVED`, `ISSUED`, and one of the three terminal values `CLOSED_COMPLIANT`, `DENIED`, or `REVOKED`.

Submitting the application is what performs the base flood elevation determination, and it is the owner's action. A submission is accepted from `DRAFT` or from `REVISION_REQUIRED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. The submitting owner's identifier must equal the permit's `property_id`. The property's governing panel must be `EFFECTIVE` (`ERR_PANEL_NOT_EFFECTIVE`), its `flood_zone` must not be `X` (`ERR_OUTSIDE_FLOOD_HAZARD_AREA`), and its `base_flood_elevation_ft` must not be null (`ERR_BFE_UNDETERMINED`). A submission may optionally revise `proposed_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`, `declared_project_cost_usd`, and `floodproofing_requested`; any supplied value overwrites the application value *before* the requirement is recomputed. On a first submission out of `DRAFT`, the server snapshots `determined_zone` and `determined_bfe_ft` from the effective panel and `freeboard_applied_ft` from the community's current `freeboard_ft`, then computes `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`. On a resubmission out of `REVISION_REQUIRED`, `revision_round` first increases by one, and all three snapshots are refreshed from current community and panel state before the requirement is recomputed. Resubmission is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 3; a fourth round is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The required lowest floor elevation is assembled in four steps and rounded once at the end. **The base** depends on the frozen zone: for `A`, `AE`, and `VE` the base is `determined_bfe_ft` directly; for `AO`, where `determined_bfe_ft` holds a depth rather than an elevation, the base is the structure's `highest_adjacent_grade_ft` plus `determined_bfe_ft`. **The freeboard** is the larger of the permit's `freeboard_applied_ft` and the zone's own regulatory floor, which is 1.0 foot for zones `A`, `AE`, and `AO` and 2.0 feet for zone `VE`. **The substantial-improvement bump** adds a further 0.5 feet whenever the structure's `substantial_status` is anything other than `NONE`, and adds nothing when it is `NONE`. **The floodproofing credit** subtracts one foot, but only when `floodproofing_requested` is true *and* the structure's `structure_use` is `NON_RESIDENTIAL`; requesting dry-floodproofing on a `RESIDENTIAL` or `ACCESSORY` structure is recorded faithfully on the permit and grants nothing. The base, the freeboard, and the bump are added together and the credit is subtracted, and that single sum is rounded half-up to one decimal place. Only `freeboard_applied_ft` is ever read here — a later change to the community's `freeboard_ft` never reaches a permit that has already been submitted.

Review is the `FloodplainAdministrator`'s action and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION`, or `DENY`, plus an optional `review_note`. Review is admitted only while the permit is `UNDER_REVIEW`, else `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves the permit to `REVISION_REQUIRED` and is itself subject to the revision ceiling, so a request that would exceed three rounds is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. `DENY` moves the permit to the terminal `DENIED`. `APPROVE` clears two gates first. The elevation gate requires `proposed_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` to be at least `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`; a shortfall is `ERR_ELEVATION_BELOW_REQUIRED`. The substantial-improvement gate applies when `project_type` is `IMPROVEMENT` or `REPAIR` and the single-event ratio of `declared_project_cost_usd` to the structure's `market_value_usd`, expressed as a percentage, reaches 50 percent or more: in that case the structure must already carry a `DeterminationCase` whose `status` is `DETERMINED`, `APPEAL_SUSTAINED`, or `APPEAL_DENIED`, and if it does not the approval is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED`. On a successful approval the permit becomes `APPROVED` and `si_determination_case_id` records the newest resolved case of the structure, ordered by `determined_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending, or null when no determination was required.

Issuance is refused unless the permit is `APPROVED` (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`) and is gated on the community's enforcement posture. A `SUSPENDED` community cannot issue any permit at all (`ERR_COMMUNITY_SUSPENDED`). A community merely `ON_PROBATION` may still issue permits for ordinary structures, but not for a structure whose `substantial_status` is anything other than `NONE` (`ERR_COMMUNITY_ON_PROBATION`). Issuance moves the permit to `ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock, and sets `expires_at` to 365 days after `issued_at`.

Closeout is refused unless the permit is `ISSUED` (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`) and requires an `ElevationCertificate` filed against this permit whose `certificate_type` is `FINISHED_CONSTRUCTION` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`; without one, the closeout fails with `ERR_FINISHED_CERTIFICATE_REQUIRED`. A successful closeout moves the permit to the terminal `CLOSED_COMPLIANT`, and when the `project_type` was `IMPROVEMENT` or `REPAIR` it also appends a ledger record for the structure — an `IMPROVEMENT` record whose `source` is `PERMIT_CLOSEOUT`, whose `permit_id` names this permit, whose `cost_usd` equals the permit's `declared_project_cost_usd`, whose `event_date` is the current logical clock, and whose `claim_paid_amount_usd` is 0. That appended record then drives the ledger aggregates and the automatic-case rule of §3.6 exactly as an administrative append would.

Two transitions into a permit are the server's own. An approved permit falls back to `REVISION_REQUIRED` whenever its structure's `substantial_status` changes or its governing panel is replaced and the consequence is material — that is, when the freshly recomputed `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` now exceeds `proposed_lowest_floor_elevation_ft`, or when the governing panel's zone or base flood elevation snapshot changed. And an `APPROVED` or `ISSUED` permit becomes the terminal `REVOKED` when a violation naming that permit in its `permit_id` escalates, as described in §3.8.

### 3.5. Elevation Certificate Collection, Review, and Retention

Surveys arrive as `ElevationCertificate` records filed by the `PropertyOwner` against an issued permit. A filing names the `permit_id`, the `structure_id` — which must equal the permit's `structure_id`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STRUCTURE_MISMATCH` — the `certificate_type`, the `datum` the surveyor worked in, the surveyed `measured_lowest_floor_ft`, and the `measured_bfe_ft` the surveyor read off the map, both expressed in the submitted datum. `certificate_type` is one of `CONSTRUCTION_DRAWINGS`, `BUILDING_UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, or `FINISHED_CONSTRUCTION`, and `datum` is either `NAVD88` or `NGVD29`. The `surveyor_license_no` is optional for the first two certificate types but mandatory for `FINISHED_CONSTRUCTION`; a finished-construction filing or amendment without it is rejected with `ERR_SURVEYOR_LICENSE_REQUIRED`. A certificate may only be filed against a permit whose `status` is `ISSUED`; otherwise the filing is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`. A new certificate begins with `status` `SUBMITTED`, an empty `deficiency_codes` list, and `version` 1.

Datum normalization runs at filing and again at every amendment, in a fixed order that must not be rearranged. The datum offset is added to `measured_lowest_floor_ft` first — the offset is 0.0 feet for `NAVD88` and negative 0.85 feet for `NGVD29` — and that sum is rounded half-up to one decimal place to yield `normalized_lowest_floor_ft`. The permit's current `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` is then subtracted from that already-rounded normalized value, and the difference is rounded half-up to one decimal place again to yield `freeboard_margin_ft`. Rounding twice is deliberate: the margin is always a single-decimal number derived from a single-decimal normalized elevation, never from the raw measurement. The same normalization is re-run whenever the permit's requirement is recomputed while the certificate is still `SUBMITTED`.

Review of a certificate is the `FloodplainAdministrator`'s action and carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `DEFICIENT` plus an optional `deficiency_codes` list. Review is admitted only while the certificate is `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVIEWABLE`. Acceptance additionally requires that the certificate's `structure_id` still equals its permit's `structure_id` and that `freeboard_margin_ft` be at least 0.0 feet; a negative margin is rejected with `ERR_ELEVATION_BELOW_REQUIRED`. An accepted certificate moves to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` with the current logical clock, and sets `retention_review_due_at` to 5 calendar years after `accepted_at`, which is the date the record-retention audit for CRS Activity 310 becomes due. Acceptance also supersedes history: every *other* certificate of the same `structure_id` that was `ACCEPTED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. And when the accepted certificate's `certificate_type` is `FINISHED_CONSTRUCTION`, the structure's `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` is set to the certificate's `normalized_lowest_floor_ft` and the structure's `compliance_status` becomes `COMPLIANT` — unless the structure carries a violation whose `status` is `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED`, in which case the elevation is recorded but the compliance status is left where enforcement put it. A `DEFICIENT` decision moves the certificate to `DEFICIENT` and records the supplied `deficiency_codes` verbatim.

The amendment loop closes the circle. A `PropertyOwner` whose identifier matches the `property_id` of the certificate's permit may amend a `DEFICIENT` certificate — amending any other status is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_DEFICIENT` — by resupplying `datum`, `measured_lowest_floor_ft`, `measured_bfe_ft`, and, where required, `surveyor_license_no`. The amendment replaces those four values, returns the certificate to `SUBMITTED`, resets `deficiency_codes` to the empty list, and recomputes `normalized_lowest_floor_ft` and `freeboard_margin_ft`. A certificate may therefore cycle between `SUBMITTED` and `DEFICIENT` as many times as the survey needs. The certificate list view doubles as the community's record-retention inventory, which is why it filters on `certificate_type` and `status` as well as on `permit_id` and `structure_id`.

### 3.6. The Cumulative Improvement Ledger

Every improvement and every flood damage a structure sustains is recorded as an `ImprovementRecord`, an append-only row in the structure's cumulative ledger. Records reach the ledger by three routes, distinguished by `source`: `PERMIT_CLOSEOUT` when a permit closeout posts the completed work and sets `permit_id`; `DISASTER_INSPECTION` when a finalized inspection posts flood damage and sets `inspection_id`; and `ADMIN_ADJUSTMENT` when a `FloodplainAdministrator` appends a row directly. A fourth value, `REVERSAL_ADJUSTMENT`, marks a compensating reversal. Whichever route created it, a record names its `structure_id`, a `record_type` of `IMPROVEMENT`, `DAMAGE`, or `REVERSAL`, and an `event_date` — the date the work or the damage actually occurred, which is what drives window membership and which may not be later than the current logical clock (`ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`). The record also carries `cost_usd`, the cost of the work or the damage; `market_value_at_event_usd`, a snapshot of the structure's `market_value_usd` taken at record creation, which is the denominator of both ratio methods; `claim_paid_amount_usd`, the NFIP claim paid for the event, defaulting to 0; and the optional `is_code_compliance_only` flag, defaulting to false, which marks work required solely to correct previously cited health and safety code violations. For a direct administrative append, `source` is `ADMIN_ADJUSTMENT` for an `IMPROVEMENT` or `DAMAGE` row and `REVERSAL_ADJUSTMENT` for a `REVERSAL`; `permit_id` and `inspection_id` are set only on the routes named above and are null otherwise.

Reversal is the only way to undo a ledger entry. A `REVERSAL` record must name the row it cancels in `reverses_record_id`, and that field must be absent on any non-reversal row and must target a record of the same structure — a missing, superfluous, or cross-structure target is rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_TARGET_INVALID`. A row that has already been reversed cannot be reversed again (`ERR_RECORD_ALREADY_REVERSED`). A reversal copies its `cost_usd`, `claim_paid_amount_usd`, and `event_date` from its target, is itself never counted, and flips the target's `reversed` flag to true. Every other row carries `reversed` as false until that happens.

Whether a row counts toward cumulative tracking is recorded on the row itself as `counted_in_cumulative`, and the rule is a conjunction of three conditions: the `record_type` must be `IMPROVEMENT` or `DAMAGE`, `is_code_compliance_only` must be false, and `cost_usd` must be at least 500.00. Reversals, code-compliance-only work, and small jobs below five hundred dollars are therefore excluded from the aggregates by construction.

The aggregates themselves are recomputed from scratch on every ledger append, on every reversal, and on every correction of a structure's `market_value_usd`. The candidate set is every record of the structure whose `counted_in_cumulative` is true and whose `reversed` is false. When that set is empty, `anchor_record_id` is set to null, `cumulative_counted_cost_usd` to 0, and both `cumulative_improvement_pct` and `single_event_improvement_pct` to 0.00, and nothing further happens. Otherwise the **anchor** is chosen as the candidate with the latest `event_date`, breaking ties by the latest `created_at` — `created_at` is the only sanctioned tiebreaker and `id` is never used for ordering — and its identifier is written to the structure's `anchor_record_id`. The **cumulative window** is closed on both ends: it starts at the anchor's `event_date` minus the community's `cumulative_period_years` counted in whole calendar years (with 29 February clamped to 28 February) and ends at the anchor's `event_date` itself, so a record dated exactly on either endpoint is inside the window. When `cumulative_period_years` is 0 the window collapses to the anchor's single date, and only records sharing the anchor's exact `event_date` are summed. `cumulative_counted_cost_usd` is the exact sum of `cost_usd` over the candidates inside that window. The shared denominator for both ratios is the anchor's `market_value_at_event_usd`. `cumulative_improvement_pct` is that windowed sum divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimals; `single_event_improvement_pct` is the anchor's own `cost_usd` divided by the same denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimals.

Every append also re-runs the property's repetitive-loss classification described in §3.9, increments the structure's `version`, and evaluates the **automatic-case rule**. When the freshly computed `cumulative_improvement_pct` reaches 50 percent or more and no `DeterminationCase` of that structure is currently `OPEN`, `DETERMINED`, or `APPEALED`, the server opens a case with `method` `CUMULATIVE` on its own initiative. The status it opens in depends on the record's `source`: a record sourced from `ADMIN_ADJUSTMENT` or `PERMIT_CLOSEOUT` produces a case in `OPEN`, awaiting a human determination, whereas a record sourced from `DISASTER_INSPECTION` produces a case created directly in `DETERMINED` with `determined_by_user_id` null. Finally, reversing the record that a case anchored moves that case to `WITHDRAWN` and reverts the structure's `substantial_status` to `NONE`, and when a linked property's `repetitive_loss_status` changes and that property has a `repetitive_loss_area_id`, the referenced area's `analysis_status` becomes `STALE`.

### 3.7. Determinations, Appeals, and Post-Disaster Inspection

A `DeterminationCase` is the formal record of a substantial improvement or substantial damage finding. A `FloodplainAdministrator` opens one by naming the `structure_id`, the `method` to be applied — `SINGLE_EVENT` or `CUMULATIVE` — and the `expected_structure_version` token of §2.7. Opening a case supersedes the structure's determination history: any case of that structure whose `status` is `OPEN` or `DETERMINED` moves to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, and if the withdrawn case had been `DETERMINED` the structure's `substantial_status` reverts to `NONE` in compensation. The new case begins with `status` `OPEN`, `trigger_source` `ADMIN`, `anchor_record_id` copied from the structure's current `anchor_record_id` (null when the structure has no counted record), `threshold_pct` 50.0, `outcome` null, and the structure's `version` incremented by one. Its `determination_type` is derived from the anchor record's `record_type`: an `IMPROVEMENT` anchor yields `SUBSTANTIAL_IMPROVEMENT` and a `DAMAGE` anchor yields `SUBSTANTIAL_DAMAGE`, and when the anchor is null the type is `SUBSTANTIAL_IMPROVEMENT`. Cases opened by the server carry `trigger_source` `PERMIT_REVIEW` or `DISASTER_INSPECTION` instead of `ADMIN`, according to what created them.

Recording the determination is admitted only while the case is `OPEN`, else `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_OPEN`. The ratio is selected by the case's `method`, and the two methods differ only in the numerator. When `anchor_record_id` is null the `computed_ratio_pct` is 0.00 — the empty-ledger edge, which is never a division by zero. When `method` is `SINGLE_EVENT`, `computed_ratio_pct` takes the structure's `single_event_improvement_pct`: the classic per-permit test applied to the anchor event alone, ignoring earlier work inside the look-back window, so a ledger with several moderate jobs can come out `NOT_SUBSTANTIAL`. When `method` is `CUMULATIVE`, `computed_ratio_pct` takes the structure's `cumulative_improvement_pct`: the adopted higher standard, whose numerator sums every counted record inside the closed window from the anchor's `event_date` minus the community's `cumulative_period_years` through the anchor's `event_date`, so the very same ledger can come out `SUBSTANTIAL`. `threshold_pct` is set to 50.00 and the `outcome` is `SUBSTANTIAL` when `computed_ratio_pct` is at least `threshold_pct` and `NOT_SUBSTANTIAL` otherwise. The aggregates read are those standing at the determination instant, but the case's `anchor_record_id` is the one snapshotted at case creation and is never re-anchored at determination time. Determination stamps `determined_by_user_id` with the acting administrator's identifier, `determined_at` with the current logical clock, and `appeal_deadline_at` with 30 days after `determined_at`. A `SUBSTANTIAL` outcome sets the structure's `substantial_status` to the case's `determination_type`; a `NOT_SUBSTANTIAL` outcome sets it to `NONE`. Either way the change re-runs the required-elevation arithmetic for every permit of that structure whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUIRED`, or `APPROVED`, and may push `APPROVED` permits back to `REVISION_REQUIRED`.

The owner's remedy is an appeal. A `PropertyOwner` whose identifier equals the `property_id` of the case's structure may appeal a `DETERMINED` case — any other status is rejected with `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_DETERMINED` — by supplying an `appeal_basis`, provided the current logical clock is at or before `appeal_deadline_at`; a late filing is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing moves the case to `APPEALED` and stamps `appealed_at`.

The appeal is decided by an `AppealsBoardMember` with a `decision` of `SUSTAIN` or `DENY` and an optional `decision_note`, and only while the case is `APPEALED`, else `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_PENDING`. Segregation of duties is absolute: the deciding identifier must differ from the `determined_by_user_id` recorded on the case, and a self-decision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The two branches diverge deliberately and carry different side-effect sets. `SUSTAIN` moves the case to the terminal `APPEAL_SUSTAINED`, records `appeal_decided_by_user_id`, flips `outcome` to `NOT_SUBSTANTIAL`, reverts the structure's `substantial_status` to `NONE`, and recomputes `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` for every permit of that structure whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUIRED`, or `APPROVED` — it fully compensates the determination. `DENY` moves the case to the terminal `APPEAL_DENIED` and records `appeal_decided_by_user_id`, and does nothing else: `outcome` and the structure's `substantial_status` are left exactly as the determination set them, and no permit is recomputed.

Post-disaster inspections arrive in bulk. A `FloodplainAdministrator` opens a sweep for a community by supplying the `declaration_code` of the disaster declaration, the `event_date` of the flood event, and the `inspection_window_days` allowed for field assessment. The `declaration_code` must be unique per community (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DECLARATION_CODE`) and the sweep's buffer-expanded window must not conflict with an existing sweep of that community, per §2.11. Eligibility is a conjunction: a property must belong to this community *and* its governing panel's `status` must be `EFFECTIVE` *and* that panel's `flood_zone` must be one of `A`, `AE`, `AO`, or `VE`. Every `Structure` of every eligible property is swept in ascending `created_at` order, and each produces one `PostDisasterInspection` with `status` `SCHEDULED`, the sweep's `declaration_code`, `disaster_event_date` equal to the sweep's `event_date`, and `deadline_at` equal to that date plus `inspection_window_days`. The sweep response reports the `declaration_code`, the `event_date`, the computed `deadline_at`, the `inspections_created` count, and the `inspection_ids` that were created; when the eligible set is empty the count is 0 and the identifier list is empty.

Assessment is admitted only while an inspection is `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SCHEDULED`) and only while the current logical clock is at or before `deadline_at` (`ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`). The administrator supplies `damage_cost_usd`, the estimated cost to restore the structure to its pre-damage condition (at least 0), and optionally `claim_paid_amount_usd`, the NFIP claim paid for the event (at least 0, defaulting to 0). Assessment snapshots `market_value_at_inspection_usd` from the structure's `market_value_usd`, computes `damage_ratio_pct` as `damage_cost_usd` divided by that snapshot, multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to two decimals, moves the inspection to `ASSESSED`, and stamps `assessed_at`.

Finalization is admitted only from `ASSESSED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_ASSESSED`) and is where the post-disaster cascade of §2.10 runs in full. The inspection moves to the terminal `FINALIZED` with `finalized_at` stamped. Hop one appends a ledger record for the structure with `record_type` `DAMAGE`, `source` `DISASTER_INSPECTION`, `inspection_id` naming this inspection, `cost_usd` equal to `damage_cost_usd`, `claim_paid_amount_usd` copied from the inspection, and `event_date` equal to `disaster_event_date`. Hop two, caused by hop one, recomputes the structure's `anchor_record_id`, `cumulative_counted_cost_usd`, `cumulative_improvement_pct`, and `single_event_improvement_pct`, and recomputes the owning property's repetitive-loss fields. Hop three, caused by hop two, opens a case directly in `DETERMINED` with `method` `CUMULATIVE` — but only when `cumulative_improvement_pct` has reached 50 percent or more and no case of the structure is `OPEN`, `DETERMINED`, or `APPEALED` — and sets the structure's `substantial_status` to `SUBSTANTIAL_DAMAGE`. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes `required_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` for every permit of the structure whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUIRED`, or `APPROVED`, now including the extra half-foot substantial-improvement bump, and moves any `APPROVED` permit whose proposed elevation now falls short to `REVISION_REQUIRED`. Hop five, caused independently by hop two, marks the `analysis_status` of the property's assigned repetitive loss area as `STALE` whenever the property's `repetitive_loss_status` changed and `repetitive_loss_area_id` is set.

### 3.8. Enforcement: Violations, Remediation Orders, and Community Sanction

A `FloodplainAdministrator` issues a `Violation` naming the offending `property_id`, a `violation_type` of `NO_PERMIT`, `ELEVATION_DEFICIENT`, `MISSING_CERTIFICATE`, or `UNPERMITTED_FILL`, and a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `CRITICAL`. A `structure_id` may be supplied and is left null for site-only violations such as `UNPERMITTED_FILL`; a `permit_id` may be supplied and is null when the offending work was never permitted. A new violation opens with `status` `OPEN`, `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `sanction_points_contributed` set to its severity weight, and `version` 1. The violation's `status` moves through `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, and `ESCALATED` to one of the terminal values `REMEDIATED` or `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED`, and `resolved_at` is stamped when it reaches either terminal value. Issuing a violation immediately recomputes the community's sanction risk, and when the `violation_type` is `ELEVATION_DEFICIENT` and a `structure_id` was supplied, that structure's `compliance_status` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT`.

Sanction risk is a single-pass, fixed-order computation, re-derived from scratch on every violation creation or resolution, every remediation order resolution, and every verification or expiry of an enforcement-activity credit. **Step one** produces the raw score: the severity weights of every violation belonging to any property of the community whose `status` is `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED` — 2 points for `MINOR`, 6 for `MAJOR`, 12 for `CRITICAL` — plus 5 points for each `DEFAULTED` remediation order whose violation is still `ESCALATED` and whose violation belongs to a property of the community. Violations that reached `REMEDIATED` or `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED` contribute nothing. **Step two** reads a provisional tier off that raw score using the tier rule below. **Step three** grants a credit of 4 points, and grants it only when that provisional tier is exactly `ON_PROBATION` *and* the community holds a higher-standards submission for the enforcement activity `A510_HIGHER_STD_ENFORCEMENT` whose `documentation_status` is `VERIFIED`; in every other case the credit is 0. **Step four** sets `sanction_risk_points` to the raw score minus the credit, floored at 0, and sets `compliance_posture` to the tier of that final value — the provisional tier of step two is discarded and is never the answer. The tier rule is inclusive on the worse side: a score of 25 points or more is `SUSPENDED`, a score of 10 points or more is `ON_PROBATION`, and anything lower is `GOOD_STANDING`. The order matters precisely because the credit depends on the provisional tier while the tier depends on the points; the computation is never iterated to a fixed point.

Ordering remediation is the next escalation. A `RemediationOrder` names its `violation_id`, the `required_action` the owner must complete, and a `deadline_at` that must be strictly after the current logical clock — a deadline at or before the clock is rejected with `ERR_DEADLINE_NOT_IN_FUTURE`. At most one order per violation may be active at a time: if an order with `status` `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED` already exists for that violation, the second is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. This is why the state gate for order issuance deliberately admits a violation that is already `ORDER_ISSUED` — the uniqueness rule, not the state gate, is what reports the duplicate. Issuing an order starts it at `ISSUED` with `extension_count` 0 and `version` 1, and moves the violation from `OPEN` to `ORDER_ISSUED`, which keeps the violation inside the counted set at its unchanged severity weight and therefore leaves the community's sanction points where they were while still triggering a recomputation.

Extensions are bounded and monotonic. An order may be extended while its `status` is `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`, while `extension_count` is strictly less than 2, and only with a `deadline_at` strictly after the order's current `deadline_at`; a third extension or a non-advancing deadline is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and an attempt to extend an order that is already `SATISFIED` or `DEFAULTED` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The first extension moves the order from `ISSUED` to `EXTENDED`; subsequent extensions are a self-loop on `EXTENDED`. Either way `extension_count` increases by one and `deadline_at` is replaced by the supplied value.

Resolving an order carries a `resolution` of `SATISFIED` or `DEFAULTED`, and is admitted only while the order is `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED`. `SATISFIED` moves the order to the terminal `SATISFIED`, stamps `resolved_at`, moves the violation to `REMEDIATED` when it is still `OPEN` or `ORDER_ISSUED`, and recomputes the community's `sanction_risk_points` and `compliance_posture`. `DEFAULTED` requires that the current logical clock be strictly past the order's `deadline_at`, else `ERR_ORDER_DEADLINE_NOT_PASSED`, and it opens the enforcement cascade. Hop one moves the order to the terminal `DEFAULTED` and stamps `resolved_at`. Hop two moves the violation from `ORDER_ISSUED` to `ESCALATED`. Hop three-a, caused by hop two, recomputes `sanction_risk_points` with the defaulted order now contributing its 5-point penalty and re-derives `compliance_posture`. Hop three-b, also caused by hop two, revokes the permit named in the violation's `permit_id` when that field is set and the permit's `status` is `APPROVED` or `ISSUED`, moving it to the terminal `REVOKED`. Hop four, caused by hop three-a, fires only when the re-derived `compliance_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED`: every `CrsCycle` of the community whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, or `UNDER_VERIFICATION` moves to the terminal `RETROGRADED` and the community's `crs_class` is reset to 10.

Resolving the violation itself carries a `resolution` of `REMEDIATED` or `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED` and an optional `resolution_note`, and is admitted from `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED` alike; attempting it on a violation already `REMEDIATED` or `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED` is rejected with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. `REMEDIATED` moves the violation to the terminal `REMEDIATED`, stamps `resolved_at`, drops `sanction_points_contributed` to 0, moves every remediation order of that violation whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `EXTENDED` to `SATISFIED` in ascending `created_at` order, recomputes the community's `sanction_risk_points` and `compliance_posture`, and — when the `violation_type` was `ELEVATION_DEFICIENT`, a `structure_id` was named, and that structure carries no other violation in `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED` — restores the structure's `compliance_status` to `COMPLIANT` if its `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` is not null and to `PENDING_CERTIFICATE` if it is null. Remediating from `ESCALATED` runs that identical side-effect list and additionally undoes the escalation penalty: the defaulted order stops contributing its 5 points because its violation is no longer `ESCALATED`, so the community's sanction score returns to its pre-violation value. `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED`, reachable from `OPEN`, `ORDER_ISSUED`, or `ESCALATED` with the same identical treatment across all three, moves the violation to the terminal `CLOSED_UNRESOLVED`, stamps `resolved_at`, drops `sanction_points_contributed` to 0, and recomputes the community's sanction points and posture — but grants no compliance restoration to any structure.

### 3.9. Repetitive Loss Analysis, Outreach, Credit, and Recertification

Repetitive loss classification is a property-level computation re-run on every ledger append or reversal touching any structure of the property, and again on every repetitive loss area analysis. The qualifying set is every `DAMAGE` record, not reversed, whose `claim_paid_amount_usd` is at least 1000.00, drawn from every `Structure` whose `property_id` is this property, sorted by `event_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. `qualifying_claim_count` is the size of that set and `cumulative_claim_paid_usd` is the exact sum of its `claim_paid_amount_usd` values. `max_window_claim_count` is found by treating each qualifying claim in turn as an anchor, counting the qualifying claims whose `event_date` falls in the closed window from the anchor's `event_date` through 10 calendar years after it, and keeping the largest count found. The classification then reads off `max_window_claim_count`: the property is `SEVERE_REPETITIVE_LOSS` when that figure is 4 or more, and also when it is 2 or more while a newest qualifying claim exists and `cumulative_claim_paid_usd` strictly exceeds that newest claim's `market_value_at_event_usd`; failing that it is `REPETITIVE_LOSS` when the figure is 2 or more; and otherwise `repetitive_loss_status` is `NONE`.

A `RepetitiveLossArea` is delineated by a `FloodplainAdministrator` with its `community_id`, its `area_code` — unique per community, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_CODE` — and a narrative `boundary_description`. An area advances along three independent axes, and no axis reads another. On the **designation axis**, `status` starts at `DRAFT` and reaches the terminal `DESIGNATED`. On the **analysis axis**, `analysis_status` starts at `PENDING_ANALYSIS` and moves among `ANALYZED` and `STALE`. On the **outreach axis**, `outreach_status` starts at `NOT_STARTED` and moves among `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, and `INSUFFICIENT`. A new area has all counts at 0 and `version` 1.

Running the analysis is admitted while `analysis_status` is `PENDING_ANALYSIS` or `STALE`, and it is the same operation in both cases with the same effects — the second form is simply the re-analysis loop. The analysis links parcels by area code: every `Property` whose `community_id` matches the area's and whose `mapped_area_code` equals the area's `area_code` has its `repetitive_loss_area_id` set to this area, processed in ascending `created_at` order. `property_count` is then recounted as the number of properties linked by this analysis and `rl_property_count` as the number of those linked properties whose `repetitive_loss_status` is anything other than `NONE`, `analysis_status` becomes `ANALYZED`, and `analyzed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock. The reverse edge is the server's: when a linked property's `repetitive_loss_status` changes, an `ANALYZED` area drops to `STALE`.

Designation requires two things: `status` must be `DRAFT`, and `analysis_status` must be `ANALYZED`. An area already `DESIGNATED` fails the own-state gate of §2.8 step 9 with `ERR_LOSS_AREA_NOT_DESIGNATED`; an area whose analysis is `PENDING_ANALYSIS` or has gone `STALE` fails the cross-axis guard of step 10 with `ERR_LOSS_AREA_ANALYSIS_STALE`. Designation moves `status` to the terminal `DESIGNATED`.

Launching outreach is the credit-bearing step and has five preconditions. The area's `status` must be `DESIGNATED`, else `ERR_LOSS_AREA_NOT_DESIGNATED`. Its `analysis_status` must be `ANALYZED`, else `ERR_LOSS_AREA_ANALYSIS_STALE`. Its `outreach_status` must be `NOT_STARTED`, `COMPLETED`, or `INSUFFICIENT` — a relaunch over an in-flight effort is rejected with `ERR_OUTREACH_ALREADY_IN_PROGRESS`. The `crs_cycle_id` supplied in the payload must name a cycle of the same community, else `ERR_CYCLE_COMMUNITY_MISMATCH`, and that cycle's `status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`. And the supplied `target_property_count`, the certified mailing-list size, must be at least the area's analyzed `property_count`, else `ERR_OUTREACH_TARGET_BELOW_ANALYZED`. A launch moves `outreach_status` to `IN_PROGRESS`, resets `delivered_count` to 0 and `coverage_pct` to 0, and snapshots `target_property_count` from the payload together with `outreach_crs_cycle_id` and `outreach_cycle_year` from the referenced cycle.

Deliveries are recorded against the in-flight effort by supplying a `delivered_increment`, admitted only while `outreach_status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_OUTREACH_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`. The increment is added to `delivered_count`, and a total that would exceed `target_property_count` is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERY_EXCEEDS_TARGET`. `coverage_pct` is then recomputed as 0.00 when `target_property_count` is 0, and otherwise as `delivered_count` divided by `target_property_count`, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Closing the effort is also admitted only from `IN_PROGRESS`, and the coverage tier decides the branch: a `coverage_pct` of at least 75.00 moves `outreach_status` to `COMPLETED`, and a `coverage_pct` below 75.00 moves it to `INSUFFICIENT`. Both branches are legitimate closures; only the label differs, and both remain eligible for the cycle score below.

The cycle outreach score aggregates the areas in a strictly specified two-stage way. The scored set is every area of the community whose `status` is `DESIGNATED`, whose `outreach_status` is `COMPLETED` or `INSUFFICIENT`, and whose `outreach_cycle_year` equals the documentation cycle year being evaluated, taken in ascending `created_at` order; areas still `IN_PROGRESS` or `NOT_STARTED` are excluded entirely. The score is 0.00 when that set is empty, and otherwise it is the arithmetic mean of the members' **already-rounded** `coverage_pct` values, rounded half-up to two decimal places a second time. Pooling the raw numerators and denominators — summing every `delivered_count` and dividing by the sum of every `target_property_count` — is forbidden and yields a different, wrong score. The outreach credit tier then reads off that score: a score of at least 90.00 earns 50 points, a score of at least 75.00 but below 90.00 earns 25 points, and a lower score earns 0.

Credit is claimed through `HigherStandard` submissions. A `FloodplainAdministrator` drafts one with the `community_id`, an `activity_code`, the `documentation_cycle_year` the submission belongs to, the `claimed_points` being claimed (at least 1), and the `expected_community_version` token of §2.7. The `activity_code` is one of `A430_FREEBOARD`, `A430_CUMULATIVE_SI`, `A420_OPEN_SPACE`, `A510_HIGHER_STD_ENFORCEMENT`, or `A330_OUTREACH`. Drafting supersedes any submission of the same `community_id`, `activity_code`, and `documentation_cycle_year` whose `documentation_status` is `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, moving it to the terminal `EXPIRED`; but a submission for that same triple whose `documentation_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED` blocks the draft outright with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_ACTIVITY`. A new submission has `documentation_status` `DRAFT`, `verified_points` 0, `max_points_allowed` computed from live state, `version` 1, and it increments the community's `version` by one.

Each activity has its own ceiling, recomputed from live community, area, and enforcement state every time it is needed. For `A430_FREEBOARD` the ceiling is 100 points for each foot of adopted `freeboard_ft`, counting no more than 5.0 feet, with the product truncated downward to a whole number of points — so 2.5 feet of freeboard yields 250 and 5.4 feet still yields 500. For `A430_CUMULATIVE_SI` the ceiling is 90 points when the community's `cumulative_period_years` is at least 10, and 0 otherwise. For `A420_OPEN_SPACE` the ceiling is 50 points for each `DESIGNATED` repetitive loss area of the community, capped at 200 points however many areas exist. For `A510_HIGHER_STD_ENFORCEMENT` the ceiling is 60 points when at least one violation belonging to any property of the community has reached `REMEDIATED`, and 0 otherwise. For `A330_OUTREACH` the ceiling is the outreach credit tier described above, evaluated for the submission's own `documentation_cycle_year`. Nothing in this computation reads the recertification cycle.

Submitting the documentation package moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_DRAFT` — stamps `submitted_at`, and recomputes the community's `crs_credit_points_claimed` as the sum of `claimed_points` over every submission whose `documentation_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`. Verification belongs to the `StateNfipCoordinator`, never to the administrator who submitted, and carries a `decision` of `VERIFY` or `REJECT` with an optional `rejection_reason`; it is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_SUBMITTED`. `VERIFY` first recomputes `max_points_allowed`, and refuses with `ERR_STANDARD_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` when that ceiling is 0 — the live community, area, and violation state must actually support the activity. Otherwise `verified_points` becomes the smaller of `claimed_points` and `max_points_allowed`, `documentation_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, `verified_at` is stamped, `doc_expires_at` is set to 5 calendar years after `verified_at`, and the community's `crs_credit_points_verified` is recomputed as the sum of `verified_points` over submissions whose `documentation_status` is `VERIFIED`. Verifying or expiring an `A510_HIGHER_STD_ENFORCEMENT` submission additionally re-runs the sanction-risk computation of §3.8, since the probation credit depends on it. `REJECT` moves the submission to `REJECTED`, records the `rejection_reason`, resets `verified_points` to 0, and recomputes `crs_credit_points_claimed`. A `VERIFIED` submission reaches the terminal `EXPIRED` — with `verified_points` reset to 0 and both `crs_credit_points_verified` and `crs_class` recomputed — when a standards amendment lowers the community below its verified level, when a later submission supersedes it, or when certification finds its `doc_expires_at` at or before the current logical clock.

Recertification runs on `CrsCycle` records opened by the `StateNfipCoordinator` with the `community_id`, the `cycle_year` — unique per community, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_YEAR` — the closed coverage interval `cycle_start_date` through `cycle_end_date` with the end strictly after the start, and the submission deadline `due_at`. A new cycle has `status` `OPEN`, `total_claimed_points` and `total_verified_points` 0, `version` 1, and `verification_visit_required` computed from its `cycle_year`. That flag is true when `cycle_year` is an exact multiple of 5, and also true whenever a `provisional_class` has been computed and is numerically lower — that is, better — than `prior_class`.

Submitting the recertification is the `FloodplainAdministrator`'s filing, admitted only from `OPEN` (`ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`), only while the logical clock is at or before `due_at` (`ERR_CYCLE_PAST_DUE`), and only when at least one `HigherStandard` of the community has `documentation_status` `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`. Submission moves the cycle to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at`, freezes `total_claimed_points` from the community's `crs_credit_points_claimed` and `prior_class` from its `crs_class`, computes `provisional_class`, and recomputes `verification_visit_required`. The class formula subtracts, from 10, the number of whole 500-point blocks the points total contains, truncating any partial block downward; the result is then raised to 1 if it came out lower, since 1 is the best attainable class; and if the community's `compliance_posture` is anything other than `GOOD_STANDING`, the class is instead forced to 10 outright — no credit accrues to a community on probation or under suspension. The boundary is inclusive on the block: exactly 500 points is class 9. The **claimed variant**, written to `provisional_class` at submission and never to the community, feeds the formula with `crs_credit_points_claimed`, which sums `claimed_points` and is therefore unbounded by any activity ceiling — so the provisional class can be better than any class the community can actually be certified at.

Recording the state verification visit is admitted only from `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_CYCLE_NOT_SUBMITTED`), takes an optional `visit_note`, moves the cycle to `UNDER_VERIFICATION`, and stamps `verification_visit_completed_at`. Certification is admitted from `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_VERIFICATION` alike, with identical preconditions and identical effects in both cases — any other status is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`. It additionally requires that `verification_visit_required` be false or `verification_visit_completed_at` be set, else `ERR_VERIFICATION_VISIT_REQUIRED`, and that no `HigherStandard` of the community still be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_UNRESOLVED_SUBMISSIONS`. Certification then runs an expiry sweep: every `VERIFIED` submission of the community whose `doc_expires_at` is at or before the current logical clock moves to `EXPIRED` in ascending `created_at` order, after which `crs_credit_points_verified` is recomputed. `total_verified_points` is frozen from that recomputed figure, `resulting_class` is computed by the **verified variant** of the same class formula — fed with `crs_credit_points_verified`, which sums `verified_points`, each already capped at its activity ceiling, and which reflects the sweep — the community's `crs_class` is set to `resulting_class`, and the cycle moves to the terminal `CERTIFIED`. Because the verified variant reads capped and swept points, it yields a numerically worse class than the provisional one whenever any claim was trimmed.

Two terminal failures remain. A lapse, recorded by the coordinator on a cycle still `OPEN` while the logical clock is strictly past `due_at` — else `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_PAST_DUE` — moves the cycle to the terminal `LAPSED` and resets the community's `crs_class` to 10. And retrogradation, driven entirely by the server when the community's `compliance_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED`, moves every cycle that is `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, or `UNDER_VERIFICATION` to the terminal `RETROGRADED` — identically for all three source statuses — and likewise resets `crs_class` to 10.
