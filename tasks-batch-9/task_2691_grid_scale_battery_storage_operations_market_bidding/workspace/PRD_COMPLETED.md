# Grid-Scale Battery Storage Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A grid-scale battery is one physical asset serving several buyers of the same scarce commodity. The energy sitting in its racks can be sold as arbitrage into a day-ahead market, held back as headroom for an ancillary regulation product, or frozen for a capacity programme that pays for the promise of availability rather than for delivered megawatt-hours. Each of those commitments is booked against the same state of charge, each degrades the same cells, and each is bounded by the same interconnection agreement and the same original-equipment-manufacturer warranty. Operators today reconcile these claims in spreadsheets, and the reconciliation fails exactly when it matters most: on the day a thermal alarm suspends the plant, when nobody can say which offers must be pulled, which dispatch instructions can no longer be honoured, and how many availability hours the capacity programme is about to charge for.

This service is the system of record for that reconciliation. It holds the storage asset, the reservations that carve up its state of charge, the market offers those reservations back, the dispatch instructions issued under cleared offers, the telemetry those instructions produce, the safety events telemetry can raise, the capacity obligations that measure the plant's availability, the periodic capacity tests that measure its state of health, and the warranty claims and augmentation installations that follow from a measured shortfall. Every quantity that more than one party can spend — stored energy, reserved energy, lifetime throughput, annual equivalent full cycles — is a ledger with a single writer and an explicit compensating action, so that an aborted delivery restores exactly what it consumed and nothing drifts.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, safety outranks commerce: a plant that is thermally unsafe is refused before the platform even asks whether the trade would have been profitable, and an interconnection limit is a regulatory obligation that outranks an internally-owned energy budget. Second, every outcome is reproducible: cascades process their candidate rows in a stated order, calculations pin their rounding order, and time comes from a logical clock the caller controls rather than from wall-clock drift.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Request Identity

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, the identifier of the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, the role that user is acting under. If either header is missing or empty — a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the seven roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. The one class of rejection that precedes even authentication is routing: a request whose path no endpoint serves, or whose method the matched path does not serve, is answered by the router as described in §2.2 without either header ever being inspected. A request whose role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the operation it targets is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `AssetManager`, `MarketTrader`, `MarketOperator`, `ControlRoomOperator`, `SiteController` and `SafetyOfficer`. Trading, control-room execution, safety clearance and asset stewardship are deliberately disjoint surfaces.

- `PlatformAdmin` registers storage assets, drives the logical clock, and acts for the warranty provider when adjudicating claims. It shares asset commissioning with `AssetManager`.
- `AssetManager` owns the asset's long-term health: the capacity-testing programme, warranty claims and augmentation installation. It shares commissioning with `PlatformAdmin`, state-of-charge reservation and release with `MarketTrader`, and capacity-programme enrolment with `MarketOperator`.
- `MarketTrader` builds and manages market offers: drafting, submitting, revising and withdrawing them, and reserving the state of charge that backs them.
- `MarketOperator` acts for the market operator: it posts clearing results, issues dispatch instructions, settles performance scores and assesses capacity obligations. It shares unavailability recording with `ControlRoomOperator`.
- `ControlRoomOperator` runs the fleet control room: it accepts dispatch instructions, aborts them, records unavailability, and may raise a safety event.
- `SiteController` is the plant controller of exactly one asset and the sole author of telemetry.
- `SafetyOfficer` owns the safety lifecycle: reporting events, recording suppression discharge, and closing events once emergency response has cleared the site.

All seven roles may read every collection and every single resource.

Identity binding differs by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteController`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting storage asset; ownership checks on any resource that references a storage asset compare the resource's asset reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SiteController` may append telemetry, drive dispatch execution, completion or failure, and start or complete a capacity test only for the asset whose `id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, reached through the instruction's or test's asset reference; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AssetManager`, `MarketTrader`, `MarketOperator`, `ControlRoomOperator` or `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition names an audit field — `submitted_by_user_id` on an offer or a claim, `accepted_by_user_id` on an instruction, `opened_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a safety event, `created_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a claim — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two operations enforce separation of duties on those recorded identifiers, and only these two. Adjudicating a warranty claim requires `X-User-ID` to differ from the claim's `created_by_user_id`, and closing a safety event requires `X-User-ID` to differ from the event's `opened_by_user_id`; either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). An event opened by the telemetry cascade carries the literal string `SYSTEM` as its `opened_by_user_id`, which no human identifier can equal, so such an event is always closable by any `SafetyOfficer`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn at random from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`. A fresh one is minted for every response, success or error.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's real wall clock at the moment the response is built, not from the logical clock of §2.8, so driving the logical clock never moves it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- The same envelope is used outside the 4xx band as well. A request to a path no endpoint serves is answered with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 404; a request whose method the matched path does not serve is answered with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 405; both are decided by the router before authentication or body screening is attempted, and in both cases a trailing slash on the path is ignored when matching, so `/storage-assets` and `/storage-assets/` reach the same endpoint. An unexpected server-side failure is answered with `error_id` `ERR_INTERNAL` and HTTP 500.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Filtering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of matching rows to skip before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A parameter supplied with an empty value — `?limit=` — counts as omitted and takes its default; surrounding whitespace and a leading `+` are tolerated around an otherwise integral value, while a decimal spelling such as `10.0` is not an integer and is rejected; `offset` has no upper bound; and query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Because these are shape checks they run at §2.6 step 3, ahead of the existence check on any `{id}` in the path, so a bad `limit` on a listing scoped to a non-existent parent reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a not-found code.

List responses carry `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` — the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination — and `has_more`, a boolean stating whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page — true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still strictly below `total_count` — all four inside `meta`. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` therefore returns an empty `data` array with `has_more` false while `total_count` still reports the full match count. Single-resource reads and write responses omit those four fields from `meta`.

Ordering is fixed per collection. Every top-level collection of assets, reservations, offers, instructions, safety events, capacity obligations, capacity tests and warranty claims is returned newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending. The one exception is the telemetry of a single dispatch instruction, which is returned in ascending `sampled_at` order so that a reader sees the delivery as it happened. Because timestamps are carried only to millisecond resolution two rows can share one, so the server settles ties with its own insertion sequence: the more recently inserted row comes first in the `created_at`-descending collections, and the earlier-inserted sample comes first among telemetry samples sharing a `sampled_at`. Every listing is therefore totally ordered and reproducible.

Filters are per collection and combine conjunctively; an omitted filter constrains nothing. Assets filter on `safety_state` and `market_state`. Reservations filter on `asset_id`, `purpose` and `status`. Offers filter on `asset_id`, `reservation_id`, `status`, `market_type` and `product`. Dispatch instructions filter on `asset_id`, `bid_id`, `execution_status` and `scoring_status`. Safety events filter on `asset_id`, `source_dispatch_id`, `status` and `severity`. Capacity obligations filter on `asset_id`, `program_year` and `status`. Capacity tests filter on `asset_id`, `baseline_test_id` and `status`. Warranty claims filter on `asset_id`, `capacity_test_id` and `status`. A filter over a fixed enumeration — `safety_state`, `market_state`, `purpose`, `status`, `market_type`, `product`, `execution_status`, `scoring_status` and `severity` — rejects a value outside its declared members with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas a filter naming another row by identifier — `asset_id`, `reservation_id`, `bid_id`, `source_dispatch_id`, `baseline_test_id` and `capacity_test_id` — is an opaque string that is neither validated in shape nor required to resolve, so an unknown identifier simply matches nothing and returns an empty page; `program_year` is neither, and is screened as an integer with no range bound of its own — the 2000-to-2100 range that governs the enrolment body does not apply to the filter, so `?program_year=1999` is accepted and simply matches nothing. A filter supplied with an empty value counts as omitted. Inside a list handler `limit` and `offset` are screened before any filter, and the filters after them in exactly the order this paragraph lists them for that collection, so a request carrying both a bad `limit` and a bad enum filter reports the pagination failure. Because telemetry samples are append-only and have no single-resource read of their own, listing the telemetry of one instruction is the only channel through which a client can observe the sample-level fields the platform computes.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Version Counter

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — a random version-4 UUID in its canonical lower-case hyphenated spelling; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. Both `created_at` and the `updated_at` described next are read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.8, so driving the clock neither back-dates nor forward-dates a row's bookkeeping stamps.

Storage assets, state-of-charge reservations, market offers, dispatch instructions, safety events, capacity obligations, capacity tests and warranty claims additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. Telemetry samples are the exception: a sample is an immutable measurement, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

A row's `version` advances even when the caller never named it. This matters most on the storage asset, whose `version` is the anchor of the concurrency guard in §2.5. The asset's `version` advances by one on commissioning, on every reservation creation, on every reservation release, on dispatch completion, on dispatch failure, on the creation of a safety event, on the closure of a safety event, on capacity-test completion, and on augmentation installation. Every one of those also refreshes the asset's `updated_at`. A cascade that a client never initiated is therefore visible to that client as an advanced asset version.

### 2.5. The Stale-Asset Guard on Reservation Creation

Exactly one write takes an optimistic-concurrency input: creating a state-of-charge reservation. The request body must carry `expected_asset_version`, a non-optional integer holding the `version` the client last observed on the target storage asset; it must be a non-negative JSON integer, so a negative value or a whole-valued float such as `3.0` is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at §2.6 step 3 rather than a version mismatch. If that value does not equal the asset's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

This guard exists because the reservation surface cannot be protected by state preconditions alone. A second holding on the same window is legitimate business whenever free energy remains, and a `CAPACITY` reservation may even supersede overlapping `ENERGY_ARBITRAGE` holdings through the preemption ladder, so a caller holding a stale view of the asset would never be caught by any status check — its intended write is, on its own terms, still valid. Reducing the conflict to a deterministic comparison against the asset's `version` is what makes the outcome predictable: a client that read the asset, then had its view invalidated by another reservation, a release, a dispatch completion, a capacity-test completion or a safety cascade, is told so instead of silently overwriting a pool position it never saw.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field of any kind. Reservation release, offer submission, dispatch acceptance and every other write are guarded by state preconditions and business rules only.

### 2.6. Request Screening Order

Checks run in a fixed order, and the first failing check decides the response; no later check runs, and no partial write survives a rejection. The order is deliberately banded: authentication and shape, then existence, then identity and state, then safety and regulatory compliance, then referential and temporal constraints, and finally capacity, cycle and fiscal budgets. A battery that is thermally unsafe must be refused before the platform looks at whether the trade would have been profitable.

1. Both headers present and non-empty, with `X-User-Role` naming one of the seven roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role appears in the target operation's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. The body is a JSON object, every required input field is present with its declared type, every enum field carries a declared member, every numeric field is finite and — unless that field's own rule says otherwise — non-negative, and `limit` and `offset` are integers inside their bounds. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Four conventions make this band deterministic. A field supplied as JSON `null` counts as absent, so a required field sent as `null` is rejected exactly as if it had been omitted while an optional one falls back to its default. Body fields and query parameters the operation does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. A request body that is missing, unparseable as JSON, or a JSON value that is not an object is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a numeric field must be a JSON number, so a numeric string and a boolean are both rejected, and a field declared as an integer must be a JSON integer rather than a whole-valued float. Text fields are judged blank or too short on their whitespace-trimmed value but are stored and echoed back exactly as supplied, without trimming, and a character ceiling such as 120 is measured on the untrimmed string.
4. The resource named by an `{id}` path segment exists; when a path carries more than one identifier they are resolved strictly left to right. A miss yields the not-found code of the entity that failed to resolve: `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFETY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
5. Every reference supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row. References are resolved in a fixed field order: `asset_id` first, then whichever of `reservation_id`, `bid_id`, `capacity_test_id`, `baseline_test_id` or `source_dispatch_id` the body carries, then `dispatch_id`. The failing reference decides which of `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TEST_NOT_FOUND` is returned. This applies to the creation of a reservation, an offer, a dispatch instruction, a telemetry sample, a safety event, a capacity obligation, a capacity test and a warranty claim.
6. Every reference in the body that transitively belongs to a storage asset resolves to the same asset as the body's `asset_id` — the referenced offer's asset, the referenced reservation's asset, the referenced instruction's asset, the referenced capacity test's asset, the originating instruction's asset and the baseline test's asset must all agree. A disagreement is `ERR_ASSET_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This applies to offer creation, dispatch-instruction creation, safety-event creation, capacity-test creation and warranty-claim creation.
7. When the acting role is `SiteController`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the storage asset the request targets, reached through the instruction, test or dispatch reference. Otherwise `ERR_ASSET_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. This applies to telemetry append, dispatch execution, dispatch completion, dispatch failure, capacity-test start and capacity-test completion.
8. Separation of duties on warranty adjudication and safety-event closure, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. The target resource occupies the state the requested transition departs from. The rejection carries the state code of the entity and transition concerned, drawn from `ERR_ASSET_NOT_OFFLINE`, `ERR_BID_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_BID_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_BID_NOT_CLEARED`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_EXECUTING`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_COMPLETED`, `ERR_SCORE_NOT_PROVISIONAL`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SUPPRESSING`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED` and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED`, all HTTP 409. Where one operation is guarded by two independent state axes, the execution axis is checked before the scoring axis: settling a performance score reports `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_COMPLETED` before it would report `ERR_SCORE_NOT_PROVISIONAL`.
10. `expected_asset_version` equals the asset's current `version` on reservation creation — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ASSET_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
11. The asset's `safety_state` is not `LOCKOUT`.
12. The asset's `market_state` is `AVAILABLE`.
13. The derate ceiling is respected while the asset's `safety_state` is `ELEVATED`.
14. Fire suppression has been discharged where closure demands it.
15. Emergency-response coordination has been recorded where closure demands it.
16. Interconnection active-power and apparent-power limits are respected by an incoming telemetry sample.
17. The reactive-power support obligation is respected by an incoming telemetry sample.
18. A dispatch setpoint window sits inside both the cleared delivery window and the backing reservation's window.
19. A warranty claim cites a completed, triggered and un-claimed capacity test.
20. A capacity test's baseline reference, when supplied, is valid.
21. An offer's delivery window does not conflict with another live offer's window under the ramp buffer.
22. A capacity test does not fall inside an augmentation outage window.
23. A warranty claim is filed inside its filing window.
24. Telemetry arrives in order.
25. An offer's power does not exceed the asset's rating or the product's bidirectional ceiling.
26. A capacity commitment does not exceed the asset's rating or the annual hour count.
27. A capacity-programme enrolment is not a duplicate.
28. An instruction does not instruct more power than the market cleared.
29. A clearing award is not priced below the offer's floor.
30. An offer has not exhausted its revision allowance.
31. Submitting an offer would not breach the annual equivalent-full-cycle ceiling.
32. Submitting an offer would not breach the lifetime warranty throughput ceiling.
33. A reservation request fits the asset's remaining state-of-charge headroom.
34. A dispatch instruction does not draw more energy than its reservation still holds.

Steps 11 through 34 are the business rules; the operations each one guards, the exact comparison it makes and the error identifier it raises are stated with the workflow that owns them in §3. The banding, however, is global: for any single request, a safety refusal always pre-empts a referential refusal, and a referential refusal always pre-empts a budget refusal.

### 2.7. Units, Precision and Rounding

Energy is always megawatt-hours, power always megawatts, apparent power always megavolt-amperes, reactive power always megavolt-amperes reactive, temperature always degrees Celsius, money always United States dollars, and time always ISO 8601 UTC. Nothing in this specification is expressed in kilowatt-hours or in minutes-past-midnight. A client-supplied instant is accepted in any ISO 8601 spelling the platform's date parser understands, including a `Z` or `z` suffix and an explicit numeric UTC offset, and one carrying no zone designator at all is read as UTC; it is normalised to UTC only for the platform's own comparisons and is stored and echoed back verbatim in whatever spelling the client used, whereas every instant the server itself writes — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `suspended_at`, `accrual_started_at` — is rendered in the canonical `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.sssZ` form with exactly three fractional digits. A value that is not a string, or a string the parser cannot read, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at §2.6 step 3.

Rounding is half up everywhere — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity class:

- Energy quantities in megawatt-hours are carried at 3 decimal places. This covers stored energy, reserved energy, consumed energy, delivered energy, signed state-of-charge movements, expected energy, rated energy, available energy, headroom, measured capacity, test throughput and remedy sizing.
- Money is carried at 2 decimal places: offer prices, cleared prices, penalty exposure and non-performance charges. Intermediate terms inside the offer-price computation are carried at 4 decimal places and only the final price is rounded to 2.
- Equivalent full cycles, whether the asset's year-to-date position or a single offer's cycle debit, are carried at 4 decimal places.
- Per-sample response error ratios are carried at 4 decimal places; performance scores and percentages are carried at 2.
- Hours and percentages on a capacity obligation are carried at 2 decimal places.
- Elapsed years in the degradation model are carried at 4 decimal places.
- Apparent power on a telemetry sample is carried at 3 decimal places.

Where a calculation pins the order in which rounding happens, that order is part of the specification and is stated with the calculation. Rounding a sequence of per-item values first and then aggregating them produces a different result from aggregating first and rounding once, and the two are not interchangeable. Rounding is performed in exact decimal arithmetic — a value is converted through its textual form so that binary floating-point artefacts never reach a midpoint decision — but each rounded quantity is emitted as a JSON number rather than as a string, and a JSON number does not preserve insignificant trailing zeros: a money value of 12.50 appears as `12.5` and a whole one as `12.0`.

### 2.8. The Logical Clock

Time is not read from the host. The platform keeps a logical UTC clock, and every window comparison, reservation expiry, filing deadline, outage window and availability accrual in this specification reads it. Before it is ever set, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time.

A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single field, `current_utc`, and returns no resource body: the response is HTTP 200 carrying the standard envelope of §2.2 with `data` set to an empty object, and it does not echo the instant back. Every request evaluated after that call sees the new instant. Because the clock is the only source of "now" for the rules above, a test can place a reservation window in the past, expire it, and observe the sweep, without waiting. The clock is a single process-wide instant: setting it replaces the previous value outright, may move it backwards as freely as forwards, normalises the supplied `current_utc` to UTC, and holds until the next set. It governs only the business rules enumerated above; the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` and the `created_at`/`updated_at` bookkeeping of §2.4 continue to come from the host's real clock.

### 2.9. Deterministic Cascades and Conserved Ledgers

Several operations in this system change many rows at once, and two properties make those changes reproducible.

The first is ordering. Whenever a cascade processes a set of candidate rows, the order is stated and is always over entity fields, never over identifiers. Market offers are processed by `delivery_start` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Dispatch instructions are processed by `setpoint_start` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Capacity obligations are processed by `program_year` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Expiring reservations are processed by `window_end` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Telemetry samples entering a score are processed by `sample_index` ascending. Where a step names no other key, `created_at` ascending is the tie-break, and it is the only permitted tie-break.

The second is conservation. Two asset quantities are ledgers with exact compensating actions. The asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` always equals its `initial_cumulative_throughput_mwh` plus the sum of `delivered_mwh` over its dispatch instructions whose `execution_status` is `COMPLETED`, plus the sum of `test_energy_throughput_mwh` over its capacity tests whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. The asset's `soc_mwh` always equals its `initial_soc_mwh` plus the sum of `soc_delta_mwh` over its dispatch instructions whose `execution_status` is `COMPLETED`. Failed instructions contribute zero to both sums because their `delivered_mwh` and `soc_delta_mwh` are reset to 0.0 when they fail. The same discipline governs reserved energy: `reserved_mwh_total` is recomputed from the reservations that are actually held whenever any reservation changes status and whenever the asset's stored or available energy changes, so a release always returns exactly what a hold withheld.

Every cascade, ledger movement and derived-field recompute completes inside the request that triggered it. There is no deferred settlement and no background worker; a compensating action restores its conserved quantity to its exact pre-action value in the same request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering and Commissioning a Storage Asset

A `PlatformAdmin` registers a plant before anything else can happen. The registration body describes the physical machine and its contractual envelope. `name` is the operator-facing plant name, 1 to 120 characters and not blank. `nameplate_energy_mwh` is the beginning-of-life usable energy of the installed racks and must be greater than 0. `power_rating_mw` is the continuous inverter power rating and must be greater than 0. `poi_mw_limit` is the interconnection point's active-power limit and must be greater than 0, while `poi_mva_limit` is that point's apparent-power limit and must be at least as large as `poi_mw_limit`. `reactive_capability_mvar` is the reactive-power support capability owed to the interconnection agreement and must be 0 or more. `initial_soc_mwh` is the energy actually stored at registration; it must be 0 or more and no greater than `nameplate_energy_mwh`. `degradation_cost_usd_per_mwh` is the marginal cost of a megawatt-hour of throughput and must be 0 or more. `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh` is the lifetime energy-throughput ceiling under the manufacturer warranty and must be greater than 0. `commercial_operation_date` is the plant's commercial-operation date and is the origin of the calendar-fade curve. `cycle_limit_per_year` is the contractual equivalent-full-cycle ceiling for a programme year and must be greater than 0.

Six inputs are optional and each has a default. `round_trip_efficiency_pct` is the AC round-trip efficiency, defaulting to 88.0, and must be at least 50.0 and at most 100.0; it is used both to gross up the cost of charge energy inside the offer price and to derate charge energy booked into stored energy. `soc_floor_pct` is the minimum reserve percentage of `nameplate_energy_mwh` that may never be reserved or dispatched; it defaults to 10.0, must be 0.0 or more, and must be strictly below `soc_ceiling_pct`. `soc_ceiling_pct` is the maximum usable percentage of available energy; it defaults to 95.0, must be strictly above `soc_floor_pct`, and must be at most 100.0. `initial_cumulative_throughput_mwh` carries historical lifetime throughput over at registration; it defaults to 0.0, must be 0 or more, and must be strictly below `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh`. `warranty_guaranteed_soh_pct` is the warranty's floor on expected state of health; it defaults to 80.0, must be greater than 0 and at most 100. `warranty_annual_fade_pct` is the calendar fade in percentage points per year used by the degradation model; it defaults to 2.5 and must be 0 or more.

Registration seeds the ledgers and the derived envelope. `soc_mwh` is set to `initial_soc_mwh` and thereafter moves only through dispatch completion and its compensation. `cumulative_throughput_mwh` is set to `initial_cumulative_throughput_mwh` and thereafter is credited by dispatch completion and capacity-test completion and debited by dispatch compensation. `soh_pct` starts at 100.0. `reserved_mwh_total` starts at 0.0. `rated_energy_mwh`, `available_energy_mwh`, `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are computed from the definitions below, evaluated in exactly that order so that each reads an input the previous step has already settled — `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` therefore reflects `initial_cumulative_throughput_mwh` from the moment of registration rather than starting at 0.0. `safety_state` starts at `NORMAL`, `market_state` at `OFFLINE`, `suspension_reason` and `suspended_at` at null, and `version` at 1. The response is the created asset with HTTP 201.

Five asset fields are never written directly and are worth stating precisely, because most of the rest of this document reads them.

- `rated_energy_mwh` is the nameplate energy plus the sum of `remedy_energy_add_mwh` over this asset's warranty claims whose `status` is `REMEDIED`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. An approved-but-not-yet-installed remedy contributes nothing.
- `soh_pct` is the `measured_soh_pct` of the capacity test of this asset that completed most recently. It starts at 100.0 on registration and is overwritten by each completion as it happens, so the ordering that matters is the order in which tests are *completed*, not their `scheduled_date`: a test whose `scheduled_date` is earlier than an already-completed one still sets `soh_pct` to its own `measured_soh_pct` when it completes later.
- `available_energy_mwh` is `rated_energy_mwh` multiplied by `soh_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. This is the usable energy envelope that every reservation, offer and obligation check is measured against.
- `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` is `cumulative_throughput_mwh` divided by twice `available_energy_mwh`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and it is recomputed after every change to either input. When `available_energy_mwh` is 0 the quotient is not attempted and the field is 0.0 instead.
- `free_dispatchable_mwh` is the smaller of `soc_mwh` and `available_energy_mwh` multiplied by `soc_ceiling_pct` divided by 100, minus `nameplate_energy_mwh` multiplied by `soc_floor_pct` divided by 100, minus `reserved_mwh_total`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places and floored at 0.

A newly registered asset cannot trade. Commissioning is the transition `market_state` `OFFLINE → AVAILABLE` and may be performed by a `PlatformAdmin` or an `AssetManager`. It requires the asset's `safety_state` to be `NORMAL` and requires that no safety event of this asset is in `OPEN` or `SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE`; an asset whose posture is `ELEVATED`, `ALARM` or `LOCKOUT` is refused. On success `suspension_reason` and `suspended_at` are set to null, `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` increases by one, and the response is HTTP 200 carrying the asset. Commissioning an asset whose `market_state` is not `OFFLINE` — one already `AVAILABLE`, or one `SUSPENDED` by a safety cascade — is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_OFFLINE`.

Assets can be read one at a time or as a list, and the list filters on either state dimension independently: `safety_state` accepts `NORMAL`, `ELEVATED`, `ALARM` or `LOCKOUT`, and `market_state` accepts `OFFLINE`, `AVAILABLE` or `SUSPENDED`. A single read exposes the live ledgers, the reservation headroom, the state of health and both state dimensions together.

### 3.2. Carving Up the State-of-Charge Pool

The stored energy of one plant is a finite pool that three obligation domains compete for, and a reservation is how a domain takes a position in it. A `MarketTrader` or an `AssetManager` creates one by naming the asset through `asset_id`, declaring a `purpose`, an amount in `reserved_mwh`, a window bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`, and the concurrency guard `expected_asset_version` described in §2.5. `purpose` is one of `ENERGY_ARBITRAGE`, `ANCILLARY` or `CAPACITY`, and it also fixes the reservation's standing in the pool: `priority_rank` is set to 1 for `CAPACITY`, 2 for `ANCILLARY` and 3 for `ENERGY_ARBITRAGE`, and a lower rank preempts a higher one. `reserved_mwh` must be greater than 0 and is held at 3 decimal places. `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start` — an intra-body well-formedness check made in the shape band of §2.6 step 3, so it precedes the existence resolution of `asset_id` and a request that both inverts its window and names an unknown asset reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the occupation interval is half-open: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is not. A new reservation is created with `status` `HELD`, `consumed_mwh` of 0.0 and `released_reason` of null, and it retains the `expected_asset_version` it was created against as a field of its own, echoed back on every read; the response is HTTP 201.

Whether the request can be honoured is settled by a waterfall evaluated before the row is inserted, in five steps and in this order.

The first step is a sweep of the asset's own expired positions. Every reservation of this asset whose `status` is `HELD` and whose `window_end` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `EXPIRED` with `released_reason` of `WINDOW_EXPIRED`, taken in ascending `window_end` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`. Each expiry refreshes the asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh`, so the energy those stale holdings occupied counts as headroom for the request now being evaluated. This is why a window that has merely elapsed never blocks a new booking.

The second step establishes the envelope. The floor is `nameplate_energy_mwh` multiplied by `soc_floor_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimal places — energy the plant may never reserve or dispatch. The usable ceiling is the smaller of two quantities: the asset's `soc_mwh`, and `available_energy_mwh` multiplied by `soc_ceiling_pct` divided by 100; that smaller value is rounded half up to 3 decimal places. A plant with plenty of nameplate energy but an empty pack is limited by what it actually holds.

The third step aggregates the competition. A held reservation of the same asset overlaps the request when its `window_start` is strictly earlier than the requested `window_end` and its `window_end` is strictly later than the requested `window_start` — the half-open geometry means two windows that merely touch at an instant do not overlap. The sum of `reserved_mwh` over those overlapping held reservations is rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and the headroom for this request is the usable ceiling minus the floor minus that overlap sum, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. Reservations in `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` occupy nothing.

The fourth step is the acceptance test, and it is inclusive: the request is accepted when `reserved_mwh` is less than or equal to the available headroom. A request for exactly the remaining headroom succeeds; a request for one hundredth of a megawatt-hour more does not.

The fifth step is the preemption ladder, and it runs only when the requested `purpose` is `CAPACITY`. A capacity obligation outranks arbitrage, so the candidates are the overlapping held reservations whose `purpose` is `ENERGY_ARBITRAGE`, taken in descending `priority_rank` order, then ascending `window_start`, then ascending `created_at`. Each candidate is released — `status` becomes `RELEASED` and `released_reason` becomes `CAPACITY_PREEMPTION` — and its `reserved_mwh` is added to the available headroom, rounded half up to 3 decimal places after each addition. The ladder stops at the first release that makes the headroom at least the requested amount. If the candidate list is exhausted without reaching sufficiency, every release performed during the ladder — and every offer withdrawal that release triggered — is rolled back field for field, including the `updated_at` and `version` those rows had before the ladder touched them, and the request is rejected with `ERR_SOC_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The rollback covers the ladder only: the expiry sweep of the first step is *not* unwound, so the reservations it moved to `EXPIRED` stay expired and the asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are left reflecting those expiries even though the request failed — and because a rejected creation never reaches the asset's version bump, that pool movement is visible without the asset's `version` having advanced. An `ANCILLARY` or `ENERGY_ARBITRAGE` request never preempts anything: if it does not fit, it is rejected with `ERR_SOC_INSUFFICIENT` immediately.

Preemption has a market consequence. When a reservation is released by the ladder, any offer of that reservation whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `CLEARED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` of `ASSET_SUSPENDED`, because the energy the market was promised is no longer held.

After acceptance, the derived pool figures are reconciled. `reserved_mwh_total` becomes the sum of `reserved_mwh` over every reservation of this asset whose `status` is `HELD`, regardless of window, rounded half up to 3 decimal places — note the asymmetry with the acceptance test, which sums only the overlapping subset. `free_dispatchable_mwh` becomes the usable ceiling minus the floor minus `reserved_mwh_total`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places and floored at 0. The asset's `version` increases by one. Reservation creation is also refused outright while the asset's `safety_state` is `LOCKOUT`, with `ERR_ASSET_SAFETY_LOCKOUT`, and while its `market_state` is not `AVAILABLE` — an asset never commissioned or currently safety-suspended — with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_MARKET_AVAILABLE`. Both of those checks run after the stale-version comparison of §2.6 step 10 and before the headroom test of step 33, so a stale `expected_asset_version` on a locked-out asset reports the version failure.

A reservation leaves `HELD` by four routes, and each records why. A `MarketTrader` or `AssetManager` may release it explicitly, giving `status` `RELEASED` and `released_reason` `OPERATOR_RELEASE`; the release is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD` when the reservation is not `HELD`, and also when any offer with `status` `SUBMITTED` or `CLEARED` still references it, since live market exposure cannot be un-backed by an operator's choice. A release does clear the reservation's drafts: every offer on this reservation whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` of `TRADER_WITHDRAWAL`. The asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are recomputed and its `version` increases by one. The second route is consumption by a completed delivery, `HELD → CONSUMED`, which imposes no precondition of its own beyond the reservation still being `HELD` — the instruction's asset scope was settled when the instruction was issued and is not re-tested, and the delivered energy is not compared against `reserved_mwh` at all, so an over-delivering plant may leave `consumed_mwh` above `reserved_mwh` rather than being refused; it sets `consumed_mwh` to the delivered energy and recomputes the pool figures. The third route is a cascade — a withdrawn offer or the preemption ladder — giving `released_reason` of `BID_WITHDRAWN` or `CAPACITY_PREEMPTION`. The fourth is the expiry sweep, giving `WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A consumed reservation has one further move: when a completed instruction is compensated, `CONSUMED → RELEASED` resets `consumed_mwh` to 0.0 and records `released_reason` of `DISPATCH_COMPENSATION`. `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

### 3.3. Constructing an Offer and Pricing the Degradation It Costs

A `MarketTrader` drafts an offer against a reservation that is already `HELD`. The draft names `asset_id` and `reservation_id`, chooses a `market_type` of `DAY_AHEAD` or `REAL_TIME`, a `product` of `ENERGY`, `REG_UP`, `REG_DOWN` or `SPIN`, and a `direction` of `DISCHARGE` when the plant sells energy to the grid or `CHARGE` when it is paid to absorb energy from it. The delivery interval is closed: both `delivery_start` and `delivery_end` are inside it, `delivery_end` must be strictly later than `delivery_start`, and both instants must fall on whole quarter-hours — a minute that is a multiple of 15, with zero seconds and zero fractional seconds. Both of those are intra-body well-formedness checks in the shape band of §2.6 step 3, taken in that order and ahead of the existence resolution of `asset_id` and `reservation_id`, so either failure reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the references would not have resolved. `offer_mw` is the offered power and must be greater than 0. `price_adder_usd_per_mwh` is the trader's own margin on top of the computed floor; it is optional, defaults to 0.0 and must be 0 or more. A draft is created with `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_count` of 0, `cleared_price_usd_per_mwh` and `cleared_mw` and `withdrawn_reason` and `submitted_by_user_id` all null, and returns HTTP 201. Drafting against a reservation that is no longer `HELD` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`.

Two quantities describe the offer's physical footprint. `expected_energy_mwh` is `offer_mw` multiplied by the delivery interval's duration expressed in hours as a decimal, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `cycle_debit_efc` is the already-3-decimal `expected_energy_mwh` divided by twice the asset's `available_energy_mwh`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and it is the equivalent-full-cycle draw this offer books against the annual cycle ceiling; when `available_energy_mwh` is 0 the quotient is not attempted and `cycle_debit_efc` is 0.0.

The heart of the draft is `offer_price_usd_per_mwh`, the floor beneath which selling this energy would sell wear below cost. It is built from four terms and a product weighting, with every intermediate term rounded half up to 4 decimal places and only the final price rounded half up to 2.

The first term is the cost of the energy the plant must buy to have something to sell. Charge energy is priced at 22.00 dollars per megawatt-hour at the meter, and because a round trip loses energy, that price is grossed up: the charge-cost term is 22.00 multiplied by 100 and divided by the asset's `round_trip_efficiency_pct`.

The second term is degradation, and it is scarcity-weighted. The asset's remaining annual cycle budget is `cycle_limit_per_year` minus `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd`. When at least 200 equivalent full cycles remain, the scarcity multiplier is 1.00. When fewer than 200 but at least 100 remain, it is 1.25. When fewer than 100 but at least 25 remain, it is 1.60. Below 25 remaining cycles it is 2.50 — a plant nearing its contractual cycle ceiling prices its remaining cycles at two and a half times their nominal wear. The degradation term is the asset's `degradation_cost_usd_per_mwh` multiplied by that multiplier.

The third term is a warranty adder that switches on only once the plant has spent half of its lifetime throughput allowance. The throughput ratio is `cumulative_throughput_mwh` divided by `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh`, taken without intermediate rounding. The adder is 8.00 dollars multiplied by the greater of 0 and that ratio minus 0.5, multiplied by 2 — so at exactly half of the warranty allowance the adder is zero, and it climbs linearly to 8.00 dollars as the allowance is exhausted.

The fourth element is the product weighting: 1.00 for `ENERGY`, 0.70 for `REG_UP`, 0.55 for `SPIN` and 0.35 for `REG_DOWN`.

Direction decides how the three cost terms combine, and the combination is itself an intermediate term carried at 4 decimal places. For a `DISCHARGE` offer the direction-combined base is the sum of the charge-cost, degradation and warranty terms, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. For a `CHARGE` offer the operator is paid to absorb energy, so the wear terms subtract instead: the base is the charge-cost term minus the degradation term minus the warranty term, floored at 0 *before* it is rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Either way the floor is that base multiplied by the product weighting, rounded half up to 4 decimal places once more — so the clamp at 0 on the charge side happens ahead of the weighting, not after it. `offer_price_usd_per_mwh` is then the greater of that floor plus `price_adder_usd_per_mwh` and a minimum offer price of 0.00 dollars, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The inputs to the price are exactly the asset's `round_trip_efficiency_pct`, `degradation_cost_usd_per_mwh`, `cycle_limit_per_year`, `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd`, `cumulative_throughput_mwh` and `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh`, together with this offer's own `product`, `direction` and `price_adder_usd_per_mwh`. The asset's `available_energy_mwh`, this offer's `offer_mw` and its delivery interval do not reach the price at all: they feed `expected_energy_mwh` and `cycle_debit_efc` only.

Offered power is bounded twice, and both bounds are checked at drafting time and again on revision — and only there: the automatic re-pricing of a `DRAFT` offer described below never re-tests them, and neither does submission, so a draft may keep and even submit an `offer_mw` that only a revision would now reject. `offer_mw` must not exceed the smaller of the asset's `power_rating_mw` and its `poi_mw_limit`. For a `REG_UP`, `REG_DOWN` or `SPIN` product `offer_mw` must additionally not exceed half of `power_rating_mw`, because a regulation or reserve product requires the plant to be able to move in both directions from its operating point. Either breach is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_MW_EXCEEDS_RATING` (HTTP 422).

A draft's price is not frozen. While an offer's `status` is `DRAFT`, `offer_price_usd_per_mwh` tracks the asset: any change to `available_energy_mwh`, `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` or `cumulative_throughput_mwh` re-prices it. Exactly four operations move those inputs and therefore re-price every `DRAFT` offer of the asset: capacity-test completion (the cascade of §3.9), dispatch completion, the compensating reversal of a completed instruction, and augmentation installation. Each re-pricing rewrites all three of `expected_energy_mwh`, `cycle_debit_efc` and `offer_price_usd_per_mwh` on the affected drafts, takes them in ascending `delivery_start` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`, and refreshes each draft's own `updated_at` and `version`. An offer in any other status is never re-priced.

### 3.4. Submission, Clearing, the Revision Loop and Withdrawal

Submission is where the asset-level gates bite. A `MarketTrader` moves an offer `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the transition requires all of the following. The asset's `market_state` must be `AVAILABLE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_MARKET_AVAILABLE`. The asset's `safety_state` must not be `LOCKOUT`, or it is refused with `ERR_ASSET_SAFETY_LOCKOUT`. While the asset's `safety_state` is `ELEVATED` the plant is derated and `offer_mw` must not exceed 50.0 percent of `power_rating_mw`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_ASSET_DERATED` (HTTP 409) — an open warning-severity thermal event does not suspend the market, it shrinks the sellable power. The backing reservation must still be `HELD` and must belong to the same asset. The reservation must cover the delivery interval, meaning its `window_start` is at or before `delivery_start` and its `window_end` is strictly later than `delivery_end`. Submitting an offer whose `status` is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_DRAFT`.

Two budgets are checked at submission and nowhere else. The cycle budget: the asset's `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` plus this offer's cycle debit — `expected_energy_mwh` divided by twice `available_energy_mwh`, the sum rounded half up to 4 decimal places — must be at most `cycle_limit_per_year`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The warranty budget: the asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` plus this offer's `expected_energy_mwh`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, must be at most `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_THROUGHPUT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409), because trading past the guaranteed throughput would void the capacity-maintenance obligation.

Window exclusivity is the remaining submission gate, and despite appearing after them here it is evaluated *ahead* of the two budget ceilings of the previous paragraph, in keeping with the banding of §2.6 — a submission that both conflicts on window and would breach the cycle or throughput budget reports `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the reservation-coverage check of the paragraph before comes ahead of both. No other offer of the same asset and the same `market_type` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `CLEARED` may occupy the requested delivery window, where occupancy is the closed interval running from 15 minutes before `delivery_start` to 15 minutes after `delivery_end`. Because the buffered interval is closed on both sides, two offers that merely touch at an endpoint do conflict, and so do two offers separated by a 15-minute gap; a conflict is refused with `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Fifteen minutes is the plant's ramp allowance between two market commitments.

On success `submitted_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` for audit — it is never an ownership gate — and `offer_price_usd_per_mwh` is frozen at its current value and stops tracking the asset's recomputes. Once submitted, the offer's window is occupied for conflict purposes with the 15-minute buffer on both sides.

The market's answer arrives from a `MarketOperator` as a clearing result on a `SUBMITTED` offer, and the single field `outcome` selects one of two divergent branches. The `CLEARED` branch requires both `cleared_price_usd_per_mwh` and `cleared_mw` in the body and produces `SUBMITTED → CLEARED`. The supplied `cleared_mw` must be greater than 0 and at most `offer_mw`; an award larger than the offer is a malformed body rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a business rejection, but because that comparison needs the stored offer it is made only after the path identifier has resolved and still before the status check — so an oversized `cleared_mw` against an unknown offer reports `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, while an oversized `cleared_mw` against an offer that is not `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The supplied `cleared_price_usd_per_mwh` must be at least the offer's `offer_price_usd_per_mwh`: the offer price *is* the floor, so an award at exactly the offer price is accepted and one a cent below it is refused with `ERR_CLEARING_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). On success both values are stored on the offer and the backing reservation stays `HELD`, so the cleared energy remains withheld until dispatch. Only this branch permits a dispatch instruction to be created. The `UNCLEARED` branch requires that neither `cleared_price_usd_per_mwh` nor `cleared_mw` appears in the body and produces `SUBMITTED → UNCLEARED`; both fields stay null, and the backing reservation also stays `HELD` so that the trader can revise and re-offer inside the same window. No dispatch may be created under an uncleared offer, but the revision loop is now open. Posting a clearing result against an offer whose `status` is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and the operation also requires the asset's `market_state` to be `AVAILABLE`.

Revision closes the negotiation loop `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → UNCLEARED → DRAFT`. A `MarketTrader` revises an offer that is `UNCLEARED`, and a revision request against an offer in any other status is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The backing reservation must still be `HELD`. At least one of `offer_mw` or `price_adder_usd_per_mwh` must be present in the body, and a revision that supplies neither is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The loop is bounded: `revision_count` must be strictly below 3, so the fourth revision of the same offer is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409) and the offer must be withdrawn instead. On success `revision_count` increases by one, whichever of `offer_mw` and `price_adder_usd_per_mwh` was supplied replaces the stored value, `submitted_by_user_id` returns to null, and `expected_energy_mwh`, `cycle_debit_efc` and `offer_price_usd_per_mwh` are recomputed against the asset's *current* `available_energy_mwh`, `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` and `cumulative_throughput_mwh` — the cycle-scarcity multiplier that applies now may sit in a different tier from the one that applied at first submission. Revision is also subject to the lockout and market-availability gates and to the offered-power bounds of §3.3, which are applied to the *effective* power — the supplied `offer_mw`, or the stored one when the body revises only the price adder — and are evaluated before the revision-allowance check, so an over-rated revision of an offer that has already exhausted its allowance reports `ERR_OFFER_MW_EXCEEDS_RATING`.

Withdrawal takes an offer out of the market, and it is the only route to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` status by trader action. A `MarketTrader` may withdraw an offer whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CLEARED`; an offer that is already `WITHDRAWN`, or one sitting in `UNCLEARED`, cannot be withdrawn and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_DRAFT`. All three withdrawable branches set `withdrawn_reason` to `TRADER_WITHDRAWAL` and release the offer's backing reservation: a reservation in `HELD` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` of `BID_WITHDRAWN`, after which the asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are recomputed — the compensating half of the pool's conservation rule, returning exactly the energy the offer had withheld. When the backing reservation is already out of `HELD` — released by an earlier withdrawal, consumed, expired or preempted — nothing is released, neither pool figure is recomputed and the asset's `updated_at` and `version` do not move at all; only the offer row changes. The reservation's `consumed_mwh` remains 0.0, because as the next paragraph shows no offer with a completed delivery underneath it is withdrawable at all. Withdrawing from `SUBMITTED` additionally requires the asset's `safety_state` not to be `LOCKOUT`.

Withdrawing a `CLEARED` offer is the branch with teeth. It is permitted only when no dispatch instruction of this offer has an `execution_status` of `ACCEPTED`, `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`; an attempt to withdraw an offer whose delivery has already been taken up by the control room is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_PENDING`. When it is permitted, every dispatch instruction of this offer whose `execution_status` is `PENDING` moves to `FAILED` with `failure_reason` of `DELIVERY_VOIDED`, because the delivery they were issued against no longer exists.

### 3.5. Dispatch Execution and the Telemetry Stream

A dispatch instruction is the physical order that turns a cleared offer into delivered energy. A `MarketOperator` issues one by naming `bid_id` and `asset_id` and supplying `instructed_mw`, `setpoint_start` and `setpoint_end`. The referenced offer must be `CLEARED`, or the creation is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_CLEARED`, and the instruction's `asset_id` must equal that offer's asset, which the scope agreement of §2.6 step 6 enforces. `instructed_mw` must be greater than 0 and must not exceed the offer's `cleared_mw`; instructing more power than the market actually cleared is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_EXCEEDS_CLEARED` (HTTP 422). `setpoint_end` must be strictly later than `setpoint_start` — an intra-body well-formedness check made in the shape band of §2.6 step 3, so it precedes the existence resolution of `asset_id` and `bid_id` and a request that both inverts its setpoint window and names an unknown offer reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the setpoint interval is closed at both ends.

The setpoint interval must sit inside two other intervals at once. It must fall within the offer's closed delivery interval, and it must fall within the backing reservation's window, meaning the reservation's `window_start` is at or before `setpoint_start` and `setpoint_end` is at or before the reservation's `window_end`. A setpoint window that escapes either is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

The instruction's own energy footprint is `expected_energy_mwh`: `instructed_mw` multiplied by the setpoint interval's duration in hours, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. That energy is charged against what the reservation still holds. Summing this instruction's `expected_energy_mwh` with the `expected_energy_mwh` of every sibling instruction on the same reservation whose `execution_status` is `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED`, `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`, and rounding the total half up to 3 decimal places, that total must be at most the reservation's `reserved_mwh`; otherwise the creation is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_EXCEEDS_RESERVATION` (HTTP 422). Failed siblings release their claim and are not counted.

A new instruction inherits `direction` from the offer it executes — `DISCHARGE` or `CHARGE` — and starts with `execution_status` of `PENDING`, `scoring_status` of `NOT_SCORED`, `delivered_mwh` of 0.0, `soc_delta_mwh` of 0.0, `telemetry_sample_count` of 0, `non_performance_charge_usd` of 0.0, and `accuracy_score`, `deadband_pct`, `composite_score`, `failure_reason` and `accepted_by_user_id` all null. Creation returns HTTP 201, and it is refused while the asset is locked out with `ERR_ASSET_SAFETY_LOCKOUT` and while its `market_state` is not `AVAILABLE` with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_MARKET_AVAILABLE`.

Every instruction carries two independent state axes: an execution axis in `execution_status` and a scoring axis in `scoring_status`. They move independently, and one axis is never inferred from the other.

Acceptance is the control room's act. A `ControlRoomOperator` moves an instruction `PENDING → ACCEPTED`, which requires the offer to still be `CLEARED`, the asset's `safety_state` not to be `LOCKOUT`, and the asset's `market_state` to be `AVAILABLE`; while the asset's `safety_state` is `ELEVATED`, `instructed_mw` must additionally not exceed 50.0 percent of `power_rating_mw`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_ASSET_DERATED`. On success `accepted_by_user_id` records the acting identifier for audit only. Accepting an instruction whose `execution_status` is not `PENDING` — including a second acceptance of the same instruction — is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_PENDING`, and the instruction's own execution axis is examined before the parent offer's status, so an instruction that is no longer `PENDING` under an offer that is no longer `CLEARED` reports `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_PENDING` rather than `ERR_BID_NOT_CLEARED`.

Execution belongs to the plant. The `SiteController` whose `X-User-ID` equals the asset's `id` moves the instruction `ACCEPTED → EXECUTING`. The asset's `safety_state` must be either `NORMAL` or `ELEVATED` — a derated plant may still deliver, a locked-out or alarmed one may not — and both refusals carry the same identifier, `ERR_ASSET_SAFETY_LOCKOUT` (HTTP 409), so an `ALARM` posture is reported as a lockout even though it is not one. The backing reservation must still be `HELD`, and `instructed_mw` multiplied by the setpoint duration in hours, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, must be at most the reservation's `reserved_mwh` — this instruction's own energy alone, with no sibling instruction summed in as there is at creation. Execution on an instruction whose `execution_status` is not `ACCEPTED` is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and that check precedes the reservation's own status check, so a non-`ACCEPTED` instruction whose reservation has already left `HELD` reports `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_ACCEPTED` rather than `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`. Once the instruction is executing, telemetry ingestion for it becomes permitted.

Telemetry is the plant's own measurement stream, and only the owning `SiteController` may append to it. A sample names its instruction through `dispatch_id` and carries `sample_index`, `sampled_at`, `measured_mw`, `measured_mvar`, `soc_mwh_reading` and `max_cell_temp_c`. `sample_index` is a 1-based monotonic index inside the instruction: it must be at least 1 — a shape constraint screened at §2.6 step 3 and reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and strictly greater than the current maximum index for that instruction, which is part of the telemetry-ordering rule of §2.6 step 24 and is reported as `ERR_TELEMETRY_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 409) instead. `sampled_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the measurement. `measured_mw` is measured active power at the point of interconnection and must be 0 or more. `measured_mvar` is measured reactive power at that point and is *signed*: a negative value, meaning the plant is absorbing reactive power, is a well-formed request, and its magnitude is policed by the reactive-capability rule below rather than by the body shape check. `soc_mwh_reading` is the site controller's own state-of-charge reading, recorded for reconciliation and never authoritative over the asset's `soc_mwh`. `max_cell_temp_c` is the hottest cell temperature the battery management system reports. A sample may be appended only while the instruction's `execution_status` is `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`; appending to an instruction in any other execution state is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_EXECUTING`. Appending returns HTTP 201.

Telemetry must arrive in order. A sample's `sampled_at` must be strictly later than the `sampled_at` of the newest existing sample of the same instruction, and must not be earlier than the instruction's `setpoint_start`; either violation, and a `sample_index` that fails its monotonicity, is refused with `ERR_TELEMETRY_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 409). The three comparisons are made in a fixed sequence — `sampled_at` against `setpoint_start` first, then `sampled_at` against the newest existing sample, then `sample_index` against the current maximum — and only the first applies to the instruction's very first sample, the other two having nothing to compare against. A sample whose `sampled_at` is later than the instruction's `setpoint_end` is accepted, but it is *late-arriving*, and §3.6 explains what that costs.

Every accepted sample is screened against the interconnection agreement before it is stored. `apparent_mva` is the square root of the sum of the squares of `measured_mw` and `measured_mvar`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `measured_mw` must be at most the asset's `poi_mw_limit` and `apparent_mva` must be at most its `poi_mva_limit`; both bounds are inclusive, so a measurement exactly at a limit is compliant and only a strictly greater one is refused, with `ERR_POI_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The magnitude of `measured_mvar`, ignoring its sign, must be at most the asset's `reactive_capability_mvar`, or the sample is refused with `ERR_REACTIVE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The active-power and apparent-power check runs before the reactive-capability check, so a sample that breaches both reports the interconnection limit.

Each stored sample also carries its own response arithmetic. `deviation_mw` is the absolute difference between `measured_mw` and the instruction's `instructed_mw`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `error_ratio` is `deviation_mw` divided by `instructed_mw`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and it is the per-sample term the performance score averages. `within_deadband` is true when `error_ratio` is at most 0.02, inclusive at the boundary. `thermal_escalation` bands the cell temperature: it is `RUNAWAY` when `max_cell_temp_c` is at or above 75.0 degrees Celsius, `WARNING` when it is at or above 65.0 degrees Celsius but below the runaway threshold, and `NONE` below 65.0. Appending a sample also increases the instruction's `telemetry_sample_count` by one, and that counter includes late-arriving samples.

A thermal band above `NONE` does not merely record a number. On a `WARNING` band, the platform auto-creates a safety event of `event_type` `THERMAL_ANOMALY` and `severity` `WARNING`. On a `RUNAWAY` band, it auto-creates one of `event_type` `THERMAL_RUNAWAY` and `severity` `CRITICAL`, which imposes a lockout and fires the whole cascade of §3.7 from its first link. Exactly one event is created per sample, and the runaway band takes precedence over the warning band. Either way the event's `source` is `TELEMETRY_AUTO`, its `opened_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM`, its `source_dispatch_id` is the sample's `dispatch_id`, its `source_sample_index` is the sample's `sample_index` and its `detected_max_cell_temp_c` is the sample's `max_cell_temp_c`; every remaining field takes the same initial value a manually reported event takes in §3.7.

Completion closes out the delivery. The owning `SiteController` moves the instruction `EXECUTING → COMPLETED`, which requires the asset's `safety_state` not to be `LOCKOUT`; completion of an instruction whose `execution_status` is not `EXECUTING` is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_EXECUTING`. Delivered energy is aggregated from on-time telemetry only: taking the samples of this instruction whose `sampled_at` is at or before `setpoint_end`, `delivered_mwh` is the sum of each sample's `measured_mw` multiplied by a fixed sample width of one quarter of an hour, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `soc_delta_mwh` is the signed movement this delivery applies to the asset's stored energy: for a `DISCHARGE` instruction it is the negative of `delivered_mwh`; for a `CHARGE` instruction it is `delivered_mwh` multiplied by the asset's `round_trip_efficiency_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, because only the energy that survives the round trip lands in the pack. The signed value is stored on the instruction precisely so that a later compensation can reverse it exactly.

The ledgers then move, in this order. The asset's `soc_mwh` becomes its previous value plus `soc_delta_mwh`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. The asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` becomes its previous value plus `delivered_mwh`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` is recomputed after the throughput credit. The backing reservation moves `HELD → CONSUMED` with `consumed_mwh` set to `delivered_mwh`, and the asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are recomputed. The scoring axis moves `NOT_SCORED → PROVISIONAL` and the provisional variant of the performance score is applied. The asset's `version` increases by one.

### 3.6. Performance Scoring, Settlement and the Compensating Reversal

Ancillary markets pay for accuracy, not merely for energy, and this platform scores every completed instruction twice — once immediately on the plant's own on-time data, and once again when the market settles, on everything that eventually arrived. The two passes share one algorithm and differ only in which samples enter it, which is exactly why identical telemetry yields two different composite scores for the same instruction.

The algorithm takes the instruction's samples in ascending `sample_index` order after the variant's filter has been applied. When the filtered set is empty, `accuracy_score`, `deadband_pct` and `composite_score` are all 0.00 — a delivery with no admissible telemetry scores zero rather than being treated as perfect. Otherwise the rounding order is pinned and is part of the specification: each sample's `error_ratio` — its stored `deviation_mw`, which is itself the absolute difference between its `measured_mw` and the instruction's `instructed_mw` already rounded half up to 3 decimal places, divided by `instructed_mw` — is rounded half up to 4 decimal places *first*; the mean of those already-rounded ratios is then taken and is *not* rounded; `accuracy_score` is the greater of 0 and one minus that mean, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Averaging the unrounded ratios and rounding once produces a different score and is not the specified behaviour. `deadband_pct` is the count of samples whose `within_deadband` is true divided by the total number of scored samples, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `composite_score` weights the two: seven tenths of `accuracy_score` plus three tenths of `deadband_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The provisional pass runs inside completion and admits only samples whose `sampled_at` is at or before `setpoint_end`. Its `non_performance_charge_usd` is forced to 0.0 regardless of how poor the composite score is — the provisional pass never charges. The scoring axis reads `PROVISIONAL` afterwards.

The settled pass is a `MarketOperator`'s act on an instruction whose scoring axis is `PROVISIONAL`, and it produces `PROVISIONAL → SETTLED`. It requires the execution axis to be `COMPLETED`, and the two guards are ordered: an instruction that is not `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_COMPLETED` before the scoring axis is examined at all, while an instruction that is `COMPLETED` but whose scoring axis is not `PROVISIONAL` — one already settled, for instance — is refused with `ERR_SCORE_NOT_PROVISIONAL`. The settled pass admits *every* sample of the instruction, including the late-arriving ones whose `sampled_at` is after `setpoint_end`; those drag both the accuracy average and the deadband percentage down, because a sample recorded after the setpoint ended is measured against a setpoint the plant was no longer holding. `accuracy_score`, `deadband_pct` and `composite_score` are recomputed from the wider set, and the charge is then assessed: `non_performance_charge_usd` is the greater of 0 and 75.00 minus `composite_score`, multiplied by `instructed_mw`, multiplied by 4.00 dollars, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. A composite score at or above the performance floor of 75.00 therefore costs nothing, and every point below it costs four dollars per instructed megawatt. The execution axis is untouched by settlement.

Failure is the escape hatch, and the same action behaves as two divergent branches depending on where the execution axis stands. The body itself may be omitted entirely, and it optionally carries `failure_reason`, a string of 1 to 120 characters that must not be blank. A supplied `failure_reason` is honoured only on the no-delivery branch, where an absent one records `OPERATOR_FAILURE`; the compensation branch overwrites the reason with `DELIVERY_VOIDED` whatever the body supplied. Either a `ControlRoomOperator` or the owning `SiteController` may invoke it.

From `EXECUTING → FAILED` — the no-delivery branch — the instruction must have a `delivered_mwh` of exactly 0.0 and a scoring axis of `NOT_SCORED`. The backing reservation moves `HELD → RELEASED` with `released_reason` of `DISPATCH_COMPENSATION`, and no energy ledger moves at all, because nothing had been credited: `soc_mwh`, `cumulative_throughput_mwh` and `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` are untouched and no `DRAFT` offer is re-priced. The release still reconciles the pool as §2.9 requires — `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are recomputed — and the asset's `version` increases by one, as it does on every dispatch failure.

From `COMPLETED → FAILED` — the compensation branch — the scoring axis must be `PROVISIONAL`; a settled instruction can no longer be compensated, and the attempt is refused on the scoring guard. This branch unwinds every effect completion applied, in this order: the asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` becomes its current value minus the instruction's `delivered_mwh`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places; the asset's `soc_mwh` becomes its current value minus the instruction's stored `soc_delta_mwh`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and because the stored value is the exact signed delta the completion applied, restoration is exact for both directions; `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` is recomputed after the debit. The backing reservation moves `CONSUMED → RELEASED` with `consumed_mwh` reset to 0.0 and `released_reason` of `DISPATCH_COMPENSATION`. The instruction's own `delivered_mwh` and `soc_delta_mwh` are reset to 0.0, its `failure_reason` becomes `DELIVERY_VOIDED`, its scoring axis moves `PROVISIONAL → NOT_SCORED`, its `accuracy_score`, `deadband_pct` and `composite_score` are set to null, and its `non_performance_charge_usd` returns to 0.0. The asset's `version` increases by one.

Two further routes into `FAILED` are driven by the system rather than by an operator, and both are described where they originate. An instruction whose `execution_status` is `PENDING` fails when the asset enters `SUSPENDED`, with `failure_reason` of `ASSET_SUSPENDED`, or when its parent offer moves `CLEARED → WITHDRAWN`, with `failure_reason` of `DELIVERY_VOIDED`. An instruction whose `execution_status` is `ACCEPTED` fails with `failure_reason` of `ASSET_SUSPENDED` when the asset enters `SUSPENDED`. `FAILED` is terminal on the execution axis and `SETTLED` is terminal on the scoring axis.

### 3.7. Safety Posture, Suppression, and the Suspension Cascade

An asset's `safety_state` is not written by anyone. It is *derived*: it is the most severe `imposed_safety_state` among that asset's safety events whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE`, where severity ascends `NORMAL`, `ELEVATED`, `ALARM`, `LOCKOUT`. With no such events, the posture is `NORMAL`. This is what makes the safety axis behave like a set rather than a sequence: closing one event does not necessarily calm the plant, because a sibling event may still be imposing a higher posture.

A `SafetyOfficer` or a `ControlRoomOperator` reports an event by naming `asset_id` and supplying `event_type`, `severity` and `detected_max_cell_temp_c`, the hottest cell temperature in degrees Celsius at detection. `event_type` is one of `THERMAL_ANOMALY`, `THERMAL_RUNAWAY`, `OFFGAS_DETECTED` or `SMOKE_DETECTED`; `severity` is one of `ADVISORY`, `WARNING` or `CRITICAL`. The event's `imposed_safety_state` follows from the pair: `THERMAL_RUNAWAY` imposes `LOCKOUT` whatever its severity, and for every other event type a `CRITICAL` severity imposes `ALARM`, a `WARNING` imposes `ELEVATED`, and an `ADVISORY` imposes `NONE` and therefore contributes nothing to the asset's posture. A manually reported event is created with `source` of `MANUAL`, `status` of `OPEN`, `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting identifier, `suppression_discharged` false, `suppression_agent_kg` null, `emergency_response_ack_at` null, `emergency_ack_agencies` as an empty array, `responder_cleared` false, `closed_by_user_id` null, and `source_dispatch_id` and `source_sample_index` null. Only the telemetry cascade of §3.5 sets those last two, and only it uses `source` of `TELEMETRY_AUTO`. Reporting returns HTTP 201.

Creation then fires a six-link cascade, and each link fires because the previous one did.

Link one recomputes the asset's `safety_state` from the definition above. On the `NORMAL → ELEVATED` transition — a `WARNING`-severity non-runaway event, or a telemetry sample crossing 65.0 degrees Celsius — `market_state` is untouched: an elevated posture derates the plant, it does not suspend it. The transitions `NORMAL → ALARM` (a `CRITICAL` non-runaway event), `NORMAL → LOCKOUT` (a `THERMAL_RUNAWAY` event of any severity, or a telemetry sample crossing 75.0 degrees Celsius), `ELEVATED → LOCKOUT` and `ALARM → LOCKOUT` all continue into link two. On `ALARM → LOCKOUT` the asset is already suspended, so only `suspension_reason` changes, to `SAFETY_LOCKOUT`, and `market_state` stays `SUSPENDED`.

Link two fires because the recomputed posture is `ALARM` or `LOCKOUT`: the asset's `market_state` moves `AVAILABLE → SUSPENDED`, `suspension_reason` becomes `SAFETY_ALARM` when the posture is `ALARM` and `SAFETY_LOCKOUT` when it is `LOCKOUT`, and `suspended_at` records the current logical instant, written by the server as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits — the same canonical spelling `created_at` carries — so a clock standing at `2026-08-10T14:20:00Z` is recorded as `2026-08-10T14:20:00.000Z` rather than echoed back in whatever spelling the clock call used. Link two fires only from `AVAILABLE`. On an asset whose `market_state` is `OFFLINE` — one never commissioned — an `ALARM` or `LOCKOUT` posture is recorded on the safety axis and nothing else moves: `market_state` stays `OFFLINE`, `suspension_reason` and `suspended_at` stay null, and links three to six do not run, though the event's creation still advances the asset's `version` by one.

Link three fires because `market_state` is now `SUSPENDED`: every offer of this asset whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CLEARED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_reason` of `ASSET_SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `delivery_start` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`.

Link four fires because those offers became `WITHDRAWN`: each withdrawn offer's backing reservation, if its `status` is `HELD`, moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` of `BID_WITHDRAWN`, and the asset's `reserved_mwh_total` and `free_dispatchable_mwh` are recomputed.

Link five fires because those offers became `WITHDRAWN`: every dispatch instruction of this asset whose `execution_status` is `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED` or `EXECUTING` moves to `FAILED` with `failure_reason` of `ASSET_SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `setpoint_start` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`.

Link six fires because `market_state` is now `SUSPENDED`: every capacity obligation of this asset whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SHORTFALL` or `PENALIZED` and whose `program_year` equals the calendar year of the current logical instant has its `accrual_started_at` set to that instant — in the same three-fractional-digit form as `suspended_at` — opening an unavailability accrual. The asset's `version` increases by one.

While the asset's `safety_state` is `LOCKOUT`, every write outside the safety-event surface is refused with `ERR_ASSET_SAFETY_LOCKOUT` (HTTP 409). Concretely, the lockout gate guards reservation creation, offer drafting, offer submission, offer revision, the withdrawal of a `SUBMITTED` offer, dispatch-instruction creation, dispatch acceptance, dispatch execution, dispatch completion, capacity-test scheduling, capacity-test start, capacity-test completion and augmentation installation — and nothing else, so recording a dispatch failure, settling a performance score, enrolling or assessing a capacity obligation, recording unavailability, and drafting, submitting or adjudicating a warranty claim all remain available on a locked-out plant. The market-availability gate — `ERR_ASSET_NOT_MARKET_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) — guards reservation creation, offer drafting, offer submission, offer revision, clearing-result posting, dispatch-instruction creation and dispatch acceptance. The derate gate — `ERR_ASSET_DERATED` (HTTP 409) — guards exactly two operations, offer submission and dispatch acceptance, and in both it compares the request's power (`offer_mw` or `instructed_mw`) against 50.0 percent of `power_rating_mw`.

Fire suppression is recorded separately from closure. A `SafetyOfficer` discharges suppression on an event whose `status` is `OPEN`, supplying `suppression_agent_kg`, the mass of agent discharged in kilograms, which is required and must be 0 or more. The event moves `OPEN → SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE`, `suppression_discharged` becomes true and `suppression_agent_kg` records the supplied mass. The transition requires `suppression_discharged` to be false beforehand, and discharging on an event whose `status` is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN`. The asset's `safety_state` is deliberately left unchanged: a discharged event is still a non-closed event, and it still imposes its posture. The asset row is not written at all, so neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moves.

Closure is the second officer's act and has two divergent branches on the same action. From `SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE → CLOSED` — the suppressed branch — closure requires that `X-User-ID` differ from `opened_by_user_id` and that `emergency_response_ack_at` be supplied when the event's `imposed_safety_state` is `LOCKOUT`. From `OPEN → CLOSED` — the unsuppressed branch — closure requires that the event's `imposed_safety_state` not be `LOCKOUT`, because a lockout-imposing event may only be closed after suppression, and again that `X-User-ID` differ from `opened_by_user_id`. No suppression is required for an `ADVISORY`, `WARNING` or non-runaway `CRITICAL` event. Attempting to close a lockout-imposing event whose `suppression_discharged` is still false is refused with `ERR_SUPPRESSION_NOT_DISCHARGED` (HTTP 409). Closing an event whose `status` is neither `OPEN` nor `SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SUPPRESSING`.

Emergency-response coordination is a closure input. The body may carry `emergency_response_ack_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant the fire department acknowledged the incident; `emergency_ack_agencies`, an array naming the coordinating agencies such as the fire service, the utility control centre and the county emergency management agency, each entry 1 to 120 characters and not blank; and `responder_cleared`, a boolean that is true once the incident commander has released the site back to the operator. For an event whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` all three are mandatory: `emergency_response_ack_at` must be supplied, `responder_cleared` must be true, and `emergency_ack_agencies` must name at least one agency, or the closure is refused with `ERR_EMERGENCY_ACK_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Emergency response must be on the record before the plant returns to market.

A successful closure sets `status` to `CLOSED`, records `closed_by_user_id` as the acting identifier, stores whichever of `emergency_response_ack_at`, `emergency_ack_agencies` and `responder_cleared` the body actually supplied — an omitted one keeps the value the event already carried, so it stays null, the empty array or false respectively — and recomputes the asset's `safety_state` over the remaining non-closed events. The recompute has five possible landings. `LOCKOUT → ALARM` occurs when the runaway event closes while a `CRITICAL` non-runaway event is still open: `suspension_reason` becomes `SAFETY_ALARM` and `market_state` stays `SUSPENDED`. `ALARM → ELEVATED` occurs when the alarm-imposing event closes while a `WARNING` event is still open: because the posture has left the alarmed band, market participation resumes and `suspension_reason` returns to null, leaving the asset in a combination — an `ELEVATED` safety axis with an `AVAILABLE` market axis — that no single event could have produced. `LOCKOUT → NORMAL`, `ALARM → NORMAL` and `ELEVATED → NORMAL` all occur when no event of the asset remains in `OPEN` or `SUPPRESSION_ACTIVE`; the first two resume market participation, while the third changes nothing on the market axis because an elevated posture never suspended it.

Whenever the recomputed posture is neither `ALARM` nor `LOCKOUT` and the asset was suspended, `market_state` moves `SUSPENDED → AVAILABLE`, `suspension_reason` and `suspended_at` return to null, and every capacity obligation of this asset whose `accrual_started_at` is not null closes its accrual: `unavailable_hours` becomes its previous value plus the number of hours between `accrual_started_at` and the current logical instant, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and `accrual_started_at` returns to null. Nothing else comes back. No withdrawn offer and no released reservation is restored — withdrawal and release are irreversible, and a trader who wants that position back must reserve and offer again. The asset's `version` increases by one on every successful closure, whichever of the five landings the recompute reaches and even when it leaves both state axes exactly where they stood.

### 3.8. Capacity Commitments, Availability Accrual and Two Ways of Assessing Them

A capacity programme pays for a promise: that the plant will be there, able to discharge a committed power for a committed duration, for a committed number of hours a year. An `AssetManager` or a `MarketOperator` enrols the asset by naming `asset_id`, the `program_name` — the programme the asset is enrolled in, such as an ISO capacity auction or a resource-adequacy programme, 1 to 120 characters and not blank — and the `program_year`, an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive. `committed_mw` is the unforced capacity committed, greater than 0 and at most the asset's `power_rating_mw`. `availability_hours_required` is the annual availability the programme demands, greater than 0 and at most 8760. `penalty_rate_usd_per_mw_hour` is the programme's penalty rate per committed megawatt per shortfall hour and must be 0 or more. `obligation_duration_hours` is optional, defaults to 4.0, must be greater than 0, and is the number of hours of continuous discharge at `committed_mw` the programme requires the plant to be able to sustain.

Two enrolment rules apply. A `committed_mw` above the asset's `power_rating_mw`, or an `availability_hours_required` above 8760, is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDS_RATING` (HTTP 422). At most one obligation may exist for a given combination of `asset_id`, `program_name` and `program_year`, whatever its status — the `program_name` half of that comparison is exact and case-sensitive and is made on the untrimmed string as supplied, so two spellings that differ only in case or in surrounding whitespace are two different programmes — and a second enrolment for the same triple is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION` (HTTP 409) rather than merged into the first. A new obligation is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `unavailable_hours` 0.0, `accrual_started_at` null, `measured_available_hours` 0.0, `availability_pct` null, `shortfall_hours` 0.0, `penalty_exposure_usd` 0.0, `assessment_mode` null and `derated_committed_mw` null, and returns HTTP 201.

Unavailability reaches an obligation two ways. The safety cascade of §3.7 opens an accrual by stamping `accrual_started_at`, and the resumption that follows folds the elapsed hours into `unavailable_hours`. Separately, a `ControlRoomOperator` or a `MarketOperator` may record hours directly — a forced outage, a reconciliation of a safety suspension, a telemetry gap — by supplying `unavailable_hours_delta`, which is required and must be 0 or more, and, optionally, a free-text `reason` of 1 to 500 characters that must not be blank and that is screened for shape but persisted nowhere. That operation adds the delta to `unavailable_hours`, rounding the result half up to 2 decimal places, and does nothing else: `availability_pct`, `shortfall_hours`, `penalty_exposure_usd` and `status` all keep the values the last assessment left them with until an assessment is actually run.

Assessment is a `MarketOperator`'s act and takes a single field, `mode`, which selects one of two variants over the very same stored data. The programme year begins at midnight UTC on 1 January of `program_year`. Under the `PRELIMINARY` variant — an in-year interim assessment — the assessed period runs from that instant to the current logical instant, and the requirement is pro-rated: the scaled requirement is `availability_hours_required` multiplied by the period's hours divided by 8760.0, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. That period is never negative: when the logical instant falls before 1 January of `program_year` it is clamped to 0.0 hours, which scales the requirement to 0.00. Under the `FINAL` variant — the end-of-programme-year settlement — the assessed period is the full 8760.0 hours of the year and the requirement is `availability_hours_required` unscaled. A mid-year outage therefore produces a positive shortfall against a pro-rated requirement while the same accrued unavailability measured against the whole year can clear the shortfall entirely, which is why the two variants can return different penalties for identical stored data.

Within the selected variant, the arithmetic is one pass. `measured_available_hours` is the period's hours minus `unavailable_hours`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and it is *not* floored at 0, so unavailability recorded beyond the assessed period yields a negative figure and, with it, a negative `availability_pct` that lands in the lowest penalty tier. `availability_pct` is `measured_available_hours` divided by the period's hours, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — except that a period of 0.0 hours is not divided by at all and `availability_pct` is exactly 0.00. `shortfall_hours` is the greater of 0 and the scaled requirement minus `measured_available_hours`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The penalty multiplier is tiered on availability, with each tier boundary inclusive on its upper side: it is 1.0 when `availability_pct` is at least 90.0, 1.5 when `availability_pct` is below 90.0 but at least 80.0, and 2.0 when `availability_pct` is below 80.0. `penalty_exposure_usd` is `committed_mw` multiplied by `penalty_rate_usd_per_mw_hour` multiplied by `shortfall_hours` multiplied by that tier multiplier, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `assessment_mode` records which variant was applied, `PRELIMINARY` or `FINAL`. An accrual still open — one whose `accrual_started_at` is not null — is *not* folded in by the assessment; only the resumption transition of §3.7 moves open accrual hours into `unavailable_hours`.

There is a second way to fall short, unrelated to hours. If the asset can no longer sustain `committed_mw` for `obligation_duration_hours` — that is, if `available_energy_mwh` divided by `obligation_duration_hours` is strictly less than `committed_mw` — the obligation is capacity-derated, and `derated_committed_mw` records `available_energy_mwh` divided by `obligation_duration_hours`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. While the plant can still sustain the commitment, `derated_committed_mw` is null. An assessment writes this field afresh every time, under both variants alike and from the asset's `available_energy_mwh` as it stands at that moment, so a value an earlier assessment or an earlier §3.9 cascade recorded is written back to null once the plant can sustain the commitment again.

Status follows from the two failure modes together. An assessment producing no shortfall hours is resolved in a fixed order: `FULFILLED` whenever the mode is `FINAL`, whatever `derated_committed_mw` holds — a final settlement with a clean availability record fulfils the obligation even on a capacity-derated plant; and otherwise, under `PRELIMINARY`, `ACTIVE` when `derated_committed_mw` is null and `SHORTFALL` when it is not, because a capacity-derated plant is in shortfall even with a perfect availability record. An assessment producing positive shortfall hours settles the obligation as `SHORTFALL` when `availability_pct` is at least 90.0 and as `PENALIZED` when it is below that tier. In transition terms this admits `ACTIVE → SHORTFALL`, `ACTIVE → PENALIZED`, `SHORTFALL → PENALIZED`, `SHORTFALL → ACTIVE` (a preliminary re-assessment that now measures no shortfall and no derate, which also resets `penalty_exposure_usd` to 0.0), `ACTIVE → FULFILLED` and `SHORTFALL → FULFILLED` (both of which set `penalty_exposure_usd` to 0.0 and `assessment_mode` to `FINAL`), and `PENALIZED → PENALIZED` and `FULFILLED → FULFILLED`, in which a re-assessment merely restates the figures by overwriting `penalty_exposure_usd`, `shortfall_hours`, `availability_pct`, `measured_available_hours`, `derated_committed_mw` and `assessment_mode` for the newly applied variant. Those last two self-transitions are how the two terminal statuses are enforced: the arithmetic above is evaluated in full and its numeric outputs are always written, but the status it would have landed on is then discarded whenever the obligation already reads `PENALIZED` or `FULFILLED`. `PENALIZED` never returns to `ACTIVE` and never reaches `FULFILLED` — the penalty stands for the programme year — and `FULFILLED` is terminal in exactly the same way.

### 3.9. Degradation Testing, Warranty Claims and Augmentation

Batteries fade, and the warranty is a promise about how slowly. A periodic capacity test measures where the plant actually is; the degradation model says where the warranty said it should be; and the difference, once it reaches a threshold, is a claim.

An `AssetManager` schedules a test by naming `asset_id` and a `scheduled_date`, the ISO 8601 UTC date the discharge is planned for, which is also the evaluation point of the calendar-fade curve. `baseline_test_id` is optional and names the earlier completed test this one is measured against; it is null for an asset's first test. When supplied, the baseline must name a capacity test of the *same* asset whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `scheduled_date` is strictly earlier than this test's `scheduled_date`, and it must not name the test itself; any of those violations is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_TEST_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Scheduling also has to respect the augmentation crew: a test window must not fall inside the outage window of any warranty claim of this asset whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `REMEDIED`, where that window is the closed inclusive interval beginning at the claim's `remedy_install_date` and running for its `remedy_outage_days` plus a further trailing buffer of 2 days. A collision is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_OUTAGE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), and the same check is applied again when the test is started, because a remedy may have been approved in the meantime. A scheduled test is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `warranty_claim_triggered` false, `test_energy_throughput_mwh` 0.0, and `measured_capacity_mwh`, `measured_soh_pct`, `expected_soh_pct` and `soh_deficit_pct` all null; it returns HTTP 201.

The owning `SiteController` starts the discharge, moving the test `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. The asset's `safety_state` must not be `LOCKOUT`, and no other capacity test of the same asset may be `IN_PROGRESS`; a start blocked by either the target's own status or by a sibling test already running is refused with `ERR_TEST_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Nothing beyond the status change and the `updated_at` refresh happens on start.

Completion is where the degradation model runs, and its evaluation order is pinned because the dependencies would otherwise look circular — a remedy is sized as a share of rated energy while rated energy itself grows by every installed remedy. The owning `SiteController` moves the test `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, supplying `measured_capacity_mwh`, the energy the test discharge actually delivered, which must be greater than 0. The asset's `safety_state` must not be `LOCKOUT`. Completion of a test whose `status` is not `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_TEST_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`.

The model's steps, in the order they must be evaluated:

1. **Rated energy** is `nameplate_energy_mwh` plus the sum of `remedy_energy_add_mwh` over this asset's warranty claims whose `status` is `REMEDIED`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. A claim that is `APPROVED` but not yet installed is excluded, so a remedy is always sized against the rated energy that existed before it was installed.
2. **Expected state of health** at the test's `scheduled_date`. The elapsed age is the number of days between the asset's `commercial_operation_date` and the test's `scheduled_date`, divided by 365, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The throughput ratio is the asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` — taken as it stood at the *start* of this completion request, before step 7's credit — divided by its `warranty_throughput_limit_mwh`. `expected_soh_pct` is the greater of the asset's `warranty_guaranteed_soh_pct` and the quantity 100 minus `warranty_annual_fade_pct` multiplied by the elapsed years minus 8.0 multiplied by the throughput ratio multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The warranty floor is the model's floor: no matter how much calendar and throughput fade accumulate, the expectation never drops below `warranty_guaranteed_soh_pct`.
3. **Measured state of health** is `measured_capacity_mwh` divided by the step-1 rated energy, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and it is stored as `measured_soh_pct`.
4. **The deficit** is `soh_deficit_pct`, the greater of 0 and `expected_soh_pct` minus `measured_soh_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `warranty_claim_triggered` is true when `soh_deficit_pct` is at least 1.00 percentage point, inclusive at the boundary.
5. **Remedy sizing** happens later, on the approval branch of adjudication: `remedy_energy_add_mwh` is the step-1 rated energy multiplied by the claim's `claimed_shortfall_pct` divided by 100, multiplied by an oversize factor of 1.10, rounded half up to 3 decimal places.
6. **Propagation** happens later still, when the remedy is installed, and its order is described at the end of this subsection.
7. **The test's own throughput credit** is applied only after step 2 has read the pre-test throughput. `test_energy_throughput_mwh` is `measured_capacity_mwh` rounded half up to 3 decimal places — a full capacity test discharges the entire measured capacity, so the test's own throughput credit *is* its measured energy — and the asset's `cumulative_throughput_mwh` becomes its previous value plus that credit, rounded half up to 3 decimal places.

Because the same measured capacity is judged against whatever rated energy the asset carries, an augmented plant is judged against a higher bar: before any remedy is installed, rated energy equals `nameplate_energy_mwh` and the measurement is read against beginning-of-life energy, whereas once at least one claim is `REMEDIED` the rated energy includes every installed remedy, so an identical `measured_capacity_mwh` yields a lower `measured_soh_pct` and a larger `soh_deficit_pct`.

Completion then cascades in four links. Link one writes the test's `expected_soh_pct`, `measured_soh_pct`, `soh_deficit_pct` and `warranty_claim_triggered` in the pinned order above. Link two fires because the test completed: the asset's `soh_pct` becomes the test's `measured_soh_pct`, its `cumulative_throughput_mwh` takes the test's throughput credit, and then `available_energy_mwh` and `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` are recomputed in that order, with `free_dispatchable_mwh` recomputed last of all, after `available_energy_mwh`. Link three fires because `available_energy_mwh` changed: every capacity obligation of this asset whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SHORTFALL` and whose `committed_mw` is now strictly greater than `available_energy_mwh` divided by `obligation_duration_hours` moves to `SHORTFALL`, records `derated_committed_mw` as `available_energy_mwh` divided by `obligation_duration_hours` rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and has its `penalty_exposure_usd` recomputed under the `PRELIMINARY` variant — that one figure and no other: the cascade evaluates the preliminary arithmetic of §3.8 over the obligation's stored `unavailable_hours` and the period running from the start of its `program_year` to the current logical instant, then writes only the resulting `penalty_exposure_usd`, leaving `assessment_mode`, `availability_pct`, `measured_available_hours` and `shortfall_hours` at whatever values they already carried — processed in ascending `program_year` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`. An obligation the cascade visits but does not find derated is left entirely untouched, `updated_at` and `version` included, so a `derated_committed_mw` an earlier cascade recorded survives a recovery in available energy until an assessment overwrites it. Link four fires because `available_energy_mwh`, `cumulative_throughput_mwh` and `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` all changed: every offer of this asset whose `status` is `DRAFT` has its `offer_price_usd_per_mwh`, `expected_energy_mwh` and `cycle_debit_efc` recomputed, processed in ascending `delivery_start` order and, among ties, ascending `created_at`. The asset's `version` increases by one.

A triggered test is a claim's evidence. An `AssetManager` drafts a warranty claim by naming `asset_id` and `capacity_test_id` and choosing a `claim_type` of `CAPACITY_SHORTFALL`, for a state-of-health deficit, or `THROUGHPUT_BREACH`, for the case where the manufacturer disputes throughput usage. The cited test must have `status` `COMPLETED` and `warranty_claim_triggered` true, and no other claim in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REMEDIED` may already cite it; any of those failures is refused with `ERR_NO_WARRANTY_TRIGGER` (HTTP 422). Filing is also time-boxed: the current logical instant must be at or before the cited test's `scheduled_date` plus 30 days, inclusive of that final instant, so 30 elapsed days passes and 31 fails, and a late filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). The window has no lower edge, so a claim drafted while the clock still stands before the cited test's `scheduled_date`, however far before, is in window. A drafted claim copies `claimed_shortfall_pct` from the cited test's `soh_deficit_pct`, starts with `status` `DRAFT`, records `created_by_user_id` as the acting identifier, leaves `submitted_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `remedy_energy_add_mwh`, `remedy_install_date`, `remedy_outage_days` and `denial_reason` null, and returns HTTP 201.

An `AssetManager` then files the claim with the provider, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; the cited test must still be `COMPLETED` with `warranty_claim_triggered` true, and the current logical instant must still be at or before the cited test's `scheduled_date` plus 30 days. `submitted_by_user_id` records the filing identifier. Submitting a claim whose `status` is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DRAFT`.

Adjudication is a `PlatformAdmin`'s act on a `SUBMITTED` claim — refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED` otherwise — and the field `decision` selects one of two branches with disjoint side effects. The approval branch requires both `remedy_install_date`, the date the augmentation racks will be installed, and `remedy_outage_days`, the length in days of the outage window that begins on that date, a JSON integer that must be at least 1; `denial_reason` must be absent. It produces `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, sizes `remedy_energy_add_mwh` by step 5 of the model above against the *pre*-remedy rated energy — evaluated at adjudication time from `nameplate_energy_mwh` plus the remedies of whichever of the asset's claims read `REMEDIED` at that moment, not from the rated energy that stood when the cited test completed — stores the two remedy fields — which opens the closed-interval outage window that blocks capacity-test scheduling — and records `adjudicated_by_user_id`. The asset's `rated_energy_mwh` does *not* change yet; only the `REMEDIED` status admits augmentation energy. The denial branch requires `denial_reason`, one of `NO_SHORTFALL`, `THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED_WARRANTY` or `LATE_FILING`, and requires both `remedy_install_date` and `remedy_outage_days` to be absent. It produces `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, stores the reason, leaves `remedy_energy_add_mwh` null, records `adjudicated_by_user_id`, opens no outage window and changes no asset field at all. `DENIED` is terminal.

Installation is the last step, and it is the only one that grows the plant. An `AssetManager` moves an `APPROVED` claim to `REMEDIED` once the current logical instant is at or after the claim's `remedy_install_date`, and while the asset's `safety_state` is not `LOCKOUT`; an attempt on a claim whose `status` is not `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED`, and so is an attempt made before the `remedy_install_date` has arrived — the same identifier and the same HTTP 409, tested after the status guard and ahead of the lockout gate. The propagation order is pinned and is followed exactly: the asset's `rated_energy_mwh` is recomputed by step 1 with this claim now `REMEDIED`, so it includes this claim's `remedy_energy_add_mwh`; then `available_energy_mwh` becomes `rated_energy_mwh` multiplied by the asset's `soh_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 3 decimal places; then `equivalent_full_cycles_ytd` is recomputed as `cumulative_throughput_mwh` divided by twice the new `available_energy_mwh`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; and `free_dispatchable_mwh` is recomputed last. The asset's `version` increases by one. `REMEDIED` is terminal.
