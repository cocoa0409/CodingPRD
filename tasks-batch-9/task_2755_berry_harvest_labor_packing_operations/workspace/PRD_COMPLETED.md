# Berry Harvest Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fresh berry grower-packer lives or dies inside a window of a few hours. Fruit that is picked at the right maturity, packed at the row, cooled quickly enough to strip the field heat, and shipped inside its remaining shelf life earns the contract price; the same fruit delayed by two hours in a hot field, or held out of commerce by a failed agricultural-water sample, becomes a shortfall penalty on a buyer program. The Berry Harvest Ops API is the system of record that ties those decisions together: it holds the production blocks and their water-quality history, the seasonal crews and the pickers on them, the harvest windows planned against a maturity forecast, the piece-rate tallies and minimum-wage guarantees that turn those windows into payroll, the field-packed lots and their journey through forced-air cooling and the optical-sorting repack line, and the buyer programs those lots are pledged against.

Three concerns drive almost every rule in this specification. The first is **food safety**: agricultural water is sampled on a rolling basis, a single exceedance quarantines a block instantly, and a quarantine has to reach forward into every harvest window, pick session, crew wage ledger and packed lot that depends on that block. The second is **labor compliance**: piece-rate pay is legitimate only when a minimum-wage guarantee sits underneath it, housing charges may never eat into that guarantee, and the same operator may not both author and approve the numbers that become a worker's pay. The third is **commercial truthfulness**: a buyer program that was promised volume, price and remaining shelf life must be settled against what was actually shipped, line by line, with shortfalls priced rather than hidden.

The API is a flat, single-namespace HTTP service. Every path is mounted under `/berry-ops`, every resource is addressed at the top level, and relationships are carried as explicit identifiers in request bodies rather than as nested collection paths. All eleven resource families — production blocks, water tests, crews, pickers, harvest windows, pick sessions, payroll runs, cooling tunnels, fruit lots, buyer commitments and lot allocations — are peers. Time is not read from the host: the service keeps a logical UTC clock that every expiry, eligibility and risk rule consults, so a full season can be driven deterministically in a test harness.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource identity, precision, rule ordering, concurrency control and the cascade discipline. Everything here applies unless a specific workflow in §3 narrows it.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven declared role names — the comparison is exact and case-sensitive — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The `X-User-ID` header is otherwise accepted verbatim, with no length or format constraint. This check runs before anything else — before the role is matched against the endpoint, before any path identifier is resolved, and before any body field is inspected.

The seven roles are `HarvestManager`, `FoodSafetyOfficer`, `CrewSupervisor`, `PayrollClerk`, `PackhouseOperator`, `SalesManager` and `Picker`. Their remits divide the season by function rather than by seniority:

- A `HarvestManager` plans variety and field-maturity forecasting, registers production blocks, recruits crews, arranges their housing and transportation, certifies and releases them, plans and drives the lifecycle of harvest windows, and owns the logical clock.
- A `FoodSafetyOfficer` runs the food safety program: agricultural water sampling, block quarantine and clearance, and the quality rejection of packed lots.
- A `CrewSupervisor` is the field supervisor of one crew. They own that crew's roster and its per-picker tallies.
- A `PayrollClerk` prices and settles piece-rate pay. They may approve tallies but never author them, and they may not both lock and settle the same pay period.
- A `PackhouseOperator` runs field-heat removal, forced-air cooling, the optical-sorting repack line, and lot rejection.
- A `SalesManager` negotiates buyer program commitments, pledges cooled fruit against them, ships and releases allocation lines, and settles the programs.
- A `Picker` is a seasonal harvest worker with read access to picker records and to their own sessions, and the right to dispute their own tally. Their session reads are narrowed to their own rows, but the single-picker read is not: it is open to any `Picker` caller.

When `X-User-Role` names a role that does not appear on the target endpoint's authorized list, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted, role by role.** Six of the seven roles carry an *opaque* identity. When `X-User-Role` is `HarvestManager`, `FoodSafetyOfficer`, `CrewSupervisor`, `PayrollClerk`, `PackhouseOperator` or `SalesManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record it where an audit trail demands it — as `quarantined_by_user_id` on a `Block` when a `FoodSafetyOfficer` opens a quarantine, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a `WaterTest`, as `tallied_by_user_id` on a `PickSession` when a `CrewSupervisor` tallies, as `approved_by_user_id` when a `PayrollClerk` approves, and as `locked_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` on a `PayrollRun` — and it does compare it against previously stored operator identifiers for the segregation-of-duties rules described in §2.10. It also compares a `CrewSupervisor` caller's `X-User-ID` against the `supervisor_user_id` value that was supplied when the crew was recruited; that field holds an opaque operator identifier, not the `id` of any row.

The seventh role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Picker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Picker` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Picker` compare that resource's `picker_id` to `X-User-ID`. A caller who presents the `Picker` role with an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing `Picker` row is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`; this identity resolution is enforced on exactly three endpoints — disputing a session, reading one session, and listing sessions. The single-picker read is the fourth endpoint the `Picker` role may reach, and it is deliberately the odd one out: it neither resolves `X-User-ID` to a `Picker` row nor compares it to the row being read, so a `Picker` caller presenting any `X-User-ID` at all may read any picker record. When a `Picker` acts on a session whose `picker_id` is not their own identifier, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_SESSION_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When any caller attempts to open or tally a session on a window whose crew names a different `supervisor_user_id`, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_SESSION_SUPERVISOR` (HTTP 403).

The `buyer_user_id` recorded on a buyer program is likewise an opaque identifier of the buyer contact. It is stored for reference only; the API grants no access on the strength of it and never compares it to `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is one of the few values taken from the host's real clock rather than from the logical harvest clock of §2.5, so it advances between requests even when no operator has moved the logical instant.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. Every field a resource family declares is always present on that resource: one it carries no value for — a reason code that was never set, a figure that is "not yet determined" — is rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted from the object.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints and are omitted entirely from single-object responses. Two reads answer with a computed payload rather than a stored row — the block traceability roll-up and the clock setter — and those carry `response_type` `object` with the computed fields directly in `data`, with no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted or supplied empty, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Neither bound is clamped: a `limit` outside 1–100 and a negative `offset` are both **rejected** with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 400), and a value that is not a whole decimal integer — `abc`, `1.5`, `10x` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An `offset` past the end of the matching set is legal and simply yields an empty `data` array. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with rows sharing a `created_at` broken by ascending order of insertion, unless a specific list endpoint declares a different ordering in §3 — the water-sample list is the one such exception, because the food safety program reads it in the same order the rolling geometric mean consumes it.

The `meta` object of a list response carries four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used for the page, `total_count` giving the number of records that match the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` reporting whether any matching record remains beyond the current page — computed as the effective `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned being strictly less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Append-Only Water Register

Every resource the API creates is identified by an `id`, which is a server-minted version-4 UUID in the canonical hyphenated form, carrying no type prefix and never supplied by the client. Ten of the eleven resource families additionally carry a full audit triple: `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits and both read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical harvest clock, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation as well — including mutations the caller did not directly request, such as the recomputation of a crew's wage ledger when one of its sessions is approved, or the release of tunnel slots when a lot finishes cooling.

`WaterTest` is the exception, and deliberately so: the agricultural-water register is append-only. A water sample carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, there is no update endpoint and no delete endpoint for it, and a sample once recorded can never be edited out of the rolling window that determines a block's compliance. This is what makes the water history evidential rather than editable.

### 2.5. The Logical Harvest Clock

No domain rule in this system reads the host machine's wall clock — the host clock is used only to stamp the audit timestamps of §2.4 and the `api_request_timestamp` of the response envelope, and never to decide an outcome. The service maintains a single logical UTC instant, and every date-sensitive rule consults it: whether a picker's hygiene training is still valid, whether a harvest window may still be opened or resumed, whether a buyer program's delivery window has closed and the program may therefore be settled, whether a lot is still inside its shelf life on the day it ships, and whether a commitment should be flagged as at risk of shortfall. At service start-up the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Before any operator changes it, it reads that default and never falls back to real time.

A `HarvestManager` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4; the response `data` carries the single field `current_utc`, echoing back the caller's string verbatim — fractional seconds included if they were supplied, since the stored instant keeps the exact text it arrived with. The instant is subject only to the RFC 3339 parse of §2.7, so the clock may be moved forwards or backwards freely and by any distance. This is an operational and test-driving capability rather than a domain workflow: it mutates no resource, bumps no `version`, and has no side effect other than moving the instant every other rule reads.

### 2.6. Units, Precision and Rounding

Money is held exclusively in **integer cents**. There is no fractional-cent field anywhere in the system, and every money field name ends in `_cents` to make that unambiguous. Decimal quantities — pounds, hours, temperatures, rates and ratios — are computed with exact decimal arithmetic, never with binary floating point, because two of the settlement rules in §3 turn on a single cent of rounding difference.

Each family of quantities has one fixed presentation. The stated digit count fixes the scale a value is *rounded to*, not the text it is rendered as: every quantity is emitted as a JSON number, so trailing zeros are not preserved and a pounds figure rounded to 12.50 appears in the body as `12.5`. Inbound numbers are likewise accepted at any precision and quantized to the declared scale on storage rather than rejected for being too precise.

- **Pounds** carry 2 decimal places and are rounded half up. This covers `net_packed_lbs`, `allocated_lbs`, `available_lbs`, `repack_out_lbs`, `cull_lbs`, `optical_reject_lbs`, `packed_lbs`, `committed_lbs`, `shipped_lbs`, `open_lbs`, `planned_volume_lbs`, `volume_commitment_lbs`, `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` and `acres`.
- **Hours** carry 2 decimal places and are rounded half up: `window_hours`, `hours_worked`, `period_hours`, `total_hours_lifetime`, `field_delay_hours` and `cool_hours`.
- **Rates in pounds per hour** carry 2 decimal places, rounded half up: `effective_pick_rate_lbs_per_hour` and `avg_lbs_per_hour`.
- **Ratios** carry 4 decimal places, rounded half up: `cull_rate` and `fill_pct` (and the coverage ratio that drives `risk_flag`).
- **Water bacteriology** carries 1 decimal place, rounded half up: `water_geomean_mpn`. Every compliance comparison is made against the *stored, rounded* value rather than the unrounded root — a raw geometric mean of 126.04 is stored as 126.0 and therefore satisfies the limit, while a raw geometric mean of 126.17 is stored as 126.2 and does not.
- **Temperatures** carry 1 decimal place: `target_pulp_temp_f`, `field_pulp_temp_f`, `entry_pulp_temp_f` and `exit_pulp_temp_f`. **Brix** carries 1 decimal place: `forecast_brix`.

Three places in the system deliberately abandon half-up rounding, and the direction matters:

- Staffing requirements round **up** to the next whole picker: a fractional picker is a whole picker.
- Shelf-life penalties charged for time — the field-heat penalty and the cooling-duration penalty — round **up**, so any part of an hour beyond the target costs a full day of shelf life.
- Shelf-life penalties charged for exit temperature round **down**, so only a full temperature step above the target costs a day; and the housing-deduction cap rounds **down**, so a rounding step can never take more from a worker than the permitted share of gross pay.

Whole-cent, whole-day and whole-picker results that are described below as "rounded" without a stated digit count are rounded half up to the whole unit.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Order in Which Rules Fire

Path identifiers are resolved **left to right**, so the outermost identifier in a URL is always checked before anything nested beneath it. The eleven path variables in the system are `block_id`, `crew_id`, `picker_id`, `harvest_window_id`, `pick_session_id`, `payroll_run_id`, `fruit_lot_id`, `cooling_tunnel_id`, `buyer_commitment_id`, `lot_allocation_id` and `water_test_id`; each resolves to its own resource family and produces its own not-found error.

Because many endpoints carry a dozen or more applicable rules, the order of evaluation is itself part of the contract. **Rules are evaluated in ascending priority, the first violated rule determines the response, and no side effect of the request is applied once a rule has fired.** A request that offends three rules reports only the lowest-numbered one. The full ordering is:

**Authentication (1–2).** (1) `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty. (2) `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared role names. Both failures report `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.

**Authorization (3–4).** (3) The acting role appears on the target endpoint's authorized list, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, on every endpoint. (4) When the acting role is `Picker`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `Picker` row, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`; this applies to disputing a pick session, reading one pick session, and listing pick sessions.

**Path existence (5–9), left to right.** (5) `block_id` in the path resolves to a `Block`, otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`. (6) `crew_id` resolves to a `Crew` (`ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`) and `picker_id` resolves to a `Picker` (`ERR_PICKER_NOT_FOUND`). (7) `harvest_window_id` resolves to a `HarvestWindow` (`ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`), `pick_session_id` to a `PickSession` (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`), and `payroll_run_id` to a `PayrollRun` (`ERR_PAYROLL_RUN_NOT_FOUND`). (8) `fruit_lot_id` resolves to a `FruitLot` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`) and `cooling_tunnel_id` to a `CoolingTunnel` (`ERR_TUNNEL_NOT_FOUND`). (9) `buyer_commitment_id` resolves to a `BuyerCommitment` (`ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`) and `lot_allocation_id` to a `LotAllocation` (`ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`).

**Body shape, enumerations and ranges (10–15).** (10) On every write endpoint, every field the endpoint declares as required is present and of the declared JSON type, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This same rule carries four further shape checks, all reporting `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a declared string must respect its length bounds, and *every* string field carries an implicit minimum of one character, so an empty string is never an acceptable value even where no explicit minimum is stated; a JSON boolean satisfies no declared type at all, integer fields included; an integer-typed field rejects a number written with a fractional part, while a decimal-typed field accepts a bare integer; and an explicit `null` is accepted only for a field the endpoint declares optional, where it is stored as no value. A missing or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object — so the first required field it lacks is what gets reported — while a body that is not valid JSON, or that parses to a JSON value other than an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` outright. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored wherever they appear, and a transition that declares no body field at all never inspects the body, so whatever is sent with it — including malformed JSON — is simply discarded. (11) Enum-typed body fields carry a declared value — `variety` is one of `STRAWBERRY`, `BLUEBERRY`, `RASPBERRY` or `BLACKBERRY`; `labor_program` is `DOMESTIC` or `H2A`; `grade` is `A`, `B` or `CULL` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`; this applies to block registration, crew recruitment, fruit-lot creation and buyer-commitment registration. (12) Numeric body fields respect their declared ranges, otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, on every write endpoint: `acres` greater than zero; `generic_ecoli_mpn` at least 1; `hours_worked` greater than zero and at most 16.00; `clamshells_filled` and `culled_clamshells` each zero or greater; `forecast_brix` between 0 and 30 inclusive; `planned_volume_lbs` greater than zero; `piece_rate_cents_per_clamshell` between 1 and 500; `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` greater than zero and at most 5, on both the window and the lot endpoints that carry it; `clamshell_count` at least 1; `pallet_slots` at least 1; `slot_capacity` at least 1; `allocated_lbs` greater than zero; `volume_commitment_lbs` greater than zero; `price_cents_per_lb` at least 1; `shelf_life_guarantee_days` at least 1; `planned_head_count` between 1 and 60; `planting_year` between 2000 and 2026; and `vehicle_seats` and `beds_confirmed` each zero or greater. (13) On a tally, `culled_clamshells` is not greater than `clamshells_filled`, otherwise `ERR_CULL_EXCEEDS_TALLY`. (14) Paired instants and dates inside one body are correctly ordered, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`: `end_at` strictly after `start_at` on window planning; `period_end_date` on or after `period_start_date` on payroll-run creation; `window_end_date` on or after `window_start_date` on commitment registration; `planned_cool_end_at` strictly after `cool_start_at` when cooling starts; and `contract_end_date` on or after `contract_start_date` on crew recruitment. (15) Instants parse as RFC 3339 UTC values ending in `Z` and dates parse as `YYYY-MM-DD`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

**Body foreign keys (16–19).** (16) A body `block_id` resolves to a `Block` (`ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`) on water-sample recording, window planning and fruit-lot creation. (17) A body `crew_id` resolves to a `Crew` (`ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`) and a body `picker_id` resolves to a `Picker` (`ERR_PICKER_NOT_FOUND`) on picker registration, window planning, payroll-run creation and pick-session creation. (18) A body `harvest_window_id` resolves to a `HarvestWindow` (`ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`), a supplied `source_lot_id` resolves to a `FruitLot` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`), and a body `cooling_tunnel_id` resolves to a `CoolingTunnel` (`ERR_TUNNEL_NOT_FOUND`), on pick-session creation, fruit-lot creation and the start of cooling. (19) On allocation creation, a body `fruit_lot_id` resolves to a `FruitLot` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`) and a body `buyer_commitment_id` resolves to a `BuyerCommitment` (`ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`).

**Ownership (20–22).** (20) On a tally, `X-User-ID` equals the `supervisor_user_id` of the crew that owns the session's window, otherwise `ERR_NOT_SESSION_SUPERVISOR`. (21) On a dispute raised with the `Picker` role, `X-User-ID` equals the session's `picker_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_SESSION_OWNER`. (22) On pick-session creation, `X-User-ID` equals the `supervisor_user_id` of the crew that owns the target window **and** the supplied picker's `crew_id` is that same crew, otherwise `ERR_NOT_SESSION_SUPERVISOR`.

**Optimistic concurrency (23–24).** (23) `expected_cooling_tunnel_version` matches the target tunnel's current `version` when cooling starts, otherwise `ERR_STALE_TUNNEL_VERSION`. (24) `expected_buyer_commitment_version` matches the target commitment's current `version` when an allocation is created, otherwise `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION`. Both are described in §2.8.

**State preconditions (25–37).** (25) A block is not already under a manual hold when it is quarantined, and *is* under one when it is cleared, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BLOCK_STATE`; the clearing endpoint's second precondition — a `water_status` of `PROVISIONAL` or `COMPLIANT` — belongs to this same rule and reports the same `ERR_INVALID_BLOCK_STATE`, evaluated after the hold-is-open check and before the segregation-of-duties comparison of rule 48. (26) A crew is `STAFFED` when certified and either `CERTIFIED` or `ACTIVE` when released, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`; the same identifier also guards window opening, where the window's assigned crew must likewise be `CERTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, but that particular comparison is not made at this position in the order — as §3.3 sets out, it is made inside the opening sequence after the block-posture gates of rules 38 and 39 and before the clock and picker-count tests, so a `RECRUITING`, `STAFFED` or `RELEASED` crew on a block that is not `CLEARED` reports the block, not the crew. (27) A crew is `RECRUITING` or `STAFFED` when its support plan is recorded — housing and transport cannot be re-cut after certification — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`. (28) A window is `PLANNED` when confirmed, `CONFIRMED` when opened, `OPEN` when closed, `SUSPENDED` when resumed, and one of `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` when cancelled, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`. (29) The window of a session is `OPEN` when the session is created, tallied or disputed; a `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED` window admits none of the three, and the rejection is `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. (30) A session's `quality_flag` is not `PENDING` when it is approved, otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_TALLIED` — and note that this precedes the session status check, so approving a never-tallied `OPEN` session reports the missing tally rather than the wrong status. (31) A session is `OPEN` or `DISPUTED` when tallied, `TALLIED` when approved, and `TALLIED` or `APPROVED` when disputed, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`. (32) A session's `payroll_run_id` is still empty when it is disputed, otherwise `ERR_SESSION_PAYROLL_LOCKED`. (33) A payroll run is `DRAFT` when locked and `LOCKED` when settled, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAYROLL_STATE`. (34) No session of the crew whose `work_date` falls inside the run's period is `OPEN`, `TALLIED` or `DISPUTED` at lock time, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_UNRESOLVED_SESSIONS`. (35) A lot's `cold_chain_status` is `PACKED` when cooling starts, `COOLING` when cooling completes, `COOLED` when it is repacked, and `COOLED` when an allocation line drawn on it is shipped, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COLD_CHAIN_STATE`. (36) A lot's `disposition_status` is `UNCOMMITTED` or `RESERVED` when cooling starts, `UNCOMMITTED` when it is repacked, and neither `DELIVERED` nor `REJECTED` nor `REPACKED` when it is rejected or allocated, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE`. (37) An allocation line is `RESERVED` when shipped or released, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`; and a buyer commitment is `OPEN` when allocated against, shipped from, released from or settled, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE`.

**Food safety and labor compliance (38–44).** (38) The window's block is not `QUARANTINED` when the window is confirmed, opened or resumed, and the traceability block is not `QUARANTINED` when a fruit lot is packed against it; the rejection in all four cases is `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED`. (39) The window's block has a `food_safety_posture` of `CLEARED` both when the window is opened and when a suspended window is resumed — a `RESTRICTED` block, whether because it has no samples at all or because its water is non-compliant, can be neither picked nor put back into the schedule — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_CLEARED` in both cases. On each of those two transitions this test sits immediately behind the quarantine test of rule 38, so a `QUARANTINED` block still reports `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED` rather than this. (40) The lot is not under a food safety hold when cooling starts, when it is repacked, when it is allocated, or when an allocation line drawn on it is shipped; the rejection in all four cases is `ERR_LOT_ON_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`. (41) The traceability block of a shipping lot has a `water_status` of `COMPLIANT`, otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_WATER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. (42) Every picker of the crew has a `hygiene_training_expires_on` that is present and strictly after the current logical clock date when the crew is certified, otherwise `ERR_HYGIENE_TRAINING_INVALID`. (43) The picker's `hygiene_training_expires_on` is present and strictly after the session's `work_date` when a pick session is created, otherwise `ERR_HYGIENE_TRAINING_INVALID`. (44) The crew's `support_plan_complete` is true when the crew is certified, otherwise `ERR_SUPPORT_PLAN_INCOMPLETE`.

**Uniqueness, duty splits and mass balance (45–51).** (45) No other payroll run of the same crew is already `LOCKED` when a run is locked, otherwise `ERR_CREW_RUN_ALREADY_LOCKED`. (46) The `X-User-ID` settling a payroll run differs from that run's `locked_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. (47) The `X-User-ID` approving a session differs from that session's `tallied_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. (48) The `X-User-ID` clearing a block differs from that block's `quarantined_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. (49) A session's `tally_revision_count` is below the revision cap of three when it is re-tallied out of `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_TALLY_REVISION_LIMIT`. (50) The three repack output counts balance the source lot exactly and the output count is at least 1, otherwise `ERR_REPACK_MASS_BALANCE`. (51) Uniqueness holds, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`: a `Block` `name` is unique, a `CoolingTunnel` `name` is unique, a `PickSession` is unique per combination of `harvest_window_id`, `picker_id` and `work_date`, and a `PayrollRun` is unique per combination of `crew_id`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`.

**Cross-entity coherence (52–54).** (52) A fruit lot's `block_id` equals the `block_id` of the window named by its `harvest_window_id` — the pack record must trace to the block that was actually picked — otherwise `ERR_LOT_BLOCK_MISMATCH`. (53) A fruit lot's `variety` equals its block's `variety`, and an allocated lot's `variety` equals the buyer commitment's `variety`; both failures report `ERR_VARIETY_MISMATCH`. (54) A payroll run's `crew_id` equals the crew of every session it would bind, and a planned window does not name a `RELEASED` crew; both failures report `ERR_CREW_MISMATCH`.

**Temporal coherence (55–59).** (55) A fruit lot's `pick_date` falls within the calendar dates spanned by its window's `start_at` and `end_at`, inclusive, otherwise `ERR_PICK_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. (56) A pick session's `work_date` falls within the calendar dates spanned by its window, inclusive, otherwise `ERR_WORK_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. (57) A candidate harvest window clears the twelve-hour block re-entry separation against every live window of the same block, otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_REENTRY_CONFLICT`; this is checked both when the window is planned and again when it is confirmed. (58) The logical clock instant is strictly before the window's `end_at` when the window is opened or resumed; `cool_start_at` is not earlier than `pack_completed_at`; `cool_complete_at` is strictly after `cool_start_at`; and a buyer commitment settles only after its `window_end_date` has passed. All four failures report `ERR_TEMPORAL_ORDER_VIOLATION`. (59) On a ship, the `shipped_at` date falls inside the commitment's delivery window inclusive **and** the current logical clock date is not after the lot's `shelf_life_expires_on`, otherwise `ERR_SHIP_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_WINDOW`.

**Capacity and quota (60–64).** (60) The crew has at least the window's `required_picker_count` pickers when the window is opened, and `required_picker_count` does not exceed the crew cap of 60 when the window is confirmed; both failures report `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICKERS`. (61) Aggregate tunnel occupancy over the requested interval plus the requested `pallet_slots` does not exceed the tunnel's `slot_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_TUNNEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. (62) `allocated_lbs` does not exceed the lot's `available_lbs`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_VOLUME_EXCEEDED`. (63) `allocated_lbs` does not exceed the commitment's `open_lbs`, otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_OVERBOOKED`. (64) A crew being released holds no accrued wages, no live window and no unresolved session, otherwise `ERR_CREW_LEDGER_NOT_CLEAR`.

**Two late-ordered rules (65–66).** (65) `water_test_id` in the path resolves to a `WaterTest`, otherwise `ERR_WATER_TEST_NOT_FOUND`. Its position after the capacity tier is observationally identical to sitting with the other path-existence rules, because the only endpoint it guards is the single-sample read, whose only other applicable rules are the authentication and authorization tiers. (66) When a harvest window is confirmed, its `forecast_brix` is at least the minimum brix for the block's variety, otherwise `ERR_BRIX_BELOW_MINIMUM`; the comparison is inclusive, so a window sitting exactly on the minimum confirms. This maturity gate is evaluated **last** on purpose: a window that is both immature and mis-staffed reports the staffing problem first, because staffing is the cheaper of the two to correct.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Tunnels and Buyer Programs

Two write operations in this system read a shared aggregate, do arithmetic against it, and then commit — and for those two the caller must prove that the figure it did its arithmetic against is still the current one. The mechanism is a mandatory expected-version field on the request. It is not optional and has no default. Both fields are declared as integers, and their absence or a non-integer value is therefore the priority-10 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` of §2.7 — a 400, not a 412: the 412 is reserved for a well-formed integer that simply no longer matches.

Pushing a lot into a forced-air tunnel requires `expected_cooling_tunnel_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CoolingTunnel`. If that value does not match the tunnel's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TUNNEL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. An advanced tunnel version means some other lot has taken or released slots in the interval the caller is reasoning about, so the caller's view of `committed_slots` is stale and the capacity arithmetic it based the request on no longer holds. Guarding that view is the only role the tunnel's `version` plays: admission itself is decided by the half-open-interval occupancy scan of §3.6 and never by a comparison against the stored `committed_slots`, which is a running total the tunnel keeps rather than the gate. A caller that re-reads the tunnel and resubmits with the current `version` succeeds whenever room genuinely remains.

Pledging pounds against a buyer program requires `expected_buyer_commitment_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `BuyerCommitment`. A mismatch with the commitment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Every accepted allocation, release, ship and cascaded release mutates `committed_lbs` or `shipped_lbs` and advances the commitment's `version`, so a stale value means the caller sized `allocated_lbs` against an `open_lbs` figure the server no longer holds. Re-reading the commitment and resubmitting succeeds while `open_lbs` still covers the request.

No other endpoint in the system takes an expected-version field. Cooling completion, repack, rejection, allocation release, allocation shipping, commitment settlement, payroll lock and settle, and every window and session transition are all guarded by state preconditions rather than by version tokens.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Adjustments

Several operations in this system reach well beyond the resource named in the path. When they do, three rules hold without exception.

First, **cascades are ordered and deterministic.** Where a cascade touches a set of rows, the rows are processed in a stated order — suspended windows in ascending `start_at` and then ascending `created_at`; affected sessions and released allocation lines in ascending `created_at` — so that the resulting figures are reproducible.

Second, **every mutated resource is versioned.** A cascade that changes a window, a session, a crew, a picker, a lot, an allocation line or a commitment increments that resource's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, exactly as a directly requested mutation would. A cascade never silently edits a row. The converse holds just as strictly: it is *mutation* that earns the increment, not mere inspection, so a row the cascade examines and leaves unchanged — a lot that already carries `food_safety_hold` true when a quarantine sweeps its block, or already carries it false when the hold is lifted — keeps its `version`. And a row reached twice in one cascade is still incremented by exactly 1 for the whole request, never once per visit.

Third, **reversals are exact compensations, not recalculations from scratch of the money involved.** When an approved session is pulled back out of the paid set, the crew's `accrued_wage_cents` is decremented by the *same stored* `net_cents` figure that was added when it was approved, so the ledger cannot drift by a rounding step. Derived aggregates that are not ledgers — a picker's lifetime productivity, a window's harvested clamshell count, a lot's available pounds, a commitment's committed pounds — are instead **fully recomputed** from the surviving rows rather than adjusted by a delta, which is what makes a reversal restore the pre-event figures exactly.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties across the Season

Three points in the season are governed by a duty split, and all three are enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against an operator identifier the system stored earlier. All three report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 409.

The operator who tallies a pick session may not approve it: the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the session's `tallied_by_user_id`. The clerk who locks a pay period may not settle it: the settling `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `locked_by_user_id`. And the officer who quarantines a block may not be the one who lifts the hold: the clearing `X-User-ID` must differ from the block's `quarantined_by_user_id`. That last comparison is why `quarantined_by_user_id` is retained on the block after the hold is cleared rather than being emptied — it is the evidence the next quarantine-and-clear cycle is checked against.

### 2.11. Filtered Reads and Discovery Channels

Every resource family exposes a single-resource read and a list read, and the list reads carry optional filters. Every filter is an exact, case-sensitive string equality against the named field, several supplied together are combined conjunctively, and a parameter that is absent or supplied empty is ignored. A filter value that matches nothing — including a value outside the field's enumeration, or an identifier that resolves to no row — is never an error and never a 404: it simply yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0. Query parameters other than the declared filters and the two pagination parameters are ignored. Blocks may be filtered by `variety`, by `food_safety_posture` or by `water_status`. Water samples may be filtered to one block with `block_id`. Crews may be filtered by `labor_program` or `status`, and pickers may be filtered to one crew with `crew_id`. Harvest windows may be filtered by `block_id`, by `crew_id` or by `status`. Pick sessions may be filtered by `harvest_window_id`, by `picker_id`, by `payroll_run_id` or by `status`. Payroll runs may be filtered by `crew_id` or `status`. Fruit lots may be filtered by `harvest_window_id`, by `block_id`, by `source_lot_id` (which is how repack lineage is walked), by `cooling_tunnel_id`, by `cold_chain_status` or by `disposition_status`. Buyer commitments may be filtered by `variety` or `status`, and allocation lines by `fruit_lot_id`, by `buyer_commitment_id` or by `status`. Cooling tunnels take no filter.

Three of these list reads are the *only* discovery channel for records the system creates or mutates on its own initiative: the pick-session list is the only way to find sessions that a quarantine cascade voided or disputed, the fruit-lot list filtered by `source_lot_id` is the only way to find the child lot a repack produced, and the allocation list is the only way to find lines that a lot rejection released. None of those events return the affected rows in their own response body, so a client that needs them must go and look.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The season is described here in the order it happens: ground and water first, then people, then the harvest windows that bring the two together, then the pay those windows generate, then the fruit they produce, and finally the buyers that fruit was promised to. A closing subsection collects the reversal rules that hold the whole thing together when something goes wrong.

### 3.1. Production Blocks and the Agricultural Water Program

A `HarvestManager` registers each production block with a grower-facing `name` of between 1 and 80 characters, which must be unique across all blocks and is compared case-sensitively; the planted `variety`, one of `STRAWBERRY`, `BLUEBERRY`, `RASPBERRY` or `BLACKBERRY`; the planted `acres`, a positive figure carried to two decimals; the `planting_year`, which must fall between 2000 and 2026 inclusive; and an `irrigation_source_code` of between 1 and 32 characters naming the agricultural water source that will be sampled. Variety is the single most consequential of these, because it drives pick rate, the maturity gates a harvest window must clear, and the base shelf life of every lot packed off the block.

A brand-new block is deliberately unusable. On registration its `water_sample_count` is 0, its `water_geomean_mpn` and `last_sample_date` carry no value, its `water_status` is `NO_SAMPLES`, its `manual_quarantine_active` flag is false with `quarantined_by_user_id` and `quarantine_reason_code` both empty, and its `food_safety_posture` is `RESTRICTED`. Nothing can be picked off it until at least one water sample is on file.

**The rolling water series.** A `FoodSafetyOfficer` records one agricultural-water sample at a time, supplying the `block_id` of the block whose irrigation source was drawn, the `sample_date` on which it was drawn as a calendar date, and `generic_ecoli_mpn`, the generic *E. coli* most-probable-number per 100 mL, which must be at least 1 and no more than 100000. The sample stores the acting operator as `recorded_by_user_id`. It also stores two derived facts about itself: `single_sample_exceedance`, true exactly when this sample's `generic_ecoli_mpn` is strictly greater than the single-sample maximum of 410 MPN per 100 mL, and `resulting_water_status`, a snapshot of the parent block's `water_status` as it stood immediately after this sample was folded in. Because the register is append-only, that snapshot is a permanent record of what the officer knew at the time. `sample_date` is otherwise unconstrained — it is compared against neither the logical clock nor the block's existing `last_sample_date` — so a back-dated sample is accepted and simply takes its place in the ordering below, where it may fall outside the trailing window altogether or displace whichever row had been the newest.

Recording a sample recomputes the block's compliance from scratch and always increments the block's `version` by exactly 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, whether or not the recomputation actually moves `water_status`, `water_geomean_mpn` or `food_safety_posture`. The block's samples are ordered by `sample_date` ascending and, within a shared date, by `created_at` ascending; only the **last five** rows of that ordering participate. Older samples are excluded entirely — they neither raise nor lower the result — and samples belonging to other blocks never participate. Let the count of rows in that trailing window be the number of samples considered. `water_sample_count` is set to that count, and `last_sample_date` is set to the `sample_date` of the newest row in the window. `water_geomean_mpn` is the geometric mean of the `generic_ecoli_mpn` values in the window: the product of those values, raised to the power of one divided by the count, then rounded half up to one decimal place. When no samples exist at all, `water_sample_count` is 0, `water_geomean_mpn` carries no value and `water_status` is `NO_SAMPLES`.

The block's `water_status` then follows a strict cascade of four tests, in this order. If the **newest** sample in the window has a `generic_ecoli_mpn` strictly greater than 410, the status is `FAILED` — one bad sample overrides an otherwise clean mean. Otherwise, if fewer than three samples are in the window, the status is `PROVISIONAL`, because one or two readings are not enough evidence of compliance. Otherwise, if the stored `water_geomean_mpn` is less than or equal to the geometric-mean criterion of 126 MPN per 100 mL, the status is `COMPLIANT`. Otherwise it is `NON_COMPLIANT`. As §2.6 sets out, the comparison against 126 uses the stored one-decimal value, not the raw root.

**Posture is derived, never supplied.** A block's `food_safety_posture` is recomputed at the end of every call that stores a water sample for it or toggles its manual hold, and it is never accepted as an input. If `manual_quarantine_active` is true, the posture is `QUARANTINED`. Otherwise, if `water_status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `COMPLIANT`, the posture is `CLEARED`. Otherwise — that is, for `NO_SAMPLES`, `NON_COMPLIANT` and `FAILED` — the posture is `RESTRICTED`. There is a deliberate interlock here: a `FAILED` water status *also* sets `manual_quarantine_active` to true inside the very same sample-recording call, with `quarantine_reason_code` set to `SINGLE_SAMPLE_EXCEEDANCE` and `quarantined_by_user_id` set to the recording officer. A single-sample exceedance therefore always lands on `QUARANTINED` and never on `RESTRICTED`. That interlock fires only when the block is not already under a manual hold: a `FAILED` recomputation on a block whose hold is already open leaves the standing `quarantine_reason_code` and `quarantined_by_user_id` exactly as they were and runs no second cascade, because the windows, sessions and lots beneath it were already dealt with when the hold went on.

Sample recording can move the posture in either direction. A block sitting at `RESTRICTED` moves to `CLEARED` when the recomputed `water_status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `COMPLIANT` and no manual hold is open. A block sitting at `CLEARED` falls back to `RESTRICTED` when the recomputed `water_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT` and no manual hold is open; in that case `water_status` is set to `NON_COMPLIANT` and `water_geomean_mpn` is refreshed. Note what that fallback does *not* do: windows already `OPEN` on the block keep running, because only the confirmation and opening of windows are gated on posture. Either move increments the block's `version` by 1.

**The quarantine cascade.** A `FoodSafetyOfficer` may also impose a hold directly, supplying a `quarantine_reason_code` — a required, non-empty string that is stored verbatim, with no enumeration and no upper length bound. The block must not already be under a manual hold. Whether the hold arrives by that route or through a single-sample exceedance, and whether the posture was `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED` beforehand, the consequence is identical and runs four levels deep:

- `manual_quarantine_active` becomes true, `quarantined_by_user_id` records the acting operator, `quarantine_reason_code` is stored, and the posture becomes `QUARANTINED`.
- **Level 1:** every harvest window on this block whose status is `CONFIRMED` or `OPEN`, processed in ascending `start_at` and then ascending `created_at` order, moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason_code` set to `BLOCK_QUARANTINE`.
- **Level 2**, caused by level 1: for each window that just entered `SUSPENDED`, its pick sessions are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Those whose status is `OPEN` or `TALLIED` move to `VOIDED` with `void_reason_code` set to `WINDOW_SUSPENDED` — a `TALLIED` session keeps its computed pay figures for audit even though it will never be paid. Those whose status is `APPROVED` move to `DISPUTED` with `dispute_reason_code` set to `WINDOW_SUSPENDED` and `approved_by_user_id` cleared back to no value, exactly as an API-raised dispute out of `APPROVED` would clear it.
- **Level 3**, caused by level 2: for each session that just left `APPROVED`, the crew's `accrued_wage_cents` is reduced by that session's stored `net_cents`, and the picker's `total_clamshells_lifetime`, `total_hours_lifetime` and `avg_lbs_per_hour`, together with the window's `harvested_clamshells`, are recomputed over the sessions that remain `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`. Sessions that went to `VOIDED` from `OPEN` or `TALLIED` produce no ledger movement at all, because they had never accrued.
- Independently of the window chain, every fruit lot whose traceability block is this block and whose `disposition_status` is `UNCOMMITTED` or `RESERVED` has `food_safety_hold` set to true.
- The block's `version` is incremented by 1, as is the `version` of every window, session, crew, picker and lot the cascade touched.

**Clearing a hold.** The clear endpoint declares no body field of its own. Lifting the hold requires three things at once, checked in this order: `manual_quarantine_active` must currently be true, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BLOCK_STATE`; the block's `water_status` must be `PROVISIONAL` or `COMPLIANT` — meaning a passing sample was recorded *after* the hold went on — otherwise the same `ERR_INVALID_BLOCK_STATE`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `quarantined_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Clearing sets `manual_quarantine_active` back to false, recomputes the posture to `CLEARED`, and sets `food_safety_hold` back to false on every lot of the block whose `disposition_status` is `UNCOMMITTED` or `RESERVED`. It increments the block's `version` by 1. What it emphatically does **not** do is resume the suspended windows: those stay `SUSPENDED` until a `HarvestManager` resumes each one explicitly, because the decision to put a crew back on a previously contaminated block is a human one.

**Traceability roll-up.** The food safety program demands a block-and-pick-date roll-up, and the API exposes it as a computed read. It walks every fruit lot whose traceability block is this block, in ascending `pick_date` and then ascending `created_at` order, and regardless of status — including lots that have been repacked away and the child lots those repacks produced, since a child carries the same traceability block. It reports the block's identifier as `block_id`, its `name` as `block_name`, its `variety`, its current `water_status` and `food_safety_posture`, the number of lots as `lot_count`, the number of distinct pick dates as `pick_date_count`, the sum of `net_packed_lbs` across all those lots as `total_packed_lbs` rounded half up to two decimals, the sum of `net_packed_lbs` across only the lots whose `disposition_status` is `DELIVERED` as `shipped_lbs` on the same rounding, the count of lots carrying `food_safety_hold` as `held_lot_count`, the count of lots whose `disposition_status` is `REJECTED` as `rejected_lot_count`, and the first and last pick dates in the ordering as `earliest_pick_date` and `latest_pick_date`. A block with no lots at all returns zeros for every count and no value for either date — it is never a 404.

### 3.2. Recruiting, Supporting and Certifying a Harvest Crew

Crews are recruited by a `HarvestManager` against a seasonal labor program. The request carries a crew `name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `labor_program` of either `DOMESTIC` or `H2A`, a `supervisor_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters naming the operator who will own this crew's tallies, a contracted `planned_head_count` between 1 and 60, and the engagement dates `contract_start_date` and `contract_end_date`, the latter on or after the former. A new crew starts at `RECRUITING` with `picker_count` 0, `support_plan_complete` false, no `housing_unit_code` and no `transport_route_code`, `beds_confirmed` and `vehicle_seats` both 0, and all three ledger figures — `accrued_wage_cents`, `settled_wage_cents` and `reconciliation_delta_cents` — at 0.

**Registering pickers.** A `HarvestManager` or a `CrewSupervisor` registers a worker onto a crew with the target `crew_id`, the worker's `full_name` of 1 to 80 characters, and optionally `hygiene_training_on`, the calendar date on which they completed produce-handler hygiene training. Neither this call nor the re-recording of training compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the crew's `supervisor_user_id`: the roster endpoints are open to any caller holding one of the two roles, and the supervisor-identity check of §2.7 governs the pick-session endpoints only. The field is omitted for a worker who has not yet been trained. Two things are derived at registration: the picker's `labor_program` is copied from the parent crew, and `hygiene_training_expires_on` is set to `hygiene_training_on` plus 365 days — one year of validity — remaining unset while `hygiene_training_on` is unset. Lifetime productivity starts empty: `total_clamshells_lifetime` 0, `total_hours_lifetime` 0 and `avg_lbs_per_hour` exactly 0.00. Registering a picker recomputes the crew's `picker_count` and increments the crew's `version` by 1, and when that count moves from zero to one the crew advances `RECRUITING → STAFFED`. The crew's status gates nothing here: a worker may be registered onto a crew in any status at all, `CERTIFIED`, `ACTIVE` and `RELEASED` included, and only a crew still `RECRUITING` takes the advance to `STAFFED` — every other status is left exactly where it was, with the `version` bump and the recomputed `picker_count` as the only effects.

Training lapses, so it can be re-recorded. Either of the same two roles may submit a fresh `hygiene_training_on` for an existing picker — the field is required on this call and may not be sent as `null` — which resets the 365-day validity clock: `hygiene_training_expires_on` is recomputed as the new date plus 365 days, and the picker's `version` is incremented by 1. The new date simply replaces the old one, whatever it says: it may be earlier than the date already on file and it may sit either side of the logical clock, and nothing but the picker row is touched — the parent crew is neither mutated nor versioned.

**Housing and transportation.** Seasonal labor is not just a headcount; it is beds and seats. In one call, a `HarvestManager` records the crew's whole support plan: a `housing_unit_code` naming the housing unit, `beds_confirmed` giving the number of beds secured there, a `transport_route_code` naming the daily field-transport route, and `vehicle_seats` giving the seats secured on it. Both counts must be zero or greater and both are required; the two codes are optional and may be sent as `null`. Because the call records the plan as a whole rather than patching it, a code that is omitted or sent as `null` is stored as no value, erasing whatever code was previously on file — which is how an `H2A` crew can be pushed back out of `support_plan_complete`. Neither code carries a maximum length. The support plan may only be recorded while the crew is `RECRUITING` or `STAFFED`; once a crew has been certified, its housing and transport cannot be re-cut, and a later attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`.

Recording the plan recomputes the derived flag `support_plan_complete`, and the rule differs by labor program. For every crew, `transport_route_code` must be present and `vehicle_seats` must be at least `planned_head_count`. For an `H2A` crew there is an additional employer-provided-housing obligation: `housing_unit_code` must also be present and `beds_confirmed` must also be at least `planned_head_count`. A `DOMESTIC` crew can therefore be complete with no housing at all. Recording the plan increments the crew's `version` by 1.

**Certification.** A `HarvestManager` certifies a crew that is `STAFFED`, moving it `STAFFED → CERTIFIED`. Two compliance gates apply, and their order matters — though the `STAFFED` check precedes them both, so a crew that is `RECRUITING`, `CERTIFIED`, `ACTIVE` or `RELEASED` reports `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE` even when its roster or its support plan is deficient as well. The hygiene gate is checked first: every picker on the crew must have a `hygiene_training_expires_on` that is present and strictly after the current logical clock date, and any picker who is untrained or whose training has lapsed causes `ERR_HYGIENE_TRAINING_INVALID`. Only then is the support-plan gate checked: `support_plan_complete` must be true, otherwise `ERR_SUPPORT_PLAN_INCOMPLETE`. Certification increments the crew's `version` by 1. A crew that is not `STAFFED` when certification is attempted is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`.

A certified crew becomes `ACTIVE` the first time one of its harvest windows opens — the `CERTIFIED → ACTIVE` move is a side effect of that opening, not a call of its own, and it increments the crew's `version` by 1.

**Release.** At the end of the engagement a `HarvestManager` releases the crew, from either `CERTIFIED → RELEASED` or `ACTIVE → RELEASED`; a crew in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`. Release is a hard accounting boundary. No harvest window of the crew may be `CONFIRMED`, `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`; no pick session of any of the crew's pickers may be `OPEN`, `TALLIED` or `DISPUTED`; and the crew's `accrued_wage_cents` must be exactly 0. If any of those three fails, the release is refused with `ERR_CREW_LEDGER_NOT_CLEAR`. Release increments the crew's `version` by 1 and is terminal — a `RELEASED` crew accepts nothing further, and a newly planned harvest window that names one is refused with `ERR_CREW_MISMATCH`.

### 3.3. Planning, Confirming and Running a Harvest Window

A harvest window is the unit that brings a block and a crew together for a single day's picking. A `HarvestManager` plans one by supplying the `block_id` of the block to be harvested and the `crew_id` of the crew assigned to it, the window's `start_at` and `end_at` instants, the `forecast_brix` reading that stands in for maturity, the `planned_volume_lbs` the block is expected to yield, the `piece_rate_cents_per_clamshell` that will be paid at the row, and the `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` for the pack format in use. `forecast_brix` is a one-decimal soluble-solids reading between 0.0 and 30.0 inclusive. `planned_volume_lbs` is positive and carried to two decimals. `piece_rate_cents_per_clamshell` is a whole number of cents between 1 and 500. `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` is positive and no greater than 5, carried to two decimals. `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at` **and** must fall on the same calendar date — a window is a shift, not a multi-day campaign; both failures report `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` under priority 14 of §2.7, the strict-ordering test being made first and the same-calendar-date test second, and both are skipped in favour of the priority-15 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` whenever either instant fails to parse at all. The two instants are stored and echoed back as the exact strings the caller supplied, with fractional seconds preserved or absent exactly as they arrived. On creation the window derives `window_hours`, the elapsed time from `start_at` to `end_at` expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimals, and starts at `PLANNED` with `harvested_clamshells` 0, `packed_lbs` 0.00, no `effective_pick_rate_lbs_per_hour`, no `required_picker_count`, and neither `suspended_reason_code` nor `cancel_reason_code` set.

**Block re-entry separation.** A picked block needs a worker re-entry and spray interval before it is walked again, and that interval is twelve hours. A candidate window is compared against every *live* window of the same block — every other window on that block whose status is `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED`, `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`. Windows on other blocks never conflict, and windows that are `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored entirely, which is what makes cancelling a window free its slot immediately. The candidate conflicts with an existing window when the candidate's `start_at` is strictly earlier than that window's `end_at` advanced by twelve hours **and** the candidate's `end_at` advanced by twelve hours is strictly later than that window's `start_at`. Instants are compared exactly as they parsed, fractional seconds included rather than truncated to whole seconds, and the buffer makes adjacency a conflict: a candidate that starts exactly at another window's `end_at` is rejected, and so is one that starts eleven hours, fifty-nine minutes and fifty-nine seconds after it. The separation is checked twice — once when the window is planned and again when it is confirmed, since the surrounding schedule can change in between — and a violation is reported as `ERR_BLOCK_REENTRY_CONFLICT`.

**Confirmation and the staffing estimate.** Confirming moves a window `PLANNED → CONFIRMED` and is where the maturity forecast becomes a staffing number. The block must not be `QUARANTINED`, and the window's `forecast_brix` must be at least the minimum brix the block's variety demands — 7.0 for `STRAWBERRY`, 10.0 for `BLUEBERRY`, 8.0 for `RASPBERRY` and 8.5 for `BLACKBERRY` — compared inclusively, so a window sitting exactly on the minimum confirms while one below it is refused with `ERR_BRIX_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The block's posture is tested here only for `QUARANTINED`, so a merely `RESTRICTED` block still confirms, and the assigned crew is not examined at all — neither its status nor its `picker_count` is consulted until the window is opened, planning's refusal of a `RELEASED` crew aside.

The staffing estimate itself is forward-looking and uses no actuals: no pick session and no picker row participates. It begins from a base pick rate in pounds per picker-hour set by the block's variety — 22 for `STRAWBERRY`, 18 for `BLUEBERRY`, 12 for `RASPBERRY` and 14 for `BLACKBERRY`. That base is then adjusted for maturity: if the window's `forecast_brix` is at least the variety's *target* brix — 9.0 for `STRAWBERRY`, 12.0 for `BLUEBERRY`, 10.0 for `RASPBERRY` and 10.5 for `BLACKBERRY` — the base is multiplied by 1.10, because riper fruit picks faster; otherwise it is multiplied by 1.00 and left alone. The result, rounded half up to two decimals, is stored as `effective_pick_rate_lbs_per_hour`. Crew capacity for the window is that effective rate multiplied by the stored, already-rounded `window_hours`. Should that capacity come out at zero or below — which happens when `window_hours` itself rounds to 0.00, on a window shorter than eighteen seconds — no picker count could ever satisfy it, so the requirement is taken to be one picker beyond the cap and the confirmation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICKERS` without any figure being stored. Otherwise `required_picker_count` is `planned_volume_lbs` divided by that capacity, rounded **up** to the next whole picker, and finally raised to a floor of 1 so that a window always needs at least one picker. If the resulting `required_picker_count` exceeds the crew cap of 60 pickers, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICKERS` — a window that needs more than sixty pickers has been planned wrong. Confirmation increments the window's `version` by 1.

**Opening.** Opening moves a window `CONFIRMED → OPEN` and is the gate every picking activity sits behind. Four conditions must hold. The block's `food_safety_posture` must be exactly `CLEARED`; a `RESTRICTED` block — whether because it has never been sampled or because its water has gone `NON_COMPLIANT` — is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_CLEARED`, and a `QUARANTINED` block is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED`. The assigned crew's status must be `CERTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CREW_STATE`; that comparison is made after both block-posture gates and before the clock and picker-count tests, so a `RECRUITING`, `STAFFED` or `RELEASED` crew on a `RESTRICTED` block still reports the block first. The crew's `picker_count` must be at least the window's `required_picker_count`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PICKERS` — and that is the last of the four to be evaluated, after the clock. And the current logical clock instant must be strictly before the window's `end_at`, otherwise `ERR_TEMPORAL_ORDER_VIOLATION` — a window whose end has already passed cannot be opened retroactively. Opening increments the window's `version` by 1 and, when the crew was still `CERTIFIED`, advances it to `ACTIVE` and increments the crew's `version` by 1 as well.

**Closing.** Closing moves a window `OPEN → CLOSED` and is refused while any pick session of the window is still `OPEN` or any fruit lot of the window is still `COOLING`; in either case the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`, since the window is not yet in a condition to leave `OPEN`. Sessions that are `TALLIED`, `DISPUTED`, `APPROVED`, `SETTLED` or `VOIDED` do not hold the window open; only a still-`OPEN` one does. Closing recomputes `packed_lbs` and then `harvested_clamshells` from the surviving rows — `packed_lbs` as the sum of `net_packed_lbs` over every fruit lot of the window whose `disposition_status` is not `REJECTED`, rounded half up to two decimals, and `harvested_clamshells` by the same rule that approval uses — and increments the window's `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

**Suspension and resumption.** A window does not suspend itself; it is suspended by the block quarantine cascade described in §3.1, moving `CONFIRMED → SUSPENDED` or `OPEN → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason_code` set to `BLOCK_QUARANTINE` and its `version` incremented by 1. Only the `OPEN → SUSPENDED` path drags pick sessions with it, because a `CONFIRMED` window has none. Recovery is manual: a `HarvestManager` resumes a `SUSPENDED` window once the block's `food_safety_posture` is back to `CLEARED` — a still-quarantined block yields `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED`, and a block that has fallen merely to `RESTRICTED` yields `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_CLEARED`, the same two gates in the same order as at opening — and while the logical clock instant is still strictly before the window's `end_at`, failing which the resume yields `ERR_TEMPORAL_ORDER_VIOLATION`. Resuming clears `suspended_reason_code`, recomputes both `effective_pick_rate_lbs_per_hour` and `required_picker_count` from the same staffing rule (the forecast may now imply a different crew size) — but the crew cap of 60 is *not* re-tested here, so a resume succeeds even when the recomputed requirement exceeds it, and the crew's own status and `picker_count` are likewise not consulted — and increments the window's `version` by 1. Critically, a resume returns the window to `CONFIRMED` and never straight to `OPEN`: the whole opening gate — block posture, crew status, picker count and the clock — must be passed again.

**Cancellation.** A `HarvestManager` may cancel a window that will not be picked, supplying a `cancel_reason_code` — a required, non-empty string stored verbatim, with no enumeration and no upper length bound, whose shape is checked before the window's own status is examined. Cancellation is admitted from `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` and `SUSPENDED`, and from no other state. From `PLANNED` it is unconditional. From `CONFIRMED` and from `SUSPENDED` it additionally requires that no pick session of the window is `OPEN`, `TALLIED`, `DISPUTED` or `APPROVED`; a window still holding live or accrued sessions is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`. Cancellation stores the `cancel_reason_code`, increments the window's `version` by 1, is terminal, and frees the block's re-entry slot immediately.

### 3.4. The Picker's Day: Tallies, the Wage Guarantee and Disputes

A pick session is one worker's day on one window. The crew's own supervisor opens it, supplying the `harvest_window_id` of the window being worked, the `picker_id` of the worker, and the `work_date` on which they worked. Three coherence rules apply beyond the ownership check of §2.7: the window must be `OPEN`, so a `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED` window admits nothing and yields `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`; the `work_date` must fall within the calendar dates spanned by the window, inclusive — which, because a window is a single shift, is the one calendar date its `start_at` and `end_at` share — otherwise `ERR_WORK_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`; and the picker's `hygiene_training_expires_on` must be present and strictly after the `work_date`, otherwise `ERR_HYGIENE_TRAINING_INVALID`. Only one session may exist for a given combination of `harvest_window_id`, `picker_id` and `work_date`, whatever status the row already on file now carries — a `VOIDED`, `DISPUTED` or `SETTLED` session still occupies the key — and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`, decided before the `work_date` span test but after the hygiene test. The supplied `work_date` is stored and echoed back verbatim.

A newly opened session is an empty shell in status `OPEN`, with `quality_flag` at `PENDING`, `hours_worked` 0.00, `clamshells_filled` 0, `culled_clamshells` 0, every money figure — `effective_piece_rate_cents`, `piece_cents`, `guarantee_floor_cents`, `makeup_cents`, `gross_cents`, `housing_deduction_cents` and `net_cents` — at 0, `cull_rate` not yet determined, `tally_revision_count` 0, no `payroll_run_id`, and none of `tallied_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `dispute_reason_code` or `void_reason_code` set.

**Tallying, and pricing the row-pack.** At the end of the row the supervisor records the tally: `hours_worked`, the clock hours on the row, greater than zero and no more than 16.00, carried to two decimals; `clamshells_filled`, the clamshells accepted at the row, zero or greater; and `culled_clamshells`, the clamshells rejected at the row for grade defects, zero or greater and never more than `clamshells_filled` — a tally claiming more culls than fills is refused with `ERR_CULL_EXCEEDS_TALLY`. Culls are a *subset* of the filled count, not an addition to it. Tallying is admitted from `OPEN` and from `DISPUTED` and from nowhere else, always on an `OPEN` window, and always by the operator named as the crew's `supervisor_user_id`. Every tally, first or repeat, reprices the session from scratch in five ordered steps:

1. **Quality grading.** `cull_rate` is `culled_clamshells` divided by `clamshells_filled`, rounded half up to four decimals; when `clamshells_filled` is zero the rate is exactly 0.0000 rather than undefined. `quality_flag` becomes `MEETS_STANDARD` when `cull_rate` is less than or equal to the maximum tolerated cull rate of 0.08 — eight percent of the pack — and `BELOW_STANDARD` otherwise.
2. **The effective piece rate.** When `quality_flag` is `MEETS_STANDARD`, `effective_piece_rate_cents` is the window's `piece_rate_cents_per_clamshell` unchanged. When it is `BELOW_STANDARD`, the window rate is derated by twenty percent — that is, multiplied by the remaining eighty percent — and rounded half up to a whole cent.
3. **Piece earnings.** The saleable clamshells are `clamshells_filled` less `culled_clamshells`. `piece_cents` is that saleable count multiplied by `effective_piece_rate_cents`. Culled clamshells are never paid for.
4. **The minimum-wage guarantee, per session.** `guarantee_floor_cents` is `hours_worked` multiplied by the statutory minimum of 1650 cents per hour — sixteen dollars fifty an hour — rounded half up to a whole cent. `makeup_cents` is the amount by which the floor exceeds the piece earnings, or zero when the piece earnings already meet or beat the floor. `gross_cents` is `piece_cents` plus `makeup_cents`.
5. **The housing deduction, capped against the made-whole gross.** A housing charge is deductible only for a crew whose `labor_program` is `H2A` **and** which has a `housing_unit_code` on file; for every other crew `housing_deduction_cents` is exactly 0. Where it does apply, `housing_deduction_cents` is the lesser of the allowable daily charge of 900 cents — nine dollars a day — and ten percent of `gross_cents` rounded **down** to a whole cent. `net_cents` is `gross_cents` less `housing_deduction_cents`.

The **ordering of steps 4 and 5 is load-bearing** and is the single most easily mis-implemented rule in this specification. The wage guarantee is satisfied *first*, and only then is the deduction cap taken as a percentage of the resulting `gross_cents`. The deduction may never be applied before the make-up, and the cap may never be computed against `piece_cents` or against `net_cents`. This is what guarantees that a housing charge can never push a worker below their hourly floor: since the cap is a fraction of a gross that already includes the make-up, and it is floored rather than rounded, no rounding step can ever take more than the permitted share.

Two worked cases make the mechanism concrete. A `DOMESTIC` picker works 3.00 hours, fills 20 clamshells with none culled, at a window rate of 45 cents: the piece earnings are 900 cents, the guarantee floor is 4950 cents, the make-up is 4050 cents, gross is 4950 cents, there is no housing deduction, and net is 4950 cents — the guarantee did all the work. An `H2A` picker with housing on file works 2.00 hours, fills 10 clamshells with none culled, at the same 45-cent rate: piece earnings 450 cents, guarantee floor 3300 cents, make-up 2850 cents, gross 3300 cents; the deduction is the lesser of 900 and ten percent of 3300 floored, which is 330 cents, so net is 2970 cents.

Only one session participates in that arithmetic. No sibling session of the same picker, the same day or the same window contributes anything — which is precisely why the per-session view and the period view of §3.5 can disagree.

**Re-tallying a disputed session.** A `DISPUTED` session can be re-tallied, moving `DISPUTED → TALLIED`. Each such repeat increments `tally_revision_count` by 1, clears the `dispute_reason_code`, and recomputes every pay figure from the new tally inputs. The count is capped: a session that has already used its three permitted revisions is refused with `ERR_TALLY_REVISION_LIMIT`. The first tally out of `OPEN` does not consume a revision — the counter records only the corrections applied after the original.

**Approval.** A `PayrollClerk` approves a tallied session, moving `TALLIED → APPROVED`. Approval is the one session transition that does not consult the window at all: a session on a `CLOSED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED` window approves exactly as one on an `OPEN` window does. The session must carry pay figures, meaning its `quality_flag` is no longer `PENDING`; an untallied session reports `ERR_SESSION_NOT_TALLIED` before any status complaint is raised. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `tallied_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval stores `approved_by_user_id`, adds the session's `net_cents` to the crew's `accrued_wage_cents`, recomputes the picker's lifetime productivity, recomputes the window's `harvested_clamshells`, and increments the `version` of the session, the crew, the picker and the window each by 1.

**Disputes.** A tally can be challenged from either side of the row. A `Picker` may dispute only the session whose `picker_id` is their own identifier; the crew's `CrewSupervisor` may dispute any session of their crew, and a supervisor whose `X-User-ID` is not the `supervisor_user_id` of the crew that owns the session's window is refused with `ERR_NOT_SESSION_SUPERVISOR`. The request carries a `dispute_reason_code`, a required, non-empty string stored verbatim with no enumeration and no upper length bound, whose shape is checked before either ownership comparison is made. Disputes are admitted from `TALLIED` and from `APPROVED`, on an `OPEN` window only, and only while the session's `payroll_run_id` is still empty — a session already bound into a locked pay period is immovable and reports `ERR_SESSION_PAYROLL_LOCKED`. A dispute out of `TALLIED` simply stores the reason code and increments the session's `version` by 1. A dispute out of `APPROVED` does considerably more: the crew's `accrued_wage_cents` is reduced by exactly the same stored `net_cents` figure that approval added, `approved_by_user_id` is cleared, the picker's lifetime productivity and the window's `harvested_clamshells` are recomputed over the sessions that remain `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`, and the `version` of the session, crew, picker and window are each incremented by 1.

**Productivity, and what counts as paid.** A picker's lifetime figures are a **full recomputation** over that picker's sessions in status `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`, never an incremental delta. `total_clamshells_lifetime` is the sum, over those sessions, of `clamshells_filled` less `culled_clamshells` — the paid, saleable clamshells only. `total_hours_lifetime` is the sum of `hours_worked` over the same set, to two decimals. `avg_lbs_per_hour` divides paid pounds by paid hours: paid pounds are the sum, over those sessions, of the saleable clamshell count multiplied by the `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` of *that session's own window*, and the quotient is rounded half up to two decimals; when `total_hours_lifetime` is zero the average is exactly 0.00 rather than undefined. Sessions that are `OPEN`, `TALLIED`, `DISPUTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing, which is what makes a reversal restore the pre-approval figures exactly. A window's `harvested_clamshells`, by contrast, is the sum of `clamshells_filled` — the gross tally, culls included — over that window's sessions in status `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`.

The session lifecycle is cyclic by design: `TALLIED → DISPUTED → TALLIED` and `APPROVED → DISPUTED → TALLIED` are both legitimate paths, bounded only by the three-revision cap. `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

### 3.5. Pay Periods: Locking the Set and Settling on the Period Average

A payroll run is the crew-level closure of a stretch of work days. A `PayrollClerk` opens one by supplying the `crew_id` being settled and the inclusive date range `period_start_date` to `period_end_date`, where `period_end_date` is on or after `period_start_date` — an inverted pair is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, while a value that does not parse as `YYYY-MM-DD` at all reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead, and both are decided before the body `crew_id` is resolved. Both dates are stored and echoed back verbatim. Only one run may exist for a given combination of `crew_id`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`; a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. A new run is in status `DRAFT` with `bound_session_count` 0, none of `period_hours`, `period_piece_cents`, `period_floor_cents`, `period_makeup_cents`, `period_deduction_cents`, `settled_gross_cents`, `settled_net_cents`, `accrued_gross_cents` or `reconciliation_delta_cents` yet determined, and neither `locked_by_user_id` nor `settled_by_user_id` set.

**Locking freezes the set.** Locking moves a run `DRAFT → LOCKED` and requires that the period be quiet: no pick session of that crew with a `work_date` inside the inclusive period may still be `OPEN`, `TALLIED` or `DISPUTED`, otherwise the lock is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_UNRESOLVED_SESSIONS`. Membership of the crew is read off the *picker*, not off the window: a session counts as the run's crew's exactly when its `picker_id` names a picker whose `crew_id` is the run's `crew_id`, and sessions in the period whose status is `APPROVED`, `SETTLED` or `VOIDED` never block the lock. A crew may have only one locked run at a time, so a second concurrent lock is refused with `ERR_CREW_RUN_ALREADY_LOCKED`, and the run's `crew_id` must match the crew whose sessions are being claimed — the window of every session about to be bound must name that same `crew_id` — otherwise `ERR_CREW_MISMATCH`, which is checked after the unresolved-session and already-locked tests and only across the sessions that would actually be bound. Locking then binds the set: every pick session of that crew whose status is `APPROVED`, whose `work_date` lies inside the inclusive period, and whose `payroll_run_id` is still empty, has its `payroll_run_id` stamped with this run's `id`. Sessions of other crews are never bound, and a session already bound to an earlier run is left alone. From the stamped set the run records `bound_session_count`, `period_hours` as the sum of `hours_worked` to two decimals, `period_piece_cents` as the sum of `piece_cents`, and `accrued_gross_cents` as the sum of `gross_cents` — the per-session figure, captured for comparison. An empty stamped set records those four as 0 and 0.00 rather than leaving them unset, while `period_floor_cents`, `period_makeup_cents`, `period_deduction_cents`, `settled_gross_cents`, `settled_net_cents` and `reconciliation_delta_cents` all stay unset until the run is settled. The run stores `locked_by_user_id`, increments its own `version` by 1, and increments the `version` of every bound session by 1. Because a dispute requires an empty `payroll_run_id`, the bound set can never change afterwards.

**Settling pays the period average.** Settling moves a run `LOCKED → SETTLED` and must be performed by a different clerk: the settling `X-User-ID` must differ from `locked_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The settlement recomputes the wage guarantee over the whole period rather than day by day, and the difference is the point of the whole exercise. `period_hours` is **summed first and multiplied once**: `period_floor_cents` is `period_hours` multiplied by the same minimum of 1650 cents per hour, rounded half up to a whole cent. It is emphatically *not* the sum of the individual sessions' `guarantee_floor_cents`. `period_makeup_cents` is the amount by which `period_floor_cents` exceeds `period_piece_cents`, or zero. `settled_gross_cents` is `period_piece_cents` plus `period_makeup_cents`. The housing cap likewise applies **once, to the period gross**: the housing session count is the number of bound sessions belonging to a crew whose `labor_program` is `H2A` and which has a `housing_unit_code` on file, and `period_deduction_cents` is the lesser of 900 cents multiplied by that count and ten percent of `settled_gross_cents` rounded **down**. `settled_net_cents` is `settled_gross_cents` less `period_deduction_cents`.

`reconciliation_delta_cents` is `settled_net_cents` less the sum of the bound sessions' `net_cents` — the difference between the period view and the per-session accrual. It is signed and, by construction, zero or negative: a per-session floor is applied to every session independently, including the short ones, so the summed per-session make-ups can only ever be greater than or equal to the single period make-up. A worked pair makes the divergence explicit. Two `DOMESTIC` sessions of the same picker at a 45-cent piece rate: session A works 3.00 hours for 20 clamshells, none culled, so its piece earnings are 900 cents against a floor of 4950, a make-up of 4050, a gross of 4950 and a net of 4950; session B works 3.00 hours for 200 clamshells, none culled, so its piece earnings are 9000 cents, its floor 4950, its make-up 0, its gross and net 9000. Per session the crew accrued 13950 cents. On the period the figures are `period_hours` 6.00, `period_piece_cents` 9900, `period_floor_cents` 9900, `period_makeup_cents` 0, `settled_gross_cents` 9900, `period_deduction_cents` 0 and `settled_net_cents` 9900 — session B's surplus absorbs session A's shortfall entirely — so `reconciliation_delta_cents` is 9900 less 13950, that is −4050.

Settling then moves every bound session `APPROVED → SETTLED`, incrementing each session's `version` by 1, and reconciles the crew: `accrued_wage_cents` is decremented by the sum of the bound sessions' `net_cents`, `settled_wage_cents` is incremented by the run's `settled_net_cents`, and `reconciliation_delta_cents` is incremented by the same signed delta the run recorded. The run stores `settled_by_user_id`, and the run's and the crew's `version` are each incremented by 1. A run whose bound set is empty is a legitimate zero-pay period, not an error: every computed figure settles to 0 and the request **succeeds**. `SETTLED` is terminal.

### 3.6. Field Packing, the Cold Chain and the Repack Line

**Cooling tunnels.** A `PackhouseOperator` registers a forced-air tunnel with a `name` of 1 to 40 characters, unique across all tunnels — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` — a `slot_capacity` of 1 to 96 pallet positions, and a `target_pulp_temp_f` between 30.0 and 40.0 inclusive at one decimal — a documentary setting that no calculation reads, since the exit-temperature penalty of the shelf-life rule is charged against a fixed 34.0 °F target rather than against the tunnel's own figure. A new tunnel starts with `committed_slots` 0. Tunnels have no lifecycle: they are a capacity register that lots book against.

**Packing a lot in the field.** A `PackhouseOperator` records a finished lot with the `harvest_window_id` it was picked under, the `block_id` it came from, the `variety` and `grade` packed, the `clamshell_count` and `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` of the pack, the `pick_date`, the `pack_completed_at` instant at which the pack closed, and the `field_pulp_temp_f` measured at that moment, which starts the field-heat clock. `clamshell_count` is at least 1; `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` is greater than 0 and no more than 5 at two decimals; `field_pulp_temp_f` is between 30.0 and 110.0 inclusive at one decimal; `grade` is one of `A`, `B` or `CULL`. Four coherence rules guard the record: the lot's `block_id` must equal the window's `block_id`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_BLOCK_MISMATCH`; the lot's `variety` must equal the block's `variety`, otherwise `ERR_VARIETY_MISMATCH`; the `pick_date` must fall within the calendar dates spanned by the window, inclusive, otherwise `ERR_PICK_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`; and a `QUARANTINED` block admits no new lots, yielding `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED`. Written last, the quarantine gate is nevertheless evaluated **first** of the four under the priority ordering of §2.7, so a lot packed against a quarantined block reports `ERR_BLOCK_QUARANTINED` even when its `block_id`, `variety` and `pick_date` are wrong as well. Nothing else about the window is inspected: its `status` is not examined at all, so a lot may be packed against a `PLANNED`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` window exactly as against an `OPEN` one; the lot's `clamshell_net_weight_lbs` need not match the pack format recorded on the window; and `pack_completed_at` need bear no relation to the `pick_date` or to the window's span.

The lot's `net_packed_lbs` is `clamshell_count` multiplied by `clamshell_net_weight_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimals, and its `available_lbs` starts equal to that figure. Its `traceability_code` is composed as the block's `name`, the `pick_date` rendered as eight digits in year-month-day order, the `grade`, and a three-digit zero-padded sequence, joined by hyphens — for example `NORTH-FIELD-1-20260801-A-001`. The sequence is one greater than the number of lots that already share the same `block_id`, `pick_date` and `grade`, counted in `created_at` ascending order, so the day's second grade-A lot off the same block is `-002`. Every sibling sharing that block, date and grade counts toward the sequence whatever state it is in — a `REJECTED` or `REPACKED` sibling, and a child lot a repack produced, each occupy a number — and the code is assigned once and never recomputed, even if a sibling lot is later rejected or repacked. The `pack_completed_at` instant is stored and echoed back as the exact string the caller supplied, fractional seconds preserved or absent exactly as they arrived, as are the three cooling instants described below. Creating a lot also recomputes the parent window's `packed_lbs` by the rule stated in §3.3 and increments that window's `version` by 1. A new lot begins with `cold_chain_status` `PACKED` and `disposition_status` `UNCOMMITTED`, `food_safety_hold` false, `allocated_lbs` 0.00, `repack_out_lbs`, `cull_lbs` and `optical_reject_lbs` at 0.00, and `source_lot_id`, `cooling_tunnel_id`, `cool_start_at`, `planned_cool_end_at`, `cool_complete_at`, `entry_pulp_temp_f`, `exit_pulp_temp_f`, `pallet_slots`, `field_delay_hours`, `cool_hours`, `forecast_shelf_life_days`, `verified_shelf_life_days`, `shelf_life_expires_on` and `reject_reason_code` all unset.

**Starting a cooling run.** A `PackhouseOperator` pushes a lot into a tunnel by supplying the `cooling_tunnel_id`, the `cool_start_at` instant, the `planned_cool_end_at` instant, the measured `entry_pulp_temp_f`, the `pallet_slots` the lot will occupy — 1 to 96 — and the `expected_cooling_tunnel_version` guard described in §2.8. The lot's `disposition_status` must be `UNCOMMITTED` or `RESERVED`, its `food_safety_hold` must be false, and `cool_start_at` must be at or after `pack_completed_at` — otherwise `ERR_TEMPORAL_ORDER_VIOLATION`. `planned_cool_end_at` must be strictly after `cool_start_at`, but that pair-ordering failure is **not** a temporal-order violation: it is the priority-14 `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` of §2.7, tested before the tunnel is even resolved and skipped in favour of the priority-15 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` whenever either instant fails to parse at all. `entry_pulp_temp_f` carries no range bound of its own — unlike the pack record's `field_pulp_temp_f` it is accepted at any magnitude and merely quantized to one decimal.

Occupancy is checked as an aggregate over a half-open interval. The candidate's booked interval runs from `cool_start_at` up to but **not including** `planned_cool_end_at`. Only lots currently in `cold_chain_status` `COOLING` in that tunnel are counted, and that status is the whole of the test: a lot that has completed cooling or shipped occupies nothing, but a lot *rejected* while still `COOLING` keeps its cold-chain status and therefore goes on occupying its `pallet_slots` across its booked interval indefinitely, since rejection never releases a tunnel booking. For every instant the candidate interval touches, the sum of the overlapping lots' `pallet_slots` plus the candidate's `pallet_slots` must not exceed the tunnel's `slot_capacity`; the practical test is to take, over the candidate's interval, the maximum concurrent committed slots and check that headroom remains. A violation is `ERR_TUNNEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The tunnel's stored `committed_slots` takes no part in this test at all — it is a running total raised when a run starts and lowered when a run completes, so a lot rejected mid-run leaves it permanently elevated without that ever admitting or refusing anything. Because the interval is half-open, **back-to-back runs are legal**: a lot booking from an instant exactly equal to another lot's `planned_cool_end_at` does not overlap it and needs no headroom against it. This is deliberately the opposite of the block re-entry rule of §3.3, which adds a twelve-hour buffer and therefore treats adjacency as a conflict; the two must not be implemented with a shared comparison.

Starting a run stores the cooling fields, computes `field_delay_hours` as the elapsed hours from `pack_completed_at` to `cool_start_at` rounded half up to two decimals, stamps `forecast_shelf_life_days` from the forecast variant of the shelf-life calculation while leaving `cool_hours` itself unset until the run completes, increments the tunnel's `committed_slots` by `pallet_slots` and the tunnel's `version` by 1, moves the lot `PACKED → COOLING`, and increments the lot's `version` by 1.

**Completing a cooling run.** Completion supplies the `cool_complete_at` instant, which must be strictly after `cool_start_at`, and the measured `exit_pulp_temp_f`. It computes `cool_hours` as the elapsed hours from `cool_start_at` to `cool_complete_at` rounded half up to two decimals, stamps `verified_shelf_life_days` and `shelf_life_expires_on` from the verified variant, releases the slots by decrementing the tunnel's `committed_slots` by the lot's `pallet_slots` — floored at 0, so the counter can never go negative — and incrementing the tunnel's `version` by 1, moves the lot `COOLING → COOLED`, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. `exit_pulp_temp_f`, like `entry_pulp_temp_f`, carries no range bound and is simply quantized to one decimal. Neither the lot's `disposition_status` nor its `food_safety_hold` is consulted here, so a lot put on hold by a block quarantine part-way through its run still completes cooling normally.

**How shelf life decays.** Shelf life starts from a variety base measured in days — 7 for `STRAWBERRY`, 14 for `BLUEBERRY`, 5 for `RASPBERRY` and 6 for `BLACKBERRY` — and is reduced by four charges. The grade derate subtracts 0 days for grade `A`, 1 for grade `B` and 3 for `CULL`. The field-heat penalty charges a full day for **any part** of an hour that `field_delay_hours` runs beyond the two-hour tolerance: it is the amount by which the delay exceeds 2.0 hours, floored at zero, then rounded **up** to a whole day. The cooling penalty works identically against the four-hour seven-eighths-cool target: the amount by which `cool_hours` exceeds 4.0, floored at zero, rounded **up**. The exit-temperature penalty is charged only for **complete** two-degree steps: the amount by which the exit pulp temperature exceeds the 34.0 °F target, floored at zero, divided by 2.0 and rounded **down** to a whole day. The result is the base less the grade derate less all three penalties, and it is never allowed below a floor of 1 day. Only the one lot participates; sibling lots sharing the tunnel affect its capacity, never its quality.

The two variants differ in exactly one input. The **forecast** stamped at start-cooling measures `cool_hours` against `planned_cool_end_at` and fixes the temperature penalty at 0, because no exit temperature has been measured — it is therefore an upper bound. The **verified** figure stamped at completion measures against the actual `cool_complete_at` and computes the temperature penalty from the measured `exit_pulp_temp_f`, so a tunnel that ran long and came out warm is charged twice, once through the cooling penalty and once through the temperature penalty. For a grade-A `STRAWBERRY` lot packed at 06:00 and pushed into the tunnel at 09:30, `field_delay_hours` is 3.50 and the field penalty is 2 days; with a planned end of 13:30 the cooling penalty is 0 and the forecast is 7 − 0 − 2 − 0 − 0 = 5 days. If the same lot actually leaves the tunnel at 15:00 at 37.0 °F, `cool_hours` is 5.50 so the cooling penalty is 2, and the temperature penalty is the whole two-degree steps in 3.0 degrees, that is 1, giving a verified figure of 7 − 0 − 2 − 2 − 1 = 2 days. `shelf_life_expires_on` is then the calendar date of `cool_complete_at` advanced by `verified_shelf_life_days` days.

**The repack line.** A cooled lot may be sorted again. A `PackhouseOperator` repacks a source lot by supplying the `output_grade` the child will carry, the `output_clamshell_count` that came out good, the `cull_clamshell_count` diverted to culls and the `optical_reject_clamshell_count` thrown out by the sorter. The source lot must be `COOLED` and `UNCOMMITTED`, off `food_safety_hold`, and mass must balance: the three output counts together must equal the source lot's `clamshell_count` exactly and `output_clamshell_count` must itself be at least 1, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REPACK_MASS_BALANCE`. None of the three counts carries a numeric range bound of its own, so a negative count is not a range error but a mass-balance refusal under that same identifier. Nothing may be created or destroyed on the sorting line.

A repack **returns the newly created child lot**, not the source, with status 201. The child copies the source's `harvest_window_id`, `block_id`, `variety`, `pick_date`, `pack_completed_at`, `field_pulp_temp_f` and `clamshell_net_weight_lbs`, and — because it was cooled inside the source pallet — also copies the source's `cooling_tunnel_id`, `pallet_slots`, `cool_start_at`, `planned_cool_end_at`, `cool_complete_at`, `field_delay_hours`, `cool_hours`, `entry_pulp_temp_f`, `exit_pulp_temp_f` and `forecast_shelf_life_days`. It takes the requested `output_grade` and an `clamshell_count` equal to `output_clamshell_count`, derives its own `net_packed_lbs`, `available_lbs` and `traceability_code` — the code sequence is computed against the child's own block, pick date and grade, so a regrade lands in a different sequence family — and records the source's identifier in `source_lot_id`. The child's `verified_shelf_life_days` is **not** recomputed from the decay formula: it is the source lot's verified figure less one day for the handling, floored at 1, and its `shelf_life_expires_on` is the calendar date of the source lot's `cool_complete_at` advanced by the child's own `verified_shelf_life_days`. The child enters `cold_chain_status` `COOLED` and `disposition_status` `UNCOMMITTED` **without occupying any tunnel slot**: it is already `COOLED`, its cooling run has finished, and the tunnel's `committed_slots` is left completely unchanged even though the child names the same `cooling_tunnel_id`. The source lot stamps `repack_out_lbs`, `cull_lbs` and `optical_reject_lbs` from the three counts at the shared clamshell weight, has its `available_lbs` driven to 0.00, moves `disposition_status` `UNCOMMITTED → REPACKED`, and has its `version` incremented by 1. A repack reaches no further than those two lots: the parent window is neither recomputed nor versioned, so its stored `packed_lbs` goes on reflecting the source lot alone until some later event recomputes it — and because that recompute excludes only `REJECTED` lots, a `REPACKED` source is at that point counted in full alongside the child it produced.

**Rejecting a lot.** A `PackhouseOperator` or `FoodSafetyOfficer` may reject a lot with a `reject_reason_code` — a required, non-empty string stored verbatim, with no enumeration and no upper length bound, whose shape is checked before the lot's own state is examined. The gate is on `disposition_status` alone: `UNCOMMITTED` and `RESERVED` are admitted, and the three terminal dispositions `DELIVERED`, `REJECTED` and `REPACKED` are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE`. There is no separate `cold_chain_status` test — fruit already on the truck is `DELIVERED` and is stopped by the disposition gate — so a lot may be rejected while it is still `PACKED` and even while it is still `COOLING`, and its `cold_chain_status` is left exactly where it stood. Rejection sets `food_safety_hold` true, moves the lot to `REJECTED`, recomputes the lot's `allocated_lbs` from its surviving `RESERVED`-or-`SHIPPED` lines — which the cascade below has just emptied, so the figure lands on 0.00 — then forces `available_lbs` to 0.00 outright rather than deriving it from the mass-balance formula, recomputes the parent window's `packed_lbs` by the rule stated in §3.3, from which the rejected lot has now dropped out, and increments the lot's and the window's `version` by 1. From `RESERVED` it cascades two levels: every lot allocation of that lot in status `RESERVED`, taken in ascending `created_at` order, moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason_code` set to `LOT_REJECTED`, and each distinct buyer commitment behind those lines then has its whole exposure block **recomputed** by the §3.7 rule — `committed_lbs`, `shipped_lbs`, `open_lbs`, `fill_pct`, coverage and `risk_flag` all rebuilt from the lines that survive, never adjusted by a stored delta. The `version` of every touched allocation and commitment is incremented by 1, and a commitment standing behind two released lines of the same lot is still incremented exactly once. `REJECTED` is terminal on the disposition dimension.

### 3.7. Buyer Programs, Allocation, Shipping and Settlement

**Opening a program.** A `SalesManager` opens a buyer commitment with the `buyer_name` of the program (1 to 80 characters), the `buyer_user_id` of the buyer contact (1 to 64 characters), the `variety` the program accepts, the `volume_commitment_lbs` contracted at two decimals and greater than zero, the `price_cents_per_lb` between 1 and 10000, the `shelf_life_guarantee_days` promised at delivery between 1 and 30, and the delivery window `window_start_date` to `window_end_date`, where the end date is on or after the start date. A new commitment is `OPEN` with `committed_lbs` 0.00, `shipped_lbs` 0.00, `open_lbs` **unset** — it is deliberately not pre-filled from `volume_commitment_lbs`, and is first derived by the exposure recompute that the program's first allocation triggers — `fill_pct` 0.0000, `fill_status` `PENDING`, and `settled_revenue_cents`, `shortfall_penalty_cents` and `settled_net_cents` all unset. `risk_flag` is not fixed at `ON_TRACK` on creation either: it is evaluated immediately by the same rule the exposure recompute uses, against a coverage of 0.0000, so a program registered when the logical clock date already sits past `window_end_date` less the three-day risk lead opens `AT_RISK`, and every other program opens `ON_TRACK`.

**Pledging pounds.** A `SalesManager` allocates fruit by naming the `fruit_lot_id` pledged, the `buyer_commitment_id` being filled, the `allocated_lbs` pledged at two decimals and greater than zero, and the `expected_buyer_commitment_version` guard described in §2.8. Four commercial rules apply. The lot's `variety` must equal the commitment's `variety`, otherwise `ERR_VARIETY_MISMATCH`. The lot must not be on `food_safety_hold`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_ON_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`. `allocated_lbs` must not exceed the lot's `available_lbs`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_VOLUME_EXCEEDED` — a lot cannot be double-sold. And `allocated_lbs` must not exceed the commitment's `open_lbs`, otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_OVERBOOKED` — a buyer program may not be overbooked. The commitment must still be `OPEN`, and the lot's `disposition_status` must be `UNCOMMITTED` or already `RESERVED`. The lot's `cold_chain_status` is not examined at all, so fruit may be pledged while it is still `PACKED` or `COOLING`; only shipping demands `COOLED`. And while a program's `open_lbs` is still unset, the overbooking comparison is made instead against `volume_commitment_lbs` less `committed_lbs` — for a program with no exposure yet, the whole contracted volume.

An accepted allocation is created in status `RESERVED` with `shipped_at`, `release_reason_code`, `shelf_life_shortfall_days`, `line_gross_cents`, `line_discount_cents` and `line_net_cents` all unset, moves the lot's `disposition_status` to `RESERVED` if it was `UNCOMMITTED`, recomputes the lot's `allocated_lbs` and `available_lbs`, recomputes the commitment's `committed_lbs`, `open_lbs`, `fill_pct`, coverage and `risk_flag`, and increments the `version` of the lot and the commitment by 1. The exposure figures are always recomputed from the surviving allocation rows rather than nudged: `committed_lbs` is the sum of `allocated_lbs` over the commitment's `RESERVED` and `SHIPPED` lines, `shipped_lbs` the sum over the `SHIPPED` lines alone, and `open_lbs` is `volume_commitment_lbs` less `committed_lbs`, floored at 0.00 and carried to two decimals. `fill_pct` is `shipped_lbs` divided by `volume_commitment_lbs` at four decimals half up, and coverage is `committed_lbs` divided by the same denominator at four decimals — the two differ precisely in whether a merely reserved line counts. Coverage exists only to drive `risk_flag` and, unlike `fill_pct`, is not exposed as a field on the commitment. `risk_flag` becomes `AT_RISK` when the logical clock date is past `window_end_date` less the three-day risk lead **and** coverage is still below the ninety-five percent fill target, and `ON_TRACK` in every other case; it is re-evaluated on every allocation event — every create, ship and release, cascaded or direct — and never at settlement, which leaves standing whatever value the last exposure recompute wrote. `fill_status` is likewise untouched by these recomputes: it stays `PENDING`, however far `fill_pct` climbs, until settlement decides it.

**Shipping a line.** Shipping supplies the `shipped_at` instant of pickup and moves an allocation `RESERVED → SHIPPED`. Five conditions must hold. The lot's `cold_chain_status` must be exactly `COOLED` — fruit that is still `PACKED` or `COOLING` has no verified shelf life to sell against, and the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_COLD_CHAIN_STATE`. The lot must not be on `food_safety_hold`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_ON_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`. The `water_status` of the lot's traceability block must be exactly `COMPLIANT` — not `PROVISIONAL`, not `FAILED`, not `NON_COMPLIANT` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_WATER_NOT_COMPLIANT`; a block whose sampling record is merely provisional may pack and cool fruit but may not ship it. The logical clock date must be on or before the lot's `shelf_life_expires_on`, otherwise `ERR_SHIP_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_WINDOW`. And the commitment must be `OPEN` with the `shipped_at` date falling inside the inclusive `window_start_date` to `window_end_date` span, otherwise `ERR_SHIP_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_WINDOW` for the date and `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE` for a commitment that is no longer open. Shipping stores `shipped_at` — kept and echoed back as the exact string the caller supplied — moves the lot's `cold_chain_status` `COOLED → SHIPPED` and its `disposition_status` to `DELIVERED`, recomputes the commitment's whole exposure block from its surviving lines exactly as an allocation does (`committed_lbs`, `shipped_lbs`, `open_lbs`, `fill_pct`, coverage and `risk_flag`, the pledge simply moving from merely-committed to shipped), and increments the `version` of the allocation, the lot and the commitment each by 1. The lot's own `allocated_lbs` and `available_lbs` are deliberately **not** recomputed, because a `SHIPPED` line still counts toward `allocated_lbs` and the figures would not move. Both lot statuses turn over on the *first* line shipped, even when other `RESERVED` lines of the same lot are still outstanding: the lot is then `SHIPPED` on the cold-chain dimension, so those remaining lines can never be shipped at all — they fail the `COOLED` test with `ERR_INVALID_COLD_CHAIN_STATE` and can only be released — and `DELIVERED` is never walked back, so releasing them leaves the lot `DELIVERED`.

**Releasing a line.** A `SalesManager` releases a `RESERVED` allocation with a `release_reason_code` — a required, non-empty string stored verbatim, with no enumeration and no upper length bound. Release requires the line to be `RESERVED` and the commitment to be `OPEN`, and it inspects the lot not at all: a lot on `food_safety_hold` still releases. The pounds are put back by **recomputation from the surviving rows**, not by a stored delta: the commitment's whole exposure block is rebuilt — `committed_lbs`, `shipped_lbs`, `open_lbs`, `fill_pct`, coverage and `risk_flag` — and its `version` is incremented by 1, which for a single release amounts to removing exactly that line's `allocated_lbs` from `committed_lbs`; the lot's `allocated_lbs` and `available_lbs` are likewise rebuilt from its remaining `RESERVED`-or-`SHIPPED` lines, landing back on their pre-allocation values, and its `version` is incremented by 1 whether or not its `disposition_status` moved; and the lot's `disposition_status` returns from `RESERVED` to `UNCOMMITTED` only when no `RESERVED` or `SHIPPED` line of that lot remains — a lot pledged to two buyers stays `RESERVED` when one of them lets go, and a lot already carried to `DELIVERED` or `REJECTED` is left there. The line stores its `release_reason_code`, has its `version` incremented by 1, and is terminal in status `RELEASED`. The lot-rejection cascade of §3.6 releases lines with the reason code `LOT_REJECTED` and rebuilds the same commitment figures, but it is not otherwise this operation: it does not run the release endpoint's lot arithmetic, so the rejected lot's `available_lbs` is forced to 0.00 rather than restored and its `disposition_status` becomes `REJECTED` rather than returning to `UNCOMMITTED`. `RELEASED` lines are excluded from every subsequent figure — exposure, revenue and fill alike — so a released line leaves no trace.

**Settling the program.** A `SalesManager` settles a commitment once the delivery window has passed. Three conditions gate it: the logical clock date must be **strictly after** `window_end_date` — settling on the last day of the window is refused with `ERR_TEMPORAL_ORDER_VIOLATION`, since the buyer still has that day to take delivery — and no allocation of the commitment may still be `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`. Every reserved line must be either shipped or released before the books close. Those two are evaluated in the order written and both sit after the commitment's own `OPEN` check, so an already-`SETTLED` or `DEFAULTED` program reports `ERR_INVALID_COMMITMENT_STATE`, and one settled a day early reports the clock even when a reserved line is outstanding as well. The third condition decides which branch is taken rather than blocking: `shipped_lbs` greater than zero settles `OPEN → SETTLED`, and `shipped_lbs` of exactly zero settles `OPEN → DEFAULTED`.

On the settled branch, the shipped lines are priced **one at a time**, processed in ascending `shipped_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. For each line, `shelf_life_shortfall_days` is the amount by which the commitment's `shelf_life_guarantee_days` exceeds the lot's `verified_shelf_life_days`, floored at zero. The discount rate is five percent per shortfall day, capped at thirty percent no matter how short the fruit came in. `line_gross_cents` is `allocated_lbs` multiplied by `price_cents_per_lb`, rounded half up to a whole cent. `line_discount_cents` is `line_gross_cents` multiplied by that discount rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. `line_net_cents` is the gross less the discount. **Each line is rounded separately and only then summed**: `settled_revenue_cents` is the sum of `line_net_cents` over the shipped lines, and rounding an aggregate discount instead produces a different, wrong number. With `price_cents_per_lb` 380 and `shelf_life_guarantee_days` 5 against two shipped lines whose lots both verified 4 days — a one-day shortfall, so a five percent discount — line A of 120.50 lbs grosses 45790, discounts 2290 (45790 × 0.05 = 2289.50, rounded half up) and nets 43500, while line B of 90.30 lbs grosses 34314, discounts 1716 (1715.70 rounded half up) and nets 32598, giving `settled_revenue_cents` 76098. Discounting the combined gross of 80104 instead yields 4005 and a revenue of 76099 — off by a cent, and wrong.

`fill_status` becomes `FILLED` when `fill_pct` is at or above the ninety-five percent fill target and `SHORTFALL` otherwise. A `FILLED` program carries `shortfall_penalty_cents` of exactly 0. A `SHORTFALL` program is charged fifteen percent of the contract value of the volume it failed to ship: `volume_commitment_lbs` less `shipped_lbs`, multiplied by `price_cents_per_lb`, multiplied by the fifteen percent penalty rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. `settled_net_cents` is `settled_revenue_cents` less `shortfall_penalty_cents`. Continuing the example above with a `volume_commitment_lbs` of 220.00 and `shipped_lbs` of 210.80, `fill_pct` is 0.9582, which clears 0.95, so the program is `FILLED`, the penalty is 0 and `settled_net_cents` is 76098. Settlement stamps the four per-line figures on every shipped allocation and increments each stamped allocation's `version` by 1, stamps the commitment's `settled_revenue_cents`, `shortfall_penalty_cents`, `settled_net_cents`, `fill_pct` and `fill_status`, and increments the commitment's `version` by 1.

On the defaulted branch nothing was shipped, so there are no lines to price. `settled_revenue_cents` is 0, `fill_pct` is 0.0000, `fill_status` is `SHORTFALL`, and `shortfall_penalty_cents` is charged against the **entire** contracted volume: `volume_commitment_lbs` multiplied by `price_cents_per_lb` multiplied by the fifteen percent rate, rounded half up. `settled_net_cents` is therefore that penalty negated — a wholly negative settlement. The commitment's `version` is incremented by 1. Both `SETTLED` and `DEFAULTED` are terminal.

### 3.8. Reversals, Cascades and the Invariants They Preserve

Three invariants must hold at the end of every successful request, and every reversal in this specification exists to keep them true.

**The crew wage ledger.** A crew's `accrued_wage_cents` is, at the close of every request, exactly equal to the sum of `net_cents` over that crew's pick sessions currently in status `APPROVED`. Approval adds the session's stored `net_cents`; a dispute out of `APPROVED` — whether raised through the API or forced by the block quarantine cascade — subtracts exactly the same stored figure; and settling a payroll run subtracts the sum of `net_cents` over the sessions it moves out of `APPROVED` into `SETTLED` while adding the run's `settled_net_cents` to `settled_wage_cents`. Because a reversal always removes precisely the amount its approval added, the ledger returns to its earlier value bit for bit; it is never rebuilt by re-adding all the surviving sessions. `settled_wage_cents` only ever grows, and `reconciliation_delta_cents` accumulates the signed period-versus-session differences across the season, so a crew's lifetime numbers stay auditable. The clear-ledger gate on releasing a crew, described in §3.2, is exactly the statement that all three of these figures have come to rest.

**The void-versus-dispute asymmetry.** The quarantine cascade of §3.1 handles a session differently depending on whether it had been approved. An `OPEN` or `TALLIED` session is voided, and because it had never accrued, **no** ledger decrement is performed — subtracting for a voided session would corrupt the ledger downward. A `TALLIED` session that is voided keeps its pay figures for audit; they simply never counted. An `APPROVED` session, by contrast, is moved to `DISPUTED`, and there the exact decrement *is* applied, because that session had contributed. This asymmetry is the single most commonly mishandled part of the cascade: the cascade must branch on the session's prior status, not apply one uniform adjustment.

**Lot and commitment mass balance.** A lot's `available_lbs` is always `net_packed_lbs` less its `allocated_lbs`, `repack_out_lbs`, `cull_lbs` and `optical_reject_lbs`, at two decimals — forced to 0.00 by rejection and driven to 0.00 by a repack. A commitment's `committed_lbs` and `shipped_lbs` are always the sums over its `RESERVED`-or-`SHIPPED` and `SHIPPED` lines respectively. Releasing a line rebuilds both sides from the rows that survive — the lot's figures from its remaining `RESERVED`-or-`SHIPPED` lines and the commitment's whole exposure block from its own — which puts the lot back on its pre-allocation values and takes exactly that line's pledge out of `committed_lbs`, so the state after allocate-then-release is indistinguishable from the state before the allocation was ever made, save for the `version` counters, which only ever climb. `RELEASED` allocations participate in nothing thereafter, and allocations belonging to other commitments never participate in a commitment's arithmetic at all.

Every cascade in this specification is deterministic and ordered. Where multiple rows of one kind are affected they are processed in a stated order: the windows of a quarantined block in ascending `start_at` and then ascending `created_at`, and the sessions of a suspended window, the allocations of a rejected lot and the sessions bound to a locking run in ascending `created_at`. Every row that is touched at any depth has its own `version` incremented by exactly 1 in the same request. A caller who watches a `version` therefore sees every effect a request had on a resource, no matter how far down the chain that resource sat.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The API exposes fifty-eight endpoints across eleven resource families plus the logical clock. Status codes follow one rule: an endpoint that brings a **new** resource into existence answers **201 Created**, and every other successful call answers **200 OK**. The twelve creating endpoints are those that register a production block, record a water sample, recruit a crew, register a picker, register a cooling tunnel, plan a harvest window, open a pick session, open a payroll run, record a field-packed fruit lot, run a repack (which creates and returns the **child** lot), register a buyer commitment, and create a lot allocation. Every state transition, every recomputation, every read and every list answers 200, and each returns the resource it acted on — with the single exception of the repack, which returns the newly created child rather than the lot the request addressed. All endpoints are mounted under the `/berry-ops` prefix, and a trailing slash on any of these paths is accepted as equivalent to the form written below.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every rejection in this specification reports exactly one error identifier, chosen by the priority ordering of §2.7: the lowest-priority-numbered rule that the request violates is the one reported, and no side effect of the request is applied. The catalogue below lists all sixty identifiers with the HTTP status each carries. Missing or malformed authentication headers answer 401; a role or ownership refusal answers 403; an unresolvable path identifier answers 404; a malformed body — a missing required field, a wrong type, an unknown enum value or an out-of-range number — answers 400; a stale optimistic-concurrency version answers 412; a state, uniqueness, duty-split, capacity or cross-entity conflict answers 409; and a semantically well-formed request that violates a domain rule answers 422. The status paired with an identifier in the table below is the one every *domain* rejection carries; two transport-level refusals reuse `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` outside that pairing, because they are not domain rules at all — a request to a path this specification does not define answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, and a method that is not defined on a path that does exist answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405.
