# Vacation-Home & Snowbird Concierge API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Owners of seasonal second homes face a problem that neither a property manager nor an alarm company solves on its own: for eight or nine months of the year nobody walks through the front door. Pipes freeze, roofs leak, packages pile up on the porch, alarms trip for no reason, and a hurricane forms four hundred miles offshore while the owner is fifteen hundred miles away. The Vacation-Home & Snowbird Concierge platform is the operational system of record for a concierge firm that fills that gap — it schedules and logs property-watch visits, drives seasonal close-down and spring re-opening, manages the winterization checklist, dispatches vetted trade vendors against findings, tracks physical keys and lockbox codes, coordinates short-term-rental turnovers, holds mail, triages security incidents, and — when a named storm enters the region — runs a completely separate emergency pipeline with its own deadlines, its own approval ceiling and its own surcharge schedule.

Two structurally different pipelines run through this system and they share one numbered decision ladder. **Pipeline R**, the routine subscription watch, runs from a scheduled visit through checklist completion, findings, an optional work order, and monthly subscription billing that consumes a per-cycle included-visit entitlement, with anything beyond the entitlement priced as an overage. **Pipeline S**, the storm response, runs from a named storm event with escalating advisory tiers through a pre-storm secure sweep measured against a landfall-ETA deadline, a post-storm damage assessment measured against an all-clear SLA, emergency vendor dispatch under a raised approval ceiling, and a per-incident surcharge that sits entirely outside the subscription entitlement. The two pipelines share the labour, vendor, work-order and invoice rungs of the ladder. They diverge on five axes, and the divergences are deliberate: authorization order (Pipeline R needs Owner approval above the owner ceiling; Pipeline S auto-approves below the emergency ceiling and never consults the Owner at all), deadline arithmetic (Pipeline S subtracts a tier-selected lead from the landfall ETA and adds a fixed SLA to the all-clear instant; Pipeline R has no deadline arithmetic whatsoever), surcharge tiers (Pipeline S multiplies net service by a tier-selected percentage; Pipeline R never does), entitlement applicability (a Pipeline S visit never consumes an entitlement unit no matter how many remain), and key-custody prerequisites, where the storm pipeline is described as able to dispatch with no key issuance at all and an access-blocked marker on the assignment — a divergence that §2.12 resolves as no divergence at all, since the key-selection rule carries no pipeline condition and a routine dispatch against a key-less property succeeds on exactly the same terms.

Money is the second organising concern. Every amount in the system is an integer number of cents, every score is a decimal with an explicitly pinned number of places, and several of the arithmetic chains in this document have a **pinned evaluation order** whose alternative reading produces a different, wrong number. Where that is the case the document says so in the sentence that defines the chain. Implementers should treat the ordering statements as part of the contract, not as commentary.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint in the system unless a later section explicitly narrows it. The API is mounted under the `/property-care` prefix.

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Identity Binding

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (also HTTP 401). Once the headers are accepted, the role must appear in the endpoint's own authorized-role list; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in the header, are `PlatformAdmin`, `Owner`, `AccountManager`, `Dispatcher`, `FieldTech`, `Vendor`, `StormCoordinator` and `Auditor`.

**`PlatformAdmin`** is the platform operator. It owns configuration — service plans, checklist templates and the logical clock — and acts as a superset of `AccountManager` and `Dispatcher` across the operational surface. It is deliberately **not** permitted to accept or decline a vendor assignment, to complete a visit, to arrive on site, to dispute an invoice, or to drive the storm advisory ladder; where the endpoint tables below omit `PlatformAdmin`, that omission is intentional. It **is** listed on work-order approval and rejection, and when it acts there neither the owner-scope rung 4 nor the pipeline-mismatch rule of the paragraph below applies to it, so a `PlatformAdmin` may approve or reject on either pipeline; the storm ceiling still applies to a `STORM` work order. Its exclusive capabilities are setting the logical clock, publishing a service plan and retiring one.

**`Owner`** is the homeowner client. An Owner approves routine work orders against a per-owner ceiling, declares away periods, requests visits, drives the owner-in-residence occupancy branch, dismisses findings, extends and releases mail holds, pays invoices, and is the only role that may dispute an invoice. An Owner cannot dispatch, cannot touch vendors or keys, and has no authority at all on the storm pipeline.

**`AccountManager`** is the commercial operator: subscriptions, billing cycles, invoices and the delinquency ladder. It exclusively owns subscription activation, pause, resume, tier change, cancellation, delinquency marking and clearing, billing-cycle closure, invoice issuing, dispute resolution, invoice voiding, and the appending of a completed work order's cost to an invoice. It cannot approve a work order, cannot dispatch a vendor and cannot perform a visit.

**`Dispatcher`** is the field-operations coordinator: the seasonal axis, vendor onboarding and eligibility, work-order creation and dispatch, key custody and access grants, findings triage and incident handling. It exclusively owns seasonal transitions, vendor suspension, reinstatement and eligibility refresh, work-order dispatch, code rotation, key issuance, key return, overdue marking and loss declaration, access-grant creation, activation and revocation, assignment withdrawal, finding triage and monitoring, and visit conversion into a turnover inspection. It cannot approve a work order it created and cannot perform the visit itself.

**`FieldTech`** is the technician who physically performs the visit. Arrival and visit completion are exclusive to this role. It shares departure, finding recording, winterization steps, turnover advance and inspection failure, package logging and the post-storm damage assessment with other roles. It cannot create a work order, cannot dispatch and cannot touch billing.

**`Vendor`** is the external contractor. Accepting an offer, declining one, starting on-site work and finishing on-site work are exclusive to this role; recording work-order actuals is shared with `Dispatcher`.

**`StormCoordinator`** is the emergency-operations lead. Driving the advisory ladder, recording landfall, declaring all-clear, closing a storm event and closing a storm response are exclusive to it; declaring the storm event itself is shared with `PlatformAdmin`, and enrolling a property in a response is shared with `Dispatcher`. It approves and rejects `STORM`-pipeline work orders that the Owner is never asked about, and cannot approve a `ROUTINE` work order.

**`Auditor`** is a read-only compliance observer. It holds read access to every collection and every single-resource read in the system and holds no write permission anywhere; every non-read request under this role is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Identity binding differs by role, and it is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Owner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Owner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an owner compare the resource's owning foreign key to `X-User-ID` — directly for a property (through its `owner_id`), and transitively for an invoice (through `Invoice.owner_id`), an away period (through `AwayPeriod.owner_id`) and a work order (through its property's owner). A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Vendor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` row, and every assignment-scoped action compares `VendorAssignment.vendor_id` to `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_VENDOR` (HTTP 403). The same check guards the recording of work-order actuals.

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `AccountManager`, `Dispatcher`, `FieldTech`, `StormCoordinator` or `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule says so — as `tech_user_id` on a visit at departure, as `approved_by_user_id` on a work order at approval, as `created_by_user_id` on a work order at creation, as `swept_by_user_id` on a storm response when the secure sweep completes, and as `assessed_by_user_id` when the damage assessment is signed — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

One authorization rule is not about ownership but about **segregation of duties**: the `X-User-ID` signing a post-storm damage assessment must differ from the `swept_by_user_id` already recorded on that storm response. If the two are equal the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A second role-band rule concerns the approval fork: an `Owner` attempting to approve a work order whose pipeline is `STORM`, or a `StormCoordinator` attempting to approve one whose pipeline is `ROUTINE`, is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_PIPELINE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from real wall-clock UTC, not from the logical clock of §2.5; the logical clock governs every deadline, expiry, window and age comparison inside the domain, and never this envelope field.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request aimed at a path this document does not define, or carrying a method that a defined path does not accept, is answered in this same error envelope with `error_id` `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sort Order and Collection Filters

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, a negative `offset`, or a `limit` or `offset` that is not parseable as an integer at all, is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when more rows remain beyond the window. Unless a subsection below states otherwise, every list endpoint returns rows sorted by `created_at` ascending, and where two rows share a `created_at` instant the one created earlier in the request sequence sorts first — `id` is never a sort key anywhere in this system.

Collections also accept domain filters, and each filter is an exact-match predicate on the named field, compared as text against the stored value's own rendering (a boolean field matches the lower-case literals `true` and `false`, and a null-valued field matches an empty value or the literal `null`). A filter value that is not a member of the field's enum is not an error: it simply matches nothing and the endpoint answers an empty page with a `total_count` of zero. Owners may be filtered by `status`. Properties may be filtered by `owner_id`, `state_code`, `seasonal_state` and `occupancy_state`. Service plans may be filtered by `tier` and `status`. Subscriptions may be filtered by `property_id`, `service_plan_id` and `status`. Billing cycles may be filtered by `subscription_id` and `status`. Away periods may be filtered by `property_id` and `owner_id`. Checklist templates may be filtered by `visit_type` and `status`. Visits expose every foreign key as a filter — `property_id`, `checklist_template_id`, `away_period_id`, `storm_response_id`, `rental_turnover_id`, `billing_cycle_id` — plus `status` and `visit_type`. Findings may be filtered by `visit_id`, `property_id`, `status` and `severity`. Vendors may be filtered by `trade`, `status` and `emergency_capable`. Work orders may be filtered by `finding_id`, `property_id`, `storm_event_id`, `key_asset_id`, `status` and `pipeline`. Vendor assignments may be filtered by `work_order_id`, `vendor_id` and `status`. Key assets may be filtered by `property_id` and `key_type`. Key issuances may be filtered by `key_asset_id`, `vendor_id`, `vendor_assignment_id` and `status`. Access grants may be filtered by `key_asset_id`, `vendor_id`, `key_issuance_id` and `status`. Winterization records may be filtered by `property_id`, `visit_id` and `status`. Rental turnovers may be filtered by `property_id` and `status`. Mail holds may be filtered by `property_id` and `status`. Security incidents may be filtered by `property_id`, `visit_id`, `status` and `incident_type`. Storm events may be filtered by `region_state_code` and `status`. Storm responses may be filtered by `storm_event_id`, `property_id`, `subscription_id` and `status`. Invoices may be filtered by `billing_cycle_id`, `property_id`, `owner_id` and `status`. Invoice lines may be filtered by `invoice_id`, `visit_id`, `work_order_id`, `security_incident_id`, `mail_hold_id`, `line_type` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Version Counter

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Every entity in this system also carries `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases as the row is mutated. There is no entity in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

**Instant rendering and instant parsing are uniform across the whole surface.** Every datetime-valued field of every resource is rendered in exactly the form given above — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, with exactly three fractional digits and a literal `Z` — and that single rule covers server-minted stamps, instants the caller supplied and the server merely echoes back, and instants derived by arithmetic from caller input alike, as well as the `current_utc` the clock capabilities report. A caller-supplied `"2026-05-01T00:00:00Z"` therefore reads back as `"2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z"`, and a caller-supplied `"2026-05-01T02:00:00+02:00"` reads back normalised to UTC as `"2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z"`; two instant fields of the same resource never disagree in format. On the way in, parsing is strict: the `T` separator and an explicit zone designator (`Z`, `z`, `±HH:MM` or `±HHMM`) are both mandatory, and between zero and six fractional digits are accepted. A space separator, a bare calendar date such as `"2026-05-01"`, and a zone-less datetime such as `"2026-05-01T00:00:00"` are all rejected, and because no identifier in the catalogue names a malformed instant that rejection is the payload gate's `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE` (rung 60).

The `version` counter is the substrate for optimistic concurrency (see §2.9), and its increment discipline must be stated precisely rather than left to intuition. **A version increments once per request per mutated row, not once per field written and not once per cascade hop.** A single request that mutates a property, the winterization record beneath it and a finding on it advances three separate counters by exactly one each. A request that writes six fields on one property advances that property's counter by exactly one. Where a rule below says "increments `Property.version` by 1", that is the whole of the increment for that request; a second sentence in the same rule that also touches the property does not add a second increment.

**Some derived fields are pure read-time aggregates, and they do not participate in the version count at all.** The following are recomputed from their sources on every read rather than stored: an owner's `lifetime_credit_balance_cents`; a property's `false_alarm_count_window` and `open_critical_finding_count`; an away period's `scheduled_visit_count` and `derived_status`; a visit's `duration_minutes`; a vendor's `outstanding_key_count`; a work order's `pipeline`; a key asset's `copies_on_hand`, `copies_issued_outstanding`, `copies_lost` and `active_grant_count`; a key issuance's `overdue_days` while it is `OVERDUE`; and a storm event's `derived_tier` and `response_count`. Wherever a rule below says one of these is "re-derived" on some event, that describes the fact that it is always current — not a write. A re-derivation of one of these is never on its own a reason to write the row: no `updated_at` refresh and no `version` increment follows from it, and a request that would otherwise leave the row untouched leaves it untouched. Every other derived field named in this document is stored, and is written only at the moments the rule that defines it names.

**The response body carries post-mutation values.** Whatever a mutation writes — including the version bump, the refreshed `updated_at`, and every derived field re-computed as a consequence — is reflected in the resource object returned in `data`. A client that reads back the `version` from a successful mutation response is holding a value it can immediately use as the expected-version input of a subsequent concurrency-guarded write; it must never receive a pre-mutation snapshot.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The system runs on a logical clock, never on wall-clock time. Before any call that sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability sets it to a supplied instant carried in a `to_iso8601` field; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity, but every subsequent deadline, expiry, window and age comparison in the system reads the new value. The clock is monotonic: an attempt to set it to an instant strictly earlier than its current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 400). A read-only capability returns the current value as `current_utc` without changing it.

Every phrase in this document of the form "the logical clock", "at the moment of", "already elapsed" or "still in the future" refers to this clock and to no other source of time.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Derived Precision

All monetary amounts are **integer cents**. All rounding in this system is round-half-up applied to the absolute value with the sign preserved — that is, half away from zero, so that a value of 87.5 rounds to 88 and a value of −87.5 rounds to −88. Where a rule multiplies an integer amount by a percentage, the product is rounded once, at the point the rule names, and the rounded integer is what flows into the next step; no intermediate value is carried forward as a floating-point quantity.

Two derived quantities are decimals rather than cents and carry **one decimal place**, rounded by the same half-away-from-zero rule: the property freeze-risk score and the finding escalation score, along with the two completion percentages (checklist completion and winterization completeness). Storm surcharge percentages and the invoice-level storm surcharge percentage are fractions carried at two decimal places.

Several rounding **orders** are pinned, and each is stated where the calculation is defined: per-leg rounding in the tier-change proration, per-line rounding in the invoice tax sum, and rounding of the storm surcharge after the entitlement offset has been applied. Each of these has an alternative reading that differs by whole cents, so the order is part of the contract.

### 2.7. The Write-Gate Ladder and the Per-Endpoint Gate Subsequence

Every write in this system is filtered through a **total order of ten gates**. The gates are evaluated strictly in ascending gate number, the **first gate that fails determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status**, and no later gate is evaluated at all. This is why a request that is simultaneously malformed and aimed at a parent that does not exist returns the 404 from the existence gate and never the 400 from the payload gate — payload validation is deliberately last.

The ten gates, in order:

1. **G1 — AUTH.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present, and `X-User-Role` is one of the eight declared roles.
2. **G2 — ROLE AND SCOPE.** The acting role is listed in the endpoint's authorized roles; then, for the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` must match the owning foreign key of the target resource — an `Owner` against the property's `owner_id` chain, a `Vendor` against `VendorAssignment.vendor_id`. The role-list half of this gate runs first of everything, before G3. The scope half cannot: ownership of a row that does not exist is undecidable, so it is deferred until its target has been resolved — after the path ids of G3 for a path-addressed target, and immediately after the relevant body foreign key has resolved for a target that arrives in the body. The scope half therefore only ever fires where a target resource exists: **list endpoints are not scoped at all** — they are neither implicitly filtered by owner nor refused to an `Owner`, and an `Owner` listing a collection receives the same unfiltered page any other authorized role would.
3. **G3 — EXISTENCE.** Path ids are resolved left to right in path order; then body foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are listed in the endpoint's request body, except where the numbered ladder of §2.8 gives two of them distinct rung numbers, in which case rung order wins — on away-period declaration `owner_id` (rung 8) resolves before `property_id` (rung 9) even though the body lists the property first. A foreign key supplied with the wrong JSON type is not resolvable at all and does not produce a 404; it is left for rung 60. That holds everywhere but one site: the optional `checklist_template_id` of incident investigation treats a non-string exactly as it treats an unresolvable string and answers `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, so it is the single body foreign key whose wrong JSON type never reaches rung 60. The first id that fails to resolve decides the 404.
4. **G4 — OCC VERSION.** Where an endpoint declares an expected-version field, that value must equal the parent's current `version` integer.
5. **G5 — TARGET STATE.** The target entity's own current state must be a declared source state for the requested transition, together with those transition preconditions that reference only the target entity.
6. **G6 — PROPERTY SEASONAL.** The property's `seasonal_state` permits the operation. This band is evaluated **before** occupancy because the seasonal axis is the slower-moving one and its violations cannot be recovered inside the request.
7. **G7 — PROPERTY OCCUPANCY.** The property's `occupancy_state` permits the operation.
8. **G8 — RESOURCE ELIGIBILITY.** Two sub-bands in a declared internal order: **G8a vendor eligibility** (status, certificate-of-insurance expiry, licence expiry, emergency capability, rating floor) is evaluated **in full before** **G8b key custody** (outstanding overdue issuances, pending rekey, access-grant validity).
9. **G9 — ENTITLEMENT.** Subscription status, billing-cycle status and the included-visit entitlement balance.
10. **G10 — PAYLOAD.** Field-level validation: required fields present, JSON types, enum membership, numeric ranges, interval well-formedness, and array membership against a declared code set.

The numbered validation ladder in §2.8 is a **refinement** of this order: every rung belongs to exactly one gate, and rung numbers never cross a gate boundary out of order. Each endpoint applies only the gate subsequence listed for it below; gates absent from an endpoint's subsequence are not evaluated for that endpoint at all. A gate that is listed for an endpoint but whose rungs happen to have no applicable condition there is simply inert, and its presence in the subsequence changes nothing.

**Payload discriminators consumed by an earlier gate.** A few fields formally belong to G10 yet an earlier gate cannot be evaluated without them: `visit_type`, `action`, `to_state`, `to_status`, `step_code` and `revoke_reason` name the very transition the target-state gate is about, and the expected-version fields are the entire content of G4. The two families behave differently, and the difference is part of the contract. A **discriminator enum** is read before the gate that depends on it, and when it is absent, carries the wrong JSON type, or is not a declared member, the request is rejected at that point with the G10 identifier it earns — `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`, `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_TYPE`, or the identifier that field's membership failure carries (`ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, or `ERR_WINTERIZATION_STEP_INVALID` for `step_code`) — ahead of every gate that depends on it, the target-state and terminal checks included. It is not read ahead of G3: an unresolvable path id or body foreign key still decides the 404 first. An **expected-version field**, by contrast, is compared only when an integer is actually supplied; when it is absent or is not an integer the concurrency band is skipped entirely, the remaining gates run in their normal order, and the missing or mistyped field surfaces at G10 only if no later gate rejects the request first.

### 2.8. The Numbered Validation Ladder

Inside the gate order sits a single numbered ladder of sixty-eight rungs, shared by both pipelines. Rungs are evaluated in ascending number and **the first rung that fails decides the response**; nothing after it is evaluated. Six rung numbers carry two rows each because the same position in the order applies to two disjoint families of endpoints with different error identifiers; in those cases the two rows can never both apply to one request, so their relative order is not observable.

**Gate G1 — authentication.**

1. Both the `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers are present and non-empty, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, HTTP 401.

**Gate G2 — role and scope.**

3. The acting role appears in the endpoint's declared role list, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, HTTP 403.
4. When the acting role is `Owner`, `X-User-ID` equals the `owner_id` of the property the request targets, or of the invoice, away period, or work-order's property it targets. This applies to every **single-resource** read and every action endpoint whose role list includes `Owner` **and whose target chains to an owner** — it is therefore silent on the plan, checklist-template and storm-event reads, whose targets belong to no owner, and silent on every list endpoint, which has no single target to compare against. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, HTTP 403.
5. When the acting role is `Vendor`, `X-User-ID` equals the `vendor_id` of the targeted assignment. This applies to every assignment-scoped action — accepting, declining, starting work, finishing work and withdrawing — and additionally to the recording of work-order actuals. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_VENDOR`, HTTP 403.
6. The `X-User-ID` signing a post-storm assessment differs from the `swept_by_user_id` recorded on that storm response. This applies only to assessment sign-off. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403.

**Gate G3 — existence.**

7. The first path id resolves to an existing row of its declared entity. This applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
8. A body field `owner_id` resolves to an existing owner. This applies to property registration and away-period declaration. Otherwise `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
9. A body field `property_id` resolves to an existing property. This applies to subscription creation, away-period declaration, visit scheduling and storm-response enrolment. Otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
10. A body field `service_plan_id` resolves to an existing service plan. This applies to subscription creation and to scheduling a plan tier change. Otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
11. A body field `checklist_template_id` resolves to an existing checklist template. This applies to visit scheduling and, with the same identifier, to secure-sweep scheduling, where the field is likewise required. Otherwise `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. The third endpoint that accepts the field — incident investigation — is not covered here, because there the key is optional and therefore carries rung 14's identifier instead.
12. A body field `vendor_id` resolves to an existing vendor. This applies to key issuance and access-grant creation. Otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
13. A body field `invoice_id` resolves to an existing invoice. **Work-order invoicing is the only endpoint this rung reaches**, because it is the only one that names an invoice in its body: invoice-line appending addresses its invoice through a *path* id and therefore answers rung 7's `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown one, not this identifier. Otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.
14. Every optional foreign key supplied in the body resolves, checked **in the order the fields are listed in that endpoint's request body**. The optional keys covered by this rung are `away_period_id`, `rental_turnover_id`, `storm_response_id`, `key_asset_id`, `key_issuance_id`, `visit_id`, `security_incident_id`, `mail_hold_id` and `work_order_id`, together with three further optional keys that name an entity a numbered rung already covers when it is required but that are optional where they appear here and therefore carry this rung's identifier instead: `storm_event_id` on work-order creation, `vendor_assignment_id` on key issuance, and `checklist_template_id` on incident investigation. One key runs the other way: `rental_turnover_id` is **required** on conversion to a turnover inspection, yet it is a member of the list above and keeps this rung's identifier there, so an unknown turnover on that endpoint answers `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` and not a dedicated 404. This rung applies on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404.

**Gate G4 — optimistic concurrency.**

15. `expected_finding_version` equals the finding's current `version`, on work-order creation against a finding. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION`, HTTP 412.
16. `expected_subscription_version` equals the subscription's current `version`, on a plan tier change. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION`, HTTP 412.
17. `expected_key_asset_version` equals the key asset's current `version`, on access-grant creation and on code rotation. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_KEY_ASSET_VERSION`, HTTP 412.

**Gate G5 — target state.**

18. The target entity's current status is a declared source state for the requested transition. This applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, HTTP 409. **This rung is also the catch-all for every transition precondition that references only the target entity and that no identifier in the catalogue names.** The preconditions routed here, exhaustively, are: the `VACANT` occupancy requirement of `BEGIN_CLOSE` and of `COMPLETE_WINTERIZATION`; the requirement that `ABORT_CLOSE` find the newest winterization record in `NOT_STARTED` or `PARTIAL`; billing-cycle closure with a `DISPUTED` invoice on the cycle; work-order submission from `DRAFT` whose parent finding is not `WORK_ORDERED`; re-submission from `REJECTED` that revises neither `estimated_labour_hours` nor `estimated_parts_cents`; work-order completion whose bound assignment is not `FINISHED`; conversion to a turnover inspection whose turnover is not `CLEANING` or `INSPECTION`; an on-time key return attempted past `return_deadline_at`; a `WINDOW_ELAPSED` revocation attempted before `window_end_at` has passed; an advisory move that is not a declared advisory edge or that is attempted at or after `landfall_eta_at`; a sweep scheduled while the parent event is not `WATCH`, `WARNING` or `IMMINENT`; a turnover `CHECKOUT` or `CHECKIN` attempted before its own instant; a non-zero `package_delta` submitted against a hold still in `REQUESTED`; and an occupancy or seasonal move that is not a declared edge of its axis. These catch-all preconditions carry rung 18's identifier and status but not rung 18's position: they are tested after the source-state check itself, and where a numbered rung of the same gate also applies to the request that numbered rung is evaluated first — billing-cycle closure, for instance, tests rung 30's live-visit condition before it notices the `DISPUTED` invoice.
19. The target entity is not already in a terminal state of its machine. This applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. Otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, HTTP 409. **Rungs 18 and 19 are evaluated in the reverse of their numbering — the terminal check first, then the source-state check.** A terminal state is never a declared source state for any transition of any machine in this system, so under the literal 18-before-19 order rung 19 would be dead everywhere; the inversion is what keeps both identifiers reachable. Where a machine declares no terminal state at all — the seasonal axis, the occupancy axis, the vendor lifecycle, the winterization record, the invoice line — rung 19 has nothing to test and only rung 18 fires.
20. A visit being completed has both `departed_at` and `arrived_at` recorded. Otherwise `ERR_VISIT_NOT_ARRIVED`, HTTP 409.
21. A finding being triaged **out of `OPEN`** has a parent visit whose status is `COMPLETED`. Otherwise `ERR_PARENT_VISIT_NOT_COMPLETED`, HTTP 409. A finding with a null `visit_id` has no parent visit to inspect and is likewise refused here. The rung is confined to that one edge: the `MONITORING`-to-`TRIAGED` re-triage of rung 22 never consults the parent visit's status at all, and a re-triage therefore succeeds even where the finding's own parent visit is not `COMPLETED`.
22. A finding moving from `MONITORING` back to `TRIAGED` has a later completed visit on the same property whose `completed_at` is strictly greater than the finding's `triaged_at`. Otherwise `ERR_NO_RECHECK_VISIT`, HTTP 409.
23. A finding being dismissed has no work order whose status lies outside the pair `REJECTED` and `VOID`. Otherwise `ERR_FINDING_HAS_LIVE_WORK_ORDER`, HTTP 409.
24. A finding moving from `TRIAGED` to `MONITORING` has a severity of `LOW` or `MEDIUM`. Otherwise `ERR_SEVERITY_TOO_HIGH_TO_MONITOR`, HTTP 409.
25. A work order being dispatched has a `dispatch_attempt_count` strictly less than three. Otherwise `ERR_DISPATCH_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409.
26. A rental turnover failing inspection has a `reclean_attempt_count` strictly less than two. Otherwise `ERR_RECLEAN_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409.
27. A mail hold being extended has an `extension_count` strictly less than two. Otherwise `ERR_HOLD_EXTENSIONS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409.
28. A storm-response assessment is accepted only while the parent storm event is in status `ALL_CLEAR`. Otherwise `ERR_STORM_NOT_ALL_CLEAR`, HTTP 409.
29. A storm event being closed has every one of its responses in status `ASSESSED` or `CLOSED`. Otherwise `ERR_STORM_RESPONSES_OPEN`, HTTP 409.
30. Two disjoint rows share this position. On invoice voiding, the invoice has `amount_paid_cents` equal to zero, otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_PAID`, HTTP 409. On billing-cycle closure, no visit bound to that cycle is in status `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_LIVE_VISIT`, HTTP 409. Both rows are, on the machines as declared, unreachable, and that is a consequence of the declarations rather than an omission. Voiding declares `DRAFT` and `ISSUED` as its only source states and a draft carries no such precondition at all, while an `ISSUED` invoice can never hold a positive `amount_paid_cents` — the first payment moves it to `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID` and neither returns to `ISSUED` — so a part-paid invoice offered for voiding is stopped by rung 18 and a paid one by rung 19. "Bound to that cycle" is the `billing_cycle_id` foreign key, which a visit acquires only at the first hop of completion, by which time it is `COMPLETED`; no `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS` visit is ever bound to a cycle.

**Gate G8a — vendor eligibility, evaluated in full before G8b.**

The four therefore fire only where a vendor is *named* rather than chosen — rungs 43, 44 and 45 on assignment acceptance, and rungs 44 and 45 again at reinstatement — and rung 46, whose only declared site is dispatch, is catalogued but never emitted, joining `ERR_VISIT_TYPE_NOT_COVERED` and `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED` as a third dead identifier.

**Gate G8b — key custody, evaluated only after every G8a rung has passed.**

On grant activation this rung is evaluated after rung 51. **Gate G9 — entitlement and subscription fiscal state.**

Of the three the resume half is **unreachable**: `PAUSED` is the only source state a resume declares and nothing reaches `PAUSED` without stamping `paused_at`, so the resume's own null-check can never fail and only the pause and tier-change halves ever emit. ### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Four writes in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency, because each of them legitimately **supersedes** something the client may not have observed. Each guarded endpoint declares an expected-version field as a **required, non-optional** input, and the value must equal the parent entity's current `version` integer at the moment the request is evaluated. Endpoints not listed here take no version field at all.

**Work-order creation against a finding** requires `expected_finding_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Finding`. If any writer has advanced the finding's version since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason the guard exists is that a newly created work order voids any prior `DRAFT` work order on the same finding, so a writer holding a stale view of the finding would silently discard work it never saw. On success the finding's `version` increments by exactly one, once for the request, and the finding row returned by any subsequent read carries the post-mutation value.

**Scheduling a plan tier change** requires `expected_subscription_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Subscription`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because a tier change supersedes any pending tier change and re-derives the open cycle's entitlement grant, so a stale writer would overwrite an entitlement re-derivation it never observed. On success the subscription's `version` increments by exactly one for the request — not once per affected field and not once per cascade hop into the billing cycle — and the mutated subscription returned in `data` carries that post-mutation value.

**Access-grant creation on a key asset** requires `expected_key_asset_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `KeyAsset`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_KEY_ASSET_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because grant creation on a `LOCKBOX` key rotates the code and invalidates every grant stamped with an earlier rotation index, so a stale writer would hand out a code snapshot the rotation has already retired. The same expected-version field, the same comparison and the same error identifier also guard explicit code rotation. On success the key asset's `version` increments by exactly one for the request, however many grants that one request invalidates.

**Signing a post-storm damage assessment** requires `expected_storm_response_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `StormResponse`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORM_RESPONSE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — the identifier the catalogue previously lacked, minted to match the shape and status of the other three. The comparison happens inside the concurrency band, after the segregation-of-duties check of rung 6 and before any target-state rung, so a stale version beats `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and `ERR_STORM_NOT_ALL_CLEAR`. On success the storm response's `version` increments by exactly one for the request.

In all four cases the count is the same: **one increment per mutated row per request.** A request that mutates the guarded parent and also mutates children advances the parent's counter once and each child's counter once. And in all four the comparison is only performed when an integer is actually supplied: an absent or wrong-typed expected-version field leaves the concurrency band with nothing to compare and the request continues down the remaining gates, as §2.7 describes.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Eleven identities must hold after every mutation. They are the safety net that catches an arithmetic chain implemented in the wrong order.

One qualification governs three of them. Visit billing exclusivity, storm guarantee conservation and turnover absorption neutrality each speak of a `VISIT_OVERAGE` or `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` line as though it existed, and the entitlement-unit identity speaks of an `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` line the same way — but no transition anywhere creates any of those three line types (see F2). The amounts they name, `billable_amount_cents` on the visit and `guarantee_credit_cents` on the storm response, are computed and stored by the transitions that produce them; the corresponding invoice line is the operator's to append through the line-appending capability. Those three identities therefore describe the state of a correctly kept ledger once the operator has appended the line, not an automatic consequence of the transition, and the system does not enforce them.

**Entitlement unit conservation.** Inside one billing cycle, `entitlement_granted_units` plus `entitlement_carried_in_units`, minus `entitlement_consumed_units`, minus `entitlement_remaining_units`, is always zero. There is no compensating action that restores a consumed unit: voiding the `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` line on the invoice removes the money but does not return the unit, and the identity therefore holds at every instant regardless — with **one declared exception**. The tier-change re-derivation of §3.2 applies a floor of zero to `entitlement_remaining_units` after subtracting the consumption, so a downgrade onto a plan whose grant plus the carry-in is smaller than what has already been consumed leaves the identity short by exactly the shortfall the floor swallowed. That is the floor doing its job, not an arithmetic error; the identity is to be read as holding everywhere the floor has not bitten.

**Entitlement rollover conservation.** Once a cycle reaches `CLOSED`, its `entitlement_remaining_units` equals `entitlement_carried_out_units` plus `entitlement_expired_units`. Opening the next cycle immediately afterwards sets that cycle's `entitlement_carried_in_units` to exactly the closed cycle's `entitlement_carried_out_units`.

**Invoice line-sum conservation.** An invoice's `subtotal_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over its `ACTIVE` lines whose `line_type` is not `GUARANTEE_CREDIT`, plus the invoice's `storm_surcharge_cents`, plus the sum of `amount_cents` over its `ACTIVE` `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` lines. Appending a line and then voiding it restores `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents` to their pre-add values exactly.

**Key copy conservation.** For every key asset, `copies_total` equals `copies_on_hand` plus `copies_issued_outstanding` plus `copies_lost`. Issuing copies and then recording their return returns `copies_on_hand` and `copies_issued_outstanding` to their pre-issuance values.

**Visit billing exclusivity.** For every visit in status `COMPLETED`, exactly one of two things is true: either `entitlement_applied` is true and there is no `ACTIVE` `VISIT_OVERAGE` line naming that visit, or `entitlement_applied` is false and there is exactly one such line. A completed visit is never both entitlement-consuming and overage-billed, and never neither.

**Storm guarantee conservation.** The sum of `guarantee_credit_cents` over storm responses equals the sum, over `ACTIVE` `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` lines on the invoices of the responded properties, of the negated `amount_cents`. A response that reaches `SECURED_LATE` issues zero and appends no credit line at all, so the owner's `total_cents` is exactly the no-guarantee total.

**Work-order cost conservation.** A work order's `total_cents` equals `labour_cost_cents` plus `parts_cost_cents` plus `markup_cents` plus `storm_surcharge_cents`. Invoicing it creates exactly one `ACTIVE` line whose `line_type` is `WORK_ORDER` and whose `amount_cents` equals that total. The corollary that "voiding that line and re-invoicing reproduces the same amount" is **not exercisable**: invoicing drives the work order to the terminal `INVOICED`, so a second invoicing call answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at rung 19 whether or not the first line has been voided. Voiding the line therefore leaves the work order billed and the invoice unbilled, with no declared path back.

**Access-grant count conservation.** A key asset's `active_grant_count` equals the number of grants on it in status `ACTIVE`. A code rotation invalidates every grant **in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`** whose `rotation_index_at_grant` is strictly less than the new `code_rotation_count` — a grant already `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED` or `INVALIDATED` keeps the terminal status and the reason it already had — and re-derives `active_grant_count` inside the same request.

**Turnover absorption neutrality.** After a watch visit is converted into a turnover inspection, `entitlement_applied` is false, the billing cycle's `entitlement_consumed_units` is unchanged, and there is exactly one `ACTIVE` `VISIT_OVERAGE` line for that visit whose `amount_cents` is half the scheduled base visit fee. Deferring instead of converting leaves both the entitlement counter and the invoice untouched.

**False-alarm ledger conservation.** The sum of `amount_cents` over `ACTIVE` `FALSE_ALARM_FEE` lines on a property's invoices equals the sum of `false_alarm_fee_cents` over that property's incidents in status `FALSE_ALARM` whose `reported_at` falls inside the rolling 365-day window. Two declared behaviours qualify it, and neither is a defect: a first occurrence carries a fee of zero and appends no line, contributing nothing to either side; and the append happens only where the property has a `DRAFT` invoice at that instant, so a reclassification against a property with no draft invoice writes `false_alarm_fee_cents` on the incident and leaves the line side short by exactly that amount, permanently. Like the three identities qualified above, this one therefore describes a correctly kept ledger rather than an enforced invariant. Reclassifying an incident back out of `FALSE_ALARM` is not a declared transition, so the ledger is append-only.

**Proration day conservation.** For a tier change, the used-day count plus the remaining-day count is always thirty, and the cycle's `prorated_fee_cents` is the sum of the two independently rounded legs. A tier change followed by a change back on the same day reproduces the original plan's entitlement grant, but not necessarily the original `prorated_fee_cents`, because each leg rounds independently.

### 2.12. Two Pipelines Through One Ladder

A work order's `pipeline` is a derived discriminator, not an input: it reads `STORM` when the work order carries a `storm_event_id`, and `ROUTINE` otherwise. That single field selects the approval ceiling, the surcharge branch and the key-custody prerequisite, and it is worth restating the five divergences precisely because everything else about the two pipelines is shared.

First, **authorization order**. On the routine pipeline the ceiling is the acting owner's `approval_ceiling_override_cents` when that value is present, and 50000 cents otherwise, and the approver must be the `Owner` role. On the storm pipeline the ceiling is a flat 250000 cents, the owner override is structurally not consulted at all, and the approver is the `StormCoordinator`. Second, **deadline arithmetic**. The storm pipeline computes a pre-storm deadline by subtracting a tier-selected lead from the landfall ETA, and a post-storm deadline by adding a fixed SLA to the all-clear instant; the routine pipeline has no deadline arithmetic anywhere. Third, **surcharge tiers**. A storm work order multiplies its pre-surcharge cost by a tier-selected percentage frozen at dispatch; a routine work order never applies a surcharge and its `storm_surcharge_cents` is always zero. Fourth, **entitlement applicability**. A visit bound to a storm response never consumes an entitlement unit regardless of how many units remain. Fifth, **key custody** — and this is the one nominal divergence that turns out not to be one. The system-level description of the two pipelines reads as though the storm pipeline may dispatch with `access_blocked_flag` true and no key issuance while the routine pipeline may not, but the key-selection rule that actually stamps that flag carries no pipeline condition, and it is the rule that governs. **Both pipelines behave identically**: when no key asset of the property has `rekey_pending_flag` false and `copies_on_hand` at least one, no key issuance and no access grant are created, the new assignment is stamped `access_blocked_flag` true, and the dispatch itself still succeeds — on a `ROUTINE` work order exactly as on a `STORM` one (see F12). No identifier in the catalogue names a key-less routine dispatch, and none is needed.

### 2.13. Open Friction Points

The specification contains a number of places where two clauses pull in different directions or where a stated rule has no derivable trigger. Each is recorded here together with the reading this system takes, because the tension is worth naming even once it has been settled. **Every point below is now adjudicated; none is left to the implementer.** Where the adjudication changes or sharpens a rule stated elsewhere in §2 or §3, that rule has been amended in place as well, and the two must be read as saying the same thing.

**F1 — the storm-response assessment carries an expected version with no declared rejection code.** Signing a post-storm damage assessment requires `expected_storm_response_version` as a required input, and the assessment endpoint's gate subsequence includes the optimistic-concurrency band. *Determined:* the field is required; a mismatch against the storm response's current `version` must reject the request inside the concurrency band, before any target-state rung is evaluated; and on success the storm response's `version` increments by exactly one. *Adjudicated:* the rejection carries `ERR_STALE_STORM_RESPONSE_VERSION` with **HTTP 412**, a fourth identifier minted for this guard and added to the catalogue in §5 alongside the three that cover findings, subscriptions and key assets. Reusing one of those three would have named the wrong parent entity, and answering 409 through a state identifier would have broken the uniform 412 the other three concurrency rejections carry. The comparison is evaluated inside the concurrency band — after the segregation-of-duties check of rung 6, before every target-state rung — so it pre-empts `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and `ERR_STORM_NOT_ALL_CLEAR`; but, like the other three, it is compared only when an integer is actually supplied, so an absent or wrong-typed `expected_storm_response_version` falls through to the payload gate instead.

**F2 — invoice lines that the conservation invariants require but no side effect creates.** The visit-billing-exclusivity invariant asserts that a completed visit with `entitlement_applied` false has exactly one `ACTIVE` `VISIT_OVERAGE` line, and the turnover-absorption-neutrality invariant asserts the same for a converted visit. The storm-guarantee invariant likewise ties `guarantee_credit_cents` to `ACTIVE` `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` lines, and the entitlement-unit invariant speaks of an `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` line as the thing a compensating void would remove. *Determined:* the amounts themselves — `billable_amount_cents` on the visit and `guarantee_credit_cents` on the storm response — are computed and stored by the transitions that produce them. *Adjudicated:* **no transition appends any of the three.** No visit transition, no storm-response transition and no entitlement rule declares an invoice-line side effect for `VISIT_OVERAGE`, `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` or `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET`, and the absence is taken at face value: those three line types reach an invoice only when an operator appends them through `POST /invoices/{id}/lines`. The line types that *are* appended automatically by a declared side effect are exactly four — `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE` at draft-invoice opening, `WORK_ORDER` at work-order invoicing, `FALSE_ALARM_FEE` at false-alarm reclassification when the fee is strictly positive, and `MAIL_DISPOSITION` on the forward edge from `ACTIVE` — and no other line type is ever created except by that endpoint. The consequence for §2.11 is stated there: the three identities that presuppose one of these lines hold of a correctly kept ledger, not of an automatic cascade.

**F3 — the rekey-pending condition has two competing codes.** Rung 50 bundles two conditions under one identifier: it requires both a sufficient `copies_on_hand` and a false `rekey_pending_flag`, and names `ERR_KEY_COPIES_UNAVAILABLE` as the rejection. Separately, `ERR_KEY_REKEY_PENDING` exists with the condition "an issuance is requested on a key with `rekey_pending_flag` true". *Determined:* an issuance on a key with a pending rekey is refused. *Adjudicated:* the two conditions are **split**, and the rekey condition is tested first, so a pending rekey answers `ERR_KEY_REKEY_PENDING` and only an insufficient `copies_on_hand` answers `ERR_KEY_COPIES_UNAVAILABLE`. Folding both under rung 50's single identifier would have left `ERR_KEY_REKEY_PENDING` — a catalogue entry whose stated condition is exactly this one — permanently dead. Rung 49 still runs ahead of both, so a vendor holding an `OVERDUE` or `LOST` issuance is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_KEY_OVERDUE` before either key condition is looked at.

**F4 — access-grant validity versus grant window.** Rung 52 bundles a stale rotation index and a clock outside the grant's closed window under `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_INVALID`. Separately, `ERR_GRANT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` exists with the condition "the logical clock lies outside the grant's closed window". *Determined:* both situations are refused. *Adjudicated:* the two conditions are **split**, with the rotation index tested first. A stale `rotation_index_at_grant` answers `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_INVALID`; a clock outside the closed window answers `ERR_GRANT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The same split governs a vendor starting on-site work: no `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` grant at all for that vendor on any key asset of the property answers `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_INVALID`, and grants that exist but none of which contains the clock answer `ERR_GRANT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Within grant activation the whole of rung 51 is evaluated before either, so a capacity excess is reported ahead of both.

**F5 — dispatch of a still-pending work order.** Rung 18 would answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` for a dispatch attempted from `PENDING_APPROVAL`, because the only declared source state for dispatch is `APPROVED`. `ERR_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` exists with exactly that condition. *Determined:* the dispatch is refused with HTTP 409. *Adjudicated:* it carries **`ERR_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`**, the identifier whose catalogue condition is exactly this state, and it is evaluated between the terminal check and rung 18 — so a dispatch attempted from `PENDING_APPROVAL` answers `ERR_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, one attempted from `INVOICED` or `VOID` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and one attempted from `DRAFT`, `REJECTED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Rung 25's dispatch-attempt cap is tested after all three.

**F6 — invoice issuing against a cancelled subscription.** Rung 58 bundles "at least one `ACTIVE` line" and "the subscription is not `CANCELLED`" under `ERR_INVOICE_HAS_NO_LINES`, while `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED` exists as its own code. *Determined:* issuing on a cancelled subscription is refused. *Adjudicated:* it carries **`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`**. The two conditions live in different gate bands — the cancellation check is the G5 gate of §2.9 and the line-set check is rung 58 in G9 — and the issuing endpoint declares both bands, so the G5 identifier fires first and rung 58 is left to answer only a genuinely empty `ACTIVE` line set.

**F7 — the credential rungs at vendor reinstatement.** Reinstatement requires an unexpired certificate of insurance, an unexpired licence and a rating of at least 3.0, and the reinstatement endpoint's gate subsequence includes the vendor-eligibility band. *Determined:* the three preconditions; and that a rating below the floor answers `ERR_VENDOR_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR`. *Adjudicated:* **all three preconditions are enforced at reinstatement, each with its own identifier**, in rung order — an expired `coi_expires_at` answers `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED` (rung 44), then an expired `license_expires_at` answers `ERR_VENDOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (rung 45), then a `rating` below 3.0 answers `ERR_VENDOR_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR`. The catalogue conditions for the two expiry codes are endpoint-agnostic and cover reinstatement, and the alternative — leaving two stated preconditions unenforceable, or routing them to the rating identifier — would have contradicted §3.6. Rung 43 does not apply here: reinstatement's declared source states are `ONBOARDING` and `SUSPENDED`, neither of which is `ELIGIBLE`.

**F8 — the mail-forward invoice line on the overflow branch.** Forwarding a hold from `ACTIVE` runs the forward fee variant **and** appends a `MAIL_DISPOSITION` line to the property's draft invoice when one exists. Forwarding a hold from `OVERFLOW` runs the same fee variant but its declared side-effect list stops there, with no line append. *Determined:* both branches compute `disposition_fee_cents` identically. *Adjudicated:* the asymmetry is **deliberate and is preserved exactly as declared**. Forwarding from `ACTIVE` writes the fee and appends a `MAIL_DISPOSITION` line to the property's draft invoice when one exists; forwarding from `OVERFLOW` writes the identical fee and appends nothing, whether or not a draft invoice exists. Symmetrising would have added a line that the `OVERFLOW` edge's declared effect list does not contain.

**F9 — two rules with no derivable trigger.** `ERR_VISIT_TYPE_NOT_COVERED` fires when an entitlement-consuming visit type is scheduled that is absent from the plan's `covered_visit_types` "and the property has no other billing route" — but the entitlement rule simply falls through to overage pricing whenever the type is not covered, so a second billing route always exists. `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED` fires when "an operation that explicitly requires a remaining entitlement unit" finds zero remaining — but no endpoint declares such a requirement; visit completion falls through to overage instead. *Determined:* nothing forces either rejection on any declared path. *Adjudicated:* **neither rule is enforced and neither identifier is ever emitted.** No code path in this system raises `ERR_VISIT_TYPE_NOT_COVERED` or `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED`. An uncovered visit type schedules normally and is priced as an overage at completion; an exhausted entitlement balance is likewise priced as an overage, with `entitlement_applied` false. Both identifiers remain in the catalogue of §5 with their declared HTTP statuses, and both are dead. Inventing a trigger would have refused schedulings and completions that the entitlement rule explicitly lets fall through.

**F10 — an unreachable owner status.** `Owner.status` is declared with the two members `ACTIVE` and `INACTIVE`, and registration sets it to `ACTIVE`. No transition, endpoint or side effect anywhere writes `INACTIVE`. *Determined:* the enum's membership, and that owners may be filtered by `status`. *Adjudicated:* **an owner never becomes inactive.** `status` is stamped `ACTIVE` at registration and no endpoint, transition or side effect writes it again — the owner-revision capability does not accept it — so `INACTIVE` is a declared member with no writer and is unreachable. The `status` filter on the owner collection stays exactly as declared, an exact-match predicate that simply never matches anything but `ACTIVE`. Inventing a deactivation path would have been a side effect no sentence in this document authorises.

**F11 — the freeze-risk fields of a freshly registered property.** `freeze_risk_score` is declared with a default of 0.0 and `risk_posture` with a default of `NORMAL`, yet property registration is declared to run both derivations, and a brand-new property has no temperature reading, a `winterization_completeness_pct` of 0.0 and an `occupancy_state` of `VACANT` — which the weighted formula turns into 25.0 plus 15.0, that is 40.0, and therefore a posture of `ELEVATED`. *Determined:* the formula, its three terms and the banding thresholds. *Adjudicated:* **the derivation wins over the column defaults.** Registration runs both calculations, so a brand-new property is read back with a `freeze_risk_score` of **40.0** and a `risk_posture` of **`ELEVATED`** — a delta term of 0.0 because no reading exists, a winterization term of 25.0 because the completeness is 0.0, and a vacancy term of 15.0 because the home is born `VACANT`. The declared defaults of 0.0 and `NORMAL` are the column initialisers the derivation immediately overwrites inside the same request; they are never observable. The registration response still carries `version` 1, because running a declared derivation on a row created in the same request is not a second mutation of it.

**F12 — whether the routine pipeline may dispatch with no key.** The pipeline description asserts that the storm pipeline may dispatch with `access_blocked_flag` true and no key issuance and that the routine pipeline may not. The key-selection rule, which is the only place the flag is written, contains no pipeline condition at all: when no key asset of the property has `rekey_pending_flag` false and `copies_on_hand` at least one, no key issuance and no access grant are created, the new assignment is stamped `access_blocked_flag` true, and the dispatch itself succeeds. *Determined:* what happens on the storm pipeline, and the exact candidate filter and ranking of key selection. *Adjudicated:* **the key-selection rule governs and it has no pipeline condition, so a routine dispatch against a property with no eligible key succeeds exactly as a storm dispatch does** — the assignment is created in `OFFERED`, `access_blocked_flag` is stamped true, and no key issuance and no access grant are created. Nothing is refused, and no identifier is needed. The pipeline description in §1 and §2.12 has been corrected to match.

**F13 — the ordering of un-numbered gates against numbered rungs.** §2.9 pins each un-numbered rejection to a gate band, and the band ordering is total. *Determined:* the band each un-numbered gate belongs to, and therefore its position relative to every rung of a different band. *Adjudicated:* the intra-band order is fixed as follows, and it is chosen so that no declared identifier is made unreachable by the choice.

Inside **G5** the default is: rung 19's terminal check, then rung 18's source-state check, then the band's numbered rungs in ascending order, then the band's un-numbered gates, and last the catch-all preconditions that rung 18 absorbs (see rung 18). Four endpoints depart from that default, and only four. On **work-order dispatch**, `ERR_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` is evaluated between the terminal check and rung 18. On **rental-turnover advance**, `ERR_TURNOVER_STAGE_INVALID` occupies rung 18's own position — it covers every target that is not the declared successor of the current stage — and rung 18 is then left to carry only the two clock preconditions of `CHECKOUT` and `CHECKIN`. The remaining two invert the tail of the default, running rung 18's catch-all **before** the band's un-numbered gate rather than after it: on **conversion to a turnover inspection** the requirement that the turnover be `CLEANING` or `INSPECTION` is tested ahead of `ERR_NO_TURNOVER_OVERLAP`, so a turnover in a wrong stage answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when the windows also fail to overlap; and on **secure-sweep scheduling** the requirement that the parent event be `WATCH`, `WARNING` or `IMMINENT` is tested ahead of `ERR_STORM_REGION_MISMATCH`, so an out-of-region property against a `FORECAST` event answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Some endpoints run no rung 18 or rung 19 check at all, because the row they guard is not the row whose machine is being driven, and they simply run their un-numbered gates in order: a plan tier change and the opening of the next billing cycle each test `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED` first and a tier change then tests `ERR_PLAN_RETIRED`; appending an invoice line tests only `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DRAFT`; voiding an invoice line tests only `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_VOID`; and retiring a service plan tests nothing at all, so a plan already `RETIRED` is retired again and answers 200. One endpoint sits between the two shapes: **extending a mail hold** runs rung 19's terminal check and then goes straight to rung 27, with no source-state check of its own, so a hold still in `REQUESTED` may be extended.

Inside **G6**, `ERR_PROPERTY_HAS_OPEN_STORM_HOLD` is evaluated **before** rungs 34 to 37, because the storm hold gates every seasonal transition while those four rungs are each specific to one action.

Inside **G8a**, rungs 43, 44 and 45 run first and `ERR_VENDOR_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR` last. Inside **G8b**, `ERR_KEY_REKEY_PENDING` runs before rung 50 (both after rung 49), rung 51 runs before rung 52, and inside rung 52 `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_INVALID` runs before `ERR_GRANT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Several other permutations of these — rung 50 ahead of the rekey check, for instance — would make a declared identifier dead, which is why this one is the order.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Homeowners, Homes and the Two Independent Property Axes

A homeowner client is registered with a `full_name`, an `email`, a `phone` and a `primary_residence_state` holding the two-uppercase-letter code of the owner's *other*, non-vacation residence. Two optional switches shape everything that follows. `snowbird_flag`, false unless supplied, declares that this owner migrates seasonally and unlocks cadence-driven absence scheduling. `notification_preference`, defaulting to `EMAIL` and otherwise taking `SMS` or `BOTH`, selects the channel used for incident notification. A third optional input, `approval_ceiling_override_cents`, replaces the platform-wide work-order approval ceiling for this owner alone; when it is left null the platform default of 50000 cents applies, and when supplied it must be zero or greater. Registration always stamps `status` to `ACTIVE` — the enum's other member is `INACTIVE`, which nothing in this system ever writes (see F10) — and `lifetime_credit_balance_cents` to zero. An owner's `email` must be unique across all owners and must contain an at-sign; a duplicate, or an address with no at-sign, is rejected with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_VIOLATION`. The two-uppercase-letter shape of `primary_residence_state` is a description of the intended input and not a validated constraint: no identifier in the catalogue names a malformed state code, and any string is accepted. Later, an operator may revise `phone`, `notification_preference`, `approval_ceiling_override_cents` and `snowbird_flag`; each such revision advances the owner's `version` by one, whether or not any stored value actually changes. A field present in the revision body is written exactly as supplied and a field absent from it is left untouched, and an explicitly null value counts as present: sending `approval_ceiling_override_cents` as null clears the per-owner ceiling and returns the owner to the platform default of 50000 cents. `email`, `full_name`, `primary_residence_state` and `status` are not revisable and are ignored if sent.

`lifetime_credit_balance_cents` is never written directly. It is the sum of the absolute values of `amount_cents` over every `ACTIVE` invoice line whose `line_type` is `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` on invoices carrying this owner's `owner_id`, and it is re-derived whenever such a line is created or voided — as a read-time aggregate in the sense of §2.4, so neither the creation nor the void advances `Owner.version`.

A vacation home is registered against an `owner_id` with a human `label` such as "Naples Beach House", an `address_line`, a two-uppercase-letter `state_code` — the code later matched against a storm event's region — and a `travel_distance_miles`, the road distance in miles from the concierge depot, which must be zero or greater. Two optional inputs complete the row: `freeze_setpoint_f`, the minimum interior temperature in Fahrenheit the home must hold, defaulting to 55 and constrained to the inclusive range from 40 to 80; and `rental_program_enrolled`, false unless supplied, marking the home as participating in short-term rental turnovers. The same four attributes — `label`, `travel_distance_miles`, `freeze_setpoint_f` and `rental_program_enrolled` — are the only ones an operator may later revise, and each revision advances the property's `version` by one, whether or not any stored value actually changes. A field present in the revision body is written exactly as supplied and a field absent from it is left untouched; the freeze-risk pair is re-derived on every revision regardless of which fields were supplied, while `travel_zone` is re-derived only when `travel_distance_miles` is itself present in the body. As with an owner's `primary_residence_state`, the two-uppercase-letter shape of `state_code` — and of a storm event's `region_state_code` — describes the intended input rather than a validated constraint; no identifier names a malformed code and any string is accepted.

**Travel zone.** `travel_zone` is derived from `travel_distance_miles` whenever that distance is written, at registration and again on revision, and every comparison is inclusive at the upper edge. A distance of at most 10 miles is `Z1`; otherwise a distance of at most 25 miles is `Z2`; otherwise a distance of at most 60 miles is `Z3`; otherwise the zone is `OUT_OF_AREA`. Ten miles exactly is `Z1` and 10.1 is `Z2`; 25.0 is `Z2`; 60.0 is `Z3` and 60.1 is `OUT_OF_AREA`. The zone carries a flat travel surcharge used later in visit pricing: nothing at all in `Z1`, 1500 cents in `Z2`, 3800 cents in `Z3`. An `OUT_OF_AREA` property cannot hold an active subscription, so the surcharge table never needs a fourth entry.

**The two axes.** Every home carries two lifecycle states that evolve completely independently of one another on the same row. `seasonal_state` starts at `OPEN` and runs as a closed cycle through `CLOSING`, `WINTERIZED` and `OPENING` back to `OPEN`. `occupancy_state` starts at `VACANT` and is star-shaped: every occupied state is entered from `VACANT` and returns to `VACANT`. Certain visit types are legal only in specific *combinations* of the two, which is why the seasonal band of the gate order is evaluated strictly before the occupancy band — the seasonal axis moves slowly and its violations cannot be repaired inside a request, whereas an occupancy conflict often can be.

The occupancy axis has six edges. An owner moves the home from `VACANT` to `OWNER_IN_RESIDENCE`, permitted only while `seasonal_state` is `OPEN` or `OPENING`; the move defers every scheduled `ROUTINE_WATCH` visit on the property, stamping each with a `defer_reason` of `OWNER_IN_RESIDENCE`, and advances the property's `version` by one. The owner returns the home from `OWNER_IN_RESIDENCE` to `VACANT` with no precondition at all; the return re-derives the freeze-risk score and posture, because the vacancy weight now applies again, and advances `version` by one. A dispatcher moves the home from `VACANT` to `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`, permitted only when `rental_program_enrolled` is true and `seasonal_state` is exactly `OPEN`, advancing `version` by one; the reverse edge from `RENTAL_OCCUPIED` to `VACANT` has no precondition, re-derives the freeze-risk score and posture, and advances `version` by one. The remaining pair is driven not by an occupancy request but by vendor activity: a vendor starting on-site work moves the home from `VACANT` to `CONTRACTOR_ONSITE`, permitted when the assignment's work order names this property, advancing `version` by one; and a vendor finishing on-site work moves it from `CONTRACTOR_ONSITE` back to `VACANT`, permitted only when no other assignment on the property is still `WORKING`, re-deriving the freeze-risk score and posture and advancing `version` by one. A move to `RENTAL_OCCUPIED` that fails its enrolment or seasonal precondition is rejected with `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_ENROLLED`; a move to `OWNER_IN_RESIDENCE` attempted while the home is `CLOSING` or `WINTERIZED` is rejected with `ERR_SEASONAL_STATE_FORBIDS_OCCUPANCY`.

**Freeze risk.** `freeze_risk_score` and `risk_posture` are **stored** columns, not read-time derivations, and the set of moments that recompute them is closed. They are recomputed at property registration; on every property revision; whenever a completing visit records an interior temperature; on every winterization step submission and every record reopening; on the `COMPLETE_WINTERIZATION` and `BEGIN_OPEN` seasonal actions and on no other seasonal action; and **on entry into `VACANT` from an occupied state, by whichever driver performs it** — an owner returning the home, a dispatcher returning it from `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`, a turnover reaching `CHECKOUT` while the home was `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`, or a vendor finishing the last live assignment on the property. Entry *into* an occupied state never recomputes them: the asymmetry is deliberate, because the vacancy term is the one that changes, and it is only when vacancy is regained that the stored score is stale in the direction that matters. Between two recomputations the stored pair is what every read returns, including the dedicated risk-posture read. Three independent terms feed the weighted score. The first term reads `last_interior_temp_f`, the most recent interior reading stamped from a completing visit alongside the `last_temp_reading_at` instant of that reading; if no reading exists the term is 0.0, and otherwise the shortfall is the property's `freeze_setpoint_f` minus that reading, floored at zero, multiplied by 6.0, and then capped at 60.0. The second term reads `winterization_completeness_pct`: if that percentage is 100.0 or greater the term is 0.0, and otherwise it is 25.0 multiplied by one minus the percentage divided by one hundred. The third term is 15.0 when `occupancy_state` is exactly `VACANT`, and 0.0 in every other occupancy state. The three terms are added, the sum is rounded to one decimal place, and `freeze_risk_score` is the smaller of that rounded sum and 100.0. Worked: a setpoint of 55 with a reading of 48, a winterization completeness of 50.0 percent and a vacant home gives a shortfall of 7.0, a first term of 42.0 after the cap comparison, a second term of 12.5, a third term of 15.0, and a score of 69.5.

Immediately after every score run, `risk_posture` is banded from it: a score of at least 60.0 is `CRITICAL`, otherwise a score of at least 30.0 is `ELEVATED`, otherwise the posture is `NORMAL`. Both comparisons include the boundary, so exactly 30.0 is `ELEVATED` and exactly 60.0 is `CRITICAL`.

**The other derived property fields.** `winterization_completeness_pct` mirrors the `completeness_pct` of the property's winterization record with the greatest `created_at`; where two records share that instant the one with the greater `completed_step_count` wins, and where they still tie the one created later in the request sequence wins; a property with no winterization record at all reads 0.0. The same "newest record" rule, with the same two tie-breaks, is what every other clause below means by "the newest winterization record". Like the freeze-risk pair, it is a **stored** column rather than a read-time derivation, and it is re-derived only where a rule below says so: on `ABORT_CLOSE`, on `BEGIN_OPEN`, on every winterization step and every reopen, and written directly to 100.0 by `COMPLETE_WINTERIZATION`. Opening a fresh winterization record — whether by `BEGIN_CLOSE` or through the standalone capability — does **not** re-derive it, so the stored percentage may briefly describe an older record than the newest one. `false_alarm_count_window` counts the property's incidents in status `FALSE_ALARM` whose `reported_at` lies within 365 days of the logical clock. `open_critical_finding_count` counts the property's findings whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is one of `OPEN`, `TRIAGED`, `WORK_ORDERED` or `MONITORING`. Those two, unlike the three above, are **pure aggregates computed at read time**, so they are always current without being written; the sentences elsewhere in this document that say one of them is "re-derived" on a finding creation, triage, dismissal or work-order transition describe that currency and not a write — no such request mutates the property row, and none of them advances `Property.version`. `storm_hold_flag` is a stored column, true while at least one storm response for this property sits in `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED`, `SECURED`, `SECURED_LATE`, `MISSED_DEADLINE`, `AWAITING_ALL_CLEAR` or `ASSESSMENT_DUE`; it is set true when a secure sweep is scheduled and re-derived when a secure-sweep visit completes, when a storm response is closed and when a storm event is closed, and at no other moment — so a response that has reached `ASSESSED` leaves the flag standing until one of those closures runs. A seasonal transition attempted while the flag is true is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_HAS_OPEN_STORM_HOLD`. A dedicated computed read exposes the risk decomposition as an object carrying `freeze_risk_score`, `risk_posture`, `winterization_completeness_pct`, `open_critical_finding_count`, `false_alarm_count_window` and `last_interior_temp_f`.

### 3.2. Plans, Subscriptions, Billing Cycles and the Entitlement Ledger

A service plan is published with a `tier` of `BASIC`, `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, a strictly positive `monthly_fee_cents`, an `included_visit_units` count of zero or more, and a non-empty `covered_visit_types` array. The reference tiers are a basic plan at 9986 cents a month granting two included visits, a standard plan at 19986 cents granting four, and a premium plan at 34944 cents granting eight. `entitlement_rollover_policy` defaults to `EXPIRE` and otherwise takes `CARRY_CAPPED`; it is the discriminator for what happens to unspent units at cycle close. `storm_guarantee_included`, false unless supplied, marks the plan as carrying the storm-readiness guarantee that can earn a credit. `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and otherwise takes `RETIRED`. Every member of `covered_visit_types` must also be one of the three entitlement-consuming visit types — `ROUTINE_WATCH`, `SEASONAL_CLOSE` and `SEASONAL_OPEN` — and an empty array or a member outside that set is rejected with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_VIOLATION`. Retiring a plan sets its `status` to `RETIRED` so that no new subscription may reference it; subscriptions already in force keep the plan. Retirement carries no state precondition of its own — a plan already `RETIRED` is simply retired again, answering 200 and advancing the plan's `version` by one — and there is no un-retire edge.

A subscription binds a `property_id` to a `service_plan_id` from a `started_on` instant, and is born in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`. No billing cycle exists until activation. Activation requires the plan's `status` to be `ACTIVE` — a retired plan is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_RETIRED` — and requires the property's `travel_zone` to be anything other than `OUT_OF_AREA`, an out-of-area property being rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_OUT_OF_AREA`. Activation creates the first billing cycle with `cycle_index` 1, a `period_start` equal to the subscription's `started_on` and a `status` of `OPEN`; sets `current_cycle_id` to that cycle's id; sets the cycle's `entitlement_granted_units` to the plan's `included_visit_units`, its `entitlement_carried_in_units` to zero and its `entitlement_remaining_units` equal to the grant; and sets the cycle's `prorated_fee_cents` to the plan's `monthly_fee_cents`.

Nothing stops a property carrying more than one subscription row, so the phrase **"the property's subscription"**, wherever it appears in this document without further qualification, needs a resolution rule: it is the **most recently created subscription on that property whose status is not `CANCELLED`**, falling back to the most recently created row of all when every one of them is cancelled. That is the rule the visit-scheduling gate and the visit-completion cascade use. Two places do not use it. Storm-response enrolment states its own filter — the property's subscription in `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `DELINQUENT`, the most recently created one when several match — and binds null when none matches. And the storm sweep settlement reads the **subscription already bound to the response** in preference to anything else, falling back to the resolution rule above only when the response's `subscription_id` is null or no longer resolves; a response enrolled while the property was covered therefore keeps settling against that same subscription even after a newer one has been created.

A billing cycle's `period_end` is its `period_start` plus thirty days, exclusive. Its ledger carries `entitlement_granted_units`, `entitlement_carried_in_units`, `entitlement_consumed_units`, `entitlement_remaining_units` — always the grant plus the carry-in minus the consumption — and, once closed, `entitlement_expired_units` and `entitlement_carried_out_units`, both of which read zero while the cycle is not `CLOSED`. `closed_at` records the closing instant. A cycle's `cycle_index` is a one-based ordinal assigned in ascending `period_start` order within its subscription.

**Pause and resume.** Pausing an active subscription requires `current_cycle_id` to be non-null and that cycle to be `OPEN`; a missing open cycle is rejected with `ERR_NO_OPEN_CYCLE`. The pause stamps `paused_at` to the logical clock and reprorates the open cycle under the mid-cycle-pause branch: the paused-day count is the whole days from `paused_at` to the earlier of the logical clock and the cycle's `period_end`, taken as the floor of the elapsed seconds divided by 86400, then clamped to at least zero and at most thirty; and `prorated_fee_cents` becomes the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by thirty minus that paused-day count, divided by thirty, rounded once to whole cents. Because `paused_at` is stamped to the clock in the same request, the paused-day count is always exactly zero at the moment of pausing, so the pause always rewrites `prorated_fee_cents` to the in-force plan's full `monthly_fee_cents` — discarding whatever an earlier tier-change proration had left there. The reduction only appears later, when the resume re-runs the same branch over the now-closed pause interval. If a draft invoice exists on that cycle, its `ACTIVE` subscription-fee line has its `amount_cents` rewritten to the new prorated figure and the invoice's `gross_service_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents` are recomputed. Finally, every visit on the property in status `SCHEDULED` whose `scheduled_start_at` falls at or after `paused_at` and strictly before the cycle's `period_end` is deferred with a `defer_reason` of `SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSED`.

Resuming requires `paused_at` to be non-null, and a resume attempted without one is likewise rejected with `ERR_NO_OPEN_CYCLE`. The resume stamps `resumed_at` to the logical clock, adds the whole days of the closed pause interval to `cumulative_paused_days` — the floor of that one interval's seconds divided by 86400, never below zero, accumulated interval by interval rather than by summing seconds across pauses and flooring once at the end — and reprorates the open cycle under the same mid-cycle-pause branch. `paused_at` is **not** cleared by the resume — it stays where the pause put it, which is what lets the reproration measure the interval — so a second pause simply overwrites it. Where the subscription has no `OPEN` cycle at the moment of the resume there is nothing to reprorate: the resume still succeeds, moving the subscription to `ACTIVE` and accumulating the paused days, and only the cycle-level rewrite is skipped. **The resume deliberately does not un-defer the visits the pause deferred**; those must be rescheduled explicitly.

**Tier changes.** Scheduling a plan tier change supplies the new `service_plan_id` together with `expected_subscription_version`, and it takes effect on the open cycle immediately while also superseding any value already sitting in `pending_tier_change_plan_id`. Because it takes effect on the open cycle, an open cycle is a precondition: a subscription with none — one still in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, or one whose cycle has been closed — is rejected with `ERR_NO_OPEN_CYCLE`, the same identifier rung 55 uses for pause and resume, and that check sits in the G9 band after the cancellation and plan-retirement gates of G5. A cancelled subscription is rejected with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED` and a retired target plan with `ERR_PLAN_RETIRED`, in that order. On success the request writes the new plan into **both** `service_plan_id` and `pending_tier_change_plan_id`: the first makes the new tier the in-force plan at once, the second is what the next cycle will read when it opens, and neither is cleared afterwards. The proration for a tier change is a different shape from the pause branch. The used-day count is the whole days from the cycle's `period_start` to the logical clock, taken as the floor of elapsed seconds divided by 86400 and clamped to at least zero and at most thirty; the remaining-day count is thirty minus that. **The rounding order is pinned: each leg is rounded to whole cents on its own and only then are the two legs added.** The first leg is the old plan's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by the used-day count and divided by thirty, rounded; the second is the new plan's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by the remaining-day count and divided by thirty, rounded; `prorated_fee_cents` is their sum. Worked: a change from the standard tier at 19986 to the premium tier at 34944 with seven used days rounds 4663.4 to 4663 and 26790.4 to 26790, for 31453 — where summing first and rounding once would have produced 31454, a one-cent divergence.

The same request re-derives the open cycle's entitlement, and **that order is pinned too**: the cycle's `entitlement_granted_units` is replaced by the new plan's `included_visit_units` first; `entitlement_remaining_units` is then re-derived as the new grant plus `entitlement_carried_in_units` minus `entitlement_consumed_units`; and only then is a floor of zero applied to the result. Applying the floor to the grant instead would stop a downgrade from ever pushing the remaining count below what has already been consumed. The subscription's `version` advances by one.

**Delinquency.** Marking a subscription delinquent requires at least one invoice on its cycles in status `ISSUED` with a positive `balance_due_cents` whose `issued_at` plus twenty-one days is at or before the logical clock — the threshold is inclusive, so exactly twenty-one days after issue already qualifies. The mark stamps `delinquent_since` to the logical clock and applies a late fee to every qualifying issued invoice: `late_fee_cents` becomes that invoice's `balance_due_cents` multiplied by 0.015 and rounded to whole cents, and the invoice's `total_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are recomputed. Worked: a balance of 85271 at day twenty-one yields 1279.065, rounded to 1279. An invoice that does not qualify keeps a `late_fee_cents` of zero. Clearing delinquency requires that no issued invoice on the subscription's cycles still carries a positive balance, and simply clears `delinquent_since` back to null.

**Cancellation.** A subscription may be cancelled from `ACTIVE`, from `PAUSED` or from `DELINQUENT`, and in all three cases the precondition is the same: no visit on the property may be `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`, a live visit being rejected with `ERR_VISIT_IN_FLIGHT`. Cancellation stamps `cancelled_at` to the logical clock and force-closes the open cycle with `entitlement_carried_out_units` driven to zero regardless of the plan's rollover policy. From the `ACTIVE` source state the cycle passes through `OPEN` to `CLOSING` to `CLOSED` inside the same request, running the rollover with the carry-out forced to zero, and `current_cycle_id` is cleared to null. From `PAUSED` and from `DELINQUENT` the cycle likewise reaches `CLOSED` with `entitlement_carried_out_units` forced to zero, and `current_cycle_id` is cleared on all three source states alike — including where the subscription had no `OPEN` cycle to close in the first place, in which case the cancellation simply skips the close and still succeeds. The forced carry-out of zero sends the whole remainder to `entitlement_expired_units`, whatever the plan's rollover policy says. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

**Closing a cycle and opening the next.** Closing a billing cycle moves it from `OPEN` to `CLOSING` and, inside the same request, from `CLOSING` to `CLOSED`; `CLOSING` is transient within the call. The first leg requires that no visit bound to the cycle is `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS` and freezes `entitlement_consumed_units` and `entitlement_remaining_units`. The second leg requires every invoice on the cycle to be in one of `ISSUED`, `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `VOID` or `DRAFT` — `DISPUTED` is the only status that list excludes, and a disputed invoice is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` as one of the rung-18 catch-alls, after rung 30's live-visit test; it runs the rollover, sets `closed_at` to the logical clock, and clears the subscription's `current_cycle_id` when it pointed at this cycle. The rollover itself is discriminated by the in-force plan's `entitlement_rollover_policy`. Under `EXPIRE` the whole remainder is destroyed: `entitlement_carried_out_units` is zero and `entitlement_expired_units` is the remainder, and the next cycle's carry-in is zero regardless of how large the remainder was. Under `CARRY_CAPPED` the carry-out is the smaller of the remainder and two, and only the excess is destroyed. The two branches diverge whenever any remainder exists at all.

Opening the next cycle for a subscription supplies a `period_start`, and is refused on a `CANCELLED` subscription with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`; every other subscription status may open a cycle. There is no precondition on the state of the cycles already there: an existing `OPEN` cycle does not block the call, a second `OPEN` cycle is simply created alongside it, and `current_cycle_id` is repointed at the new one. The new cycle's `cycle_index` is the previous maximum plus one; its `entitlement_carried_in_units` is set to the `entitlement_carried_out_units` of the highest-indexed existing cycle exactly, and to zero when no earlier cycle exists; its `entitlement_granted_units` is set from the in-force plan, applying `pending_tier_change_plan_id` when one is present; its `entitlement_remaining_units` is that grant plus the carry-in; its `prorated_fee_cents` is that same plan's `monthly_fee_cents` in full, since a cycle that opens at its own `period_start` has nothing to prorate; its `period_end` is `period_start` plus thirty days; and the subscription's `current_cycle_id` is pointed at it.

### 3.3. Absence Windows, Checklist Templates and Scheduling a Visit

A snowbird absence is declared as an away period naming a `property_id` and an `owner_id`, an inclusive `start_at`, an exclusive `end_at` and a requested `cadence_days` between watch visits, which must be at least 3 and at most 30. The declared `owner_id` must equal the property's own `owner_id`, and the window may span at most 240 days; either violation is rejected with `ERR_AWAY_PERIOD_INVALID`. An away period has no state machine of its own — its `derived_status` is read from the logical clock on every read, and is `PLANNED` while the clock is strictly before `start_at`, `IN_EFFECT` while the clock is at or after `start_at` and strictly before `end_at`, and `ENDED` once the clock reaches `end_at`.

`projected_visit_count` is a forward-looking figure and it is computed differently on the initial declaration than on a later revision. At declaration, the away-day count is the whole days between `start_at` and `end_at`, taken as the floor of the elapsed seconds divided by 86400, and the projection is the floor of that away-day count divided by `cadence_days`. Worked: a ninety-day absence at a cadence of fourteen projects six visits. On a mid-period revision the calculation is anchored differently: the anchor instant is the later of the logical clock and `start_at`; the remaining-day count is the whole days from that anchor to `end_at`, again by flooring seconds divided by 86400; and the new projection is `scheduled_visit_count` plus the floor of the remaining-day count divided by the new `cadence_days`. Worked: thirty days into the same absence with two visits already on the books, revised to a cadence of seven, the remaining-day count is sixty and the projection becomes two plus eight, which is ten. A revision is **non-retroactive**: already-scheduled visits are never cancelled or re-timed, and instead are counted into the new projection. Each revision advances `cadence_revision_count` by one. `scheduled_visit_count` itself counts the visits bound to this away period whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`.

A checklist template is published for one `visit_type` with an author-supplied `version_label` such as "2026.1" and a non-empty `item_codes` array whose members are unique. `critical_item_codes`, optional, is a subset of `item_codes` naming the items whose failure automatically raises a critical finding; when it is absent or null it is stored as an empty array, never as null. `default_finding_category`, defaulting to `OTHER`, is the `category` stamped on every finding auto-created from a failed critical item of this template, and takes any of `PLUMBING`, `HVAC`, `ELECTRICAL`, `STRUCTURAL`, `SECURITY`, `PEST`, `LANDSCAPE` or `OTHER`. `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and otherwise takes `RETIRED`. `required_item_count` is not supplied; it is set to the length of `item_codes` at publication. Three of the shape statements just made are descriptions rather than validated constraints, because no identifier in the catalogue names their violation: `item_codes` is required and must be an array of strings, but its non-emptiness and the uniqueness of its members are **not** checked; the subset relation between `critical_item_codes` and `item_codes` is **not** checked; and `status`, when supplied, is stored exactly as given without a membership test. An empty `item_codes` array is therefore accepted and yields a `required_item_count` of zero. The seven `visit_type` members are `ROUTINE_WATCH`, `SEASONAL_CLOSE`, `SEASONAL_OPEN`, `TURNOVER_INSPECT`, `STORM_SECURE`, `STORM_ASSESS` and `ALARM_RESPONSE`.

Scheduling a visit names a `property_id`, a `checklist_template_id`, a `visit_type`, a `trigger_source`, and the inclusive `scheduled_start_at` and exclusive `scheduled_end_at` of the planned window; the end must be strictly after the start. Three optional links may be supplied: an `away_period_id` naming the cadence window that produced the visit, a `storm_response_id` set only on the storm pipeline, and a `rental_turnover_id` when the visit is bound to a turnover. `trigger_source` takes `SCHEDULED`, `OWNER_REQUESTED`, `ALARM_TRIGGERED` or `STORM`, and it is the discriminator for the base fee. The bound template's own `visit_type` must equal the submitted `visit_type` and the template must be `ACTIVE`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_TYPE_MISMATCH`. A new visit is born `SCHEDULED`, with `entitlement_applied` false, `billable_amount_cents` zero, `after_hours_flag` false, `deferral_count` zero, `checklist_completion_pct` 0.0, empty `completed_item_codes` and `failed_item_codes`, and a null `billing_cycle_id`, `departed_at`, `arrived_at`, `completed_at`, `tech_user_id`, `interior_temp_f` and `defer_reason`.

**Turnover overlap at scheduling.** Every scheduling and rescheduling request is checked against every rental turnover of the property whose status is neither `CHECKIN` nor `CANCELLED`. Each such turnover is padded by ninety minutes on both sides: the padded low edge is its `checkout_at` less ninety minutes and the padded high edge is its `checkin_at` plus ninety minutes. The overlap test is **half-open and strict**: the visit collides when its `scheduled_start_at` is strictly before the padded high edge **and** its `scheduled_end_at` is strictly after the padded low edge. Touching endpoints therefore do not collide — a visit ending exactly at the padded low edge does not overlap, and one starting exactly at the padded high edge does not overlap either. A collision at scheduling or rescheduling is rejected with `ERR_TURNOVER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409.

### 3.4. Executing a Visit, and the Cascade that Completion Fires

A technician departs, which advances the visit from `SCHEDULED` to `EN_ROUTE`. Departure is the point at which the seasonal and occupancy bands are **re-evaluated** — they are not checked only at scheduling — and it stamps `departed_at` and copies the acting `X-User-ID` into `tech_user_id`. Arrival advances the visit from `EN_ROUTE` to `IN_PROGRESS`, requires `departed_at` to be non-null, stamps `arrived_at` to the logical clock, and freezes `after_hours_flag`. That flag reads the UTC hour field of `arrived_at` alone, ignoring minutes and seconds, and is true when the hour is 22 or later or strictly earlier than 6: an arrival at 21:59 is not after hours, one at 22:00 is, one at 05:59 is, and one at 06:00 is not.

Completion carries `completed_item_codes` and `failed_item_codes` — the checklist items marked pass and fail — and an optional `interior_temp_f` reading, null when the visit type does not read temperature. The two arrays must be disjoint, every member of each must appear in the bound template's `item_codes`, and their combined length must equal the template's `required_item_count`; any violation is `ERR_CHECKLIST_MISMATCH`. Completion is refused with `ERR_VISIT_NOT_ARRIVED` if either `departed_at` or `arrived_at` is missing. Because the combined length is forced to equal `required_item_count`, `checklist_completion_pct` — one hundred multiplied by the combined length of the two arrays and divided by `required_item_count`, rounded to one decimal place — lands on exactly 100.0 for a successful completion in every case but one: a template published with an empty `item_codes` array has a `required_item_count` of zero, the division is undefined, and the percentage is written as **0.0** instead. That is the sole completion that reads back below 100.0. A read of a visit also exposes a duration in whole minutes under the field name **`duration_minutes`**: the floor of the seconds between `arrived_at` and `completed_at` divided by sixty, and zero when either instant is missing. The field is present on every visit read, not only on completed ones, so a visit that has not yet arrived or not yet completed simply reads zero.

Completion is the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs in a fixed order.

**Hop one.** The visit becomes `COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped, and `billing_cycle_id` is stamped with the `OPEN` cycle of the property's subscription — null when no such cycle exists.

**Then the entitlement decision, before any pricing.** The visit consumes one entitlement unit exactly when *all* of the following hold: an `OPEN` billing cycle exists on the property's subscription; that subscription's `status` is `ACTIVE`; the visit's `trigger_source` is `SCHEDULED`; the visit's `visit_type` appears in the in-force plan's `covered_visit_types`; the visit's `visit_type` is one of `ROUTINE_WATCH`, `SEASONAL_CLOSE` or `SEASONAL_OPEN`; the visit's `storm_response_id` is null; and the cycle's `entitlement_remaining_units` is at least one. The boundary is inclusive, so a cycle with exactly one unit left still consumes. When all of that holds, `entitlement_applied` is set true, the cycle's `entitlement_consumed_units` rises by one and its `entitlement_remaining_units` falls by one. Otherwise `entitlement_applied` is set false and no counter moves. **Consumption order is pinned**: units are consumed strictly in ascending visit `completed_at`, and where two visits share a `completed_at` the one with the smaller `created_at` consumes first. No other ordering key is consulted.

**Then the price.** The base fee is discriminated by `trigger_source`. A `SCHEDULED` visit has a base of 6500 cents and no expedite component, and this is the only branch that can ever consume entitlement. Any other trigger source has a base of 11000 cents, with an expedite component of 2500 cents added only when the trigger source is exactly `OWNER_REQUESTED` and zero for `ALARM_TRIGGERED` and `STORM`. The travel surcharge is the flat figure for the property's `travel_zone`. `billable_amount_cents` then follows one of two structurally different branches, discriminated by `entitlement_applied`. On the **entitled** branch the base fee and the expedite component are both waived and the billable amount is the travel surcharge alone; the after-hours multiplier is structurally absent from that expression, because there is no base for it to multiply. On the **overage** branch the after-hours multiplier of 1.35 is applied **to the base fee only** — never to the expedite component and never to the travel surcharge — when `after_hours_flag` is true, with the product rounded to whole cents; the multiplied-or-plain base, the expedite component and the travel surcharge are then added as flat addends. Worked: a scheduled visit in `Z2`, not after hours, on the overage branch, is 6500 plus nothing plus 1500, which is 8000. The same visit on the entitled branch is 1500. An owner-requested visit in `Z3` arriving after hours is 11000 multiplied by 1.35 and rounded to 14850, plus 2500, plus 3800, which is 21150.

**Hop two.** For every code that appears in `failed_item_codes` and also in the template's `critical_item_codes`, a finding is created with `severity` `CRITICAL`, `status` `OPEN`, a `category` equal to the template's `default_finding_category`, and a `description` that is exactly the literal text "Auto-created from failed critical checklist item " followed by the item code. These are processed **in the order the codes appear in the template's `item_codes`**, not in the order they appear in `failed_item_codes`.

**Hops three and four, on the storm pipeline only.** A critical finding raised on a `STORM_ASSESS` visit is auto-triaged to `TRIAGED` and produces a work order with `work_type` `STORM_MITIGATION`, the storm event bound through `storm_event_id`, an `estimated_labour_hours` of 2.0 and an `estimated_parts_cents` of 25000, submitted straight to `PENDING_APPROVAL`. That storm-pipeline work order, if its `total_cents` is at or below 250000, auto-approves with `approved_by_user_id` set to the literal string "system" and auto-dispatches over the window running from the logical clock to twenty-four hours later, creating a vendor assignment in `OFFERED`. Three details of this continuation are worth pinning. The work order takes its `storm_event_id` from the **storm response bound to the completing visit**, so a `STORM_ASSESS` visit that carries no `storm_response_id` produces a `ROUTINE` work order instead, and a routine one never auto-approves here however small its total — it rests in `PENDING_APPROVAL` for a human. The auto-approve threshold consulted at this point is the flat storm ceiling of 250000 and not the submitting fork of §3.5, so the work order does not auto-approve at submission and then again here; it is submitted straight to `PENDING_APPROVAL` and lifted to `APPROVED` only by the storm test. And the auto-dispatch is **best-effort**: when vendor selection returns an empty candidate set the dispatch is simply skipped, the work order stays `APPROVED` with `dispatch_attempt_count` still zero, no assignment is created, and the visit completion that fired the cascade still succeeds — a side effect never fails its parent request.

**Hops five through eight.** The new assignment creates a key issuance on the key asset chosen by the key-selection rule; the issuance creates an access grant; the grant rotates the lockbox code, advancing the key's `code_rotation_count`; and the rotation invalidates every access grant whose `rotation_index_at_grant` is strictly less than the new rotation count.

**The remaining completion effects.** When `interior_temp_f` is supplied, the property's `last_interior_temp_f` and `last_temp_reading_at` are stamped from it and the freeze-risk score and posture are re-derived. A temperature breach is detected by a **strict** comparison: the reading is a breach when it is strictly less than the property's `freeze_setpoint_f` minus a tolerance band of 3 degrees. With a setpoint of 55 the trip point is 52.0, so 52.0 is not a breach and 51.9 is. A breach creates a security incident with `incident_type` `FREEZE_ALARM`, `severity` `HIGH` and `status` `REPORTED`, whose `visit_id` names the completing visit and whose `reported_at` is the logical clock. When the visit's `visit_type` is `STORM_SECURE`, the bound storm response has its `secured_at` and `swept_by_user_id` stamped and is settled to `SECURED` or `SECURED_LATE` by the timeliness rule; where the visit carries no `storm_response_id`, or the response it names is not in `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED`, the settlement is skipped in silence and the completion still succeeds. The completion then re-derives the property's `storm_hold_flag`, which a settlement to `SECURED` or `SECURED_LATE` leaves standing. When the visit's `visit_type` is `SEASONAL_CLOSE` and a winterization record for the property sits in `NOT_STARTED`, `PARTIAL` or `REOPENED`, that record's `visit_id` is bound to this visit — the **earliest-created** such record, and only that one, even where several qualify.

**The three ways out that are not completion.** A dispatcher may **defer** a scheduled visit, moving it from `SCHEDULED` to `DEFERRED` and supplying a `defer_reason` from `TURNOVER_OVERLAP`, `OWNER_IN_RESIDENCE`, `WEATHER`, `ACCESS_BLOCKED` or `SUBSCRIPTION_PAUSED`; deferral advances `deferral_count` by one and records the reason, leaves `billable_amount_cents` at zero and consumes no entitlement unit. A visit in `DEFERRED` is returned to `SCHEDULED` by rescheduling, which supplies a fresh `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, re-runs the turnover-overlap test against the new window, and clears `defer_reason` back to null. A dispatcher may **convert** a scheduled visit into a rental turnover's inspection, supplying the `rental_turnover_id`; the target turnover must be in `CLEANING` or `INSPECTION` and the visit's window must overlap that turnover's padded window, an absent overlap being rejected with `ERR_NO_TURNOVER_OVERLAP`. Those two are tested **in that order** — the stage first, the overlap second — so a turnover sitting in a wrong stage answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when the windows also fail to overlap (see F13). Conversion sets the visit's `rental_turnover_id`, points the turnover's `absorbed_visit_id` at this visit, leaves `entitlement_applied` false, and books an absorption charge of half the scheduled base fee — 6500 multiplied by 0.50, rounded, which is 3250 — into `billable_amount_cents`. The visit reaches the terminal state `CONVERTED`. The deferral and conversion branches are the two arms of one fork over an overlapping visit: entitlement is untouched in both, and they differ purely in money — nothing moves on the deferred arm, 3250 cents moves on the converted arm, and only the deferred arm leaves the visit re-schedulable. Finally, a dispatcher may **abort** a visit from `SCHEDULED`, from `EN_ROUTE` or from `IN_PROGRESS`, with no precondition on any of the three edges; in all three cases `billable_amount_cents` stays at zero and no entitlement unit is consumed. `COMPLETED`, `CONVERTED` and `ABORTED` are all terminal.

A finding may also be recorded manually against a visit, supplying an `item_code` naming the checklist item it relates to, a `severity`, a `category` and a free-text `description`. The new finding is born `OPEN` with `property_id` copied from the visit, and the property's `open_critical_finding_count` — a read-time aggregate — is current from that moment without the property row being written. **Neither of the two things this endpoint reads as requiring is enforced.** The visit need not be `COMPLETED`, or in any particular status: a finding may be recorded against a visit at any point in its life, including a terminal one, because §3 declares no rejection for the case and the catalogue names no identifier for it. Likewise the supplied `item_code` is **not** checked against the bound template's `item_codes`; any string is accepted. Both are stated expectations of the operator rather than gates, and the endpoint's declared G5 band is inert in consequence.

### 3.5. Findings, Triage and the Work-Order Cost Chain

A finding carries the `visit_id` of the visit that surfaced it, the `property_id` copied from that visit, the `item_code` it relates to, a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, a trade `category`, a free-text `description`, an `escalation_score` that starts at 0.0, a `monitor_recheck_count` of zero, null `triaged_at` and `dismissed_at`, and a `status` that starts at `OPEN`. `visit_id` is null in exactly one situation: a finding synthesised when a lost key is declared on a property that has no completed visit yet.

Triage moves a finding from `OPEN` to `TRIAGED` and requires the parent visit to be `COMPLETED`, an incomplete parent being rejected with `ERR_PARENT_VISIT_NOT_COMPLETED`. It stamps `triaged_at` and computes `escalation_score`, a weighted figure with three terms. The severity term is 5.0 for `LOW`, 15.0 for `MEDIUM`, 30.0 for `HIGH` and 50.0 for `CRITICAL`. The recheck term is 8.0 multiplied by `monitor_recheck_count`, capped at 24.0. The posture term reads the property's current `risk_posture` and is 0.0 for `NORMAL`, 10.0 for `ELEVATED` and 20.0 for `CRITICAL`. The three are added, rounded to one decimal place, and `escalation_score` is the smaller of that rounded sum and 100.0.

From `TRIAGED` the finding forks three ways. It may be **parked** in `MONITORING`, which is permitted only when `severity` is `LOW` or `MEDIUM` — a `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` finding is rejected with `ERR_SEVERITY_TOO_HIGH_TO_MONITOR` — and creates no work order. It may be **dismissed**, which requires that no work order on it sits outside the pair `REJECTED` and `VOID`; a live work order is rejected with `ERR_FINDING_HAS_LIVE_WORK_ORDER`. Dismissal stamps `dismissed_at` and re-derives the property's `open_critical_finding_count`; `DISMISSED` is terminal. Or it may move to `WORK_ORDERED` by having a work order created against it. A monitored finding may return to `TRIAGED`, but only when a later completed visit exists on the same property whose `completed_at` is strictly greater than the finding's `triaged_at`; without one the re-triage is rejected with `ERR_NO_RECHECK_VISIT`. The return advances `monitor_recheck_count` by one and re-runs the escalation score, which is why the recheck term exists at all. A monitored finding may also be dismissed directly, under the same live-work-order precondition and with the same two side effects as dismissal from `TRIAGED`.

Creating a work order against a triaged finding supplies a `work_type` of `REPAIR`, `REKEY`, `WINTERIZE_FIX`, `STORM_MITIGATION` or `CLEANING`, an `estimated_labour_hours` of zero or more, an `estimated_parts_cents` of zero or more, and the concurrency guard `expected_finding_version`. Two optional links may be supplied: a `storm_event_id`, which is what makes the work order a storm-pipeline work order, and a `key_asset_id`, set only on a rekey. The finding moves from `TRIAGED` to `WORK_ORDERED`; any prior work order on the same finding still in `DRAFT` moves to `VOID` and produces no invoice line; the new work order is created in `DRAFT`, has `finding_id` bound and `property_id` copied from the finding, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `created_by_user_id`, starts with a `dispatch_attempt_count` of zero and a null `approved_by_user_id`, `rejection_reason`, `actual_labour_hours` and `actual_parts_cents`, and is **immediately submitted to `PENDING_APPROVAL` inside the same request**, running the whole cost chain; and the finding's `version` advances by one.

**The cost chain, in its pinned order.** The markup tier reads like a function of the total while the total is a function of the markup, and the specification breaks the apparent circularity by fixing this sequence.

*Step one, labour.* The hour count is `actual_labour_hours` when that value has been recorded and `estimated_labour_hours` otherwise. The rate is the `hourly_rate_cents` of the vendor on this work order's assignment in status `ACCEPTED`, `WORKING` or `FINISHED`, and 8500 cents per hour when no such assignment exists yet. `labour_cost_cents` is the hour count multiplied by the rate, rounded to whole cents. Worked: 3.5 hours at 8500 is 29750.

*Step two, parts.* `parts_cost_cents` is `actual_parts_cents` once the work order has reached `COMPLETED` or beyond, and `estimated_parts_cents` before that.

*Step three, the base.* The base is `labour_cost_cents` plus `parts_cost_cents`.

*Step four, markup.* Two discriminators apply. The first is the bound vendor's `billing_mode`, treated as `MANAGED` when no vendor is bound yet: `MANAGED` starts at 18 percent of the base and `PASS_THROUGH` starts at 5 percent. The second is the value tier, and **the tier is selected on the base — labour plus parts, before any markup — never on `total_cents`**, even though the tier reads colloquially as the work order's value. When the base is at or above 100000 cents, 3 percentage points of relief are subtracted from the percentage. The percentage is applied to the base and rounded to whole cents, and `markup_cents` is the smaller of that rounded figure and a cap of 60000 cents. Worked: a base of 41750 on a managed vendor below the volume tier gives 7515.0, rounded to 7515. A base of 120000 on a managed vendor at or above the tier gives a percentage of 0.15 and a markup of 18000. On the same base the two billing modes differ by 13 percent of the base, and the volume relief can push a pass-through vendor to 2 percent while a managed vendor lands at 15.

*Step five, the storm surcharge.* On a `ROUTINE` work order `storm_surcharge_cents` is always zero. On a `STORM` work order the pre-surcharge amount is `labour_cost_cents` plus `parts_cost_cents` plus `markup_cents` — **the basis includes the markup**; applying the percentage to labour and parts only is the tempting wrong reading — and `storm_surcharge_cents` is that pre-surcharge amount multiplied by the work order's `surcharge_pct_applied`, rounded to whole cents.

**When the chain runs, and when it does not.** The six steps are evaluated at exactly five moments: when a work order is created against a finding and immediately submitted, when a rejected work order is re-submitted, when actuals are recorded at completion, when the rekey remediation of §3.7 mints a work order, and when the `STORM_ASSESS` continuation above mints and submits one — that fifth site is the same submission machinery as the first, differing only in that its auto-approve fork is suppressed. **Dispatch does not re-run the chain.** Dispatch freezes `surcharge_pct_applied` onto the work order but recomputes nothing, and because `surcharge_pct_applied` is 0.0 until that freeze happens, a `STORM` work order carries a `storm_surcharge_cents` of zero — and a `total_cents` that omits the surcharge — for the whole interval between its submission and its completion. Approval, rejection and invoicing likewise recompute nothing; invoicing bills whatever `total_cents` the last chain run left.

*Step six, the total.* `total_cents` is the base plus `markup_cents` plus `storm_surcharge_cents`. Worked routine: 3.5 hours at 8500 gives 29750 labour, plus 12000 parts, for a base of 41750; markup 7515; surcharge zero; total 49265. Worked storm at the imminent tier of 0.35: the same base gives markup 7515, a pre-surcharge amount of 49265, a surcharge of 17242.75 rounded to 17243, and a total of 66508.

**The approval fork.** `approval_ceiling_cents` is discriminated by `pipeline`. On `ROUTINE` it is the owner's `approval_ceiling_override_cents` when that value is present and 50000 cents otherwise, and the approver must be the `Owner`. On `STORM` it is a flat 250000 cents, the owner override is structurally not consulted, and the approver is the `StormCoordinator`. `approval_required` is true exactly when `total_cents` is **strictly greater** than `approval_ceiling_cents`: a total of exactly 50000 against the default ceiling does not require approval, and 50001 does. When `approval_required` comes out false the work order **continues to `APPROVED` inside the same submitting request**, with `approved_by_user_id` set to the literal string "system".

An owner approving a routine work order must be the property's owner; approval simply records the acting `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id`. A storm coordinator approving a storm work order may do so only while `total_cents` is at or below 250000 — a larger total is rejected with `ERR_STORM_CEILING_EXCEEDED` — and the Owner rung is not consulted at all on that pipeline. Rejection requires a `rejection_reason` and returns the parent finding from `WORK_ORDERED` to `TRIAGED` — conditionally, only where that finding is actually in `WORK_ORDERED`; a finding already moved elsewhere is left alone and the rejection still succeeds. **Rejection carries no pipeline rule of any kind.** `ERR_WORK_ORDER_PIPELINE_MISMATCH` guards the approve edge alone, so an `Owner` who owns the property may reject a `STORM` work order and a `StormCoordinator` may reject a `ROUTINE` one, even though neither could have approved it; the owner-scope rung 4 is the only role-band constraint rejection applies, and it too is silent for a `StormCoordinator` or a `PlatformAdmin`. The storm ceiling is likewise not consulted on rejection. A rejected work order may be re-submitted, which moves it from `REJECTED` back to `DRAFT`, but only when `estimated_labour_hours` or `estimated_parts_cents` differs from the rejected values; the re-submission clears `rejection_reason` and re-runs the cost calculations. It stops at `DRAFT`: the auto-approve fork does **not** run on that edge, so a re-submitted work order never lands in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` in the same request however small its total. Submission from `DRAFT` requires the parent finding to be in `WORK_ORDERED`.

Once the vendor has finished, recording the actuals moves the work order from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED`. It requires the accepted assignment to be `FINISHED` and requires both `actual_labour_hours` and `actual_parts_cents` to be supplied, and it re-runs labour, markup, storm surcharge and total from the actuals. On a `REKEY` work order it additionally clears the key asset's `rekey_pending_flag` to false; `copies_total` and `copies_lost` are both left exactly as they were, so `copies_on_hand` stays reduced by the lost copies. Invoicing a completed work order supplies an `invoice_id` whose invoice must be in `DRAFT` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DRAFT`, tested inside G5 after the work order's own source-state check — creates one `ACTIVE` invoice line with `line_type` `WORK_ORDER`, an `amount_cents` equal to the work order's `total_cents`, a `taxable` of true and its `work_order_id` pointing back at the work order, and re-derives the invoice's `storm_surcharge_pct`, `gross_service_cents`, `storm_surcharge_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents`. `INVOICED` and `VOID` are the two terminal states.

### 3.6. Vendors: Eligibility, Selection, Dispatch and On-Site Work

A contractor is onboarded with a `legal_name`, a `trade` of `PLUMBING`, `HVAC`, `ELECTRICAL`, `GENERAL`, `SECURITY`, `LOCKSMITH` or `LANDSCAPE`, a `coi_expires_at` certificate-of-insurance expiry instant, a `license_expires_at` trade-licence expiry instant, a strictly positive `hourly_rate_cents`, and a `rating` on a zero-to-five scale. `billing_mode` defaults to `MANAGED` and otherwise takes `PASS_THROUGH`; `emergency_capable`, false unless supplied, is what admits the vendor to storm dispatch. A new vendor is born `ONBOARDING`, with `outstanding_key_count` zero and `last_eligibility_evaluated_at` null. `outstanding_key_count` counts that vendor's key issuances in status `ISSUED` or `OVERDUE` and is re-derived on every issuance transition.

The vendor lifecycle has two re-entrant loops. Reinstatement promotes an `ONBOARDING` or a `SUSPENDED` vendor to `ELIGIBLE`, and both edges carry the same three preconditions: `coi_expires_at` strictly after the logical clock, `license_expires_at` strictly after the logical clock, and a `rating` of at least 3.0, compared at one decimal place. All three are enforced here and each carries its own identifier, tested in rung order: an expired certificate is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`, then an expired licence with `ERR_VENDOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`, then a rating below the floor with `ERR_VENDOR_RATING_BELOW_FLOOR` (see F7). Both edges stamp `last_eligibility_evaluated_at`. Suspension moves an `ELIGIBLE` vendor to `SUSPENDED` with no precondition, and it is deliberately **not retroactive**: assignments already `ACCEPTED` or `WORKING` are untouched and only new offers are blocked. An eligibility refresh drops an `ELIGIBLE` vendor to `LAPSED` when either `coi_expires_at` or `license_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, stamping `last_eligibility_evaluated_at` and leaving completed work untouched. `ELIGIBLE` is its only source state, so a refresh attempted on a vendor in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and when neither expiry has passed the refresh still succeeds, leaving the vendor `ELIGIBLE` and moving only the evaluation stamp. A credentials update — which may revise any of `coi_expires_at`, `license_expires_at`, `rating` and `hourly_rate_cents` — returns a `LAPSED` vendor to `ELIGIBLE` when the expiries are both strictly after the logical clock, stamps `last_eligibility_evaluated_at`, and advances the vendor's `version` by one. Three details of that sentence are pinned. Only fields **present and non-null** in the body are written; an explicitly null value is ignored rather than clearing the stored one, so a credentials call may legitimately carry nothing at all. The expiry comparison reads the **resulting stored values**, not only the ones this request supplied, so renewing one expiry while the other was already in the future is enough to restore eligibility. And the stamp and the version bump are unconditional: every credentials call refreshes `last_eligibility_evaluated_at` and advances `version`, whatever the vendor's status and whether or not any value changed — but only a `LAPSED` vendor's status moves, and an `ONBOARDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `ELIGIBLE` vendor keeps the status it had.

**Selection.** Dispatch selects a vendor rather than being told one. The required trade is derived from the finding's `category` by a fixed map: `PLUMBING` needs a `PLUMBING` vendor, `HVAC` needs `HVAC`, `ELECTRICAL` needs `ELECTRICAL`, `SECURITY` needs `SECURITY`, `LANDSCAPE` needs `LANDSCAPE`, and each of `STRUCTURAL`, `PEST` and `OTHER` needs a `GENERAL` vendor. The candidate pool is every vendor whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, whose `trade` equals the required trade, whose `coi_expires_at` and `license_expires_at` are both strictly after the logical clock, whose `rating` is at least 3.0 compared at one decimal place, who — on the `STORM` pipeline only — has `emergency_capable` true, who is not in the exclusion set of vendors that have already declined an offer on this work order, and who holds no key issuance in status `OVERDUE` or `LOST`. The pool is ranked by `rating` descending, then by `hourly_rate_cents` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; `created_at` ascending is the final tie-break and `id` is never consulted. When the pool comes out empty no assignment is created, `dispatch_attempt_count` is **not** incremented, the work order stays `APPROVED`, and the request fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR`, HTTP 409. Because the candidate filter already tests status, both expiries, the rating floor and — on the storm pipeline — emergency capability, rungs 43 to 46 can never fail at dispatch: a vendor that would violate one of them is not in the pool to be selected, and the whole of G8a collapses at this endpoint into rung 47. Those four identifiers stay reachable elsewhere — 43, 44 and 45 on assignment acceptance and 44 and 45 at reinstatement — while `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_EMERGENCY_CAPABLE`, whose only declared site is dispatch, is catalogued but never emitted.

Dispatch itself supplies the `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` of the intended on-site window and requires `dispatch_attempt_count` to be strictly less than three. It advances `dispatch_attempt_count` by one, creates a vendor assignment in `OFFERED` for the selected vendor with `offered_at` stamped to the logical clock and `coi_valid_at_offer` snapshotting whether the vendor's `coi_expires_at` was strictly after the clock at that instant — a later lapse never retro-invalidates completed work — and, on the storm pipeline, freezes both the storm response's `surcharge_pct_at_dispatch` and the work order's `surcharge_pct_applied`. The response-level freeze happens **once**, at the first storm-pipeline dispatch for that response; every later dispatch on the same response copies the already-frozen figure onto its own work order rather than re-reading the tier, which is what makes a subsequent downgrade unable to lower it. A `STORM` work order whose event has no storm response on that property has nothing to freeze and simply reads the event's current tier at its own dispatch. The same request then runs key selection: the candidate keys are the property's key assets whose `rekey_pending_flag` is false and whose `copies_on_hand` is at least one, ranked with `LOCKBOX` keys before `PHYSICAL` keys and then by `created_at` ascending, with `created_at` again the final tie-break and `id` never consulted. When that pool is empty no key issuance and no access grant are created, the new assignment is stamped with `access_blocked_flag` true, **and the dispatch itself still succeeds** — on the routine pipeline exactly as on the storm one (see F12). When the pool is not empty the chain runs against the highest-ranked key: **one** key issuance is created for the selected vendor with a `copies_issued` of exactly one and the new assignment's id as its `vendor_assignment_id`, and then one access grant is created for the same vendor over **the assignment's own `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`** — the grant window mirrors the dispatch window and is not derived from anything else. The issuance's `return_deadline_at` is still the key-type window of §3.7 measured from the logical clock, not from the assignment window, so the two intervals do not generally coincide. Grant creation here is the ordinary grant creation of §3.7 and carries all of its effects, including the `LOCKBOX` code rotation and the invalidation of every earlier grant. Neither the issuance nor the grant is created when the key pool is empty, and neither failure mode exists: the chain either runs whole or does not run.

**The assignment lifecycle.** A vendor accepts an offer, which requires the vendor's `status` to be `ELIGIBLE`, requires `coi_expires_at` and `license_expires_at` to be strictly after the logical clock — the credential pair is re-tested here and not merely snapshotted at the offer, so a vendor whose certificate lapsed between the offer and the acceptance is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED` or `ERR_VENDOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` — requires that the window does not overlap another assignment of the same vendor, and last requires that the vendor holds no key issuance in status `OVERDUE` or `LOST`. Those five run in exactly that order, which is rungs 43, 44, 45 and 48 of G8a in full before rung 49 of G8b. The `rating` floor is **not** re-tested at acceptance: it gates selection and reinstatement only. That overlap test is a **closed-interval exclusive occupancy check with no buffer and a capacity of exactly one**: a conflict exists when some other assignment of the same vendor, in status `ACCEPTED` or `WORKING`, has a `window_start_at` at or before this assignment's `window_end_at` and a `window_start_at` of this assignment at or before that other assignment's `window_end_at`. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Acceptance stamps `accepted_at`.

A vendor declines an offer with an optional `decline_reason`, and the decline has three consequences beyond recording the reason: the parent work order returns from `DISPATCHED` to `APPROVED` so it can be re-dispatched, with `dispatch_attempt_count` deliberately **not** decremented; any key issuance created for this assignment still in `ISSUED` moves to `RETURNED` and its access grant is `INVALIDATED` with an `invalidated_reason` of `ASSIGNMENT_WITHDRAWN`; and the declining vendor joins the exclusion set consumed by the selector on the next dispatch. `DECLINED` is terminal.

A vendor starts on-site work, which requires an access grant for this vendor on the property's key to be in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` with the logical clock inside its window, inclusive at both ends. Where several of the vendor's grants on that property qualify, the **earliest-created** one is the one used. Starting work moves the assignment from `ACCEPTED` to `WORKING`, moves that access grant from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` (a grant already `ACTIVE` is left alone and is not written again), moves the parent work order from `DISPATCHED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and moves the property's occupancy from `VACANT` to `CONTRACTOR_ONSITE`. The last two are conditional and never fail the request: a work order in any status other than `DISPATCHED` is left where it is, and a property in any occupancy other than `VACANT` is left where it is, with the assignment still moving to `WORKING`. Entry into `CONTRACTOR_ONSITE` runs no freeze-risk re-derivation, as §3.1 states. Rung 51's capacity aggregate and rung 52's rotation-index comparison are both **inert on this endpoint**: no capacity test runs here at all, and a rotation always invalidates every `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` grant it supersedes, so no grant that survives the candidate filter can carry a stale index in the first place. Finishing work moves the assignment from `WORKING` to the terminal `FINISHED`, stamps `finished_at`, and returns the property's occupancy from `CONTRACTOR_ONSITE` to `VACANT` when no other assignment on that property is still `WORKING`. A dispatcher may instead withdraw an accepted assignment, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`; withdrawal invalidates every access grant belonging to that assignment's key issuance with an `invalidated_reason` of `ASSIGNMENT_WITHDRAWN` and returns the parent work order from `DISPATCHED` to `APPROVED` — and, unlike a decline, it stops there: the key issuance itself is **left in `ISSUED`**, so the copies stay out and `copies_on_hand` stays reduced until the key is returned or marked overdue by its own capability. The work-order return is conditional in the same way the decline's is, firing only on a work order actually sitting in `DISPATCHED`.

### 3.7. Keys, Lockbox Codes and Access Custody

A key asset is registered against a property with a `key_type` of `PHYSICAL` or `LOCKBOX`, a human `label` such as "Front door lockbox", a `copies_total` of at least one, and an optional `current_code`. Physical keys are handed over; lockbox keys are code-based and rotate. Registration sets `copies_on_hand` equal to `copies_total` and `code_rotation_count` to zero. `copies_total` doubles as the aggregate concurrency capacity for simultaneous access windows on the key. `current_code` is null for physical keys — a value supplied on a `PHYSICAL` registration is discarded, not rejected.

**When the key asset's own `version` moves.** It advances by one on an explicit code rotation, on access-grant creation — on a `PHYSICAL` key as well as on a `LOCKBOX` one, even though only the lockbox creation rotates anything — on the declaration that an issuance is lost, and on the clearing of `rekey_pending_flag` when a `REKEY` work order completes. It does **not** advance on key issuance, on a key return, on overdue marking, on grant activation or on grant revocation: those write the issuance or the grant, and the key's own copy ledger and `active_grant_count` are read-time aggregates in the sense of §2.4, so re-reading them is not a write to the key.

The copy ledger is re-derived on every issuance transition and is a strict identity: `copies_issued_outstanding` is the sum of `copies_issued` over the key's issuances in status `ISSUED` or `OVERDUE`; `copies_lost` is the same sum over issuances in status `LOST` or `REKEY_ORDERED`; and `copies_on_hand` is `copies_total` minus those two. `copies_on_hand` may legitimately reach zero, but an issuance that would drive it negative is refused.

**Issuance.** Handing key copies to a vendor supplies the `vendor_id`, an optional `vendor_assignment_id` naming the assignment that justified the issuance — null for standing issuances — and an optional `copies_issued` count of at least one, defaulting to one. Three custody checks apply, in this order: the vendor must hold no key issuance in status `OVERDUE` or `LOST`, otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_KEY_OVERDUE`; the key's `rekey_pending_flag` must be false, otherwise `ERR_KEY_REKEY_PENDING`; and the key must have a `copies_on_hand` at least equal to the requested `copies_issued`, otherwise `ERR_KEY_COPIES_UNAVAILABLE` (see F3). Issuance stamps `issued_at` to the logical clock, computes `return_deadline_at` as `issued_at` plus a window that is 24 hours for a `LOCKBOX` key and 48 hours for a `PHYSICAL` key, sets `status` to `ISSUED` and `overdue_days` to zero, and re-derives the key's `copies_issued_outstanding` and `copies_on_hand`.

Returning a key on time moves the issuance from `ISSUED` to the terminal `RETURNED`, and is permitted only while the logical clock is at or before `return_deadline_at`. It stamps `returned_at` and re-derives `copies_issued_outstanding`, `copies_on_hand` and the vendor's `outstanding_key_count`. Marking an issuance overdue moves it from `ISSUED` to `OVERDUE` and is permitted only once the logical clock is strictly past `return_deadline_at` plus a grace of 6 hours; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_ISSUANCE_NOT_OVERDUE`. The overdue mark blocks any further issuance to that vendor until the issuance leaves `OVERDUE`, and it computes `overdue_days`. That day count is zero while the clock is at or before `return_deadline_at`, and is otherwise the **ceiling** of the seconds since the deadline divided by 86400 — one second past the deadline is already one overdue day. The six-hour grace window gates the *transition* into `OVERDUE` and is deliberately **not** subtracted from the day count. Returning from `OVERDUE` moves the issuance to the terminal `RETURNED_LATE` with no precondition, stamping `returned_at` and re-deriving the same ledger fields.

**Loss and rekey.** Declaring an overdue key lost requires `overdue_days` to be at least 7, an earlier attempt being rejected with `ERR_LOST_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. That count is **recomputed from the logical clock and rewritten onto the issuance before the comparison**, so the stale figure the overdue mark left behind never decides the test, and a refused declaration still leaves the freshened count on the row. The declaration moves the issuance to `LOST`, re-derives the key's `copies_lost` and `copies_on_hand`, and invalidates every access grant carrying this issuance's id that is still `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, with an `invalidated_reason` of `KEY_LOST`. Inside the same request the issuance continues from `LOST` to the terminal `REKEY_ORDERED`, permitted only when the key's `rekey_pending_flag` is currently false; when a rekey is already pending the continuation simply does not fire, the issuance rests in `LOST`, no remediation is raised, and **the request still succeeds**. That continuation sets `rekey_pending_flag` true and raises remediation: a finding with `severity` `HIGH`, `category` `SECURITY` and `status` `TRIAGED`, bound to the property's completed visit with the greatest `completed_at` — the later-created one where two share that instant — or carrying a null `visit_id` when the property has no completed visit at all, carrying the literal `item_code` `"KEY_LOST"` and the literal `description` `"Key lost; rekey ordered."`, with `triaged_at` stamped to the logical clock and `escalation_score` computed by the §3.5 formula; and a work order with `work_type` `REKEY` whose `estimated_labour_hours` is 1.0, whose `estimated_parts_cents` is 32500 and whose `key_asset_id` names this key. Raising it moves the finding on from `TRIAGED` to `WORK_ORDERED`, exactly as an operator-created work order would. That work order then runs the ordinary labour, markup and total chain, so its total is **not** simply 32500: with no vendor bound the labour is 1.0 hour at the 8500 default, the base is 41000, the managed markup is 7380 and `total_cents` is 48380. It nevertheless **rests in `PENDING_APPROVAL`** — the auto-approve fork of §3.5 does not run on this path, even though 48380 is below the default routine ceiling, because this rule names the resulting status outright.

**Access grants.** A grant is created against a key asset for a `vendor_id` over an inclusive `window_start_at` to `window_end_at`, with an optional `key_issuance_id` naming the issuance the grant accompanies — null for code-only grants — and the concurrency guard `expected_key_asset_version`. A new grant is born `PENDING`. On a `LOCKBOX` key the creation **rotates the code first**, so the grant carries the post-rotation snapshot: `code_rotation_count` advances by one; `current_code` is rewritten to the previous code's integer value plus one, rendered as a four-character zero-padded decimal string, so that "1111" becomes "1112", and to the literal "0001" when the previous code was null or was not numeric; `rotation_index_at_grant` and `code_snapshot` are stamped from the post-rotation values; every other grant in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` whose `rotation_index_at_grant` is strictly less than the new count is moved to `INVALIDATED` with an `invalidated_reason` of `CODE_ROTATION`; and the key asset's `version` advances by one. On a `PHYSICAL` key none of that rotation happens: `code_rotation_count` is untouched, `rotation_index_at_grant` is stamped from the unchanged count, and `code_snapshot` is stamped from the key's `current_code`, which on a physical key is always null — so a grant on a physical key is born with a null `code_snapshot` and can never be invalidated by a rotation, there being none. The key asset's `version` still advances by one.

An explicit rotation supplies an explicit `new_code` together with `expected_key_asset_version`. It advances `code_rotation_count` by one, rewrites `current_code` to the supplied value, invalidates every grant in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` whose `rotation_index_at_grant` is strictly less than the new count with an `invalidated_reason` of `CODE_ROTATION`, re-derives `active_grant_count`, and advances the key asset's `version` by one. An `ACTIVE` grant invalidated this way also decrements `active_grant_count` by one, while a `PENDING` grant invalidated this way does not, because a pending grant was never counted.

Activating a grant moves it from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` and carries three preconditions, **evaluated in this order**. First, the concurrent grant count on the key must be strictly less than the key's `copies_total`, an excess being rejected with `ERR_ACCESS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Second, the grant's `rotation_index_at_grant` must equal the key's current `code_rotation_count`; a stale index is rejected with `ERR_ACCESS_GRANT_INVALID`. Third, the logical clock must lie inside the closed window running from `window_start_at` to `window_end_at`, inclusive at both ends; a clock outside it is rejected with `ERR_GRANT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` and not with the grant-invalid identifier (see F4). None of the three is tested when the grant is *created* — creation runs no capacity, rotation or window check at all, which is why a grant may legitimately be minted for a window that has not yet opened. The capacity rule itself is a **closed-interval aggregate** test — deliberately a different geometry from the half-open turnover test: the concurrent count is the number of grants on the same key, excluding the candidate itself, whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` and whose window satisfies both "that grant's `window_start_at` is at or before the candidate's `window_end_at`" and "the candidate's `window_start_at` is at or before that grant's `window_end_at`". **Touching endpoints do collide here**: a grant ending at 12:00 and one starting at 12:00 are concurrent. Activation is allowed while that concurrent count is strictly below `copies_total`, and it advances `active_grant_count` by one.

Revoking a grant supplies a `revoke_reason`. With a reason of `OPERATOR` the grant moves from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `REVOKED` with no precondition. With a reason of `WINDOW_ELAPSED` it moves from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `EXPIRED`, permitted only once the logical clock is strictly past `window_end_at`. Both edges decrement `active_grant_count` by one. `INVALIDATED` is the third terminal state, reached only through a code rotation, a key loss or an assignment withdrawal, and `invalidated_reason` records which of the three it was: `CODE_ROTATION`, `KEY_LOST` or `ASSIGNMENT_WITHDRAWN`. A key asset's `active_grant_count` is at all times exactly the number of `ACTIVE` grants on it.

### 3.8. Seasonal Close, Winterization and Spring Open

The seasonal axis is a closed cycle driven by a single capability that takes an `action`. `BEGIN_CLOSE` moves the property from `OPEN` to `CLOSING`. It requires `occupancy_state` to be `VACANT` and requires that no rental turnover on the property sits in `CHECKOUT`, `CLEANING`, `INSPECTION` or `READY`, an in-flight turnover being rejected with `ERR_TURNOVER_IN_FLIGHT`. It creates a winterization record for the property in status `NOT_STARTED` and advances `Property.version` by one.

`ABORT_CLOSE` returns the property from `CLOSING` to `OPEN` and requires the newest winterization record to be in `NOT_STARTED` or `PARTIAL`. It moves that record to `REOPENED` with a `reopen_reason` of `SPRING_OPEN`, re-derives `winterization_completeness_pct`, and advances `Property.version` by one. Unlike `BEGIN_OPEN` below, this edge performs **only** the status and reason write: the record's four step flags, its `thermostat_set_f`, its `completed_step_count` and its `completeness_pct` are all left exactly as they were, so a record reopened out of `PARTIAL` keeps the progress it had and the property's re-derived percentage is unchanged by the move. It re-derives neither `freeze_risk_score` nor `risk_posture`.

`COMPLETE_WINTERIZATION` moves the property from `CLOSING` to `WINTERIZED`. It requires the newest winterization record to be in status `COMPLETE` — anything less is rejected with `ERR_WINTERIZATION_INCOMPLETE` — and requires `occupancy_state` to be `VACANT`. It sets `winterization_completeness_pct` to 100.0, re-derives `freeze_risk_score` and `risk_posture`, and advances `Property.version` by one.

`BEGIN_OPEN` moves the property from `WINTERIZED` to `OPENING`. It requires that no key issuance on any key asset of the property is `OVERDUE` or `LOST`, a custody problem being rejected with `ERR_KEY_CUSTODY_BLOCKS_OPEN`. It moves the newest winterization record from `COMPLETE` to `REOPENED` with a `reopen_reason` of `SPRING_OPEN` — and that move is the **full reopen** of the standalone reopen capability below, not a bare status change: it clears `water_shutoff_done`, `lines_drained_done` and `valve_tagged_done` to false, clears `thermostat_set_f` and `completed_at` to null, and drives `completed_step_count` to zero and `completeness_pct` to 0.0. The record's status is a precondition of that effect and not of the transition: where the newest record is in any status other than `COMPLETE`, or where the property has no winterization record at all, nothing is written to any record and the seasonal move still succeeds. Either way the action then re-derives `winterization_completeness_pct` from whatever the newest record now reads, re-derives `freeze_risk_score` and `risk_posture`, and advances `Property.version` by one.

`COMPLETE_OPEN` closes the cycle from `OPENING` back to `OPEN`. It requires at least one visit on the property with `visit_type` `SEASONAL_OPEN` and status `COMPLETED` whose `completed_at` is at or after the newest winterization record's `created_at`; without one the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_SEASONAL_OPEN_VISIT`. Where the property has no winterization record at all there is no anchor to clear, and any completed `SEASONAL_OPEN` visit whatever satisfies the requirement. Its only effect is to advance `Property.version` by one. The seasonal axis has no terminal state.

A winterization record may also be opened outside the seasonal-close flow, optionally naming the `visit_id` it belongs to. Either way it is born `NOT_STARTED` with a `completeness_pct` of 0.0, `completed_step_count` zero, `water_shutoff_done`, `lines_drained_done`, `valve_tagged_done` and `antifreeze_added_done` all false, and `thermostat_set_f`, `completed_at` and `reopen_reason` all null.

**Steps.** A step submission supplies a `step_code` and, for the thermostat step, a `thermostat_f` value. The four required step codes are `WATER_SHUTOFF`, `LINES_DRAINED`, `THERMOSTAT_SET` and `VALVE_TAGGED`; the single optional code is `ANTIFREEZE_ADDED`; and the literal `ALL_REQUIRED` is a shortcut. Anything else, or a `THERMOSTAT_SET` step with no `thermostat_f`, is rejected with `ERR_WINTERIZATION_STEP_INVALID`. `completed_step_count` counts the four required steps recorded as done: one for `water_shutoff_done`, one for `lines_drained_done`, one for `valve_tagged_done`, and one for the thermostat step **only when `thermostat_set_f` is non-null and at least 50** — a `THERMOSTAT_SET` step submitted with a `thermostat_f` of 49 records the value but does not increase the count, while 50 exactly does. `antifreeze_added_done` is deliberately not counted at all. `completeness_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `completed_step_count` and divided by four, rounded to one decimal place, so one of four is 25.0 and three of four is 75.0.

The record's own state follows the count. From `NOT_STARTED` a step that leaves the count at least one and strictly below four moves the record to `PARTIAL`. From `PARTIAL` a further step keeps it in `PARTIAL` while the resulting count is still strictly below four, and requires that the submitted step is not already recorded as done — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_STEP_ALREADY_RECORDED`. A single step declares `NOT_STARTED`, `PARTIAL` and `REOPENED` as its source states, a submission against a `COMPLETE` record being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the already-recorded test runs on all three of them and not only on `PARTIAL`, and for the thermostat step "already recorded" means a stored `thermostat_set_f` that is itself at or above 50, so a thermostat step that followed a sub-50 reading may be submitted again. A step that brings the count to exactly four moves the record to `COMPLETE` from whichever of the three it was in, stamps `completed_at` and sets `completeness_pct` to 100.0. The `ALL_REQUIRED` shortcut moves a record straight from `NOT_STARTED` to `COMPLETE` and declares `NOT_STARTED` as its **only** source state, so the shortcut offered against a `PARTIAL` or `REOPENED` record is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; it requires a `thermostat_f` of at least 50 — a lower value is rejected with `ERR_THERMOSTAT_BELOW_MINIMUM` — and records all four required steps at once, stamping `completed_at` and setting `completeness_pct` to 100.0. Every step submission re-derives the property's `winterization_completeness_pct`, `freeze_risk_score` and `risk_posture`.

Reopening a `COMPLETE` record requires a `reopen_reason` of `FREEZE_ALARM`, `SPRING_OPEN`, `RENTAL_TURNOVER` or `INSPECTION_FAIL`. It moves the record to `REOPENED`, clears the four required step flags to false, clears `thermostat_set_f` and `completed_at` to null, recomputes `completeness_pct` to 0.0, and re-derives the property's `winterization_completeness_pct`, `freeze_risk_score` and `risk_posture`. A step submitted against a `REOPENED` record that leaves the count at least one and strictly below four returns it to `PARTIAL`, recomputing `completed_step_count` and `completeness_pct`. The winterization machine has no terminal state.

### 3.9. Rental Turnovers, Mail Holds and Security Incidents

**Turnovers.** A rental turnover is booked against a property with a guest `checkout_at`, the next guest's `checkin_at` — which must be strictly after the checkout — and a `guest_reference` naming the outgoing booking. It is born `BOOKED`, with `clean_started_at`, `inspect_started_at`, `ready_at` and `absorbed_visit_id` all null and `reclean_attempt_count` zero. A single advance capability takes a `to_status` and drives the stage machine; an advance that targets a stage which is not the declared successor of the current one is rejected with `ERR_TURNOVER_STAGE_INVALID`.

`CHECKOUT` is reachable from `BOOKED` once the logical clock is at or after `checkout_at`, and it flips the property's `occupancy_state` from `RENTAL_OCCUPIED` to `VACANT` when it was `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`. `CLEANING` is reachable from `CHECKOUT` with no precondition and stamps `clean_started_at`. `INSPECTION` is reachable from `CLEANING` with no precondition and stamps `inspect_started_at`. `READY` is reachable from `INSPECTION` with no precondition and stamps `ready_at`. `CHECKIN` is reachable from `READY` once the logical clock is at or after `checkin_at`, and it flips the property's `occupancy_state` from `VACANT` to `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`; it is terminal. That flip is a bare write and consults **neither** of the preconditions rung 41 imposes on an operator-driven move to `RENTAL_OCCUPIED`: a property that is not `rental_program_enrolled`, or whose `seasonal_state` is not `OPEN`, is still flipped, because the enrolment gate lives on the occupancy-transition endpoint and not on this one. Both occupancy flips — the `CHECKOUT` one and this one — advance the property's `version` by one, and only the `CHECKOUT` one re-derives the freeze-risk pair, exactly as §3.1 describes. `CANCELLED` is reachable from `BOOKED` alone, with no precondition; any visit bound to this turnover and still `SCHEDULED` **keeps its schedule** and only the link is cleared. `CANCELLED` is likewise terminal. Failing an inspection is a separate capability that sends the turnover back from `INSPECTION` to `CLEANING`, requires `reclean_attempt_count` to be strictly less than two — a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_RECLEAN_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` — advances `reclean_attempt_count` by one, and clears `inspect_started_at` back to null.

**Mail holds.** A hold is opened against a property with an inclusive `start_on`, an exclusive `end_on` that must be strictly later, and an optional `forwarding_address`. `hold_days` is the whole days between the two, taken as the floor of the elapsed seconds divided by 86400. A creation whose `hold_days` exceeds 30 is rejected with `ERR_HOLD_TOO_LONG`, and so is an extension whose resulting `hold_days` would exceed 60. A new hold is born `REQUESTED`, with `package_count`, `overflow_events`, `extension_count` and `disposition_fee_cents` all zero.

Package logging supplies a signed `package_delta`, positive on accumulation and negative on collection, and the new count is the previous `package_count` plus the delta, floored at zero so the count can never go negative. Overflow is reached when the new count is **at or above** six, so exactly six packages is already an overflow. A logging call with a `package_delta` of exactly zero moves the hold from `REQUESTED` to `ACTIVE`, permitted only once the logical clock is at or after `start_on`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_MAIL_HOLD_NOT_STARTED`, and no fee is assessed. That start-date condition is tested on **every** logging call and not only on the activating one — the catalogue states it without qualification, and the clock's monotonicity makes the two readings agree in practice — and it is tested **before** the requirement that a hold still in `REQUESTED` carry a delta of exactly zero, a non-zero delta from `REQUESTED` being one of the preconditions rung 18 absorbs. A positive delta that brings the count to at least six moves the hold from `ACTIVE` to `OVERFLOW` and advances `overflow_events` by one; **no security incident and no finding is created — overflow is a billing and scheduling signal only**. A negative delta that brings the count back below six moves the hold from `OVERFLOW` to `ACTIVE`, reducing `package_count` by the collected amount.

The disposition fee has two structurally different branches selected by the `disposition` chosen. On the **forward** branch the fee is an affine function of packages: a base of 1800 cents plus 250 cents for every package in `package_count`, so a forward with six packages costs 3300 cents and the fee grows without bound as packages accumulate. On the **hold-extension** branch the fee is purely multiplicative in extensions with no base at all: 900 cents multiplied by `extension_count`, so a hold extended twice carries 1800 cents, and because extensions are capped at two the branch is capped at 1800. The two branches read entirely different fields.

Extending a hold requires `extension_count` to be strictly below two, an exhausted hold being rejected with `ERR_HOLD_EXTENSIONS_EXHAUSTED`. It declares **no source state of its own**: every non-terminal status may be extended, `REQUESTED` and `OVERFLOW` included, and only the two terminal states `RELEASED` and `FORWARDED` are refused, with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` ahead of the extension cap. The extension adds 15 days to `end_on`, rewrites `hold_days` from the widened window, advances `extension_count` by one, and runs the hold-extension fee branch over the already-advanced count. Forwarding a hold requires a non-null `forwarding_address`, a missing one being rejected with `ERR_FORWARDING_ADDRESS_MISSING`. Forwarding is reachable from `ACTIVE` and from `OVERFLOW`, and on both edges it runs the forward fee branch and writes `disposition_fee_cents`; on the `ACTIVE` edge it additionally appends an `ACTIVE` invoice line with `line_type` `MAIL_DISPOSITION`, an `amount_cents` equal to the freshly written `disposition_fee_cents`, a `taxable` of true and its `mail_hold_id` naming this hold, to the property's draft invoice when one exists, and the declared effects of the `OVERFLOW` edge stop at the fee — a deliberate asymmetry, not an omission (see F8). **"The property's draft invoice"**, here and at the false-alarm append below, resolves to the **most recently created** invoice on that property whose status is `DRAFT`; when the property has no `DRAFT` invoice at all no line is appended and the transition succeeds regardless. `FORWARDED` is terminal. Releasing a hold is reachable from `ACTIVE` and requires `package_count` to be exactly zero, outstanding packages being rejected with `ERR_PACKAGES_OUTSTANDING`; `disposition_fee_cents` stays at whatever the extension ladder left it. `RELEASED` is terminal.

**Security incidents.** An incident is reported against a property with an `incident_type` of `ALARM_INTRUSION`, `ALARM_SMOKE`, `FREEZE_ALARM`, `WATER_LEAK`, `VANDALISM` or `UNAUTHORIZED_ACCESS`, a `severity`, and an optional `visit_id` naming the visit that detected it — null for externally reported alarms. It is born `REPORTED`, with `reported_at` stamped to the logical clock, `escalation_count` zero, `triggered_visit_id` and `resolution_note` null, `false_alarm_sequence` null and `false_alarm_fee_cents` zero.

Acknowledgement moves the incident from `REPORTED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` with no precondition and no billing effect. Investigation moves it from `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `INVESTIGATING`, and on that first pass an incident whose `incident_type` is `ALARM_INTRUSION`, `ALARM_SMOKE` or `FREEZE_ALARM` forces an `ALARM_RESPONSE` visit with a `trigger_source` of `ALARM_TRIGGERED`, whose id is written into `triggered_visit_id`; the request may supply the `checklist_template_id`, `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` for that visit. Each of the three has a declared fallback. The template is the one supplied, and otherwise the **earliest-created `ACTIVE` template whose `visit_type` is `ALARM_RESPONSE`**; a supplied `checklist_template_id` that resolves to nothing — or that is not a string at all, this being the one body foreign key in the system whose wrong JSON type answers 404 rather than falling through to rung 60 — is rejected with `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, but when none is supplied and no active alarm-response template exists at all, **no visit is created and the transition still succeeds** with `triggered_visit_id` left null. The window is the one supplied, and otherwise runs from the logical clock to twenty-four hours later; a supplied start with no supplied end likewise runs twenty-four hours from that start. The forced visit is minted directly by this side effect and does **not** pass through the visit-scheduling gates: no active subscription is required, the seasonal and occupancy bands are not consulted, and the turnover-overlap test is not run. Escalation moves the incident from `INVESTIGATING` to `ESCALATED`, requires a `severity` of `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` — a lower severity is rejected with `ERR_SEVERITY_TOO_LOW_TO_ESCALATE` — and advances `escalation_count` by one. Investigation is also the return edge from `ESCALATED` back to `INVESTIGATING`, and on that and every later pass **no additional visit is created**. Resolution is reachable from `INVESTIGATING` and from `ESCALATED`, requires a `resolution_note`, assesses no fee, and reaches the terminal `RESOLVED` on both edges.

Reclassification as a false alarm is reachable from `INVESTIGATING` and from `ESCALATED`, and the two edges carry identical effects. It is permitted only when `incident_type` is `ALARM_INTRUSION`, `ALARM_SMOKE`, `FREEZE_ALARM` or `UNAUTHORIZED_ACCESS`; a `WATER_LEAK` or `VANDALISM` incident is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ALARM_CLASS`. An optional `resolution_note` may be supplied. The reclassification first sets `false_alarm_sequence`: the peer set is the property's incidents in status `FALSE_ALARM` whose `reported_at` lies within the trailing 365 days of the logical clock, ordered by `reported_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending — `id` is never consulted — and the sequence is this incident's one-based index inside that set once it has joined it. It then computes `false_alarm_fee_cents` from that sequence, and **the municipal ladder is deliberately not monotone**: the first occurrence costs nothing, the second costs 5000 cents, the third costs 15000 cents, the fourth **drops** to 10000 cents because it falls under a different citation class, and the fifth and every later occurrence costs 25000 cents. Interpolating, or assuming the schedule only rises, produces the wrong fee. The reclassification then re-derives the property's `false_alarm_count_window`; appends an `ACTIVE` invoice line with `line_type` `FALSE_ALARM_FEE` and an `amount_cents` equal to `false_alarm_fee_cents` to the property's draft invoice when one exists **and the fee is strictly greater than zero**; and re-derives that invoice's `gross_service_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents`. `FALSE_ALARM` is terminal, so a reclassification is never undone and the fee ledger is append-only.

### 3.10. Storm Response

A storm event is declared with a `name` such as "Hurricane Delta", a two-uppercase-letter `region_state_code` and a forecast `landfall_eta_at`, which must be strictly after the logical clock — an ETA already in the past is rejected with `ERR_LANDFALL_ETA_IN_PAST`. It is born `FORECAST`, with `landfall_recorded_at` and `all_clear_at` null and `downgrade_count` zero. `response_count` counts the storm responses bound to it. The rule that only properties whose `state_code` matches `region_state_code` may receive a response is a statement about where a response is *useful*, not a gate on enrolment: **enrolment performs no region comparison at all** and will happily bind an out-of-region property. The comparison is made once, later, when the secure sweep is scheduled, and that is the only place `ERR_STORM_REGION_MISMATCH` is emitted — so an out-of-region response can exist, can be read and can be listed, and simply cannot be swept.

**`derived_tier` versus `status`.** The event carries an operator-driven `status` and a clock-driven `derived_tier`, and the two may legitimately disagree once an operator has downgraded. `derived_tier` reads the hours from the logical clock to `landfall_eta_at`, which may be negative after landfall, and **every boundary is strict on the upper side**: more than 48 hours out is `FORECAST`; otherwise more than 24 hours out is `WATCH`; otherwise more than 6 hours out is `WARNING`; otherwise the tier is `IMMINENT`. Exactly 48.0 hours out is therefore `WATCH`, exactly 24.0 is `WARNING`, and exactly 6.0 is `IMMINENT`.

**The advisory ladder.** A single advisory capability takes a `to_status`. `FORECAST` to `WATCH`, `WATCH` to `WARNING`, `WARNING` to `WATCH` and `WARNING` to `IMMINENT` all require the logical clock to be strictly before `landfall_eta_at`. The move to `WATCH` from `FORECAST` creates no responses automatically. The move to `WARNING` leaves every `PENDING` response's status untouched and **deadlines are not recomputed on an advisory change**. The downgrade from `WARNING` back to `WATCH` — an operator's response to a forecast track moving away — advances `downgrade_count` by one, and already-scheduled secure sweeps keep their deadline: a downgrade never extends a deadline. The move to `IMMINENT` is the point from which storm work orders may auto-approve under the 250000-cent emergency ceiling.

Landfall moves the event from `IMMINENT` to `LANDFALL` with no precondition. It stamps `landfall_recorded_at`, moves every response in `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED` whose `secure_sweep_deadline_at` is earlier than the logical clock and whose `secured_at` is still null to `MISSED_DEADLINE`, and then moves every response in `SECURED`, `SECURED_LATE` or `MISSED_DEADLINE` to `AWAITING_ALL_CLEAR`. All-clear moves the event from `LANDFALL` to `ALL_CLEAR`, stamps `all_clear_at`, and moves every response in `AWAITING_ALL_CLEAR` to `ASSESSMENT_DUE` while writing its `assessment_deadline_at`.

There is a **secondary-landfall edge** back from `ALL_CLEAR` to `LANDFALL`, for a stalled system that re-intensifies after the all-clear. It rewrites `landfall_recorded_at` to the logical clock and clears `all_clear_at` back to null, and those two writes are its **whole** effect on the graph: no response of any status is inspected or moved. Responses already in `ASSESSMENT_DUE` keep that status and keep their `assessment_deadline_at` and are **not** rewound; and, symmetrically, a response still sitting in `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED` past its deadline is **not** swept into `MISSED_DEADLINE` by this second landfall the way the first one would have done, because the whole settlement block is skipped on this edge. This is the only path that can leave a response in `ASSESSMENT_DUE` while its parent event is not `ALL_CLEAR`, and it is precisely what makes the "storm not all clear" rung reachable. Closing the event moves it from `ALL_CLEAR` to the terminal `CLOSED`, requires every response to be in `ASSESSED` or `CLOSED` — an open response is rejected with `ERR_STORM_RESPONSES_OPEN` — moves every `ASSESSED` response to `CLOSED`, and clears `storm_hold_flag` on every responded property that has no other open response.

**Responses.** Enrolling a property supplies its `property_id`. The response is born `PENDING` with `secured_at`, `assessed_at`, `swept_by_user_id`, `assessed_by_user_id`, `damage_level` and `secure_sweep_visit_id` all null, `guarantee_credit_cents` zero, `sweep_on_time` false and `surcharge_pct_at_dispatch` 0.0, and it binds `subscription_id` to the property's subscription when one exists in `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `DELINQUENT`, and null when none does.

Scheduling the secure sweep supplies a `checklist_template_id` and a `scheduled_start_at`. It requires the parent event to be in `WATCH`, `WARNING` or `IMMINENT`, and requires the property's `state_code` to equal the event's `region_state_code` — a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STORM_REGION_MISMATCH`. It also runs the occupancy band for the `STORM_SECURE` visit it is about to create, so rung 40 applies here exactly as it does at visit scheduling and a `RENTAL_OCCUPIED` property is refused with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_FORBIDS_VISIT`; the seasonal band is listed too but is inert, because no rung of G6 constrains a `STORM_SECURE` visit. It writes `secure_sweep_deadline_at` under the pre-storm branch, **anchored on the landfall ETA and subtracting a tier-selected lead**: 48 hours at the `FORECAST` tier, 36 at `WATCH`, 18 at `WARNING` and 2 at `IMMINENT`, with the tier read at the moment of scheduling and the deadline then frozen — it is never recomputed. **"Tier" here means `derived_tier` and not `status`.** The two are separate fields that the paragraph above says may legitimately disagree, and the lead is selected by the clock-derived one: an event whose `status` an operator has advanced to `WATCH` while its landfall ETA is still more than 48 hours out derives `FORECAST` and takes the 48-hour lead, even though `FORECAST` is not a status this endpoint would have accepted. It creates a visit with `visit_type` `STORM_SECURE` and `trigger_source` `STORM`, with `storm_response_id` bound and a scheduled window whose `scheduled_start_at` is the supplied one and whose `scheduled_end_at` equals `secure_sweep_deadline_at` — that pair is **not** interval-checked, so a start supplied later than the computed deadline yields a visit whose window runs backwards and the request still succeeds. It points `secure_sweep_visit_id` at that visit, sets the property's `storm_hold_flag` true, and moves the response to `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED`.

The post-storm deadline uses a **different anchor field and the opposite direction**: `assessment_deadline_at` is `all_clear_at` **plus** a fixed 72-hour SLA, with no tier lookup at all, and it stays null until the event reaches `ALL_CLEAR`. Anchoring the post-storm SLA on landfall instead of all-clear is the single most tempting wrong reading and produces a deadline earlier than the assessment could physically begin.

**Sweep settlement and the guarantee.** When the bound secure-sweep visit completes, timeliness is decided by comparing `secured_at` with `secure_sweep_deadline_at`, **inclusive at the boundary**: securing at exactly the deadline instant is on time. On time, the response moves from `SECURE_SWEEP_SCHEDULED` to `SECURED`, stamping `secured_at` and `swept_by_user_id`, and runs the earned branch of the guarantee. Late, it moves to `SECURED_LATE`, stamping the same two fields, and runs the forfeited branch, writing a `guarantee_credit_cents` of zero. The earned branch applies only when the sweep was on time **and** the in-force plan's `storm_guarantee_included` is true. Its basis is the sum of `storm_surcharge_cents` over the work orders bound to the same `storm_event_id` and the same `property_id` whose status is `COMPLETED` or `INVOICED`. When that basis is exactly zero the credit is a flat 15000 cents; otherwise the credit is the smaller of 15000 cents and half the basis, rounded to whole cents. Worked: on time with the guarantee and no completed storm work order gives 15000; with a basis of 40000 the credit is the smaller of 15000 and 20000, which is 15000; with a basis of 20000 it is the smaller of 15000 and 10000, which is 10000; and a sweep late by one second gives zero. The forfeited branch collapses to the literal zero and appends no credit line at all, which is exactly why a forfeited invoice total is strictly larger than an earned one.

**The surcharge percentage.** At the instant the first storm-pipeline work order for a response is dispatched, the percentage is read from the event's **`derived_tier`** — 0.0 at `FORECAST`, 0.10 at `WATCH`, 0.20 at `WARNING` and 0.35 at `IMMINENT` — and frozen onto both the response's `surcharge_pct_at_dispatch` and the work order's `surcharge_pct_applied`. It is the clock-derived tier and not the operator-driven `status` that is read, the same choice the sweep-deadline lead makes, so a dispatch against an event whose `status` reads `IMMINENT` while its ETA is still 30 hours out freezes 0.10 and not 0.35. The response the freeze attaches to is the **earliest-created** response of that event on the work order's property. A later advisory downgrade **never lowers an already-frozen percentage**. A `ROUTINE` work order carries a `surcharge_pct_applied` of 0.0.

**Assessment and closure.** Signing the post-storm assessment supplies a `damage_level` of `NONE`, `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, together with `expected_storm_response_version`. It moves the response from `ASSESSMENT_DUE` to `ASSESSED`, and carries two preconditions beyond the state check, which are evaluated in gate order **ahead of** it: first the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `swept_by_user_id`, an identical signer being rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (rung 6, in G2); then the expected version must equal the response's current `version`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORM_RESPONSE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 (in G4, see F1). Only then are the source state and `ERR_STORM_NOT_ALL_CLEAR` considered. An assessment attempted while the parent event is not `ALL_CLEAR` is rejected with `ERR_STORM_NOT_ALL_CLEAR`. Signing stamps `assessed_at`, `assessed_by_user_id` and `damage_level`, and advances the response's `version` by exactly one. Closing an assessed response moves it to the terminal `CLOSED` with no precondition and clears the property's `storm_hold_flag` when no other open response exists for that property. The three transitions into `AWAITING_ALL_CLEAR` — from `SECURED`, from `SECURED_LATE` and from `MISSED_DEADLINE` — all fire on landfall, all have no precondition, and all perform no arithmetic whatever; the `MISSED_DEADLINE` entry itself, which fires on landfall when the deadline has passed and `secured_at` is still null, leaves `guarantee_credit_cents` at zero.

### 3.11. Invoicing, Tax, Payment and Delinquency

A draft invoice is opened against a billing cycle. It copies `property_id` and `owner_id` from the cycle's subscription, is born `DRAFT` with `amount_paid_cents` zero, `dispute_count` zero and `issued_at` and `paid_at` null, and it seeds exactly one `ACTIVE` line whose `line_type` is `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE` and whose `amount_cents` is the cycle's `prorated_fee_cents`. A cycle that has never been paused and never had a tier change carries a `prorated_fee_cents` exactly equal to the plan's `monthly_fee_cents`, so on that ordinary path the seeded line is simply the monthly fee. Opening the invoice then runs the full total pipeline.

Further lines are appended one at a time with a `line_type`, a human `description`, a signed `amount_cents`, an optional `taxable` flag defaulting to true, and up to four optional links naming the thing the line bills: `visit_id`, `work_order_id`, `security_incident_id` and `mail_hold_id`. The nine line types are `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE`, `VISIT_OVERAGE`, `TRAVEL_SURCHARGE`, `WORK_ORDER`, `FALSE_ALARM_FEE`, `MAIL_DISPOSITION`, `REKEY_FEE`, `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` and `GUARANTEE_CREDIT`. Sign is constrained by type: an `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` or a `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` line carries an `amount_cents` at or below zero and every other type carries one at or above zero, a violation being rejected with `ERR_LINE_SIGN_INVALID`. Lines may be appended only while the invoice is `DRAFT`; an append against any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DRAFT`. Each new line is born with a `status` of `ACTIVE` and receives a one-based `sequence_index` assigned in ascending `created_at` order, and that index is what pins the per-line rounding sequence. A line's `status` takes only `ACTIVE` or `VOID`, and `VOID` lines are excluded from every invoice aggregate.

**Per-line tax.** A line's `tax_cents` is its `amount_cents` multiplied by a tax rate of 0.07 and rounded to whole cents when `taxable` is true and its `status` is `ACTIVE`, and zero otherwise. **The rounding order is pinned: tax rounds per line and only then is summed.** Worked: two `ACTIVE` taxable lines of 1250 each round 87.5 to 88 twice, for 176 — where taxing the 2500 sum in one step and rounding once would give 175. A negative line rounds half away from zero, so −1250 yields −88.

**The nine-step total.** The invoice total is recomputed on every mutation that changes the `ACTIVE` line set or any line's `amount_cents`, and again on issuing, on dispute resolution, on invoice voiding, and at the moment the delinquency rule writes a `late_fee_cents`. The `ACTIVE` lines are walked in ascending `sequence_index`; `VOID` lines are excluded from every step.

1. `gross_service_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the `ACTIVE` lines whose `line_type` is one of `SUBSCRIPTION_FEE`, `VISIT_OVERAGE`, `TRAVEL_SURCHARGE`, `WORK_ORDER`, `FALSE_ALARM_FEE`, `MAIL_DISPOSITION` or `REKEY_FEE`.
2. `entitlement_offset_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the `ACTIVE` `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` lines, and is therefore zero or negative.
3. The net service figure is `gross_service_cents` plus `entitlement_offset_cents`.
4. `storm_surcharge_cents` is that net service figure multiplied by the invoice's `storm_surcharge_pct` and rounded to whole cents. **The order matters: the surcharge multiplies the net service, after the entitlement offset has been subtracted.** Applying it to `gross_service_cents` instead yields a strictly larger total whenever an `ENTITLEMENT_OFFSET` line exists.
5. `guarantee_credit_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the `ACTIVE` `GUARANTEE_CREDIT` lines, and is therefore zero or negative.
6. `subtotal_cents` is the net service figure plus `storm_surcharge_cents` plus `guarantee_credit_cents`.
7. `tax_cents` is the sum of the already-rounded per-line `tax_cents` over the `ACTIVE` lines, plus the invoice-level `storm_surcharge_cents` multiplied by 0.07 and rounded to whole cents.
8. `late_fee_cents` is zero unless the delinquency rule has applied a fee to this invoice.
9. `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`, and `balance_due_cents` is `total_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents`.

`storm_surcharge_pct` at the invoice level is the maximum `surcharge_pct_applied` over the work orders behind this invoice's `ACTIVE` `WORK_ORDER` lines whose `pipeline` is `STORM`, and 0.0 when that pool is empty. It is a single numeric maximum, so when two work orders tie the value is identical and no tie-break is observable. It is re-derived when a work order is invoiced and on every subsequent total run.

**Issuing and paying.** Issuing a draft invoice requires the subscription's status to be anything other than `CANCELLED`, and requires at least one `ACTIVE` line. A third requirement is sometimes stated — that the parent billing cycle be in `OPEN`, `CLOSING` or `CLOSED` — and it is **vacuous and unenforced**: those are every status the cycle machine has, `CLOSING` is never even persisted because closure passes through it inside one request, and no comparison against the cycle's status is performed at issuing. No identifier is reserved for it and none is needed. The two requirements that remain carry different identifiers and sit in different gate bands, so their order is observable: a cancelled subscription is rejected first, in G5, with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`, and only an invoice whose subscription is live reaches the G9 line test, where an empty `ACTIVE` line set is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_HAS_NO_LINES` (see F6). Issuing recomputes every step of the total, freezes the line set, stamps `issued_at`, and moves the invoice to `ISSUED`. A payment supplies an `amount_cents` that must be strictly greater than zero against an invoice that is not already `PAID`, a violation being rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`. A payment strictly between zero and `balance_due_cents` moves an `ISSUED` invoice to `PARTIALLY_PAID`, raising `amount_paid_cents` and recomputing `balance_due_cents`. A payment at or above `balance_due_cents` moves an `ISSUED` invoice straight to `PAID`, setting `amount_paid_cents` to `total_cents`, `balance_due_cents` to zero and `paid_at` to the logical clock; the same threshold moves a `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice to `PAID`, setting `balance_due_cents` to zero and `paid_at` to the logical clock.

**Disputes and voids.** Only the owner named by the invoice's `owner_id` may dispute an issued invoice, optionally attaching a `resolution_note`; the dispute advances `dispute_count` by one and moves the invoice from `ISSUED` to `DISPUTED`. That `resolution_note` is accepted and type-checked and then **discarded** — the dispute writes it nowhere, and the invoice carries no field for it, so a note supplied here is unobservable in every later read. Resolving a dispute returns the invoice from `DISPUTED` to `ISSUED` with no precondition and recomputes every step of the total over the current `ACTIVE` line set. Voiding an invoice is declared only from `DRAFT` and from `ISSUED`. From `ISSUED` it requires `amount_paid_cents` to be exactly zero — a part-paid invoice is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_PAID` — and moves every `ACTIVE` line to `VOID`; voiding a draft invoice has no such precondition and likewise voids every `ACTIVE` line, with the totals recomputed over the now-empty `ACTIVE` set. That recomputation drives `gross_service_cents`, `entitlement_offset_cents`, `storm_surcharge_cents`, `guarantee_credit_cents`, `subtotal_cents` and `tax_cents` to zero, but **`late_fee_cents` is not a line and survives the void**, so an invoice the delinquency rule had already charged reads back with `total_cents` and `balance_due_cents` equal to that surviving late fee rather than to zero. `PAID` and `VOID` are the two terminal invoice states. Those declarations together make the `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_PAID` clause unreachable, as rung 30 records: an invoice that has taken a part payment is `PARTIALLY_PAID`, which is not a declared source state for voiding and is therefore stopped by rung 18; one that has been paid in full is terminal and is stopped by rung 19; a `DISPUTED` invoice is likewise stopped by rung 18; and an `ISSUED` invoice never carries a positive `amount_paid_cents`, because the first payment always moves it out of `ISSUED`.

Voiding a single line is the compensating action behind the line-sum identity: the line's `status` becomes `VOID`, its `tax_cents` is set to zero, and the full total pipeline is re-run, restoring the pre-append `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents` exactly. Voiding a line that is already `VOID` is rejected with `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_VOID`. The invoice-line machine declares neither a terminal state nor a source-state list for this action, so that identifier is the whole of the gate: a second void answers `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_VOID` and never `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is mounted under the `/property-care` prefix. **The success status is decided by what the response returns, not by what the request creates.** An endpoint whose own purpose is to mint the row it then returns in `data` answers 201 Created; every other successful request answers 200 OK, returning the mutated resource in `data`. The distinction matters because a great many 200 endpoints mint rows as a *side effect* of driving some other row's machine, and none of them is thereby a 201: subscription activation opens billing cycle 1, work-order dispatch mints a vendor assignment and usually a key issuance and an access grant, secure-sweep scheduling and incident investigation each mint a visit, declaring a key lost mints a finding and a work order, visit completion mints findings and may mint a security incident, and work-order invoicing, false-alarm reclassification and mail forwarding each append an invoice line — every one of those answers 200, because the resource in `data` is the pre-existing row the request was addressed to. The twenty-two endpoints that answer 201 are exactly the nine collection `POST`s — owners, properties, service plans, subscriptions, away periods, checklist templates, visits, vendors and storm events — together with the thirteen sub-collection `POST`s that create and return a child row: a billing cycle under a subscription, a work order under a finding, a finding under a visit, a key asset, a mail hold, a rental turnover, a security incident and a winterization record under a property, a key issuance and an access grant under a key asset, a storm response under a storm event, a draft invoice under a billing cycle, and a line under an invoice. The gate subsequence, the request body and the success status of each endpoint are given in §2.7; the behaviour behind each one is in §3.
