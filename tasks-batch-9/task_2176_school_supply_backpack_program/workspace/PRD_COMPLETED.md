# School Supply Backpack Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community backpack programs live or die on a single, unglamorous fact: a child cannot be handed three quarters of a kit. A grade-band needs list either has every line covered — the notebooks, the binder, the hygiene pouch, the backpack itself — or the family walks away with nothing. Between the moment a corporate partner pledges a bin and the moment a fifth-grader signs for a backpack sit a dozen operational hazards: donations arrive damaged or unsealed, a cash gift is restricted to an item nobody needs, a distribution event is published at a school whose partnership lapsed, two volunteer sessions are booked into the same warehouse floor an hour apart, and a purchase order is approved by the very person who submitted it. This document specifies the API that keeps all of that honest.

The system tracks four flows that must never drift apart from one another. **Needs** flow downward from a partner school, expressed as a published grade-band kit specification and the per-student quantities on its lines. **Goods** flow inward through collection sites as in-kind donation batches, are inspected, accepted or quarantined, and settle into a per-cycle, per-item inventory position that is reserved and then distributed. **Cash** flows inward as restricted and unrestricted gifts and outward as purchase orders drawn through a strictly ordered funding waterfall. **Students** flow through registration, eligibility verification, seat confirmation, waitlisting and fulfilment. Two conservation identities — one over physical units, one over cents — are asserted after every mutation, and any implementation that lets either identity drift has a defect regardless of what the endpoint returned.

Finally, the program answers to funders. Two impact numbers are maintained side by side for every partner school: an interim figure that operations watches while events are still running, computed from optimistic in-transit expectations and provisional confirmations, and an audited figure that only counts kits actually handed over at closed events and that carries a no-show penalty. The two numbers are deliberately different, are computed from different event sets and different fill fields, and must never be conflated. The remainder of this document defines the global contract that every endpoint honours (§2), the domain workflows and their rules (§3), the endpoint surface (§4), and the complete error catalogue (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. The five roles are `ProgramDirector`, `SchoolLiaison`, `SiteCoordinator`, `WarehouseLead` and `DonorRelations`; spelling is case-sensitive and no other value is accepted. Once the caller is authenticated, the role is checked against the list of roles authorised for the requested endpoint in §4. A role that is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the operator identifier is not an opaque login but the primary key of a record the operator speaks for. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `School` compare the resource's governing school reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CollectionSite`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CollectionSite` compare the resource's governing site reference to `X-User-ID`. A liaison acting outside its own school is rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403); a coordinator acting outside its own site is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The other three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `approved_by_user_id` on a `PurchaseOrder`, or as `submitted_by_user_id` when a director submits an order — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `WarehouseLead`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `PurchaseOrder` but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DonorRelations`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never recorded on or compared to any entity.

The program is deliberately structured around **segregation of duties**. A `ProgramDirector` runs the program — partnering with schools, opening drive cycles and collection sites, curating the catalogue, holding the logical clock, and approving purchase orders and supplemental requests. A `SchoolLiaison` acts for exactly one partner school: maintaining its needs lists, running its distribution events, registering students and filing teacher requests. A `SiteCoordinator` acts for exactly one collection site: taking in donations, driving the corporate pledge dimension, and scheduling sorting and assembly sessions. A `WarehouseLead` owns physical stock: accepting, quarantining, remediating or discarding inspected batches, adjusting inventory counts, drafting and submitting purchase orders, receiving them, and handing out fulfilled teacher requests. A `DonorRelations` operator books cash gifts and their restrictions. All five roles may read every read-only endpoint. Where a purchase order is concerned, the separation is enforced rather than merely encouraged: the operator identifier that approves an order must differ from the one recorded as having submitted it, and an attempt to self-approve is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first, and records sharing an identical `created_at`, which is the ordinary case because the logical clock of §2.5 only moves when an operator moves it, are left in server insertion order with the earliest-created record first. List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads set `response_type` to `"object"` and omit the four pagination fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every persisted resource in this system — `School`, `SupplyItem`, `GradeKitSpec`, `KitSpecLine`, `DriveCycle`, `CollectionSite`, `DonationBatch`, `InventoryPosition`, `CashDonation`, `PurchaseOrder`, `VolunteerEvent`, `DistributionEvent`, `StudentRegistration` and `TeacherRequest` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by the client. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or was produced as a downstream effect of some other operation. Whenever a resource's `version` is incremented, its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant in the same write.

The version counter is not decorative. It is the coordination token for the concurrency rules of §2.8, and it is also the primary evidence that a cascade actually ran: when an operation is specified below as touching five entities, all five carry an incremented `version` afterwards, not just the one named in the request. Resources that a cascade evaluated but did not change do not have their `version` incremented.

Enumerated values are exact, case-sensitive strings and are never abbreviated on the wire. Grade bands are `K_2`, `G3_5`, `G6_8` and `G9_12`. Catalogue categories are `WRITING`, `PAPER`, `BINDER`, `HYGIENE` and `BACKPACK`. Site types are `RETAIL`, `CORPORATE`, `SCHOOL` and `LIBRARY`. Batch conditions are `NEW`, `GENTLY_USED` and `DAMAGED`. Cash restriction types are `UNRESTRICTED`, `ITEM_RESTRICTED` and `SCHOOL_RESTRICTED`. Volunteer session types are `SORTING` and `ASSEMBLY`. Drive cadences are `BACK_TO_SCHOOL` and `YEAR_ROUND`. The lifecycle enumerations of each entity are given with their workflows in §3.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a **logical clock** which, before any operator has moved it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramDirector` may advance or rewind that clock through an administrative, test-driving capability that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the ISO 8601 UTC instant the clock should now report, and answers with the resulting `current_utc`, rendered with the same three fractional-second digits as every other timestamp in this system. Every lead-time comparison, every `verified_at` stamp on a registration and every `approved_at` stamp on a teacher request reads that logical clock, as do `created_at` and `updated_at`.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve decides the response with HTTP 404. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KIT_SPEC_NOT_FOUND` (used for both a needs list and one of its lines), `ERR_DRIVE_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLECTION_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DONATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASH_DONATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_TEACHER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. The same code set is used for foreign keys carried in a request body; body references are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and again the first failure decides. The reference codes reachable from a request body are `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KIT_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVE_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLECTION_SITE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The entity graph here is flat: every resource is created as a top-level collection member with its parents named by explicit foreign keys in the request body, never by a nested creation path. Nested paths exist only as **reverse-reachability reads** — listing a school's needs lists, a cycle's collection sites, a site's volunteer sessions, or an event's student registrations — and for transition sub-resources. A reverse-reachability read resolves its owning identifier first and returns 404 for that owner before any filter is considered.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder

Validation is not a set of independent checks. Every rule in this system has a fixed priority, the rules are evaluated in strictly ascending priority order, and **the first rule that fails decides the response** — no later rule can override or mask an earlier one, and no earlier rule may be skipped because a later one looks more specific. The ladder, in order, is:

1. Authentication: both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. Authorisation: the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. Request shape: every required body field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every integer inside its declared range, every ISO 8601 datetime parseable, `window_start` strictly earlier than `window_end`, and pagination `limit` between 1 and 100 with `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400.
4. Path existence: every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code, 404.
5. Body-reference existence: every foreign key in the body resolves, in the endpoint's declared input order — otherwise the matching not-found code, 404.
6. School scope: a `SchoolLiaison`'s `X-User-ID` equals the governing school of the resource. This guards eight operations — creating a needs list, publishing or retiring a needs list, adding a needs-list line, creating a distribution event, transitioning a distribution event, creating a student registration, transitioning a student registration, and creating a teacher request. Otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
7. Site scope: a `SiteCoordinator`'s `X-User-ID` equals the governing collection site of the resource. This guards five operations — moving the pledge dimension, creating a donation batch, transitioning a donation batch, creating a volunteer session, and transitioning a volunteer session. Otherwise `ERR_SITE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
8. Segregation of duties: on a purchase-order approval, the approving `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403.
9. State legality: the requested transition is declared from the entity's current state and the requested `target_status` is a legal member of that entity's enumeration. The code depends on the entity: `ERR_INVALID_SCHOOL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_TEACHER_REQUEST_STATE`, all at HTTP 409.
10. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_distribution_event_version` equals the governing event's `version`, and `expected_drive_cycle_version` equals the governing cycle's `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`, 412. This step guards student-registration transitions and purchase-order transitions.
11. Hygiene safety on batch acceptance and remediation — `ERR_UNSAFE_ITEM_CONDITION`, 422.
12. Volunteer supervision ratio on session creation — `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_UNMET`, 422.
13. Governing school partnership is `ACTIVE`. This guards four operations — publishing or retiring a needs list, creating a distribution event, publishing a distribution event, and approving a teacher request. Otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_PARTNERSHIP_INACTIVE`, 409.
14. A published needs list exists for the event's school and grade band and carries at least one line, checked when a distribution event is published — otherwise `ERR_KIT_SPEC_NOT_PUBLISHED`, 409.
15. The referenced catalogue item is active. This guards four operations — adding a needs-list line, creating a donation batch, creating a purchase order, and creating a teacher request. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_INACTIVE`, 422.
16. The governing drive cycle is in a phase that permits the operation. This guards five operations, and on a transition only the target named here — creating a donation batch, accepting a donation batch, submitting a purchase order, publishing or locking a distribution event, and approving a teacher request. Every other transition of those same entities carries no cycle-phase guard at all. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`, 409.
17. Cadence gate on reopening a cycle from distribution back to collection — `ERR_CADENCE_FORBIDS_REOPEN`, 409.
18. Event window containment inside the cycle window, on distribution-event creation and on volunteer-session creation — `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`, 422.
19. Registration lead time on registration creation — `ERR_REGISTRATION_LEAD_TIME`, 422.
20. Site occupation conflict on volunteer-session creation — `ERR_SITE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409.
21. School aggregate seat throughput on distribution-event creation — `ERR_SCHOOL_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`, 409.
22. Uniqueness, guarding three operations: publishing a needs list (`ERR_DUPLICATE_KIT_SPEC`), creating a catalogue item (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`) and creating a student registration (`ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_REGISTRATION`), all at 409.
23. Grade-band agreement on registration creation — `ERR_GRADE_BAND_MISMATCH`, 422.
24. Eligibility evidence when a registration is verified — `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_MET`, 422.
25. Seat capacity when a registration is confirmed — `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_FULL`, 409.
26. Assembly output ceiling when a volunteer session is completed — `ERR_ASSEMBLY_OVER_CAPACITY`, 422.
27. Pledge completion when a corporate pledge is marked fulfilled — `ERR_PLEDGE_SHORTFALL`, 409.
28. Inventory availability. This guards three operations — student-registration transitions, teacher-request transitions, and warehouse cycle-count adjustments. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`, 409.
29. Cash restriction consistency on cash-gift creation — `ERR_CASH_RESTRICTION_VIOLATION`, 422.
30. Funding coverage when a purchase order is submitted — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, 409.
31. Cycle supplemental cap when a teacher request is approved — `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 409.
32. Per-school supplemental sub-cap when a teacher request is approved — `ERR_SCHOOL_SUBCAP_EXCEEDED`, 409.

Because several business rules of steps 11 through 32 restate a precondition that also appears in a state machine's transition definition, the boundary of step 9 needs stating precisely. **Step 9 never reports a condition that a rule of priority 11 or higher also states.** Hygiene sealing, volunteer supervision, school partnership status, published-needs-list existence, catalogue activity, the cycle-phase guards on the five operations named at step 16, the cadence reopen gate, window containment, registration lead time, site occupation overlap, school seat throughput, uniqueness, grade-band agreement, eligibility, seat capacity, the assembly ceiling, pledge completion, inventory coverage, cash restriction consistency, funding coverage and the two supplemental caps are all deferred to their own priority and their own error code. What step 9 does cover is the entity's own from-state legality plus exactly those guards that no later rule names: the drive-cycle-phase guard and the corporate pledge guard on collection-site activation (`ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`), the open-batch guard, the live-event guard and the cash-balance identity guard on drive-cycle transitions (`ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`), the at-least-one-line guard and the band-in-use guard on needs-list transitions (`ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`), and the live-event guard on school archival (`ERR_INVALID_SCHOOL_STATE`). It likewise covers the governing site's `operational_status` guard on batch inspection (`ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`) and on starting a volunteer session (`ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`), the open-batch guard on closing a collection site together with the remaining pledge-dimension preconditions (`ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`), and the draft-list guard on adding a needs-list line (`ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`).

One further ordering lives inside step 9 itself. On a student-registration transition two state checks apply — the registration's own current `eligibility_status`, and the governing event's `status`. The registration's own from-state is checked **first** and fails with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`; only when that passes is the event's status precondition examined, failing with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Governing Parent

Two write operations in this system read decision inputs that live on a parent record rather than on the record being written, and both therefore carry an explicit stale-view guard.

A student-registration transition must carry `expected_distribution_event_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the governing `DistributionEvent`. Seat capacity, waitlist ordering and the provisional fill projection all live on the event, so a client whose view of the event is out of date may be claiming a seat that an already-committed transition took, or reasoning against a fill projection that an inventory cascade has since moved. If the supplied value does not equal the event's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written.

A purchase-order transition must carry `expected_drive_cycle_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the governing `DriveCycle`. The cash waterfall draws from that cycle's donation pool, so a stale view of the cycle means the caller is allocating against balances an already-committed order has drawn down. If the supplied value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written — no allocation is made, no compensation is replayed, no inventory is credited.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Creating a registration, creating an order, moving a school, cycle, site, batch, volunteer session, distribution event or teacher request, and adjusting inventory all proceed without any expected-version input.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and the Two Conservation Identities

Money is held in whole cents as integers throughout; there is no floating-point currency anywhere in the model. Where a rate is applied to cents, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and the point at which that rounding happens is pinned by the rule that applies it — rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different, incorrect answer, so §3 states the order explicitly wherever it matters. Percentages that are surfaced as resource fields — the two fill projections and the two impact indices — are rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places, once, at the end of their computation. Two caps in the supplemental budget are floored rather than rounded, and that too is called out where it applies. Quantities of physical goods and kits are always whole integers, obtained by flooring where a division or a rate is involved.

Two identities are invariants of the system and are asserted after every mutation that could disturb them.

The **unit conservation identity** governs one `InventoryPosition`, which is exactly one catalogue item inside exactly one drive cycle. For every position, the sum of `on_hand_qty`, `reserved_qty`, `distributed_qty` and `damaged_qty` equals the sum of `accepted_donated_qty`, `purchased_received_qty` and `adjustment_qty`. It is asserted after batch acceptance, after a batch is discarded, after purchase receipt, after a warehouse cycle-count adjustment, and after every reservation, release and fulfilment leg of a student registration. Positions belonging to different cycles are independent and are never summed together. Note the consequence for reservations: moving units from `on_hand_qty` into `reserved_qty` and back again shifts terms only on the left-hand side, so both sides must be untouched by any reserve or release.

The **cash conservation identity** governs one `DriveCycle`. Its `cash_received_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over every cash gift of the cycle regardless of restriction; its `cash_allocated_cents` equals both the sum of `allocated_cents` over those gifts and the sum of `total_cost_cents` over the cycle's purchase orders whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `RECEIVED`; and `cash_available_cents` equals `cash_received_cents` minus `cash_allocated_cents`. Orders in `DRAFT` and `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. It is asserted after every cash-gift creation and after every purchase-order transition that allocates or restores funds. Submitting an order and then cancelling it must return every gift to the exact `allocated_cents` it held beforehand, gift by gift.

### 2.10. Cascades, Recomputation and Derived Fields

A large fraction of this system's difficulty lives in what happens *after* the primary write. Several transitions here are multi-hop: hop one mutates the named entity, hop two mutates what hop one implies, hop three mutates what hop two implies, and so on. Each hop is specified in §3 in the workflow that owns it, and each hop's mutations carry their own `version` increment and `updated_at` refresh. Where a cascade cancels or withdraws a record, the cancellation is one-way: reversing the triggering transition later does not resurrect what the cascade tore down.

Derived counters are never client-supplied and are never allowed to drift. A school's `target_student_count`, `students_served_interim_count`, `students_served_audited_count`, `no_show_count`, `kits_delivered_count`, `impact_index_interim` and `impact_index_audited`; a cycle's `cash_received_cents`, `cash_allocated_cents`, `cash_available_cents`, `unrestricted_gross_cents`, `supplemental_cap_cents` and `total_items_on_hand`; a site's `accepted_item_count`; a needs list's `kit_unit_cost_cents` and `line_count`; an event's `provisional_fill_pct`, `confirmed_count`, `waitlist_count` and `served_student_count`; a registration's `reserved_unit_count`; and every quantity field on an inventory position are all maintained by the server as a consequence of the operations described in §3. Each starts at 0 (or at `0.00` for the percentage and index fields, or at an empty list for `funding_allocations`) when its resource is created. A single field is deliberately *not* recomputed once written: a distribution event's `final_fill_pct` is frozen at the moment the event is locked and never moves again.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Partnering With a School and Building Its Grade Needs Lists

Everything downstream begins with a partner school. A `ProgramDirector` registers one by supplying its display `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `district_code` of 1 to 16 characters used for reporting groupings, a `title_i_pct` between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive carried to two decimal places and recording the share of the student body qualifying under Title I (any number inside that range is accepted and stored exactly as supplied, finer precision included), an `enrollment_count` of at least 1, and a `max_concurrent_seats` of at least 1 describing how many distribution seats the school can host at any single instant. The school lands in `PROSPECT` with every computed aggregate at zero.

Partnership then walks a small lifecycle driven by a single `target_status` field: `PROSPECT → MOU_PENDING → ACTIVE`, with `ACTIVE ↔ SUSPENDED` in both directions and `ARCHIVED` as the terminal resting state. Moving to `MOU_PENDING` requires the school to be in `PROSPECT`. Moving from `MOU_PENDING` to `ACTIVE` requires the school to be in `MOU_PENDING` and to have a `max_concurrent_seats` of at least 1. Only an `ACTIVE` school may publish a needs list or stand up a distribution event, which makes the `ACTIVE` state the gate the rest of §3 keeps referring back to. Each of these two moves increments the school's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`.

Suspension is the sharpest edge in this subsection, because it runs a four-hop cascade rather than merely flipping a flag. A school in `ACTIVE` moved to `SUSPENDED` triggers, at hop one, the cancellation of every one of its distribution events whose status is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, each moving to `CANCELLED`; events already in `LOCKED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` are left exactly as they are. Hop two, caused by hop one, withdraws every student registration on each just-cancelled event whose `eligibility_status` is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`, moving each to `WITHDRAWN`. Hop three, caused by hop two, releases the reservations of exactly those registrations that were `CONFIRMED` at hop two: for every line of the event's published needs list, that line's `qty_per_student` is subtracted from the position's `reserved_qty` and added back to its `on_hand_qty`, and the registration's `reserved_unit_count` becomes 0. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes `total_items_on_hand` for every drive cycle whose position moved. The school's `students_served_interim_count`, `students_served_audited_count`, `impact_index_interim` and `impact_index_audited` are then recomputed, the school's `version` is incremented by 1, and every distribution event, student registration and inventory position the cascade touched has its own `version` incremented by 1.

Reactivation from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` is emphatically **not** a compensation. It requires only that the school currently be `SUSPENDED`, it increments the school's `version` by 1, and it leaves the wreckage in place: events cancelled by the suspension cascade stay `CANCELLED` and withdrawn registrations stay `WITHDRAWN`.

Archival is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and in both cases the preconditions and effects are identical: the school's `partnership_status` must currently be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and no distribution event of the school may be sitting in `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` — a live event blocks archival and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_SCHOOL_STATE`. On success every needs list of the school still in `PUBLISHED` moves to `RETIRED`, the school's `target_student_count` is recomputed and therefore becomes 0, both impact indices are refreshed alongside it, and the school's `version` is incremented by 1. `ARCHIVED` is terminal.

The catalogue that needs lists draw on is curated separately by a `ProgramDirector`. A catalogue item carries an `sku` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across the whole catalogue — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` — a human-readable `name`, a `category` of `WRITING`, `PAPER`, `BINDER`, `HYGIENE` or `BACKPACK`, a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 1 recording the replacement cost of a single unit, and a `bulk_pack_size` of at least 1 recording how many units come in a wholesale pack. Two booleans tune its handling: `hygiene_sealed_required`, which defaults to false and, when true, subjects donated stock of that item to the sealed-packaging safety rule of §3.3, and `catalog_active`, which defaults to true and, when false, bars the item from being referenced by any new needs-list line, donation batch, purchase order or teacher request with `ERR_ITEM_INACTIVE`.

A `SchoolLiaison` builds the school's needs lists. Each needs list is opened as a `DRAFT` against a `school_id`, a `grade_band` of `K_2`, `G3_5`, `G6_8` or `G9_12`, and a `student_target_count` of at least 1 stating how many students the school intends to serve in that band this program year; it is created with `kit_unit_cost_cents` at 0 and `line_count` at 0. Lines are then added one at a time to a draft list, each naming its `grade_kit_spec_id`, the `supply_item_id` it requires, a `qty_per_student` of at least 1, and an optional `substitutable` flag that defaults to false; a line aimed at a list that is no longer `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`, a check made before the catalogue-activity rule. The flag is recorded for the assembly floor's benefit — it says an equivalent item may be swapped in during assembly — and it **never** relaxes the inventory coverage rule of §3.7.

Adding or removing a line recomputes the list's two derived numbers, and so does publication. `line_count` is simply the number of lines attached to the list. `kit_unit_cost_cents` is the cost of one complete kit for the band: for each line of the list, multiply that line's `qty_per_student` by the `unit_cost_cents` of the catalogue item it references, and add those products together across every line of the list. There is no status filter — a line has no lifecycle of its own, so all of them participate — and a list with no lines yields 0. Every term is an integer product, so no rounding step arises. Adding a line increments the needs list's `version` by 1.

Publication moves the list from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and is guarded by five conditions: the list must currently be in `DRAFT`; it must carry at least one line, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`; the governing school's `partnership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_PARTNERSHIP_INACTIVE`; no other needs list for the same `school_id` and `grade_band` may already be `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KIT_SPEC`; and every one of its lines must reference a catalogue item whose `catalog_active` is true, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_INACTIVE`. On success `kit_unit_cost_cents` is recomputed, the school's `target_student_count` is recomputed as the sum of `student_target_count` over that school's `PUBLISHED` needs lists, and both the needs list's `version` and the school's `version` are incremented by 1.

Retirement moves a `PUBLISHED` list to `RETIRED`, the terminal state. It requires the list to be `PUBLISHED` and requires that no distribution event for that school and grade band be sitting in `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED`; a band still in use blocks retirement with `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`. Retirement recomputes the school's `target_student_count` over the needs lists that remain `PUBLISHED` and increments both the list's `version` and the school's `version` by 1. Needs lists of one school can be listed with an optional `status` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`, and lines can be listed filtered by `grade_kit_spec_id` and by `supply_item_id`.

### 3.2. Drive Cycles, Cadence, and the Collection Network

A **drive cycle** is the campaign container: a `ProgramDirector` opens one with a `name` such as "Back-to-School 2026", a `cadence`, and the ISO 8601 UTC instants `window_start` and `window_end` that bound it. Cadence is the single most consequential input on the record. A `BACK_TO_SCHOOL` cycle is a one-pass seasonal campaign and demands a long registration lead time; a `YEAR_ROUND` cycle may reopen collection after distribution has already begun and works on a much shorter lead time. Both are covered in the subsections that consume them. The cycle is created in `DRAFT` with every cash aggregate at 0, `total_items_on_hand` at 0 and `version` at 1.

The phase machine is `DRAFT → COLLECTING → SORTING → DISTRIBUTION → RECONCILED → CLOSED`, with two loops back to `COLLECTING`. Opening collection requires the cycle to be in `DRAFT` and requires `window_start` to be strictly earlier than `window_end`. Moving from `COLLECTING` to `SORTING` requires only that the cycle be `COLLECTING`; while a cycle sits in `SORTING`, no new donation batch may be recorded at any of its sites. The first loop returns a `SORTING` cycle to `COLLECTING` and is available to both cadences — its only precondition is that the cycle be in `SORTING`. Moving from `SORTING` to `DISTRIBUTION` additionally requires that no donation batch at any of the cycle's sites still be sitting in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REMEDIATED`; an unresolved batch blocks the move with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. That transition recomputes every inventory position of the cycle and refreshes `total_items_on_hand`.

The second loop is the cadence-gated one. Returning a cycle from `DISTRIBUTION` to `COLLECTING` requires the cycle to be in `DISTRIBUTION` and requires its `cadence` to be `YEAR_ROUND`. A `BACK_TO_SCHOOL` cycle may never make that move, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CADENCE_FORBIDS_REOPEN`. Moving from `DISTRIBUTION` to `RECONCILED` requires the cycle to be in `DISTRIBUTION` and requires that none of its distribution events still be `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED`; when it succeeds, every purchase order of the cycle still in `DRAFT` moves to `CANCELLED`, and the audited impact index is recomputed for every school that hosted a `CLOSED` event of this cycle. The final move from `RECONCILED` to `CLOSED` requires the cycle to be `RECONCILED` and requires the cash books to balance: `cash_allocated_cents` plus `cash_available_cents` must equal `cash_received_cents`. A cycle whose books do not balance cannot be closed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. `CLOSED` is terminal, and every one of these seven moves increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

Collection sites hang off a cycle. A `ProgramDirector` opens one against a `drive_cycle_id`, a display `name`, a `site_type` of `RETAIL`, `CORPORATE`, `SCHOOL` or `LIBRARY`, a `bin_capacity` of at least 1 counting the collection and assembly bins physically standing on the floor, and an optional `pledged_item_count` of at least 0 that defaults to 0 and records the units a corporate partner has committed to collect. The site is created with `operational_status` at `PENDING`, `pledge_status` at `NONE` and `accepted_item_count` at 0. Sites of one cycle can be listed with an optional `site_type` filter.

What makes a site interesting is that it carries **two independent status dimensions that advance separately**. The first, `operational_status`, decides whether the site may receive donations and host volunteer sessions and runs `PENDING → ACTIVE ↔ PAUSED → CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal; a `ProgramDirector` drives it. The second, `pledge_status`, is the corporate-drive pledge lifecycle and runs `NONE → PLEDGED → PLEDGE_ACTIVE → FULFILLED` or `LAPSED`, with both `FULFILLED` and `LAPSED` terminal; a `SiteCoordinator` drives it, and only for its own site. Non-corporate sites stay at `NONE` forever — an attempt to move the pledge dimension of a site whose `site_type` is not `CORPORATE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`, as is any pledge move not declared from that dimension's current value.

Activating a site is the one place where the two dimensions read each other. It requires `operational_status` to be `PENDING`, requires the governing cycle to be in `COLLECTING` or `SORTING`, and — when the site's `site_type` is `CORPORATE` — additionally requires its `pledge_status` to already be `PLEDGED` or `PLEDGE_ACTIVE`. All three of these guards report `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`. Pausing an `ACTIVE` site cancels every volunteer session of that site still in `SCHEDULED`, moving each to `CANCELLED`. Resuming requires the site to be `PAUSED`. Closing is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` and in both cases requires that `operational_status` currently be `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` and that no donation batch of the site still be in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REMEDIATED`. Closing also carries a conditional pledge consequence: if the site's `site_type` is `CORPORATE`, its `pledge_status` is `PLEDGE_ACTIVE`, and its `accepted_item_count` is strictly less than its `pledged_item_count`, then the pledge moves to `LAPSED` in the same write. Every operational move increments the site's `version` by 1.

On the pledge axis, a corporate partner's commitment is registered by moving `NONE` to `PLEDGED`, which requires the pledge dimension to be at `NONE`, requires `site_type` to be `CORPORATE`, and requires `pledged_item_count` to be at least 1. The pledge advances regardless of where the operational dimension stands, so a site still in `PENDING` may already be `PLEDGED` — indeed for a corporate site that is the required order. Moving `PLEDGED` to `PLEDGE_ACTIVE` requires the pledge to be at `PLEDGED` and requires `operational_status` to be `ACTIVE`. Marking a pledge `FULFILLED` requires it to be at `PLEDGE_ACTIVE` and requires `accepted_item_count` to be greater than or equal to `pledged_item_count`; a partner that came up short is rejected with `ERR_PLEDGE_SHORTFALL`. A pledge may also be walked to `LAPSED` deliberately, either from `PLEDGE_ACTIVE` or from `PLEDGED`, in each case requiring only that the pledge currently sit in that state. Every one of these pledge preconditions save the shortfall reports `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`, and so does the open-batch guard that blocks closing a site. Each pledge move increments the site's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. In-Kind Donation Intake, Inspection, and the Acceptance Cascade

A `SiteCoordinator` records an in-kind gift as a **donation batch**, which holds exactly one catalogue SKU. The payload names the `collection_site_id` where the drop-off happened, the `supply_item_id` donated, a `qty_declared` of at least 1 counting the units the donor declared at intake, a `condition` of `NEW`, `GENTLY_USED` or `DAMAGED` recorded on the loading dock, and an optional `sealed` boolean defaulting to false that states whether the units are factory sealed. Intake is phase-gated: the governing drive cycle must be in `COLLECTING`, and a batch recorded against a cycle in any other phase is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`. The referenced catalogue item must have `catalog_active` set to true, or `ERR_ITEM_INACTIVE` applies. The batch is created in `RECEIVED` with `qty_accepted` at 0, `qty_rejected` at 0 and `hold_reason` null. Creation alone moves no stock: it recomputes the `in_transit_qty` of the position for that cycle and item — creating that position if none exists yet, and counting the refresh as a mutation of it, so a position first minted here stands at `version` 2 — and every `PUBLISHED` distribution event whose kit lines touch that item has its `provisional_fill_pct` recomputed as a result. Batches can be listed filtered by `collection_site_id`, `supply_item_id` and `status`.

The batch lifecycle is `RECEIVED → INSPECTED`, then a fork: `INSPECTED → ACCEPTED` or `INSPECTED → QUARANTINED`, with `QUARANTINED → REMEDIATED → ACCEPTED` rejoining the accept path and `QUARANTINED → DISCARDED` leaving it. `ACCEPTED` and `DISCARDED` are the terminal states. Inspection may be driven by either role the route admits — the `SiteCoordinator` who took the drop-off or the `WarehouseLead` — and requires the batch to be in `RECEIVED` and the governing site's `operational_status` to be `ACTIVE`, both guards reporting `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`; it recomputes `in_transit_qty` for the cycle-and-item position and increments the batch's `version` by 1. Everything after inspection belongs to the `WarehouseLead`. A batch move that is not declared from the batch's current `status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

Acceptance is reachable from two states, `INSPECTED` and `REMEDIATED`, and the two paths are specified identically. Both require the batch's `status` to currently be `INSPECTED` or `REMEDIATED`, both require the governing cycle to be in `COLLECTING` or `SORTING`, both require the supplied `qty_rejected` — an optional field defaulting to 0 — to be at least 0, judged as a plain range check at ladder step 3, and no greater than the batch's `qty_declared`, a comparison that needs the batch and is therefore made after the from-state check of step 9 but before the hygiene rule and the cycle-phase gate, although it still answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400, and both enforce the hygiene rule: when the referenced catalogue item has `hygiene_sealed_required` set to true, the batch's `sealed` must be true **and** its `condition` must be `NEW`. A hygiene item that arrived unsealed, or sealed but merely `GENTLY_USED` or `DAMAGED`, is rejected with `ERR_UNSAFE_ITEM_CONDITION`.

Acceptance runs a five-hop cascade, and every hop matters. Hop one sets the batch's `qty_accepted` to its `qty_declared` minus the supplied `qty_rejected` and moves its `status` to `ACCEPTED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, finds the inventory position for the cycle-and-item pair — creating it if none exists yet — and then adds `qty_accepted` to `on_hand_qty`, adds the full `qty_declared` to `accepted_donated_qty`, adds `qty_rejected` to `damaged_qty`, and recomputes `in_transit_qty`. Note carefully that `accepted_donated_qty` takes the *declared* figure, not the accepted one: every unit that entered the building is counted once as received and once, if written off, as damaged, which is precisely what keeps the unit conservation identity of §2.9 balanced. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes the cycle's `total_items_on_hand` — the sum of `on_hand_qty` over every inventory position of that cycle, with reserved, distributed, damaged and in-transit units all excluded — and adds `qty_accepted` to the site's `accepted_item_count`. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes `provisional_fill_pct` on every distribution event of that cycle that is currently `PUBLISHED` and whose grade band's published needs list has a line for this item.

Hop five, caused by hop four, is the **waitlist promotion sweep**, and it is where fresh stock turns into confirmed seats. On each event touched by hop four, registrations sitting in `WAITLISTED` are promoted to `CONFIRMED` in order of `verified_at` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the sweep continues only while two conditions both hold: the event's `confirmed_count` is strictly less than its `seat_capacity`, and every line of the published needs list has an `on_hand_qty` at least equal to that line's `qty_per_student`. Each promotion reserves its units — for every line, subtract `qty_per_student` from `on_hand_qty` and add it to `reserved_qty` — and then refreshes the event's `confirmed_count` and `waitlist_count` along with the school's `students_served_interim_count` and `impact_index_interim`. Versions are incremented by 1 on the batch, the inventory position, the drive cycle and the collection site, and on every distribution event, student registration and school that hops four and five actually mutated — an event whose projection moves and which then promotes one or more registrations takes one increment per such mutation rather than a single combined one.

Quarantine is the divergent branch and must not be given the acceptance branch's effects. Moving an `INSPECTED` batch to `QUARANTINED` requires only that the batch be `INSPECTED`, and takes an optional free-text `hold_reason` from the request, recording null when the field is absent. It records that `hold_reason` and credits **nothing** to inventory. It recomputes the position's `in_transit_qty` downward, because a quarantined batch leaves the in-transit expectation set, and it recomputes `provisional_fill_pct` on every `PUBLISHED` distribution event of the cycle that touches this item — a recomputation that can only lower a projection, and which therefore never promotes anybody. The batch's `version` is incremented by 1.

Remediation walks a `QUARANTINED` batch back onto the accept path. It requires the batch to be `QUARANTINED` and, when the item has `hygiene_sealed_required` set to true, again requires `sealed` to be true and `condition` to be `NEW`, failing with `ERR_UNSAFE_ITEM_CONDITION` otherwise. It recomputes `in_transit_qty` upward as the batch rejoins the expectation set, clears `hold_reason` back to null, and increments the batch's `version` by 1. Discarding, by contrast, requires the batch to be `QUARANTINED` and writes the whole consignment off: `qty_declared` is added to the position's `damaged_qty` and the same `qty_declared` is added to its `adjustment_qty`, so the conservation identity still balances on both sides, and the batch's `version` is incremented by 1.

The inventory position itself is a per-cycle, per-item ledger row identified by its `drive_cycle_id` and `supply_item_id`, and it exists to make the identity of §2.9 auditable. Its `on_hand_qty` counts units physically available and unreserved; `reserved_qty` counts units held against `CONFIRMED` registrations; `distributed_qty` counts units handed to families on `FULFILLED` registrations; `damaged_qty` counts units written off at acceptance or by a negative cycle count; `accepted_donated_qty` and `purchased_received_qty` count the cumulative inflow from accepted batches and received purchase orders respectively; and `adjustment_qty` accumulates the signed deltas applied by warehouse cycle counts. `in_transit_qty` is different in kind from the rest: it is an *expectation*, not a holding, computed as the sum of `ordered_qty` over this cycle-and-item pair's purchase orders in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, plus the sum of `qty_declared` over donation batches of this item at this cycle's sites sitting in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REMEDIATED`. It participates in no conservation identity and is consumed only by the provisional fill variant of §3.6. Positions can be listed filtered by `drive_cycle_id` and `supply_item_id`.

Physical counts drift, so a `WarehouseLead` may post a cycle-count adjustment naming a `drive_cycle_id`, a `supply_item_id`, a signed `delta_on_hand_qty`, an optional signed `delta_damaged_qty` that is treated as 0 when omitted, and a `reason` explaining the correction; the position is created if it does not yet exist, and that creation stands even when the adjustment is then refused. The adjustment adds `delta_on_hand_qty` to `on_hand_qty`, adds `delta_damaged_qty` to `damaged_qty`, and adds the sum of those two deltas to `adjustment_qty` so that the conservation identity continues to hold. An adjustment that would drive `on_hand_qty` below zero is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`; no comparable floor is applied to `damaged_qty`. On success the cycle's `total_items_on_hand` is recomputed, every `PUBLISHED` distribution event touching the item has its `provisional_fill_pct` recomputed and its waitlist promoted by exactly the sweep described as hop five above, and the cycle's `version` is incremented by 1. The position's `version` is incremented by 1 as well when the position already existed before this adjustment; when the adjustment is itself the write that creates the position, that creation is not also counted as a mutation of it, so the new position stands at the `version` 1 that §2.4 gives a freshly created resource before any further cascade hop touches it.

### 3.4. Sorting and Assembly Sessions on the Site Floor

A `SiteCoordinator` schedules volunteer sessions at its own site. The request names the `collection_site_id`, an `event_type` of either `SORTING` or `ASSEMBLY`, the ISO 8601 UTC `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the occupation, and a `volunteer_count` of at least 1 counting everybody expected including minors. Two safety inputs are optional and both default to 0: `minor_volunteer_count`, which counts volunteers under 18 and must be at least 0 and no greater than `volunteer_count`, and `adult_supervisor_count`, which counts designated adult supervisors and must be at least 0. The session is created in `SCHEDULED`, with `assembled_kit_count` at 0.

Child safety comes before scheduling. The program requires one designated adult supervisor for every eight minors or part thereof: `adult_supervisor_count` must be at least the number of minors divided by eight and rounded **up** to a whole number. Eight minors need one supervisor, nine need two, sixteen need two and seventeen need three; zero minors need none. A session that cannot meet the ratio is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_UNMET`. The session's window must also sit entirely inside the governing drive cycle's window — the cycle's `window_start` must be no later than the session's `window_start`, and the session's `window_end` must be no later than the cycle's `window_end` — or the request is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`.

A site has one floor, and turning that floor around between sessions takes time. Volunteer session intervals at one site are treated as half-open, including their `window_start` and excluding their `window_end`, and are then padded by a turnover buffer of forty-five minutes on **both** sides. Concretely, a proposed session collides with an existing one when the proposed `window_start` is earlier than the existing `window_end` plus forty-five minutes **and** the existing `window_start` is earlier than the proposed `window_end` plus forty-five minutes. Only existing sessions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` are considered; sessions already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` never block anything. A collision is reported as `ERR_SITE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

When the session survives those gates, the server pins its `planned_kit_capacity` once, at creation, and never recomputes it afterwards. The computation takes the smallest of three independent ceilings. The **volunteer ceiling** starts from the session's duration in hours, taken as an exact fractional value from `window_start` to `window_end`. An assembly session builds finished kits at a base rate of 6 kits per volunteer-hour; a sorting session triages stock rather than building kits, so its base rate is 0 and a `SORTING` session therefore always pins a `planned_kit_capacity` of 0. A fatigue factor then discounts long shifts: a session lasting four hours or less carries a factor of exactly 1.0, and beyond that the factor is 1.0 minus five hundredths for each whole hour, rounded up, by which the duration exceeds four hours, with a hard floor of 0.70 — that is, the factor is the larger of 0.70 and that computed value. The volunteer ceiling is `volunteer_count` multiplied by the base rate, multiplied by the duration in hours, multiplied by the fatigue factor, and then floored to a whole number of kits; the floor is applied here, before any comparison, never afterwards. The **station ceiling** is the site's `bin_capacity` multiplied by 12, on the accounting that each bin supports a dozen kit stations. The **demand ceiling** is the total kit demand still open in the same drive cycle: the sum, over every distribution event of that cycle whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, of that event's `seat_capacity` minus its `served_student_count`; events in `LOCKED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. `planned_kit_capacity` is the smallest of the volunteer ceiling, the station ceiling and the demand ceiling.

The session lifecycle is short. Starting a session moves it from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and requires both that the session be `SCHEDULED` and that the site's `operational_status` be `ACTIVE`, both guards reporting `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. Completing it moves `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED` and takes an `assembled_kit_count` from the request recording the kits actually built; the session must be `IN_PROGRESS` — a check made before the field's own presence is judged, so a completion aimed at a session in the wrong state answers `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` even when `assembled_kit_count` was omitted — and the reported figure must be present, at least 0, and no greater than the pinned `planned_kit_capacity`; a negative figure fails the range check of ladder step 3 and a missing one is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an overstated count is refused with `ERR_ASSEMBLY_OVER_CAPACITY`. Cancellation is reachable from either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, requires the session to currently be in one of those two states, and releases the floor: once cancelled, the session's interval stops blocking the site and a later session may occupy that window. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, and a session move not declared from the session's current `status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE` — volunteer sessions and distribution events share that code. Every one of these moves increments the session's `version` by 1, and sessions of one site can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.5. Cash Gifts, Restrictions, and Buying What the Drive Did Not Collect

Cash is booked by a `DonorRelations` operator against a `drive_cycle_id`, naming the `donor_name` of record, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and a `restriction_type` of `UNRESTRICTED`, `ITEM_RESTRICTED` or `SCHOOL_RESTRICTED`. Two optional foreign keys carry the restriction itself, and exactly which of them may be populated is decided by the restriction type. Both are resolved as body references first, in the declared input order `drive_cycle_id`, then `restricted_supply_item_id`, then `restricted_school_id`, so an identifier that names nothing answers its own 404 even when the restriction type forbids that field being populated at all. When `restriction_type` is `ITEM_RESTRICTED`, `restricted_supply_item_id` is required and `restricted_school_id` must be null. When it is `SCHOOL_RESTRICTED`, `restricted_school_id` is required and `restricted_supply_item_id` must be null. When it is `UNRESTRICTED`, both must be null. Any other combination is refused with `ERR_CASH_RESTRICTION_VIOLATION`. A gift is created with `allocated_cents` at 0, `available_cents` equal to its `amount_cents`, and `status` at `RECEIVED`.

A gift's `status` is not something anyone sets; it is read straight off the allocation figures. It is `RECEIVED` while `allocated_cents` is 0, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` while `allocated_cents` is greater than 0 but strictly less than `amount_cents`, and `FULLY_ALLOCATED` once `allocated_cents` equals `amount_cents`. `available_cents` is always `amount_cents` minus `allocated_cents`. Booking a gift refreshes four numbers on the cycle: `cash_received_cents`, which sums `amount_cents` over every gift of the cycle regardless of restriction; `cash_available_cents`, which is `cash_received_cents` minus `cash_allocated_cents`; `unrestricted_gross_cents`, which sums `amount_cents` over only those gifts whose `restriction_type` is `UNRESTRICTED` and which is taken **gross**, never net of what has already been allocated; and `supplemental_cap_cents`, described in §3.8. The cycle's `version` is incremented by 1 in the same write. Gifts can be listed filtered by `drive_cycle_id`, `restricted_school_id`, `restricted_supply_item_id` and `restriction_type`.

When a needs list cannot be filled from donated stock alone, a `WarehouseLead` drafts a purchase order naming a `drive_cycle_id`, a `supply_item_id` whose `catalog_active` must be true, and a `requested_unit_gap` of at least 1 stating how many units of unfilled need the order is meant to close before pack rounding. The order is created in `DRAFT` with an empty `funding_allocations` list, a null `submitted_by_user_id` and a null `approved_by_user_id`, and its costing is computed once at creation and frozen for the life of the order. Orders can be listed filtered by `drive_cycle_id`, `supply_item_id` and `status`.

Costing runs in a pinned order, because the order of the steps changes the answer. First, the wholesaler only sells whole packs, so the number of packs is the `requested_unit_gap` divided by the item's `bulk_pack_size` and rounded **up** to a whole number, and `ordered_qty` is that pack count multiplied back by `bulk_pack_size`. Second, `line_subtotal_cents` is `ordered_qty` multiplied by the item's `unit_cost_cents`. Third — and this is the step most easily got wrong — the administration fee rate is selected from the **subtotal**, never from the total: the rate is nine percent when `line_subtotal_cents` is 50,000 cents or less, seven percent when `line_subtotal_cents` is above 50,000 cents and at most 200,000 cents, and five percent when `line_subtotal_cents` is above 200,000 cents. Reading the tier off `total_cost_cents` is circular, because the total depends on the fee, which depends on the tier, and it is wrong. Fourth, the fee is levied **per pack and rounded per pack**: the cost of one pack is `bulk_pack_size` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`, the fee for one pack is that pack cost multiplied by the selected rate and then rounded half up to a whole cent, and `admin_fee_cents` is that rounded per-pack fee multiplied by the number of packs. Rounding the whole line's fee once instead — taking `line_subtotal_cents` times the rate and rounding that — is a different and rejected reading; for a pack size of 3 at 175 cents a unit across 7 packs it yields 331 where the correct answer is 329. Finally, `total_cost_cents` is `line_subtotal_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`, and it is the amount the cash waterfall must cover in full.

A purchase-order move that is not declared from the order's current `status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Every transition of a purchase order carries `expected_drive_cycle_version` and is checked against the cycle's current `version` as described in §2.8. Submission moves the order from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and may be performed by either a `WarehouseLead` or a `ProgramDirector`. It requires the order to be in `DRAFT` and the governing cycle to be in `COLLECTING`, `SORTING` or `DISTRIBUTION` — any other phase is `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID` — and it requires the funding waterfall to cover `total_cost_cents` completely.

The **funding waterfall** draws from two buckets in a fixed sequence. Bucket one holds the cash gifts of the same drive cycle whose `restriction_type` is `ITEM_RESTRICTED`, whose `restricted_supply_item_id` equals the order's `supply_item_id`, and whose `available_cents` is greater than 0, ordered by `created_at` ascending. Bucket two holds the gifts of the same cycle whose `restriction_type` is `UNRESTRICTED` and whose `available_cents` is greater than 0, again ordered by `created_at` ascending. Gifts marked `SCHOOL_RESTRICTED` are never eligible at all: a purchase order is a cycle-level object with no beneficiary school. Starting with the order's `total_cost_cents` as the amount still to be funded, the waterfall walks bucket one first and then bucket two, and within a bucket walks gifts oldest first so the oldest gift drains before a newer one is touched; `created_at` is the only tie-break and entity identifiers are never consulted. From each gift it takes the smaller of the amount still outstanding and that gift's `available_cents`, appends an entry recording that gift's `cash_donation_id` together with the `amount_cents` taken to the order's `funding_allocations` list, adds the taken amount to the gift's `allocated_cents`, and reduces the outstanding amount accordingly, stopping the moment the outstanding amount reaches zero. Every take is an exact integer minimum, so no rounding arises. If any amount is still outstanding after both buckets are exhausted, the submission is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` and **nothing at all is written** — no partial allocation is left behind.

A successful submission therefore writes the ordered `funding_allocations` list, raises `allocated_cents` on each gift it drew from and recomputes each of those gifts' derived `status`, recomputes the cycle's `cash_allocated_cents` and `cash_available_cents` and increments the cycle's `version` by 1, raises `in_transit_qty` on the cycle-and-item inventory position by `ordered_qty` and consequently recomputes `provisional_fill_pct` on every `PUBLISHED` distribution event touching that item, records the caller's `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id`, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Approval belongs to a `ProgramDirector` alone and moves `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`. It requires the order to be `SUBMITTED` and requires the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id` — the segregation-of-duties bar of §2.1, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It records the approver in `approved_by_user_id` and increments both the order's `version` and the cycle's `version` by 1. No stock and no cash move at approval.

Receipt belongs to the `WarehouseLead` and moves `APPROVED` to `RECEIVED`, the terminal success state. It requires the order to be `APPROVED`. On receipt the goods land: `ordered_qty` is added to the position's `on_hand_qty` and to its `purchased_received_qty`, and subtracted from its `in_transit_qty`, so what was expectation becomes holding without disturbing the conservation identity. The cycle's `total_items_on_hand` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by 1, every `PUBLISHED` distribution event of the cycle touching the item has its `provisional_fill_pct` recomputed and its waitlist promoted by exactly the sweep specified as hop five of §3.3, and the order's `version` is incremented by 1.

Cancellation is reachable from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `APPROVED`, and in all three cases the precondition is the same — the order's `status` must currently be one of those three — and either a `WarehouseLead` or a `ProgramDirector` may perform it. From `SUBMITTED` and from `APPROVED` the effects are identical and are a true compensation: the `funding_allocations` list is replayed in **reverse** order, each entry's gift has its `allocated_cents` decreased by exactly that entry's `amount_cents` and its derived `status` recomputed, and the list is then emptied. Every gift must end up holding precisely the `allocated_cents` it held before the order was submitted, gift by gift. The position's `in_transit_qty` is reduced by `ordered_qty` and every `PUBLISHED` event touching the item has its `provisional_fill_pct` recomputed; the cycle's `cash_allocated_cents` and `cash_available_cents` are recomputed and its `version` incremented by 1; and the order's `version` is incremented by 1. Cancelling from `DRAFT` is the degenerate case: the `funding_allocations` list is already empty, so no cash is restored, and a draft order never entered the in-transit expectation set, so nothing is removed from it — but the cycle's `version` and the order's `version` are each still incremented by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.6. Distribution Events, Seat Throughput, and the Two Fill Projections

A `SchoolLiaison` stands up a distribution event for its own school, naming the `school_id`, the `drive_cycle_id` whose inventory the event will draw on, the `grade_band` served, the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the day, and a `seat_capacity` of at least 1 stating the maximum number of confirmed registrations. The governing school must be `ACTIVE` at creation time, or `ERR_SCHOOL_PARTNERSHIP_INACTIVE` applies, and the window must sit inside the cycle window exactly as volunteer sessions must, reported as `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`. The event is created in `DRAFT` with `provisional_fill_pct` and `final_fill_pct` both at `0.00` and `confirmed_count`, `waitlist_count` and `served_student_count` all at 0.

Distribution intervals behave differently from volunteer intervals, and the difference is deliberate. A distribution event's interval is **closed**: it includes both its `window_start` and its `window_end`, so two events that merely touch at an endpoint do overlap. There is no turnover buffer, and overlapping events at one school are perfectly legal — a school may run two grade bands in adjacent gyms at the same hour. What is not legal is exceeding the school's aggregate floor capacity. The request is refused only when, at some instant inside the proposed interval, the sum of `seat_capacity` over the proposed event together with every overlapping event of the same school whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` exceeds that school's `max_concurrent_seats`. Cancelled and closed events contribute nothing. Breaching that ceiling is reported as `ERR_SCHOOL_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`.

The event lifecycle is `DRAFT → PUBLISHED → LOCKED → CLOSED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from `DRAFT` and from `PUBLISHED`; `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Publishing requires the event to be in `DRAFT`, requires the governing school's `partnership_status` to be `ACTIVE`, requires a needs list with status `PUBLISHED` to exist for that exact combination of `school_id` and `grade_band` and to carry at least one line — otherwise `ERR_KIT_SPEC_NOT_PUBLISHED` — requires the governing cycle to be in `COLLECTING`, `SORTING` or `DISTRIBUTION`, and re-asserts the aggregate seat ceiling described above, again reporting `ERR_SCHOOL_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`. On success the provisional fill projection is computed and written to `provisional_fill_pct`, and the event's `version` is incremented by 1.

Both fill projections answer the same question — what fraction of the interested students could actually walk away with a complete kit — but they answer it from different evidence, and the two must never be conflated. Both start from the needs list with status `PUBLISHED` for the event's `school_id` and `grade_band`; needs lists in `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` are excluded outright, and when no published list exists at all the projection is `0.00`. For each line of that list, the projection looks up the inventory position for the event's `drive_cycle_id` paired with that line's `supply_item_id`, treating a position that does not exist as all zeros, and computes how many students that one line could cover: the available units for that line divided by the line's `qty_per_student` and floored to a whole number of students. Because a kit is not a kit unless every line is present, the number of coverable students is the **smallest** of those per-line figures across all lines of the list. The projected number served is then the smaller of that coverable figure and the variant's headcount. If the variant's headcount is 0 the projection is `0.00` — the empty set is a boundary case answered directly and never a division. Otherwise the percentage is 100 multiplied by the projected number served, divided by the headcount, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the rounding is applied to the percentage value, not to the underlying ratio.

The **provisional** variant is the optimistic operations number. Its available units for a line are the position's `on_hand_qty` plus its `in_transit_qty` — that is, it counts stock already in the building together with the purchase orders in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` and the donation batches in `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED` or `REMEDIATED` that are still expected to arrive. Its headcount is the number of the event's registrations whose `eligibility_status` is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`. It is written to `provisional_fill_pct` when the event is published and recomputed on every subsequent inventory movement that touches an item on the event's needs list while the event remains `PUBLISHED`, as well as whenever a new registration grows the headcount.

The **final** variant is the conservative closing number. Its available units for a line are the position's `on_hand_qty` alone; in-transit expectation is discarded entirely. Its headcount counts only the event's registrations whose `eligibility_status` is `VERIFIED` or `CONFIRMED`, so `PENDING` and `WAITLISTED` registrations drop out of the denominator. It is computed exactly once, at the moment the event moves from `PUBLISHED` to `LOCKED`, written to `final_fill_pct`, and never recomputed afterwards.

Locking requires the event to be `PUBLISHED` and requires the governing drive cycle to be in `DISTRIBUTION`; a cycle in any other phase yields `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`. Besides freezing `final_fill_pct`, locking closes the door on new registrations for that event, recomputes the school's `impact_index_interim`, and increments the event's `version` by 1.

Closing a `LOCKED` event runs a three-hop settlement cascade. Hop one moves every registration of the event still sitting in `CONFIRMED` to `NO_SHOW`, processing them in order of `verified_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Hop two, caused by hop one, releases each of those reservations: for every line of the needs list, that line's `qty_per_student` is subtracted from the position's `reserved_qty` and added back to its `on_hand_qty`, and the registration's `reserved_unit_count` becomes 0. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes the cycle's `total_items_on_hand` and the school's `no_show_count`, `students_served_interim_count`, `students_served_audited_count`, `impact_index_interim` and `impact_index_audited`, with the audited index computed by its own variant. Separately, every registration still sitting in `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and because none of those states holds a reservation there is nothing to release for them. The event's `served_student_count` is refreshed, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the event, on the governing drive cycle whether or not its `total_items_on_hand` actually moved, and on every registration, inventory position and school the cascade mutated.

Cancelling an event is reachable from `DRAFT` and from `PUBLISHED`, requires the event's `status` to currently be one of those two, and behaves identically in both cases: every registration whose `eligibility_status` is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and those that were `CONFIRMED` release their reservations exactly as in hop two of the closing cascade. The governing cycle's `total_items_on_hand` and the school's aggregates are refreshed as well, but here — unlike at closing — neither the cycle's nor the school's `version` is incremented; only the event's `version` is incremented by 1, alongside those of the registrations and positions the cancellation moved. Distribution events can be listed filtered by `school_id`, `drive_cycle_id` and `status`, and the registrations of one event can be listed with an optional `eligibility_status` filter.

### 3.7. Registering Students, Reserving Kits, and Working the Waitlist

A `SchoolLiaison` registers a student against a `distribution_event_id`, supplying a `student_reference` of 1 to 40 characters as issued by the school, the student's `grade_band`, the `guardian_name` of the registering guardian of record, and two optional eligibility booleans that both default to false: `free_lunch_eligible`, recording free or reduced lunch status, and `income_verified`, recording that household income documentation has been received. The registration is created in `PENDING` with `verified_at` null and `reserved_unit_count` at 0.

Four gates apply at registration time, and they fire in the ladder order of §2.7. The event must be `PUBLISHED`; a registration aimed at an event in any other state — including a `LOCKED` event, which accepts no further registrations at all — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. The cadence-dependent lead time then applies, measured against the logical clock of §2.5: the interval from the current logical instant to the event's `window_start` must be at least forty-eight hours when the governing cycle's `cadence` is `BACK_TO_SCHOOL`, and at least two hours when it is `YEAR_ROUND`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_LEAD_TIME`. Uniqueness follows: at most one registration may exist for a given pairing of `distribution_event_id` and `student_reference` among registrations whose `eligibility_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, so a withdrawn registration frees the reference for reuse and a live one does not; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_REGISTRATION`. Finally the student's `grade_band` must equal the event's `grade_band`, or `ERR_GRADE_BAND_MISMATCH` applies. A successful registration recomputes the event's `provisional_fill_pct`, because the provisional headcount just grew, and increments the event's `version` by 1.

The registration lifecycle is the most cyclic in the system: `PENDING → VERIFIED`, then `VERIFIED ↔ CONFIRMED` by way of `WAITLISTED` in both directions, with `FULFILLED`, `NO_SHOW`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` as the four terminal states. Every registration transition requested through the API carries `expected_distribution_event_version` and is subject to the stale-view guard of §2.8; the promotion sweep that runs inside an inventory-crediting cascade performs the same `WAITLISTED → CONFIRMED` move without any client-supplied version, since no client is involved. Every leg below that moves units also refreshes the governing cycle's `total_items_on_hand`, but exactly one of them — the compensating waitlist leg — increments the cycle's own `version`.

Verification moves `PENDING` to `VERIFIED`. It requires the registration to be `PENDING`, requires the governing event to be `PUBLISHED`, and requires eligibility evidence: at least one of `free_lunch_eligible` or `income_verified` must be true, and a registration with neither is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_MET`. On success `verified_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant — the primary sort key of every waitlist ordering in this document — and both the event's `version` and the registration's `version` are incremented by 1. A `PENDING` registration may instead be moved to `REJECTED`, which requires only that it be `PENDING` and which increments the event's and the registration's `version` by 1 apiece.

Confirmation is reachable from `VERIFIED` and from `WAITLISTED`, and the two paths are specified identically. Both require the registration's `eligibility_status` to currently be `VERIFIED` or `WAITLISTED`, both require the event to be `PUBLISHED`, both require the event's `confirmed_count` to be strictly less than its `seat_capacity` — a full house is `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_FULL` — and both require that, for every line of the event's published needs list, the position's `on_hand_qty` be at least that line's `qty_per_student`, since a partial kit is no kit; a shortfall is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`. Note that a line's `substitutable` flag has no bearing here whatsoever. Confirmation is the forward leg of the unit conservation identity: for every line, `qty_per_student` is subtracted from `on_hand_qty` and added to `reserved_qty`, leaving both sides of the identity untouched. The registration's `reserved_unit_count` is set to the sum of `qty_per_student` over the needs list's lines, the event's `confirmed_count` and `waitlist_count` are refreshed, and the school's `students_served_interim_count` and `impact_index_interim` are recomputed. Versions are incremented by 1 on the event, the registration, the inventory positions touched and the school.

Waitlisting is likewise reachable from two states, and here the two paths differ in exactly one respect. Both require the registration to currently be `VERIFIED` or `CONFIRMED` and both require the event to be `PUBLISHED`. Coming from `VERIFIED` there is nothing to give back, so the move only refreshes the event's `waitlist_count` and increments the versions. Coming from `CONFIRMED` the move is the compensating leg of the unit conservation identity: for every line of the needs list, `qty_per_student` is subtracted from `reserved_qty` and added back to `on_hand_qty`, the registration's `reserved_unit_count` becomes 0, the event's `confirmed_count` and `waitlist_count` are refreshed, the school's `students_served_interim_count` and `impact_index_interim` are recomputed, the cycle's `total_items_on_hand` is recomputed, and `version` is incremented by 1 on the registration, the event, the positions, the cycle and the school. A seat released this way is immediately available to the next promotion sweep.

Fulfilment is the happy ending: a `CONFIRMED` registration moves to `FULFILLED` when the event is `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED`. For every line of the needs list, `qty_per_student` moves out of `reserved_qty` and into `distributed_qty` — the units leave the building rather than returning to the shelf — and `reserved_unit_count` becomes 0. The event's `served_student_count` and `confirmed_count` are refreshed, and the school's `students_served_audited_count`, `kits_delivered_count`, `impact_index_interim` and `impact_index_audited` are all recomputed, with one kit counted per fulfilled registration. Versions are incremented by 1 on the registration, the event, the positions and the school.

A `CONFIRMED` registration whose family never arrives is moved to `NO_SHOW`, which likewise requires the event to be `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` and which releases the reservation back to `on_hand_qty` in exactly the manner of the compensating waitlist leg. The school's `no_show_count` and `impact_index_audited` are then recomputed and the versions incremented by 1. This is also the move the closing cascade of §3.6 performs on its own initiative.

Withdrawal is the general escape hatch and is available whenever the registration's `eligibility_status` is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`. If the registration was `CONFIRMED` its reservation is released exactly as above; otherwise there is nothing to release. In either case the event's counters and the school's aggregates are refreshed and the versions are incremented by 1. All four of `FULFILLED`, `NO_SHOW`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal, and no transition leads out of them. A registration move that is not declared from the registration's current `eligibility_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`, and — as §2.7 sets out — that check is made before the governing event's own status precondition, which fails with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`.

### 3.8. The Teacher Supplemental Programme and Its Two Budget Caps

Classroom needs do not stop when the backpacks are handed out, so a `SchoolLiaison` may file a supplemental request on behalf of a teacher. The request names the `school_id`, the `drive_cycle_id` whose supplemental budget it draws on, the `supply_item_id` wanted, the `teacher_name` making the ask, a `qty_requested` of at least 1, and a `justification` explaining the classroom need. The referenced catalogue item must have `catalog_active` set to true, or `ERR_ITEM_INACTIVE` applies. On creation the server computes `request_cost_cents` as `qty_requested` multiplied by the item's `unit_cost_cents`, and files the request in `SUBMITTED` with `approved_at` null. Requests can be listed filtered by `school_id`, `drive_cycle_id`, `supply_item_id` and `status`.

The budget this draws on is a fixed share of the cycle's unrestricted giving. Whenever a cash gift is booked, the cycle's `supplemental_cap_cents` is recomputed as fifteen percent of its `unrestricted_gross_cents`, **floored** rather than rounded to a whole cent. Only unrestricted gifts count towards that base, and they count gross — what has already been allocated to purchase orders does not shrink the supplemental cap.

Approval belongs to a `ProgramDirector` and moves `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`. It requires the request to be `SUBMITTED`, requires the governing school's `partnership_status` to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SCHOOL_PARTNERSHIP_INACTIVE`), and requires the governing cycle to be in `COLLECTING`, `SORTING` or `DISTRIBUTION` (`ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_INVALID`). Two spending caps then apply, in that order.

The **cycle cap** compares the cycle's committed supplemental spend against `supplemental_cap_cents`. Committed spend is the sum of `request_cost_cents` over this cycle's requests whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `FULFILLED`; requests still `SUBMITTED` and requests already `DENIED` never consume budget. The approval passes when that committed spend plus this request's own `request_cost_cents` is less than or equal to `supplemental_cap_cents` — **equality passes**, and only a strict overshoot fails, with `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The **per-school sub-cap** is conditional and lies dormant in small programmes. Count the schools whose `partnership_status` is `ACTIVE` and that have at least one needs list in `PUBLISHED`. If that count is fewer than three, the sub-cap does not apply at all and the approval passes on the cycle cap alone. If the count is three or more, the sub-cap activates: the per-school ceiling is forty percent of `supplemental_cap_cents`, again floored rather than rounded to a whole cent, and the approval passes only when the sum of `request_cost_cents` over this cycle's requests belonging to the **same** `school_id` with `status` in `APPROVED` or `FULFILLED`, plus this request's own `request_cost_cents`, is less than or equal to that ceiling. Equality passes here too; a strict overshoot is `ERR_SCHOOL_SUBCAP_EXCEEDED`. A successful approval stamps `approved_at` with the current logical-clock instant and increments the request's `version` by 1.

A `ProgramDirector` may instead move a `SUBMITTED` request to `DENIED`, which requires only that it be `SUBMITTED` and which increments the request's `version` by 1. Fulfilment belongs to the `WarehouseLead`: an `APPROVED` request moves to `FULFILLED` provided the inventory position for the request's `drive_cycle_id` and `supply_item_id` has an `on_hand_qty` of at least `qty_requested`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`. Fulfilment subtracts `qty_requested` from `on_hand_qty` and adds it to `distributed_qty`, recomputes the cycle's `total_items_on_hand`, and increments `version` by 1 on the request, the position and the cycle. `DENIED` and `FULFILLED` are both terminal, and a request move not declared from the request's current `status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TEACHER_REQUEST_STATE`.

### 3.9. Impact Reporting: The Interim Number and the Audited Number

Every partner school carries two impact indices side by side, and the difference between them is the whole point. `impact_index_interim` is the operations dashboard number, available while events are still running and computed from optimistic evidence. `impact_index_audited` is the funder-facing number, computed only from kits actually handed over at events that have closed, and discounted by a no-show penalty. Both are stored to 2 decimal places, and both are recomputed on the same set of occasions: whenever a student registration moves into or out of `CONFIRMED`, `FULFILLED` or `NO_SHOW`; when a distribution event is locked, when it is cancelled and again when it is closed; whenever `target_student_count` moves, which is to say when a needs list is published or retired and when a school is archived; during the four-hop school-suspension cascade of §3.1; and when a drive cycle is reconciled.

The two variants share one scoring shape. Start with the **served ratio**: the variant's served count divided by the school's `target_student_count`, except that the divisor is taken as the larger of 1 and `target_student_count` so a school that has published no needs list never divides by zero, and the resulting ratio is capped at 1.0 so over-service cannot inflate the score. Next the **fill quality**: if the variant's event set is empty the fill quality is exactly 0.0, and otherwise it is the average, over the events in that set, of the event's stored fill percentage divided by 100. That divisor of 100 is applied to the **already-rounded stored percentage field** — the value sitting on the event record — and never to a freshly recomputed unrounded ratio. Next the **equity weight**, which is simply the school's `title_i_pct`. The base score is then fifty-five hundredths of the served ratio, plus thirty hundredths of the fill quality, plus fifteen hundredths of the equity weight. The base score and the variant's penalty multiplier are multiplied together at full precision, that product is multiplied by 100, and the single rounding of the whole computation — half up, to 2 decimal places — happens once, last, on that 0-to-100 value.

For the **interim** variant, the served count is the school's `students_served_interim_count`, which is the number of registrations on the school's distribution events whose `eligibility_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `FULFILLED`. The event set is the school's events whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, `LOCKED` or `CLOSED`, and the fill field read from each of them is `provisional_fill_pct`. The penalty multiplier is exactly 1.0 — the interim number carries no no-show penalty at all.

For the **audited** variant, the served count is the school's `students_served_audited_count`, which counts only registrations whose `eligibility_status` is `FULFILLED`. The event set narrows to the school's events whose `status` is `CLOSED`, and the fill field read from each is `final_fill_pct`. The penalty multiplier is 1 minus one half of the no-show rate, where the no-show rate is the school's `no_show_count` divided by the larger of 1 and the sum of `no_show_count` and `students_served_audited_count`. A school with no no-shows therefore keeps its full base score, and a school where every seat went unclaimed keeps half of it.

Three further school-level counters support this reporting and are maintained alongside the indices. `no_show_count` is the number of the school's registrations sitting in `NO_SHOW`. `kits_delivered_count` counts the registrations that reached `FULFILLED`, one kit per fulfilled registration. `target_student_count` is the sum of `student_target_count` over the school's `PUBLISHED` needs lists, as described in §3.1. A dedicated computed read presents the whole picture in one payload: `impact_index_interim` and `impact_index_audited` side by side, together with `students_served_interim_count`, `students_served_audited_count`, `no_show_count`, `target_student_count`, and `closed_event_count` — the number of the school's distribution events currently in `CLOSED`.

The programme year ends where §3.2 left it. Reconciling a cycle cancels its leftover draft purchase orders and refreshes the audited index of every school that hosted a closed event of that cycle, so the funder-facing number settles before the books do. Closing the cycle then requires the cash identity of §2.9 to hold exactly, which is the system's final assurance that every cent booked was either spent or is still sitting available, and that no purchase order left a phantom allocation behind.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint is mounted at the root of the service host; there is no shared URL prefix. Requests that create a brand-new resource answer with HTTP 201 and the created resource in `data`; every other successful call — reads, list pages, status changes, transitions, adjustments and the clock capability — answers with HTTP 200 and the updated or requested resource in `data`. The table below gives only the HTTP contract and the roles authorised to call each route; the request payloads, the preconditions, the cascades and the derived-field effects are specified in §3 and must be read from there. Where a route admits two roles but §3 reserves a particular `target_status` for one of them, a caller holding the other role is refused that target with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) at step 2 of the ladder — before the path identifier is resolved and before any body field beyond `target_status` is read.
