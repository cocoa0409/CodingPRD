# Orthopedic In-Office DME Dispensing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An orthopedic group that hands a patient a brace on the way out of the exam room is doing three regulated things at once: it is practising medicine, it is acting as a durable medical equipment (DME) supplier, and it is self-referring to an ancillary service it owns. Each of those roles carries its own paperwork — a signed medical-necessity note, a DMEPOS supplier-standards attestation and accreditation that has not lapsed, a second physician's attestation that in-office ancillary services (IOAS) supervision was actually furnished, a coverage verification that is still inside its quoted validity, a prior authorization where the payer demands one, an advance beneficiary notice before any non-covered item changes hands, and a delivery signature. Today those artefacts live in five different places and the practice discovers a missing one only when a claim is denied or an audit letter arrives. This API is the single system of record that refuses to let a device leave the shelf until every one of those artefacts exists and is current.

The system is mounted under `/dme` and is built around a **DispenseEncounter** — one patient, one catalog item, one quantity, at one clinic site. An encounter carries two independent progress dimensions on the same record: a clinical dimension that tracks documentation, device sourcing, fitting and delivery, and a financial dimension that tracks coverage, authorization, beneficiary notice and billing. The two advance on their own schedules and are joined only at the moment of dispense, where every clinical and every fiscal precondition must hold simultaneously. Around the encounter sit the practice's master data (clinic sites, physician and fitter rosters, patients, suppliers and the device catalog), the shelf itself (per-clinic, per-product par levels with reservation and stockout accounting), and the downstream artefacts (coverage verifications, prior-authorization requests and appeals, advance beneficiary notices with cash quotes, fitting records, custom-fabrication orders and product returns).

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: ordering, compliance attestation, inventory custody, payer coordination and patient fitting are five separate responsibilities held by five different roles, and two of those pairs are additionally separated at the level of the individual user — the physician who attests supervision may not be the physician who ordered, and the fitter who issues a refit may not be the fitter whose fitting failed. The second is **arithmetic that does not drift**: every money amount is an integer number of cents, every rounding site is named, and the order in which buckets are filled and lines are rounded is fixed by this specification rather than left to the implementation. A clinic can therefore reconcile its unit ledger and its cash ledger to the cent and to the unit at any point in the lifecycle.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including the read endpoints and the test-driving clock endpoint, must carry two headers: `X-User-Id`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. (HTTP header names are case-insensitive, so `X-User-ID` is the same header as `X-User-Id`.) If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401. If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The six roles are `PracticeAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `InventoryManager`, `InsuranceCoordinator`, `OrthoPhysician` and `DmeFitter`.

A `PracticeAdmin` is the practice operations administrator and owns the group's master data: clinic sites, the physician and fitter rosters, patient registration, supplier onboarding and the device catalog. The role has broad read access across the system but performs no clinical, coverage or inventory-custody action. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns supplier-standards and accreditation attestations and audits the dispensing trail; the role deliberately holds no ability to create encounters, verify coverage, move stock or fit patients, so the party that certifies a supplier is never also the party that dispenses from it. An `InventoryManager` holds physical custody: par levels, stock receipts, the supplier side of custom fabrication, and the inspection of returned devices. An `InsuranceCoordinator` owns coverage verification, prior authorization and appeals, and the whole advance-beneficiary-notice lifecycle including its cash quote — the party that determines what the patient owes never also hands the device over. An `OrthoPhysician` orders devices, signs the medical-necessity note for encounters they themselves ordered, and attests in-office ancillary-services supervision for encounters ordered by a **different** group physician. A `DmeFitter` reserves shelf stock, places custom-fabrication orders, records fitting sessions, accepts fabricated devices, dispenses against a delivery signature, opens returns, and issues refits for fittings performed by a **different** fitter.

Role identity is resolved differently for the two clinical roles than for the four operational ones. When `X-User-Role` is `OrthoPhysician`, `X-User-Id` is the `id` of the acting `Physician` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Physician` compare the resource's physician reference to `X-User-Id`, which is how the medical-necessity note is bound to `ordering_physician_id` and how the attestation is stamped into `ioas_attesting_physician_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DmeFitter`, `X-User-Id` is the `id` of the acting `Fitter` row; the fitter's own `clinic_id`, `credential`, `credential_expires_on` and `privilege_scope` are read from that row, and the value is stamped into `fitter_id` on fitting records and encounters. If the acting user for either of these two entity-backed roles is not the `id` of an existing `Physician` or `Fitter` respectively, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 403. One carve-out applies: on the two operations that attribute a record to a fitter — recording a fitting session and issuing a refit — an unknown fitter actor is reported instead as `ERR_FITTER_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, so that the more specific diagnosis stays reachable; `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND` is therefore not evaluated for the `DmeFitter` role on those two operations.

When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `InventoryManager` or `InsuranceCoordinator`, `X-User-Id` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. The server may record it for audit — for example, the identifier of the acting `InsuranceCoordinator` who issues an advance beneficiary notice is persisted verbatim as `witness_user_id` on that notice — but no existence check is performed against it for these four roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. Both are reflected back in the `meta` object of the response alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the filter before paging is applied, and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Those four pagination fields appear in `meta` only for list endpoints.

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first. Two encounter-scoped lists deliberately invert this because the reader almost always wants the newest artefact: the coverage-verification list for an encounter is returned newest-sequence-first, and the advance-beneficiary-notice list for an encounter is likewise returned newest-sequence-first.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every date, day-count, month-count and interval rule in this specification is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any clock call has been made the server must read that instant and must not substitute the host's current time. A test-driving capability, available to all six roles, sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and answers with both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` value it replaced. Setting the clock mutates no entity — it only moves the reference instant against which accreditation expiry, credential expiry, coverage-quote expiry, authorization expiry, note contemporaneity, return windows, restock windows, fabrication turnaround and same-or-similar lookbacks are all measured. Because the clock can move backwards as well as forwards, no rule may be implemented by caching a derived date at write time unless this document explicitly says the value is snapshotted.

Two conventions apply throughout. A calendar-date expiry field is **inclusive**: something that expires on a given date is still valid for the whole of that date, and lapses only once the logical clock's calendar date is strictly later. An instant-valued expiry field is likewise inclusive: a quote or authorization whose expiry instant equals the logical clock instant is still usable, and becomes unusable only once the clock is strictly past it.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the API creates carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits. Clients never supply either value.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and an integer `version`. `version` starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations that only touch derived fields — a par level whose `available_qty` and `stock_posture` are recomputed by a reservation has been mutated, and so has an encounter whose money fields are written at dispense. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutations. Where a single request mutates several resources (and there are many such requests in this system), each mutated resource's own `version` advances by 1 and its own `updated_at` is refreshed; the counters are per-resource and never shared.

One entity is exempt. A `CoverageVerification` is an immutable audit row: once written it is never updated, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field. Corrections are made by writing a further verification row with a higher sequence number, never by editing an existing one. Every other entity in the system — `Clinic`, `Physician`, `Fitter`, `Supplier`, `DeviceProduct`, `ClinicParLevel`, `Patient`, `DispenseEncounter`, `PriorAuthRequest`, `AbnNotice`, `FittingRecord`, `CustomFabOrder` and `ProductReturn` — carries all four common properties.

### 2.6. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response: an allowed amount of five hundred dollars is `50000`. Fractional inputs exist only as rates — a coinsurance fraction, a restocking-fee fraction — and those are decimals between 0 and 1 inclusive, with the patient coinsurance fraction accepted to at most four decimal places.

Wherever a monetary result would be fractional, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two whole cents rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. What matters as much as the method is *where* rounding is applied, and this specification pins every site. Rounding is applied per line, and the rounded lines are then added; the sum of unrounded lines is never rounded once at the end. Concretely: in the insurance-assigned pricing of an encounter, the coinsurance line is rounded on its own and the discounted cash unit price is rounded on its own, and their sum is never rounded again. In a cash quote on an advance beneficiary notice, rounding is applied exactly once, to the single-unit discounted price, and that already-rounded unit price is then multiplied by the number of quoted units — multiplying first and rounding the product yields a different and incorrect figure. In a return refund, the device component is rounded, and then each of the two refund lines is rounded separately, and only then are the two rounded refunds added. In the supplier turnaround rollup, rounding is applied only to the average.

Unit quantities are integers throughout, and the only place a unit quantity is rounded is the critical-stock floor described in §3.2, which rounds a halved par minimum **up** to the next whole unit. Elapsed-time quantities follow the direction stated at each site: fitting duration is rounded **up** to whole minutes, while day counts used for return windows, restock windows and fabrication turnaround are **floored** to whole days, and month counts in the same-or-similar lookback are whole calendar months computed day-of-month-aware with no fractional part. Turnaround scores are integer points clamped to the range 0 through 100 inclusive.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shelves and Encounters

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent resource's `version`. In both cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional hint, and in both cases the comparison is against the stored integer only.

Reserving par stock for an encounter requires the request to carry `expected_par_level_version`, holding the `version` of the targeted `ClinicParLevel` as the fitter last observed it. If that value does not equal the par level's current stored `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PAR_LEVEL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The fitter's availability view is stale in that case: another reservation, a stock receipt, a rejected fitting that released units, or a restocked return has already moved `on_hand_qty` or `reserved_qty` on that shelf, so the quantity the fitter believed was free is no longer the quantity the shelf reports, and committing the reservation would over-promise the shelf.

Issuing an advance beneficiary notice requires the request to carry `expected_encounter_version`, holding the `version` of the target `DispenseEncounter` as the coordinator last observed it. If that value does not equal the encounter's current stored `version`, the issuance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENCOUNTER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Issuance legitimately supersedes every earlier outstanding notice on the encounter, so without this guard the operation would succeed against a stale view and silently void a notice the coordinator never saw — for example one issued after a prior-authorization decision landed and changed the encounter's financial posture.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names no existing row determines the response. A request that targets a physician roster under a clinic id that does not exist is answered with `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the roster lookup; a request that targets a sub-resource of an encounter is answered with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND` before any body field is examined. Body-borne foreign keys are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are resolved in the order given by the rejection ladder in §2.9 — clinic, then supplier, then product, then patient, then physician — so a creation request naming three non-existent rows always reports the leftmost one on that list.

### 2.9. The Rejection Ladder

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the rules are evaluated in a fixed ascending priority order and the **first** violated rule produces the response. This ordering is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, because a great many requests in this system are capable of violating five or six rules at once. The ladder runs in six bands.

The first band is authentication and authorization: `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, then `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, then `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND`. The second band is a single request-shape gate, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, which fires when a required input field is absent, a value has the wrong type, an enum value is not a declared member, or a quantity or money field is negative. The third band is existence, and within it the order is `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FITTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAR_LEVEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ABN_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAB_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FITTING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, and then the cross-entity scoping check `ERR_CLINIC_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

The fourth band is state, in the order `ERR_INVALID_CLINICAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FINANCIAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PRIOR_AUTH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ABN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FAB_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE` — so an encounter in the wrong clinical state is always diagnosed as such even when its financial state is also wrong. Immediately after state come the two optimistic-concurrency checks, `ERR_STALE_PAR_LEVEL_VERSION` and then `ERR_STALE_ENCOUNTER_VERSION`; a stale version is therefore reported only once the target has been confirmed to exist and to be in a state that permits the operation at all.

The fifth band is regulatory and clinical safety, in this order: `ERR_CLINIC_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_STANDARDS_NOT_ATTESTED`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_QUALITY_HOLD`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE`, `ERR_IOAS_SUPERVISION_MISSING`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_STARK_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`, `ERR_MEDICAL_NECESSITY_MISSING`, `ERR_FITTER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`, `ERR_FITTER_PRIVILEGE_SCOPE`, `ERR_REFIT_SAME_FITTER`, `ERR_DELIVERY_SIGNATURE_MISSING`. Coverage and authorization constraints follow: `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_MISSING`, `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_SAME_OR_SIMILAR_CONFLICT`, `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_ALREADY_EXISTS`, `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_APPROVED_UNITS_EXCEED_REQUEST`, `ERR_ABN_REQUIRED_FOR_EXCESS_UNITS`, `ERR_ABN_NOT_SIGNED`, `ERR_ABN_SUPERSEDED`.

The sixth band closes the ladder with constraint satisfaction and then capacity. Uniqueness and temporal constraints come first — `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAR_LEVEL`, `ERR_FITTING_SLOT_CONFLICT`, `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_RETURN_ALREADY_EXISTS`, `ERR_REFIT_REASON_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_CUSTOM_FAB_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `ERR_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` — and inventory capacity comes last of all: `ERR_CLINIC_DISPENSING_SUSPENDED`, then `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PAR_STOCK`, then `ERR_PAR_MAX_EXCEEDED`. Placing stock capacity at the bottom is deliberate: a request that is both non-compliant and short of stock is reported as non-compliant, because restocking a shelf would not have made it legal.

Three carve-outs are pinned into this ladder so that more specific diagnoses stay reachable, and they are the only exceptions to a literal top-to-bottom reading. First, the unknown-fitter-actor carve-out already described in §2.1. Second, on a return request the clinical-state check is evaluated only when the encounter has no return yet, so a second return request against an encounter that already has one is diagnosed as `ERR_RETURN_ALREADY_EXISTS` rather than as an invalid clinical state, even though the post-return clinical states are not declared origins of the return transition. Third, on a dispense request two financial-state violations are re-routed: an encounter still unverified and holding no coverage verification at all is diagnosed as `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_MISSING`, and an encounter whose financial dimension sits at `ABN_ISSUED` is diagnosed as `ERR_ABN_NOT_SIGNED`. Every other non-dispensable financial state — `NON_COVERED`, `AUTH_PENDING`, `AUTH_DENIED`, `BILLED`, `REFUNDED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` — yields `ERR_INVALID_FINANCIAL_STATE`, and the clinical-state check still precedes all of it.

### 2.10. Accreditation, Supplier Standards and the Compliance Hold

In-office dispensing is only lawful from an accredited site by an accredited supply chain, and this system treats a lapse as a hard stop rather than a warning. A clinic's `accreditation_expires_on` is valid through and including that calendar date; once the logical clock's date is strictly later, three operations stop working — signing a medical-necessity note, reserving par stock, and dispensing — each rejected with `ERR_CLINIC_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED` and HTTP 422. The lapse blocks those steps even when stock and coverage are otherwise perfect.

On the supply side two separate supplier attributes gate work. `ERR_SUPPLIER_STANDARDS_NOT_ATTESTED`, HTTP 422, fires when the supplier of the encounter's product either has not attested to the DMEPOS supplier standards or has an `accreditation_expires_on` strictly before the logical clock date; it guards four operations — reserving par stock, placing a custom-fabrication order, driving a fabrication event, and dispensing. `ERR_SUPPLIER_QUALITY_HOLD`, also HTTP 422, fires when the supplier is on quality hold, and it guards three of those four: reserving par stock, placing a custom-fabrication order, and driving a fabrication event. Dispensing is deliberately *not* guarded by the quality-hold check directly, because a raised quality hold reaches an in-flight encounter by a different route, described next.

A `DispenseEncounter` carries a boolean `compliance_hold` and, while that boolean is true, a `hold_reason` naming why; when the hold is cleared, `hold_reason` returns to null. There are exactly two reasons. `CLINIC_SUSPENDED` is stamped when the encounter's clinic falls into a suspended dispensing posture: the suspension sets `compliance_hold` to true and `hold_reason` to `CLINIC_SUSPENDED` on every **other** encounter of that clinic whose clinical dimension currently sits at `RESERVED`. `SUPPLIER_QUALITY_HOLD` is stamped through the fabrication chain: raising a supplier's quality hold moves every one of that supplier's fabrication orders that is currently submitted, in fabrication, or awaiting an adjustment into a supplier-hold state, and each such order in turn sets `compliance_hold` to true and `hold_reason` to `SUPPLIER_QUALITY_HOLD` on its own encounter. Both chains reverse: an improvement in the clinic's posture clears `compliance_hold` and `hold_reason` on that clinic's encounters whose reason was `CLINIC_SUSPENDED`, and clearing a supplier's quality hold returns its held orders to fabrication and clears `compliance_hold` where the reason was `SUPPLIER_QUALITY_HOLD`. While a hold is active, two operations are refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE` and HTTP 409: recording a fitting session, and dispensing.

### 2.11. Self-Referral Compliance and Duty Separation

Because the group owns the ancillary service it is referring to, two physician facts must be true of every encounter before a device may be furnished. The ordering physician must be a member of the group that owns the clinic and must have a written self-referral (Stark) financial-relationship disclosure on file for the site, and the physician who attests supervision must also be a group member. A violation of any of those three conditions is reported as `ERR_STARK_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` with HTTP 422, and the check guards three operations: signing the medical-necessity note, attesting supervision, and dispensing.

Two further rules separate duties at the level of the individual user rather than the role. The physician who attests in-office ancillary-services supervision must not be the encounter's ordering physician — supervision of a self-referral is attested by a *second* group physician — and an attempt by the ordering physician to attest their own encounter is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409. Symmetrically, the fitter who issues a refit must not be the fitter recorded on the returned encounter, because a poor fit may not be re-fitted by the person who produced it; that violation is refused with `ERR_REFIT_SAME_FITTER`, also HTTP 409.

Supervision has a level as well as an attester. A clinic declares the highest level it can furnish in `ioas_supervision_level`, and a catalog item declares the minimum level it needs in `ioas_supervision_required`; both take the values `DIRECT` or `GENERAL`, and `DIRECT` outranks `GENERAL`. When an item requires `DIRECT`, dispensing requires that the clinic's declared level is `DIRECT` **and** that the level the attesting physician actually observed, recorded as `ioas_supervision_level_observed`, is also `DIRECT`; a `GENERAL` value in either place is insufficient for that class of item. A dispense attempted with no attestation at all, or with insufficient supervision for a `DIRECT` item, is refused with `ERR_IOAS_SUPERVISION_MISSING` and HTTP 422.

### 2.12. The Two Dimensions of a Dispensing Encounter

A `DispenseEncounter` carries two independent state fields on one row, and they never share a value, so any state name in this document unambiguously identifies its dimension.

The clinical dimension is `clinical_status`. It begins at `DRAFT` and may take the values `DRAFT`, `DOCUMENTED`, `AWAITING_DEVICE`, `RESERVED`, `FITTED`, `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_PENDING`, `RETURNED` and `VOIDED`. `RETURNED` and `VOIDED` are terminal: no operation moves an encounter out of either. The financial dimension is `financial_status`. It begins at `UNVERIFIED` and may take the values `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED`, `NON_COVERED`, `AUTH_PENDING`, `AUTH_APPROVED`, `AUTH_DENIED`, `ABN_ISSUED`, `SELF_PAY`, `BILLED`, `REFUNDED` and `WRITTEN_OFF`. `REFUNDED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are terminal.

The dimensions advance independently. Signing the medical-necessity note moves the clinical dimension and leaves the financial dimension exactly where it was; recording a coverage verification moves the financial dimension and leaves the clinical dimension untouched. Only three requests move both dimensions in one transaction: dispensing, which advances `clinical_status` from `FITTED` to `DELIVERED` and `financial_status` to `BILLED`; return inspection, which advances `clinical_status` from `RETURN_PENDING` to `RETURNED` and `financial_status` from `BILLED` to `REFUNDED`; and signing an advance beneficiary notice with the refusal option, which advances `financial_status` from `ABN_ISSUED` to `WRITTEN_OFF` and cascades `clinical_status` to `VOIDED`.

An operation whose requested clinical transition has no declared origin at the encounter's current `clinical_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLINICAL_STATE` and HTTP 409 — which is also what replaying a one-shot trigger, or acting on a terminal `RETURNED` or `VOIDED` encounter, produces. The equivalent financial violation, including any action on a terminal `REFUNDED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` encounter, is `ERR_INVALID_FINANCIAL_STATE`, also HTTP 409, subject to the two dispense carve-outs in §2.9. One operation moves neither dimension and therefore has its state rule expressed as an explicit window rather than as a transition lookup: attesting supervision is permitted while `clinical_status` is `DOCUMENTED`, `AWAITING_DEVICE`, `RESERVED` or `FITTED` — supervision may be attested any time after the note is signed and before the device is handed over, and may be re-attested to correct the observed level — while `DRAFT`, `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_PENDING`, `RETURNED` and `VOIDED` are refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLINICAL_STATE`.

Four other resources carry their own lifecycles, each with its own state field named `status` and its own invalid-state error. A `PriorAuthRequest` moves among `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `PENDED` and the terminal `EXPIRED`, and violations are reported as `ERR_INVALID_PRIOR_AUTH_STATE`. An `AbnNotice` moves from `ISSUED` to one of the terminal values `SIGNED`, `DECLINED` or `VOIDED`, and violations are `ERR_INVALID_ABN_STATE`. A `CustomFabOrder` moves among `SUBMITTED`, `IN_FABRICATION`, `READY_FOR_FITTING`, `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED`, `SUPPLIER_HOLD` and the terminal values `ACCEPTED` and `CANCELLED`, and violations are `ERR_INVALID_FAB_ORDER_STATE`. A `ProductReturn` moves from `REQUESTED` to `RESTOCKED` or `DESTROYED` and from either of those to the terminal `REFIT_ISSUED`, and violations are `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`. A `ClinicParLevel` carries a derived `stock_posture` taking `OK`, `BELOW_PAR`, `CRITICAL` or `STOCKOUT`, and a `Clinic` carries a derived `dispensing_status` taking `ACTIVE`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`; neither of those two is ever set by a client, and both are recomputed by the rules in §3.2.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Master Data: Sites, Rosters, Suppliers and the Device Catalog

Everything in the system hangs off a **Clinic**, the physical site at which devices are stocked and handed over. A `PracticeAdmin` registers a site with a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the US state of the dispensing site, an `accreditation_body` drawn from `ABC`, `BOC`, `ACHC` or `NONE`, the `accreditation_expires_on` calendar date on which DMEPOS accreditation lapses, the `ioas_supervision_level` the site can furnish, and `dispensing_specialties` — a non-empty, duplicate-free list of the specialty lines the site is licensed to stock and dispense, whose members are drawn from `SPORTS_MEDICINE`, `SPINE`, `FOOT_ANKLE`, `HAND` and `TOTAL_JOINT`. Calendar dates throughout this system are written as `YYYY-MM-DD`. A newly registered site opens with a `dispensing_status` of `ACTIVE` and a `stockout_par_level_count` of 0; both of those are derived and neither is ever supplied by a client. Any request naming a clinic that does not exist — whether by path or by a `clinic_id` in the body — is answered with `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_FOUND`.

Physicians are added to a site's roster by a `PracticeAdmin`. The site is taken from the path rather than from the body, so the created `Physician` inherits its `clinic_id` from the clinic being addressed. The body carries a `full_name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, an `npi` National Provider Identifier of exactly 10 digits, a `specialty` from the same five-member specialty enumeration, and two optional compliance booleans: `group_member`, which defaults to true and records membership of the orthopedic group that owns the clinic, and `stark_disclosure_on_file`, which defaults to false and records that the physician's written self-referral disclosure is on file for the site. The identifier is unique practice-wide: creating a physician whose `npi` already exists anywhere in the practice is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI` and HTTP 409. A clinic's physician roster can be listed and optionally narrowed by `specialty`.

Fitters are added the same way and likewise take their `clinic_id` from the path. A `Fitter` carries a `full_name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `credential` drawn from `CO`, `CPO`, `CFO`, `CFm` and `NONE` — where `NONE` means the person may not perform any fitting at all — a `credential_expires_on` calendar date through which that credential remains valid inclusively, and a `privilege_scope` of either `OFF_SHELF_ONLY` or `CUSTOM_AND_OFF_SHELF`. The scope decides which catalog categories the person may fit: `OFF_SHELF_ONLY` covers off-the-shelf prefabricated items only, while `CUSTOM_AND_OFF_SHELF` covers all three categories. Every fitter also carries a derived `fitting_minutes_booked` rollup that opens at 0 and is described in §3.7. A fitter roster can be listed for a clinic and narrowed by `privilege_scope`, and a single fitter can be read directly; reading a fitter that does not exist yields `ERR_FITTER_NOT_FOUND`.

Patients are registered against a site by either a `PracticeAdmin` or an `InsuranceCoordinator`, again inheriting `clinic_id` from the path. A `Patient` carries a `full_name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `dob` date of birth, a `payer_type` drawn from `MEDICARE`, `MEDICAID`, `COMMERCIAL` and `SELF_PAY`, an optional `member_id` payer member identifier that defaults to null and is expected to be null for self-pay patients, and an optional `medicare_beneficiary` boolean defaulting to false. That last flag matters later: a Medicare beneficiary must be given an advance beneficiary notice before any non-covered item may be dispensed. A clinic's patient list may be narrowed by `payer_type`. A request naming a `patient_id` that does not resolve is answered with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, and one naming an `ordering_physician_id` that does not resolve with `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`.

**Suppliers** are onboarded by a `PracticeAdmin` with a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, an `accreditation_body` from the same four-member enumeration used for clinics, an `accreditation_expires_on` date, and a `promised_turnaround_days` count of at least 1 calendar day, which is the contractual turnaround the supplier promises for custom fabrication and the baseline against which both turnaround-score variants are measured. Three booleans may be supplied at onboarding and all three default to false: `dmepos_standards_attested`, recording attestation to the DMEPOS supplier standards required for in-office dispensing; `custom_fab_capable`, recording that the supplier can accept custom-fabricated device orders; and `quality_hold`, recording that the practice has placed the supplier on quality hold. A new supplier opens with a derived `turnaround_score` of 100 and an `accepted_fab_order_count` of 0. A request naming a supplier that does not exist, by path or by a body `supplier_id`, is answered with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may subsequently change a supplier's compliance posture. That operation carries `dmepos_standards_attested` and `quality_hold` as required inputs and an optional `accreditation_expires_on` that replaces the stored date when supplied, and it answers 200 OK with the updated supplier. It advances the supplier's `version` by 1, and — as described in §2.10 — raising the quality hold cascades into the supplier's open fabrication orders and, through them, onto the encounters waiting on those orders, while clearing it reverses both links.

The **device catalog** is maintained by a `PracticeAdmin` or an `InventoryManager`. A `DeviceProduct` names its `supplier_id` of record and carries an `hcpcs_code` of 1 to 8 non-blank characters, a `description` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a `category`, a `specialty`, a `same_or_similar_group` key of 1 to 40 non-blank uppercase characters, a `reasonable_useful_lifetime_months` count of at least 1 whole month, and three money-in-cents amounts: `allowed_amount_cents`, the payer allowed amount per unit and the basis of insurance-assigned pricing; `cash_price_cents`, the posted self-pay price per unit and the basis of both the cash quote and any uncovered excess units; and `unit_cost_cents`, the acquisition cost per unit, which is carried for margin reporting only and is never used anywhere in patient pricing. Two optional flags complete the record: `requires_prior_auth`, defaulting to false, and `ioas_supervision_required`, defaulting to `GENERAL`. The `category` takes one of `OFF_SHELF_PREFAB`, `CUSTOM_FITTED_PREFAB` or `CUSTOM_FABRICATED`; the first two are drawn from clinic par stock, while a `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` item must be ordered from a supplier and never touches a shelf. Two products that share a `same_or_similar_group` value are same-or-similar for the reasonable-useful-lifetime lookback in §3.4, and that group's width in months is the product's `reasonable_useful_lifetime_months`. A supplier's catalog can be listed and narrowed by `category`, and a single product read directly; an unresolvable `product_id` yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.2. Par Levels, Stock Receipts and the Clinic's Dispensing Posture

An `InventoryManager` establishes a **par level** — the practice's term for one product's shelf at one clinic — by naming a `clinic_id` and a `product_id` together with a `par_min_qty` of at least 1 unit, the minimum on-shelf availability the site must maintain, and a `par_max_qty`, the maximum stocking level. The maximum must be at least the minimum; a creation request whose `par_min_qty` exceeds its `par_max_qty` is refused with `ERR_PAR_MAX_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. At most one par level may exist per clinic-and-product pair, and a second attempt at the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAR_LEVEL` and HTTP 409. A shelf may only be opened for a product the site is licensed to dispense: if the product's `specialty` is not a member of the clinic's `dispensing_specialties`, the request is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_SCOPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. And a shelf may not be opened at all for a `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` product, since such items are fabricated to order and never stocked; that attempt is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422.

A new shelf opens empty. Its `on_hand_qty` — the physical units actually on the shelf — is 0, its `reserved_qty` — the units committed to encounters that have reserved but not yet taken delivery — is 0, its derived `available_qty` is 0, and its `lifetime_received_qty`, the cumulative count of units ever received into this shelf across its whole history, is 0. Its derived `stock_posture` opens at `STOCKOUT`, because nothing is physically there. Crucially, though, a shelf that has never received anything is not a dispensing failure, so this newly created `STOCKOUT` shelf does **not** count against the clinic and the clinic's `dispensing_status` is unchanged by the creation.

Units arrive through a **stock receipt**, an `InventoryManager` operation against an existing shelf carrying a single `received_qty`. A receipt adds that quantity to both `on_hand_qty` and `lifetime_received_qty` and advances the shelf's `version` by 1. A receipt may not push `on_hand_qty` above `par_max_qty`; one that would is refused with `ERR_PAR_MAX_EXCEEDED`. A request against a shelf identifier that does not resolve — or an encounter operation that needs a shelf for its clinic-and-product pair when no such shelf exists at all — is answered with `ERR_PAR_LEVEL_NOT_FOUND`. Par levels can be read individually and listed with optional `clinic_id`, `product_id` and `stock_posture` filters.

Two quantities on a shelf are deliberately not interchangeable, and conflating them is the single easiest way to get this system wrong. `available_qty` is what is *uncommitted*: it is `on_hand_qty` less `reserved_qty`, it may never be negative, and it is the only quantity consulted by the stock-sufficiency check. `stock_posture` is what is *physically* on the shelf, and it is derived from `on_hand_qty` alone. It follows that a reservation lowers `available_qty` but never moves `stock_posture`, and that releasing a reservation raises `available_qty` back without moving `stock_posture` either. Only three events move the posture: a dispense that consumes units, a stock receipt that adds them, and a restocked return that puts them back.

The posture itself is a four-way threshold read off `on_hand_qty` against a **critical floor** equal to `par_min_qty` halved and rounded up to the next whole unit. When `on_hand_qty` is 0 or below, the posture is `STOCKOUT`. Otherwise, when `on_hand_qty` is strictly below the critical floor, the posture is `CRITICAL`. Otherwise, when `on_hand_qty` is strictly below `par_min_qty`, the posture is `BELOW_PAR`. In every remaining case the posture is `OK`. The boundaries are exact and inclusive in the stated direction: an `on_hand_qty` exactly equal to the critical floor is `BELOW_PAR` and not `CRITICAL`, and an `on_hand_qty` exactly equal to `par_min_qty` is `OK` and not `BELOW_PAR`. This recomputation runs on every mutation of either `on_hand_qty` or `reserved_qty` — shelf creation, stock receipt, reservation, reservation release, dispense consumption and restocked return — and it consults only the single shelf being mutated, never a sibling shelf of the same clinic or the same product. In posture terms, a shelf climbs `STOCKOUT → OK` when a receipt brings `on_hand_qty` up to at least `par_min_qty`, and slides down `OK → BELOW_PAR`, then `BELOW_PAR → CRITICAL`, and finally `CRITICAL → STOCKOUT` as consumption eats into it; a single dispense large enough to clear the shelf takes it straight from `OK → STOCKOUT`.

Whenever a shelf's posture enters or leaves `STOCKOUT`, the owning clinic's posture is recomputed. The input is `stockout_par_level_count`, a **full recount** — not an increment — of that clinic's shelves whose `stock_posture` is `STOCKOUT` and whose `lifetime_received_qty` is greater than 0. Shelves that have never received a unit are excluded, which is exactly why opening a new shelf does not degrade the clinic. Every shelf of the clinic is counted regardless of the product's category, and shelves belonging to other clinics are never counted even for the same product. A count of 0 makes `dispensing_status` `ACTIVE`; a count of exactly 1 makes it `RESTRICTED`; a count of 2 or more makes it `SUSPENDED`. Because the count is a recount rather than a delta, a single mutation that drives two shelves to `STOCKOUT` lands the clinic directly on `SUSPENDED` — either as `RESTRICTED → SUSPENDED` or as `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` — and the intermediate posture is never observable. Recovery runs the same way in reverse: a stock receipt or a restocked return that lifts one shelf out of `STOCKOUT` moves the clinic `SUSPENDED → RESTRICTED` when one stocked-out shelf remains, or `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` when none does. Each of these posture changes advances the clinic's `version` by 1.

Posture has teeth. Entering `SUSPENDED` sets `compliance_hold` to true and `hold_reason` to `CLINIC_SUSPENDED` on every encounter of that clinic whose `clinical_status` is `RESERVED`, and any improvement out of `SUSPENDED` clears `compliance_hold` and `hold_reason` on that clinic's encounters whose reason was `CLINIC_SUSPENDED`. Independently of the holds, the clinic's posture gates three encounter operations — reserving par stock, placing a custom-fabrication order, and dispensing — with `ERR_CLINIC_DISPENSING_SUSPENDED` and HTTP 409. A `SUSPENDED` clinic blocks all three outright. A `RESTRICTED` clinic is narrower: it blocks the operation only when the targeted shelf is itself the stocked-out one, so other products at that site remain reservable and dispensable.

### 3.3. Opening an Encounter, Documenting Necessity and Attesting Supervision

A dispensing encounter is opened either by an `OrthoPhysician` acting for themselves or by a `PracticeAdmin` acting on a physician's behalf. The creation payload names the `clinic_id` of the dispensing site, the `patient_id` of the patient receiving the device, the `product_id` of the catalog item, and the `ordering_physician_id` of the group physician who ordered it — the last of these is carried in the body precisely so an administrator can open the encounter for a physician who is not the caller. It also carries a `quantity` of at least 1 and at most 20 units, and an optional `service_date`; when `service_date` is omitted it defaults to the logical clock's calendar date at the moment of creation. The new encounter opens with `clinical_status` `DRAFT`, `financial_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `compliance_hold` false, and every money field at 0.

Three referential facts are checked before the encounter exists. The named patient's `clinic_id` must equal the encounter's `clinic_id`; the named ordering physician's `clinic_id` must equal it too; and the ordering physician's `specialty` must equal the product's `specialty`, because a spine surgeon does not order a foot-and-ankle orthosis under this practice's rules. Any of those three failures is reported as `ERR_CLINIC_SCOPE_MISMATCH` with HTTP 403. Encounters can afterwards be read individually — an unresolvable encounter identifier anywhere yields `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND` — and listed with a broad set of optional filters that give reverse reachability for every reference the encounter carries: `clinic_id`, `patient_id`, `product_id`, `ordering_physician_id`, `mn_physician_id`, `ioas_attesting_physician_id`, `fitter_id`, `refit_of_encounter_id`, `clinical_status`, `financial_status` and `compliance_hold`.

The first clinical step is the **medical-necessity note**, and it belongs to the ordering physician alone: the note may only be signed by the `OrthoPhysician` whose `Physician` identity equals the encounter's `ordering_physician_id`, and a request from any other physician does not satisfy the transition's precondition. The note body carries `mn_diagnosis_code`, the ICD-10 diagnosis code; `mn_functional_deficit`, a free-text statement of the functional deficit the device addresses; and `mn_expected_duration_months`, the expected number of months of medical need. Signing stores those three values on the encounter, stamps `mn_physician_id` with the acting physician's identity and `mn_signed_at` with the logical clock instant, and advances the clinical dimension `DRAFT → DOCUMENTED` while leaving `financial_status` exactly where it was. The encounter's `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed. Two guards precede the write: the clinic's accreditation must not have lapsed, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED`, and the ordering physician's Stark posture must be clean, or it is refused with `ERR_STARK_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`.

The second physician step is the **in-office ancillary-services supervision attestation**, and it belongs to a *different* group physician. Its payload is a single `ioas_supervision_level_observed`, either `DIRECT` or `GENERAL`, recording the supervision level actually observed at the time of furnishing. The attestation stamps `ioas_attesting_physician_id` with the acting physician's identity, `ioas_attested_at` with the logical clock instant, and the observed level from the body, and it advances the encounter's `version` by 1 — but it moves **neither** state dimension, which is why its permitted-state rule is expressed as the explicit window given in §2.12 rather than as a transition. Its two compliance guards are the segregation rule and the group-membership rule of §2.11: an attempt by the ordering physician is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and an attesting physician who is not a group member is refused with `ERR_STARK_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`. Because the attestation may be replayed, a physician who recorded the wrong level can simply attest again and overwrite the observed level and instant.

### 3.4. Coverage Verification, the Same-or-Similar Lookback, and Prior Authorization

The financial dimension is driven entirely by the `InsuranceCoordinator`, and it starts with a **coverage verification**: an immutable record of what the payer said about this item for this patient. The payload carries `covered`, the payer's yes-or-no coverage answer; `deductible_remaining_cents`, the patient's remaining deductible in cents as quoted; `coinsurance_pct`, the patient coinsurance fraction applied once the deductible is exhausted, between 0 and 1 inclusive with at most four decimal places; and `valid_for_days`, the number of whole days the quote stays usable, at least 1 and at most 90. The server assigns `verification_seq`, a 1-based sequence within the encounter equal to the previous maximum plus 1, stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, and derives `expires_at` as `verified_at` plus `valid_for_days` whole days. It also captures `allowed_amount_cents_snapshot` from the product's `allowed_amount_cents` at that moment, so that a later catalog price edit cannot retroactively change what an encounter was priced against. Each verification carries the `encounter_id` of its parent.

Verifications are never updated and never merged. The encounter's **effective** verification is always the row with the greatest `verification_seq`, and it is selected by that sequence and never by identifier or by creation timestamp. Recording a verification with `covered` true advances the financial dimension `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED`; recording one with `covered` false advances it `UNVERIFIED → NON_COVERED`. Either may also be recorded when the dimension already sits at `NON_COVERED`, which is how a coordinator corrects a wrong answer. What the operation will not do is touch a closed encounter: the clinical dimension must not be `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_PENDING`, `RETURNED` or `VOIDED`. The clinical dimension is otherwise untouched — this dimension advances on its own — and the encounter's `version` advances by 1. An encounter's verifications can be listed newest-sequence-first, which also provides reverse reachability from `prior_delivered_encounter_id`.

Every verification runs the **same-or-similar equipment lookback** before the row is written, because Medicare and most commercial payers will not pay twice for the same class of device inside its reasonable useful lifetime. The candidate set is every other dispensing encounter of the *same* patient whose `clinical_status` is `DELIVERED` and whose product shares this product's `same_or_similar_group`, gathered across every clinic of the practice. Encounters of other patients are excluded even when the product matches, and encounters of the same patient in any other clinical status are excluded even when the product matches — a `RETURNED`, `RETURN_PENDING`, `VOIDED`, `FITTED`, `RESERVED`, `AWAITING_DEVICE` or `DRAFT` encounter has not consumed the lifetime, because only a device that was delivered and kept does. When the candidate set is empty, `prior_delivered_encounter_id`, `prior_delivered_on` and `months_since_prior_delivery` are all null and `same_or_similar_conflict` is false.

When the candidate set is not empty, the predecessor is the candidate with the greatest `delivered_at`; should two candidates share that instant, the tie is broken by the earlier `created_at`, and never by identifier. Its identifier is recorded in `prior_delivered_encounter_id` and the calendar date of its `delivered_at` in `prior_delivered_on`. The elapsed interval, `months_since_prior_delivery`, is a whole calendar-month count computed day-of-month-aware: take the difference in years between the logical clock and the predecessor's delivery, multiply by twelve, add the difference in month numbers, and then subtract one further month if the clock's day-of-month is earlier than the predecessor's day-of-month. The occupation window is **half-open**: `same_or_similar_conflict` is true when `months_since_prior_delivery` is strictly less than the product's `reasonable_useful_lifetime_months`, and false at exactly that many months or more, because at exactly the lifetime the window has closed. A conflict blocks only a *covered* verification: an attempt to record a verification with `covered` true while a conflict exists is refused with `ERR_SAME_OR_SIMILAR_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409, while a non-covered verification records the conflict fields and proceeds.

**Prior authorization** applies only where the product's `requires_prior_auth` is true. From a `VERIFIED` financial dimension the coordinator submits a request carrying `requested_units`, at least 1 and at most 20, which creates a `PriorAuthRequest` with `status` `SUBMITTED` and `appeal_round` 0 against the parent `encounter_id`, and advances the financial dimension `VERIFIED → AUTH_PENDING`. At most one authorization request may exist per encounter: a second submission, or a submission for a product that does not in fact require authorization, is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_ALREADY_EXISTS` and HTTP 409. An encounter's authorization requests can be listed. An unresolvable authorization identifier yields `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_NOT_FOUND`.

The payer's answer is recorded as a **decision** carrying a `decision` value of `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `PENDED`, with an optional `approved_units`, an optional `auth_number` and an optional `valid_for_days`. A decision is only accepted while the request's `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `PENDED`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PRIOR_AUTH_STATE` and HTTP 409. `APPROVED` sets `status` to `APPROVED`, writes `approved_units` — which the payer may set below the requested figure — writes the payer's `auth_number`, stamps `decision_at` from the logical clock, and derives `expires_at` as `decision_at` plus `valid_for_days` whole days; the encounter dimension advances `AUTH_PENDING → AUTH_APPROVED`. The approved figure must be at least 1 and at most `requested_units`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_APPROVED_UNITS_EXCEED_REQUEST` and HTTP 422. `DENIED` sets `status` to `DENIED`, stamps `decision_at`, and advances the encounter dimension `AUTH_PENDING → AUTH_DENIED`. `PENDED` is accepted only from `SUBMITTED`, sets `status` to `PENDED` and stamps `decision_at`, and leaves the encounter dimension sitting at `AUTH_PENDING` — a self-loop for as long as the payer keeps the request open. Every decision advances the authorization request's `version` by 1, and the two that move the encounter dimension advance the encounter's `version` by 1 as well.

A pended or denied request may be **appealed**, with an optional free-text `appeal_note`. An appeal is accepted while `status` is `PENDED` or `DENIED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PRIOR_AUTH_STATE`. It returns `status` to `SUBMITTED`, increases `appeal_round` by 1, clears `decision_at` back to null, and returns the encounter dimension to `AUTH_PENDING` — from `PENDED` that is a no-op on the encounter, and from `DENIED` it is the move `AUTH_DENIED → AUTH_PENDING`. Both the request's and the encounter's `version` advance by 1. Appeals are capped at two rounds: an appeal submitted when `appeal_round` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 422, so the cycle from `PENDED` back to `SUBMITTED` and onward to a fresh decision may run at most twice. Finally, an approved authorization can go stale. When a dispense is attempted and the logical clock instant is strictly past the authorization's `expires_at`, the system moves the request `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, advances its `version` by 1, and rejects the dispense that observed the expiry with `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED`. `EXPIRED` is terminal, so no decision and no appeal is accepted against it afterwards.

### 3.5. Advance Beneficiary Notices and Cash Pricing

When an item will not be paid for, the patient must be told in writing what it will cost them before it is handed over. That document is the **advance beneficiary notice**, issued by an `InsuranceCoordinator`, and for a Medicare beneficiary it is mandatory before any non-covered item may be dispensed. Issuance carries exactly two inputs: a `reason_non_covered` drawn from `NOT_MEDICALLY_NECESSARY`, `SAME_OR_SIMILAR_IN_LIFETIME`, `NON_COVERED_ITEM`, `AUTH_DENIED` and `UNITS_EXCEED_AUTH`, and the `expected_encounter_version` demanded by the optimistic-concurrency rule of §2.7. Each notice belongs to its parent `encounter_id` and is stamped with `witness_user_id`, the opaque identifier of the acting coordinator, held for audit only.

Issuance is reachable from three financial states and from itself. `NON_COVERED → ABN_ISSUED` is the ordinary path after the payer says no. `AUTH_DENIED → ABN_ISSUED` is the path after an authorization is denied and the appeal route is exhausted or declined. `AUTH_APPROVED → ABN_ISSUED` is permitted only with `reason_non_covered` equal to `UNITS_EXCEED_AUTH` and only when the encounter's `quantity` is strictly greater than the authorization's `approved_units`; that notice covers the uncovered excess units only, and the approved units stay on the insurance path. And `ABN_ISSUED → ABN_ISSUED` is a self-loop that re-issues a corrected notice, permitted only while the clinical dimension is not `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_PENDING`, `RETURNED` or `VOIDED`.

Every issuance behaves identically once admitted. Each earlier notice of that encounter whose `status` is `ISSUED` is moved to `VOIDED` and has its own `version` advanced by 1. A new notice is then created with `notice_seq` equal to the previous maximum for the encounter plus 1 and `status` `ISSUED`, the cash quote below is computed onto it, the financial dimension is set to `ABN_ISSUED`, and the encounter's `version` advances by 1. The encounter's **effective** notice is always the one with the greatest `notice_seq`. The self-loop is the only path in the system that produces a `VOIDED` notice, and therefore the only path that makes the superseded-notice rejection reachable at all: a notice that a later issuance has voided can no longer be signed, and an attempt to sign one is refused with `ERR_ABN_SUPERSEDED` and HTTP 409. An unresolvable notice identifier yields `ERR_ABN_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, and an encounter's notices can be listed newest-sequence-first and narrowed by `status`.

The **cash quote** written onto each notice is computed from scratch against the product's current `cash_price_cents`; superseded and declined notices on the same encounter contribute nothing to it. It runs in four ordered steps. First, the number of quoted units is decided: when `reason_non_covered` is `UNITS_EXCEED_AUTH` it is the encounter's `quantity` less the authorization's `approved_units`, and in every other case it is the encounter's whole `quantity`. Second, the per-unit net price is set — and here the evaluation order is pinned. If the quoted unit count is 2 or more, the practice's multi-unit cash discount of 15 percent applies, so `unit_net_price_cents` is the product's `cash_price_cents` multiplied by 0.85 and then rounded half up to a whole cent; if the quoted unit count is 1, `unit_net_price_cents` is the `cash_price_cents` unchanged. Third, `estimated_subtotal_cents` is that already-rounded `unit_net_price_cents` multiplied by the quoted unit count — rounding first and multiplying second is required, and multiplying first and rounding the product is a different and incorrect number. Fourth, the flat dispensing fee of 2500 cents, twenty-five dollars, is charged as `dispensing_fee_cents` when `estimated_subtotal_cents` is strictly below the waiver threshold of 50000 cents, five hundred dollars, and is 0 when the subtotal reaches that threshold or exceeds it — the fee is waived **at** the threshold, and the threshold is keyed on the subtotal and never on the total. `estimated_cost_cents` is then `estimated_subtotal_cents` plus `dispensing_fee_cents`, and it is the cash figure disclosed to the patient on the notice.

The patient's response is recorded as a **signature** against the notice, and the shape of the payload decides the outcome. Supplying an `option_selected` signs the notice: `OPTION_1` means the patient wants the item and asks that the payer be billed, `OPTION_2` means the patient wants the item and does not want the payer billed, and `OPTION_3` means the patient does not want the item. Signing sets the notice's `status` to `SIGNED`, records `option_selected` and the `signed_by` party (`PATIENT` or `CAREGIVER`), stamps `patient_signed_at` from the logical clock, and advances both the notice's and the encounter's `version` by 1. Omitting `option_selected` records a **declined** signature instead: the notice's `status` becomes `DECLINED`, `patient_signed_at` is stamped, and the encounter's financial dimension deliberately stays at `ABN_ISSUED` so a replacement notice can be issued. Because a declined notice is not a signed greatest-sequence notice, dispensing stays blocked. A signature is only accepted while the notice's `status` is `ISSUED`; against a notice already `SIGNED`, `DECLINED` or `VOIDED` it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ABN_STATE` and HTTP 409.

The two accepting options and the refusing option diverge sharply. With `OPTION_1` or `OPTION_2` the financial dimension advances `ABN_ISSUED → SELF_PAY` and the encounter is now dispensable on the cash path. With `OPTION_3` the financial dimension advances `ABN_ISSUED → WRITTEN_OFF`, and the clinical dimension cascades to `VOIDED` from wherever it stood — `DRAFT → VOIDED`, `DOCUMENTED → VOIDED` or `RESERVED → VOIDED` — with both `patient_responsibility_cents` and `billable_amount_cents` set to 0 and the encounter's `version` advanced by 1. When the cascade fires from `RESERVED`, it also releases the par reservation: the shelf's `reserved_qty` is reduced by the encounter's `quantity`, its `version` advances by 1, its `available_qty` is restored to the pre-reservation figure, and its `stock_posture` is unchanged because `on_hand_qty` never moved. This cascade fires only for the notice whose `notice_seq` is the greatest for its encounter.

### 3.6. Committing a Device: Par Reservations and Custom Fabrication

Once an encounter is `DOCUMENTED`, the `DmeFitter` decides where the device comes from, and the product's `category` decides which of two mutually exclusive routes is legal. `OFF_SHELF_PREFAB` and `CUSTOM_FITTED_PREFAB` items come off the clinic shelf through a reservation; `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` items are ordered from a supplier. Taking the wrong route — reserving a `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` item, or ordering fabrication for a prefabricated one — is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422.

A **reservation** advances the clinical dimension `DOCUMENTED → RESERVED` and carries `expected_par_level_version` as its only body field. It requires the clinic's posture not to block it, the clinic's accreditation to be current, the product's supplier to be standards-attested and not on quality hold, a shelf to exist for the encounter's clinic-and-product pair, and that shelf's `available_qty` to be at least the encounter's `quantity` — a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PAR_STOCK` and HTTP 422. On success the shelf's `reserved_qty` increases by the encounter's `quantity`, the shelf's `version` advances by 1, and `available_qty` is recomputed downward. The shelf's `stock_posture` is deliberately **not** affected, because `on_hand_qty` did not move, and consequently the clinic's `dispensing_status` is not recomputed either: the clinic recount runs only when a shelf's posture actually changes, and a reservation cannot change it. The encounter's `version` advances by 1.

A **custom-fabrication order** advances the clinical dimension `DOCUMENTED → AWAITING_DEVICE` and carries a `supplier_id` naming the central-fabrication vendor. The named supplier must have `custom_fab_capable` true, or the request is refused with `ERR_CUSTOM_FAB_NOT_SUPPORTED` and HTTP 422; it must be standards-attested and off quality hold; and the clinic's posture must not block. On success a `CustomFabOrder` is created against its parent `encounter_id` with `status` `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and two supplier values are snapshotted onto it: `promised_turnaround_days_snapshot`, copied from the supplier's `promised_turnaround_days`, and `promised_delivery_on`, derived as the calendar date of `submitted_at` advanced by that many whole days. Both scoring variants read the snapshot rather than the live supplier field, so renegotiating a contract does not retroactively rescore old orders. No par stock is reserved and the encounter never touches a shelf. The encounter's `version` advances by 1. Fabrication orders can be read individually — an unresolvable identifier yields `ERR_FAB_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` — and listed with optional `encounter_id`, `supplier_id` and `status` filters.

The supplier side of fabrication is driven by an `InventoryManager` through **fabrication events**, each carrying a single `event` value. `ACCEPT_ORDER` starts or resumes work: it moves `SUBMITTED → IN_FABRICATION`, and it also closes the rework cycle by moving `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED → IN_FABRICATION` and lifts a hold by moving `SUPPLIER_HOLD → IN_FABRICATION`. Starting work from `SUBMITTED` additionally requires the supplier to be standards-attested, off quality hold, and holding an `accreditation_expires_on` on or after the logical clock date; resuming from `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED` requires the supplier to be off quality hold. `DELIVER_TO_CLINIC` moves `IN_FABRICATION → READY_FOR_FITTING` and likewise requires the supplier to be off quality hold. Each event advances the order's `version` by 1. On the **first** delivery event only, `first_delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `first_delivery_days` and `projected_turnaround_score` are derived; later deliveries after a rework round leave `first_delivered_at` untouched, so the projected figure always describes the original shipment. An event that is not declared from the order's current `status` — including any event against a terminal `ACCEPTED` or `CANCELLED` order — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FAB_ORDER_STATE` and HTTP 409.

The fitter's side is the **fitting check**, carrying an `outcome` and an optional free-text `note`. `ACCEPT` moves `READY_FOR_FITTING → ACCEPTED`, requires the acting fitter's `privilege_scope` to be `CUSTOM_AND_OFF_SHELF`, stamps `accepted_at`, derives `total_elapsed_days` and writes `final_turnaround_score`, and then rolls the supplier's reputation forward as described below. `REQUEST_ADJUSTMENT` moves `READY_FOR_FITTING → ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED` and increases `adjustment_rounds` by 1; rework is capped at two rounds, and a request made when `adjustment_rounds` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 422, so the cycle from `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED` through `IN_FABRICATION` back to `READY_FOR_FITTING` may repeat at most twice. `CANCEL` moves `SUPPLIER_HOLD → CANCELLED`, abandoning an order stuck behind a supplier's quality problem; it also reverts the waiting encounter's clinical dimension `AWAITING_DEVICE → DOCUMENTED` and clears its `compliance_hold`, so the fitter can re-route the encounter to a different supplier, and it advances both the order's and the encounter's `version` by 1. Every fitting check requires the acting fitter's credential to be valid, and a fitting check on a custom device by an `OFF_SHELF_ONLY` fitter is refused with `ERR_FITTER_PRIVILEGE_SCOPE`.

Supplier reliability is scored twice from the same order on two different bases, and the two figures deliberately disagree. The common baseline is the order's `promised_turnaround_days_snapshot`. The **projected** figure, written at first delivery, measures `first_delivery_days` as the whole days from `submitted_at` to `first_delivered_at`, floored; its lateness is that count less the promised days, or 0 when the count does not exceed the promise; and `projected_turnaround_score` is 100 less 4 points per late day, floored at 0. It charges **no** penalty for rework at all, so it credits a supplier that shipped something on time and then needed adjustment. The **final** figure, written at acceptance, measures `total_elapsed_days` as the whole days from `submitted_at` to `accepted_at`, floored — an interval that includes every rework round — computes its lateness the same way, and sets `final_turnaround_score` to 100 less 4 points per late day less a further 6 points per adjustment round recorded in `adjustment_rounds`, again floored at 0. For identical order data the final figure is therefore strictly lower than the projected one whenever any rework occurred. Both scores are integers clamped to the range 0 through 100.

Only the final figure feeds the supplier. On acceptance, the supplier's `accepted_fab_order_count` is set to the number of that supplier's fabrication orders whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, and `turnaround_score` becomes the mean of `final_turnaround_score` across exactly that set, rounded half up to a whole point; when the supplier has no accepted orders at all the score stands at 100. Orders in `SUBMITTED`, `IN_FABRICATION`, `READY_FOR_FITTING`, `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED`, `SUPPLIER_HOLD` and `CANCELLED` are excluded from both the count and the average — which means cancelling a badly late order removes it from the supplier's reputation entirely. The supplier's `version` advances by 1 on each acceptance, as does the order's.

### 3.7. The Fitting Session

Fitting is the documentation of physical fit, and it is the only place in the system where a clinical interval is booked against a person. A `DmeFitter` records a session by supplying `fitting_start_at` and `fitting_end_at` — both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional-second digits — a `measurement_set`, and a `fit_outcome`. The `measurement_set` is an opaque, non-empty key-and-value map of recorded anthropometric measurements, persisted verbatim as the fitting documentation; the server neither interprets nor validates its interior. The resulting `FittingRecord` belongs to its parent `encounter_id`, carries `fitter_id` stamped from the acting fitter's identity — the value is never accepted from the body — and carries a derived `fitting_minutes`, the whole minutes from `fitting_start_at` to `fitting_end_at` rounded up, always at least 1. Each record also reserves space for `delivery_signature_role` and `delivery_signed_at`, both null until the encounter is dispensed. Records can be read individually — an unresolvable identifier yields `ERR_FITTING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` — and listed with optional `encounter_id`, `fitter_id` and `fit_outcome` filters.

Before a session is accepted the acting fitter must clear four gates. The fitter must exist: an unknown fitter actor on this operation is reported as `ERR_FITTER_NOT_FOUND` rather than as a generic unknown actor. The fitter's `clinic_id` must equal the encounter's `clinic_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_SCOPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. The fitter's `credential` must not be `NONE` and `credential_expires_on` must be on or after the logical clock date, or the request is refused with `ERR_FITTER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED` and HTTP 422. And when the product's `category` is `CUSTOM_FITTED_PREFAB` or `CUSTOM_FABRICATED`, the fitter's `privilege_scope` must be `CUSTOM_AND_OFF_SHELF`; an `OFF_SHELF_ONLY` fitter may fit only `OFF_SHELF_PREFAB` devices and is otherwise refused with `ERR_FITTER_PRIVILEGE_SCOPE` and HTTP 403. An encounter under a compliance hold accepts no fitting at all and is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE`.

The interval itself is checked twice. First for internal sanity: `fitting_end_at` must be strictly after `fitting_start_at`, and neither instant may fall on a calendar date strictly later than the logical clock date — a same-day instant later than the current clock time is accepted, so a session booked for later this afternoon is fine while one booked for tomorrow is not. A violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` and HTTP 422. Second for exclusivity against the same fitter's existing sessions. The fitter's interval is **closed** and padded by a fifteen-minute changeover buffer on each side, so a new interval conflicts with an existing one when the new start is at or before the existing end plus fifteen minutes **and** the new end is at or after the existing start minus fifteen minutes. Touching endpoints therefore conflict, and so does a gap of exactly fifteen minutes; only a gap strictly greater than fifteen minutes is clear. A conflict is refused with `ERR_FITTING_SLOT_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409. Sessions of other fitters are never consulted.

The `fit_outcome` then decides where the encounter goes, and the three branches carry deliberately different consequences. `ACCEPTABLE` advances the clinical dimension to `FITTED` — either `RESERVED → FITTED` for a shelf device or `AWAITING_DEVICE → FITTED` for a fabricated one — and sets the encounter's `fitter_id` to the acting fitter, which is the value the refit segregation rule will later compare against. `ADJUST_NEEDED` leaves the encounter exactly where it was, so `RESERVED → RESERVED` is a self-loop that may be replayed as many times as the fit remains unsatisfactory, and the par reservation is retained throughout. `REJECTED` sends the encounter back for a new device decision: it reverts `RESERVED → DOCUMENTED` and compensates the reservation by reducing the shelf's `reserved_qty` by the encounter's `quantity`, advancing the shelf's `version` by 1, and restoring `available_qty` to its pre-reservation value — while `stock_posture` stays exactly as it was, because `on_hand_qty` never moved. In all three branches the record is created, the acting fitter's `fitting_minutes_booked` increases by this record's `fitting_minutes` and the fitter's `version` advances by 1, and the encounter's `version` advances by 1.

Fitting a fabricated device carries one extra cross-entity gate. When the encounter's `clinical_status` is `AWAITING_DEVICE`, the encounter's fabrication order must have reached `ACCEPTED` before a fitting session may be recorded, because a device that is still `SUBMITTED`, `IN_FABRICATION`, `READY_FOR_FITTING`, `ADJUSTMENT_REQUESTED` or `SUPPLIER_HOLD` has not been handed over to the clinic yet. A fitting attempted against an unaccepted order is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FAB_ORDER_STATE` and HTTP 409.

### 3.8. Dispensing the Device and the Inventory Cascade

Dispensing is the moment the two dimensions meet, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. Its payload is a single `delivery_signature_role`, naming who signed for the device: `PATIENT`, `CAREGIVER`, or `REFUSED`. A `REFUSED` signature cannot complete a delivery, and neither can a missing one; either is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_SIGNATURE_MISSING` and HTTP 422.

The clinical dimension must be `FITTED` and the financial dimension must be one of `VERIFIED`, `AUTH_APPROVED` or `SELF_PAY` — those three are the only dispensable financial postures, and each advances to `BILLED` on this same request. Any other financial posture is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FINANCIAL_STATE`, with the two re-routings of §2.9: an encounter still at `UNVERIFIED` and holding no verification at all is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_MISSING`, and an encounter sitting at `ABN_ISSUED` — a notice was issued but the patient has not yet responded, or responded with a declined signature — is refused with `ERR_ABN_NOT_SIGNED` and HTTP 422, because the outstanding notice must be signed before the device may be handed over. Beyond the two state gates the request must satisfy a long list of compliance and coverage conditions, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.9. The clinic's accreditation must be current, or `ERR_CLINIC_ACCREDITATION_EXPIRED`. The product's supplier must be standards-attested with current accreditation, or `ERR_SUPPLIER_STANDARDS_NOT_ATTESTED`. The encounter's `compliance_hold` must be false, or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE`. Supervision must be attested and, for a `DIRECT`-requiring item, sufficient at both the clinic and the observed level, or `ERR_IOAS_SUPERVISION_MISSING`. The Stark posture of the ordering physician must be clean, or `ERR_STARK_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`. The acting fitter's credential must be valid, or `ERR_FITTER_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. The clinic's dispensing posture must not block, or `ERR_CLINIC_DISPENSING_SUSPENDED`.

The medical-necessity note must be not merely present but **usable**: `mn_signed_at`, `mn_diagnosis_code`, `mn_functional_deficit` and `mn_expected_duration_months` must all be non-null, and the note must be contemporaneous — the whole days from `mn_signed_at` to the logical clock instant must be at most 90. A note signed more than 90 whole days before delivery is stale and the physician must re-document before the device may be handed over; either failure is refused with `ERR_MEDICAL_NECESSITY_MISSING` and HTTP 422. On the coverage side, at least one coverage verification must exist, or `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_MISSING`; the effective verification's `expires_at` must be on or after the logical clock instant, or `ERR_COVERAGE_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`; and when the product's `requires_prior_auth` is true, the encounter's authorization must be `APPROVED` with an `expires_at` on or after the logical clock instant, or `ERR_PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED`. Finally, whenever the encounter's `quantity` exceeds the number of units the payer will cover, a notice with `status` `SIGNED`, the greatest `notice_seq` for the encounter, and an `option_selected` of `OPTION_1` or `OPTION_2` must exist, or the dispense is refused with `ERR_ABN_REQUIRED_FOR_EXCESS_UNITS` and HTTP 422.

On success the server stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock and records `delivery_signature_role` on the encounter, and copies both of those values down onto the encounter's most recent fitting record as its `delivery_signature_role` and `delivery_signed_at`. `delivered_at` is the anchor for two later windows: the same-or-similar occupation window of §3.4 and the return windows of §3.9. The clinical dimension advances `FITTED → DELIVERED` and the financial dimension advances to `BILLED` — `VERIFIED → BILLED`, `AUTH_APPROVED → BILLED`, or `SELF_PAY → BILLED` — and the encounter's `version` advances by 1.

**Patient responsibility** is computed on this transition, in a pinned order, and written into `covered_units`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, `billable_amount_cents` and `responsibility_variant`. It reads exactly one encounter, that encounter's effective coverage verification, its authorization when the product requires one, its greatest-sequence signed notice when the cash variant applies, and the product for cash pricing; voided and declined notices are excluded, and encounters of other patients and other encounters of the same patient are never included.

Step one selects the effective verification: the row with the greatest `verification_seq`. Step two computes `covered_units`. If that verification's `covered` is false, `covered_units` is 0. Otherwise, if the product's `requires_prior_auth` is true, `covered_units` is the lesser of the encounter's `quantity` and the authorization's `approved_units`. Otherwise `covered_units` equals the encounter's `quantity` outright. The excess unit count is the encounter's `quantity` less `covered_units`. Step three selects the variant: when `covered_units` is 0 the variant is `ABN_CASH_SELFPAY`, and otherwise it is `INSURANCE_ASSIGNED`. The chosen variant is recorded verbatim in `responsibility_variant`.

Under `INSURANCE_ASSIGNED` — which applies whenever at least one unit is coverable, meaning the effective verification says covered and, where required, the payer approved at least one unit — three buckets fill in a fixed order, and re-ordering them changes the answer whenever the deductible does not absorb the whole allowed amount. The allowed total is the verification's `allowed_amount_cents_snapshot` multiplied by `covered_units`. The deductible bucket takes the lesser of the verification's `deductible_remaining_cents` and that allowed total. The coinsurance base is the allowed total less whatever the deductible bucket took, and the coinsurance line is that base multiplied by the verification's `coinsurance_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent on its own. The cash-excess bucket then prices the units beyond `covered_units` at the cash unit price: if the **excess** unit count alone is 2 or more, the per-unit figure is the product's `cash_price_cents` multiplied by 0.85 and rounded half up to a whole cent, and otherwise it is `cash_price_cents` unchanged — the multi-unit discount here is decided by the excess unit count alone and never by the encounter's whole `quantity`. The excess cash amount is that per-unit figure multiplied by the excess unit count, and the dispensing fee is **never** added on this path, because it belongs to a full self-pay quote only. `patient_responsibility_cents` is the deductible amount plus the rounded coinsurance line plus the excess cash amount, with no further rounding of the sum. `billable_amount_cents` — the payer's share, which is non-zero on this path — is the allowed total less the deductible amount less the rounded coinsurance line.

Under `ABN_CASH_SELFPAY` — which applies when `covered_units` is 0, whether because the verification says not covered or because authorization approved zero coverable units — there is no insurance line at all. `allowed_amount_cents_snapshot`, the remaining deductible and the coinsurance fraction are all ignored, and `patient_responsibility_cents` is copied verbatim from the `estimated_cost_cents` of the signed greatest-sequence notice, a figure that **includes** the dispensing fee. That quote is not recomputed at dispense time, so a change to the product's `cash_price_cents` after the patient signed does not move what they owe. `billable_amount_cents` is 0. The same encounter data therefore yields a different `patient_responsibility_cents` under the two variants, which is exactly why `responsibility_variant` is persisted.

Then the inventory cascade fires, in four linked steps, and only for products that are not `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` — a fabricated device never touched a shelf, so it consumes nothing. First, the shelf's `on_hand_qty` and `reserved_qty` are each reduced by the encounter's `quantity` and the shelf's `version` advances by 1. Second, because `on_hand_qty` actually moved this time, the shelf's posture is recomputed and may enter `STOCKOUT`. Third, a posture transition into `STOCKOUT` triggers the clinic recount, raising `stockout_par_level_count` and possibly moving `dispensing_status` to `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`. Fourth, a clinic transition into `SUSPENDED` sets `compliance_hold` to true and `hold_reason` to `CLINIC_SUSPENDED` on every **other** encounter of that clinic whose `clinical_status` is `RESERVED` — this is the intended serialisation point between one fitter emptying a shelf and another fitter's in-flight reservation, and each affected encounter has its own `version` advanced. A dispense that leaves the shelf above its critical floor stops after the first two links; a dispense that empties the second shelf of a clinic runs all four.

### 3.9. Returns, Inspection, Refunds, Refits and Clinic Reconciliation

A delivered device may come back. A `DmeFitter` opens the return with a `reason` — `POOR_FIT`, `DEFECT`, `WRONG_ITEM` or `PATIENT_REFUSED` — and an optional free-text `return_note`, and the encounter moves `DELIVERED → RETURN_PENDING`. The return window depends on the reason and is measured in whole days from `delivered_at` to the logical clock instant: 30 days for `POOR_FIT` and `PATIENT_REFUSED`, and 90 days for `DEFECT` and `WRONG_ITEM`, since a manufacturing defect or a mis-picked item is the practice's fault and earns the longer window. A return opened past its window is refused with `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and HTTP 422, and an encounter that already has a return is refused with `ERR_RETURN_ALREADY_EXISTS` and HTTP 409. The created record begins at `status` `REQUESTED`, records `days_since_delivery` as that same whole-day count, and sets `quantity_returned` to the encounter's full `quantity` — the system does not support partial returns, so the returned quantity is always the entire dispensed quantity. `condition`, the fee and refund figures, `inspected_at` and `refit_encounter_id` are all null at this point. Returns of an encounter can be listed by that encounter, and an unresolvable return identifier yields `ERR_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`.

Inspection is the fork. An `InventoryManager` supplies the physical `condition` — `SELLABLE`, `SOILED` or `DAMAGED` — and the server decides the branch from three facts at once: the device is **restock-eligible** only when `condition` is `SELLABLE` **and** the product's `category` is not `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` **and** `days_since_delivery` is at most 30. A custom-fabricated device is never restockable no matter how pristine, and a `DEFECT` return arriving on day 60 is inside its 90-day return window yet outside the 30-day restock window, so it is validly returned but cannot go back on the shelf. An eligible return goes `REQUESTED → RESTOCKED`; an ineligible one goes `REQUESTED → DESTROYED`. The decision is recorded in `restock_eligible`, `inspected_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and inspecting a return that is not `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE` and HTTP 409.

**The refund** is computed on that same inspection, and it splits the money before it applies any fee. The restock fee fraction comes from the reason: 0.00 for `DEFECT` and 0.00 for `WRONG_ITEM`, because those are not the patient's fault; 0.10 for `POOR_FIT`; and 0.20 for `PATIENT_REFUSED`. That table is then overridden in one case: when the return is **not** restock-eligible and the reason is `POOR_FIT` or `PATIENT_REFUSED`, the fraction becomes 0.25 — a patient-caused return of a device that can no longer be sold carries the heaviest fee, above either of that reason's own base rates. A `DEFECT` or `WRONG_ITEM` return keeps 0.00 even when it is destroyed. The resulting fraction is stored in `restock_fee_pct`.

The amount at risk is the encounter's `patient_responsibility_cents` — what the patient was actually charged, not the allowed amount and not the payer's share. It is split into two components in a pinned order: `device_component_cents` is that charge multiplied by 0.85 and rounded half up, and `fitting_component_cents` is the remainder, the charge less the device component, so the two always re-sum to the charge exactly with no rounding drift. Each component is then refunded at its own rate: `device_refund_cents` is the device component multiplied by one minus `restock_fee_pct`, rounded half up; `fitting_refund_cents` is the fitting component multiplied by one minus **half** of `restock_fee_pct`, rounded half up, because the fitter's time was genuinely consumed and is only partially chargeable back. `refund_cents` is the sum of the two refunds, and `restock_fee_cents` is the charge less `refund_cents` — derived by subtraction, never by multiplying the charge by the fee fraction, so that the two figures always reconcile to the original charge to the cent. When `restock_fee_pct` is 0.00 the full charge is refunded and the retained fee is 0.

The inspection also closes out the encounter and, on the restock branch, restores stock. The encounter moves `RETURN_PENDING → RETURNED` on the clinical dimension and `BILLED → REFUNDED` on the financial dimension, and `billable_amount_cents` is set to 0 because the payer's claim is withdrawn — while `patient_responsibility_cents` is deliberately left at its original value, preserving the historical charge that the refund was computed from. Both dimensions are now terminal. On the `RESTOCKED` branch only, the shelf's `on_hand_qty` increases by `quantity_returned` and the shelf's `version` advances by 1, the shelf's posture is recomputed, and the clinic's `stockout_par_level_count` and `dispensing_status` are recomputed with it — a restock that lifts the clinic's last stocked-out shelf out of `STOCKOUT` can move the clinic out of `SUSPENDED`, and a departure from `SUSPENDED` clears `compliance_hold` and `hold_reason` on every encounter of that clinic whose hold was set with `hold_reason` `CLINIC_SUSPENDED`, unblocking the fitters who were frozen mid-reservation. The `DESTROYED` branch touches no shelf and no posture.

The two inspection branches then re-join. A `DmeFitter` may issue a refit from either `RESTOCKED` or `DESTROYED` — both converge on `REFIT_ISSUED`, which is terminal — and the operation takes no request body at all. Two guards apply. The reason must be `POOR_FIT` or `WRONG_ITEM`, because only a fit failure or a wrong pick warrants a second attempt at practice expense; `DEFECT` and `PATIENT_REFUSED` are refused with `ERR_REFIT_REASON_NOT_ELIGIBLE` and HTTP 422. And the acting fitter's identity must differ from the returned encounter's `fitter_id`, so that the fitter whose work is being redone cannot certify the redo; a self-refit is refused with `ERR_REFIT_SAME_FITTER` and HTTP 409. The acting fitter must also belong to the encounter's clinic, or `ERR_CLINIC_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. On success the server creates a **new** `DispenseEncounter` for the same `clinic_id`, `patient_id`, `product_id`, `ordering_physician_id` and `quantity`, sets that new encounter's `refit_of_encounter_id` to the returned encounter, and copies `mn_diagnosis_code`, `mn_functional_deficit`, `mn_expected_duration_months` and `mn_signed_at` forward so the physician need not re-document — the new encounter therefore opens at `clinical_status` `DOCUMENTED` rather than `DRAFT`, while its `financial_status` starts fresh at `UNVERIFIED` because coverage must be re-verified. The return's `refit_encounter_id` is set to the new encounter and the return's `version` advances by 1. Issuing a refit from a return that is still `REQUESTED`, or twice from the same return, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`.

**Clinic reconciliation** is a computed read that closes the loop on both the units and the cash. It reports the clinic's `clinic_id`, its current `dispensing_status` and `stockout_par_level_count`, and then two ledgers, and it is available to a `PracticeAdmin`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or an `InventoryManager`. The unit ledger counts only devices that pass through a shelf, so every unit total excludes `CUSTOM_FABRICATED` products entirely and covers `OFF_SHELF_PREFAB` and `CUSTOM_FITTED_PREFAB` only. `units_received_total` sums `lifetime_received_qty` across the clinic's shelves — a monotonic figure that stock receipts raise and nothing lowers. `units_on_hand_total` sums `on_hand_qty` and `units_reserved_total` sums `reserved_qty`; reserved units are a **subset** of units on hand, held for named encounters but still physically present, and must never be added to the on-hand figure. `units_dispensed_total` sums `quantity` over every encounter that has ever reached delivery, which means those currently in `DELIVERED`, `RETURN_PENDING` or `RETURNED` — a returned encounter still counts as having been dispensed. `units_restocked_total` sums `quantity_returned` over returns that reached `RESTOCKED` or `REFIT_ISSUED` whose `condition` was `SELLABLE`, and `units_destroyed_total` sums `quantity_returned` over returns in `DESTROYED` or in `REFIT_ISSUED` whose `condition` was not `SELLABLE`; because `REFIT_ISSUED` is reachable from both branches, the recorded `condition` is what assigns a refit-closed return to one bucket or the other. `units_accounted_total` is then `units_on_hand_total` plus `units_dispensed_total` less `units_restocked_total`.

That arithmetic is the system's central inventory invariant, and it must hold at **every** point in a clinic's life, not merely at rest: `units_accounted_total` always equals `units_received_total`. Each individual movement preserves it. A stock receipt raises both `on_hand_qty` and `lifetime_received_qty` by the same amount, so both sides rise together. A reservation moves nothing but `reserved_qty`, which appears in no total on either side. A dispense lowers `on_hand_qty` and raises `units_dispensed_total` by the same `quantity`, so the accounted side is unchanged. A restock raises `on_hand_qty` and raises `units_restocked_total` by the same `quantity_returned`, which is why the restocked figure is subtracted rather than added — without that subtraction a restocked unit would be counted both as on hand and as dispensed. A destroyed return raises neither, leaving the unit permanently on the dispensed side of the ledger, which is correct: it left the building and never came back to the shelf. `units_destroyed_total` is reported for visibility and is deliberately **not** a term in the accounting identity.

The cash ledger runs alongside it. `gross_charged_cents` sums `patient_responsibility_cents` over the clinic's encounters in `BILLED` or `REFUNDED` — a refund does **not** remove an encounter from this total, because `patient_responsibility_cents` records what was charged and is never zeroed; only `billable_amount_cents` is zeroed on refund. `refunded_cents` sums `refund_cents` and `restock_fee_retained_cents` sums `restock_fee_cents`, both over the clinic's returns that have been inspected, meaning those in `RESTOCKED`, `DESTROYED` or `REFIT_ISSUED`. `net_cash_position_cents` is `gross_charged_cents` less `refunded_cents`. Because every inspected return satisfies `refund_cents` plus `restock_fee_cents` equalling the source encounter's `patient_responsibility_cents` exactly, the cash ledger reconciles by construction: once every billed encounter at a clinic has been returned and inspected, `net_cash_position_cents` equals `restock_fee_retained_cents` to the cent, and the only cash the practice has retained is the fees it charged. A clinic whose returns were all `DEFECT` returns therefore ends at a net cash position of 0.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The table below is the complete surface of the API. Every path is prefixed with `/dme` except the logical-clock control, which sits outside the domain namespace. An operation that brings a new resource into existence answers HTTP 201; a read or a modification of an existing resource answers HTTP 200. Two operations are worth calling out because their shape does not match the naive reading of that rule: recording a supplier's standards attestation answers **200**, because it mutates the existing supplier rather than creating anything, and setting the logical clock answers **200**. Every other `POST` in the table answers 201, including those that principally advance a state machine, because each of them also creates a subordinate record — a note, an attestation, a reservation, a fitting record, a decision, a signature, an inspection or a refit. The `Authorized Role(s)` column is exhaustive: any other role receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and role checking precedes every other check on the request.
