# Warehouse Lending Facility API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A warehouse line of credit is the working-capital engine of an independent mortgage banker. Loans are closed with the lender's money, parked on the line for a few days or a few weeks, and then sold to a take-out investor whose purchase proceeds retire the borrowing. Across that whole interval the warehouse lending group carries the collateral risk, and today it manages that risk from a drawer full of spreadsheets: advance rates per loan product in one workbook, wet-funding deadlines in a custodian's inbox, take-out concentration in a monthly report that is already stale on the day it is circulated. This document specifies the service that replaces those artefacts with one auditable system of record for the entire facility.

The system covers the full arc of a warehouse relationship. A credit officer books a facility with a committed line size, a wet sublimit, an aged sublimit, and a scratch-and-dent sublimit, and configures an advance-rate grid per loan product together with the dwell limits and curtailment schedule that govern aging collateral. Operations then requests advances against individual mortgage loans, clears each funding wire through a callback-verification control designed to defeat payee-account fraud, and funds either wet — before the collateral file reaches the document custodian — or dry, only once a clear custodian receipt is in hand. From funding through investor purchase the system tracks collateral dwell time, marks collateral to market, issues margin calls when valuations or take-out commitments deteriorate, curtails advance rates on aged loans, demands repurchase of ineligible collateral, and monitors the borrower's net-worth, liquidity and profitability covenants. Bailee letters govern the window during which a shipped collateral file may be purchased.

Three technical commitments shape the whole design. First, every money movement is mirrored into an append-only ledger, so that facility and advance balances are never stored figures but projections replayed from that log — which in turn makes two conservation invariants exactly assertible. Second, the service never reads the wall clock: dwell days, cure deadlines, bailee expiries and fee accruals all read a logical clock that the test harness sets explicitly. Third, three separate segregation-of-duties gates compare the acting user identity against a recorded originator, so that no single operator can both open and approve a facility, both verify and release a wire, or both raise and waive a margin call. Every collection is addressed absolutely from the API root `/`; there is no shared resource prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). This check runs before the path is resolved, so a request that both omits a header and addresses a non-existent identifier returns 401, never 404. If the supplied role is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint in §4, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) — and role authorisation likewise precedes existence, so a `CollateralAnalyst` attempting a facility approval receives 403 even when the facility identifier does not exist.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `CreditOfficer`, `CollateralAnalyst`, `WireOperator`, `Borrower`, `Custodian`, and `Investor`. The first three are lender-side operator roles; the last three are counterparty roles bound to a persisted entity.

`CreditOfficer` is the warehouse lending group's credit authority. It books facilities, configures advance-rate grids and sublimits, curates the approved-investor list, records take-out commitments, issues and waives margin calls, waives covenant breaches, and escalates repurchase demands. It may not decide a wire instruction and may not fund an advance; that is the operational side of the wall. `CollateralAnalyst` is middle-office collateral operations: it values collateral, ships files under bailee letters, drives purchases and repurchases, may act on wire decisions and on funding, and shares receipt filing and cure with the custodian — but it approves nothing and waives nothing. `WireOperator` is the treasury wire desk; it exists so that the four-eyes rule is testable, because it shares both the wire-decision capability and the funding capability with `CollateralAnalyst`, meaning authorisation alone never guarantees that the two steps were performed by two different people. Its read access reaches only the facility, the advance and the advance's wire instructions.

When `X-User-Role` is `CreditOfficer`, `CollateralAnalyst`, or `WireOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow step demands provenance — as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a facility, as `decided_by_user_id` on a wire instruction, as `issued_by_user_id` and `waived_by_user_id` on a margin call, and as `certified_by_user_id` and `waived_by_user_id` on a covenant test — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The three counterparty roles are entity-backed, and for them `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `WarehouseFacility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a facility compare the resource's facility reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Custodian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DocumentCustodian`; a custodian may read and act only on advances whose `custodian_id` equals `X-User-ID` and on receipts whose `custodian_id` equals `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Investor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TakeoutInvestor`; an investor may read and act only on commitments and bailee letters whose `investor_id` equals `X-User-ID`. For all three, a supplied `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a row of the backing entity at all is itself an authorisation failure and yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; an identity that resolves but addresses a row belonging to a different aggregate yields `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCH` with HTTP 404, deliberately reported as a not-found so that the existence of another counterparty's data is never disclosed.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Filtering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three collections order differently and the ordering is part of the contract: the advance list is ordered by `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending as the tie-break; covenant test history is ordered by `period_end` ascending; and ledger entries are ordered by `posted_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Filters are supplied as query parameters and combine conjunctively. The facility list, the custodian list, the investor list, the commitment list, the bailee-letter lists, the repurchase-demand list, the receipt list of a custodian, the margin-call lists and the covenant-test list each accept `status`. The facility's commitment list additionally accepts `investor_id`. The advance list accepts `facility_id`, `custodian_id`, `investor_commitment_id`, `funding_status`, `custody_status` and `is_aged`. A facility's ledger view accepts `entry_type`, `direction` and `advance_id`; an advance's ledger view accepts `entry_type`. A facility's margin-call list also accepts `trigger_reason`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Most resources also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions.

Two resources are append-only records rather than mutable entities, and they are deliberately thinner: a `CurePayment` and a `LedgerEntry` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, because neither is ever modified after it is written.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a logical clock rather than the wall clock. The server's mandatory default clock value before any call is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The harness moves the clock by supplying `current_utc`; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with the `previous_utc` value it replaced. Setting the clock changes no business state and recomputes nothing — the new instant takes effect on the next business call. Uniquely among all endpoints, the clock endpoint requires no authentication headers. Every datetime the system writes (`approved_at`, `last_accrual_at`, `funded_at`, `wet_receipt_deadline_at`, `shipped_at`, `purchased_at`, `received_at`, `applied_at`, `posted_at`, `raised_at`, `issued_at`, `cure_deadline_at`, `deadline_at`, `effective_from`, `expires_at`) and every dwell, deadline, expiry and accrual comparison reads this clock.

### 2.5. Monetary Amounts, Rates and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in the system is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere, in storage or in a response. Every rate — an advance rate, a haircut, a curtailment step, a concentration limit, a note rate, an unused-line fee rate, a take-out price — is a decimal carried to four decimal places.

Rounding direction is not uniform, and the differences are deliberate. Wherever a rate is applied to a principal amount to size the lender's exposure, the product is **floored**, that is truncated toward zero, so that the lender never over-advances by a rounding cent: this governs the provisional advance amount, the eligible collateral value, the facility-level margin-call amount, and the concentration solve's headroom figure. Wherever the system computes a rate that will be stored and shown, that rate is rounded half up to four decimal places: this governs `effective_advance_rate`. Fee accrual rounds half up to whole cents, but the order of operations is pinned — each advance's interest figure is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded figures are then summed, and the unused-line fee is rounded once as its own separate line. Summing the unrounded per-advance figures and rounding the total once at the end produces a different, incorrect answer. The cure-payment waterfall likewise pins its order: each covered advance's share is floored first, and only then are the leftover cents distributed one at a time. Covenant arithmetic is pure integer cents with no rounding at all.

### 2.6. The Append-Only Facility Ledger and Its Two Conservation Invariants

No balance in this system is a stored counter. Every movement of money writes one `LedgerEntry` naming the `facility_id` it moves, the `advance_id` it is attributable to (null for facility-level entries), an `entry_type`, a `direction`, an `amount_cents` magnitude that is never negative, the `posted_at` logical-clock instant, and a human-readable `memo` recording provenance such as funding an advance, applying cure principal, or receiving investor proceeds. The log is append-only: entries are never edited and never deleted, so replaying it in `posted_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break is fully deterministic.

Seven entry types exist, and they fall into three families. Four of them — `ADVANCE_FUNDED`, `CURE_PRINCIPAL`, `INVESTOR_PROCEEDS` and `REPURCHASE` — are the principal-moving types. Two of them, `FEE_ACCRUAL` and `FEE_PAYMENT`, move the fee balance only. The seventh, `MARGIN_CALL_WAIVED`, is purely informational and moves neither balance. `direction` is either `DEBIT`, which increases whichever balance the entry type governs, or `CREDIT`, which decreases it.

From that log the facility's balances are projected. `outstanding_principal_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the facility's principal-moving entries with direction `DEBIT`, less the sum over the facility's principal-moving entries with direction `CREDIT`. `accrued_fee_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the facility's `FEE_ACCRUAL` entries less the sum over its `FEE_PAYMENT` entries. `availability_cents` is the committed line size less the outstanding principal, and is never negative — a computed shortfall is reported as zero. An advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` applies the same principal-moving filter restricted to entries whose `advance_id` is that advance.

Two invariants follow, and the reconciliation view exposes both sides of each so that they can be asserted in a single call. The first is a ledger invariant: `outstanding_principal_cents` equals `ledger_debit_total_cents` minus `ledger_credit_total_cents`, where those two figures are the debit and credit sums of `amount_cents` over the facility's principal-moving entries. The second is an aggregation invariant: `outstanding_principal_cents` equals `advance_outstanding_sum_cents`, the sum of `outstanding_balance_cents` over the facility's advances whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`. Because `MARGIN_CALL_WAIVED` entries enter neither sum, waiving a call never disturbs either invariant. The reconciliation view also republishes `facility_id`, `committed_amount_cents`, `availability_cents`, `accrued_fee_balance_cents`, `wet_outstanding_cents`, `aged_outstanding_cents`, `scratch_and_dent_outstanding_cents`, `open_margin_call_count` and an `investor_exposures` array carrying one entry per take-out investor of the facility with that investor's identifier and its `exposure_cents`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Booking, Cure and Valuation

Three write operations are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency token naming the current `version` of a parent row. On each of them the token field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is a schema failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`.

A `POST /advances` request must carry `expected_facility_version` matching the target `WarehouseFacility`'s current `version`. Booking a second advance against the same line is entirely legitimate under the product semantics — a line has room for many advances — so the rejection is purely a stale-view check: if any other writer has advanced the facility's `version` since the client last read it, the client's picture of availability, sublimit headroom and investor exposure predates that writer's cascade, and the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A `POST /margin-calls/{id}/cure-payments` request must carry `expected_margin_call_version` matching the target `MarginCall`'s current `version`. Partial cures are legitimately repeatable, so a later payment can never be refused on state grounds; the token instead protects the borrower from sizing a payment against an obsolete `cured_amount_cents`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MARGIN_CALL_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A `POST /advances/{id}/valuation` request must carry `expected_advance_version` matching the target `Advance`'s current `version`. Re-marking collateral is likewise repeatable, so the token exists to stop a client from overwriting a valuation that has already driven a margin call. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ADVANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

Validation is strictly ordered. Rules are evaluated in ascending priority and the **first** rule that fails ends the request: no later rule is evaluated, and no partial write is persisted. Where two rules could both plausibly describe a rejection, the ladder position decides which `error_id` the caller sees. The ladder is as follows.

**1 — Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty on every endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint, failing with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).

**2 — Authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the addressed endpoint's declared roles, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). An entity-backed role whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no row of its backing entity also fails here.

**3 — Path existence.** Every path-parameter identifier resolves to an existing row of the addressed collection, failing with 404 and the code that matches the addressed collection segment: a facility segment yields `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, an advance segment `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_FOUND`, an investor segment `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a custodian segment `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_FOUND`, a commitment segment `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a margin-call segment `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, a wire-instruction segment `ERR_WIRE_INSTRUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, a receipt segment `ERR_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, a covenant-test segment `ERR_COVENANT_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, and a repurchase-demand segment `ERR_REPURCHASE_DEMAND_NOT_FOUND`. Nested paths resolve left to right: the outer identifier is resolved and reported before the inner one is examined.

**4 — Aggregate ownership for entity-backed callers.** The addressed row belongs to the caller's own aggregate, failing with `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCH` (404). This applies to exactly ten operations: reading one facility, reading a facility's exposure summary, reading a facility's ledger entries, reading one advance, listing a custodian's receipts, listing an investor's commitments, listing an investor's bailee letters, filing a receipt against an advance, curing a receipt, and purchasing an advance.

**5 — Body existence.** Every identifier supplied in a request body resolves, and resolves within the addressed facility, failing with the same 404 codes as priority 3. This applies to advance creation, commitment creation and manual margin-call creation. Advance creation validates `facility_id`, `custodian_id` and, when it is non-null, `investor_commitment_id`. A custodian or commitment that exists but belongs to a different facility yields exactly the same not-found code as one that does not exist at all.

**6 — Rate-row existence.** A `ProductAdvanceRate` row exists on the facility for the requested `product_code`, failing advance creation with `ERR_ADVANCE_RATE_NOT_CONFIGURED` (422). Sizing is impossible without a rate row, so this is resolved as an existence question before any schema-independent business rule runs.

**7 — Request schema.** All required body fields are present; types, enum members, string formats and numeric ranges are valid; `limit` lies in the inclusive range 1 to 100 and `offset` is not negative. Failure is `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). This tier also rejects negative or zero cent amounts, rates outside the inclusive range from 0 to 1, a `period_end` that is not a calendar date, a `market_value_cents` that is not strictly positive, an `amount_cents` of zero on a cure payment, and any attempt to set a computed or system-owned field from the body — although such a field is ignored rather than rejected when the supplied value merely echoes what is already stored.

**8 — Optimistic concurrency.** Where the endpoint declares a version token it equals the parent row's current `version`, failing with the 412 code of §2.7 that matches the parent. The comparison happens after existence and schema but **before** any state check, so a stale token against a suspended facility returns 412, not 409.

**9 — Facility own-state.** The addressed facility is in the state its trigger requires: `PENDING_APPROVAL` for approval, `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED` for suspension, `SUSPENDED` for reinstatement, and `PENDING_APPROVAL` for adding a product advance rate. Failure is `ERR_FACILITY_INVALID_STATE` (409). Re-approving an already `ACTIVE` facility is not idempotent — it is an error.

**10 — Facility posture for booking.** The owning facility is `ACTIVE`. Five booking endpoints are gated this way: advance creation, advance funding, commitment creation, investor registration and custodian registration; failure is `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Servicing operations — shipment, purchase, repurchase, receipt filing, receipt cure, cure payments, the aging run and covenant certification — remain available while the facility is `RESTRICTED` and even while it is `SUSPENDED`, so a borrower can always de-risk its way back out. This rule sits above every fiscal rule: a suspended facility whose availability is also exhausted reports `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`, never `ERR_FACILITY_AVAILABILITY_EXCEEDED`.

**11 — Advance funding-axis state.** `funding_status` permits the trigger: `REQUESTED` for funding; `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED` for shipment, valuation, repurchase and raising a repurchase demand; `SHIPPED` for purchase; and `REQUESTED`, `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED` for filing a receipt. Failure is `ERR_ADVANCE_INVALID_STATE` (409). `PURCHASED`, `REPURCHASED` and `REJECTED` are terminal on the funding axis, so every one of those triggers fails from those three states. Re-shipping an already `SHIPPED` advance is the one permitted self-loop and is governed by priority 24 instead.

**12 — Advance custody-axis state.** `custody_status` permits the trigger: `PENDING_RECEIPT` or `RECEIPT_OVERDUE` for a new receipt, and `RECEIVED_EXCEPTION` for a cure. Failure is `ERR_CUSTODY_INVALID_STATE` (409). The custody axis is independent of the funding axis, so a second receipt against an advance already in `RECEIVED_CLEAR` fails here even though its `funding_status` is `FUNDED`.

**13 — Wire state.** The addressed wire instruction is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, failing with `ERR_WIRE_INSTRUCTION_NOT_PENDING` (409). A wire is decided exactly once; `VERIFIED` and `BLOCKED` accept no further decision.

**14 — Margin-call state.** The addressed call is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`, failing both cure payments and waiver with `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_NOT_OPEN` (409). `CURED`, `WAIVED` and `DEFAULTED` calls accept no payment and cannot be waived.

**15 — Commitment state.** The addressed commitment is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, failing withdrawal with `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). `WITHDRAWN`, `EXPIRED` and `FULLY_ALLOCATED` commitments cannot be withdrawn again.

**16 — Covenant-test state.** The addressed test is in `BREACH`, failing waiver with `ERR_COVENANT_TEST_NOT_BREACHED` (409). A `PASS` test has nothing to waive and a `WAIVED` test cannot be waived twice.

**17 — Repurchase-demand state and timing.** The addressed demand is `OPEN` **and** the logical clock has reached its `deadline_at`. Both halves surface the same code, `ERR_REPURCHASE_DEMAND_NOT_OPEN` (409): an `ESCALATED` or `SATISFIED` demand is not escalatable, and an `OPEN` demand may not be escalated before its own deadline, because the borrower owns the full cure window.

**18 — Cross-aggregate gate on reinstatement.** The facility has no margin call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` and no covenant test in `BREACH`, failing reinstatement with `ERR_OPEN_MARGIN_CALL` (409). This gate is notable because it turns on a *different* aggregate's state: the facility row itself is a perfectly valid `SUSPENDED` row, yet the request fails because a child margin call or a breached covenant test is still live. Curing or waiving every one of them flips the identical request to success with no change at all to the facility row.

**19 — Wire verified before funding.** The advance's wire instruction is `VERIFIED`, failing funding with `ERR_WIRE_NOT_VERIFIED` (409). No advance funds on an unverified or blocked wire, whatever the collateral looks like.

**20 — Payee account matches the settlement account.** The wire's `payee_account_last4` equals the owning facility's `settlement_account_last4`, failing with `ERR_PAYEE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). This is the core wire-fraud control: funds leave only to the facility's pre-registered settlement account. It is enforced on the wire decision and again at funding time, so that a settlement account changed after verification cannot be exploited. On the decision endpoint the check applies only when the decision is to verify; a decision to block always succeeds.

**21 — Dual control on funding.** `X-User-ID` differs from the wire's `decided_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION` (409). The operator who verified the wire may not also release it. Because both `CollateralAnalyst` and `WireOperator` are authorised for both steps, this is an identity rule and not a role rule.

**22 — Dry funding requires the file.** For an advance whose `funding_type` is `DRY`, `custody_status` is `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED`, failing with `ERR_CUSTODY_RECEIPT_REQUIRED` (409). Wet funding is exempt — that exemption is exactly what the wet haircut and the wet sublimit price — so the rule is conditional on `funding_type`.

**23 — Live bailee letter before purchase.** The advance has a bailee letter in `ISSUED` status whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, failing purchase with `ERR_BAILEE_LETTER_REQUIRED` (409). An `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` letter fails identically to a missing one.

**24 — Bailee window non-overlap.** No existing `ISSUED` bailee letter of the advance has a window overlapping the new letter's, failing shipment with `ERR_BAILEE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (422). Windows are half-open — inclusive of `effective_from`, exclusive of `expires_at` — so a new letter issued at exactly the prior letter's `expires_at` instant is permitted with zero gap, while one instant earlier is rejected. `HONORED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` letters never conflict.

**25 — Investor approved.** The relevant take-out investor's `status` is `APPROVED`, failing with `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_APPROVED` (422). Three operations are guarded, and the investor is resolved differently on each: shipment and commitment creation check the `investor_id` supplied in the request body, while purchase resolves through the advance's commitment's `investor_id`. A `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `SUSPENDED` investor blocks all three. Because this rule outranks every constraint rule, a suspended investor whose commitment also mismatches reports `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_APPROVED`, never `ERR_COMMITMENT_INVESTOR_MISMATCH`.

**26 — Custodian approved.** The document custodian's `status` is `APPROVED`, failing advance creation and receipt filing with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ACTIVE` (422). A suspended custodian can neither be assigned new collateral nor file new receipts.

**27 — Shipment target matches the allocation.** The shipment's target is the advance's own `investor_commitment_id`, and that commitment's `investor_id` equals the `investor_id` in the request, failing with `ERR_COMMITMENT_INVESTOR_MISMATCH` (422). Collateral cannot be bailee-shipped to any investor other than the one whose commitment it was allocated against.

**28 — Wet funding permitted for the product.** The product's `wet_funding_allowed` is true when `funding_type` is `WET`, failing advance creation with `ERR_WET_FUNDING_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Some products, notably scratch-and-dent, are dry-only by policy.

**29 — One rate row per product.** No `ProductAdvanceRate` row already exists on the facility with the same `product_code`, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADVANCE_RATE` (409).

**30 — One covenant test per period.** No `CovenantTest` already exists on the facility with the same `period_end`, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COVENANT_PERIOD` (409). Restating a quarter is a waiver, never a re-submission.

**31 — Approval needs a priced product.** The facility has at least one `ProductAdvanceRate` row, failing approval with `ERR_NO_ADVANCE_RATES_CONFIGURED` (422). A facility with zero configured rates cannot be approved because every subsequent advance would be unpriceable; exactly one rate row is sufficient.

**32 — Cure payment within the obligation.** A cure payment's `amount_cents` is at most the facility's `accrued_fee_balance_cents` plus the call's remaining obligation, that remaining obligation being `call_amount_cents` less `cured_amount_cents`. Failure is `ERR_CURE_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION` (422). The waterfall has exactly two buckets and no overflow destination, so an over-payment is refused rather than credited somewhere.

**33 — Manual margin call must be non-zero.** The computed `call_amount_cents` is strictly greater than zero, failing manual margin-call creation with `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_AMOUNT_ZERO` (422). A manual call against fully-collateralised collateral computes to zero and is refused.

**34 — Repurchase pays to the cent.** The supplied `payment_amount_cents` equals the advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` exactly, failing with `ERR_REPURCHASE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Partial repurchases do not exist; this exactness is what makes the ledger conservation invariant exactly checkable.

**35 — Minimum advance size.** The stage-four result of the sizing waterfall — the amount after every hard cap but before the concentration solve — is at least the minimum advance size, failing funding with `ERR_ADVANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422). Two carve-outs keep this code disjoint from its neighbours: the rule is skipped entirely when any stage-four cap is zero or negative, because that case belongs to the fiscal tier at priorities 38 to 43 which names the offending bucket; and a shortfall arising in the concentration solve belongs to priority 36 instead.

**36 — Concentration headroom.** The concentration solve returned at least the minimum advance size, failing funding with `ERR_INVESTOR_CONCENTRATION_EXCEEDED` (422). This fires only when the binding constraint was a take-out investor's concentration limit, so the borrower knows which knob to turn, and it is not evaluated at all while the hypothetical post-funding outstanding is at or below the de-minimis concentration floor.

**37 — Commitment window conflict.** A new commitment's window, widened by two days on each side, does not intersect the window of any existing `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` commitment of the same facility-and-investor pair. Failure is `ERR_COMMITMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (422). Windows are closed on both ends, so with the two-day operational buffer a window beginning the day after a prior window ends still conflicts, and the second day after still conflicts; the third day is clear. `WITHDRAWN`, `EXPIRED` and `FULLY_ALLOCATED` commitments reserve no window.

**38 — Facility availability.** `availability_cents` is strictly greater than zero at the moment of funding, failing with `ERR_FACILITY_AVAILABILITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Availability that is merely *smaller* than the provisional amount is not an error at all — the advance simply funds at the reduced amount. Only zero headroom is fatal.

**39 — Wet bucket headroom.** When `funding_type` is `WET`, `wet_sublimit_cents` less `wet_outstanding_cents` is strictly greater than zero, failing with `ERR_WET_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Same semantics as priority 38, restricted to the wet bucket.

**40 — Aged bucket ceiling.** `aged_outstanding_cents` is at most `aged_sublimit_cents`, failing with `ERR_AGED_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). This is an aggregate rule measured on *other* advances: no single advance is individually at fault, but once the facility's aged bucket has passed its sublimit no new advance funds until the aged collateral is curtailed or repurchased.

**41 — Scratch-and-dent ceiling.** When `product_code` is `SCRATCH_AND_DENT`, `scratch_and_dent_outstanding_cents` is strictly less than `scratch_and_dent_sublimit_cents`, failing with `ERR_SCRATCH_AND_DENT_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).

**42 — Per-product ceiling.** When the rate row declares a `product_sublimit_cents`, that sublimit less the product's current outstanding is strictly greater than zero, failing with `ERR_PRODUCT_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). A null `product_sublimit_cents` disables the rule entirely.

**43 — Commitment capacity.** When the advance names a commitment, that commitment's `remaining_amount_cents` is strictly greater than zero, failing with `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). This is the lowest-ranked fiscal rule, so a facility-level shortfall is always reported in preference to an investor-level one.

**44 — Investor own-state.** `TakeoutInvestor.status` permits the trigger: `PENDING_APPROVAL` for approval and `APPROVED` for suspension. Failure is `ERR_INVESTOR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Approving an already `APPROVED` investor, approving a `SUSPENDED` one, or suspending a `PENDING_APPROVAL` one are all rejected rather than silently re-applied; `SUSPENDED` is terminal on this axis.

**45 — Custodian own-state.** `DocumentCustodian.status` is `APPROVED`, failing suspension with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_INVALID_STATE` (409). `SUSPENDED` is terminal for a custodian, so a second suspension is rejected rather than re-stamping the row.

**46 — Segregation of duties on approval-type actions.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the recorded originator of the same workflow step, failing with `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION` (409). Facility approval compares against the facility's `created_by_user_id`; margin-call waiver compares against the call's `issued_by_user_id`. The rule is **skipped** whenever the comparand is null, which is exactly the case for every system-issued margin call. Its position at the very bottom of the ladder is deliberate: the cheaper cardinality and state checks short-circuit first, so an attempt by a facility's own creator to approve a facility that has no configured advance rates reports `ERR_NO_ADVANCE_RATES_CONFIGURED` from priority 31, not this rule.

Priorities 44 and 45 sit at the tail purely because they were added late, and their position is order-irrelevant: their scopes are disjoint from every other rule of priority 4 or above, so no single request can ever violate one of them together with another business rule.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three gates in the system compare the acting identity against a stored originator identity, and all three are identity rules rather than role rules — the acting role is authorised in every case, and the request still fails. A facility may not be approved by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in its `created_by_user_id`. An advance may not be funded by the same `X-User-ID` recorded as the wire's `decided_by_user_id`. A margin call may not be waived by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in its `issued_by_user_id`, and because system-issued calls leave `issued_by_user_id` null, that third gate never blocks the waiver of a system-issued call. All three report `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION`.

### 2.10. Cascade Semantics

Cascades are synchronous and complete within the request that triggers them. When one operation mutates several rows, every mutated row has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, and the response reflects the fully settled state — there is no eventual consistency and no deferred queue. Because a cascade bumps versions, it is also the mechanism by which an optimistic-concurrency token held by another client goes stale: booking an advance advances the facility's `version`, and funding it advances the facility's `version` again along with the advance's, the wire's and the commitment's.

Cascades chain. The deepest chain in the system runs four hops from a single investor suspension: the investor's commitments are withdrawn; each advance allocated to a withdrawn commitment is re-valued as orphaned collateral, which lowers its eligible collateral value and raises its required paydown; each advance whose required paydown has become positive receives a margin call; and the first such margin call moves the facility's posture from `ACTIVE` to `RESTRICTED`. Each hop is a consequence of the previous one, and all four complete before the response is returned.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Facility: Limits, the Advance-Rate Grid and the Custodian Panel

A `CreditOfficer` opens the relationship by booking the facility. The booking payload names the mortgage banker in `borrower_legal_name`, which must be non-empty and at most 200 characters, and states the committed line size in `committed_amount_cents`, which must be at least 100,000,000 cents — one million dollars. Three bucket ceilings are declared alongside it: `wet_sublimit_cents` caps aggregate outstanding attributable to wet-funded advances, `aged_sublimit_cents` caps aggregate outstanding attributable to advances that have become aged, and `scratch_and_dent_sublimit_cents` caps aggregate outstanding attributable to scratch-and-dent product. Each of the three must be at least zero and at most the committed line size. The covenant floors follow — `min_tangible_net_worth_cents`, `min_liquidity_cents` and `min_ttm_net_income_cents`, each of which must be at least zero — together with `max_investor_concentration_pct`, the facility-wide ceiling on any single take-out investor's share of outstanding principal, which must be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than one and is carried to four decimal places. `unused_line_fee_rate` is the annual non-usage fee applied to the undrawn portion of the line and must be at least zero and strictly less than one. `settlement_account_last4` records the last four digits of the borrower's verified settlement account and must be exactly four digits. One optional field, `bailee_period_days`, sets how many days a bailee letter stays live after issuance; it defaults to 30 and must be at least 1 and at most 90.

A newly booked facility lands in `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Its `created_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, while `approved_by_user_id`, `approved_at` and `last_accrual_at` are all null. Every computed figure starts empty: `outstanding_principal_cents`, `accrued_fee_balance_cents`, `wet_outstanding_cents`, `aged_outstanding_cents`, `scratch_and_dent_outstanding_cents`, `open_margin_call_count`, `consecutive_breach_count` and `escalated_demand_count` are all zero, `availability_cents` equals the committed line size, and `last_covenant_status` reads `NOT_TESTED` because no covenant test exists yet.

Before that facility can be approved it needs at least one priced product. Each row of the advance-rate grid is configured by the `CreditOfficer` and applies to exactly one `product_code`, drawn from `CONFORMING`, `JUMBO`, `FHA_VA`, `NON_QM` and `SCRATCH_AND_DENT`; a second row for a product already configured on the same facility is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADVANCE_RATE`. The row's `advance_rate` is the base rate applied to the loan amount for dry funding and must be strictly greater than zero and at most one, to four decimal places. `wet_funding_allowed` says whether wet funding is permitted for the product at all and defaults to true; `wet_haircut` is the rate reduction applied while a wet advance's collateral file is still outstanding, defaults to 0.02, and must be at least zero and strictly less than the row's own `advance_rate`. Three fields govern aging: `dwell_limit_days` is the number of days of dwell a loan may sit before curtailment begins and must be at least 1 and at most 365; `aging_step_days` is the size in days of each curtailment step beyond that limit, defaults to 15, and must be at least 1; and `curtailment_step_rate` is the rate reduction applied per completed step, defaults to 0.005, and must be at least zero and strictly less than one. Finally, `product_sublimit_cents` is an optional per-product outstanding cap that defaults to null, meaning no product cap; when supplied it must be at least zero. Rate rows may only be added while the facility is still `PENDING_APPROVAL` — an attempt after approval is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_INVALID_STATE`.

Approval moves the facility from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `ACTIVE`. It requires the facility to actually be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, requires at least one configured rate row, and requires that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded at booking — the four-eyes rule of §2.9, which is checked last of the three so that a rate-less facility approved by its own creator reports the missing-rates condition rather than the dual-control one. On success the facility's `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting user, `approved_at` is set to the logical clock, `last_accrual_at` is set to the same instant to give fee accrual its baseline, and the facility's `version` increments.

The custodian panel is registered next. Each `DocumentCustodian` row names the facility it belongs to, carries a `custodian_name` that is non-empty and at most 200 characters, and declares `wet_receipt_deadline_hours` — the number of hours after a wet funding within which that custodian must file its receipt. That deadline defaults to 72 hours and must be at least 1 and at most 240 hours, so it may be set as tight as one hour or as loose as ten days. A registered custodian's `status` begins at `APPROVED`. A `CreditOfficer` may later suspend one, supplying a `reason`, which moves `status` from `APPROVED` to `SUSPENDED` and increments the row's `version`; from that point on, funding an advance that names the suspended custodian and filing a receipt by that custodian are both rejected with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. `SUSPENDED` is terminal on this axis, so a second suspension is rejected with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.2. The Approved-Investor List and Take-Out Commitments

Collateral only leaves the warehouse to an investor the credit officer has vetted. Adding a take-out investor to the facility's approved list requires the facility to be `ACTIVE`, and the payload supplies `investor_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), a `counterparty_rating` that is one of `A`, `B` or `C`, and a `concentration_limit_pct` strictly greater than zero and at most one to four decimal places. That per-investor limit is not the only ceiling in play: when concentration is measured, the binding cap is whichever of the investor's `concentration_limit_pct` and the facility's `max_investor_concentration_pct` is smaller. A new investor row starts in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `exposure_cents` of zero and `approved_at` null.

Moving an investor from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED` is a `CreditOfficer` act; it is permitted while the owning facility is in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, and it sets `approved_at` to the logical clock and increments the investor's `version`. Approving an investor that is not in `PENDING_APPROVAL` — including one already `APPROVED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVESTOR_INVALID_STATE`, as is suspending one that is not `APPROVED`. An investor's `exposure_cents` is at all times the sum of `outstanding_balance_cents` over the advances whose commitment belongs to that investor and whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`.

Suspending an approved investor is the single deepest operation in the system, and a `reason` accompanies it. The investor moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and its `version` increments. Then, in the first hop, every commitment of that investor whose status is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. In the second hop, because those commitments are now withdrawn, every advance pointing at them whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED` is re-valued as orphaned collateral, its rate capped at the orphan ceiling of 0.85, which lowers `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and raises `required_paydown_cents`. In the third hop, each such advance whose `required_paydown_cents` has become strictly positive receives a margin call with `trigger_reason` of `COMMITMENT_WITHDRAWN` and status `ISSUED`. In the fourth hop, because at least one open margin call now exists, an `ACTIVE` facility moves to `RESTRICTED`. Separately, every bailee letter still in `ISSUED` that names the suspended investor moves to `REVOKED`.

A take-out commitment is the forward purchase window an approved investor grants. Recording one requires the facility to be `ACTIVE` and the named investor to be `APPROVED`, and it carries `investor_id`, the `product_code` covered, the face amount the investor will buy in `committed_amount_cents` (strictly positive), and the two calendar dates `effective_on` and `expires_on` in year-month-day form in UTC, where `expires_on` must be on or after `effective_on`. The covered interval is closed at both ends: both the first date and the last date are inside the window. The payload also carries `price_pct`, the recorded take-out purchase price as a fraction of the loan amount, which must be strictly greater than 0.5 and at most 1.2 to four decimal places. `price_pct` is economic documentation only and enters no calculation anywhere in this specification, because advance sizing, take-out settlement and the investor-proceeds ledger entry are every one of them denominated in the advance's outstanding principal and never in the investor's purchase price.

Commitment windows for the same facility-and-investor pair may not crowd each other. A proposed window is widened by two days on each side and must not intersect the window of any existing commitment of that pair whose status is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`; an intersection is rejected with `ERR_COMMITMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the underlying windows are closed on both ends and the buffer is two days wide, a window that starts on the day immediately after a prior window's last date still conflicts, a window that starts two days after still conflicts, and only the third day is clear. Commitments in `WITHDRAWN`, `EXPIRED` or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` reserve nothing and never conflict.

Three capacity buckets track a commitment's life. `allocated_amount_cents` is the sum of `advance_amount_cents` over the advances pointing at the commitment whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`; `settled_amount_cents` is the same sum restricted to advances in `PURCHASED`; and `remaining_amount_cents` is the committed face amount less both of those, never below zero. A commitment is created `OPEN` with both allocated and settled at zero and remaining equal to the committed amount, and its status thereafter mirrors those buckets: `OPEN` while remaining equals the committed amount, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` while remaining is strictly between zero and the committed amount, and `FULLY_ALLOCATED` when remaining reaches zero. Funding drives those transitions — an allocation that leaves remaining strictly between zero and the committed amount moves `OPEN → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, and an allocation that exhausts the commitment moves `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → FULLY_ALLOCATED`. A take-out settlement produces the one self-loop on this axis: `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, in which `allocated_amount_cents` decreases and `settled_amount_cents` increases by the identical amount so that `remaining_amount_cents` does not move at all. Every one of these transitions increments the commitment's `version`.

Withdrawal removes a commitment from play. Either the `CreditOfficer` or the owning `Investor` may withdraw a commitment whose status is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` or `FULLY_ALLOCATED`, supplying a `reason`; the status becomes `WITHDRAWN` and `remaining_amount_cents` drops to zero. Withdrawal from `WITHDRAWN`, `EXPIRED` or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The withdrawal cascade mirrors hops two through four of investor suspension: every advance pointing at the commitment whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED` is re-valued at the orphan rate, each such advance whose `required_paydown_cents` is strictly positive receives a `COMMITMENT_WITHDRAWN` margin call, and the first such call moves an `ACTIVE` facility to `RESTRICTED`. Expiry has the same downstream shape: during the nightly aging run a commitment in `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` whose `expires_on` date is strictly earlier than the logical clock's UTC calendar date moves to `EXPIRED`, its advances are re-valued at the orphan rate, and each of those advances with a strictly positive `required_paydown_cents` receives a `COMMITMENT_WITHDRAWN` margin call. `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal.

### 3.3. Booking an Advance and Clearing the Funding Wire

An advance request is a single mortgage loan pledged against the line, and either a `CollateralAnalyst` or the `Borrower` itself may raise one, but only while the facility is `ACTIVE`. The request names the `facility_id` being drawn on, the `custodian_id` that will hold the collateral file, the `product_code` of the loan — which must already have a rate row on that facility, failing with `ERR_ADVANCE_RATE_NOT_CONFIGURED` — the original note amount in `loan_amount_cents`, which must be strictly positive, and the coupon in `note_rate`, which must be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than 0.5 to four decimal places. `funding_type` chooses between `WET`, which funds before the collateral file is in the custodian's hands, and `DRY`, which does not. Three payee fields describe the closing-agent wire: `payee_name`, non-empty and at most 200 characters, plus `payee_account_last4` and `payee_routing_last4`, each exactly four digits. `investor_commitment_id` is optional and may be omitted or null, in which case the advance is orphaned from the outset. The facility version token `expected_facility_version` is mandatory and is discussed in §2.7.

A created advance is off-balance-sheet. Its `funding_status` is `REQUESTED` and its `custody_status` is `PENDING_RECEIPT` — the two axes of the parallel state machine, which evolve independently of each other from this point on. `market_value_cents` initialises to the loan amount, `advance_amount_cents` is zero, `concentration_capped` is false, `dwell_days` is zero, `is_aged` is false, and `rejected_reason`, `funded_at`, `wet_receipt_deadline_at`, `shipped_at` and `purchased_at` are all null. No ledger entry is written and no exposure figure moves, because a merely requested advance has consumed nothing. The facility's `version` does increment, however, which is precisely why every booking invalidates any facility version token another client is holding.

Booking also mints the paired funding wire. A `WireInstruction` is created in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, copying `payee_name`, `payee_account_last4` and `payee_routing_last4` from the advance, and carrying an `amount_cents` equal to the advance's `provisional_advance_amount_cents` as of wire creation. Its `callback_reference` and `decided_by_user_id` are both null until the wire is decided, and its `block_reason` is null unless and until it is blocked.

Deciding the wire is the wire-fraud control at the heart of the funding path, and either a `CollateralAnalyst` or a `WireOperator` may perform it. The `decision` field is either `VERIFY` or `BLOCK`, and a wire may be decided exactly once — a second decision on a wire that has left `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is refused with `ERR_WIRE_INSTRUCTION_NOT_PENDING`. On a verification the request must supply a `callback_reference` that is present and non-empty, recording the out-of-band callback confirmation obtained from the closing agent, and the wire's `payee_account_last4` must equal the owning facility's `settlement_account_last4`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PAYEE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. A successful verification moves the wire `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED`, stores the supplied `callback_reference` and stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, increments the wire's `version`, and deliberately changes nothing else — the advance stays `REQUESTED` and no ledger entry is written.

Blocking is the divergent branch, and its side effects are entirely different. A block supplies an optional `block_reason` which defaults to `OPERATOR_BLOCK`, and an operator-initiated block requires that reason to be `OPERATOR_BLOCK`; the payee-account comparison is not applied to a block, which always succeeds on that dimension. The wire moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → BLOCKED` with `block_reason` stored and `decided_by_user_id` stamped, and the paired advance moves `REQUESTED → REJECTED` with `rejected_reason` set to `WIRE_BLOCKED`. Both the wire's and the advance's `version` increment. Where a verification leaves the advance fundable, a block terminates it: `REJECTED` is terminal on the funding axis, no ledger entry is written, and `outstanding_principal_cents` never moves. There is a second, system-driven route into `BLOCKED`: when the owning facility is suspended, every wire of that facility's requested advances that is still `PENDING_VERIFICATION` moves to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `FACILITY_SUSPENDED`, `decided_by_user_id` left null because no operator decided it, and its `version` incremented; the matching advances move `REQUESTED → REJECTED` with `rejected_reason` of `FACILITY_SUSPENDED`.

Funding is where money actually leaves, and either a `CollateralAnalyst` or a `WireOperator` may release it — but not the same person who verified the wire. The advance must be `REQUESTED` and the facility `ACTIVE`; the wire must be `VERIFIED`, failing with `ERR_WIRE_NOT_VERIFIED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the wire's `decided_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION`; the wire's payee account must still match the facility's settlement account, re-checked here so that a settlement account changed after verification cannot be exploited; the named custodian must be `APPROVED`, failing with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; a dry funding additionally requires `custody_status` to be `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED`, failing with `ERR_CUSTODY_RECEIPT_REQUIRED`; and in no case may `custody_status` be `RECEIVED_EXCEPTION`. When the advance names a commitment, that commitment must be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, its `product_code` must match the advance's, and the logical clock's calendar date must fall inside the closed window from `effective_on` to `expires_on`. The sizing and fiscal gates of priorities 35, 36 and 38 through 43 then apply.

A successful funding moves `REQUESTED → FUNDED` and does a great deal at once. The sizing waterfall of §3.4 runs and stores `provisional_advance_amount_cents`, `advance_amount_cents` and `concentration_capped`. `funded_at` is set to the logical clock, and for a wet funding `wet_receipt_deadline_at` is set to `funded_at` advanced by the custodian's `wet_receipt_deadline_hours`. One ledger entry of type `ADVANCE_FUNDED` and direction `DEBIT` is appended for `advance_amount_cents`, attributed to this advance, which is what makes `outstanding_principal_cents`, `availability_cents`, `wet_outstanding_cents`, `aged_outstanding_cents` and `scratch_and_dent_outstanding_cents` move. The named commitment's `allocated_amount_cents` rises by the funded amount, its `remaining_amount_cents` and status are re-derived, and the investor's `exposure_cents` is re-projected. The wire moves `VERIFIED → RELEASED`, which is terminal for it. The collateral valuation of §3.6 then runs and stores `effective_advance_rate`, `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and `required_paydown_cents`; a strictly positive required paydown at the moment of funding is impossible by construction, so funding never issues a margin call. Both the advance's and the facility's `version` increment.

### 3.4. Sizing the Advance: the Five-Stage Waterfall and the Concentration Solve

The funded amount is the output of a five-stage waterfall that runs exactly once, at funding. Its inputs are the single advance being funded, that advance's rate row on the same facility, its commitment when one is named, and the facility's rollups over sibling advances — and those rollups count **only** advances whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`. Advances in `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `PURCHASED` or `REPURCHASED` are excluded from every outstanding rollup the waterfall reads.

**Stage one selects the base rate.** The rate begins as the `advance_rate` of the rate row matching the advance's `product_code` on the advance's facility. When `funding_type` is `WET`, the row's `wet_haircut` is subtracted from it.

**Stage two applies the orphan cap.** If the advance names no commitment, or the commitment it names has status `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED`, the rate is replaced by the smaller of itself and the orphan ceiling of 0.85. Collateral with no live take-out behind it is never advanced against at more than eighty-five per cent.

**Stage three produces the provisional amount.** The rate from stage two is multiplied by `loan_amount_cents` and the product is floored to whole cents — truncated toward zero, never rounded half up, so the lender never over-advances by a rounding cent. The result is stored as `provisional_advance_amount_cents`.

**Stage four applies the hard caps, and the smallest number wins.** The candidate list always contains the provisional amount and the facility's `availability_cents`. When the rate row declares a non-null `product_sublimit_cents`, the list also contains that sublimit less the product's current outstanding, where a product's current outstanding is the sum of `outstanding_balance_cents` over the facility's advances that share the same `product_code` and whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`. When `funding_type` is `WET`, the list also contains `wet_sublimit_cents` less `wet_outstanding_cents`. When `product_code` is `SCRATCH_AND_DENT`, the list also contains `scratch_and_dent_sublimit_cents` less `scratch_and_dent_outstanding_cents`. When a commitment is named, the list also contains that commitment's `remaining_amount_cents`. The stage-four result is the smallest member of the list. A cap that binds here is emphatically **not** an error: the advance funds at the reduced amount.

**Stage five solves the concentration constraint,** and the ordering of its steps is pinned because the constraint is circular by construction — the concentration ratio is measured on the post-funding outstanding, which itself contains the amount being solved for. Step one takes the stage-four result as the proposed principal. Step two reads the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` as it stands *before* this funding. Step three forms the hypothetical post-funding outstanding by adding the proposed principal to that pre-funding outstanding. Step four short-circuits in two cases: if the advance names no commitment there is no investor to concentrate against and the proposed principal is returned unchanged; and if the hypothetical post-funding outstanding is at or below the de-minimis floor of 5,000,000,000 cents — fifty million dollars — the proposed principal is likewise returned unchanged, because the test only engages once that figure is strictly above the floor. Step five reads the investor's `exposure_cents` as it stands *before* this funding, and takes the binding cap to be whichever is smaller of the facility's `max_investor_concentration_pct` and the investor's `concentration_limit_pct`. Step six tests the proposal directly: if the pre-funding exposure plus the proposed principal is at most the binding cap multiplied by the hypothetical post-funding outstanding, the proposed principal is returned unchanged. This comparison uses exact decimal arithmetic with no intermediate rounding. Step seven, reached only when that test fails, computes the largest whole-cent amount that would satisfy the constraint: multiply the binding cap by the pre-funding outstanding, subtract the pre-funding exposure, divide by one minus the binding cap, and floor the quotient to whole cents; if the result is negative it is treated as zero. Step eight returns whichever is smaller of the proposed principal and that solved headroom. Evaluating the ratio against the pre-funding outstanding instead of the post-funding outstanding yields a different and wrong answer.

The waterfall's output is stored as `advance_amount_cents`, and `concentration_capped` is set true exactly when stage five reduced the amount below the stage-four result and false otherwise.

Three error codes surround the waterfall and they are disjoint by construction. A stage-four cap that is zero or negative means a bucket has no headroom at all, and that case is reported by the fiscal rule naming the offending bucket — `ERR_FACILITY_AVAILABILITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_WET_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_AGED_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_SCRATCH_AND_DENT_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_PRODUCT_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — in the priority order of §2.8. A stage-four result that is positive but smaller than the minimum advance size of 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand dollars — is reported as `ERR_ADVANCE_BELOW_MINIMUM`; a cap chain that grinds the advance down that far funds nothing at all. And a stage-five result below that same minimum, where the binding constraint was the investor concentration limit, is reported as `ERR_INVESTOR_CONCENTRATION_EXCEEDED` so that the borrower knows which knob to turn.

The two funding variants are worth stating side by side, because the same loan produces different money under each. Under wet funding — permitted only when the product's `wet_funding_allowed` is true, and otherwise refused with `ERR_WET_FUNDING_NOT_ALLOWED` — stage one subtracts the `wet_haircut` from the base `advance_rate` and stage four adds the facility's wet-bucket headroom to the cap list, so the same `loan_amount_cents` yields a strictly smaller `advance_amount_cents` than dry funding would. Under dry funding, stage one uses the unreduced `advance_rate` and stage four omits the wet-bucket cap entirely, but the funding transition additionally demands that `custody_status` already be `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED`.

### 3.5. Document Custody: Receipts, Exceptions and Cures

The custody axis of an advance tracks the collateral file rather than the money, and it moves on its own schedule. A receipt may arrive while the advance is still `REQUESTED` — that is the ordinary dry-funding sequence — or after it has already been funded, which is the ordinary wet-funding sequence. Either the `Custodian` itself or a `CollateralAnalyst` files one. The payload names the `custodian_id` filing it and a `document_types` array that must be non-empty and drawn from `NOTE`, `MORTGAGE`, `ASSIGNMENT` and `TITLE_POLICY`. An optional `exception_codes` array, defaulting to empty, reports document defects found on intake and is drawn from `MISSING_NOTE`, `UNSIGNED_ASSIGNMENT`, `STALE_TITLE` and `NAME_MISMATCH`. An empty array means a clear file.

Filing requires the advance's custody axis to be in `PENDING_RECEIPT` or `RECEIPT_OVERDUE` — a second receipt against an advance already in `RECEIVED_CLEAR` fails with `ERR_CUSTODY_INVALID_STATE` even though its funding axis may be perfectly healthy — and it requires that no receipt already exists for the advance with status `CLEAR` or `CURED`. The filing custodian must be `APPROVED`, failing with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and when the caller is acting in the `Custodian` role its `X-User-ID` must equal the advance's `custodian_id`, failing with `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCH`. On success the receipt records `received_at` as the logical clock instant and starts with an empty `cured_exception_codes` array; its `status` is `CLEAR` when `exception_codes` was empty at filing and `EXCEPTION` when it was not — a receipt with defects is created directly in `EXCEPTION` rather than passing through `CLEAR`.

A clear receipt moves the advance from `PENDING_RECEIPT → RECEIVED_CLEAR`, or equally from `RECEIPT_OVERDUE → RECEIVED_CLEAR` when the file finally turns up after a missed wet deadline; both routes carry identical consequences. Because the file is now in hand, the wet haircut falls away, so the collateral valuation re-runs and rewrites `effective_advance_rate`, `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and `required_paydown_cents`. Any margin call on this advance whose `trigger_reason` is `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION` and whose status is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` moves to `CURED`, and that closure re-evaluates the facility's `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` return. The advance's `version` increments.

A receipt with defects moves the advance `PENDING_RECEIPT → RECEIVED_EXCEPTION` instead. The valuation re-runs with a flat five-hundredths reduction — the document-exception haircut of 0.05 — applied on top of everything else, which lowers `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and may push `required_paydown_cents` above zero. When the advance's `funding_status` is `FUNDED` and the recomputed `required_paydown_cents` is strictly positive, a margin call is created with `trigger_reason` of `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION`, attributed to this advance, and sized at that required paydown; that creation in turn moves an `ACTIVE` facility to `RESTRICTED`. The advance's `version` increments either way.

Curing clears the defects. The cure payload supplies `cured_exception_codes`, and the receipt must be in `EXCEPTION`, failing otherwise with `ERR_CUSTODY_INVALID_STATE`; a caller acting as `Custodian` must have an `X-User-ID` equal to the receipt's own `custodian_id`. A cure is complete only when the union of the codes already stored in `cured_exception_codes` and the codes in this request covers every code in the receipt's `exception_codes`. On a complete cure the receipt moves `EXCEPTION → CURED`, the stored `cured_exception_codes` is updated, the advance moves `RECEIVED_EXCEPTION → EXCEPTION_CURED`, the document-exception haircut is removed and the valuation re-runs; when that re-run drives `required_paydown_cents` down to zero, every margin call of this advance with `trigger_reason` of `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION` still in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` moves to `CURED`, and a `RESTRICTED` facility returns to `ACTIVE` once no other open call, no unwaived breach and no escalated demand remain. A partial cure — one whose union still leaves a code uncovered — leaves both the receipt's status and the advance's custody status exactly where they were and merely appends the newly-cleared codes to `cured_exception_codes`. Either way the receipt's `version` increments.

Wet funding buys speed by accepting a deadline. When a wet advance was funded, `wet_receipt_deadline_at` was set to `funded_at` plus the custodian's `wet_receipt_deadline_hours`, and if the nightly aging run finds a wet advance that is `FUNDED`, still in `PENDING_RECEIPT`, and whose deadline instant the logical clock has reached or passed, the advance moves `PENDING_RECEIPT → RECEIPT_OVERDUE`. Where the recomputed `required_paydown_cents` is strictly positive, that overdue file also produces a `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION` margin call sized at the required paydown, and where such a call was created an `ACTIVE` facility moves to `RESTRICTED`. The advance's `version` increments.

The custody axis ends at `RELEASED_TO_INVESTOR`, which is terminal. It is reached only by shipment, from either `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED`, when the collateral file is physically released to the investor under a fresh bailee letter; both source states behave identically and both increment the advance's `version`.

### 3.6. Marking Collateral, Curtailment, Margin Calls and Their Cure

Collateral is valued by one rule that runs everywhere it could possibly matter: at funding, when a receipt is filed, when a receipt is cured, when collateral is marked to market, when a commitment is withdrawn, when an investor is suspended, and on every aging run. Each of those occasions stores three figures on the advance — `effective_advance_rate`, `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and `required_paydown_cents`. Only advances whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED` are valued at all: for an advance in `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `PURCHASED` or `REPURCHASED`, `effective_advance_rate` keeps whatever value it last held and `required_paydown_cents` is forced to zero.

The rate is built up in four steps from the advance's rate row, and each step subtracts from the one before. The first step is the base `advance_rate`, from which the row's `wet_haircut` is subtracted when the advance's `funding_type` is `WET` **and** its `custody_status` is `PENDING_RECEIPT` or `RECEIPT_OVERDUE` — that is, while the file is not yet in hand. This is the crucial difference between the sizing waterfall and the ongoing valuation: sizing charges the wet haircut on every wet advance, whereas the valuation charges it only while the file is genuinely still outstanding, so a clear receipt lifts it.

The second step is aging curtailment. When the advance's `dwell_days` is **strictly** greater than the rate row's `dwell_limit_days`, the number of curtailment steps is the excess of `dwell_days` over `dwell_limit_days`, divided by `aging_step_days` and rounded **up** to a whole number; otherwise the number of steps is zero. The rate is then reduced by the number of steps multiplied by `curtailment_step_rate`. A loan that has dwelt exactly to its limit is not curtailed at all; a loan one day past its limit already incurs a full step.

The third step is the document-exception haircut: when `custody_status` is `RECEIVED_EXCEPTION`, a further 0.05 is subtracted. The fourth step is the orphan cap: when the advance names no commitment, or the commitment it names is `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED`, the rate is replaced by the smaller of itself and 0.85. The result is floored at zero — a rate can never be negative — and rounded half up to four decimal places to become `effective_advance_rate`.

From that rate, `eligible_collateral_value_cents` is the smaller of `loan_amount_cents` and `market_value_cents`, multiplied by `effective_advance_rate`, with the product floored to whole cents. `required_paydown_cents` is the advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` less that eligible value, floored at zero — when the collateral supports more than is drawn against it, the required paydown is simply zero rather than a negative number.

Dwell is measured against the logical clock. While `funded_at` is null, or while the advance's `funding_status` is anything other than `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, `dwell_days` is zero and `is_aged` is false. Otherwise `dwell_days` is the number of complete twenty-four-hour periods — each of 86,400 seconds — that have elapsed from `funded_at` to the logical clock, with any partial period discarded, and `is_aged` is true exactly when `dwell_days` is strictly greater than the applicable `dwell_limit_days`. Aging feeds the facility's aged bucket: `aged_outstanding_cents` sums `outstanding_balance_cents` over the facility's advances that are aged and whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`. When that bucket passes `aged_sublimit_cents` the aging run itself raises no error, because it is a system job, but every subsequent funding attempt on the facility fails with `ERR_AGED_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED` until the excess is cured or the aged collateral is repurchased.

Marking collateral to market is a `CollateralAnalyst`-only act on an advance that is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`. The payload carries `market_value_cents`, which must be strictly positive, together with `expected_advance_version`. The supplied value is stored, the valuation re-runs, and the advance's `version` increments. If the recomputed `required_paydown_cents` is strictly positive **and** the advance has no margin call currently in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`, a margin call is issued with `trigger_reason` of `VALUATION_DECLINE`, status `ISSUED`, and `call_amount_cents` snapshotted at this instant, and an `ACTIVE` facility moves to `RESTRICTED`. If an open call already exists on the advance, the freshly recomputed `required_paydown_cents` is still stored on the advance but the existing call's `call_amount_cents` is deliberately left at its original snapshot — obligations are never silently re-sized underneath a borrower who is part-way through curing them.

A `CreditOfficer` may also raise a call by hand. The payload names the `facility_id`, a `trigger_reason` drawn from `VALUATION_DECLINE`, `COMMITMENT_WITHDRAWN`, `DWELL_EXCEEDED`, `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION` and `COVENANT_BREACH`, an optional `advance_id`, and an optional `cure_days` which defaults to 2. When `advance_id` is supplied the call is advance-level and its `call_amount_cents` is that advance's `required_paydown_cents`; when `advance_id` is null the call is facility-level — the shape used for a covenant breach — and its `call_amount_cents` is the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` multiplied by one tenth, floored to whole cents. `issued_at` is the logical clock, `cure_deadline_at` is `issued_at` advanced by `cure_days` days, `cured_amount_cents` starts at zero, `status` starts at `ISSUED`, `waived_by_user_id` is null, and `issued_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`. A call whose computed amount works out to zero is never created: the system silently skips such a call when it would have issued one automatically, and a manual attempt is refused with `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_AMOUNT_ZERO`. Any newly issued call moves an `ACTIVE` facility to `RESTRICTED`, and the facility's `open_margin_call_count` is at all times the number of its calls in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`.

Curing a call is the borrower's route back. Either the `Borrower` or a `CollateralAnalyst` posts a payment carrying `amount_cents`, which must be strictly positive, together with `expected_margin_call_version`. The call must be `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`, and the payment may not exceed the facility's `accrued_fee_balance_cents` plus the call's remaining obligation, that remaining obligation being `call_amount_cents` less `cured_amount_cents` and never treated as less than zero; an over-payment is refused with `ERR_CURE_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION` rather than credited anywhere.

The payment then runs through a two-bucket waterfall. Bucket one is accrued fees: `applied_to_fees_cents` is the smaller of the payment amount and the facility's `accrued_fee_balance_cents`. Whatever is left after that goes to bucket two, the margin-call principal obligation: `applied_to_principal_cents` is the smaller of the remainder and the remaining obligation. Because an over-payment was already refused upstream, nothing is ever left over after bucket two.

Bucket two is then split across the covered advances. When the call names an `advance_id`, the covered set is that single advance; when the call is facility-level, the covered set is every advance of the facility whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending — advances in `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `PURCHASED` or `REPURCHASED` never receive an allocation. The rounding order is pinned. First, each covered advance's share is `applied_to_principal_cents` multiplied by that advance's `outstanding_balance_cents`, divided by the total `outstanding_balance_cents` across the covered set, floored to whole cents. Second, the leftover — the principal amount less the sum of those floored shares — is distributed one single cent at a time, walking the covered advances in `created_at` ascending order until the leftover is exhausted. Computing the shares from a pre-rounded total instead produces a different and incorrect per-advance split. The outcome is recorded in `principal_allocation`, an ordered array of objects each naming an `advance_id` and an `amount_cents`, listed in `created_at` ascending order of the covered advances. The payment itself records `margin_call_id` and an `applied_at` equal to the logical clock; being an immutable record, a cure payment carries no `version`.

The postings follow the allocation exactly. When `applied_to_fees_cents` is strictly positive, one `FEE_PAYMENT` entry with direction `CREDIT` is appended for that amount with no advance attribution. Then, for every entry of `principal_allocation` whose `amount_cents` is strictly positive, one `CURE_PRINCIPAL` entry with direction `CREDIT` is appended for that amount, attributed to that advance. The call's `cured_amount_cents` rises by `applied_to_principal_cents`, and its status becomes `CURED` when `cured_amount_cents` has reached or passed `call_amount_cents` and `PARTIALLY_CURED` while it is still short. Both the call's and the facility's `version` increment. When the call reaches `CURED` and the facility has no other open call, no breached and unwaived covenant test and no escalated demand, a `RESTRICTED` facility returns to `ACTIVE`.

Waiving is the credit officer's route back. A call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` may be waived with a supplied `reason`, provided the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the call's `issued_by_user_id` — a comparison skipped entirely when that field is null, which is exactly the case for every system-issued call. The call moves to `WAIVED`, `waived_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting user, and one `MARGIN_CALL_WAIVED` entry with direction `CREDIT` is appended for the uncured obligation, that is `call_amount_cents` less `cured_amount_cents` and never below zero. That entry exists purely for audit: it is excluded from both the principal projection and the fee projection, so neither `outstanding_principal_cents` nor `accrued_fee_balance_cents` moves. The waiver re-evaluates the facility's return to `ACTIVE` and increments the call's `version`. `CURED`, `WAIVED` and `DEFAULTED` are all terminal, and a call in any of them refuses both further payment and waiver with `ERR_MARGIN_CALL_NOT_OPEN`.

Calls also close without any payment at all. A clear receipt or a completed cure closes the `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION` calls of that advance; an investor purchase or a borrower repurchase closes every open call of that advance; and a covenant waiver closes every `COVENANT_BREACH` call of the facility. Each of those closures re-evaluates the facility's `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` return. In the other direction, a call still `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` when the logical clock reaches its `cure_deadline_at` is defaulted by the aging run, and that default drives the facility from `RESTRICTED` to `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.7. Shipment Under Bailee Letter, Investor Purchase and Repurchase

Shipping the collateral file is a `CollateralAnalyst` act, and the payload names the `investor_id` the file is going to. The advance must be `FUNDED`, its `custody_status` must be `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED`, the named investor must be `APPROVED` (failing with `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_APPROVED`), the advance must actually have an `investor_commitment_id` and that commitment's `investor_id` must equal the `investor_id` in the request (failing with `ERR_COMMITMENT_INVESTOR_MISMATCH`), and the advance must have no margin call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`. It must also have no bailee letter still `ISSUED` whose window covers the logical clock.

A successful shipment moves the funding axis `FUNDED → SHIPPED` and stamps `shipped_at` with the logical clock, while the custody axis moves from `RECEIVED_CLEAR` or `EXCEPTION_CURED` to the terminal `RELEASED_TO_INVESTOR`. A `BaileeLetter` is created in `ISSUED` naming the advance and the investor, with `effective_from` equal to the logical clock, `bailee_period_days` copied as a snapshot from the facility's own `bailee_period_days` at the moment of issuance, and `expires_at` equal to `effective_from` advanced by that many days. The window is half-open: the letter covers instants from `effective_from` inclusive up to `expires_at` exclusive. Shipment moves documents rather than money, so the commitment's `allocated_amount_cents` does not change and no ledger entry is written. The advance's `version` increments.

A shipped advance may be shipped again, and that self-loop `SHIPPED → SHIPPED` is the only cycle on the funding axis. It requires every existing bailee letter of the advance to be in `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`, the named investor to be `APPROVED`, and the advance to have no margin call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED`. It re-stamps `shipped_at`, leaves the custody axis at `RELEASED_TO_INVESTOR`, and issues a fresh letter whose `effective_from` may equal the prior letter's `expires_at` exactly — half-open windows make that zero-gap adjacency legal, while an `effective_from` even one instant earlier overlaps and is refused with `ERR_BAILEE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Letters in `HONORED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` never conflict with anything.

Purchase is the intended exit, and either a `CollateralAnalyst` or the `Investor` may settle it, optionally recording a `proceeds_reference`. The advance must be `SHIPPED`, it must have a bailee letter in `ISSUED` whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock and whose `expires_at` is strictly after it — otherwise `ERR_BAILEE_LETTER_REQUIRED` — and its `outstanding_balance_cents` must be strictly greater than zero. When the caller acts in the `Investor` role, that letter's `investor_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`. Settlement stamps `purchased_at` with the logical clock, moves that letter `ISSUED → HONORED`, and appends one `INVESTOR_PROCEEDS` entry with direction `CREDIT` for exactly the advance's `outstanding_balance_cents`: the take-out settles at par outstanding, so the advance's balance becomes precisely zero. The funding axis moves to the terminal `PURCHASED`. The facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` and `availability_cents` are re-projected; the commitment moves the amount from `allocated_amount_cents` into `settled_amount_cents`, leaving `remaining_amount_cents` untouched and its status at `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` when remaining capacity is still positive; and the investor's `exposure_cents` drops by the settled amount. Every margin call attributed to this advance that is still `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` moves to `CURED`, which re-evaluates the facility's return to `ACTIVE`. The advance's `version` increments.

Repurchase is the other exit, and it is the borrower buying its own ineligible collateral off the line. Either a `CollateralAnalyst` or the `Borrower` may do it, from either `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, and the payload carries `payment_amount_cents` which must equal the advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` exactly — a difference of a single cent is refused with `ERR_REPURCHASE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, because partial repurchases do not exist and that exactness is what keeps the ledger invariant checkable. One `REPURCHASE` entry with direction `CREDIT` is appended for the payment amount, the funding axis moves to the terminal `REPURCHASED`, and the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents`, `availability_cents` and every sublimit rollup are re-projected along with the commitment's allocation buckets and the investor's `exposure_cents`. When the advance was `SHIPPED`, every bailee letter of that advance still in `ISSUED` moves to `REVOKED`. Every repurchase demand of that advance in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` moves to `SATISFIED`, and where a demand leaves `ESCALATED` the facility's `escalated_demand_count` falls, which re-evaluates the `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` return. Every margin call attributed to the advance still in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` moves to `CURED`, which likewise re-evaluates that return. Both the advance's and the facility's `version` increment.

Demanding a repurchase is how ineligible collateral is formally called out. Either a `CreditOfficer` or an `Investor` may raise a demand against an advance that is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, supplying a `reason` drawn from `DWELL_EXCEEDED`, `CUSTODY_EXCEPTION_UNCURED`, `BAILEE_EXPIRED`, `INVESTOR_SUSPENDED` and `INELIGIBLE_PRODUCT`. The demand is created `OPEN`, its `demand_amount_cents` snapshots the advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` at that instant, `raised_at` is the logical clock, and `deadline_at` is ten days after it. That ten-day window is the borrower's, and it is inviolable: a `CreditOfficer` may escalate a demand only once it is still `OPEN` **and** the logical clock has reached or passed its own `deadline_at`, and both halves of that condition surface the same `ERR_REPURCHASE_DEMAND_NOT_OPEN`. Escalation moves the demand `OPEN → ESCALATED`, raises the facility's `escalated_demand_count` — which is the number of demands in `ESCALATED` across all advances of the facility — and thereby moves an `ACTIVE` facility to `RESTRICTED`. Both the demand's and the facility's `version` increment. `SATISFIED` is terminal, reachable from `OPEN` or from `ESCALATED`, and only ever by repurchase.

### 3.8. Covenant Certification, Breach Escalation and Facility Posture

Once a quarter the borrower certifies its financial condition, and either a `CreditOfficer` or the `Borrower` itself may lodge the certificate. The payload carries `period_end` as an ISO calendar date, together with `tangible_net_worth_cents`, `liquidity_cents` and `net_income_cents`. The `period_end` must be strictly later than the `period_end` of every test already stored against the facility, and a certificate carrying a `period_end` that a stored test already holds is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COVENANT_PERIOD`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stamped into `certified_by_user_id`, and `waived_by_user_id` starts null.

Three covenants are then tested, and the result is computed at creation rather than supplied — a test is born either `PASS` or `BREACH`. Net worth breaches when the certified `tangible_net_worth_cents` is strictly below the facility's `min_tangible_net_worth_cents`, and liquidity breaches when the certified `liquidity_cents` is strictly below `min_liquidity_cents`; a figure exactly equal to its floor passes.

Profitability is the interesting one, because the same certified numbers produce opposite outcomes depending on how much history the facility has. The prior set is every other covenant test of this facility whose status is `PASS`, `BREACH` or `WAIVED`, ordered by `period_end` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break; a waived prior still counts, and tests are never deleted, so the prior set only ever grows. When the facility holds at least three such priors, `profitability_variant` is `TTM_ROLLING` and `profitability_metric_cents` is the certified `net_income_cents` plus the `net_income_cents` of the three most recent priors — a trailing-four-quarter sum, so weak earlier quarters can breach the covenant even when the quarter just certified is strong. When the facility holds fewer than three priors, `profitability_variant` is `ANNUALIZED_QUARTER` and `profitability_metric_cents` is the certified `net_income_cents` multiplied by four; the identical `net_income_cents` that breaches under the rolling variant can pass here. Profitability breaches when `profitability_metric_cents` is strictly below `min_ttm_net_income_cents`. Everything here is exact integer cents with no rounding at any step.

The `breach_codes` array collects `NET_WORTH`, `LIQUIDITY` and `PROFITABILITY` as applicable and is stored sorted, which puts `LIQUIDITY` before `NET_WORTH` before `PROFITABILITY` regardless of the order in which the breaches were detected. The test's `status` is `PASS` when `breach_codes` is empty and `BREACH` when it is not.

A passing test resets the facility's `consecutive_breach_count` to zero. A breaching test increases it, moves an `ACTIVE` facility to `RESTRICTED`, and issues a facility-level margin call with `trigger_reason` of `COVENANT_BREACH` sized at one tenth of `outstanding_principal_cents` — skipped silently when that amount works out to zero. When the breach brings `consecutive_breach_count` to two, the facility goes further and lands in `SUSPENDED`, which forcibly rejects every advance of the facility still in `REQUESTED` with a `rejected_reason` of `FACILITY_SUSPENDED` and blocks each of their wire instructions still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with a `block_reason` of `FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. Throughout, the facility's `last_covenant_status` mirrors the status of the test with the greatest `period_end`, with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, and reads `NOT_TESTED` while no test exists at all.

A `CreditOfficer` may waive a breach with a supplied `reason`. Only a test in `BREACH` is waivable: a test in `PASS` or already in `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_COVENANT_TEST_NOT_BREACHED`. The waiver moves the test to `WAIVED`, stamps `waived_by_user_id`, sets the facility's `last_covenant_status` to `WAIVED`, resets `consecutive_breach_count` to zero, and moves every `COVENANT_BREACH` margin call of the facility still in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` to `WAIVED`. A `RESTRICTED` facility returns to `ACTIVE` once no other open margin call and no escalated demand remain. Crucially, a waiver forgives the consequence and not the history: the waived test stays in the prior set, so it still counts toward both the variant selection and the trailing-four-quarter sum of every later certificate. Both the test's and the facility's `version` increment.

The facility's own posture ties all of this together, and it has no terminal state — a facility can always come back. A facility is created in `PENDING_APPROVAL`. A `CreditOfficer` approves it, which requires that it still be `PENDING_APPROVAL`, that it already carry at least one product advance rate, and that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from its `created_by_user_id`. Those two checks are ordered deliberately: the rate-cardinality check ranks ahead of the four-eyes check, so a facility with no rate rows reports the missing rates even when the approver is also the creator. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` and `approved_at` with the caller and the logical clock, and sets `last_accrual_at` to the same instant as the baseline from which fees will accrue.

From `ACTIVE`, the facility moves to `RESTRICTED` whenever any margin call reaches `ISSUED`, any covenant test is stored in `BREACH`, or any repurchase demand reaches `ESCALATED`. `RESTRICTED` is a lending freeze, not a shutdown: requesting a new advance and funding an existing one both fail with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`, while cures, repurchases, receipt filings, valuations, shipments and purchases all remain permitted, precisely so the borrower can work its way out. The return from `RESTRICTED` to `ACTIVE` is re-evaluated after every margin-call status change, every covenant waiver, every demand satisfaction and every advance repurchase, and it happens only when all three conditions hold at once: `open_margin_call_count` is zero, `last_covenant_status` is one of `NOT_TESTED`, `PASS` or `WAIVED`, and `escalated_demand_count` is zero. That closes a genuine cycle — the same facility may traverse `ACTIVE`, `RESTRICTED` and back any number of times.

`SUSPENDED` is the severe posture, and it is reached three ways: automatically from `RESTRICTED` when a breaching test brings `consecutive_breach_count` to two, automatically from `RESTRICTED` when any margin call reaches `DEFAULTED`, and manually by a `CreditOfficer` from either `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED`. Every route runs the same collateral-damage side effects on `REQUESTED` advances and their `PENDING_VERIFICATION` wires described above. Returning from `SUSPENDED` is a deliberate manual act by a `CreditOfficer`, and it demands a fully clean facility: `open_margin_call_count` zero, no margin call of the facility in `DEFAULTED`, `last_covenant_status` one of `NOT_TESTED`, `PASS` or `WAIVED`, and `escalated_demand_count` zero. Any one of those unmet refuses the reinstatement with `ERR_OPEN_MARGIN_CALL`. Note that the defaulted-call condition is stricter than the one governing the return from `RESTRICTED`: a `DEFAULTED` call is terminal and can never be cured or waived, so it blocks reinstatement permanently even though it does not count toward `open_margin_call_count`.

### 3.9. The Nightly Aging Run and Fee Accrual

One endpoint drives every time-based consequence in the system. Either a `CreditOfficer` or a `CollateralAnalyst` triggers the facility's aging run, which takes no request body and returns a job summary rather than a stored entity: `facility_id`, a `run_at` equal to the logical clock instant, `advances_evaluated`, `curtailed_advance_ids`, `margin_calls_issued`, `bailee_letters_expired`, `receipts_overdue`, `repurchase_demands_raised`, `fee_accrual_cents` and `accrual_days`.

The run performs seven pieces of work. First it refreshes `dwell_days` and `is_aged` for every advance of the facility whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, and the count of those advances is what `advances_evaluated` reports. Second it re-runs the collateral valuation for each of them, rewriting `effective_advance_rate`, `eligible_collateral_value_cents` and `required_paydown_cents`; every advance whose recomputed `required_paydown_cents` is strictly positive and which has no margin call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` receives one with `trigger_reason` of `DWELL_EXCEEDED`, while an advance that already has an open call receives nothing further. The advances whose curtailment step count came out at one or more are listed in `curtailed_advance_ids` in `created_at` ascending order, and the number of calls actually created is `margin_calls_issued`.

Third, every bailee letter still in `ISSUED` whose `expires_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves to `EXPIRED`, and each such letter whose advance is still `SHIPPED` also produces a repurchase demand with a `reason` of `BAILEE_EXPIRED`, `raised_at` at the clock and `deadline_at` ten days later. Those counts populate `bailee_letters_expired` and `repurchase_demands_raised`. A system-raised demand leaves the facility's posture untouched until somebody escalates it. Fourth, every wet advance still in `PENDING_RECEIPT` whose `wet_receipt_deadline_at` the clock has reached or passed moves to `RECEIPT_OVERDUE`, and that count is `receipts_overdue`. Fifth, every investor commitment in `OPEN` whose `expires_on` date falls strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `EXPIRED`, which orphans the advances that named it and therefore lowers their `effective_advance_rate` to the orphan cap on the next valuation. Sixth, every margin call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` whose `cure_deadline_at` the clock has reached or passed moves to the terminal `DEFAULTED`, and a `RESTRICTED` facility then moves to `SUSPENDED`. Finally, if the facility was `ACTIVE` and the run issued at least one margin call, it moves to `RESTRICTED`.

The seventh piece of work is fee accrual, and it is a single facility-level posting. The number of days to accrue, reported as `accrual_days`, is the count of complete twenty-four-hour periods of 86,400 seconds elapsed from `last_accrual_at` to the logical clock, with any partial period discarded. When that count is zero or negative, `fee_accrual_cents` is zero and no ledger entry is written at all.

Otherwise interest is computed advance by advance, and the rounding order is pinned. For each advance of the facility whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `SHIPPED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, the interest is that advance's `outstanding_balance_cents` multiplied by the sum of its own `note_rate` and the facility spread of 0.0225, divided by a day-count basis of 360, multiplied by `accrual_days`, and rounded half up to whole cents **for that advance**. Those per-advance whole-cent figures are then summed. Summing the unrounded per-advance figures and rounding once at the end produces a total that differs by cents and is wrong. Advances that this very run has just curtailed are still included, at the `outstanding_balance_cents` they held at the start of the run.

The unused-line fee is a separate line rounded once on its own. It is the facility's `committed_amount_cents` less its `outstanding_principal_cents`, floored at zero, multiplied by `unused_line_fee_rate`, divided by 360, multiplied by `accrual_days`, and rounded half up to whole cents. `fee_accrual_cents` is the sum of the per-advance interest total and that unused-line fee. When `fee_accrual_cents` is strictly positive, exactly one `FEE_ACCRUAL` entry with direction `DEBIT` is appended for that amount with no advance attribution, which raises `accrued_fee_balance_cents` by the same figure. `last_accrual_at` advances to the logical clock whether or not an entry was written.

That accrual rule is what makes the whole job idempotent at a single clock instant. A second run at the same logical clock value finds `accrual_days` at zero, so it accrues nothing and writes no fee entry; it also issues no duplicate margin call, because every advance that needed one received it on the first pass and now has an open call. Advancing the clock and running again is the only way to accrue more fees.

Endpoints that create a new stored resource return HTTP 201; every other successful call returns HTTP 200.
