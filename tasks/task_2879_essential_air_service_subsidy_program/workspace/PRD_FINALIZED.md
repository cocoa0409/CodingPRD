# Essential Air Service Subsidy API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Small communities whose airports sit far from any large or medium hub cannot sustain scheduled commercial air service on ticket revenue alone. The federal Essential Air Service program closes that gap by paying a negotiated annual subsidy to a carrier that commits to a minimum service pattern — so many round trips a week, so many seats made available, on equipment the program has vetted. Running that program by spreadsheet has become untenable: eligibility rests on distance and enplanement thresholds that must be recomputed whenever a community's data is corrected, competing carrier proposals must be scored against a community's own stated preferences, monthly subsidy payments must be reconciled against departures that were actually flown, and a community may never be left without air service simply because its incumbent carrier wanted out.

This document specifies the Essential Air Service Subsidy API, the system of record for the whole program cycle. It carries fourteen registers — communities, carriers, aircraft types, published fuel indices, eligibility determinations, solicitations, proposals, community preference filings, service agreements, monthly service periods, subsidy payments, performance reviews, termination petitions and rate renegotiations — and every business rule that binds them together. The service is mounted under `/eas`, with a single additional administrative surface under `/_clock` used to drive the program's logical clock during testing.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **fiscal conservation**: every dollar the program commits to an agreement is either still obligated or already disbursed, never both and never neither, and no certification, disbursement or repricing may violate that identity. The second is **continuity of service**: a community that loses its carrier enters a hold-in period during which the incumbent keeps flying at a premium rate and a replacement solicitation is opened automatically, and the incumbent's agreement cannot be released until a successor agreement is actually in force.

## 2. Global Specifications & Program-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the five roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `EligibilityAnalyst`, `SelectionOfficer`, `CommunityRepresentative` and `Carrier`. A `ProgramAdmin` is a program-office operator: it maintains all reference data, drives solicitation status, opens and certifies monthly reporting periods, approves and disburses payments, runs performance reviews and negotiates rates on the program's behalf. An `EligibilityAnalyst` determines community eligibility, distance bands and per-passenger caps, and nothing else. A `SelectionOfficer` issues carrier selection orders and decides community termination petitions. A `CommunityRepresentative` acts on behalf of exactly one community: it files preference rankings over submitted proposals and files or withdraws termination petitions. A `Carrier` acts on behalf of exactly one air carrier: it drafts and submits proposals, files monthly operating reports and amendments, and files, revises or withdraws rate renegotiation requests.

Identity binding differs by role. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `EligibilityAnalyst` or `SelectionOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule requires an audit trail — as `certified_by_user_id` on an eligibility determination, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a performance review, `approved_by_user_id` and `disbursed_by_user_id` on a subsidy payment, and `decided_by_user_id` on a termination petition — but never compares it to any row's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Community`; ownership checks on any resource that references a community compare the resource's community foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Carrier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a carrier compare that resource's carrier foreign-key field — directly on a proposal, or reached through the parent agreement for a service period or a rate renegotiation — to `X-User-ID`.

An authenticated, endpoint-authorised caller that reaches for a resource belonging to somebody else is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Concretely, a `Carrier` may only act on proposals whose `carrier_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, on service periods whose agreement's `carrier_id` equals it, and on rate renegotiations whose agreement's `carrier_id` equals it; a `CommunityRepresentative` may only file preferences against solicitations whose community is itself, and may only file or withdraw termination petitions whose `community_id` equals it. The ownership rule guards exactly nine write operations: proposal creation, proposal submission-dimension actions, preference filing, monthly report filing, report amendment, petition filing, petition withdrawal, renegotiation filing and renegotiation actions.

One endpoint splits authorisation below the role level. The rate-renegotiation action endpoint admits both `ProgramAdmin` and `Carrier`, but `COUNTER`, `ACCEPT` and `REJECT` are reserved to `ProgramAdmin` while `REVISE` and `WITHDRAW` are reserved to `Carrier`. A `Carrier` attempting to counter, accept or reject, or a `ProgramAdmin` attempting to revise or withdraw, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The program also enforces **segregation of duties** across two independent pairs of operations, described in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Successful list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless an endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Five list endpoints deliberately depart from that default: the community list is ordered by `created_at` descending, so the most recently registered community appears first; the fuel index list is ordered by `index_month` ascending; the service-period list is ordered by `period_month` ascending; the preference rows filed against a solicitation are ordered by `filing_sequence` ascending and then `rank` ascending within a filing; and the preference rows that rank a given proposal are ordered by `filing_sequence` ascending.

Filters on list endpoints are conjunctive: supplying more than one narrows the result set. Communities may be filtered by `status` and `state_code`; eligibility determinations of a community by `outcome`; the solicitations of a community by `status` and `created_reason`; fuel indices by `index_month`; preference rows of a solicitation by `filing_sequence`; proposals by `solicitation_id`, `carrier_id`, `aircraft_type_id`, `submission_state` and `evaluation_state`; service agreements by `community_id`, `carrier_id`, `proposal_id`, `predecessor_agreement_id` and `status`; the performance reviews of an agreement by `basis` and `tier`; service periods by `agreement_id`, `status` and `period_month`; subsidy payments by `service_period_id` and `status`; termination petitions by `community_id`, `agreement_id` and `status`; and rate renegotiations by `agreement_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Nested Path Resolution

Every stored row is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID string. Identifiers carry no type prefix of any kind, and clients never supply them on creation. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, taken from the program's logical clock at the moment of creation.

Ten of the fourteen registers additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. Those ten are `Community`, `Carrier`, `AircraftType`, `Solicitation`, `Proposal`, `ServiceAgreement`, `ServicePeriod`, `SubsidyPayment`, `TerminationPetition` and `RateRenegotiation`. The remaining four registers are append-only records that are never modified after creation and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from `FuelIndex`, `EligibilityDetermination`, `CommunityPreference` and `PerformanceReview` responses.

Several read endpoints address a sub-resource beneath a parent — a community's determinations, a community's solicitations, a solicitation's preference filings, a proposal's preference rows, an agreement's performance reviews. Path segments are resolved strictly left to right: the parent identifier is looked up first, and if it does not resolve, the parent's own not-found error is returned before anything further in the request is examined. A request naming a community that does not exist therefore yields `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND` even if the rest of the request is also invalid.

### 2.5. The Program's Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All temporal reasoning — solicitation closing windows, renegotiation cooling-off periods, rate intervals, carrier re-entry buffers, and every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `effective_at`, `rate_effective_from`, `rate_effective_to` and `terminated_at` stamp — reads a single server-held logical clock. Before any caller has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to the machine's real time.

A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the clock's new reading as `current_utc`. Moving the clock has no side effect other than changing what every subsequent temporal rule and stamp observes.

### 2.6. Validation Order and Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the ladder below. Checks are evaluated in this order and the first failure wins.

1. Authentication — both headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the five roles: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint authorisation — the acting role appears on the called endpoint (including the action-level split on renegotiation actions): `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request shape — the body parses, every required field of the endpoint is present, enum values are members of their declared sets, numbers respect their declared ranges and decimal widths, month strings match the `YYYY-MM` form, datetimes are ISO 8601 UTC, and a requested annual subsidy does not exceed 1.25 times the agreement's current `annual_subsidy_usd`: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Existence — every identifier referenced in the path or the body resolves to a stored row, checked left to right: the path identifier first, then body foreign keys in this declaration order — `community_id`, `carrier_id`, `aircraft_type_id`, `solicitation_id`, `proposal_id`, `agreement_id`, `service_period_id`. The failing lookup selects the error: `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND` (all HTTP 404).
5. Resource ownership for the two entity-backed roles: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
6. State admissibility — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. The error names the register and the attempted move: `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_CLOSED`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_REPORTABLE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_AMENDABLE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_APPROVABLE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_DISBURSABLE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`, `ERR_PETITION_NOT_DECIDABLE` or `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (all HTTP 409).
7. Stale-view rejection on the two endpoints that carry an expected parent version: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
8. Carrier standing on proposal submission and on agreement reinstatement: `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`.
9. Segregation of duties on a selection order and on a payment disbursement: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
10. The continuity-of-service guard on an immediate termination grant: `ERR_HOLD_IN_REQUIRED`.
11. The replacement-agreement guard on terminating a hold-in agreement: `ERR_REPLACEMENT_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`.
12. Retired equipment on proposal submission: `ERR_AIRCRAFT_TYPE_RETIRED`.
13. Preference rankings naming a proposal outside the addressed solicitation or one not in a submitted state: `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_IN_SOLICITATION`.
14. Renegotiation trigger coherence and fuel-index availability: `ERR_MISSING_FUEL_INDEX`.
15. At most one open renegotiation per agreement: `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_ALREADY_OPEN`.
16. The solicitation submission window: `ERR_SOLICITATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
17. The renegotiation cooling-off period: `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_COOLING_OFF`.
18. Rate-interval non-intersection for a new agreement: `ERR_RATE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`.
19. The carrier re-entry buffer: `ERR_CARRIER_REENTRY_BUFFER`.
20. One period per agreement and month: `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD`.
21. Monthly period sequencing: `ERR_PERIOD_SEQUENCE_GAP`.
22. Uniqueness of `code` on a community, `certificate_number` on a carrier, `code` on an aircraft type and `index_month` on a fuel index: `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.
23. The per-period departure identity and the passenger-versus-seat check: `ERR_DEPARTURE_COUNTS_UNBALANCED`.
24. Completeness and contiguity of a preference ranking: `ERR_INVALID_PREFERENCE_RANKING`.
25. Weekly frequency at or above the community minimum: `ERR_FREQUENCY_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
26. Weekly seats at or above the community minimum, and seats per departure within the aircraft type's certificated seat count: `ERR_SEATS_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
27. A non-empty candidate set for the selection order: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PROPOSALS`.
28. A usable departure denominator on a report, an amendment or a performance review: `ERR_NO_SCHEDULED_DEPARTURES`.
29. The per-passenger subsidy cap at submission: `ERR_SUBSIDY_PER_PASSENGER_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
30. The cancellation-adjustment ceiling at certification: `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
31. Obligation sufficiency at certification, at disbursement and on a renegotiation acceptance: `ERR_OBLIGATION_EXCEEDED`.
32. The renegotiation revision limit: `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Because state admissibility (rung 6) is evaluated before the stale-view check (rung 7), an amendment against a period that is not in a reported state reports the state error even if the supplied expected version is also wrong. Because carrier standing (rung 8) precedes the equipment, frequency, seat and cap gates, a suspended carrier submitting an under-frequency proposal on retired equipment is told about its standing first.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Pinned Rounding Order

Money in this program is carried to two decimal places and every monetary rounding is round-half-up. Ratios that express service performance — the completion factor of a period or a review, seat fulfilment, load factor, and each component of the carrier selection score — are carried to four decimal places, also round-half-up. A service-quality score is carried to two decimal places. Fuel prices and per-gallon deltas are carried to four decimal places. A community's `driving_miles_to_nearest_hub` and an agreement's `annual_block_hours` are carried to one decimal place, and `avg_daily_enplanements` on a determination to two.

Where a formula rounds an intermediate result, the order is **pinned** and must not be rearranged, because "sum then round" and "round then sum" differ in the last cent or the last basis point. Four such pinned orders exist and each is restated where it is used: the per-departure subsidy rate is rounded to cents *before* it multiplies any departure count; each of the three selection-score components is rounded to four decimals *before* the weighted sum is taken and the sum is then rounded again; the normalised load factor inside a quality score is *not* pre-rounded and the weighted sum is rounded exactly once at the end; and the pro-rated delta applied to an agreement's committed total on a rate acceptance is rounded to cents before it is added to that total, which is then rounded again.

Integer-valued quantities are exact. Where a formula produces a projected passenger count, a monthly seat floor, an annual block-hour figure or a cancellation allowance from a fractional expression, the fractional part is discarded — the value is rounded *down* to the next whole number, never to nearest.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Superseding Writes

Two write operations replace figures that other computations already depend on, and both therefore require the client to prove that it is working from a current view of the parent row.

A preference filing — the request that files a community's ranked preference over a solicitation's submitted proposals — must carry `expected_solicitation_version`, a non-optional field whose value is the `version` of the target `Solicitation` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the solicitation's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because the selection ranking reads only the preference rows belonging to the newest filing, so a later filing silently supersedes an earlier one; a client whose view of the solicitation is stale must re-read the solicitation before re-filing rather than overwrite a ranking that is already in force.

An amendment to a monthly operating report must carry `expected_agreement_version`, a non-optional field whose value is the `version` of the period's parent `ServiceAgreement` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the amendment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An amendment re-bases the agreement's cumulative departure counters by subtracting the previously reported figures and adding the new ones, so amending from a stale view of the agreement would re-base against counters that no longer reflect the ledger.

No other endpoint in this API takes an expected-version field, and no other endpoint may be rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties in the Program Office

Two decisions in this program are deliberately split between two human beings, and the split is enforced on the `X-User-ID` value rather than on the role.

When a selection order is issued for a community, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `certified_by_user_id` recorded on that community's latest eligibility determination — the determination with the greatest `created_at` for the community. The analyst who certified a community's eligibility may not also decide which carrier serves it. When a subsidy payment is disbursed, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the payment's `approved_by_user_id`: the operator who approved a payment may not release its cash. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.10. The Obligation Ledger and Its Conservation Identities

Each service agreement carries its own small ledger of program dollars. `total_committed_usd` is the total obligation booked against the agreement, `disbursed_to_date_usd` is the sum of the net payments already released for the agreement's periods, and `obligated_remaining_usd` is what is left. The program guarantees the identity that `obligated_remaining_usd` plus `disbursed_to_date_usd` always equals `total_committed_usd` — before and after every disbursement, before and after every reversal of a disbursed payment, and after every accepted rate renegotiation. `hold_in_premium_paid_usd`, which accumulates the premiums paid while an agreement is in hold-in status, is deliberately outside that identity: hold-in premiums are extra cash that never disturbs the committed-obligation arithmetic.

A second identity governs reported operations. An agreement's `cumulative_scheduled_departures` always equals the sum of its `cumulative_completed_departures`, `cumulative_cancelled_carrier_departures` and `cumulative_cancelled_weather_departures`, and that must hold after every monthly report and after every amendment, including repeated amendments of the same month.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this program touch more than one register. When they do, every row the operation mutates is a real mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock and its `version` increases by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. A performance review that finds a carrier deficient, for example, mutates the carrier, the reviewed agreement and the served community, and creates a replacement solicitation — four rows, four version bumps, one request.

Cascades run to completion within the single request that triggered them; there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this system. A caller that reads any affected row immediately after a cascading write sees the fully settled result, and a caller holding a version captured before the cascade will be told its view is stale the next time it attempts a write that requires an expected version.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Registers and Reference Data

Everything in the program starts from four registers that only a `ProgramAdmin` may write. Registering a community records its `code` — a three-letter uppercase airport code, exactly three letters A through Z, unique across every community on file — together with a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, the highway `driving_miles_to_nearest_hub` from the community to the nearest large or medium hub airport (zero or more statute miles, carried to at most one decimal place), and the community's `enplanements_prior_year`, a non-negative count of passenger enplanements at the community airport in the prior calendar year. Registration also fixes the service floor any awarded carrier will have to meet: `min_required_weekly_round_trips`, an integer from 1 through 21, and `min_required_weekly_seats`, a non-negative count of one-way seats per week. A newly registered community has `status` `REGISTERED`, no `distance_band`, no `subsidy_per_passenger_cap_usd`, no `latest_determination_outcome`, and an `active_agreement_count` of 0.

Registering a carrier is deliberately thin: a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a DOT operating `certificate_number` of 1 to 32 characters that no other carrier already holds. A new carrier opens in `ACTIVE` standing, with `latest_completion_factor`, `latest_quality_score` and `latest_performance_tier` all empty because no review has been run, and `consecutive_watch_count` at 0.

Aircraft types describe the equipment a carrier may offer. Each carries a `code` of three to five uppercase letters and digits, unique across the register; a descriptive `name` of 1 to 120 characters naming manufacturer and model; `seats`, the maximum certificated passenger seats, from 1 through 100; `is_jet`, true for turbofan equipment and false for turboprop or piston equipment; `fuel_burn_gal_per_hour`, a strictly positive average block-hour fuel burn in US gallons carried to at most one decimal place; and `hourly_operating_cost_usd`, a strictly positive average non-fuel direct operating cost per block hour recorded for program reference. A newly registered type has `is_retired` false. When a type leaves the program's approved list, an administrator retires it — optionally recording a `note` — which sets `is_retired` to true and bars the type from backing any further proposal submission.

Fuel indices are the program's published price series. Each row names an `index_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, unique across the register, and the `price_per_gallon_usd` published for that month: strictly positive and carried to exactly four decimal places. Fuel index rows are append-only; nothing in the program ever rewrites one, which is why they carry no version of their own.

All four registers share one guard. A community `code`, a carrier `certificate_number`, an aircraft type `code` or a fuel `index_month` that duplicates an existing row is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

### 3.2. Community Eligibility and the Per-Passenger Cap

Only an `EligibilityAnalyst` may determine whether a community qualifies for subsidised service, and the determination is a record in its own right rather than a mutable field. The filing takes a single optional input, `waiver_requested`, which defaults to false and signals that the community is asking for relief from the enplanement floor. The determination may be recorded while the community's `status` is `REGISTERED`, `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`; a community that is already `SUBSIDIZED`, in `HOLD_IN`, or `TERMINATED` cannot be re-determined and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The assessment reads exactly one community row and nothing else, and it runs in a fixed order. First the **distance band**. The community's current `driving_miles_to_nearest_hub` is snapshotted onto the determination as `driving_miles_at_determination`, and the band follows from it: a community less than 70 statute miles by road from the nearest hub is `TOO_CLOSE`; a community at least 70 miles but less than 210 miles away is `CAPPED`; a community 210 miles or more away is `UNCAPPED`. Both boundaries are inclusive on the upper side of the comparison — a community exactly 70.0 miles out is `CAPPED`, and one exactly 210.0 miles out is `UNCAPPED`.

Second the **enplanement floor**. The determination records `avg_daily_enplanements`, the community's `enplanements_prior_year` divided by 365 and rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Third the **outcome**, which is written to the determination's `outcome` field. If the band is `TOO_CLOSE` the outcome is `INELIGIBLE_DISTANCE`, and the enplanement figure is never consulted — a community too close to a hub is never reported as failing on traffic. Otherwise, if `avg_daily_enplanements` is at least 10 the outcome is `ELIGIBLE`. If it is below 10 but this filing set `waiver_requested` to true, the outcome is `ELIGIBLE_WITH_WAIVER`. If it is below 10 and no waiver was requested, the outcome is `INELIGIBLE_ENPLANEMENTS`.

Fourth the **per-passenger ceiling**. A determination in the `CAPPED` band records a `subsidy_per_passenger_cap_usd` of 200.00; a determination in the `TOO_CLOSE` or `UNCAPPED` band records no cap at all. The reasoning is statutory: communities in the middle distance band may be subsidised only up to two hundred dollars per passenger, while genuinely remote communities face no per-passenger ceiling.

Each determination also stamps `certified_by_user_id` with the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` — the value later checked by the segregation-of-duties rule on the selection order — and `effective_at` with the logical clock reading at creation. A determination row has no `version` and is never amended; a correction is made by filing a new determination.

The determination then moves the community. An outcome of `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_WAIVER` takes a community from `REGISTERED` to `ELIGIBLE`, or from `INELIGIBLE` back to `ELIGIBLE` — the path taken after a waiver request or a corrected enplanement count. An outcome of `INELIGIBLE_DISTANCE` or `INELIGIBLE_ENPLANEMENTS` takes a community from `REGISTERED` to `INELIGIBLE`, or from `ELIGIBLE` to `INELIGIBLE`. In every case the community's own `distance_band` and `latest_determination_outcome` are refreshed from the new determination, and its `subsidy_per_passenger_cap_usd` is refreshed from the new determination whenever the outcome is `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_WAIVER`. `latest_determination_outcome` is always the outcome of the determination with the greatest `created_at` for that community, and it is consulted much later — when the community's last agreement ends, it decides whether the community falls back to `ELIGIBLE` or all the way to `TERMINATED`.

### 3.3. Solicitation Rounds and the Proposal Pipeline

A solicitation is the program's invitation to carriers to bid on one community's service. A `ProgramAdmin` opens one by naming the `community_id` it seeks service for and the `closes_at` instant after which submissions will no longer be accepted. A solicitation may only be opened for a community whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `SUBSIDIZED` or `HOLD_IN`; a `REGISTERED`, `INELIGIBLE` or `TERMINATED` community may not be solicited and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A newly opened solicitation has `status` `OPEN`, `round_number` 1, `created_reason` `INITIAL`, `preference_filing_count` 0, and neither a `selected_proposal_id` nor a `candidate_count_at_award` yet.

Not every solicitation is opened by hand. When a community loses its carrier, the program itself opens a solicitation for that community with `created_reason` `REPLACEMENT` — the mechanics are in §3.9 — and those rounds are otherwise identical to hand-opened ones.

Three administrative actions drive the solicitation state machine, each accepting an optional `note` alongside the required `action`. **Closing** a round moves it `OPEN → CLOSED` and is refused with `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN` from any other status; as it closes, every proposal of that solicitation whose `submission_state` is `SUBMITTED` and whose `evaluation_state` is still `NOT_EVALUATED` moves `NOT_EVALUATED → UNDER_EVALUATION`, which is how the candidate pool for scoring is formed. **Reopening** a round moves it `CLOSED → OPEN`, is refused with `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_CLOSED` from any other status, increments `round_number` by 1, and moves every proposal of that solicitation whose `evaluation_state` is `UNDER_EVALUATION` to `EXPIRED` while leaving each proposal's `submission_state` untouched. Preference filings already recorded survive a reopening and `preference_filing_count` is not reset, so the community's most recent ranking stays in force across rounds. **Cancelling** a round moves it to `CANCELLED` from either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, is refused with `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_CLOSED` when the solicitation is already `AWARDED` or `CANCELLED`, and expires every proposal of the solicitation whose `evaluation_state` is `NOT_EVALUATED` or `UNDER_EVALUATION`. Both `AWARDED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a solicitation.

A proposal answers exactly one solicitation round, and only the bidding `Carrier` may draft it. The draft names the `solicitation_id` it answers, the `carrier_id` of the proposing carrier — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` — and the `aircraft_type_id` of the equipment offered. It states a `service_level` of `ESSENTIAL_MINIMUM`, `ENHANCED` or `HUB_CONNECT`; the `weekly_round_trips` it commits to operate, from 1 through 21; the `seats_per_departure` it will offer on each departure, from 1 through 100; the `annual_subsidy_request_usd` it asks for, strictly greater than zero and carried to two decimal places; a `term_months` commitment of 12, 24, 36 or 48 months; and a `payment_basis` of either `COMPLETED_FLIGHT_PRORATION` or `SCHEDULED_FLIGHT_FLAT`, which fixes for the whole resulting agreement which of the two payment variants the program will pay on.

Drafting immediately prices the bid on a per-passenger basis, because that is the figure the statutory cap is measured against. The program projects annual traffic as the weekly round trips multiplied by two — each round trip is two departures — multiplied by the seats offered on each departure, multiplied by 52 weeks in the year, multiplied by an assumed load factor of 0.375, with any fractional passenger discarded: the result is `projected_annual_passengers`. Dividing `annual_subsidy_request_usd` by that projected count and rounding half-up to two decimal places gives `subsidy_per_passenger_usd`, the number a community's per-passenger ceiling will be compared against. A fresh draft opens with `submission_state` `DRAFT`, `evaluation_state` `NOT_EVALUATED`, and no `composite_score`.

A proposal carries **two independent state dimensions**, and they evolve separately. The `submission_state` dimension tracks what the carrier has done: `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. The `evaluation_state` dimension tracks what the program has done: `NOT_EVALUATED`, `UNDER_EVALUATION`, and the terminal `SELECTED`, `NOT_SELECTED` and `EXPIRED`. A `SUBMITTED` proposal remains `NOT_EVALUATED` until its solicitation closes; a withdrawal never touches `evaluation_state`, so a proposal can legitimately sit at `submission_state` `WITHDRAWN` while its `evaluation_state` is still `UNDER_EVALUATION`. Such a proposal simply drops out of contention, because the candidate filter of the selection ranking demands a `submission_state` of `SUBMITTED`.

The carrier drives the submission dimension with a single `action` field valued `SUBMIT` or `WITHDRAW`. **Submitting** moves the proposal `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, leaving `evaluation_state` at `NOT_EVALUATED`, and it is the most heavily gated operation a carrier performs. The proposal must currently be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. Its solicitation must be `OPEN`, or the refusal is `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock must be at or before the solicitation's `closes_at` — an attempt exactly at the closing instant is accepted, one strictly after it is refused with `ERR_SOLICITATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The proposing carrier's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; a `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED` carrier is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. The named aircraft type must not have been retired, or the refusal is `ERR_AIRCRAFT_TYPE_RETIRED`.

Three service gates then compare the bid against what the community demands. The offered `weekly_round_trips` must be at least the community's `min_required_weekly_round_trips`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_FREQUENCY_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Weekly one-way seats — the offered round trips multiplied by two and then by `seats_per_departure` — must be at least the community's `min_required_weekly_seats`, and `seats_per_departure` must not exceed the `seats` certificated on the named aircraft type; either failure is refused with `ERR_SEATS_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Finally the per-passenger price is re-derived at submission time, so that a community's cap is applied to the figures actually on the proposal: when the community's `subsidy_per_passenger_cap_usd` is set, the proposal's `subsidy_per_passenger_usd` must not exceed it, and a value exactly equal to the cap is accepted while anything above it is refused with `ERR_SUBSIDY_PER_PASSENGER_CAP_EXCEEDED`. When the community's cap is absent — a `TOO_CLOSE` or `UNCAPPED` band — this gate does not apply at all.

**Withdrawing** moves the proposal to `submission_state` `WITHDRAWN` from either `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` and changes nothing else; a proposal that is already `WITHDRAWN` cannot be withdrawn again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`.

### 3.4. Community Preference Filings

Communities are not passive recipients of whatever carrier the program picks. Between the closing of a round and the selection order, the `CommunityRepresentative` for the solicited community files a ranked preference over the proposals on the table. The filing carries three things: `expected_solicitation_version`, the solicitation `version` the representative last read; `rankings`, an array in which every entry pairs a `proposal_id` with a `rank`; and an optional `rationale`, a free-text explanation that defaults to empty and is stored identically on every row the filing creates.

A ranking must be complete and contiguous. It ranks exactly those proposals of the addressed solicitation whose `submission_state` is `SUBMITTED`, each of them once and only once, and the `rank` values it assigns must form the whole contiguous run of integers from 1 through the number of such proposals, with 1 meaning most preferred. A filing that omits a submitted proposal, names one twice, skips a rank, or starts anywhere other than 1 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PREFERENCE_RANKING`. A filing that names a proposal belonging to some other solicitation, or one whose `submission_state` is not `SUBMITTED`, is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_IN_SOLICITATION`; a `proposal_id` that resolves to no stored proposal at all is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`. Only the representative of the solicitation's own community may file, and any other community's representative is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

An accepted filing increments the solicitation's `preference_filing_count` by 1, creates one preference row per ranking entry stamped with a `filing_sequence` equal to that new count, records `filed_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` — which is the community's own `id` — and increments the solicitation's `version` by 1. The filing answers with the updated solicitation rather than the rows it created; the rows themselves are read back through the solicitation's or the proposal's preference listing, and they carry no `version` of their own because they are never edited.

Filings supersede rather than merge. Earlier filings remain stored and remain readable, but the selection ranking consults **only** the rows whose `filing_sequence` equals the solicitation's current `preference_filing_count`. A community that changes its mind simply files again; the previous ranking contributes nothing from that moment on. This is exactly why the filing must carry `expected_solicitation_version` and why a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` — a representative working from a stale read would otherwise silently displace a ranking it never saw.

### 3.5. The Selection Order, the Award and Agreement Booking

Before committing, a `ProgramAdmin` or `SelectionOfficer` may preview the outcome. The read-only ranking preview scores the solicitation's current candidate set and answers with `candidate_count`, the `top_proposal_id` and its `top_composite_score`, the full `ranked_proposal_ids` list in ranked order, and the `excluded_proposal_ids` that the candidate filter rejected. The preview persists nothing at all — no scores are written and no states move.

Issuing the selection order is the `SelectionOfficer`'s exclusive act, takes an optional `order_note`, and is only admissible while the solicitation's `status` is `CLOSED`; from any other status the refusal is `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_CLOSED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `certified_by_user_id` on the community's latest eligibility determination, or the order is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The **candidate set** is formed by an explicit filter: a proposal qualifies only if it belongs to this solicitation, its `submission_state` is `SUBMITTED`, its `evaluation_state` is `UNDER_EVALUATION`, and its carrier's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. A carrier that has been suspended or debarred since it bid takes its proposals out of contention. If the filter leaves nothing, the order is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PROPOSALS`.

Each candidate then earns three component scores, all on a nominal hundred-point scale and each rounded half-up to four decimal places *before* it is weighted. **Subsidy points** reward the cheapest bid on a per-passenger basis: the program takes the smallest `subsidy_per_passenger_usd` among the candidates and divides it by the candidate's own `subsidy_per_passenger_usd`, multiplied by one hundred — so the cheapest candidate scores exactly 100 and a candidate twice as expensive per passenger scores 50. **Service points** reward frequency: the candidate's `weekly_round_trips` divided by the community's `min_required_weekly_round_trips`, capped at one before it is multiplied by one hundred, so a candidate that merely meets the community's minimum scores 100 and offering more than the minimum earns no additional credit. **Preference points** reward the community's stated ranking, over the current filing only: with N preference rows at the solicitation's current `filing_sequence`, a candidate ranked at position `rank` scores one hundred multiplied by the quantity N minus `rank` plus one, divided by N — the top-ranked proposal scores 100 and the last-ranked scores one hundred divided by N. When no rows exist at the current filing sequence, or when the current filing does not name this candidate at all, its preference points are 0.0000.

The `composite_score` is the weighted sum of those three rounded components — preference points weighted 0.40, subsidy points weighted 0.35, service points weighted 0.25 — with the sum itself rounded half-up to four decimal places. Every candidate's `composite_score` is written to its row, so the scores of the losers remain auditable.

The winner is the candidate with the greatest `composite_score`. Ties are broken deterministically and in this order: the lower `annual_subsidy_request_usd` wins; if those tie, the greater `weekly_round_trips` wins; if those still tie, the earlier `created_at` wins. Row identifiers are never used as a tiebreaker. The winning proposal moves `UNDER_EVALUATION → SELECTED` while every other candidate moves `UNDER_EVALUATION → NOT_SELECTED`, and the solicitation moves `CLOSED → AWARDED` recording `selected_proposal_id` as the winner and `candidate_count_at_award` as the size of the candidate set that was evaluated.

The award then books a **service agreement**, created directly in `ACTIVE` status, which fixes the winning proposal's terms so that later edits to the proposal register can never move the money. The agreement records the served `community_id`, the obligated `carrier_id` and the source `proposal_id`. It copies the winner's round trips into `min_weekly_round_trips`, its `term_months`, its `payment_basis`, and its requested annual amount into `annual_subsidy_usd`. It derives `min_monthly_seats` — the monthly seat-availability floor a later performance review measures against — as the committed weekly round trips multiplied by two, multiplied by the winning proposal's `seats_per_departure`, multiplied by 52 weeks and divided by 12 months, with the fractional part discarded. It derives `annual_block_hours` as the committed weekly round trips multiplied by two, multiplied by 52 weeks, multiplied by an assumed block time of 1.5 hours per departure. It stamps `rate_effective_from` with the logical clock reading at award and computes `rate_effective_to` as that instant advanced by `term_months` calendar months; together they form the half-open rate interval that starts at `rate_effective_from` and stops short of `rate_effective_to`. It captures `baseline_fuel_index_usd` as the `price_per_gallon_usd` of the fuel index whose `index_month` is the calendar month of `rate_effective_from` — the baseline every later fuel renegotiation is measured against. And it opens the ledger: `total_committed_usd` is the annual subsidy multiplied by `term_months` and divided by 12, rounded half-up to cents; `disbursed_to_date_usd` and `hold_in_premium_paid_usd` start at 0.00; `obligated_remaining_usd` is the committed total less the disbursed total, rounded to cents. All four cumulative departure counters — `cumulative_scheduled_departures`, `cumulative_completed_departures`, `cumulative_cancelled_carrier_departures` and `cumulative_cancelled_weather_departures` — start at 0, and `terminated_at` is empty.

Two temporal guards protect the award. The first is **rate-interval non-intersection**: the new agreement's half-open interval must not intersect the corresponding interval of any other agreement for the same community whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Agreements in `HOLD_IN` or `TERMINATED` status are excluded from that sweep entirely, and two intervals that merely touch — one's `rate_effective_to` exactly equal to the other's `rate_effective_from` — do not intersect. An actual overlap is refused with `ERR_RATE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. This is what allows a community to hold more than one `ACTIVE` agreement at a time, provided their rate periods are disjoint. The second is the **carrier re-entry buffer**: if the winning carrier's previous agreement for this same community reached `TERMINATED`, the new agreement may not begin until the logical clock is at or after that agreement's `terminated_at` advanced by 14 days. Exactly at the buffer boundary the award proceeds; strictly before it, the order is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_REENTRY_BUFFER`. Note the difference in shape between the two: the first compares two intervals, while the second compares a single instant against one buffered point.

Finally the award settles the community and, when there is one, the outgoing agreement. If the community is `ELIGIBLE` it moves `ELIGIBLE → SUBSIDIZED`; if it was already `SUBSIDIZED` and this is an additional, non-overlapping agreement it stays `SUBSIDIZED`; in both cases `active_agreement_count` increases by 1. When the community is in `HOLD_IN`, the award resolves the new agreement's `predecessor_agreement_id` to the community's hold-in agreement — the agreement for this community whose `status` is `HOLD_IN`, choosing the one with the earliest `created_at` if somehow more than one qualifies, and leaving the field empty when none does. Resolving a predecessor sets off the release chain described in §3.9: the predecessor moves `HOLD_IN → TERMINATED`, its granted-with-hold-in petition moves `GRANTED_WITH_HOLD_IN → CLOSED`, and the community moves `HOLD_IN → SUBSIDIZED` with `active_agreement_count` increased by 1.

### 3.6. Monthly Service Reporting and Departure Reconciliation

Subsidy is earned month by month, and each month of an agreement has its own reporting bucket. A `ProgramAdmin` opens one by naming the `agreement_id` and the `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form. Months must be opened strictly in sequence: the requested `period_month` must be the month immediately following the greatest `period_month` already recorded for that agreement, or — when the agreement has no periods at all yet — the calendar month of the agreement's `rate_effective_from`. Re-opening a month that the agreement already has is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD`, and that duplicate check is made *before* the sequencing check so that an already-recorded month is named a duplicate rather than a gap; any other month that is not the expected next one is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_SEQUENCE_GAP`. A fresh period opens with `status` `OPEN`, all six reported figures at 0, no `period_completion_factor`, and an `amendment_count` of 0.

Filing the month's operating report is the `Carrier`'s obligation. The report supplies `scheduled_departures`, the departures published in the schedule for the month; `completed_departures`, the ones actually flown; `cancelled_carrier_departures`, those cancelled for carrier-controllable reasons; `cancelled_weather_departures`, those cancelled for weather or air-traffic reasons; `seats_offered`, the one-way seats actually made available during the month; and `passengers_enplaned`, the passengers actually carried. A report is admissible only while the period's `status` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_REPORTABLE` — and only while the parent agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `HOLD_IN`, since a suspended or terminated agreement has no service to report; a report against any other agreement status is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The month must have had at least one scheduled departure; a `scheduled_departures` of 0 is refused with `ERR_NO_SCHEDULED_DEPARTURES`.

Every reported month must balance. The completed departures plus the carrier-controllable cancellations plus the weather cancellations must add up to exactly the scheduled departures, and the passengers enplaned may not exceed the seats offered; either failure is refused with `ERR_DEPARTURE_COUNTS_UNBALANCED`. An accepted report moves the period `OPEN → REPORTED`, writes its `period_completion_factor` as the completed departures divided by the scheduled departures rounded half-up to four decimal places, and adds the month's four departure counts into the agreement's four cumulative counters.

Reports can be corrected. An amendment replaces all six reported figures at once and is admissible only while the period's `status` is `REPORTED`; against any other status the refusal is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_AMENDABLE`. The amendment carries `expected_agreement_version` and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` when it does not match the agreement's current `version`. It must satisfy the same balance identity and the same at-least-one-scheduled-departure requirement as the original report, with the same two error codes. An accepted amendment increases `amendment_count` by 1, rewrites `period_completion_factor` from the new figures, and re-bases the agreement by first subtracting the period's previously stored four counts from the four cumulative counters and then adding the four new ones — never by adding alone. It also increases the agreement's `version` by 1, which is precisely why the next amendment must re-read the agreement first. The period stays in `REPORTED` afterwards, so a month may be amended repeatedly, and the agreement-level conservation identity of §2.10 holds after each one.

### 3.7. Certification, Subsidy Computation and Cash Movement

Before certifying, any `ProgramAdmin`, `SelectionOfficer` or the reporting `Carrier` may pull a read-only payment preview for a reported month. The preview answers with the net amount the month would pay under each of the two bases — `completed_flight_proration_net_usd` and `scheduled_flight_flat_net_usd` — together with the `variant_in_effect` for this agreement, the `divergence_usd` between the two figures, the `per_departure_rate_usd` the month resolves to, and the `credited_departures` the proration basis would credit. Nothing is persisted; no payment is created and no state moves.

Certification is the `ProgramAdmin`'s act, takes an optional `certification_note`, and moves the period `REPORTED → CERTIFIED`. It is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CERTIFIABLE` when the period is in any other status, and also when any earlier month of the same agreement is still `OPEN` — months are settled in order. Certification creates a subsidy payment in `COMPUTED` status, bound to the certified period through `service_period_id`, and records in `variant_used` which of the two payment bases was in force on the agreement at that moment. Although it creates that payment row, certification is an action on the period rather than a creation endpoint: it answers 200 OK with the mutated service period in `data`, and the payment it created is read back through the subsidy-payment endpoints.

The payment arithmetic runs in four steps, and its rounding order is pinned. **Step one** establishes the rates: `monthly_entitlement_usd` is the agreement's `annual_subsidy_usd` divided by 12 and rounded half-up to cents, and `per_departure_rate_usd` is that monthly entitlement divided by the month's `scheduled_departures` and rounded half-up to cents. The per-departure rate is rounded to cents *before* it multiplies anything; summing first and rounding last would differ by cents and is not what this program pays.

**Step two** computes `gross_subsidy_usd`, and this is where the two payment bases diverge on identical operating data. Under `COMPLETED_FLIGHT_PRORATION` the program pays per departure actually flown, with a limited credit for excusable weather cancellations: the weather allowance is 5 percent of the month's scheduled departures with the fractional part discarded, `credited_departures` is the completed departures plus the smaller of the weather cancellations and that allowance, and the gross is the per-departure rate multiplied by the credited departures, rounded half-up to cents. Weather cancellations beyond the allowance earn nothing. Under `SCHEDULED_FLIGHT_FLAT` the program pays the whole monthly entitlement less the per-departure rate for each carrier-controllable cancellation, rounded half-up to cents; weather cancellations are paid in full and the weather allowance is irrelevant, and `credited_departures` is recorded as 0.

**Step three** applies the cancellation adjustment, which is identical under both bases. The carrier allowance is 2 percent of the month's scheduled departures with the fractional part discarded; the excess is the carrier-controllable cancellations less that allowance, floored at zero so a carrier inside its allowance is never charged; and `cancellation_adjustment_usd` is the per-departure rate multiplied by that excess, multiplied by a penalty rate of one half, rounded half-up to cents. The adjustment is capped for fairness: if it would exceed 25 percent of `monthly_entitlement_usd` — that ceiling itself rounded half-up to cents — certification is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_CAP` rather than producing a punitive payment.

**Step four** applies performance withholding, tiered on the month's own `period_completion_factor`. A completion factor of 0.9500 or better withholds nothing, so `withholding_pct` is 0.00; a factor from 0.9000 up to but not including 0.9500 withholds 0.02; a factor from 0.8500 up to but not including 0.9000 withholds 0.05; anything below 0.8500 withholds 0.10. `performance_withholding_usd` is the gross subsidy less the cancellation adjustment, multiplied by that rate and rounded half-up to cents. `net_payment_usd` is then the gross subsidy less the cancellation adjustment less the performance withholding, rounded half-up to cents — and that net figure, not the gross, is what the obligation ledger books.

Certification will not be allowed to overrun the agreement's obligation. Before the payment is created, the program checks that the monthly entitlement less the cancellation adjustment, rounded half-up to cents, is no greater than the agreement's `obligated_remaining_usd`; if it is greater, certification is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_EXCEEDED`.

An agreement flying under hold-in earns a premium, and the premium tier is keyed on the **pre-premium** net payment so that the arithmetic is never circular. When the agreement's `status` is `HOLD_IN` at certification, the program takes `net_payment_usd` as the base: a base of 120,000.00 dollars or more earns a premium rate of 0.03, a base of at least 100,000.00 dollars but under 120,000.00 earns 0.05, and a base below 100,000.00 earns 0.08. `hold_in_premium_usd` is the base multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to cents, and `total_disbursement_usd` — computed last — is the base plus the premium, rounded half-up to cents. For an agreement that is not in hold-in, `hold_in_premium_usd` is 0.00 and `total_disbursement_usd` equals `net_payment_usd`.

Cash then moves in two hands. A `ProgramAdmin` **approves** a payment, which moves it `COMPUTED → APPROVED` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`; approval requires the payment to be in `COMPUTED` status, with its period `CERTIFIED`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_APPROVABLE`. A different `ProgramAdmin` **disburses** it, which moves it `APPROVED → DISBURSED`; a payment in any other status is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_DISBURSABLE`, an acting user equal to `approved_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and a `net_payment_usd` greater than the agreement's `obligated_remaining_usd` is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_EXCEEDED`. Disbursement records `disbursed_by_user_id`, adds `net_payment_usd` to the agreement's `disbursed_to_date_usd`, adds `hold_in_premium_usd` to its `hold_in_premium_paid_usd`, recomputes `obligated_remaining_usd` as the committed total less the disbursed total rounded to cents, and moves the period `CERTIFIED → PAID`.

**Reversal** is the compensating action and requires a `reversal_reason_code` of `AUDIT_FINDING`, `REPORTING_ERROR` or `OVERPAYMENT`. A payment may be reversed from either `APPROVED` or `DISBURSED`; from any other status the refusal is `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`, and `REVERSED` is terminal. Reversing an `APPROVED` payment records the reason code and moves the period `CERTIFIED → REPORTED` without touching the ledger, because nothing was ever paid. Reversing a `DISBURSED` payment records the reason code, subtracts `net_payment_usd` back out of `disbursed_to_date_usd`, subtracts `hold_in_premium_usd` back out of `hold_in_premium_paid_usd`, recomputes `obligated_remaining_usd`, and moves the period `PAID → REPORTED`. Either way the month lands back in `REPORTED`, where it can be amended and certified afresh — the cycle `REPORTED → CERTIFIED → PAID → REPORTED` is deliberate, and the obligation identity of §2.10 holds on both sides of the reversal.

### 3.8. Performance Review, Carrier Standing and Enforcement

Periodically a `ProgramAdmin` reviews how a carrier has actually performed on an agreement. The review names the `agreement_id`, a `basis` of either `STRICT` or `ADJUSTED`, and the inclusive month window bounded by `window_start_month` and `window_end_month`, both in `YYYY-MM` form, with the start month no later than the end month. `months_in_window` is the inclusive count of calendar months from the start month to the end month — a window whose start and end are the same month counts as one month.

The review basis is drawn from the agreement's settled months only. A month is included when it belongs to the reviewed agreement, its `period_month` falls inside the window with both bounds inclusive, and its `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `PAID`; `included_period_count` records how many qualified. Months still `OPEN` or `REPORTED` are excluded, which has a deliberate consequence: reversing a payment pushes its month back to `REPORTED` and thereby removes that month from any subsequent review's basis. Over the included months the review totals `sum_scheduled_departures`, `sum_completed_departures`, `sum_cancelled_weather_departures`, `sum_seats_offered` and `sum_passengers_enplaned`.

The two bases treat weather differently, and the difference can move a carrier a whole tier. If the summed scheduled departures are zero the review is refused with `ERR_NO_SCHEDULED_DEPARTURES`. Under `STRICT`, `completion_factor` is the summed completed departures divided by the summed scheduled departures, rounded half-up to four decimal places — weather cancellations are charged against the carrier. Under `ADJUSTED`, the denominator is the summed scheduled departures less the summed weather cancellations, and `completion_factor` is the summed completed departures divided by that reduced denominator, again rounded half-up to four decimal places; if that reduced denominator comes out at zero, the review is likewise refused with `ERR_NO_SCHEDULED_DEPARTURES`. The same months of data can therefore read `WATCH` under `STRICT` and `COMPLIANT` under `ADJUSTED`, which is exactly why the basis is an explicit input and is recorded on the review.

The completion factor selects the compliance `tier`: 0.9500 or better is `COMPLIANT`, from 0.9000 up to but not including 0.9500 is `WATCH`, and anything below 0.9000 is `DEFICIENT`.

Alongside the completion factor the review computes a service-quality score. `seat_fulfillment` is the summed seats offered divided by the agreement's `min_monthly_seats` multiplied by the number of months in the window, capped at one and rounded half-up to four decimal places, so a carrier that met its monthly seat floor across the window scores 1.0000 and cannot score above it. `load_factor` is the summed passengers enplaned divided by the summed seats offered, rounded half-up to four decimal places. The load figure is then normalised against the program's target load factor of 0.375 — the load factor divided by that target, capped at one — and this normalised value is deliberately *not* pre-rounded. `quality_score` is one hundred multiplied by the sum of the completion factor weighted 0.60, the seat fulfilment weighted 0.30, and the normalised load weighted 0.10, with the whole expression rounded half-up to two decimal places exactly once at the end.

Every review refreshes the carrier's headline figures: `latest_completion_factor`, `latest_quality_score` and `latest_performance_tier` take the values of the review with the greatest `created_at` across all of that carrier's agreements. `consecutive_watch_count` counts how many of the carrier's most recent reviews in a row, walking backwards from the newest, carried a `WATCH` tier; a `COMPLIANT` review resets it to 0. The review also stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, and like every determination it is an append-only record with no `version`.

Enforcement is decided after the tier is known and is recorded on the review as `action_taken`. A `COMPLIANT` review, or a `WATCH` review whose resulting `consecutive_watch_count` is below 2, takes no action and records `NONE`. A `WATCH` review whose resulting `consecutive_watch_count` is 2 or more, on a carrier whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, moves the carrier `ACTIVE → PROBATION` and records `CARRIER_PLACED_ON_PROBATION` — two consecutive marginal reviews are treated as a pattern. A `DEFICIENT` review is the severe branch and records `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED_AND_HOLD_IN`: a carrier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` moves to `SUSPENDED`, the reviewed agreement moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, the served community moves `SUBSIDIZED → HOLD_IN` with its `active_agreement_count` decreased by 1, and the program immediately opens a replacement solicitation for that community with `created_reason` `REPLACEMENT`, `status` `OPEN`, `round_number` 1 and a `closes_at` 60 days after the current logical clock reading. One request, four registers touched plus one new solicitation.

A `ProgramAdmin` may also move a carrier's standing by hand, supplying an `action` and an optional `note`. Placing a carrier on probation moves it `ACTIVE → PROBATION` and requires the carrier to be `ACTIVE`. Suspending moves it to `SUSPENDED` from either `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, and every agreement of that carrier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with it. Debarring moves it to `DEBARRED` from `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, and withdraws the carrier from every open competition by moving each of its proposals whose `submission_state` is `DRAFT` to `WITHDRAWN`; `DEBARRED` is terminal, so any standing action against an already-debarred carrier is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Reinstating moves a carrier `PROBATION → ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and resets `consecutive_watch_count` to 0; reinstating a carrier that is already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Reinstatement of the carrier does **not** revive the agreements that were suspended alongside it — each of those must be reinstated individually.

Agreement standing is driven separately, again by a `ProgramAdmin` supplying an `action` and an optional `note`. Suspending an agreement moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. Reinstating one moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` but only while the obligated carrier's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; a suspended or debarred carrier blocks the reinstatement with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. When the served community is in `HOLD_IN` and the reinstated agreement is its only non-terminal agreement, the community moves `HOLD_IN → SUBSIDIZED` on the same request. An action attempted against an agreement whose status does not admit it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; terminating an agreement is covered in §3.9.

### 3.9. Termination Petitions, the Hold-In Requirement and Agreement Release

A community that no longer wants its incumbent carrier petitions the program rather than simply walking away. The `CommunityRepresentative` files the petition naming its own `community_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID` — and the `agreement_id` it asks to end, whose own community must be that same community. The filing states a `reason_code` of `SERVICE_QUALITY`, `COMMUNITY_PREFERENCE_CHANGE`, `CARRIER_NONPERFORMANCE` or `AIRPORT_CLOSURE`, a supporting `narrative` of between 1 and 2000 characters, and the `requested_effective_at` instant at which the community wishes service to end. A new petition opens with `status` `FILED`, `return_count` 0, no `decision_note` and no `decided_by_user_id`, and stamps `filed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, which equals the petitioning community's `id`.

Decisions belong to the `SelectionOfficer`, who supplies an `action` and an optional `decision_note`. **Opening review** moves the petition `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW`. **Returning for information** moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → FILED`, increases `return_count` by 1 and records the note — the cycle `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW → FILED` may repeat as often as the officer needs. The three terminal-ish decisions all require the petition to be `UNDER_REVIEW`: **denying** it moves it to `DENIED`, while the two grant branches also require the target agreement's `status` to be `ACTIVE`. Any decision attempted from a status the action does not admit — opening review from something other than `FILED`, or any other decision from something other than `UNDER_REVIEW` — is refused with `ERR_PETITION_NOT_DECIDABLE`. Every terminal decision records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`. `GRANTED_IMMEDIATE`, `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `CLOSED` are terminal petition states.

The community may also **withdraw** its own petition, supplying an optional `withdraw_note`. Withdrawal is admissible from `FILED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the petition's `community_id`, and moves the petition to `WITHDRAWN` while touching neither the agreement nor the community. From any other status the refusal is again `ERR_PETITION_NOT_DECIDABLE`.

**Granting with hold-in** is the ordinary outcome and the mechanism that guarantees continuity of service. The petition moves `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED_WITH_HOLD_IN` and the agreement moves `ACTIVE → HOLD_IN`, which means the carrier must keep flying — at the hold-in premium rate of §3.7 — until somebody else takes over. In the same request the community moves `SUBSIDIZED → HOLD_IN` with its `active_agreement_count` decreased by 1, and the program opens a replacement solicitation for that community with `created_reason` `REPLACEMENT`, `status` `OPEN`, `round_number` 1 and a `closes_at` 60 days after the current logical clock reading.

**Granting immediately** is the exception and is fenced by the continuity guard. It is admissible only when the petition's `reason_code` is `AIRPORT_CLOSURE` — there is nothing left to serve — or when the community holds another agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, so service continues regardless. Otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_HOLD_IN_REQUIRED`: the community may not be left with no air service. When it is admissible, the petition moves `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED_IMMEDIATE`, the agreement moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the community's `active_agreement_count` decreases by 1. If that leaves the community with no agreement in `ACTIVE`, `HOLD_IN` or `SUSPENDED` status, the community's own status follows its `latest_determination_outcome`: an outcome of `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_WAIVER` moves it `SUBSIDIZED → ELIGIBLE`, so it keeps its eligibility and may be solicited again, while an outcome of `INELIGIBLE_DISTANCE` or `INELIGIBLE_ENPLANEMENTS` moves it `SUBSIDIZED → TERMINATED`, which is terminal for a community.

A `ProgramAdmin` may also terminate an agreement directly. Direct termination is admissible from `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and only while no month of that agreement is still `REPORTED` or `CERTIFIED` — unsettled money must be resolved or reversed first, and a termination attempted over an unsettled month is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Termination stamps `terminated_at` from the logical clock and releases the unspent obligation: `total_committed_usd` is reset to `disbursed_to_date_usd` rounded to cents and `obligated_remaining_usd` is set to 0.00, so the conservation identity still holds with nothing left committed. The community cascade is the same as for an immediate grant — `active_agreement_count` decreases by 1 and, once the community has no agreement left in `ACTIVE`, `HOLD_IN` or `SUSPENDED` status, it moves `SUBSIDIZED → ELIGIBLE` or `SUBSIDIZED → TERMINATED` according to its `latest_determination_outcome`.

An agreement in `HOLD_IN` is the one case direct termination cannot reach. A hold-in agreement may only be released once a successor agreement whose `predecessor_agreement_id` equals it has actually reached `ACTIVE` status; until then the attempt is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`. This is the statutory hold-in requirement, and the release therefore happens as a cascade of the replacement award rather than as an administrative act: when the successor is booked, the hold-in agreement moves `HOLD_IN → TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` stamped, its `total_committed_usd` reset to `disbursed_to_date_usd` and its `obligated_remaining_usd` set to 0.00, its petition moves `GRANTED_WITH_HOLD_IN → CLOSED`, and the community closes the loop `SUBSIDIZED → HOLD_IN → SUBSIDIZED`.

### 3.10. Rate Renegotiation on Fuel and Cost Grounds

A multi-year agreement priced at yesterday's fuel is not viable, so a `Carrier` may ask the program to reopen the annual rate. The request names the `agreement_id`, a `trigger_type` of either `FUEL_INDEX` or `COST_INCREASE`, the `requested_annual_subsidy_usd` the carrier asks for — strictly positive, carried to two decimal places — and a `justification` of between 1 and 2000 characters. Exactly one trigger-specific field accompanies it: a `FUEL_INDEX` request must carry `fuel_index_month` and must omit `documented_cost_increase_pct`, while a `COST_INCREASE` request must carry `documented_cost_increase_pct` — a non-negative fraction of the current annual rate carried to four decimal places — and must omit `fuel_index_month`. Supplying the wrong field, omitting the required one, or naming a month for which no fuel index has been published is refused with `ERR_MISSING_FUEL_INDEX`.

Three gates precede the arithmetic. An agreement may carry only one live negotiation: if it already has a renegotiation whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `COUNTERED`, the new filing is refused with `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_ALREADY_OPEN`. A rate may not be reopened during its cooling-off period: the logical clock must be at or after the agreement's `rate_effective_from` advanced by 180 days, with a filing exactly at that boundary accepted and anything earlier refused with `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_COOLING_OFF`. And the ask must be facially sane: a `requested_annual_subsidy_usd` greater than 1.25 times the agreement's `annual_subsidy_usd` currently in force — that product rounded half-up to cents — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The program then computes what it is prepared to pay, working from the rate currently in force on the agreement. On **fuel** grounds it records `current_fuel_index_usd` as the `price_per_gallon_usd` of the named month's index, derives the agreement's annual fuel consumption as its `annual_block_hours` multiplied by the `fuel_burn_gal_per_hour` of the aircraft type on the agreement's source proposal, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and takes the per-gallon movement as `current_fuel_index_usd` less the agreement's `baseline_fuel_index_usd`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. `fuel_cost_delta_usd` is that per-gallon movement multiplied by the annual gallons, rounded half-up to cents, and `justified_delta_usd` is 80 percent of that annual fuel cost change, again rounded half-up to cents — the program passes through four fifths of a fuel swing, not all of it. Note that a falling index yields a negative movement and therefore a negative justified delta, which cuts the rate rather than raising it. On **documented cost** grounds `current_fuel_index_usd` and `fuel_cost_delta_usd` are both left empty, and `justified_delta_usd` is the rate in force multiplied by the documented increase — floored at zero so a negative submission cannot cut the rate, and capped at 0.10 so no more than a ten percent non-fuel increase is ever justified — rounded half-up to cents.

The justified delta is then squeezed between a cap and a floor, in that order. The cap is 15 percent of the rate in force, rounded half-up to cents: the delta is replaced by whichever of the two is smaller. The floor is 5 percent of the rate in force taken negatively, also rounded half-up to cents: the result is replaced by whichever of the two is larger, so no renegotiation can cut a rate by more than five percent. `ceiling_annual_subsidy_usd` is the rate in force plus that capped-and-floored delta, rounded half-up to cents, and it is the highest annual rate the program will approve on this request. The renegotiation opens with `status` `REQUESTED`, a `revision_count` of 0, no `countered_annual_subsidy_usd`, no `approved_annual_subsidy_usd` and no `effective_at`.

Five actions drive the negotiation, all through one action field with an optional `action_note`, and the role split of §2.1 applies throughout. A `ProgramAdmin` **counters** a `REQUESTED` renegotiation, which moves it `REQUESTED → COUNTERED` and writes `countered_annual_subsidy_usd` equal to the computed `ceiling_annual_subsidy_usd` — the program's formal statement of its own ceiling. The `Carrier` **revises** a `COUNTERED` renegotiation, which moves it `COUNTERED → REQUESTED`; the revision must carry `revised_annual_subsidy_usd`, which replaces `requested_annual_subsidy_usd` and causes `justified_delta_usd` and `ceiling_annual_subsidy_usd` to be recomputed from scratch, and it increases `revision_count` by 1. Only three revisions are permitted: a revision attempted when `revision_count` already stands at 3, or one that omits `revised_annual_subsidy_usd`, is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The negotiation loop `REQUESTED → COUNTERED → REQUESTED` therefore terminates.

A `ProgramAdmin` may **reject** a renegotiation from either `REQUESTED` or `COUNTERED`, moving it to `REJECTED`, and the `Carrier` may **withdraw** it from either of the same two states, moving it to `WITHDRAWN`; both are terminal and neither touches the agreement. An action attempted from a status that does not admit it — countering something not `REQUESTED`, revising something not `COUNTERED`, or accepting, rejecting or withdrawing something that is neither `REQUESTED` nor `COUNTERED` — is refused with `ERR_RENEGOTIATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`.

**Accepting** is the `ProgramAdmin`'s act, is admissible from `REQUESTED` or `COUNTERED`, and additionally requires the agreement's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `HOLD_IN`; against a suspended or terminated agreement it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Acceptance moves the renegotiation to the terminal `ACCEPTED` state — `REQUESTED → ACCEPTED` or `COUNTERED → ACCEPTED` — and writes `approved_annual_subsidy_usd` as the smaller of `ceiling_annual_subsidy_usd` and `requested_annual_subsidy_usd` — the carrier never gets more than it asked for, nor more than the program's ceiling — stamps `effective_at` with the logical clock reading, replaces the agreement's `annual_subsidy_usd` with the approved figure, and advances the agreement's `rate_effective_from` to that same clock reading. The agreement's `rate_effective_to` is never moved by a renegotiation: the term ends when it was always going to end.

Acceptance then re-books the agreement's obligation, and only the unserved remainder of the term is repriced. The program counts the agreement's months whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `PAID` as settled, subtracts that count from the agreement's `term_months` to get the remaining term in months, and takes the delta as the approved annual rate less the annual rate that was in force immediately before acceptance, rounded half-up to cents. That delta is pro-rated over the remaining months — multiplied by the remaining months and divided by 12, rounded half-up to cents *before* it is applied — then added to `total_committed_usd`, which is rounded again to cents. `obligated_remaining_usd` is recomputed as the new committed total less `disbursed_to_date_usd`, rounded to cents. Should that recomputation drive `obligated_remaining_usd` below 0.00 — a rate cut deep enough that the program has already paid out more than the repriced term commits — the acceptance is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_EXCEEDED` and nothing is written.
