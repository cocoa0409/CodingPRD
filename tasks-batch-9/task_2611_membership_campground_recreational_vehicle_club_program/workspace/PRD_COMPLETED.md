# Membership Campground RV Club Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A membership campground network sells long-lived recreational-vehicle club contracts and then has to honour them across a portfolio of physical parks whose capacity, operating seasons and amenity condition all vary independently of the promises printed on the membership certificate. A member who paid a five-figure upfront cost expects a site to be available inside the advance window their tier grants; the transient public, who pay cash nightly, keep the parks solvent in shoulder seasons; and the club's own balance sheet depends on annual dues arriving on time and on capital reinvestment landing at the parks that are actually degrading. These three constituencies compete for the same finite number of pads on the same nights, and today that competition is arbitrated by spreadsheets, phone calls between park offices, and a great deal of goodwill.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. It owns the full contract lifecycle — tier definition, membership sale, annual dues billing, delinquency suspension, transfer and resale, termination — alongside the operational lifecycle of the parks themselves: seasonal opening and closure, maintenance windows that take pads out of service, amenity inspections that band a park's compliance standing, and post-stay satisfaction surveys that feed both the park's public rating and the priority ordering of its capital projects. Between those two lifecycles sits the reservation engine, which must decide, for every requested night, whether a stay draws on the member allocation, borrows from the transient allocation, waits on a list, or is refused outright. It must also police long-term occupancy — the practice of re-booking around a stay-length limit until a member has effectively taken up permanent residence — and it must respect the terms of affiliate agreements with partner campground systems whose parks the club does not own.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **entitlement and quota counters are conserved**: every night debited from a membership's per-class allowance or from a partner's annual quota is credited back exactly when the stay that consumed it is cancelled, force-cancelled, or checked out early, so that a counter never drifts from the set of stays that justify it. Second, **the service is deterministic and clock-driven**: all window arithmetic, delinquency ageing and recency weighting evaluate against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that a seasonal park, a 120-day write-off horizon and a 730-day resale hold can all be exercised inside a single test run.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section is a contract. Where §3 narrates business intent, §2 fixes the shapes, headers, orderings and rounding rules that every endpoint obeys without restating them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request except the logical-clock test hook must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. There are exactly five roles, spelled precisely as follows: `Member`, `ParkManager`, `ClubAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `TransientGuest`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds any string outside that five-value set, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the addressed operation — the list given in §4 — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are backed by persisted entities, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the owning `Membership`'s `member_id` field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Member` acting on a membership, a reservation, a dues invoice, a transfer request or an occupancy exception must be the holder of the membership that owns it, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ParkManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Park`; a park manager may only reach a park directly, or indirectly through a maintenance window, an amenity inspection, a capital project or a reservation whose park is that same park, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_PARK_MANAGER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ClubAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an author — as `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on `CapitalProject`, and as `decided_by_user_id` on `TransferRequest` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; it is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on `OccupancyException` and, when a compliance officer files an inspection, as `inspector_user_id` on `AmenityInspection`, and it is never matched against an entity `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `TransientGuest`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque public-booking identifier not tied to any persisted entity and not compared to any entity's `id`; a transient guest's reach is limited to creating a public booking, cancelling a reservation, and reading a reservation or a park.

Because `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` may hold either a `Park` identifier (when a `ParkManager` proposed the project) or an opaque operator identifier (when a `ClubAdmin` did), the service must treat those columns as free-form strings and compare them only for equality. That equality comparison is the entire mechanism behind the club's segregation-of-duties rule: the `X-User-ID` that decides a capital project must differ from the `X-User-ID` recorded as having created it, and the `X-User-ID` that decides a transfer request must differ from the `X-User-ID` that submitted it. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Both are integers, and a value that does not parse as an integer, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an empty-string value for either parameter is treated exactly as if the parameter had been omitted, falling back to its default.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — and, because the logical clock lets many rows share one `created_at` to the millisecond, ties are broken by the server's own monotonically increasing insertion sequence, so rows created at the same logical instant come back in the order they were created — unless a specific endpoint says otherwise; the only such exception in this service is the per-park amenity-inspection list, which returns its rows newest first, that is, in descending order of `inspected_at`, with ties on that stamp broken by *descending* insertion sequence — the whole ordering is simply the ascending one reversed, so two inspections filed at the same logical instant come back in the reverse of the order they were filed. Alongside the echoed `limit` and `offset`, every list response's `meta` object carries `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Filters supplied on a list endpoint narrow the result set before pagination, never after. The membership list accepts `member_id`, `tier_id`, `transferred_from_membership_id`, `membership_status` and `dues_status`; the tier list accepts `code`; the park list accepts `park_class`, `park_status`, `affiliate_agreement_id` and `amenity_compliance_status`; the reservation list accepts `park_id`, `membership_id`, `status`, `booker_type`, and the inclusive arrival-window bounds `arrival_from` and `arrival_to`; the transfer-request list accepts `membership_id`, `from_member_id`, `to_member_id`, `successor_membership_id` and `status`. Sub-resource lists accept their own narrow filters: a membership's dues invoices may be filtered by `status` and `cycle_year`, a park's maintenance windows by `status`, a park's capital projects by `status` and `fiscal_year`, and a reservation's occupancy exceptions by `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Alongside `id`, most resources carry three audit columns: `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the row was created; `updated_at`, the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two resources are deliberately append-only and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — `AmenityInspection` and `SatisfactionSurvey` have no `updated_at` and no `version` at all, because once filed neither is ever modified.

All date and time arithmetic in this service — advance windows, season boundaries, maintenance intervals, dues ageing, resale holds, survey recency decay — evaluates against a server-held **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back; it mutates no entity. Phrases such as "the current logical date" throughout this document mean the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC, and "the current logical instant" means the full timestamp.

Stamps that record when something happened all read from that clock: `purchased_at` and `tenure_origin_at` on a membership, `checked_in_at` and `checked_out_at` on a reservation, `paid_at` on a dues invoice, `decided_at` on a transfer request, `inspected_at` on an amenity inspection, and `submitted_at` on a satisfaction survey. Fields typed as dates rather than instants — `arrival_date`, `departure_date`, `scheduled_departure_date`, `season_open_date`, `season_close_date`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `due_date`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `entitlement_year_start` and `dues_paid_through` — are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component.

### 2.5. Nested Read Paths and Left-to-Right Resolution

Eight read operations are nested one level deep beneath a parent: a membership's dues invoices and a membership's satisfaction surveys; a park's amenity inspections, satisfaction surveys, maintenance windows and capital projects; and a reservation's satisfaction surveys and occupancy exceptions. Path parameters on these routes are resolved strictly left to right. The parent identifier is validated first, so a request naming a membership that does not exist returns `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, one naming a park that does not exist returns `ERR_PARK_NOT_FOUND`, and one naming a reservation that does not exist returns `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` — in each case before any consideration of the child collection, which is simply empty when the parent exists but has no children. Every mutation in this service, by contrast, targets a flat collection and names its parents through body fields rather than through the URL.

### 2.6. Money, Nights and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are always integers denominated in **whole cents**; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the data model, and any field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. Wherever a monetary formula multiplies by a percentage or a multiplier, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** — a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero, upward. The number of roundings and their position in a formula are fixed per calculation and are stated in §3 with the formula they belong to; implementers must not consolidate two roundings into one or move a rounding earlier, because both changes shift the answer by whole cents.

Night counts and site counts are integers and are never rounded half up. The member share of a park's pads is taken with a **floor**, discarding any fraction. Affiliate quota nights are taken with a **ceiling**, always rounding a fractional night up to the next whole night. Ratings and scores are the only non-integer stored values: `composite_score`, `weighted_score` and `rating_score` are numbers carried to **exactly two decimal places**, rounded half up, and `priority_score` is an integer produced by a single half-up rounding at the very end of its formula.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Membership and Park Writes

Two write operations in this service require the client to prove it read its parent resource at the parent's current version, because neither can be made safe by a state precondition alone.

Submitting a transfer request is the first. The request body must carry `expected_membership_version` — this field is required, not optional — holding the `version` the client last observed on the `Membership` named by `membership_id`. If that value does not equal the membership's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBERSHIP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product reason is that a great deal can legitimately have moved the membership since the client read it: a prior transfer request may have just been decided, a dues payment may have advanced `dues_paid_through`, or a reservation may have shifted the entitlement counters. Any of those makes the client's picture of the contract obsolete, so the writer must prove it is working from the current one.

Deciding a capital project is the second. That request body must carry `expected_park_version` — again required, not optional — holding the `version` the approver last observed on the project's `Park`. A mismatch with the park's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A funding decision is only meaningful against the park state that produced the project's `priority_score`, and an intervening amenity inspection, satisfaction survey, maintenance cascade or sibling funding decision changes that score; rejecting the stale decision forces the approver to re-read and re-judge.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. In particular, booking, cancelling, checking in, checking out, extending, paying dues, reversing a payment, suspending, reinstating, deciding a transfer, deciding an occupancy exception, changing an affiliate agreement's status and every operations run take no `expected_*_version` input at all.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

When more than one rule could reject a request, the rules are evaluated in a single fixed order and the **first** failing rule determines the response. The ordering matters because a later, more specific rejection must never mask an earlier, more general one; a request that is both unauthenticated and malformed is an authentication failure, and a booking that both exceeds its advance window and exhausts its entitlement reports the window. The complete ladder, in evaluation order, is:

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every operation except the logical-clock test hook. That hook alone accepts a request carrying neither header, and when it is handed an `X-User-Role` outside the five-value set it answers `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) at rung 2 rather than `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, on every operation.
3. Member ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on member booking, reservation cancellation, check-in, check-out, extension, survey submission, dues payment, transfer submission, and the reads of one membership, one membership's dues invoices, and one reservation. This rung bites only when the acting role is `Member` **and** the addressed resource actually resolves to a `Membership`: a resource that names no membership — a public `TRANSIENT` booking, or a path identifier that matches nothing — passes the rung untested and falls through to the existence rung below, so a `Member` may cancel or read a membership-less public booking and a `TransientGuest`, whose `X-User-ID` is never compared to anything, is never owner-checked at all.
4. Park-manager ownership — `ERR_NOT_PARK_MANAGER`, on season opening, season closure, amenity inspection filing, maintenance-window scheduling, maintenance activation, maintenance completion, and capital-project proposal.
5. Membership existence for every membership id in a path or body — `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one membership, listing a membership's dues invoices, suspension, reinstatement, member booking, and transfer submission.
6. Tier existence for `tier_id` — `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one tier and on selling a membership.
7. Member existence for `member_id` or `to_member_id` — `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, on selling a membership and on transfer submission.
8. Park existence for every park id in a path or body — `ERR_PARK_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one park, on all four park sub-resource lists (amenity inspections, satisfaction surveys, maintenance windows, capital projects), on season opening, season closure, amenity inspection filing, member booking, transient booking, maintenance-window scheduling, and capital-project proposal.
9. Reservation existence — `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one reservation, listing a reservation's satisfaction surveys, listing a reservation's occupancy exceptions, cancellation, check-in, check-out, extension, and survey submission.
10. Dues invoice existence — `ERR_DUES_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, on dues payment and on payment reversal.
11. Transfer request existence — `ERR_TRANSFER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one transfer request and on a transfer decision.
12. Maintenance window existence — `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, on maintenance activation and maintenance completion.
13. Capital project existence — `ERR_CAPITAL_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, on a capital-project decision.
14. Occupancy exception existence — `ERR_OCCUPANCY_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, on an occupancy-exception decision.
15. Affiliate agreement existence for `affiliate_agreement_id` — `ERR_AFFILIATE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, on reading one agreement, on park creation, and on an agreement status change.
16. Body well-formedness — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), on every creating or mutating operation. All required body fields must be present, every enum-typed field must hold one of its declared values, every integer amount must be at least 0, every date must be a valid ISO 8601 calendar date, and a departure date must be strictly greater than its arrival date.
17. Membership version pinning — `ERR_STALE_MEMBERSHIP_VERSION`, on transfer submission.
18. Park version pinning — `ERR_STALE_PARK_VERSION`, on a capital-project decision.
19. Reservation state reachability — `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE` (HTTP 409), on cancellation, check-in, check-out, extension and survey submission. Cancellation is reachable from `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` and `WAITLISTED`; check-in only from `CONFIRMED`; check-out only from `CHECKED_IN`; extension only from `CHECKED_IN`; survey submission only from `COMPLETED`.
20. Park state reachability — `ERR_INVALID_PARK_STATE` (HTTP 409), on season opening, season closure, amenity inspection filing, check-in, maintenance activation and maintenance completion. Season opening is reachable only from `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, season closure only from `OPEN`, an inspection may be filed only while the park is `OPEN` or `MAINTENANCE_HOLD`, a check-in requires the park to be `OPEN`, an activation requires it to be `OPEN` and a completion requires it to be `MAINTENANCE_HOLD` — on the two maintenance operations this rung is evaluated *after* rung 21, since the window's own state is judged first. A season opening additionally requires the current logical date to lie on or after `season_open_date` minus the season-opening lead allowance and on or before `season_close_date`.
21. Maintenance window state — `ERR_INVALID_MAINTENANCE_STATE` (HTTP 409): a window must be `SCHEDULED` to be activated and `ACTIVE` to be completed.
22. Transfer request state — `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE` (HTTP 409): a transfer decision requires the request to be `SUBMITTED`.
23. Occupancy exception state — `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409): an exception decision requires the exception to be `REQUESTED`.
24. Dues invoice state — `ERR_DUES_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE` (HTTP 409): a payment requires the invoice to be `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE`, and a reversal requires it to be `PAID`.
25. Membership contract state — `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), on member booking, extension, transfer submission, check-in and direct suspension: the acting membership's `membership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so `SUSPENDED`, `TRANSFER_PENDING` and `TERMINATED` memberships can neither book, extend, transfer, nor be suspended again.
26. Affiliate agreement activity — `ERR_AFFILIATE_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE` (HTTP 409), on member booking and transient booking: when the target park carries a non-null `affiliate_agreement_id`, that agreement's `status` must be `ACTIVE`.
27. Agreement transition reachability — `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409), on an agreement status change: the requested `new_status` must be reachable from the agreement's current `status`, the permitted moves being `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, and `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`.
28. Park open for business — `ERR_PARK_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 422), on member booking and transient booking: the target park's `park_status` must be `OPEN`, so bookings are refused at `SEASONAL_CLOSED` and `MAINTENANCE_HOLD` parks.
29. Maintenance conflict — `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), on member booking, transient booking and extension: no night of the requested or extended stay may fall inside the closed inclusive interval running from `start_date` through `end_date` of any `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` maintenance window at that park.
30. Absolute occupancy cap — `ERR_ABSOLUTE_OCCUPANCY_CAP` (HTTP 422), on member booking, extension and an occupancy-exception decision: the merged occupancy run containing the stay must not exceed the network's absolute run ceiling, and this cap binds even when a `GRANTED` occupancy exception exists.
31. Advance window — `ERR_ADVANCE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on member booking.
32. Season containment — `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON` (HTTP 422), on member booking, transient booking and extension.
33. Stay length — `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on member booking and extension.
34. Out-and-back gap — `ERR_OUT_AND_BACK_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422), on member booking.
35. Self-overlap — `ERR_OVERLAPPING_STAY` (HTTP 409), on member booking and extension.
36. Affiliate tier eligibility — `ERR_AFFILIATE_TIER_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), on member booking.
37. Resale permission — `ERR_RESALE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422), on transfer submission.
38. Resale hold — `ERR_RESALE_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422), on transfer submission.
39. Transfer chain limit — `ERR_TRANSFER_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422), on transfer submission.
40. Open reservations block a transfer — `ERR_TRANSFER_BLOCKED_OPEN_RESERVATIONS` (HTTP 409), on transfer submission: the membership must hold no reservation in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `WAITLISTED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `EXTENSION_REVIEW`.
41. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), on a capital-project decision and a transfer decision.
42. Dues standing — `ERR_DUES_NOT_CURRENT` (HTTP 409), on member booking, extension, transfer submission and a transfer decision.
43. Grace-period class restriction — `ERR_CLASS_A_RESTRICTED_IN_GRACE` (HTTP 422), on member booking and extension.
44. Entitlement headroom — `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NIGHTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 422), on member booking, extension and an occupancy-exception decision.
45. Affiliate quota headroom — `ERR_AFFILIATE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 422), on member booking and extension.
46. Transient capacity — `ERR_NO_TRANSIENT_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409), on transient booking.
47. Payment amount exactness — `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), on dues payment.
48. Reinstatement preconditions — `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), on reinstatement.

Note the consequence of rungs 41 and 42 sitting where they do: on a transfer decision the segregation-of-duties check is evaluated **before** the dues-standing check, so a self-approval by the same operator who submitted the request reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the membership's dues have also fallen out of `CURRENT`. Note likewise that on a booking, all of the temporal and length rules (rungs 31 through 36) are evaluated before the fiscal rules (rungs 42 through 45), so a stay that is both too far in advance and beyond its entitlement reports the advance window. Capacity allocation is not a validation rung at all for member bookings: it runs only after every rung above has passed, and its outcome selects between `CONFIRMED` and `WAITLISTED` rather than producing an error.

### 2.9. Counter Conservation and Cascade Integrity

Three families of counters in this system are derived aggregates that must remain exactly consistent with the rows that justify them, and every operation that changes a row must complete the corresponding counter movement inside the same request. The first family is entitlement: a membership's `nights_used_class_a`, `nights_used_class_b`, `nights_used_class_c` and their sum `annual_nights_used`. The second is affiliate quota: an agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd` and the `settlement_owed_cents` derived from it. The third is capacity occupancy, expressed on each reservation as `pool_charged`, which names the pool a stay currently holds a seat in.

Whenever a reservation that had debited nights leaves the set of stays that occupy capacity — by member-requested cancellation, by the suspension cascade, by maintenance force-cancellation, by season-closure force-cancellation, or by checking out earlier than planned — the nights it debited are credited back. Concretely, the value in `entitlement_nights_charged` is subtracted from the membership's class bucket and from `annual_nights_used` and the field is then reset, and the value in `affiliate_nights_charged` is subtracted from the agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd`, after which `settlement_owed_cents` is recomputed from the reduced total. Each such compensating movement leaves the counters at exactly the value they would have held had the reservation never existed. Every entitlement or quota movement on a membership also increments that `Membership`'s `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`; every mutation of a park row, including a compliance re-derivation or a funded-capital update, increments that `Park`'s `version` by 1; and every movement of an agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd`, debit or credit alike, likewise increments that `AffiliateAgreement`'s `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`. A movement of exactly 0 nights is not a movement at all — the counter is left alone and no `version` moves on its account — and every credit is floored at 0, so no counter is ever driven negative even if a compensating movement is asked for more nights than the counter holds.

Cascades run to completion within the triggering request; there is no deferred or eventually-consistent settlement anywhere in this service. When a membership is suspended, the cascade reaches its reservations, their entitlement and quota debits, the freed pool seats, the waitlist at each affected park, and any open transfer request — all before the response is written. When a maintenance window is activated, the cascade reaches the window, the park's operating status, the intersecting reservations, their debits, the park's forced-cancellation tally, the park's compliance banding, and the priority scores of the park's live capital projects. Nested cascade links are enumerated with the workflows that trigger them in §3.

### 2.10. Half-Open Stays and Closed Maintenance Intervals

Two interval conventions coexist in this service and must not be confused. A reservation's occupied night set is **half-open**: it contains every date from `arrival_date` up to but not including `departure_date`. The departure day is never an occupied night, which is why a stay departing on a given date and another stay arriving on that same date do not collide and do not compete for the same pad. The count in `nights` is therefore `departure_date` minus `arrival_date` measured in whole days.

A maintenance window, by contrast, is a **closed** interval: both `start_date` and `end_date` are blocked nights, inclusive. A stay conflicts with a window when at least one date in the stay's half-open night set also lies within the window's closed interval. A park's operating season is likewise closed on both ends: `season_open_date` is the first operable date and `season_close_date` is the last, inclusive, and `season_close_date` must be strictly after `season_open_date`. An affiliate agreement's `effective_from` and `effective_to` are recorded as an interval of that same inclusive shape and describe the term the club and the partner have agreed, but they are **not** enforced when a stay is booked or extended: the agreement's `status` alone gates partner access, so a stay whose nights fall wholly outside the agreement's validity dates is still accepted so long as the agreement is `ACTIVE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing Tiers and Selling Memberships

Everything a member is entitled to is written down once, on a **membership tier**, and then inherited by every contract sold on that tier. A `ClubAdmin` defines a tier by supplying a short `code` — between 2 and 24 characters, conventionally uppercase and conventionally unique across all tiers, though the server enforces only the length bounds and will accept a duplicate or a lower-case code without complaint — together with a marketing `name` and a `tier_rank` between 1 and 5, where a higher rank denotes a more privileged tier and is the value partner networks compare against when deciding whether the club's members may enter. The commercial terms follow: `upfront_cost_cents`, the one-time purchase price, and `annual_dues_cents`, the base annual dues before any capital assessment or loyalty credit, both of which must be at least 0. Usage entitlements are declared per park class as `entitlement_nights_class_a`, `entitlement_nights_class_b` and `entitlement_nights_class_c`, each at least 0, and are capped in aggregate by `annual_total_night_cap`, which must be at least 1 — a tier may therefore grant 40 nights of one class and 40 of another while capping the year at 60, so the two per-class allowances are individually available but not simultaneously exhaustible.

The reservation rules a tier carries are four numbers and two flags. `advance_window_days`, between 1 and 365, is the furthest ahead of the current logical date a member on this tier may set an arrival. `max_consecutive_stay_nights`, between 1 and 45, is the longest merged occupancy run permitted at a single park without a granted long-term-occupancy exception. `min_out_nights_between_stays`, between 0 and 30, is the out-and-back requirement — the number of nights the member must spend off the network between one stay and the next. `affiliate_access_enabled` decides whether contracts on this tier may book partner-network parks at all, and `resale_allowed` decides whether such a contract may change hands for money. Finally `transfer_fee_cents`, at least 0, is the base fee before the transfer-type multiplier and the resale surcharge are applied.

Separately from contracts, the club registers the natural persons who may hold them. A `Member` record captures `full_name`, an `email` that is conventionally unique across all member records but whose uniqueness the server does not enforce, and an optional `home_region` that is free text used only for reporting and never for any rule. A member record carries a `status` that the server manages, defaulting to `ACTIVE` and reaching `CLOSED` when the person is retired from the register; the only two permitted values are `ACTIVE` and `CLOSED`.

Selling a membership joins the two: a `ClubAdmin` supplies the `member_id` of the receiving person, the `tier_id` being purchased, and `upfront_paid_cents`, the amount actually collected at the counter. The `tier_id` is pinned to the contract at purchase and is carried unchanged through every subsequent transfer, so a tier's terms may be edited for future sales without silently re-writing an existing member's rights. Upon creation the contract opens on both of its state axes at once — `membership_status` is `ACTIVE` and `dues_status` is `CURRENT` — with `suspension_reason` null and `transferred_from_membership_id` null, marking it an original purchase rather than a successor. Both `purchased_at` and `tenure_origin_at` are stamped with the current logical instant; `entitlement_year_start` is set to the current logical date; `nights_used_class_a`, `nights_used_class_b`, `nights_used_class_c` and `annual_nights_used` all start at 0; `transfer_count` starts at 0; `tenure_years` starts at 0; and `resale_eligible_at` is set to `purchased_at` advanced by the resale hold of 730 days, which is two full years.

Tenure is what makes a long-standing member cheaper to keep. `tenure_years` is recomputed as the number of whole 365-day periods elapsed between the calendar date of `tenure_origin_at` and the current logical date — that is, the elapsed days divided by 365 with any fraction discarded. It is re-derived from the logical clock every time the membership is serialised, so advancing the clock and then merely reading the membership already reports the grown figure without refreshing `updated_at` or moving `version`. Because `tenure_origin_at` records the *original* purchase in a transfer chain and is copied verbatim onto every successor contract, tenure survives a gift or a sale intact even though the successor's own `purchased_at` is the date of the transfer.

Selling a membership also issues the first annual dues invoice immediately, for the current `cycle_year`, in status `OPEN`, with `due_date` set to the current logical date plus the dues net term of 30 days. The invoice amount is produced by a five-step waterfall whose **evaluation order is fixed**, because the loyalty band keys off an intermediate figure rather than off the base dues. Step one captures `base_dues_cents` as the tier's `annual_dues_cents`, frozen onto the invoice. Step two computes `capital_assessment_cents` as the base dues multiplied by the capital assessment rate of 8 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three forms a subtotal by adding the capital assessment to the base dues. Step four selects a loyalty percentage from the **subtotal**, not from the base dues: it is 10 percent when the subtotal is at least 60,000 cents (six hundred dollars) *and* the membership's `tenure_years` is at least 5; 5 percent when the subtotal is at least 60,000 cents but tenure is below 5 years; 3 percent when tenure is at least 5 years but the subtotal is below 60,000 cents; and 0 percent otherwise. `loyalty_credit_cents` is then that percentage of the subtotal, rounded half up to whole cents. Step five sets `amount_cents` to the subtotal minus the loyalty credit. Exactly two roundings occur, one at step two and one at step four; the subtraction at step five is integer arithmetic. Reading the band off the base dues instead of the subtotal is the classic error — a base of 58,000 cents sits below the band while its subtotal of 62,640 cents sits above it.

The invoice opens with `late_fee_cents` at 0, `paid_amount_cents` at 0, `paid_at` null and `days_past_due` at 0. The membership's `dues_balance_cents` is then recomputed as the sum, over that membership's invoices whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE`, of the invoice's `amount_cents` plus its `late_fee_cents` less its `paid_amount_cents`; invoices in `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` contribute nothing. `dues_paid_through` records the date through which dues are settled, starts null on a newly sold contract, and is advanced by a full payment and rolled back by a payment reversal, as described in §3.7.

### 3.2. The Park Network: Capacity Split, Seasons and Partner Agreements

A park enters the network with a `name`, a `park_class` drawn from `CLASS_A`, `CLASS_B` and `CLASS_C`, a `total_sites` count between 1 and 500 describing the physical RV pads, a `member_allocation_pct` between 0 and 100, and its operating-season bounds `season_open_date` and `season_close_date`. The class is a property of the park and never of the tier, and it alone decides which of a membership's three per-class buckets a stay at that park debits: a stay at a `CLASS_A` park draws down `nights_used_class_a`, a `CLASS_B` park draws down `nights_used_class_b`, and a `CLASS_C` park draws down `nights_used_class_c`.

The allocation percentage is turned into two hard site counts at creation. `member_pool_sites` is the total number of pads multiplied by the allocation percentage, divided by 100, with any fraction **discarded** — a floor, never a half-up rounding, so 47 pads at 55 percent yields 25 member sites and not 26. `transient_pool_sites` is then `total_sites` less `member_pool_sites`, so the two pools always sum exactly to the physical pad count. Optionally the creating `ClubAdmin` supplies an `affiliate_agreement_id`; when present the park belongs to a partner campground system and its computed `network_type` reads `AFFILIATE`, and when null the park is club-owned and `network_type` reads `CLUB_OWNED`.

A new park is not open for business. Its `park_status` starts at `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, its `amenity_compliance_status` at `COMPLIANT`, its `rating_score` at 0.00, its `forced_cancellation_count` at 0, its `funded_capital_cents_ytd` at 0, and its `last_inspection_at` null because no inspection has yet been filed. Opening the park for its season advances `park_status` from `SEASONAL_CLOSED` to `OPEN`. A `ParkManager` (or a `ClubAdmin`) may do this only within the season-opening lead allowance of 30 days: the current logical date must be on or after `season_open_date` minus 30 days, and on or before `season_close_date`. The park must additionally have no maintenance window in status `ACTIVE`. An attempt from any status other than `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, one outside that date band, or one blocked by an `ACTIVE` window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PARK_STATE` — all three faults share the one identifier, and they are tested in that order: status first, then the date band, then the window scan. A successful opening increments the park's `version` by 1.

Closing the season is the reverse move, `OPEN` to `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, and it is destructive because stays booked into the closed period can no longer be honoured. The closure is refused outright while any reservation at that park is `CHECKED_IN` or `EXTENSION_REVIEW` — the club will not evict a rig that is physically on site. Otherwise, every reservation at that park in status `CONFIRMED` whose `arrival_date` is on or after the current logical date moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `SEASON_CLOSURE`, and every reservation at that park in status `WAITLISTED` moves to `EXPIRED` — regardless of its arrival date, and carrying `cancellation_reason` set to `SEASON_CLOSURE` just as the cancelled stays do, since a waitlisted hope is defeated by the closure quite as thoroughly as a confirmed booking. Each force-cancelled stay credits its `entitlement_nights_charged` back to the owning membership's class bucket and to `annual_nights_used`, credits its `affiliate_nights_charged` back to the park's agreement, and releases its pool seat. The park's `forced_cancellation_count` then increases by the number of `CONFIRMED` reservations that were cancelled, the park's `amenity_compliance_status` is re-derived under the probation floor described in §3.9, `priority_score` is recomputed for every capital project at the park, and the park's `version` increments by 1.

Partner access is governed by an **affiliate agreement**, registered by a `ClubAdmin` with a `partner_name`, which is a filing convention rather than a constraint — it is stored exactly as supplied and is **not** enforced to be unique, so two agreements may legitimately carry the same partner name — and the inclusive validity dates `effective_from` and `effective_to`, which must satisfy `effective_to` no earlier than `effective_from` on pain of a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, an equal pair being accepted as a one-day agreement, and seven commercial terms. `annual_member_night_quota`, at least 1, is the number of quota nights the club may consume per calendar year under the agreement. `reciprocity_ratio`, between 0.5 and 3.0, converts occupied nights into quota nights. `min_tier_rank`, between 1 and 5, is the lowest `tier_rank` the partner will admit. `advance_window_cap_days`, between 1 and 365, caps how far ahead a member may book at the partner's parks regardless of what their own tier allows. `per_stay_night_cap`, between 1 and 45 — the network's own absolute run ceiling serving as the upper bound here, not any shorter figure — caps the nights in any single stay at one of those parks. `settlement_rate_cents_per_night`, at least 0, is what the club owes the partner per quota night consumed. A newly registered agreement opens in status `DRAFT` with `nights_consumed_ytd` at 0 and `settlement_owed_cents` at 0.

Agreements move along a four-state axis under `ClubAdmin` control, the target being supplied as `new_status`. From `DRAFT` an agreement may become `ACTIVE`, at which point member bookings at its parks become permitted. An `ACTIVE` agreement may be moved to `SUSPENDED`, after which new bookings at its parks are refused with `ERR_AFFILIATE_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE` while reservations already `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` are left entirely untouched. A `SUSPENDED` agreement may be returned to `ACTIVE`, at which point bookings resume and `nights_consumed_ytd` is exactly what it was before the suspension — the suspend-and-resume cycle consumes and releases nothing. An `ACTIVE` agreement may be moved to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal: no further bookings are accepted at its parks, and `nights_consumed_ytd` together with `settlement_owed_cents` freeze at their current values. Any other requested move — including `DRAFT` directly to `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED` to `EXPIRED`, or any move out of `EXPIRED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

Quota consumption itself is a ceiling operation. When a member stay at a park under an agreement takes effect, the quota nights it charges are the stay's `nights` multiplied by the agreement's `reciprocity_ratio`, **rounded up** to the next whole night — never rounded half up. That figure is written to the reservation's `affiliate_nights_charged` and added to the agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd`, after which `settlement_owed_cents` is recomputed as `nights_consumed_ytd` multiplied by `settlement_rate_cents_per_night`. The `nights_consumed_ytd` figure is the sum of `affiliate_nights_charged` across reservations at that agreement's parks whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `EXTENSION_REVIEW` or `COMPLETED` and whose `arrival_date` falls in the current calendar year. Before any such debit is written, the club checks headroom: the agreement's current `nights_consumed_ytd` plus the quota nights this stay would consume must be no greater than `annual_member_night_quota`. Landing exactly on the quota is permitted; exceeding it by even one night is refused with `ERR_AFFILIATE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`.

### 3.3. Booking a Member Stay: From `PENDING` to `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`

A member books a stay by naming the `park_id`, the `membership_id` the stay is charged to, an `arrival_date` and a `scheduled_departure_date`. A reservation always begins life at `PENDING`, but that state is never observable through the API on a successful booking: the fork to `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` is resolved inside the same request. `booker_type` is set to `MEMBER` because `membership_id` is present, `guest_name` stays null, `departure_date` is initialised to `scheduled_departure_date`, and `nights` is computed from the half-open night set. The `scheduled_departure_date` recorded at booking is never mutated thereafter, which is what allows a later check-out to compare what was promised against what was actually occupied. Every counter on a freshly created reservation starts empty: `pool_charged` is `NONE`, `entitlement_nights_charged`, `affiliate_nights_charged`, `total_charge_cents`, `extension_count`, `extension_denied_count` and `consecutive_run_nights` are all 0, `cancellation_reason` is null, and `checked_in_at` and `checked_out_at` are both null.

Before any capacity question is asked, the booking must clear the club's reservation rules. The park must be `OPEN`, refused otherwise with `ERR_PARK_NOT_OPEN`; if the park belongs to a partner network, that agreement's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_AFFILIATE_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE`; and the booking membership's `membership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. No requested night may fall inside the closed inclusive interval of any `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` maintenance window at that park, or the booking is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — note that a merely *scheduled* window already blocks bookings, well before it is activated, which is how the club protects work it has already planned.

**How far ahead a member may book.** The days until arrival — the `arrival_date` minus the current logical date, in whole days — must be no greater than the tier's `advance_window_days`. At a partner-network park the effective window is the **smaller** of the tier's `advance_window_days` and the agreement's `advance_window_cap_days`, so a tier granting a full year of lead time is trimmed to whatever the partner will accept. Exceeding the effective window is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`. Every night of the stay must also lie inside the park's inclusive operating season, from `season_open_date` through `season_close_date`; a stay that begins in season and runs past `season_close_date` is refused with `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON`.

**Long-term occupancy prevention.** The club's stay-length limit is not measured on a single reservation; it is measured on the **merged occupancy run** at one park by one membership, which is what stops a member from parking permanently by re-booking around the limit. The merged run is computed as follows. Take every reservation of the same membership at the same park whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `EXTENSION_REVIEW` or `COMPLETED`, and add the request under evaluation; reservations in `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`, `WAITLISTED` or `PENDING` never join a run. Sort that set by `arrival_date` ascending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending and any remaining tie by insertion sequence, with the request under evaluation sorted last among stays that share its arrival date and instant. Begin a run with the first stay. For each following stay, if its `arrival_date` is no later than the current run's departure date advanced by the link gap of 1 night, the stay merges into the run and the run's departure date becomes the later of the run's existing departure date and that stay's `departure_date`; otherwise a new run begins with that stay. The value of `consecutive_run_nights` for the request is the run containing the request measured in whole days from the run's arrival date to the run's departure date. A single night off the network therefore does *not* break a run — only a gap of two or more nights does.

Two ceilings apply to that run. The merged run must be no greater than the tier's `max_consecutive_stay_nights`, unless a `GRANTED` occupancy exception covers the reservation, and a new booking that overruns the tier limit is refused with `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED`. Independently, and whether or not an exception exists, the merged run must be no greater than the network's absolute run ceiling of 45 nights; breaching that is refused with `ERR_ABSOLUTE_OCCUPANCY_CAP`, and no exception, grant or approval anywhere in this system lifts it. At a partner-network park a third limit binds: the stay's own `nights` must be no greater than the agreement's `per_stay_night_cap`, and exceeding it is also refused with `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` — an occupancy exception never lifts the partner's per-stay cap.

**The out-and-back requirement** is measured network-wide across all parks and all classes, in contrast to the merged run which is strictly per-park. Identify the membership's previous stay: among that membership's reservations whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `EXTENSION_REVIEW` or `COMPLETED` and whose `departure_date` is on or before the requested `arrival_date`, take the one with the greatest `departure_date`, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending and any remaining tie by insertion sequence. `WAITLISTED`, `PENDING`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` stays are ignored entirely. If no such previous stay exists, the rule passes. Otherwise the gap is the requested `arrival_date` minus that previous stay's `departure_date`, in whole nights, and the rule passes when the gap is at least the tier's `min_out_nights_between_stays`. A gap exactly equal to the requirement passes; only a strictly smaller gap is refused, with `ERR_OUT_AND_BACK_VIOLATION`. Because the buffer is measured departure-to-arrival, two back-to-back stays yield a gap of 0, which passes only on a tier whose requirement is 0.

**Self-overlap** is checked separately: the requested half-open night set must not intersect the night set of any other reservation of the same membership in status `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `EXTENSION_REVIEW` at **any** park. A member cannot occupy two pads on the same night, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_OVERLAPPING_STAY`.

**Partner eligibility and fiscal standing.** At a partner-network park, the booking tier must have `affiliate_access_enabled` true and its `tier_rank` must be at least the agreement's `min_tier_rank`; failing either is refused with `ERR_AFFILIATE_TIER_INELIGIBLE`. On the billing axis, a booking requires `dues_status` to be either `CURRENT` or `GRACE`; a membership in `DELINQUENT` or `WRITTEN_OFF` cannot book at all and is refused with `ERR_DUES_NOT_CURRENT`. A membership in `GRACE` retains partial privileges — it may still book `CLASS_B` and `CLASS_C` parks — but is refused at `CLASS_A` parks with `ERR_CLASS_A_RESTRICTED_IN_GRACE`, which is the club's gentle first consequence of an unpaid invoice.

**Entitlement headroom** is the last gate, and it is evaluated *before* any debit is written. Which bucket the stay would debit is fixed by the park's `park_class`. Two conditions must both hold: the requested nights added to the current `nights_used_class_*` figure for that class must be no greater than the tier's `entitlement_nights_class_*` for the same class, and the requested nights added to `annual_nights_used` must be no greater than the tier's `annual_total_night_cap`. Landing exactly on either limit is permitted; exceeding either is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NIGHTS_EXHAUSTED`. The counters against which headroom is judged reflect only the current entitlement year: they are derived from the membership's reservations in status `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `EXTENSION_REVIEW` or `COMPLETED` whose `arrival_date` is on or after `entitlement_year_start` and strictly before `entitlement_year_start` advanced by the entitlement-year length of 365 days. Rows in `WAITLISTED`, `PENDING`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` are excluded and debit nothing.

**Then, and only then, capacity is allocated.** The allocator works night by night over the requested half-open night set and considers only reservations at that park whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `EXTENSION_REVIEW`; rows in `PENDING`, `WAITLISTED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED` and `COMPLETED` occupy nothing. For each date, the member commitment is the count of such reservations whose `pool_charged` is `MEMBER_POOL` and whose night set contains that date, and the transient commitment is the count of such reservations whose `pool_charged` is either `TRANSIENT_POOL` or `BORROWED_TRANSIENT` and whose night set contains that date. For a member request the allocator asks two questions in order. First: on **every** requested night, does the member commitment plus one still fit within `member_pool_sites`? If so the seat is granted from the member pool and `pool_charged` is set to `MEMBER_POOL`. If not, second: on **every** requested night, does the transient commitment plus one still fit within `transient_pool_sites` reduced by the public floor of 1 site? If so the member borrows a public pad and `pool_charged` is set to `BORROWED_TRANSIENT`. Members may borrow, but never so far as to leave the public with no pad at all. If neither question can be answered yes for all requested nights, no seat is granted.

A member request that clears every rule but wins no seat resolves to `WAITLISTED` rather than being refused. A waitlisted stay debits absolutely nothing: `pool_charged` stays `NONE`, `entitlement_nights_charged` stays 0, `affiliate_nights_charged` stays 0, and `departure_date` is still set from `scheduled_departure_date` so the desired night set is on record for the promotion sweep. A member request that wins a seat resolves to `CONFIRMED` and writes the full set of debits: `entitlement_nights_charged` is set to the **provisional** night count — the `scheduled_departure_date` minus the `arrival_date` in whole days — and that same figure is added to the membership's class bucket, with `annual_nights_used` recomputed as the sum of the three class buckets. At a partner-network park, `affiliate_nights_charged` is set to the ceiling of `nights` multiplied by `reciprocity_ratio`, that amount is added to `nights_consumed_ytd`, and `settlement_owed_cents` is recomputed. `consecutive_run_nights` is stored from the merged-run computation, `total_charge_cents` stays 0 because members pay dues rather than nightly cash, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. The park row, by contrast, is left entirely untouched: a member booking never bumps the park's `version`, unlike the public booking of §3.4 which does. A booking that lands on `WAITLISTED` touches nothing at all — neither the membership nor the park gains a version. The provisional charge is deliberately generous to the club: a member who departs early has over-charged entitlement until check-out reconciles it.

### 3.4. Transient Public Bookings and Nightly Pricing

The public books differently and is treated differently. A `TransientGuest` supplies a `park_id`, a `guest_name`, an `arrival_date` and a `scheduled_departure_date`; no membership is named, so `membership_id` stays null and `booker_type` is set to `TRANSIENT`. The park must be `OPEN` and, if it belongs to a partner network, its agreement must be `ACTIVE`, so `ERR_PARK_NOT_OPEN` and `ERR_AFFILIATE_AGREEMENT_INACTIVE` both apply to public bookings exactly as they do to member bookings. Every requested night must fall inside the park's inclusive season or the booking is refused with `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON`, and no requested night may touch the closed interval of a `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` maintenance window or the booking is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. None of the membership-shaped rules — advance window, merged run, out-and-back, entitlement, dues standing, partner tier rank — apply to the public at all.

Capacity for the public is drawn from the transient pool only, and the whole of it: on every requested night the transient commitment plus one must fit within `transient_pool_sites`, with no floor deduction, because the floor exists to protect the public from member borrowing rather than the reverse. When that succeeds, `status` becomes `CONFIRMED` and `pool_charged` is set to `TRANSIENT_POOL`. When it fails on even one night, the booking is refused with `ERR_NO_TRANSIENT_CAPACITY`: the public is never waitlisted and never borrows a member pad. A public booking debits no entitlement and no partner quota, so `entitlement_nights_charged` and `affiliate_nights_charged` both remain 0. Creating a transient reservation increments the park's `version` by 1.

What the public does pay is cash, computed into `total_charge_cents` at confirmation, and the **rounding order of this calculation is pinned**. The base nightly rate is selected by the park's class: 8,500 cents for a `CLASS_A` park, 6,200 cents for a `CLASS_B` park, and 4,100 cents for a `CLASS_C` park. Then, for each individual night in the half-open set from `arrival_date` up to but not including `departure_date`, two multipliers are applied to that base. The peak multiplier is 1.35 when the night's month-and-day falls on or after July 1st and on or before September 7th, and 1.00 otherwise. The weekend multiplier is 1.15 when the night falls on a Friday or a Saturday, and 1.00 otherwise. The charge for that night is the base multiplied by both multipliers, **rounded half up to whole cents for that night**. Only after every night has been individually rounded are the night charges summed into a subtotal. The resort fee is then that subtotal multiplied by 9 percent, rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_charge_cents` is the subtotal plus the resort fee.

That per-night-then-sum order is not interchangeable with summing the unrounded nightly rates and rounding once at the end; the two answers differ by as much as one cent per night. Two peak-weekend nights at a `CLASS_B` park illustrate it: each night is 6,200 multiplied by 1.35 and by 1.15, which is 9,625.5 cents, rounding half up to 9,626, so the subtotal is 19,252 cents — not the 19,251 that a single rounding of 19,251.0 would produce. Only the single reservation being priced participates in this calculation; no other stay, and no membership, affects it.

### 3.5. Occupancy in Progress: Arrival, Extension, Exception Review and Departure

Arrival moves a reservation from `CONFIRMED` to `CHECKED_IN` and is performed either by the member themselves or by the `ParkManager` at the gate — and, as the absence of check-in and check-out from rung 4 of §2.8 implies, the manager is not scoped to the park in question: any `ParkManager` may admit or release any stay anywhere in the network, the park-scope rung guarding only the park-administration operations it names. Only the acting `Member` is scoped, and then only to their own membership. Four conditions must hold. The reservation must be `CONFIRMED`, and an attempt from any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE`. The park must be `OPEN`, so a park that has slipped into `MAINTENANCE_HOLD` or been closed for the season cannot admit anyone, and a park in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PARK_STATE`. The owning membership's `membership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the current logical date must be on or after `arrival_date` and strictly before `departure_date` — a member may arrive late but not after the stay has already run out — a date outside that band being refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE`, the same identifier the status precondition uses. The four are evaluated in exactly that order: reservation status, then park status, then membership status, then the date band. Check-in stamps `checked_in_at` with the current logical instant and changes nothing else; no entitlement moves, because the provisional debit was taken at confirmation.

Extending a stay in progress is where the club's long-term-occupancy policy earns its keep, and the operation has **two distinct outcomes rather than a success and a failure**. A member supplies a `new_departure_date` and may supply a `reason`, which is the policy basis on which the member claims the right to exceed the long-term occupancy limit and must be one of `MEDICAL`, `EMPLOYMENT`, `WEATHER` or `RIG_REPAIR`; when `reason` is omitted it defaults to `RIG_REPAIR`. The reservation must be `CHECKED_IN`, and the same rule set that guards a new booking guards the extension: the extended night set must not touch a `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` maintenance window (`ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), must stay inside the park's inclusive season (`ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON`), must not overlap another active stay of the same membership at any park (`ERR_OVERLAPPING_STAY`), must respect a partner park's `per_stay_night_cap` (`ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED`), requires `membership_status` to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`), requires `dues_status` to be `CURRENT` or `GRACE` (`ERR_DUES_NOT_CURRENT`), is refused at a `CLASS_A` park while in `GRACE` (`ERR_CLASS_A_RESTRICTED_IN_GRACE`), must leave entitlement headroom for the added nights (`ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NIGHTS_EXHAUSTED`), must leave partner quota headroom (`ERR_AFFILIATE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`), and must keep the merged run within the absolute ceiling of 45 nights (`ERR_ABSOLUTE_OCCUPANCY_CAP`). The added nights are measured as `new_departure_date` minus the reservation's current `departure_date`, in whole days, and `new_departure_date` must therefore be strictly after the current `departure_date`: an equal or earlier date is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, judged immediately after the `CHECKED_IN` status check and before any of the policy rules. The order in which those policy rules are listed above is a catalogue and not an evaluation order — the ladder of §2.8 governs, so the sequence actually walked is membership state, maintenance conflict, absolute ceiling, season containment, partner per-stay cap, self-overlap, dues standing, the grace-period class restriction, entitlement headroom and finally partner quota headroom. Note also which quantity each rule measures: the partner `per_stay_night_cap` and both run ceilings are tested against the *whole extended stay*, whereas entitlement headroom is tested against the added nights alone and quota headroom against the quota delta defined below.

What the extension does **not** do is refuse a merged run that merely exceeds the tier's own `max_consecutive_stay_nights`. That is the essential asymmetry with a new booking: on a new booking such an overrun is refused outright with `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED`, whereas on an extension it routes into review instead. Consequently, on the extension operation only the partner per-stay-cap half of the stay-length rule can ever raise `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_EXCEEDED`.

The two branches are as follows, and they carry equal weight. **When the merged run with the extension applied stays within the tier's `max_consecutive_stay_nights`**, the reservation remains `CHECKED_IN` — a self-loop on the same state — `departure_date` is set to `new_departure_date`, `nights` is recomputed, the added nights are added to `entitlement_nights_charged` and to the membership's class bucket with `annual_nights_used` recomputed, the partner quota at a partner park is debited not by the added nights but by the *delta* between the charge the whole extended stay now attracts and the `affiliate_nights_charged` already recorded on the reservation — `ceil(new nights × reciprocity_ratio)` less what was already charged — that same delta being added to `affiliate_nights_charged` and to `nights_consumed_ytd` with `settlement_owed_cents` recomputed, and the whole quota step skipped when the delta is not positive, `extension_count` increases by 1, `consecutive_run_nights` is recomputed, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. **When the merged run with the extension applied exceeds the tier's `max_consecutive_stay_nights` but is still within the absolute ceiling of 45 nights**, the reservation moves `CHECKED_IN` to `EXTENSION_REVIEW` and one occupancy exception is opened in status `REQUESTED`, carrying `requested_run_nights` set to the projected merged run length, `tier_limit_nights` set to the tier's `max_consecutive_stay_nights` captured at request time, `reason` taken from the request body, `requested_by_user_id` set to the requesting member's `X-User-ID`, `decided_by_user_id` null, `decision_note` null and `granted_run_nights` at 0. On this branch `departure_date` is set to `new_departure_date` immediately and `nights` is recomputed to the extended length, but **no entitlement is debited yet**: the added nights are charged only if the exception is granted, and `departure_date` is rolled back if it is denied — the pre-extension `departure_date` being retained internally for that purpose without ever appearing as a wire field of the reservation. Neither the partner quota nor `affiliate_nights_charged` moves on this branch either, yet the membership's `version` still increments by 1 exactly as on the immediate branch, because the review branch touches the membership row even though no field of it changes. The exception a member's extension opens is only discoverable by reading the occupancy exceptions attached to that reservation, which is the reason that read path exists.

A `ComplianceOfficer` — and only a `ComplianceOfficer` — resolves the review by supplying a `decision` of either `GRANT` or `DENY`, optionally with a `decision_note`, and on a grant with `granted_run_nights`. The exception must be `REQUESTED`; a second decision on an already-resolved exception is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. On a grant, `granted_run_nights` must be at least the recorded `tier_limit_nights` and no greater than the absolute ceiling of 45 nights, and the membership must still have entitlement headroom for the granted nights, failing which the decision is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NIGHTS_EXHAUSTED`; a granted figure beyond 45 is refused with `ERR_ABSOLUTE_OCCUPANCY_CAP`, because a grant lifts only the tier limit and never the network ceiling. `granted_run_nights` is required and at least 1 on a grant and is not read at all on a deny, and a figure below the recorded `tier_limit_nights` is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a refusal evaluated *before* the already-decided rung, so an already-resolved exception handed a too-small grant reports the request fault rather than `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The entitlement figure that must fit is not the whole granted run but the delta between the recomputed stay length and the `entitlement_nights_charged` already sitting on the reservation, and the headroom test is skipped outright when that delta is zero or negative. Should the granted run be so short that the recomputed departure would land on or before the stay's own `arrival_date`, the grant is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both branches return the `OccupancyException` rather than the reservation.

A granted exception moves to `GRANTED` with `granted_run_nights` and `decided_by_user_id` stamped, and the reservation returns from `EXTENSION_REVIEW` to `CHECKED_IN`. Its `departure_date` becomes the `arrival_date` advanced by the granted run length less the nights already sitting in the merged run ahead of this reservation — which equals the requested `new_departure_date` whenever `granted_run_nights` equals `requested_run_nights`, and is shorter when the officer grants only part of what was asked. `entitlement_nights_charged` is then rewritten to the recomputed stay length rather than incremented, and only the positive delta against its previous value is debited to the membership's class bucket; the partner quota is debited by the same shape of delta, `ceil(new nights × reciprocity_ratio)` less the `affiliate_nights_charged` already on the reservation, and both debits — entitlement and quota alike — are skipped entirely when the entitlement delta is not positive, so a grant that merely ratifies nights already charged moves no counter at all. `extension_count` increases by 1, `consecutive_run_nights` is recomputed, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. Because the reservation is `CHECKED_IN` again, the member may request a further extension, which may in turn open another exception — the review cycle has no fixed depth, bounded only by the 45-night ceiling.

A denied exception moves to `DENIED` with `decided_by_user_id` and any `decision_note` stamped while `granted_run_nights` stays 0. The reservation also returns from `EXTENSION_REVIEW` to `CHECKED_IN`, but its `departure_date` is rolled back to the value it held before the extension was requested — or, if that retained value has somehow gone missing, to the date it currently holds, in which case nothing is rolled back — `nights` is recomputed from the restored date, no entitlement or partner quota is debited at all, and `extension_denied_count` increases by 1. The membership's `version` increments by 1 on this branch too, even though no field of the membership changes. The two branches therefore differ in exactly one respect beyond bookkeeping: a grant keeps the longer stay and pays for it, and a denial keeps the original stay and pays nothing.

Departure closes the stay and reconciles the money-shaped fiction that was the provisional debit. A member or the `ParkManager` supplies `actual_departure_date`, which must be strictly greater than `arrival_date` and no later than the current `departure_date`; the reservation must be `CHECKED_IN`, a condition judged first, after which a date outside that band is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The reservation moves to `COMPLETED`, `checked_out_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, `departure_date` is overwritten with `actual_departure_date`, and `nights` is recomputed. The entitlement charge now switches to its **final** form: the final night count is `actual_departure_date` minus `arrival_date` in whole days, the difference between the previously charged `entitlement_nights_charged` and that final count — a figure that is 0 on an on-time departure and positive on an early one — is subtracted from the membership's class bucket, `annual_nights_used` is recomputed, and `entitlement_nights_charged` is rewritten to the final count. The partner quota is re-derived the same way, as the ceiling of the final `nights` multiplied by `reciprocity_ratio`, with the difference credited back to `nights_consumed_ytd` and `settlement_owed_cents` recomputed. The membership's `version` increments by 1. Because the provisional and final forms of the same charge disagree whenever a member leaves early, the same reservation legitimately reports one `nights_used_class_*` figure between confirmation and check-out and a smaller one afterwards. Finally the pool seat is released — and released for *every* night of the stay rather than only those from `actual_departure_date` onward, because a `COMPLETED` reservation is no longer counted as occupying anything at all, while `pool_charged` is deliberately left standing on the row as a historical record of which pool the stay was drawn from. The waitlist at that park is then worked unconditionally and without any restriction to the nights just freed, on every check-out including an exactly on-time one.

### 3.6. Releases: Cancellation, Maintenance, and Working the Waitlist

Cancelling before arrival is available to the member who holds the stay, to a `ClubAdmin`, and — for a public booking — to the `TransientGuest`. The reservation must be in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`; cancelling anything else, including a `COMPLETED` stay, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE`. `cancellation_reason` is recorded as `MEMBER_REQUEST`. Cancelling a `WAITLISTED` stay changes nothing else at all, because nothing was ever debited: no entitlement moves, no quota moves, and no seat is released. Cancelling a `CONFIRMED` member stay runs the full compensating action — `entitlement_nights_charged` is credited back to the membership's class bucket and to `annual_nights_used` and then reset to 0, `affiliate_nights_charged` is credited back to the agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd` with `settlement_owed_cents` recomputed and then reset to 0, `pool_charged` returns to `NONE` releasing the seat on every night of the stay, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. The entitlement invariant is left exactly as it stood before the booking. A released member-pool or borrowed seat then triggers the waitlist sweep at that park — a sweep that considers every waitlisted stay at the park without restriction to the nights just freed, so a cancellation can promote a stay whose dates never touched the cancelled one. A cancelled public booking, whose `pool_charged` was `TRANSIENT_POOL`, releases no member seat and therefore triggers no sweep at all.

**Working the waitlist** is a repeated, deterministic promotion walk, and it runs on any member-pool release at a park: a member-requested cancellation, the suspension cascade, a check-out with an early `actual_departure_date`, and the completion of a maintenance window. It never touches any other park. The candidate set is built from reservations at the released park whose `status` is `WAITLISTED` and whose `booker_type` is `MEMBER`, filtered so that, on every night of the candidate's night set, the member commitment recomputed *after* the release plus one still fits within `member_pool_sites`; the candidate's membership must have `membership_status` `ACTIVE` and `dues_status` either `CURRENT` or `GRACE`; and the membership must still have entitlement headroom for the candidate's nights. Candidates are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and where two share the same `created_at` the earlier `arrival_date` wins, with any remaining tie broken by insertion sequence. The first candidate is promoted; the remaining candidates are then re-evaluated against the now-reduced free capacity, and the walk repeats until the candidate set is empty. A candidate that fails the eligibility filter is simply skipped and stays `WAITLISTED` — the promotion sweep never expires anyone. If the candidate set is empty, no promotion occurs and no error is raised.

Promotion moves a reservation from `WAITLISTED` to `CONFIRMED` and applies the debits that were withheld while it waited: `pool_charged` becomes `MEMBER_POOL`, `entitlement_nights_charged` is set to the stay's `nights` and added to the membership's class bucket with `annual_nights_used` recomputed, the partner quota is debited when the park belongs to an agreement — unconditionally, because the promotion filter tests entitlement headroom but never re-tests partner quota headroom, so a promotion may carry `nights_consumed_ytd` past `annual_member_night_quota` — `consecutive_run_nights` is recomputed, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. A promoted stay is charged with the provisional form of the entitlement calculation, exactly as a stay confirmed at booking time is.

Maintenance takes pads out of service on a planned interval. A `ParkManager` schedules a window by naming the `park_id`, a `start_date`, an `end_date` and a `work_type` drawn from `ROUTINE`, `CAPITAL` and `EMERGENCY`. `end_date` must not precede `start_date` — an equal pair is a legitimate single-night window — and a violation is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The window opens in status `SCHEDULED` with `cancelled_reservation_count` at 0, and — as noted in §3.3 — from that moment its closed inclusive night interval already blocks new bookings and extensions at the park even though the park itself is still `OPEN`.

Activating the window is a five-link cascade and it is the widest-reaching single operation in this service. It requires the window to be `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MAINTENANCE_STATE` — and the park to be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PARK_STATE`. Link one moves the window from `SCHEDULED` to `ACTIVE` and the park's `park_status` from `OPEN` to `MAINTENANCE_HOLD`. Link two force-cancels every reservation at that park whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` and whose half-open night set intersects the window's closed interval from `start_date` through `end_date`: a `CONFIRMED` stay becomes `CANCELLED` and a `WAITLISTED` stay becomes `EXPIRED`, with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `MAINTENANCE`. Link three credits each cancelled reservation's `entitlement_nights_charged` back to its membership's class bucket and `annual_nights_used`, incrementing that `Membership`'s `version` by 1, and credits its `affiliate_nights_charged` back to the agreement's quota with `settlement_owed_cents` recomputed. Link four adds the cancelled count to the park's `forced_cancellation_count`, and once that tally reaches 2 or more for the calendar year, the park's `amenity_compliance_status` is moved from `COMPLIANT` to `PROBATION`. Link five propagates that compliance change into money: `priority_score` is recomputed for every capital project at the park whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `DEFERRED`. The window's `cancelled_reservation_count` is set to the number of reservations cancelled, and the park's `version` increments by 1. Crucially, **no waitlist promotion runs on an activation** — the park cannot host anyone while the hold is in force, so freeing seats would be meaningless.

Completing the window is the revert leg of that cycle. It requires the window to be `ACTIVE` and the park to be in `MAINTENANCE_HOLD`. The window moves to `COMPLETED`, `park_status` returns from `MAINTENANCE_HOLD` to `OPEN`, and the waitlist is worked at that park for the remaining `WAITLISTED` reservations whose night set begins after the window's `end_date`. The park's `version` increments by 1. A park may be held and reopened any number of times inside one season, and each cycle leaves `forced_cancellation_count` at the elevated value it reached — the tally is a calendar-year record, not something a completion erases. It is strictly a record of the *current* logical calendar year: the server keeps the tally bucketed by year and reports only the bucket for the year the logical clock is in, so advancing the clock across a New Year makes a park's `forced_cancellation_count` read 0 again and lifts the disruption-driven probation floor of §3.9 with it.

### 3.7. The Billing Axis: Dues, Delinquency, Suspension and Reinstatement

A membership carries **two independent state axes on the same row**, and understanding that they are independent is essential. `membership_status` is the contract axis, moving among `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `TRANSFER_PENDING` and `TERMINATED`, and it is driven by administrative action, transfer decisions and the delinquency cascade. `dues_status` is the billing axis, moving among `CURRENT`, `GRACE`, `DELINQUENT` and `WRITTEN_OFF`, and it is advanced **only** by the delinquency sweep and by invoice payment or payment reversal. Neither axis constrains the other's reachable values except through the explicit preconditions named below; a membership can be `SUSPENDED` on the contract axis while sitting at `CURRENT` on the billing axis, and vice versa.

Each entitlement year the club reissues dues. The annual dues run takes a `cycle_year` and, for every membership whose entitlement year has rolled over — that is, whose `entitlement_year_start` advanced by 365 days is on or before the current logical date — creates one invoice for that cycle using the same five-step waterfall described in §3.1 — base dues, an 8 percent capital assessment rounded half up, a subtotal, a loyalty percentage keyed off that subtotal, and the subtotal less the loyalty credit. Because the loyalty band reads `tenure_years`, which grows by one for every whole 365 days since `tenure_origin_at`, a member's dues fall as their tenure crosses the 5-year threshold. Alongside issuing the invoice the run rolls `entitlement_year_start` forward by the entitlement-year length of 365 days, resets `nights_used_class_a`, `nights_used_class_b`, `nights_used_class_c` and `annual_nights_used` all to 0, recomputes `dues_balance_cents` and `tenure_years`, and increments each invoiced membership's `version` by 1. The run walks the memberships in `created_at` order and skips two populations outright, invoicing neither and rolling neither entitlement year: any membership whose `membership_status` is `TERMINATED`, and any whose `dues_status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`. The run reports back the `cycle_year` it processed, `invoices_issued` as the number of invoices created, and `total_billed_cents` as the sum of their `amount_cents`.

Paying an invoice is all-or-nothing. The payer — the holding `Member` or a `ClubAdmin` — supplies `amount_cents`, and it must equal the invoice's outstanding balance exactly: the invoice's `amount_cents` plus its `late_fee_cents` less any `paid_amount_cents` already applied. Neither a partial payment nor an over-payment is accepted, and either is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The invoice must be `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE`, and a payment attempt against a `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` invoice is refused with `ERR_DUES_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`. A settling payment moves the invoice to `PAID`, writes the tendered figure into `paid_amount_cents` and stamps `paid_at` with the current logical instant. On the billing axis, `dues_status` returns to `CURRENT` from either `GRACE` or `DELINQUENT`, provided no other invoice of that membership remains `PAST_DUE` after the payment; `dues_paid_through` advances to the settled invoice's `due_date` plus the dues cycle length of 365 days; `dues_balance_cents` is recomputed; and the membership's `version` increments by 1. Note carefully what a payment does **not** do: a membership that was suspended for delinquency stays `SUSPENDED` on the contract axis even after its dues return to `CURRENT`. Lifting the suspension is a separate `ClubAdmin` act.

A payment can be undone — for a chargeback or misapplied cash — by a `ClubAdmin` supplying a free-text `reason`. The invoice must be `PAID`, and a reversal against anything else is refused with `ERR_DUES_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`. The reversal is a genuine compensating action: `paid_amount_cents` is reset to 0, `paid_at` is cleared to null, `dues_paid_through` is rolled back to the value it held before the payment, `dues_balance_cents` is restored to exactly its pre-payment figure, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. Where the invoice lands depends on the calendar. When the current logical date is on or before the invoice's `due_date`, the invoice returns to `OPEN`, `late_fee_cents` is set back to 0, and `dues_status` stays `CURRENT` — nothing was ever late. When the invoice's `due_date` is strictly before the current logical date, the invoice returns to `PAST_DUE` with `late_fee_cents` recomputed by the late-fee rule, and `dues_status` moves from `CURRENT` to `GRACE`.

The delinquency sweep is the club's ageing engine, run by a `ClubAdmin` with no request body. For every invoice whose `status` is `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE`, it recomputes `days_past_due` as the current logical date minus the invoice's `due_date` in whole days, floored at 0 so that an invoice not yet due reports 0. `days_past_due` reflects the most recent sweep and reads 0 before the first sweep has run. Any such invoice whose `days_past_due` has reached the grace threshold of 1 day moves to `PAST_DUE` and has its `late_fee_cents` recomputed. **The late fee is one multiplication and exactly one rounding.** The number of months late is `days_past_due` divided by 30 and **rounded up** to a whole number, so one day late is already one month late. `late_fee_cents` is then the invoice's `amount_cents` multiplied by the late-fee rate of 1.5 percent and multiplied by that month count, with a single half-up rounding applied to the product at the end. Rounding the rate-times-amount first and then multiplying by the month count yields a different cent figure and is wrong.

Three ageing bands then advance the billing axis. A membership with an `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE` invoice whose `days_past_due` is at least 1 and below 30 moves from `CURRENT` to `GRACE`. A membership whose invoice has reached at least 30 days past due but is still below 120 moves to `DELINQUENT`, whether it arrives from `CURRENT` or from `GRACE`; a membership can therefore reach `DELINQUENT` having never been observed in `GRACE`, simply because no sweep happened to run while it sat inside the grace band. A membership already `DELINQUENT` whose `PAST_DUE` invoice has reached 120 days past due moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, and the invoice itself moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`; both are terminal. A membership still at `CURRENT` or `GRACE` when the sweep meets a 120-day invoice traverses both bands inside that one sweep — it is driven first to `DELINQUENT`, running the full six-link cascade described next, and then immediately on to `WRITTEN_OFF` — and is therefore counted once in `memberships_to_delinquent` and once again in `memberships_written_off`. A membership whose `dues_status` is already `WRITTEN_OFF` when the sweep reaches one of its invoices is left untouched by every band. Each of these advances increments the membership's `version` by 1. After the ageing pass the sweep recomputes `dues_balance_cents` and `tenure_years` on every membership that held one of the swept `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE` invoices, skipping any membership already sitting at `WRITTEN_OFF` when the sweep reached its invoice. Within the sweep a membership row is then rewritten — `updated_at` refreshed and `version` moved as §2.4 prescribes — only where the sweep has actually moved one of that row's stored business fields; a band advance, the cascade described next, the write-off, and a `dues_balance_cents` that shifts because a late fee was recomputed all count as such movement. A membership that the sweep leaves with every business field exactly as it found it is not rewritten at all, so its `updated_at` and `version` stand still: the holder of an invoice merely due today, whose `days_past_due` is 0, whose invoice stays `OPEN` with `late_fee_cents` at 0 and whose balance therefore does not move, comes out of the sweep at the `version` it went in at. Swept invoices carry no such condition — every `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE` invoice the sweep visits is rewritten, even one whose recomputed `days_past_due` equals the figure it already carried.

Reaching `DELINQUENT` fires a six-link cascade. Link one moves the invoice to `PAST_DUE` with `late_fee_cents` recomputed. Link two sets `dues_status` to `DELINQUENT`. Link three moves the contract axis: a membership in `ACTIVE` **or** in `TRANSFER_PENDING` becomes `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `DUES_DELINQUENCY`. Link four cancels every reservation of that membership whose `status` is `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` and whose `arrival_date` is strictly after the current logical date, recording `cancellation_reason` as `MEMBERSHIP_SUSPENDED`; a `PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` stay lands in `CANCELLED` and a `WAITLISTED` one in `EXPIRED`. Link five credits each cancellation's `entitlement_nights_charged` back to the membership's class bucket and `annual_nights_used`, credits its `affiliate_nights_charged` back to the agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd` with `settlement_owed_cents` recomputed, and releases its pool seat. Link six works the waitlist at every park that released a member-pool seat. In the same sweep, any transfer request of that membership still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `VOIDED`; when the membership was in `TRANSFER_PENDING`, the void records `rejection_reason` as the free-text dues-delinquency basis — the literal string `dues delinquency` — and leaves `decided_by_user_id` null, because no human decided it. Reaching `WRITTEN_OFF` carries a shorter cascade: the contract axis moves from `SUSPENDED` to the terminal `TERMINATED`, and every remaining reservation of the membership in `PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED` while every one in `WAITLISTED` becomes `EXPIRED`, each recording `cancellation_reason` `MEMBERSHIP_SUSPENDED` and each releasing its debits; unlike link four this leg applies no arrival-date filter, so a stay already under way by the calendar is swept too, and every reservation it touches counts in `reservations_cancelled`.

The sweep also tidies the waitlist independently of any membership's standing: every `WAITLISTED` reservation whose `arrival_date` is before the current logical date moves to `EXPIRED`, with no entitlement or capacity change, because nothing was ever debited. The sweep reports `invoices_swept`, `memberships_to_grace`, `memberships_to_delinquent`, `memberships_written_off`, `reservations_cancelled` and `waitlist_promotions`.

Suspension is also available directly. A `ClubAdmin` suspends a membership by supplying a `suspension_reason` — which the server also accepts under the alternative body key `reason` whenever `suspension_reason` itself is absent — and which for a manual suspension is either `ADMIN_ACTION` or `OCCUPANCY_POLICY`, the latter being the club's lever against a member who has been gaming the long-term-occupancy rules. The membership must be `ACTIVE`, and a suspension aimed at a membership already `SUSPENDED`, or one sitting at `TRANSFER_PENDING` or `TERMINATED`, is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE` — but only after the reason enum has been validated, since body well-formedness is the earlier rung. The contract axis moves to `SUSPENDED`, the supplied reason is recorded, every reservation of the membership still awaiting arrival — those in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` with an `arrival_date` after the current logical date — moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `MEMBERSHIP_SUSPENDED`, each such cancellation credits its `entitlement_nights_charged` back to `nights_used_class_*` and `annual_nights_used` and its `affiliate_nights_charged` back to the affected agreement's `nights_consumed_ytd`, each releases its pool seat by returning `pool_charged` to `NONE`, the waitlist is worked at every affected park, any `SUBMITTED` transfer request on the membership moves to `VOIDED` with `rejection_reason` set to the free-text basis `membership suspended` and `decided_by_user_id` left null, and the membership's `version` increments by 1.

Reinstatement is the revert leg. A `ClubAdmin` supplies `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents`, and three things must all hold: `membership_status` must be `SUSPENDED`, `dues_status` must be back at `CURRENT`, and `dues_balance_cents` must be exactly 0 with the tendered fee at least the reinstatement fee of 15,000 cents, which is one hundred and fifty dollars. Any one of those failing — a `membership_status` other than `SUSPENDED`, a `dues_status` other than `CURRENT`, a non-zero balance, or a short fee — is refused with the single `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_REQUIRED`; the balance is recomputed from the membership's open invoices immediately before the check, and the tendered fee is verified but stored nowhere. A successful reinstatement returns `membership_status` to `ACTIVE`, clears `suspension_reason` to null, and increments the membership's `version` by 1. Reservations that the suspension cancelled are **not** restored — the member must book again, subject to whatever capacity now exists.

### 3.8. Transfer, Resale and Succession

A membership is a transferable asset, within limits the club sets tier by tier. A `Member` initiates a transfer by naming the `membership_id` being transferred, the `to_member_id` receiving it, a `transfer_type` of `GIFT`, `RESALE` or `ESTATE`, the pinned `expected_membership_version` described in §2.7, and — for a resale only — a `sale_price_cents`. On any other transfer type `sale_price_cents` must be omitted or supplied as 0, a non-zero figure being refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The server copies `from_member_id` from the membership's current `member_id`, so the outgoing holder is recorded even after the contract changes hands.

Submission is heavily gated because a membership under review must be frozen. The membership's `membership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, and its `dues_status` must be `CURRENT` — the transfer path is stricter than the booking path, which tolerates `GRACE` — refused otherwise with `ERR_DUES_NOT_CURRENT`. The membership must hold no reservation in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `WAITLISTED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `EXTENSION_REVIEW`, refused otherwise with `ERR_TRANSFER_BLOCKED_OPEN_RESERVATIONS`. For a resale specifically, the pinned tier must have `resale_allowed` true, refused otherwise with `ERR_RESALE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and at least the resale hold of 730 days must have elapsed between the membership's `purchased_at` and the current logical date, refused otherwise with `ERR_RESALE_HOLD_ACTIVE` — the same two-year horizon the computed `resale_eligible_at` field publishes, and a hold that gates resales only, never gifts or estate transfers. Finally the chain limit binds: `transfer_count` must be strictly below the maximum of 2 transfers per chain, refused otherwise with `ERR_TRANSFER_LIMIT_REACHED`, so an original purchase may change hands twice and no more.

A successful submission creates the request in status `SUBMITTED` with `successor_membership_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `rejection_reason` all null, moves the membership's `membership_status` from `ACTIVE` to `TRANSFER_PENDING`, and increments the membership's `version` by 1. It also freezes the fee. **The fee is two independent roundings followed by an integer addition.** A type multiplier is selected first: 1.00 for a `RESALE`, 0.50 for a `GIFT`, and 0.00 for an `ESTATE` transfer, so an estate succession is free of the base fee entirely. The base component is the tier's `transfer_fee_cents` multiplied by that type multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. The resale surcharge is the submitted `sale_price_cents` multiplied by the resale surcharge rate of 6 percent, rounded half up to whole cents, and is 0 for any transfer type other than `RESALE`. `transfer_fee_cents` on the request is the sum of those two rounded components, and it is frozen at submission and never recomputed. The submission also records, internally, the `X-User-ID` that lodged the request, purely so the segregation-of-duties comparison below has something to compare against; that identity is **not** a wire field of a `TransferRequest` and is never serialised, so the only field naming a human on a fresh request is the null `decided_by_user_id`.

A `ClubAdmin` resolves the request by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `rejection_reason`. The request must be `SUBMITTED`, and a second decision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE`. The deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the `X-User-ID` that submitted the request, refused otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; as noted in §2.8, that check sits above the dues check, so a self-decision on a membership whose dues have also lapsed reports the segregation failure. Approval additionally requires the membership to be in `TRANSFER_PENDING` with `dues_status` still `CURRENT`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DUES_NOT_CURRENT`. Both outcomes stamp `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`.

The two branches diverge sharply. **Approval** moves the request to `APPROVED` and the source membership from `TRANSFER_PENDING` to the terminal `TERMINATED`, then mints a successor membership for `to_member_id`. The successor keeps the same `tier_id`, and copies `tenure_origin_at` and `dues_paid_through` verbatim from the source so that tenure and billing position survive the change of hands; its `transferred_from_membership_id` points at the source membership; its `transfer_count` is the source's `transfer_count` plus 1; its `purchased_at` is stamped with the current logical instant; `nights_used_class_a`, `nights_used_class_b` and `nights_used_class_c` all reset to 0 with `annual_nights_used` accordingly 0; it opens `ACTIVE` on the contract axis and `CURRENT` on the billing axis with `suspension_reason` null and `dues_balance_cents` 0; and a fresh `OPEN` dues invoice is issued for it, for the calendar year the logical clock is in. Three further fields are minted rather than copied: `upfront_paid_cents` opens at 0, because the successor paid the outgoing holder and not the club; `entitlement_year_start` is set to the current logical date, so the successor's entitlement year restarts on the day the transfer completes rather than continuing the source's; and `resale_eligible_at` is computed afresh as the current logical instant advanced by the resale hold of 730 days, so a resold membership cannot be flipped again for two more years even though its `tenure_origin_at` reaches back further. `tenure_years` is then derived from that copied `tenure_origin_at` in the usual way. The request's `successor_membership_id` is set to the new membership's `id`, and any reservations still `WAITLISTED` under the source membership move to `EXPIRED` with `cancellation_reason` left null, since a transfer is not a cancellation of anything. **Rejection** moves the request to `REJECTED`, returns the source membership from `TRANSFER_PENDING` to `ACTIVE`, mints no successor at all, leaves `successor_membership_id` null, and collects no fee — though `transfer_fee_cents` remains on the record at the figure frozen at submission, since that figure is never recomputed and never zeroed; a rejection simply means it is never charged. Either way the source membership's `version` increments by 1, and either way the operation returns the `TransferRequest` rather than any membership.

The third and final exit from `SUBMITTED` is never a human decision. When the membership becomes `SUSPENDED` while a request is still `SUBMITTED` — whether through a `ClubAdmin` suspension or through the delinquency cascade — the request moves to `VOIDED` with `rejection_reason` recording the suspension basis and `decided_by_user_id` left null. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal.

### 3.9. Member Satisfaction, Amenity Standards and Capital Reinvestment

Where the club spends its capital is decided by evidence, and the evidence comes from two independent channels: what members say after they stay, and what inspectors find when they walk the property.

**Post-stay surveys.** Once a stay has reached `COMPLETED`, the member who took it may submit one satisfaction survey by naming the four ratings `overall_rating`, `cleanliness_rating`, `staff_rating` and `value_rating`, each an integer from 1 through 5. Submitting a survey against a reservation in any state other than `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE`. The server records the `reservation_id` being rated, copies `park_id` and `membership_id` from that reservation so the survey can be found from either side, and stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical instant. Surveys are immutable once filed and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. The survey's own `weighted_score` is the sum of four weighted contributions — 40 percent of `overall_rating`, 25 percent of `cleanliness_rating`, 20 percent of `staff_rating` and 15 percent of `value_rating` — with a single half-up rounding of that sum to two decimal places.

**The park rating has two forms, and they deliberately disagree.** The first form is the recency-weighted lifetime score, which is what gets stored on the park's `rating_score` and recomputed on every survey submission. Each survey is weighted by its age in whole days from `submitted_at` to the current logical date: a survey aged at most 90 days weighs 1.0, one aged at most 365 days weighs 0.6, and anything older weighs 0.3. The score is the sum over all of the park's surveys of the survey's weight multiplied by its `weighted_score`, divided by the sum of those same weights, with a single half-up rounding of the resulting quotient to two decimal places. A park with no surveys at all reports a `rating_score` of exactly 0.00. Because nothing is ever dropped, an old run of glowing reviews keeps propping the stored score up at reduced weight.

The second form is the trailing-window mean, which is never stored and is computed on demand inside the capital priority calculation. It hard-drops every survey older than 180 days and takes the plain unweighted arithmetic mean of the surviving surveys' `weighted_score` values, rounded half up to two decimal places. When the trailing window is empty it yields exactly 3.00 as its default. The same survey set therefore produces two different numbers — a park can carry a healthy stored `rating_score` while its reinvestment scoring treats it as failing, which is precisely the signal the club wants when a good park has recently gone bad.

**Amenity inspections.** A `ParkManager` (or an auditing `ComplianceOfficer`) files an inspection with four standards scores, each an integer from 1 through 5: `restroom_score` for restrooms and shower houses, `hookup_score` for water, sewer and electric hookups, `utility_score` for the utility plant and dump station, and `road_score` for interior roads and pads. The park must be `OPEN` or `MAINTENANCE_HOLD`; filing against a `SEASONAL_CLOSED` park is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PARK_STATE`. The server stamps `inspected_at` with the current logical instant and records the filing operator's `X-User-ID` as `inspector_user_id`. Like surveys, inspections are append-only and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

The inspection's `composite_score` is a weighted sum — 35 percent of `restroom_score`, 25 percent of `hookup_score`, 25 percent of `utility_score` and 15 percent of `road_score` — rounded half up to two decimal places. Its `result` is banded from that composite with **inclusive lower edges**: `PASS` when the composite is at least 4.00, `PROBATION` when the composite is at least 3.00 but below 4.00, and `FAIL` below 3.00. Exactly 4.00 is a `PASS` and exactly 3.00 is a `PROBATION`. Only the newest inspection drives the park, and its result maps onto `amenity_compliance_status` as `COMPLIANT` for a `PASS`, `PROBATION` for a `PROBATION`, and `NON_COMPLIANT` for a `FAIL`. There is one override: a `PASS` never clears a probation caused by disruption. While the park's `forced_cancellation_count` for the current calendar year stands at 2 or more, the status floor stays `PROBATION` even when the newest result is `PASS` — a park that has thrown two sets of members out mid-season does not buy its way back with a clean restroom score. Filing an inspection also sets the park's `last_inspection_at` to that `inspected_at`, recomputes `priority_score` for every capital project at the park, and increments the park's `version` by 1. A survey submission likewise recomputes `priority_score` for every capital project at the park and increments the park's `version` by 1, since the stored rating it moves is an input to that score.

**Proposing capital work.** A `ParkManager` or a `ClubAdmin` proposes a project by naming the `park_id`, a short `title`, a `category` drawn from `SAFETY`, `UTILITY`, `AMENITY` and `EXPANSION`, a `requested_cost_cents` of at least 0 — a zero-cost project is accepted, and is simply one the reinvestment run can always afford — and the `fiscal_year` the project competes in, which is validated only as a non-negative integer rather than being confined to any plausible calendar range, so an implausible year is a filing error and not a request the server refuses. The project opens in status `PROPOSED` with `funded_amount_cents` at 0, `decided_by_user_id` null, and `requested_by_user_id` set from the proposer's `X-User-ID` — which is a park identifier when a `ParkManager` proposed and an opaque operator identifier when a `ClubAdmin` did. Its `priority_score` is computed immediately, and is recomputed on every decision, on any change to the park's `amenity_compliance_status` or `rating_score`, and at the start of every reinvestment run. Only projects in `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `DEFERRED` are rescored by those park-driven recomputations; a `FUNDED` or `REJECTED` project keeps the score it was last given. And a recomputation rewrites the project row — refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version` — only when the newly computed figure actually differs from the stored one, so a park event that leaves a project's score unmoved leaves that project's `version` unmoved as well.

**The priority score is five factors multiplied together and scaled.** The compliance factor is 1.30 when the park's `amenity_compliance_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, 1.15 when it is `PROBATION`, and 1.00 when it is `COMPLIANT` — a degraded park is favoured, not penalised, because that is where reinvestment is needed. The category weight is 1.40 for `SAFETY`, 1.20 for `UTILITY`, 1.00 for `AMENITY` and 0.80 for `EXPANSION`. The satisfaction gap is the target rating of 4.50 less the park's trailing-window mean rating, floored at 0.00 so that a park already at or above target contributes no gap. The usage intensity is the park's year-to-date stayed member nights divided by the product of `total_sites` and the usage denominator of 30 nights — with that denominator product treated as 1 whenever it would otherwise be 0 — rounded half up to four decimal places, and then capped at 1.50. Those stayed member nights are the sum of `entitlement_nights_charged` over reservations at that park whose `status` is `CHECKED_IN`, `EXTENSION_REVIEW` or `COMPLETED` and whose `arrival_date` falls in the current calendar year; `CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED`, `WAITLISTED` and `EXPIRED` nights are excluded, so a cancelled stay never inflates a park's score. The cost factor is 1.20 when `requested_cost_cents` is at most 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars), 1.00 when it is at most 20,000,000 cents (two hundred thousand dollars), and 0.85 above that. The raw figure is the category weight multiplied by the compliance factor, multiplied by one plus the satisfaction gap, multiplied by one plus the usage intensity, multiplied by the cost factor. `priority_score` is that raw figure multiplied by 1,000 and rounded half up to a whole integer — a single rounding at the very end, after the four-decimal usage intensity has been fixed.

**Deciding a project** is a `ClubAdmin` act requiring the pinned `expected_park_version` of §2.7, a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and optionally a `decision_note`. The project must be `PROPOSED` — a project in any other status is refused with a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a state-shaped identifier, since the project axis has no state error of its own — the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and the supplied park version must match (`ERR_STALE_PARK_VERSION`). Those three are evaluated in the reverse of the order just named: the park version pin fires first, then the `PROPOSED` check, then segregation, so a stale version reported against an already-decided project reports the staleness. Approval is further constrained by compliance standing: a project at a park whose `amenity_compliance_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT` may be approved only when its `category` is `SAFETY` or `UTILITY`, so a non-compliant park cannot fund an `AMENITY` upgrade or an `EXPANSION` while its basics are failing; that refusal too is a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is the last rung of the four, below segregation. A `REJECT` is never blocked by compliance standing — the restriction guards approvals alone. Both outcomes stamp `decided_by_user_id`, recompute `priority_score` against current park state, increment the project's own `version` by 1 unconditionally — the decision writes the row whether or not the recomputed score moved — and increment the park's `version` by 1; any supplied `decision_note` is retained internally as an audit trace and is **not** a wire field of a `CapitalProject`, so it is never echoed back; a rejection leaves `funded_amount_cents` at 0 and is terminal, while an approval places the project into the funding queue.

**The reinvestment run** allocates a budget by strict priority with **no partial funding and no early stop**. A `ClubAdmin` supplies a `fiscal_year` and a `budget_cents`. Candidates are the capital projects whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `DEFERRED`, whose `fiscal_year` matches the run's, and whose park's `park_status` is `OPEN`, `SEASONAL_CLOSED` or `MAINTENANCE_HOLD`; projects in `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED` or already `FUNDED` never enter the walk. Every candidate's `priority_score` is recomputed **before** ordering. Candidates are then ordered by `priority_score` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending and any remaining tie by the insertion sequence of §2.3 ascending, so the walk is fully deterministic even when several projects were created at the same logical instant. `budget_cents` and the run's `fiscal_year` are each validated only as non-negative integers, and a candidate whose park has somehow gone missing is skipped rather than treated as an error. Starting with the full budget as the remaining amount, the run walks the ordered list: when a project's `requested_cost_cents` is no greater than the remaining amount, the project becomes `FUNDED`, its `funded_amount_cents` is set to its `requested_cost_cents`, and the remaining amount is reduced by that figure; when it is greater, the project becomes `DEFERRED` with `funded_amount_cents` left at 0 and **the walk continues to the next candidate**. A cheap low-priority project can therefore be funded after an expensive high-priority project has been skipped. A `DEFERRED` project is not dead: a later run whose remaining budget covers it when the walk reaches it funds it exactly as an `APPROVED` one.

When the walk finishes, `allocated_cents` is the supplied `budget_cents` less whatever remains and `unallocated_cents` is that remainder, so the budget is always conserved: the allocated and unallocated figures sum to the budget, and the allocated figure equals the sum of the `funded_amount_cents` written in this run. The run also reports the `fiscal_year` and `budget_cents` it processed, `funded_project_count` and `deferred_project_count`. For every park with a newly funded project, `funded_capital_cents_ytd` is recomputed as the sum of `funded_amount_cents` over that park's projects in status `FUNDED` for the current fiscal year, and that park's `version` increments by 1. An empty candidate set is not an error: the run simply reports zero allocated, the whole budget unallocated, and no projects funded.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted at the service root; there is no common path prefix. Operations that bring a new resource into existence return **201 Created**; every other successful operation, including every state transition, every operations run, every read and the logical-clock hook, returns **200 OK**. A trailing slash is insignificant in path matching, so `/parks` and `/parks/` address the same operation. Request bodies, response bodies and side effects are deliberately not repeated here — they are specified in §2 and §3, and this table exists only to bind each business operation described there to its HTTP contract and its authorised roles. One rule does hold across the whole table, and it settles what an operation that mutates several rows at once hands back: **the response body is always the resource named in the path, never a collateral one.** So a membership suspension or reinstatement returns the `Membership`; a dues payment returns the `DuesInvoice` and not the membership whose standing it just changed; a season opening or closure returns the `Park`; a maintenance activation or completion returns the `MaintenanceWindow` and not the park it just put on hold; an agreement status change returns the `AffiliateAgreement`; a booking, cancellation, check-in, check-out or extension returns the `Reservation`; an occupancy-exception decision returns the `OccupancyException`; a transfer decision returns the `TransferRequest` and not the successor membership it minted; a capital-project decision returns the `CapitalProject`. The operations runs of §3.10 and the logical-clock hook are the only exceptions, each returning its own summary object rather than any stored entity — and the clock hook's summary is the single field `current_utc`, carrying the instant the clock was moved to, normalised to the three-fractional-digit UTC form of §2.4 even when the caller supplied it with an offset.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every error response uses the envelope of §2.2, carrying the identifier below in `error_id`. Trigger conditions are not restated here — each one is specified beside the rule it guards in §2 and §3, and the evaluation order among them is the ladder of §2.8. Two framework-level rejections reuse `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at a status other than 400: a request to a path this service does not route answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 404, and a request using a method the addressed path does not support answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 405.
