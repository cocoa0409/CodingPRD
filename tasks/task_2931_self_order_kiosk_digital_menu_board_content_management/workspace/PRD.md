# Self-Order Kiosk Content Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A chain restaurant no longer merchandises from a printed menu board. Every price, every daypart layout, every combo suggestion and every allergen announcement now originates in a central content system and lands, minutes later, on a self-order kiosk or a digital menu board standing in a store that may be two thousand kilometres away and one price zone removed from the store next to it. The operational cost of getting this wrong is immediate and visible: a board showing a stale price while the kiosk charges a new one, a kiosk suggesting an item the kitchen has just 86'd, a screen-reader user who reaches a tile with no alternative text, or a payment terminal whose PCI attestation lapsed overnight and is still taking cards.

This document specifies the **Self-Order Kiosk Content Ops API**, the service that governs that pipeline end to end. It carries nine concerns that chain operators today handle in nine disconnected spreadsheets: daypart content scheduling, store clustering with content inheritance, local price zones with psychological rounding, a segregation-of-duties price change approval workflow, item availability synchronised from store inventory and 86 lists, kiosk upsell and combo suggestion configuration, kiosk accessibility requirements including screen-reader copy and contrast measurement, hardware health monitoring with offline caching and failover, promotional campaign versioning with strict window enforcement, and payment-device compliance with a receipt and order-number handoff to the kitchen. All of it is mounted under the URL prefix `/kiosk-ops`.

Two design commitments run through every rule below. The first is that **derived state is never supplied by a client** — a store's operating posture, a device's health band, an item's chain-wide availability and a bundle's price drift are all recomputed by the server from primary data, so an operator cannot assert that a broken kiosk is healthy. The second is that **content and money are traceable** — every price movement, every 86, every publish and every void is recorded in an append-only audit trail whose rows are never mutated and never deleted, because a chain that cannot reconstruct what a board displayed at 11:59 local time cannot defend the receipt it printed at 12:00.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, blank, or if `X-User-Role` names a role that is not one of the seven declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being addressed, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authoritative role list per endpoint is the fourth column of the table in §4.

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ContentManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, `PricingApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `StoreOperator` and `DeviceAgent`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is the chain-level operator. It owns the physical topology — price zones, stores, devices — and the logical clock, and acts as a break-glass operator permitted to drive any workflow action.
- A `ContentManager` owns menu content: content bundles, their daypart slots, promotional campaign versions and upsell rules. It may neither price items nor approve prices.
- A `PricingAnalyst` proposes zone price changes and applies them once they are approved. Segregation of duties forbids it from approving anything.
- A `PricingApprover` approves, rejects or returns price change requests, and may roll an applied change back. It may never approve a request it opened itself.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` owns accessibility audits and kiosk payment-device attestations and reads the audit trail. It authors neither content nor prices.
- A `StoreOperator` acts as exactly one store: inventory synchronisation, the 86 list, its own devices, and the kitchen handoff.
- A `DeviceAgent` acts as exactly one kiosk or menu board: it sends heartbeats, pulls cached content and captures orders.

Five of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ContentManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, `PricingApprover` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `requested_by_user_id` on a price change request, as an entry of `approval_user_ids`, or as `actor_user_id` on an audit row — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles carry an **entity-backed** identity, and this is what ties authorisation to ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a store compare the resource's store reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `StoreOperator` may address the store itself, that store's availability rows, that store's devices, and any order handoff captured by one of those devices; addressing a resource owned by a different store is rejected with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `DeviceAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Device`, and it must equal the device identifier named in the request path; a device acting as any device other than itself is rejected with `ERR_DEVICE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). This device-identity rule guards heartbeat reporting, cache synchronisation and order capture.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, collections are ordered by their creation timestamp ascending.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described above.

Collections also accept equality filters, and the accepted filter names are fixed per collection: price zones by `zone_code`; store clusters by `parent_cluster_id` and `tier`; stores by `cluster_id`, `price_zone_id` and `content_posture`; menu items by `category`, `accessibility_state` and `chainwide_availability_state`; price change requests by `menu_item_id`, `price_zone_id` and `state`; availability rows by `store_id`, `menu_item_id` and `availability_state`; campaign versions by `cluster_id`, `campaign_code` and `state`; upsell rules by `cluster_id`, `trigger_item_id`, `suggested_item_id` and `state`; content bundles by `cluster_id` and `state`; devices by `store_id`, `cached_bundle_id`, `device_kind` and `health_state`; order handoffs by `device_id`, `applied_campaign_version_id` and `handoff_state`; and audit rows by `subject_kind`, `subject_id` and `action`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All temporal reasoning — daypart resolution, campaign windows, 86 expiry, heartbeat arithmetic, attestation expiry, price effectivity — is evaluated against a single server-held **logical clock**. Before any request has moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That startup default is mandatory, and the server must never fall back to real time.

A `PlatformAdmin` may reposition the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant. This is a test-driving capability, and repositioning the clock is not a passive act: it re-derives every device's `consecutive_missed_heartbeats`, `rolling_health_score`, `worst_case_health_score` and `health_state`, then the `content_posture` of every store those devices belong to; it advances every promotional campaign version whose window has opened or closed; it releases every availability row whose automatic 86 hold the clock has now passed, running the full restore cascade for each; and it re-derives every device's `payment_compliance_state` from its stored attestation expiry. The response reports the resulting `current_utc` together with three counters: `devices_rescored`, `campaign_versions_advanced` and `availabilities_released`.

Two interval conventions are used throughout and they are deliberately different. Daypart slots are **half-open** in store-local minutes: a slot contains a minute when the minute is at or after `start_minute_of_day` and strictly before `end_minute_of_day`. Campaign activation windows are **closed** on both ends: a campaign version is live at exactly `window_start_utc`, still live at exactly `window_end_utc`, and expires only strictly after `window_end_utc`.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every resource except the audit trail carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, unique across the system.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was created.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. It is the value an optimistic-concurrency writer must echo.

Audit rows are the single exception. An audit row carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because it is written once and never touched again.

A derived field being recomputed is a mutation. When a price cascade flips a content bundle's staleness marker, when a heartbeat re-scores a device, or when an availability rollup changes a menu item's chain-wide state, the row that changed refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version` — which is exactly why an optimistic-concurrency writer that has been asleep across a cascade will be rejected.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers in **minor units** (cents). No amount is ever expressed as a decimal number, and no amount is ever negative. Rates are expressed in **basis points**, that is, hundredths of a percent, so a factor of one is 10000 basis points, five percent is 500 basis points and twenty percent is 2000 basis points; whenever a basis-point rate is applied to an amount, the amount is multiplied by the rate and divided by 10000.

Every division in this document rounds **half up** to the nearest whole minor unit, and every rounding step is applied exactly once, at the position the relevant rule names. A value that has already been rounded is never re-rounded, and a rounded value never feeds back into the input of the step that produced it. Where a rule sums several rounded components — the per-line discount of an order is the canonical case — the components are rounded first and the sum is taken afterwards; summing unrounded components and rounding the total once is a different number and is not the specified behaviour.

Health scores are integer points in the range 0 to 100 and require no rounding, because every weight and every penalty that contributes to them is an integer. Minute-of-day values are integers in the range 0 to 1439 inclusive; a value outside that range is a malformed request.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency, and both guard the **parent** row's `version`.

The first guards the pricing aggregate. Opening a price change request against a menu item requires the request body to carry `expected_menu_item_version`, holding the `version` of that menu item as the client last read it. The field is mandatory. If the value does not equal the menu item's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MENU_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because several open requests may legitimately coexist on one item — each targets a different price zone — and because a later request may re-price a zone an earlier request already applied; without the guard, a create carrying a stale view of the item would succeed and would silently compute its baseline from prices the analyst never saw. The same `expected_menu_item_version` guard, with the same error and status, protects a rewrite of a menu item's accessibility copy.

The second guards content editing. Appending a daypart slot to a content bundle requires the request body to carry `expected_content_bundle_version`, holding the `version` of that bundle as the client last read it. The field is mandatory. If the value does not equal the bundle's current `version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTENT_BUNDLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two non-overlapping slots may legitimately be appended to the same draft bundle, so an append carrying a stale version would otherwise succeed while the editor's view of the existing slot geometry — and of any staleness cascade that has already touched the bundle — is out of date.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. An endpoint not named in this subsection performs no concurrency check, and a version field sent to it is not part of its contract.

### 2.8. Addressing, Cluster Inheritance and Path Resolution

Resource addressing is deliberately shallow: almost every resource is reachable directly by its own identifier, because in this domain no resource is reachable by exactly one path — content descends a cluster chain while pricing arrives on an orthogonal price-zone axis. Where a path does carry identifiers, they are resolved **left to right**, and the first identifier that does not resolve to an existing row terminates the request with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Availability rows, for example, are created underneath a store, so a request naming an unknown store fails on the store before the body is examined at all. The path identifiers used by this API are `price_zone_id`, `cluster_id`, `store_id`, `menu_item_id`, `request_id`, `availability_id`, `version_id`, `rule_id`, `bundle_id`, `device_id` and `order_id`. Identifiers arriving in a request **body** are a separate concern: a foreign key that does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and this covers `price_zone_id`, `cluster_id`, `parent_cluster_id`, `store_id`, `menu_item_id`, `trigger_item_id`, `suggested_item_id`, `cached_bundle_id`, `applied_campaign_version_id`, the `menu_item_id` of every ordered line and every entry of `accepted_upsell_rule_ids`.

Store clusters form a **self-referential inheritance chain**. A cluster may name a `parent_cluster_id`, and content declared on an ancestor is inherited by every descendant. The chain of a store is the list beginning with the store's own cluster and continuing through its ancestors, **nearest first**, and it is used by daypart resolution, campaign selection, upsell eligibility and cache-sync validation alike. A chain may hold at most five clusters including the cluster itself, and it may never revisit a cluster it has already visited; an attempt to attach a parent that would close a cycle or push the chain beyond five clusters is rejected with `ERR_CLUSTER_CHAIN_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Store Posture

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row, and the cascades are specified as ordered chains: a link fires because the previous link changed something. Three chains recur, and each is spelled out in full where it is triggered — a price override reaching a menu item, an item becoming unavailable at a store, and a bundle being published or rolled back. Their shared shape is worth stating once: a change to pricing or availability reaches the **content bundles** that snapshotted the affected data, marks them drifted, and each such bundle refreshes `updated_at` and increments its `version`; the drift reaches every **device** whose cache holds one of those bundles, whose content dimension becomes stale and which likewise increments its own `version`; and each such device's change reaches its owning **store**, whose posture is then re-derived.

A store's `content_posture` is never accepted from a request body. It is one of `OPEN`, `DEGRADED` or `FROZEN` and is recomputed from the store's devices and its cluster chain whenever the store is read and after every device registration, heartbeat, cache synchronisation or payment attestation, every bundle publication or rollback, every price cascade and every clock movement. The evaluation order is pinned, and the tests are applied in exactly this sequence:

1. **The frozen test runs first.** The posture is `FROZEN` when the store's cluster inheritance chain resolves no content bundle in the `PUBLISHED` state, or when the store has at least one `KIOSK` device and every one of its `KIOSK` devices has a `health_state` of `OFFLINE` or `QUARANTINED`. A store whose posture is `FROZEN` refuses every kiosk order with `ERR_STORE_CONTENT_FROZEN`, and the transition into `FROZEN` is recorded on the audit trail with subject kind `STORE` and action `STORE_CONTENT_FROZEN` attributed to the system.
2. **The degraded test runs second.** With a published bundle still resolvable, the posture is `DEGRADED` when at least one device of the store has a stale content cache or a `DEGRADED` health band, or when at least one `KIOSK` device of the store has a `payment_compliance_state` of `UNATTESTED`, `EXPIRING` or `NON_COMPLIANT`. A `DEGRADED` store still permits kiosk orders, and its boards keep serving the content they already cached.
3. **Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.** That requires a published bundle on the chain, no device with a stale cache or a `DEGRADED` band, no `KIOSK` device in a non-compliant, expiring or unattested payment state, and — where the store has kiosks at all — at least one kiosk that is neither `OFFLINE` nor `QUARANTINED`. A store with no devices whatsoever therefore reads `OPEN` as soon as its chain resolves a published bundle, and reads `FROZEN` before that.

Because the frozen test is evaluated before the degraded test, a store that has simultaneously lost its published content and acquired a stale device cache reports `FROZEN`, not `DEGRADED`.

### 2.10. The Append-Only Audit Trail

Every consequential act in this system appends one audit row, and audit rows are immutable: they are never rewritten, never deleted, and never renumbered. Each row records `subject_kind` — one of `MENU_ITEM`, `PRICE_CHANGE_REQUEST`, `CONTENT_BUNDLE`, `DEVICE`, `ITEM_AVAILABILITY`, `CAMPAIGN_VERSION`, `ORDER_HANDOFF` or `STORE` — together with `subject_id`, which holds the `id` of the row of that kind and is a polymorphic reference rather than a declared foreign key. Alongside them the row carries `action`, an upper-snake token naming what happened; `actor_role`, the role of the request that produced the event, or the literal `system` for a clock-driven or cascade-driven transition; `actor_user_id`, the acting `X-User-ID`, or null for a system transition; `at_utc`, the logical-clock instant of the event; `before_value_cents` and `after_value_cents`, the monetary value before and after the change when the action moved money and null when it did not; `detail`, a short human-readable string carrying the justification, reason or finding token that motivated the event; and `sequence_no`, a 1-based counter assigned within each `subject_kind` and `subject_id` pair in creation order, which never skips a number.

The audit collection is readable by a `PlatformAdmin`, a `ComplianceOfficer` and a `PricingApprover`, and it is ordered by `sequence_no` ascending rather than by creation timestamp. The action tokens the system emits are `REQUEST_OPENED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_SUBMITTED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_APPROVAL_RECORDED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_APPROVED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_RESUBMITTED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_REJECTED`, `PRICE_REQUEST_WITHDRAWN`, `PRICE_OVERRIDE_APPLIED`, `PRICE_OVERRIDE_ROLLED_BACK`, `ACCESSIBILITY_COPY_UPDATED`, `BUNDLE_VALIDATED`, `BUNDLE_VALIDATION_FAILED`, `BUNDLE_ACCESSIBILITY_AUDITED`, `BUNDLE_PUBLISHED`, `BUNDLE_SUPERSEDED`, `BUNDLE_ROLLED_BACK`, `BUNDLE_REPUBLISHED`, `ITEM_EIGHTY_SIXED`, `ITEM_AUTO_EIGHTY_SIXED`, `ITEM_AUTO_86_LAPSED`, `ITEM_RESTORED`, `ITEM_DISCONTINUED`, `DEVICE_ATTESTED`, `DEVICE_NON_COMPLIANT`, `DEVICE_QUARANTINED`, `CAMPAIGN_SCHEDULED`, `CAMPAIGN_ACTIVATED`, `CAMPAIGN_EXPIRED`, `CAMPAIGN_WITHDRAWN`, `CAMPAIGN_SUPERSEDED`, `ORDER_FLUSHED_TO_KITCHEN`, `ORDER_ACCEPTED_BY_KITCHEN`, `ORDER_VOIDED` and `STORE_CONTENT_FROZEN`.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

Requests frequently violate more than one rule at once, and the response must be deterministic. The server therefore evaluates its checks in a fixed order and the **first** violated check determines the response; later violations are neither evaluated nor reported. The order is:

1. Authentication — the headers are present, non-blank and name a declared role → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list → `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Device self-identity — a `DeviceAgent`'s `X-User-ID` equals the device identifier in the path → `ERR_DEVICE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403). Applies to heartbeat reporting, cache synchronisation and order capture.
4. Store scope — a `StoreOperator`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the store that owns the addressed resource, whether that is the store itself, one of its availability rows, one of its devices, or the device that captured the addressed order → `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every `StoreOperator`-permitted endpoint that addresses a store-owned resource.
5. Path existence — every path identifier resolves, left to right → `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint with path identifiers.
6. Body reference existence — every foreign key in the body resolves → `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every creating endpoint that carries foreign keys.
7. Request shape — required fields present, enum values inside their declared domain, integers non-negative where declared non-negative, minute-of-day within 0 to 1439, ISO 8601 UTC instants parseable, every ordered quantity at least 1, and no unknown action verb → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
8. Natural-key uniqueness → `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (409), on price zone, cluster, store, menu item, device, campaign version and upsell rule creation.
9. Menu-item optimistic concurrency → `ERR_STALE_MENU_ITEM_VERSION` (412), on price change request creation and on an accessibility-copy rewrite.
10. Content-bundle optimistic concurrency → `ERR_STALE_CONTENT_BUNDLE_VERSION` (412), on a daypart slot append.
11. Terminality — the addressed row is not already in a terminal state → `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to every action endpoint. The terminal states are `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `ROLLED_BACK` for a price change request; `ROLLED_BACK` for a content bundle; `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` for a campaign version; a voided kitchen dimension for an order handoff; and a discontinued listing dimension for an availability row.
12. Apply-specific approval state — applying a price change requires the request to be `APPROVED`; a `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` request cannot be applied → `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` (409). This specialisation is evaluated **before** the generic transition check that follows it.
13. Declared transition — the requested action verb is a declared transition out of the row's current state → `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
14. Bundle publication — the bundle a device caches, or that a store's projection or order resolves, is `PUBLISHED` → `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
15. Store posture — the capturing device's store is not `FROZEN` → `ERR_STORE_CONTENT_FROZEN` (409).
16. Device operability — the capturing device's health band is `ONLINE` or `DEGRADED` and its content cache is not missing → `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (409).
17. Segregation of duties — the approving `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id` and from every identifier already in `approval_user_ids` → `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (403).
18. Payment-device compliance — a card-tendered order is captured on a device whose payment dimension is `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING` → `ERR_PAYMENT_DEVICE_NON_COMPLIANT` (422).
19. Accessibility sign-off — publication requires the bundle's accessibility audit to have passed → `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_AUDIT_REQUIRED` (422).
20. Item accessibility readiness — every snapshot item has non-blank screen-reader copy and a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 → `ERR_SCREEN_READER_TEXT_MISSING` (422), enforced on technical validation, on the accessibility audit and at publication.
21. Daypart geometry — a new slot's half-open interval does not overlap an existing slot of the same bundle and its start is strictly below its end → `ERR_DAYPART_SLOT_OVERLAP` (409).
22. Campaign stacking — scheduling a version whose closed window intersects that of another scheduled or active version in the same stacking group of the same cluster → `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
23. Upsell acyclicity — a rule must not close a cycle in its cluster's active suggestion graph, and its trigger and suggested items must differ → `ERR_UPSELL_CYCLE_DETECTED` (422).
24. Cluster chain integrity — a parent must not close a cycle and the resulting chain must not exceed five clusters → `ERR_CLUSTER_CHAIN_INVALID` (422).
25. Availability uniqueness — at most one availability row per store and menu item pair → `ERR_DUPLICATE_AVAILABILITY` (409).
26. Offline cache capacity → `ERR_OFFLINE_CACHE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on cache synchronisation and on order capture.
27. Device-kind support — only a menu board may omit printer hardware, a kiosk must declare its PIN-pad firmware version, and a payment attestation is refused for a menu board → `ERR_DEVICE_KIND_NOT_SUPPORTED` (422).
28. Price effectivity — a request's effective instant is not earlier than the clock at creation, and an apply is refused before the clock reaches it → `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (422).
29. Campaign window validity — scheduling is refused when the window end is at or before the clock, or at or before the window start → `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).
30. Store accessibility certification — the capturing store's certification instant is at or after the clock when a kiosk caches a bundle → `ERR_STORE_ACCESSIBILITY_LAPSED` (422).
31. Ordered item in snapshot — every ordered line's item is present in the pinned bundle's snapshot → `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_PUBLISHED_BUNDLE` (422).
32. Price zone applicability — a request's zone is used by at least one store, and an override may only be written for the zone named on the request → `ERR_PRICE_ZONE_MISMATCH` (422).
33. Upsell acceptance eligibility — every accepted rule is active, sits on the store's cluster chain, and has both its trigger and its suggested item in the cart → `ERR_UPSELL_RULE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
34. Item availability — no ordered line's availability row at the capturing store is unavailable → `ERR_ITEM_EIGHTY_SIXED` (409).
35. Approval count — applying requires at least as many recorded approvals as the request demands → `ERR_DUAL_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (409).
36. Zone uplift cap — the uplift computed from the raw proposed price does not exceed the zone's cap → `ERR_UPLIFT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).

Three consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Applying a price change to a request that is still `PENDING_APPROVAL` **and** short of its second approval returns `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED`, not `ERR_DUAL_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, because the approval-state specialisation sits at position 12 and the approval-count check at position 35. A card-tendered order captured on an unattested device for an item that is also missing from the pinned snapshot returns `ERR_PAYMENT_DEVICE_NON_COMPLIANT`, not `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_PUBLISHED_BUNDLE`. And a price change request that is both backdated and above its zone's uplift cap returns `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST`, not `ERR_UPLIFT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Chain Topology: Price Zones, Clusters and Stores

Two orthogonal axes organise the chain. **Price zones** carry money; **store clusters** carry content; and a store sits at the intersection of exactly one of each. Two stores in the same cluster may render identical boards at different prices, and two stores in the same price zone may render entirely different content.

A `PlatformAdmin` registers a price zone with a `zone_code` — a human-readable local zone code of 1 to 16 characters that must be unique chain-wide and is compared case-sensitively — and a `currency_code`, the ISO-4217 currency of every price in the zone, given as exactly three uppercase letters and defaulting to `USD` when omitted. The commercial parameters follow. `price_multiplier_bp` is the basis-point factor applied to a menu item's chain-wide list price to derive that item's zoned price, and it must be at least 5000 and at most 20000, so a zone may halve a price or double it but no more. `psychological_rounding` names the rounding style applied after the multiplier and is one of `NONE`, `NEAREST_05` or `NINE_ENDING`; it is optional at creation, and a zone created without it applies no psychological rounding at all, which is the behaviour of `NONE`. `tax_rate_bp` is the sales tax in basis points applied to the discounted net subtotal of every order placed in the zone, at least 0 and at most 2500. `max_uplift_bp` is the hard ceiling on the uplift any single price change request targeting the zone may carry, at least 0 and at most 10000.

A `PlatformAdmin` or a `ContentManager` creates a store cluster with a `cluster_code` of 1 to 24 characters, unique across all clusters, and a `tier` of `FLAGSHIP`, `STANDARD` or `EXPRESS`. The tier is recorded for reporting and feeds no calculation. A cluster created without a `parent_cluster_id` is a root cluster; one created with a `parent_cluster_id` inherits that cluster's content and, transitively, its ancestors'. Two derived numbers travel with a cluster. `chain_depth` counts the clusters on the inheritance chain including the cluster itself, so a root cluster has a chain depth of 1 and a cluster whose parent is a root has 2; the deepest legal chain is five clusters. `published_bundle_version` is the highest bundle version among the cluster's **own** content bundles that are currently `PUBLISHED`, and it reads 0 when the cluster has published nothing — note that this counts only the cluster's own bundles, not those it inherits.

Opening a store is a `PlatformAdmin` operation. A store is created with a `store_code` of 1 to 12 characters, unique chain-wide, which becomes the prefix of every kitchen order number the store ever prints; a `cluster_id` naming the content cluster whose published bundle drives its boards and kiosks; a `price_zone_id` naming its local price zone; a `timezone_offset_minutes`, the fixed offset from UTC used to convert the logical clock into the store-local minute of day for daypart resolution, which must be at least -720 and at most 840; and an `accessibility_certified_until_utc`, the instant through which the store's kiosk accessibility certification remains valid, inclusive.

Four further store fields are managed entirely by the server. `daily_order_seq_counter` is the store's monotonic per-UTC-date order counter, initialised to 0, incremented on every successfully captured order and **never** decremented — a voided order leaves a permanent gap in the day's numbering. `seq_counter_date` records the UTC calendar date, formatted as a `YYYY-MM-DD` string, that the counter belongs to; when the logical clock crosses into a later date the counter restarts at 0 for the new date. `open_order_count` counts the store's order handoffs that are still open, and `content_posture` is the derived operating posture specified in §2.9 that gates kiosk order creation. Reading a single store re-derives both `content_posture` and `open_order_count` before returning them.

### 3.2. The Menu Catalogue, Accessibility Copy and Zone Pricing

The **menu item** is the chain-wide product record. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `ContentManager` creates one with a `sku` of 1 to 24 characters that is unique chain-wide, a `display_name` of 1 to 80 characters as rendered on kiosks and boards, a `category` of `ENTREE`, `SIDE`, `DRINK` or `DESSERT`, and a `base_price_cents` — the chain-wide list price in minor units before any zone multiplier, rounding, override or campaign discount — of at least 1 and at most 100000.

Three optional inputs carry the item's accessibility profile. `allergen_tags` is an array of uppercase allergen tokens the kiosk screen reader announces; it defaults to an empty array and every element must be one of `GLUTEN`, `DAIRY`, `NUTS`, `SOY`, `EGG`, `FISH` or `SESAME`. `screen_reader_text` is the alternative text the screen reader announces for the item; it defaults to the empty string, may run to 240 characters, and an empty string means the item has no accessible description at all. `contrast_ratio` is the measured foreground-to-background contrast ratio of the item's board tile artwork, expressed to one decimal place between 0 and 21 inclusive and defaulting to 0.

From those two accessibility inputs the server derives `accessibility_state`, and the derivation order is pinned: the state is `READY` when the screen-reader text is non-blank **and** the contrast ratio is at least 4.5; it is `MISSING_ALT_TEXT` when the screen-reader text is blank, whatever the contrast ratio; and it is `CONTRAST_FAIL` in the remaining case, which is a non-blank text whose contrast ratio falls below 4.5. A blank text therefore reports the missing-text state even when the artwork also fails contrast. Either a `ContentManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may later replace both accessibility inputs in one write, supplying `expected_menu_item_version` alongside the new `screen_reader_text` and `contrast_ratio`. That write increments the item's `version` by 1, re-derives `accessibility_state`, resets the accessibility audit of every non-terminal content bundle whose snapshot contains the item back to `PENDING` so the change is re-reviewed before it can be published again, and records an audit row with subject kind `MENU_ITEM` and action `ACCESSIBILITY_COPY_UPDATED`.

Four derived pricing fields accompany every item. `zone_price_overrides` is an array of objects, each holding a `price_zone_id`, the overriding `price_cents`, the `applied_request_id` of the price change request that wrote the entry and the `applied_at_utc` instant at which it was written; it starts empty, holds at most one entry per price zone, and is written **only** by the application of an approved price change request. `zone_override_total_cents` is the sum of `price_cents` across every entry of that array, and reads 0 while the array is empty; it is the conserved quantity that makes a price rollback verifiable. `pending_price_request_count` counts the item's price change requests whose state is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. `pricing_lock_state` is `PENDING_CHANGE` when that count is at least 1 and `UNLOCKED` otherwise. A fifth derived field, `chainwide_availability_state`, rolls up the item's per-store availability and is specified in §3.6; a freshly created item has no availability rows and therefore reads `UNAVAILABLE`, with `zone_price_overrides` empty, `zone_override_total_cents` at 0, `pending_price_request_count` at 0 and `pricing_lock_state` at `UNLOCKED`.

**Deriving the effective zone price** of one item in one zone is the most reused calculation in the system, and it runs in two steps. The first step picks the basis. If the item carries a `zone_price_overrides` entry for that zone, the basis is that entry's `price_cents`. Otherwise the basis is the item's `base_price_cents` multiplied by the zone's `price_multiplier_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The second step applies the zone's `psychological_rounding` to the basis — **and it applies in both branches, an override included**. When the style is `NONE` the price is the basis unchanged. When the style is `NEAREST_05` the price is the basis divided by 5, rounded half up to a whole number, then multiplied by 5 again. When the style is `NINE_ENDING` the server takes the basis divided by 10 with any remainder discarded, multiplies that whole number of tens by 10 and adds 9; if the result is greater than or equal to the basis it is the price, and otherwise 10 is added to it. The multiplier's rounding and the psychological rule are each applied exactly once, and the psychological result is never fed back into the multiplier step.

This one calculation is consumed in three places, and two of those places deliberately disagree. When a content bundle is published, the price of every snapshot item in every price zone reachable from the cluster's stores is computed and frozen into the bundle. The **board display price** — the price a menu board renders and the projection returns — is read back out of that frozen snapshot, so a price change applied after publication does **not** move it. The **kiosk charge price** — the unit price of an order line — is recomputed from live item data at the moment the order is captured, so it diverges from the board display price for exactly those items whose zone price changed after the device's cached bundle was published. That divergence is intentional and observable, and the drift markers described in §3.4 exist to surface it.

### 3.3. The Price Change Approval Cycle

Changing what a store charges is never a direct edit. A `PricingAnalyst` opens a **price change request** naming the `menu_item_id` of the item and the single `price_zone_id` it re-prices, a `proposed_price_cents` between 1 and 100000 which is the raw proposal **before** any psychological rounding, a `justification` of 1 to 280 characters recorded verbatim on the audit trail, and an `effective_from_utc` instant at or after which an approved change may be applied. The request must also carry `expected_menu_item_version` as described in §2.7. A request whose `effective_from_utc` lies earlier than the logical clock at creation is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST`, and a request naming a price zone that no store actually uses is refused with `ERR_PRICE_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

At creation — and **only** at creation — the server computes three numbers in a pinned order, and none of them is ever re-derived afterwards. First, `baseline_price_cents` is the effective zone price of the item in that zone as of this moment, which means the existing override entry when one is present and the multiplier-and-psychological-rounding result when it is not. Second, `uplift_bp` is the basis-point uplift of the raw proposal over that baseline: the baseline is subtracted from `proposed_price_cents`, the difference is multiplied by 10000, the product is divided by `baseline_price_cents`, and the quotient is rounded half up to a whole basis point. A price cut yields a negative uplift. Third, `required_approvals` is 2 when `uplift_bp` reached 500 basis points — five percent — and 1 otherwise. Only then, against that same `uplift_bp`, does the server test the zone's ceiling: an uplift strictly greater than the zone's `max_uplift_bp` is refused with `ERR_UPLIFT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and the refusal is absolute — a proposal above the cap is never quietly clamped down to it. Because the uplift is taken from the raw proposal, the psychological rounding that will later be applied at apply time never influences the tier or the cap; deriving the uplift from the rounded price instead would reject proposals that this specification accepts.

A successful creation increments the item's `version` by 1, leaves the request in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, records the creating operator's `X-User-ID` as `requested_by_user_id`, pins `pinned_menu_item_version` to the item's new version, raises the item's `pending_price_request_count` by 1 and sets its `pricing_lock_state` to `PENDING_CHANGE`, and appends an audit row with subject kind `PRICE_CHANGE_REQUEST` and action `REQUEST_OPENED`. `approval_user_ids` starts as an empty array, `applied_price_cents` and `last_decision_comment` start null, and the request does not enter the approval queue until it is submitted.

The workflow itself is driven by an `action` verb, optionally accompanied by a `comment` and, on a resubmission, a revised `proposed_price_cents`. The verbs are `SUBMIT`, `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, `REQUEST_CHANGES`, `APPLY`, `WITHDRAW` and `ROLLBACK`, and the workflow **cycles**:

- **Submission.** The analyst that opened the request submits it, moving `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL`. Only the operator whose `X-User-ID` equals `requested_by_user_id` may submit; another operator's attempt is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`. Submission resets `approval_user_ids` to an empty array and appends `PRICE_REQUEST_SUBMITTED`.
- **Approval, first of two.** A `PricingApprover` approving a `PENDING_APPROVAL` request whose recorded approvals plus this one would still fall short of `required_approvals` leaves the request in `PENDING_APPROVAL` — the state machine self-loops — appends the approver's `X-User-ID` to `approval_user_ids` and records `PRICE_REQUEST_APPROVAL_RECORDED`.
- **Approval, the last one.** When the approver's `X-User-ID` appended to `approval_user_ids` brings the count to exactly `required_approvals`, the request moves `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, `pinned_menu_item_version` is re-pinned to the item's live `version` at that instant, and `PRICE_REQUEST_APPROVED` is recorded.
- **Segregation of duties.** In both approval branches the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id` and must not already appear in `approval_user_ids`; either violation is refused with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN`. A request needing two approvals therefore needs two genuinely different approvers, neither of whom is the analyst who wrote it.
- **Return for changes.** A `PricingApprover` other than the requester may return a `PENDING_APPROVAL` request, moving it to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, setting `last_decision_comment` from the supplied `comment`, clearing `approval_user_ids` back to an empty array and recording `PRICE_REQUEST_CHANGES_REQUESTED`. An attempt by the requester is refused with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN`.
- **Resubmission closes the cycle.** The original analyst submits a `CHANGES_REQUESTED` request again, moving it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → PENDING_APPROVAL`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1, clearing `approval_user_ids`, optionally replacing `proposed_price_cents`, and recording `PRICE_REQUEST_RESUBMITTED`. This loop may repeat without bound. Crucially, `baseline_price_cents`, `uplift_bp` and `required_approvals` are **never** re-derived across it: a request that needed two approvals in its first round still needs two in its fourth, and the baseline stays frozen at the price the analyst saw when the request was opened.
- **Rejection.** A `PricingApprover` other than the requester may reject a `PENDING_APPROVAL` request, moving it to the terminal `REJECTED` state. Rejection recomputes the item's `pending_price_request_count` and `pricing_lock_state`, increments the item's `version` by 1, and records `PRICE_REQUEST_REJECTED`.
- **Withdrawal.** The requesting analyst may withdraw the request from `DRAFT`, from `PENDING_APPROVAL` or from `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state, with the same recomputation of `pending_price_request_count`, `pricing_lock_state` and the item's `version`, and an audit row of `PRICE_REQUEST_WITHDRAWN`.

**Applying an approved change** is where the cascade lives. The request must be in `APPROVED` — an attempt on any other state is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` — the logical clock must have reached or passed `effective_from_utc`, otherwise the apply is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST`, and the item's live `version` must still equal `pinned_menu_item_version`, otherwise the apply is refused with `ERR_STALE_MENU_ITEM_VERSION`. Applying is available to the `PricingAnalyst` and, as a break-glass operator, to a `PlatformAdmin`; the system also applies an approved request of its own accord when the logical clock is moved to or past that request's `effective_from_utc`. The apply itself is an ordered chain of five links, each caused by the one before it:

1. `applied_price_cents` is set to the raw `proposed_price_cents` after the request's price zone `psychological_rounding` has been applied to it. This is the only moment psychological rounding touches a price change request.
2. Because that price now exists, the item's `zone_price_overrides` array receives — or replaces — the entry for the request's price zone, holding that `price_zone_id`, the `applied_price_cents` as its `price_cents`, the request's own identifier as `applied_request_id` and the logical clock as `applied_at_utc`. An override may only ever be written for the zone named on the request; writing one for another zone is a `ERR_PRICE_ZONE_MISMATCH` violation. The item's `zone_override_total_cents`, `pending_price_request_count` and `pricing_lock_state` are recomputed and its `version` increments by 1. The request moves `APPROVED → APPLIED`.
3. Because the item's price moved, every content bundle that is `PUBLISHED` and whose cluster owns at least one store in that price zone recomputes its live price checksum, raises its drift marker to true, and increments its own `version` by 1.
4. Because those bundles are now drifted, every device whose cache holds one of them has its content dimension set to `STALE` and increments its own `version` by 1.
5. Because those devices changed, every store owning one of them re-derives its `content_posture`, which is what turns an `OPEN` store into a `DEGRADED` one until its devices re-sync.

The apply appends an audit row with subject kind `MENU_ITEM`, action `PRICE_OVERRIDE_APPLIED`, `before_value_cents` holding the request's `baseline_price_cents` and `after_value_cents` holding `applied_price_cents`.

**Rolling an applied change back** is the compensation, and it is a `PricingApprover` action. It requires the request to be `APPLIED` and requires the item's override entry for that zone to still name this request as its `applied_request_id` — if a later request has since overwritten the same zone's override, the rollback is refused, because reversing it would discard a price nobody asked to reverse. The rollback removes the override entry this request wrote, which restores the multiplier-and-rounding price for that zone, recomputes `zone_override_total_cents` and increments the item's `version` by 1. It then walks the same cascade in reverse: every `PUBLISHED` bundle the forward apply touched recomputes its live price checksum and its drift marker, which returns to false when no other drift remains; every device caching such a bundle recomputes its content dimension, returning to `FRESH` when the bundle is published, undrifted and version-matched; and every affected store re-derives its `content_posture`. The request lands in the terminal `ROLLED_BACK` state and an audit row records `PRICE_OVERRIDE_ROLLED_BACK`, with `before_value_cents` holding `applied_price_cents` and `after_value_cents` holding `baseline_price_cents`. Because `zone_override_total_cents` is the sum of live overrides, a rolled-back price is verifiable: the item's override total returns to exactly the value it held before the apply.

Every one of these verbs appends its audit row with the acting role, and every verb that carries a `comment` also stores it as `last_decision_comment`, so the most recent decision is always legible on the request itself.

### 3.4. Content Bundles: Slot Geometry, the Validation Fork and Publication

A **content bundle** is the unit of content that reaches hardware. It belongs to one cluster, and it carries the cluster's daypart slots, the frozen list of items those slots expose, and the frozen prices the boards display. A `ContentManager` opens a bundle by naming its `cluster_id` and, optionally, an `effective_from_utc` recording the intended go-live of the content; that instant is informational only and gates nothing. The new bundle starts in `DRAFT` with its `accessibility_audit_state` at `PENDING`, its `bundle_version` set to 1 more than the highest `bundle_version` among the cluster's existing bundles — 1 for the cluster's first bundle — its drift marker `stale_flag` false, and `daypart_slots`, `snapshot_item_ids`, `price_snapshot`, `validation_findings` and `accessibility_findings` all empty, `price_snapshot_checksum` and `live_price_checksum` at 0, and `published_at_utc` null.

Slots are appended one at a time by a `ContentManager`, each append carrying `expected_content_bundle_version` per §2.7. A slot declares a `daypart_code`, a `start_minute_of_day` and an `end_minute_of_day` in store-local minutes, a `layout_code` naming the board layout to render, a `buffer_minutes` count marking how long before the slot's end the kiosk should begin announcing the coming transition, and a `category_codes` array listing which menu categories the slot exposes. The interval is half-open, so a slot running from 600 to 900 covers minute 899 but not minute 900, and two slots of the same bundle must not overlap; an overlapping interval, or one whose start is not strictly below its end, is refused with `ERR_DAYPART_SLOT_OVERLAP`. A successful append increments the bundle's `version` by 1, adds the slot to `daypart_slots` with its own `created_at` so that the array remains ordered by creation ascending, and re-derives `snapshot_item_ids` as the identifier of every menu item whose `category` appears in the `category_codes` of at least one of the bundle's slots, ordered by item creation ascending. Because the content changed, the append also resets `accessibility_audit_state` to `PENDING` and returns a bundle that had already reached `VALIDATED` to `DRAFT`.

Publication is a **fork-join**: two branches advance independently, carry different side effects, and both must be complete before the bundle may go live. Either branch may run first, and running one never disturbs the other's fields.

The **technical branch** is driven by a `ContentManager` validating the bundle, which moves it `DRAFT → VALIDATING` and requires at least one slot in `daypart_slots` — a bundle with no slots cannot be validated. Validation recomputes `validation_findings`: it emits the token `EMPTY_CATEGORY:` followed by the category code for every category code appearing in a daypart slot for which no menu item exists, and the token `SUSPENDED_UPSELL_RULE:` followed by the rule identifier for every upsell rule of this cluster whose state is `SUSPENDED`. Validation also refuses to proceed with `ERR_SCREEN_READER_TEXT_MISSING` when any item in `snapshot_item_ids` has blank `screen_reader_text` or a `contrast_ratio` below 4.5. The system then immediately resolves the intermediate state in the same request: with an empty `validation_findings` the bundle moves `VALIDATING → VALIDATED` and records `BUNDLE_VALIDATED`; with at least one finding it falls back `VALIDATING → DRAFT` and records `BUNDLE_VALIDATION_FAILED` with the first finding token as the audit row's `detail`. Neither technical outcome touches any accessibility field.

The **accessibility branch** is driven by a `ComplianceOfficer` auditing the bundle and supplying an `audit_result`. It may run while the bundle is `DRAFT` or while it is `VALIDATED`, and in both cases it leaves the bundle's own state exactly where it found it — auditing a `VALIDATED` bundle does **not** send it back to `DRAFT`. The audit sets `accessibility_audit_state` to `PASSED` when the supplied outcome is a pass and to `FAILED` otherwise, replaces `accessibility_findings` with the supplied findings array, and records `BUNDLE_ACCESSIBILITY_AUDITED`. Like validation, the audit refuses with `ERR_SCREEN_READER_TEXT_MISSING` when a snapshot item's screen-reader copy is blank or its contrast ratio falls below 4.5.

**Publishing is the join**, performed by a `ContentManager`. The bundle must be in `VALIDATED` — any other state is an invalid transition — and its `accessibility_audit_state` must be `PASSED`, otherwise the publication is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_AUDIT_REQUIRED`. Every item in `snapshot_item_ids` must additionally have non-blank `screen_reader_text` and an `accessibility_state` of `READY`; an item that regressed since the audit is refused with `ERR_SCREEN_READER_TEXT_MISSING`. Publication then freezes the content:

- `snapshot_item_ids` is frozen to the identifier of every menu item whose `category` appears in at least one slot's `category_codes`, ordered by item creation ascending. Items created afterwards are absent from this bundle and remain invisible to kiosks until a newer bundle is published.
- `price_snapshot` is frozen to one object per snapshot item and price zone pair, each holding a `menu_item_id`, a `price_zone_id` and the `price_cents` that is the effective zone price of that pair computed at this instant. The zones included are exactly those referenced by at least one store of this cluster, and the array is ordered by item creation ascending and, within an item, by price zone creation ascending.
- `price_snapshot_checksum` is set to the sum of `price_cents` across that array, `live_price_checksum` is set to the same value, and `stale_flag` is set to false.
- `published_at_utc` is set to the logical clock, the bundle moves `VALIDATED → PUBLISHED`, and `BUNDLE_PUBLISHED` is recorded.
- The cluster's previously `PUBLISHED` bundle, if it had one, moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and records `BUNDLE_SUPERSEDED`. Because that bundle is no longer live, every device whose cache holds it has its content dimension set to `STALE`; and because those devices changed, every store of the cluster re-derives its `content_posture`. The cluster's `published_bundle_version` is recomputed.

Drift is what keeps a published bundle honest. `live_price_checksum` is the sum, over the same item-and-zone pairs the snapshot holds, of the effective zone price computed from **live** item data; it equals `price_snapshot_checksum` while the bundle is fresh. `stale_flag` is true whenever `live_price_checksum` differs from `price_snapshot_checksum`, and also whenever an upsell rule of the bundle's cluster has been suspended since publication. Reading a single bundle recomputes `live_price_checksum` from current pricing and raises `stale_flag` when the two checksums disagree, so drift is observable without waiting for a device to notice it.

**Rolling a published bundle back** is available to a `ContentManager` and to a `PlatformAdmin`, and it is the compensation for a bad publication. The bundle moves `PUBLISHED → ROLLED_BACK`, which is terminal for that bundle. The cluster's `SUPERSEDED` bundle with the highest `bundle_version`, if the cluster has one, is returned `SUPERSEDED → PUBLISHED` and its `stale_flag` is recomputed from live prices and from any remaining upsell-rule suspensions, recording `BUNDLE_REPUBLISHED`; note that supersession is therefore **not** a terminal state. Every device whose cache held the rolled-back bundle has its content dimension set to `STALE`, every store of the cluster re-derives its `content_posture` — reaching `FROZEN` when the rollback leaves the store's whole cluster chain without a single published bundle — and the cluster's `published_bundle_version` is recomputed to the republished bundle's version, or to 0 when nothing remains published. The rollback records `BUNDLE_ROLLED_BACK`.

### 3.5. Resolving the Daypart and Rendering the Store Projection

What a given store's screens should show at a given moment is a computed answer, and it is available as a single read for a `PlatformAdmin`, a `ContentManager`, a `PricingAnalyst`, a `ComplianceOfficer`, a `StoreOperator` or a `DeviceAgent`. The resolution proceeds in four stages.

**Stage one assembles the effective slot set by inheritance.** The server walks the store's cluster chain nearest first — the store's own cluster, then its parent, then its grandparent, up to five clusters in total. For each cluster in turn it takes that cluster's `PUBLISHED` bundle with the highest `bundle_version`, skipping clusters with no published bundle, and walks that bundle's `daypart_slots` in creation order, appending each slot whose `daypart_code` is not already in the accumulating set. A nearer cluster's slot therefore **shadows** an ancestor's slot bearing the same daypart code, while an ancestor's slot with a code the child never declared is additive. Only bundles in `PUBLISHED` contribute: `DRAFT`, `VALIDATING`, `VALIDATED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `ROLLED_BACK` bundles are excluded outright.

**Stage two computes the local minute of day.** The whole minutes elapsed in the current UTC day according to the logical clock are added to the store's `timezone_offset_minutes`, and the sum is reduced modulo 1440 so that it always lands in 0 to 1439 — a negative offset before dawn therefore wraps to the previous evening. The caller may override the result by supplying `at_minute_of_day`, which is what makes daypart behaviour testable without moving the clock. The value used is returned as `local_minute_of_day`.

**Stage three ranks the candidates.** A slot is a candidate when the resolved minute is at or after its `start_minute_of_day` and strictly before its `end_minute_of_day`. When no slot qualifies, the projection falls back: `daypart_code` is null, `layout_code` is the literal `STATIC_FALLBACK`, and `phase` is `FALLBACK`. Otherwise the winner is chosen by three tie-breakers applied in order: the **narrowest** window first, meaning the smallest difference between `end_minute_of_day` and `start_minute_of_day`; then the earliest `start_minute_of_day`; then the earliest slot creation. Because ancestor slots may legitimately overlap a child's slot with a different code, this ranking is what makes the answer deterministic. The winner's `daypart_code` and `layout_code` are returned, and `phase` is `TRANSITIONING` when the resolved minute is at or after the winner's `end_minute_of_day` minus its `buffer_minutes`, and `ACTIVE` otherwise.

**Stage four assembles the rest of the projection.** `resolved_bundle_id` and `resolved_bundle_version` identify the bundle the store's own position on the chain resolves to. The `items` array carries each snapshot item with its board display price read out of that bundle's frozen `price_snapshot` — never recomputed live — together with its per-store availability. `stale_prices` is true exactly when the resolved bundle's `stale_flag` is set, which is the operator's warning that kiosks will charge something other than what the board shows. `selected_campaign_version_id` names the single campaign version selected for this store at this instant per §3.7, or null when none is live. `suggested_upsell_rule_ids` carries the ranked suggestion list per §3.6, computed from the `cart_item_ids` the caller supplies; when the caller also supplies a `device_id`, that device's screen-reader flag determines the size of the suggestion cap.

### 3.6. Store Inventory, the 86 List and Suggestion Rules

Availability is tracked per store and per item. A `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `StoreOperator` attaches an item to a store's inventory feed by naming the `menu_item_id`, the `on_hand_units` physically counted by the last inventory sync (0 to 100000), an optional `reserved_units` count of units already committed to open orders and therefore not sellable (0 to 100000, defaulting to 0), and the `par_units` target par level that serves as the denominator of the low-stock threshold (1 to 100000). At most one row may exist for a given store and item pair; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AVAILABILITY`. The new row derives its stock fields immediately, starts with a `listing_state` of `LISTED` and an `eighty_six_state` of `NONE`, sets `last_sync_utc` to the logical clock, and triggers a re-derivation of the item's `chainwide_availability_state`.

An availability row carries **two independent state dimensions** on one record. `supply_state` is the inventory dimension and is moved exclusively by inventory numbers. `listing_state` is the merchandising dimension and is moved by operator decisions, by the automatic 86 that a depletion triggers, and by the lapse of an automatic 86 on the clock. Neither dimension constrains the other, and `availability_state` is the single kiosk-facing rollup of both. The rollup is computed as follows, and it is recomputed on every write to the row, on every 86, restore or discontinuation, and on every clock movement:

- `available_units` is `on_hand_units` minus `reserved_units`, floored at 0 so it is never negative.
- The low-stock threshold is 20 percent of `par_units` — that is, `par_units` multiplied by 2000 basis points and divided by 10000 — rounded half up to a whole unit.
- `supply_state` is `DEPLETED` when `available_units` is 0 or below; `LOW` when it is at or below the threshold, the boundary being inclusive; and `IN_STOCK` otherwise.
- `availability_state` is `UNAVAILABLE` when `listing_state` is `EIGHTY_SIXED` or `DISCONTINUED`, or when `supply_state` is `DEPLETED`; `LIMITED` when `supply_state` is `LOW`; and `AVAILABLE` otherwise.
- The item's `chainwide_availability_state` then bubbles up over **every** availability row of that item, regardless of the owning store's posture and including discontinued rows, which still count as unavailable and are never removed: it is `UNAVAILABLE` when the item has no rows at all or when every row is unavailable; `LIMITED` when no row is available and at least one is limited; and `AVAILABLE` otherwise.

Four verbs drive the row, supplied as an `action`: `SYNC_ON_HAND`, `EIGHTY_SIX`, `RESTORE` and `DISCONTINUE`. An inventory sync, available to a `PlatformAdmin` and to the owning `StoreOperator`, overwrites `on_hand_units` and `reserved_units` with the values supplied, sets `last_sync_utc` to the logical clock, and re-derives the stock fields and the item's chain-wide rollup. A sync that leaves the sellable count strictly positive but at or below the threshold moves `IN_STOCK → LOW`; a sync that lifts it back above the threshold moves `DEPLETED → IN_STOCK` and — this is the point that catches implementations out — leaves `listing_state` **untouched**, so an automatic 86 survives restocking until it lapses or is explicitly restored.

A sync that drives the sellable count to 0 or below moves `LOW → DEPLETED` and fires the **auto-86 cascade**, five links deep:

1. `listing_state` is set to `EIGHTY_SIXED`, `eighty_six_state` to `AUTO_86`, `eighty_six_reason` to the token `INVENTORY_DEPLETED`, and `eighty_six_until_utc` to two hours after the logical clock; `availability_state` becomes `UNAVAILABLE`.
2. Because that row is unavailable, the item's `chainwide_availability_state` is recomputed.
3. If that rollup reached `UNAVAILABLE`, every upsell rule whose `suggested_item_id` is this item and whose state is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to the token `SUGGESTED_ITEM_UNAVAILABLE`.
4. Because rules were suspended, every `PUBLISHED` content bundle of each suspended rule's cluster raises its `stale_flag` to true and increments its `version` by 1.
5. Because those bundles drifted, every device caching one of them has its content dimension set to `STALE`, and each owning store re-derives its `content_posture`.

An audit row records `ITEM_AUTO_EIGHTY_SIXED` with subject kind `ITEM_AVAILABILITY`.

A `StoreOperator` may also 86 an item deliberately, and only for its own store — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the store that owns the row. A manual 86 requires the row's `listing_state` to be `LISTED`, sets `eighty_six_state` to `MANUAL_86`, takes `eighty_six_until_utc` from the supplied `until_utc`, records the supplied `eighty_six_reason`, drives `availability_state` to `UNAVAILABLE` and runs exactly the same five-link cascade, recording `ITEM_EIGHTY_SIXED`. **Restoring** reverses it: available to the owning `StoreOperator` on a row whose `listing_state` is `EIGHTY_SIXED`, it returns the listing to `LISTED`, sets `eighty_six_state` back to `NONE`, clears `eighty_six_until_utc` and `eighty_six_reason` to null, re-derives `availability_state` from `supply_state` alone and re-derives the item's chain-wide rollup. Every upsell rule suspended with the reason `SUGGESTED_ITEM_UNAVAILABLE` whose suggested item is no longer chain-wide unavailable returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspended_reason` cleared to null; every `PUBLISHED` bundle of the affected clusters recomputes `stale_flag`, which returns to false when neither price drift nor a remaining suspension justifies it; every device caching such a bundle recomputes its content dimension and every owning store its posture. The restore records `ITEM_RESTORED`.

The clock lapses an 86 only when the 86 was automatic. When the logical clock reaches or passes an `eighty_six_until_utc` on a row whose `eighty_six_state` is `AUTO_86`, the system runs the identical compensation chain and records `ITEM_AUTO_86_LAPSED` attributed to the system. A `MANUAL_86` is explicitly **not** lapsed by the clock, even long after its `until_utc` has passed — an operator's decision to pull an item stands until an operator reverses it. Reading a single availability row likewise re-derives `supply_state` and `availability_state` and expires an automatic hold the clock has passed.

Discontinuation, available to a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `StoreOperator` from either `LISTED` or `EIGHTY_SIXED`, is terminal: `listing_state` becomes `DISCONTINUED`, `availability_state` becomes `UNAVAILABLE` permanently, `eighty_six_state` returns to `NONE`, the item's chain-wide rollup is recomputed, suggested-item upsell rules are suspended exactly as in the 86 cascade, and `ITEM_DISCONTINUED` is recorded. A discontinued row can never be restored.

**Suggestion rules** are the other half of this subsection, because they live and die by availability. A `ContentManager` configures a rule with the `cluster_id` whose kiosks evaluate it, the `trigger_item_id` whose presence in the cart arms the suggestion, the `suggested_item_id` offered to the guest, a `rule_kind` of `UPSELL` or `COMBO_SUGGESTION` naming the presentation style, a `min_cart_subtotal_cents` giving the inclusive minimum gross cart subtotal before the rule is eligible (at least 0, defaulting to 0), a `suggestion_slot` of 1, 2 or 3 naming the screen slot the suggestion occupies, and a `max_prompt_seconds` between 1 and 60 capping how long the kiosk may hold the prompt. Because a screen-reader user must be able to hear the whole announcement before it disappears, `max_prompt_seconds` must be at least 5; a shorter value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Within one cluster the suggestion edges from trigger to suggested item must remain **acyclic**, and a rule may not point an item at itself; a rule that would close a cycle in its cluster's active suggestion graph, or whose trigger and suggested items are the same, is refused with `ERR_UPSELL_CYCLE_DETECTED`. Among a cluster's `ACTIVE` rules, the combination of cluster, `trigger_item_id` and `suggestion_slot` must be unique, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`. A new rule starts `ACTIVE` with `suspended_reason` null, and it is the cascades above — never an operator — that move it to `SUSPENDED` and back.

Selecting the suggestions a kiosk should display is deterministic. The cart subtotal is the sum, over the cart, of each item's kiosk charge price in the store's price zone multiplied by its quantity. A rule is eligible when its state is `ACTIVE`, its `cluster_id` lies on the store's cluster chain, its `trigger_item_id` is among the cart's items, its `suggested_item_id` is **not** among them, the suggested item's availability row **at this store** is either available or limited, the cart subtotal is at or above the rule's `min_cart_subtotal_cents`, and its `max_prompt_seconds` is at least 5. Suspended rules are excluded even when their suggested item has become available again but the reactivation cascade has not yet run, and because the availability filter is per store, the same rule is eligible at one store and not at another. Eligible rules are ordered by `suggestion_slot` ascending, then by the position of their cluster on the chain with the nearest cluster first, then by rule creation ascending. Finally the list is capped: a device reporting a working screen-reader subsystem receives at most 2 suggestions, and any other device receives at most 3. The result is the first entries of the ranked list up to that cap, and an empty list when nothing is eligible.

### 3.7. Promotional Campaign Versioning

Campaigns are versioned rather than edited. A `ContentManager` drafts a version with a `campaign_code` of 1 to 24 characters identifying the campaign that all of its versions share, a `version_no` of at least 1 that must be unique within that code, the `cluster_id` whose stores the version prices, a `discount_kind` of `PERCENT_OFF` or `AMOUNT_OFF`, a `discount_value` of at least 1 — read as basis points off each line when the kind is `PERCENT_OFF`, in which case it may not exceed 10000, and as whole cents off each line when the kind is `AMOUNT_OFF` — a `window_start_utc` and a `window_end_utc` bounding the closed activation window, a `stacking_group` of 1 to 24 characters naming the mutual-exclusion group, and a `priority_rank` between 1 and 999 that is the primary tie-break when several live versions compete for one order, lower winning. A draft whose `window_end_utc` is at or before its `window_start_utc` is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, as is one whose `window_end_utc` is at or before the logical clock. The version starts in `DRAFT` with `pinned_bundle_version` null.

Scheduling, driven by an `action` of `SCHEDULE` from a `ContentManager` or a `PlatformAdmin`, is where the constraints bite. The version must be in `DRAFT`. Its cluster must already have published content — the cluster's `published_bundle_version` must be at least 1 — which is the cross-entity dependency that stops a campaign going live over an empty board. Its `window_end_utc` must be at or after the logical clock, otherwise the schedule is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. And no other version of the same cluster and the same `stacking_group` whose state is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` may have a closed window that intersects this one, because at most **one** version per cluster and stacking group may occupy any single instant; an intersection is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A successful schedule moves `DRAFT → SCHEDULED`, pins `pinned_bundle_version` to the cluster's `published_bundle_version` at that instant, moves every version sharing the same `campaign_code` with a lower `version_no` and a state of `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state, records `CAMPAIGN_SCHEDULED`, and then immediately evaluates activation in the same request.

Activation and expiry are clock-driven and the boundaries are closed. A `SCHEDULED` version moves to `ACTIVE` when the logical clock is at or after `window_start_utc` and at or before `window_end_utc`, recording `CAMPAIGN_ACTIVATED` attributed to the system. An `ACTIVE` version moves to the terminal `EXPIRED` state only once the clock advances **strictly** past `window_end_utc` — at exactly `window_end_utc` the version is still active and still discounts orders — recording `CAMPAIGN_EXPIRED`. A version that never activated expires the same way, moving `SCHEDULED → EXPIRED` when the clock passes its window end before it ever opened. A `ContentManager` or `PlatformAdmin` may withdraw a version from `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state and recording `CAMPAIGN_WITHDRAWN`; and a version is moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the system, recording `CAMPAIGN_SUPERSEDED`, whenever a higher `version_no` of the same campaign is scheduled while it is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`. Reading a single version re-evaluates its window against the clock before answering, so a scheduled version whose window has opened reads `ACTIVE` and an active version whose window has closed reads `EXPIRED`.

**Selecting the campaign that discounts a given order** — the same selection the projection reports — considers only versions whose state is `ACTIVE`, whose `cluster_id` lies on the store's cluster chain (so an ancestor cluster's campaign reaches a descendant's stores), and whose closed window contains the logical clock at both ends. Versions in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded even when their window contains the clock. When no version qualifies, the order carries no discount at all. When several qualify, exactly one wins, chosen by `priority_rank` ascending, then by `discount_value` descending, then by creation ascending — stacking is forbidden, and the winner's identifier is stored on the order as `applied_campaign_version_id`.

### 3.8. Kiosk Hardware: Heartbeats, Health Bands, Cache and Payment Attestation

A **device** is one piece of hardware standing in one store. A `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `StoreOperator` registers one with the `store_id` it is installed in, a `device_kind` of `KIOSK` or `MENU_BOARD`, a `device_code` of 1 to 16 characters that must be unique within that store — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` — and an `offline_cache_capacity` between 0 and 50 bounding how many cached-offline orders the device may hold before it must be flushed. A kiosk takes orders and holds a payment terminal, so it must declare its `pin_pad_firmware_version` at registration; a menu board only renders content and has neither printer nor touch panel, so it may omit that field, and a kiosk registered without it is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_KIND_NOT_SUPPORTED`. A new device starts with all four hardware flags — `printer_ok`, `touch_ok`, `screen_reader_ok` and `network_ok` — false, `last_heartbeat_utc` null, `consecutive_missed_heartbeats` at 0, `offline_order_count` at 0, `cached_bundle_id` null with `cached_bundle_version` at 0, `attestation_expiry_utc` null, `p2pe_certified` false, its health dimension `OFFLINE` and its content dimension `MISSING`. Its payment dimension starts `UNATTESTED` for a kiosk and `NOT_APPLICABLE` for a menu board. Registration also re-derives the owning store's `content_posture`.

A device carries **three independent state dimensions**, each held in its own field: its health band in `health_state`, its content-cache condition in `cache_state`, and its payment-attestation condition in `payment_compliance_state`. No dimension constrains another: a `QUARANTINED` device may be `COMPLIANT` and `FRESH`, and a `NON_COMPLIANT` device may be `ONLINE`. Wherever this section speaks of a device's health dimension, content dimension or payment dimension, it means `health_state`, `cache_state` and `payment_compliance_state` respectively.

**The health dimension** is scored, not asserted. A `DeviceAgent` acting as itself, or a `PlatformAdmin`, reports the four hardware channels as booleans. The score is built from four weights: the printer channel is worth 20 points, the touch panel 30, the screen-reader subsystem 25 and the network 25. A menu board is awarded the printer's 20 points and the touch panel's 30 points **unconditionally**, because it has neither; a kiosk earns each of them only when the corresponding flag is true. Both kinds earn the screen-reader points only when `screen_reader_ok` is true and the network points only when `network_ok` is true. The four components sum to the device's base score, which is at most 100.

Missed heartbeats then erode that base. Heartbeats are expected every 5 minutes, so the missed count is the whole minutes between `last_heartbeat_utc` and the logical clock divided by 5 with any remainder discarded; a device that has never reported has a missed count of 0. `rolling_health_score` is the base minus 15 points for each miss, where the total penalty is capped at 60 points and the result is floored at 0. `worst_case_health_score` is computed from the same base and the same data but charges **two additional** hypothetical misses, again with the 60-point penalty cap and the floor at 0. The two scores exist to answer different questions, and they deliberately disagree: the rolling score drives the health band, the store's posture and the order-capture gate, while the worst-case score is consulted for one decision only, quarantine.

The health band is then assigned in a **pinned order** of tests, and only the first matching clause applies:

1. A device whose `last_heartbeat_utc` is null is `OFFLINE`, whatever its flags would have scored.
2. Otherwise, a device whose missed count has reached 3 **and** whose `worst_case_health_score` is below 50 is `QUARANTINED`. Quarantine is only ever entered by the clock advancing the missed count, never by a heartbeat, and it appends an audit row with subject kind `DEVICE` and action `DEVICE_QUARANTINED` attributed to the system. Note the asymmetry this creates: a kiosk one interval late with a failed printer scores below 50 on the worst-case measure yet is not quarantined, because its missed count has not yet reached 3.
3. Otherwise, a device whose `rolling_health_score` is below 50 is `OFFLINE`.
4. Otherwise, a device whose `rolling_health_score` is below 80 is `DEGRADED`.
5. Otherwise the device is `ONLINE`.

A heartbeat sets `last_heartbeat_utc` to the logical clock, resets `consecutive_missed_heartbeats` to 0, recomputes both scores and the band, and re-derives the owning store's `content_posture`. A heartbeat therefore lifts a device out of `OFFLINE` or out of `QUARANTINED` to `ONLINE` in one step whenever the resulting rolling score reaches 80. Moving the clock does the opposite: it raises the missed count on every device and may walk `ONLINE → DEGRADED`, `DEGRADED → OFFLINE` or `ONLINE → QUARANTINED` without any device having said a word. Reading a single device recomputes `consecutive_missed_heartbeats`, `rolling_health_score`, `worst_case_health_score` and the health band against the clock before answering.

**The content dimension** is the offline cache. Pulling content, available to a `DeviceAgent` acting as itself, to a `PlatformAdmin` or to the owning `StoreOperator`, names the `bundle_id` to cache and may set `flush_offline_orders`. The named bundle must be `PUBLISHED` and its cluster must lie on the device's store's cluster inheritance chain; a bundle that is not published, or whose cluster is off that chain, is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. For a kiosk, the owning store's `accessibility_certified_until_utc` must be at or after the logical clock, otherwise the pull is refused with `ERR_STORE_ACCESSIBILITY_LAPSED` — a store whose kiosk accessibility certification has lapsed may not receive new content. A device asked to retain more distinct bundles than its `offline_cache_capacity` allows is refused with `ERR_OFFLINE_CACHE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful pull stores `cached_bundle_id` and `cached_bundle_version`, sets the content dimension to `FRESH` — or straight to `STALE` when the bundle's `stale_flag` is already true — and re-derives the owning store's `content_posture`. With `flush_offline_orders` true, every order this device is holding offline is released to the kitchen as described in §3.9 and `offline_order_count` is recomputed. The content dimension moves `FRESH → STALE` on the system's initiative whenever the cached bundle's `stale_flag` becomes true or the bundle leaves `PUBLISHED`, and it returns `STALE → FRESH` either when the device pulls the current published bundle again or when the system recomputes it after the drift has been compensated — which requires the cached bundle to be `PUBLISHED`, to have `stale_flag` false, and to have a `bundle_version` still equal to the device's `cached_bundle_version`.

**The payment dimension** is attested, not measured. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` records an attestation for a kiosk by supplying the terminal's `pin_pad_firmware_version`, a `p2pe_certified` boolean declaring whether point-to-point encryption is certified, and the `attestation_expiry_utc` instant at which the attestation lapses, inclusive. An attestation submitted for a menu board is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_KIND_NOT_SUPPORTED`; a menu board's payment dimension is `NOT_APPLICABLE` from registration and never moves. For a kiosk the resulting dimension is `COMPLIANT` when `p2pe_certified` is true and the expiry lies more than thirty days beyond the logical clock; `EXPIRING` when `p2pe_certified` is true and the expiry lies after the clock but within thirty days of it; and `NON_COMPLIANT` when `p2pe_certified` is false or the expiry is at or before the clock. The attestation stores the three supplied fields, re-derives the owning store's `content_posture` and records `DEVICE_ATTESTED`; a device arriving in `NON_COMPLIANT` records `DEVICE_NON_COMPLIANT`. The same three bands are re-derived from `attestation_expiry_utc` whenever the device is read and whenever the clock moves, so a `COMPLIANT` kiosk drifts to `EXPIRING` as the thirty-day horizon closes in and then to `NON_COMPLIANT` once the expiry passes, with no request touching it. A fresh attestation restores a `NON_COMPLIANT` kiosk to `COMPLIANT` under the same conditions as the first one.

### 3.9. Order Capture, Pricing and the Kitchen Handoff

An order is captured on a device, by a `DeviceAgent` acting as itself or by the owning `StoreOperator`. The payload carries `line_items`, an array of objects each holding a `menu_item_id` and a `qty` between 1 and 99, with at least one element and no repeated item; a `tender_kind` of `CARD`, `CASH` or `MOBILE_WALLET`; an optional `accepted_upsell_rule_ids` array naming the suggestion rules the guest accepted, defaulting to empty; and an optional `created_offline` flag, defaulting to false, that is true when the kiosk captured the order while its network was down.

Capture is heavily gated, and the gates are evaluated in the precedence order of §2.11. The device's cached bundle must be `PUBLISHED`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The store's `content_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or it is refused with `ERR_STORE_CONTENT_FROZEN`. The capturing device's health band must be `ONLINE` or `DEGRADED` — a `QUARANTINED` or `OFFLINE` device may not take orders — and its content dimension must not be `MISSING`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. A card-tendered order requires the device's payment dimension to be `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING`; an `UNATTESTED` or `NON_COMPLIANT` device is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_DEVICE_NON_COMPLIANT`, whereas a `CASH` order needs no attestation at all. A device already holding as many cached-offline orders as its `offline_cache_capacity` permits may not capture another offline order and is refused with `ERR_OFFLINE_CACHE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Every ordered `menu_item_id` must appear in the pinned bundle's `snapshot_item_ids`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_PUBLISHED_BUNDLE`. Every entry of `accepted_upsell_rule_ids` must be an `ACTIVE` rule of the store's cluster chain whose `trigger_item_id` **and** whose `suggested_item_id` are both in the cart — the guest having accepted it, the suggested item is expected to be there — or the capture is refused with `ERR_UPSELL_RULE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. And no ordered line's availability row at the capturing store may be `UNAVAILABLE`, or the capture is refused with `ERR_ITEM_EIGHTY_SIXED`.

**Pricing an order** proceeds line by line, in the order the lines arrived, and the evaluation order is pinned. First the single campaign version that discounts this order is selected per §3.7; there may be none. Then, for each line: `unit_price_cents` is the item's kiosk charge price in the store's price zone, recomputed from live item data as described in §3.2; `gross_line_cents` is that unit price multiplied by the line's `qty`; and `line_discount_cents` is 0 when no campaign was selected, the gross line amount multiplied by the campaign's `discount_value` basis points and divided by 10000 and then rounded half up when the campaign's kind is `PERCENT_OFF`, and the smaller of the campaign's `discount_value` multiplied by the line's `qty` and the gross line amount when the kind is `AMOUNT_OFF` — a per-unit cash discount is capped at the line it discounts and can never push a line negative. The three values are stored on the line in `priced_lines`, alongside its `menu_item_id` and `qty`, in the same order as `line_items`.

The order totals then follow. `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `gross_line_cents` across the priced lines. `discount_cents` is the sum of the **already-rounded** `line_discount_cents` across those lines — the rounding happens once per line and the sum is taken afterwards, so an implementation that sums the unrounded line discounts and rounds the total once will report a different figure and is wrong. `tax_cents` is the net amount — `subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents` — multiplied by the store's price zone `tax_rate_bp` and divided by 10000, rounded half up. `total_cents` is that same net amount plus `tax_cents`. A two-line order therefore performs exactly three roundings: one per line for the discounts, and one for the tax.

**Numbering and the receipt** are allocated next. The capturing device's store owns the sequence. If the store's `seq_counter_date` is not the UTC calendar date of the logical clock, `daily_order_seq_counter` is reset to 0 and `seq_counter_date` is set to that date. The next sequence number is the counter plus 1, `order_number` is the store's `store_code` followed by a hyphen and that sequence zero-padded to exactly four digits, and the counter is then advanced to the new value. `pinned_bundle_version` is set to the capturing device's `cached_bundle_version`, so the receipt is reproducible from that bundle alone. `receipt_hash` is the lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256 of a canonical string formed by joining, with a vertical bar between each pair and in exactly this order, the `order_number`, `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents`, `tax_cents`, `total_cents` and `pinned_bundle_version`. `daypart_code` is the daypart the store resolves at capture time per §3.5, and it is null when the resolved slot set was empty and the store fell back to the static layout.

Capture then commits the rest of its side effects. `payment_state` starts `AUTHORIZED`. `handoff_state` starts `QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN` for an online capture and `CACHED_OFFLINE` when `created_offline` is true, in which case the device's `offline_order_count` also rises. Each ordered item's availability row at that store has its `reserved_units` increased by the line's `qty`, after which `available_units`, `supply_state` and `availability_state` are re-derived — which is how a large order can push a row into `LOW` or trigger the auto-86 cascade of §3.6. The store's `open_order_count`, the count of its devices' orders whose kitchen dimension is `QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN`, `ACCEPTED` or `CACHED_OFFLINE`, rises accordingly.

Like an availability row, an order carries **two parallel dimensions**: `payment_state`, which is `AUTHORIZED`, `CAPTURED` or `VOIDED`, and `handoff_state`, which is `QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN`, `CACHED_OFFLINE`, `ACCEPTED` or `VOIDED`. Different actions move different dimensions. A **flush** — the release of offline orders that accompanies a cache pull with `flush_offline_orders` true — moves the kitchen dimension `CACHED_OFFLINE → QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN`, recomputes the device's `offline_order_count`, leaves `payment_state` untouched at `AUTHORIZED`, and records `ORDER_FLUSHED_TO_KITCHEN` with subject kind `ORDER_HANDOFF`. A **kitchen acknowledgement**, driven by the `StoreOperator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the store of the capturing device, moves the kitchen dimension `QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN → ACCEPTED` and, in the same request, the payment dimension `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED`; both dimensions move together here. The store's `open_order_count` is unchanged by an acknowledgement, because queued and accepted orders both count as open. The audit row records `ORDER_ACCEPTED_BY_KITCHEN`.

A **void** is the compensation, available to a `StoreOperator` or a `PlatformAdmin` with an optional `void_reason`, and it may be taken from `QUEUED_TO_KITCHEN`, from `CACHED_OFFLINE` or from `ACCEPTED`. It drives both dimensions to `VOIDED`, which is terminal for the order. Every priced line's `qty` is released from its availability row's `reserved_units`, and `available_units`, `supply_state` and `availability_state` are re-derived so that the row returns to **exactly** the values it held before the order was captured. The store's `open_order_count` falls back to its pre-order value, and the device's `offline_order_count` is recomputed when the voided order had been held offline. One quantity is deliberately **not** compensated: the store's `daily_order_seq_counter` is never decremented, so the voided order's number is permanently retired and the day's numbering carries a visible gap. The audit row records `ORDER_VOIDED` with `before_value_cents` holding the order's `total_cents` and `after_value_cents` holding 0.

The Authorized Role(s) column lists the roles permitted to call the endpoint; a caller holding some other role is rejected per §2.1, and role membership alone is not sufficient where §3 additionally restricts the caller to a specific store, a specific device or a specific participant in a workflow.
