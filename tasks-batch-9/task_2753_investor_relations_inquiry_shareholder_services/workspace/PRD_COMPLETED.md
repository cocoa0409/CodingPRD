# Investor Relations & Shareholder Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A publicly listed issuer's investor relations desk sits on a fault line. On one side are shareholders, sell-side analysts and activist funds who all want more detail, faster, and who interpret silence as information. On the other side sits securities law, which makes the selective release of material non-public information to a favoured subset of the market a serious offence. Today that fault line is managed with spreadsheets, shared inboxes and the institutional memory of two or three people: someone remembers that the company is in a quiet period, someone else remembers that a particular guidance figure was withdrawn last Thursday, and the answer that goes out to a hedge fund analyst on Friday morning quotes a number that no longer exists in the public record.

This document specifies the Investor Relations & Shareholder Services API, a single system of record that makes those constraints mechanical instead of remembered. It covers the full desk workload: intake and routing of shareholder and analyst inquiries against a sensitivity score and a service-level deadline; authoring, disclosure review and dispatch of every outbound answer, with a hard boundary against selective disclosure; maintenance of the shareholder register with ownership analysis and outreach tiering; activist monitoring; conference and non-deal roadshow scheduling with prioritised meeting allocation; collection of sell-side consensus estimates and maintenance of the published guidance framework; and coordination with the transfer agent on record dates, dividend payments and shareholder record inquiries.

Two design commitments run through every requirement that follows. First, the disclosure boundary is a **gate, not a warning**: when the issuer's disclosure posture is closed, the operations that would push information into the market are refused outright, and the refusal is auditable. Second, every duty that could be abused by a single actor is split across two: the officer who reviews an answer may not be the analyst who wrote it, and the liaison who pays a dividend may not be the administrator who instructed it. Engineers implementing this specification should read the disclosure posture, the segregation-of-duties walls and the optimistic-concurrency checks as the load-bearing walls of the system; almost every other rule hangs off one of them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The whole API is mounted at the server root, so resource collections such as `/companies` and `/response-drafts` are top-level paths. A trailing slash is not significant: `/companies` and `/companies/` address the same collection. A path that matches no route at all, and a method the matched path does not support, are answered with the standard error envelope of §2.2 carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under the transport's own status code — 404 for the unmatched path and 405 for the unsupported method — and the role checks of §2.1 never run for such a request, so a missing header does not turn either of them into a 401.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear on the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is checked before authorisation, and both are checked before anything else about the request — including whether the request body parses.

The five roles are `IRAdmin`, `IRAnalyst`, `DisclosureOfficer`, `TransferAgentLiaison` and `Investor`.

An `IRAdmin` is the investor-relations operations lead: it registers issuers and contacts, maintains the share register, performs shareholder-servicing actions, schedules events, runs the allocation waterfall, releases allocated slots, submits transfer-agent instructions, and is the only role permitted to move the server's logical clock. An `IRAnalyst` staffs the IR desk: it routes inquiries, authors response drafts, revises them after review, and sends approved answers — but it can neither approve its own work nor override a computed routing queue. A `DisclosureOfficer` sits on the disclosure committee: it owns the disclosure record end to end (drafting, publication, withdrawal), decides every response-draft review, may override a routing queue, and services model access — but it can never author or send a draft. A `TransferAgentLiaison` is the transfer agent's counterpart inside the system: it acknowledges or rejects instructions, freezes the record-date holder snapshot, executes dividend payments and reverses them inside the reversal window; it may also submit instructions, in which case segregation of duties bars it from acknowledging or executing that same instruction. An `Investor` is an external shareholder or sell-side analyst acting as themselves.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `IRAdmin`, `IRAnalyst`, `DisclosureOfficer` or `TransferAgentLiaison`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a workflow calls for an audit stamp — as `author_user_id` and `reviewer_user_id` on a response draft, and as `submitted_by_user_id`, `acknowledged_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` on a transfer-agent instruction — and compares it to previously recorded stamps to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The `Investor` role is different: its identity is **entity-backed by a Contact**. When `X-User-Role` is `Investor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contact` row, and every ownership check compares that value against the contact the request touches. An `Investor` filing a consensus estimate or requesting a management meeting must supply its own `id` as the `contact_id` in the body; an `Investor` reading a single contact record, or listing the estimates filed under a contact, may only address the contact whose `id` equals its `X-User-ID`. Acting on another contact's behalf is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). This ownership check is evaluated after authentication, authorisation, request-shape validation and existence checks, and before optimistic-concurrency checks.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the decimal digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the server's logical clock (§2.4) rather than from the machine's wall clock, so every response carries the same value until an `IRAdmin` moves that clock, and before any client moves it every response reports `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes, and the 5xx envelope an unhandled internal fault would produce) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. Both are supplied as base-ten integer literals, and both bounds are enforced by refusal rather than by clamping: a value that does not parse as an integer, a `limit` below 1 or above 100, and a negative `offset` are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at the request-shape stage of §2.10. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is legal and simply yields an empty `data` array.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, ascending. Because the logical clock of §2.4 stands still between moves, rows created in the same clock position share a creation timestamp; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were inserted into the store, earliest insertion first, and never by identifier.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, window, staleness test and overlap comparison in this system reads a server-held **logical UTC clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the machine's real time. An `IRAdmin` may move it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response's `data` object carries the single field `current_utc` and nothing else, reporting the resulting clock in the three-fractional-digit form of §2.9. The move is unconditional and the clock is free to run **backwards**: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the clock's present position is accepted exactly as a later one is, so rows already written may afterwards carry a `created_at` that lies ahead of the clock. The envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` on that response is read after the move and therefore already reports the new position. Moving the clock mutates no entity and triggers no recomputation of any derived field.

That last sentence has teeth. Because the clock move is inert, a company whose earnings blackout window the clock has just entered remains at its previously stored disclosure posture until some later write re-derives it. This is deliberate: the stored posture is what every gate reads, so the system's behaviour is a function of what has been recorded, not of what a fresh calculation would say.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Version Semantics

Every resource in this system — issuers, contacts, disclosures, inquiry tickets, response drafts, estimate submissions, roadshow events, meeting requests and transfer-agent instructions alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted random (version 4) UUID, rendered in the canonical 36-character lower-case hyphenated form. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recording the logical clock's position when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row. At creation it is written equal to `created_at`.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`; the four properties are universal. A "mutation" includes the refresh of a derived field: when an issuer's `open_inquiry_count` or `disclosure_posture` is re-derived, when a roadshow event's counters are recomputed, or when a holder's `dividend_received_cents` is credited, that row's `updated_at` and `version` move even though the client never addressed the row directly. Cascades therefore leave a version trail on every row they touch, and because reviewers and estimate submitters pin versions (§2.6), a cascade on a row is observable to anyone holding a stale view of it.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations require the client to declare the version of a **parent** row it last observed. These are not general-purpose locks: only the three operations named below take a version field, and no other endpoint accepts one.

- Filing a consensus estimate requires `expected_contact_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the analyst `Contact` named by `contact_id`. If it does not equal that contact's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A fresh filing legitimately supersedes the analyst's previous accepted estimate for the same metric and period, so the only reason to refuse it is that the submitter's view of the analyst record has been overtaken.
- Recording a new share position requires `expected_company_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the issuer the holder sits under. If it does not equal that issuer's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMPANY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The resulting ownership percentage is only meaningful against the share count the client actually read, so an issuer row that has moved on invalidates the write.
- Deciding a response-draft review requires `expected_inquiry_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the draft's parent `InquiryTicket`. If it does not equal that inquiry's current `version`, the review is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INQUIRY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An inquiry may carry several successive drafts and several successive cascades; refusing a stale review prevents an officer from deciding against an inquiry state that an earlier decision or a disclosure cascade has already replaced.

In all three cases the field is a required input, not an optional one: omitting it is a request-shape failure, not a bypass.

### 2.7. The Disclosure Posture Gate

Each issuer carries a stored `disclosure_posture`, one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `BLACKOUT`. It is the system's single expression of "how freely may information leave the building right now", and five operations consult it: approving a response draft, sending an approved answer, granting model access to an analyst, running a roadshow allocation waterfall, and scoring an inquiry at intake. The first four are hard gates — when the stored posture is `BLACKOUT` they are refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT` (HTTP 409). The fifth is a soft input: the posture in force at intake raises the inquiry's sensitivity score rather than blocking the intake.

Posture is derived from two independent sources. The first is the issuer's scheduled earnings quiet period, delimited by `blackout_window_start` and `blackout_window_end`; the issuer is inside that window when a start instant has been configured, that start instant is at or before the logical clock, and the logical clock is strictly before the end instant. The window is therefore half-open: the instant equal to `blackout_window_start` is inside it, and the instant equal to `blackout_window_end` is outside it. When either bound is absent, no scheduled blackout exists. The second source is activist pressure, read from the `threat_level` of every contact of that issuer whose `activist_flag` is true; the threat level of a contact that is not flagged as an activist is ignored entirely.

The derivation itself is a three-way decision, evaluated top to bottom, first match winning. If the issuer is inside its blackout window, or if any flagged activist of that issuer carries threat level `CRITICAL`, the posture is `BLACKOUT`. Otherwise, if any flagged activist carries threat level `ELEVATED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Whenever the posture is re-derived, `posture_recomputed_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant.

`disclosure_posture` is a **stored** field, and only four events re-derive it: an operator-triggered posture recompute, a shareholder-servicing action that sets a threat level, a share-position update that automatically escalates a contact's threat level, and the fourth link of the disclosure-withdrawal cascade described in §3.4. Advancing the logical clock into or out of a blackout window does nothing on its own. A team that expects the gate to close by the passage of time alone will find the system still `OPEN`, and that observable staleness is intended behaviour rather than a defect.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties and Authorship

Two independent walls separate duties that must not be performed by one person, and both are enforced against `X-User-ID`.

The **review wall** protects the disclosure boundary. The `X-User-ID` recorded as a draft's `author_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` that decides that draft's review; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The wall runs in the other direction too, as an authorship rule: only the user recorded as `author_user_id` may submit that draft for review, revise it, or send it, and anyone else attempting one of those three actions is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_AUTHOR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

The **dividend wall** protects issuer cash. The `X-User-ID` recorded as a transfer-agent instruction's `submitted_by_user_id` may neither acknowledge nor execute that same instruction; both attempts are refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This matters because the `TransferAgentLiaison` role is permitted to submit instructions as well as to decide and execute them, so the wall — not the role list — is what keeps a single liaison from instructing and paying a dividend alone.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Money

Rounding in this system is always **round half up**, and the number of decimal places depends on the quantity:

- Ownership percentages (`ownership_pct`) are carried to 4 decimal places. Every tier comparison and every activist-stake threshold test uses the *rounded* percentage, never the raw quotient.
- Meeting priority scores (`provisional_priority_score` and `final_priority_score`) are carried to 2 decimal places, with the rounding applied exactly once, to the final product.
- Consensus means (`simple_mean`, `consensus_mean`) and the guidance band bounds (`band_low`, `band_high`) are carried to 4 decimal places; `dispersion_pct` is carried to 2 decimal places.
- All money in this system is held in **whole cents** as integers: `dividend_reserve_cents`, `dividend_paid_cents`, `dividend_received_cents`, `total_payout_cents` and each payout row's `amount_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored balance.
- A declared dividend rate (`declared_rate_cents_per_share`) is a rate, not a balance, and carries up to 4 decimal places of cents per share.
- All timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits, and that width is exact on output regardless of how many fractional digits the client supplied. On input a timestamp is accepted with a trailing `Z` (in either letter case) or with an explicit numeric UTC offset, and is normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared; a value carrying no zone marker at all is read as already being UTC. A value that is not a string, or that does not parse as ISO 8601, is a request-shape failure yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two instruction date fields `record_date` and `payable_date` (§3.9) are calendar dates rather than timestamps, and are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`.
- Counts and scores that the specification describes as integers — `sensitivity_score`, `open_inquiry_count`, `shares_held`, `shares_outstanding`, `snapshot_holder_count`, `snapshot_shares_total`, `revision_round`, `capacity_slots`, `slot_minutes`, and the roadshow-event counters — are computed with exact integer arithmetic and never rounded.
- Every fractional quantity named above is emitted in a JSON response as a bare JSON number, never as a string, and is never zero-padded out to its nominal scale: an `ownership_pct` rounded to 5.0000 is emitted as `5.0`, and a `final_priority_score` rounded to 42.00 is emitted as `42.0`. A field that holds no value is emitted as JSON `null`.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

A single malformed or ill-timed request commonly violates several rules at once, and the response must be deterministic. Checks therefore run in a fixed order, and the **first** check to fail decides the response; later checks are never reached. In ascending order of precedence:

1. Authentication — headers present and the role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Authorisation — the acting role appears on the endpoint's role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. Request shape — the body and query parameters parse; every required field is present and non-null; enum-typed values are members of their declared enumeration; numeric fields are non-negative; `shares_held` and `capacity_slots` are integers; `window_start` is strictly before `window_end`; `guidance_low` is less than or equal to `guidance_high`; and any reason-style string supplied is non-empty (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). Typing at this stage is strict and no coercion is attempted: a number-typed field must arrive as a JSON number and not as a numeric string such as `"12.5"`; an integer-typed field must arrive as a JSON integer and not as a float such as `5.0`; a boolean-typed field must arrive as a JSON `true` or `false` and not as `"true"` or `1`; a string-typed field must arrive as a JSON string, and is rejected when it is empty or consists only of whitespace. With exactly one exception in the whole system — the `cited_disclosure_id` of a response-draft revision, pinned in §3.6 — a field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, so a required field sent as `null` fails here and an optional one falls back to its documented default. A request whose body is absent or zero-length is read as the empty JSON object — an operation that needs no body therefore succeeds without one, and no `Content-Type` header is required — while a body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, fails here. Among query parameters, a filter supplied with an empty value is treated as though it had been omitted, and a boolean-valued filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` and `0`, in any letter case.
4. Existence of every resource named by a path segment, resolved left to right, the first missing segment deciding the error.
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, checked in body-field declaration order — `company_id` first, then `contact_id`, then `event_id`, then `inquiry_id`, then `cited_disclosure_id`, then `linked_prior_inquiry_id` — with the error being the not-found code of whichever referenced entity is missing.
6. Acting-identity ownership for the `Investor` role (`ERR_CONTACT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`).
7. Optimistic concurrency, per §2.6.
8. The addressed resource's own state precondition for the requested transition.
9. Cross-entity referential state — whether the rows this write joins are mutually compatible and in the states the write requires of them.
10. Segregation of duties, draft authorship, and the routing-queue override restriction.
11. Disclosure-boundary and access gates — blackout, selective disclosure, NDA, model-access eligibility, model access required, coverage status.
12. Uniqueness constraints.
13. Temporal validity — slot windows, slot durations, record and payable dates, and the reversal window.
14. Interval and cycle overlap.
15. The revision-round limit.
16. Aggregate and collective checks — summed holder positions, the presence of pending requests, the presence of eligible estimates.
17. Fiscal checks — instruction payability and reserve sufficiency, evaluated last of all.

Where two checks sit at the same level, they are mutually exclusive by construction — two different entity types, or two different not-found targets — so the shared level never creates ambiguity, with exactly four enumerated exceptions and no others. First, the two level-11 gates on a response-draft approval, blackout and selective disclosure, can both apply to the same request, and §3.6 pins the blackout gate as the one that answers. Second, the two level-9 cross-entity checks on a meeting-request creation, the parent event's openness and the contact-to-issuer match, can both apply, and `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS` is the one that answers. Third, the two level-13 slot checks on that same creation, window containment and slot duration, can both apply, and `ERR_SLOT_OUT_OF_WINDOW` is the one that answers. Fourth, the two level-13 date checks on a transfer-agent instruction submission, the record date not being in the past and the payable-date lag, can both apply, and `ERR_RECORD_DATE_IN_PAST` is the one that answers.

### 2.11. Stored Aggregates, Cascade Depth and Nested Path Resolution

Several fields in this system look like queries but are **stored** values maintained by an enumerated set of triggers: an issuer's `disclosure_posture`, `posture_recomputed_at` and `open_inquiry_count`; a roadshow event's `allocated_count`, `waitlisted_count`, `released_count`, `declined_count` and `request_ledger_total`; a contact's `ownership_pct`, `target_tier` and `dividend_received_cents`. Reads return the stored value. Gates evaluate the stored value. Nothing recomputes on read.

`open_inquiry_count` illustrates the pattern precisely. It is the number of that issuer's inquiry tickets whose status is `NEW`, `ROUTED`, `DRAFTING`, `AWAITING_APPROVAL` or `ESCALATED` — that is, every status except `ANSWERED`. It is refreshed by inquiry intake, by the dispatch of an approved answer, and by every cascade link that changes an inquiry's status, and each refresh is a **full recount** over the issuer's inquiries rather than an incremental adjustment. Ordering is irrelevant to the result, and no time window applies.

Every cascade in this system completes inside the request that triggered it — there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation. Cascades are also **transitive**: a link may be caused by the preceding link rather than by the original call, and the deepest chain in this specification runs four links deep, from a withdrawn disclosure through the drafts that cited it, through those drafts' inquiries, to those inquiries' issuers. When reasoning about which rows a single call mutates, follow the chain and not just the addressed row.

Two endpoints address a resource through a parent path — the computed consensus read under an issuer, and the estimate listing under a contact. In both, the path is resolved strictly left to right: the parent identifier is checked first, and a missing parent yields that parent's not-found error before the system looks at anything nested beneath it. The two nevertheless meet the request-shape stage of §2.10 differently, and the difference is observable: the consensus read resolves its issuer *before* it validates its own `metric`, `period_label` and `variant` query parameters, so a missing issuer yields `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` even when those parameters are absent or malformed, whereas the estimate listing validates its pagination and filter parameters *first*, so a malformed `limit` or filter yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the contact named by the path does not exist. This same left-to-right discipline governs the single-segment paths as well: the addressed row must exist before any state, ownership or business rule about it is evaluated.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Issuer

Everything in this system hangs off a **Company** — the registrant whose shares are held, whose guidance is published and whose transfer agent is instructed. An `IRAdmin` registers one by supplying a `ticker`, a `legal_name` and a `shares_outstanding` count. The ticker is the exchange symbol: between one and eight characters, none of which may be a lower-case letter — the test applied is that the supplied string equals its own upper-case form, so digits and any other characters left unchanged by case-folding are admitted alongside upper-case letters — and unique across every issuer already on file, compared as an exact string; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKER`. The legal name is the registrant name printed on shareholder communications and must be non-empty and no longer than 200 characters. The share count is the denominator of every ownership percentage the system will ever compute and must be strictly greater than zero.

Three further inputs are optional at registration. `dividend_reserve_cents` is the cash, in whole cents, available to fund dividend execution; when omitted it starts at zero, and it may never be negative. `blackout_window_start` and `blackout_window_end` delimit the scheduled earnings quiet period described in §2.7; when either is omitted it is stored as null, and a null start means the issuer has no scheduled blackout at all. No ordering constraint is imposed between the two — unlike the roadshow window of §3.8, an end instant equal to or earlier than the start is accepted at registration and simply yields a window no instant can ever fall inside. Registration also stamps the derived fields: `disclosure_posture` is derived immediately and `posture_recomputed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `open_inquiry_count` and `dividend_paid_cents` both begin at zero, and `version` begins at 1.

Reading a single issuer returns its stored posture alongside `open_inquiry_count`, `dividend_reserve_cents` and `dividend_paid_cents`; addressing an issuer that does not exist yields `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`. The issuer collection may be filtered by `ticker` or by `disclosure_posture` and is paginated per §2.3; `ticker` matches the stored symbol exactly and case-sensitively, and a `disclosure_posture` outside the three-member posture enumeration fails the request-shape stage of §2.10 rather than yielding an empty page.

Because posture is stored rather than queried, the system offers an operator-triggered refresh, available to an `IRAdmin` or a `DisclosureOfficer`. It is the only way to make a blackout window that the logical clock has since entered — or left — take effect without some other business write happening first. The refresh re-derives `disclosure_posture` and recounts `open_inquiry_count`, stamps `posture_recomputed_at` with the logical clock, and increments the issuer's `version` by 1. When the refresh lands on `BLACKOUT`, it does not stop at the issuer row: every response draft of that issuer sitting in `IN_REVIEW` whose `disclosure_class` is `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC` is forced to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `POSTURE_BLACKOUT`, and each of those drafts' inquiry tickets is rewound from `AWAITING_APPROVAL` to `DRAFTING` with a freshly computed `sla_due_at`. Every row touched by that chain has its own `version` incremented. A refresh that lands on `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` fires no such chain.

### 3.2. The Shareholder Register, Ownership Analysis and Activist Escalation

A **Contact** is one row on the issuer's register, and it deliberately carries two personalities on the same row, discriminated by `contact_type`: an `INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDER` or a `RETAIL_HOLDER` is a holder of record, while a `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST` is a covering analyst who holds nothing but submits estimates. An `IRAdmin` adds a contact by naming the issuer through its `company_id` and supplying `contact_type`, a `display_name` (non-empty, at most 160 characters, and the name printed on outreach lists) and an `email` whose only format rule is that it must contain an `@` character — no maximum length and no other test apply to it. Four inputs are optional: `shares_held` defaults to 0 and may never be negative; `nda_on_file` defaults to false; `coverage_status` defaults to `NOT_COVERING` and may otherwise be `INITIATED` or `SUSPENDED`; and `activist_flag` defaults to false. Three fields are seeded by the system and cannot be supplied at creation: `model_access_status` starts at `NONE`, `threat_level` starts at `NONE`, and `dividend_received_cents` starts at 0. A body that nevertheless carries one of those three is not refused for it: the supplied value is simply ignored, as is any other field the operation does not read. A `company_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`; addressing a contact that does not exist yields `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`. Adding a contact leaves the issuer row entirely untouched — no posture re-derivation, no `open_inquiry_count` recount and no increment of the issuer's `version` — even when the new row arrives with `activist_flag` true.

Ownership analysis is the reason the register exists. On creation, and again on every position update, the system divides the contact's `shares_held` by the issuer's `shares_outstanding` as stored at that moment, multiplies the quotient by 100, and rounds the result half up to 4 decimal places to obtain `ownership_pct`. From that rounded figure — never from the raw quotient — it derives the outreach tier stored in `target_tier`, evaluated top to bottom with the first matching clause winning: a percentage of at least 5.0 is `TIER_1`; otherwise a percentage of at least 1.0 is `TIER_2`; otherwise any percentage strictly greater than zero is `TIER_3`; and a percentage of exactly zero is `UNTIERED`. Both tier boundaries are inclusive on their low side, so exactly 1.0000 is `TIER_2` and exactly 5.0000 is `TIER_1`, while a holder of no shares is `UNTIERED` and never `TIER_3`.

Recording a new position is an `IRAdmin` operation that replaces `shares_held` outright and requires `expected_company_version` per §2.6. It is not restricted by `contact_type`: an analyst row's position may be recorded exactly as a holder's may, and that row's `ownership_pct` and `target_tier` are re-derived from it in the same way — the analyst's shares are merely left out of the collective sum below, both as the patched row's own contribution and as any other analyst row's. Individually plausible positions can still be collectively impossible, so after the replacement the system sums `shares_held` across every contact of that issuer whose `contact_type` is `INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDER` or `RETAIL_HOLDER` — analyst rows are excluded from the sum — and refuses the write with `ERR_SHARES_EXCEED_OUTSTANDING` if that total would exceed the issuer's `shares_outstanding`. A position write that survives every check re-derives `ownership_pct` and `target_tier`, then re-derives the issuer's `disclosure_posture` and stamps `posture_recomputed_at`, incrementing both the contact's `version` and the issuer's `version` by 1. The issuer's `open_inquiry_count` is deliberately **not** recounted here; only the operator-triggered refresh of §3.1, inquiry intake, the dispatch of an answer and the fourth link of §3.4's withdrawal cascade recount it.

Between those two steps sits the activist escalation rule, which is the only place in the system where a threat level rises without an operator asking for it. Once the patched contact's `ownership_pct` has been recomputed, if that contact's `activist_flag` is true, its rounded `ownership_pct` is at least 5.0 (the boundary being inclusive), and its current `threat_level` is either `NONE` or `WATCH`, then `threat_level` is raised to `ELEVATED` — and it is that raised value the subsequent posture re-derivation reads, so a stake crossing five percent can move the issuer from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` within the same request. There is no automatic de-escalation: a position sold back below five percent leaves `threat_level` exactly where the escalation left it, and only an explicit servicing action can lower it again. An escalation that lands the issuer in `BLACKOUT` — which happens when the posture re-derivation finds a `CRITICAL` activist or an active blackout window — carries the same downstream draft-blocking chain described in §3.1.

The contact collection is the targeting and ownership-analysis surface: it may be filtered by `company_id`, by `contact_type`, by `target_tier`, by `coverage_status` and by `activist_flag`, and it is paginated per §2.3. The `company_id` filter is matched as an exact string and is not existence-checked, so an identifier naming no issuer yields an empty page rather than `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.3. Shareholder Servicing: Model Access and Threat Assessment

A single servicing operation, open to an `IRAdmin` or a `DisclosureOfficer`, carries all three per-contact administrative actions, selected by the `action` field: `GRANT_MODEL_ACCESS`, `REVOKE_MODEL_ACCESS` and `SET_THREAT_LEVEL`. The first two move the contact's model-distribution dimension, tracked in `model_access_status`; the third moves the activist-monitoring dimension, tracked in `threat_level`. The two dimensions are genuinely independent and never gate each other: an analyst whose access is `REVOKED` can still be escalated to `CRITICAL`, and a contact already at `CRITICAL` can still be granted model access.

Granting model access moves `model_access_status` from `NONE` to `GRANTED`, and equally from `REVOKED` back to `GRANTED` — the re-grant closing a `GRANTED` → `REVOKED` → `GRANTED` cycle on that dimension. The grant carries no state precondition on `model_access_status` at all: granting to a contact already sitting at `GRANTED` is permitted, re-stores the same value, and still increments the contact's `version` by 1. Three preconditions guard both directions of the grant. The target's `contact_type` must be `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST`, because financial models go to covering analysts and to nobody else; a holder row is refused with `ERR_MODEL_ACCESS_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The target's `nda_on_file` must be true, because the model is confidential; a missing NDA is refused with `ERR_NDA_REQUIRED`. And the issuer's stored `disclosure_posture` must not be `BLACKOUT`, because distributing a model during a quiet period is exactly the selective disclosure this system exists to prevent; a blackout is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT`. A successful grant increments the contact's `version` by 1 and touches nothing else.

Revoking model access moves `model_access_status` from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED` and requires the current value to be exactly `GRANTED`; revoking an access that was never granted, or revoking twice, is refused with `ERR_MODEL_ACCESS_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Revocation is deliberately non-destructive to history: estimate submissions that analyst has already filed keep their `ACCEPTED` status untouched, but for as long as `model_access_status` remains `REVOKED` those rows fall out of the consensus eligible set described in §3.7 — so revoking access silently changes what the consensus read reports without deleting a single row. A successful revocation increments the contact's `version` by 1.

Setting a threat level is the third action, and it is the noisiest. The supplied `threat_level` must be one of `NONE`, `WATCH`, `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL`; anything else is a request-shape failure yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It is *required* only for this action, but it is validated against that enumeration whenever it is present at all, so a `threat_level` outside the enumeration sent alongside `GRANT_MODEL_ACCESS` or `REVOKE_MODEL_ACCESS` still fails the shape stage even though a well-formed one would simply be ignored by those two actions. Any threat level may be set from any other, in either direction, and this is the only mechanism by which a threat level can be lowered. The action then cascades in two further links. The first stores the supplied value and increments the contact's `version` by 1. The second, caused by the first, re-derives the issuer's `disclosure_posture` per §2.7, stamps `posture_recomputed_at`, and increments the issuer's `version` by 1; it fires unconditionally — even when the value supplied equals the value already stored, and even when the serviced contact's `activist_flag` is false so the re-derivation cannot possibly change the answer — and it re-derives posture only, leaving `open_inquiry_count` exactly as it stands. The third link fires only when that re-derivation lands on `BLACKOUT`: every response draft of that issuer in status `IN_REVIEW` whose `disclosure_class` is `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC` transitions to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `POSTURE_BLACKOUT`, and each such draft's inquiry ticket rewinds from `AWAITING_APPROVAL` to `DRAFTING` with `sla_due_at` recomputed — each affected row incrementing its own `version`. Escalating a flagged activist to `CRITICAL` therefore has a much larger blast radius than escalating them to `ELEVATED`, which only moves the posture to `RESTRICTED` and blocks nothing.

### 3.4. The Disclosure Record: Drafting, Publication, Supersession and Withdrawal

The **Disclosure** row is the system's model of the public record — the set of facts an IR analyst is permitted to repeat. A `DisclosureOfficer` owns it exclusively, and no other role may create, publish or withdraw one. Creation names the issuer through `company_id` and supplies a `disclosure_type`, which is one of `EARNINGS_RELEASE`, `GUIDANCE_UPDATE`, `PRESS_RELEASE` or `FORM_8K`, together with a `fact_key`: a stable, non-empty string of at most 80 characters that names the disclosed fact and that response drafts later cite. Guidance rows carry the guidance framework itself: `metric` is one of `REVENUE`, `EPS`, `FCF_MARGIN` or `NONE` and defaults to `NONE`; `period_label` is a label such as FY2026 and defaults to null; and `guidance_low` and `guidance_high` bound the guided range, both defaulting to null and both required when `disclosure_type` is `GUIDANCE_UPDATE`, with the low end required to be less than or equal to the high end. The `is_guidance` flag is not an operator's opinion but a derivation: it is true exactly when `disclosure_type` is `GUIDANCE_UPDATE` and `metric` is anything other than `NONE`, and it is that flag which selects the row into the consensus guidance band of §3.7. An `is_guidance` supplied in the creation body is nevertheless still read as a field before it is thrown away: it must arrive as a JSON `true` or `false` or the request fails the shape stage of §2.10, and a well-formed one is then discarded in favour of the derivation. The four guidance-framework fields are likewise not fenced off by `disclosure_type`: a `metric`, `period_label`, `guidance_low` or `guidance_high` supplied on an `EARNINGS_RELEASE`, `PRESS_RELEASE` or `FORM_8K` row is accepted, stored verbatim and echoed back on every read of that row, and the low-end-not-above-high-end check bites whenever both bounds are present whatever the `disclosure_type` — only the *requirement* that both bounds be present is specific to `GUIDANCE_UPDATE`. A new row is born in `DRAFT` with `published_at` null and `version` 1.

A fact key is unique per issuer, but only among the rows that are live: no two disclosures of the same issuer may share a `fact_key` while both are in `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACT_KEY`. The comparison is exact and case-sensitive, and the string is stored exactly as supplied with no trimming or case folding, so `FY26-REV` and `fy26-rev` are two different keys that may both be live at once. Once a row reaches `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` it releases its key, and the same key may be used again. Addressing a disclosure that does not exist — or citing one that does not exist — yields `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`. The collection is the lookup surface a draft author cites from and may be filtered by `company_id`, `status`, `disclosure_type`, `metric`, `period_label` and `is_guidance`. Filter values are matched by exact equality and none of them is existence-checked: a `company_id` naming no issuer at all is a perfectly legal filter that simply matches no row, so the response is an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0 rather than `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`. `period_label` is matched as an exact, case-sensitive string, and `is_guidance` is matched against the stored derivation rather than against `disclosure_type`.

Publication moves a disclosure `DRAFT` → `PUBLISHED`. Only a row currently in `DRAFT` may be published; anything else is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INVALID_STATE`. A guidance row faces one extra gate at this moment: if `disclosure_type` is `GUIDANCE_UPDATE`, then `metric` must not be `NONE`, `period_label` must not be null, both `guidance_low` and `guidance_high` must be present, and the low end must be less than or equal to the high end — a guidance row that fails any of these is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at publication time even though it was allowed to exist as a draft. That completeness gate reads stored fields rather than request fields, and it is therefore evaluated **after** the `DRAFT` state precondition rather than at the request-shape position of §2.10 that its error code would otherwise imply: an incomplete guidance row that has already reached `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INVALID_STATE` and never reaches the gate at all. A successful publication stamps `published_at` with the logical clock, sets `status` to `PUBLISHED` and increments the row's `version` by 1.

Publication is also the only producer of `SUPERSEDED`. When a guidance row is published, every *other* disclosure of the same issuer that is currently `PUBLISHED`, has `is_guidance` true, and carries the same `metric` and the same `period_label` transitions `PUBLISHED` → `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` incremented by 1. At most one published guidance row can therefore survive per issuer, metric and period. Supersession deliberately does **not** block anything downstream: an approved response draft citing the row it just superseded keeps its `APPROVED` status and keeps the now-stale `pinned_disclosure_version` it captured at approval, and the staleness surfaces only when the author tries to dispatch the answer, which is rejected with `ERR_PINNED_DISCLOSURE_STALE`.

Withdrawal is the destructive operation, and it takes a required non-empty `reason` string which is stored on the row as `withdrawal_reason` — a field that is null on every disclosure that has not been withdrawn. A disclosure may be withdrawn from `DRAFT` or from `PUBLISHED`; withdrawing one that is already `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INVALID_STATE`. Both origins produce `WITHDRAWN` and both increment the disclosure's `version` by 1, but their blast radii are deliberately different.

Withdrawing from `DRAFT` stops at the disclosure row. Nothing downstream can exist, because an unpublished row could never have been legitimately cited by an approved draft, so no draft, inquiry or issuer is touched.

Withdrawing from `PUBLISHED` fires a four-link transitive cascade, each link caused by the one before it rather than by the call itself. The first link sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, stores `withdrawal_reason`, and increments the disclosure's `version`. The second link, caused by the first, finds every response draft whose `cited_disclosure_id` points at this disclosure and whose status is `IN_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, and forces each to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `CITED_DISCLOSURE_WITHDRAWN` and its `version` incremented by 1; the `pinned_disclosure_version` on those drafts is deliberately left untouched, so the audit trail of what was pinned survives the block. The third link, caused by the second, takes each draft blocked in the second link whose inquiry ticket sits in `AWAITING_APPROVAL` and rewinds that inquiry `AWAITING_APPROVAL` → `DRAFTING`, recomputing `sla_due_at` from the logical clock and the service-level hours of the inquiry's current `routing_queue`, incrementing the inquiry's `version` by 1. The fourth link, caused by the third, takes the issuer of each inquiry touched in the third link and recounts `open_inquiry_count`, re-derives `disclosure_posture`, stamps `posture_recomputed_at` with the logical clock, and increments the issuer's `version` by 1. An issuer reached through several rewound inquiries is visited exactly once, so its `version` advances by 1 in total rather than once per inquiry. And the chain genuinely stops here: a fourth-link re-derivation that happens to land on `BLACKOUT` does **not** go on to fire the draft-blocking chain of §3.1, which is why the deepest cascade in this specification is four links and not five. Drafts already `SENT`, `DRAFT`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `BLOCKED` are not part of the second link and therefore contribute nothing to the chain.

### 3.5. Inquiry Intake, Sensitivity Scoring and Queue Routing

An **InquiryTicket** is a question from the market. Either an `IRAdmin` or an `IRAnalyst` intakes one on the inquirer's behalf, naming the issuer through `company_id` and the inquirer through `contact_id`, and supplying a `topic_category` — one of `DIVIDEND`, `RECORD_DATE`, `PROXY`, `GUIDANCE`, `MODEL_REQUEST` or `OTHER` — plus a `subject` line that must be non-empty and at most 200 characters. Two inputs are optional: `requests_nonpublic_detail` defaults to false and is set when the inquiry explicitly asks for detail beyond what has been published, and `linked_prior_inquiry_id` defaults to null and points at an earlier inquiry that this one follows up on, making the entity self-referential. A `contact_id` or `linked_prior_inquiry_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INQUIRY_NOT_FOUND` respectively, and a contact that exists but belongs to a different issuer than the one named is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_COMPANY_MISMATCH`. That issuer-consistency rule binds the `contact_id` only: no equivalent constraint binds `linked_prior_inquiry_id`, which is checked for existence and for nothing else, so a follow-up may legitimately name a prior ticket belonging to an entirely different issuer and will still draw the escalated-follow-up term below from it. A new ticket is born in status `NEW`, with `answered_at` null, and intake recounts the issuer's `open_inquiry_count` and increments the issuer's `version` by 1. The recount runs after the new ticket has been written, so the freshly created `NEW` ticket is itself inside the figure; and it is the only thing intake writes to the issuer row, because intake is not one of the four posture-re-deriving events of §2.7 — `disclosure_posture` and `posture_recomputed_at` are left exactly as they stand even though the issuer's `version` moves.

Intake computes three derived fields, and the first of them is **frozen forever**. `sensitivity_score` is a disclosure-risk score on a 0-to-100 scale, computed once at intake and never recomputed no matter what happens afterwards to the contact, the issuer or the linked inquiry. It is built by summing a base term with five conditional terms and then capping the total at 100. The base term comes from the topic: 40 for `GUIDANCE`, 30 for `MODEL_REQUEST`, 20 for `PROXY`, 15 for `DIVIDEND`, 15 for `RECORD_DATE` and 10 for `OTHER`. Add 25 when `requests_nonpublic_detail` is true, and nothing when it is false. Add 15 when the submitting contact's `contact_type` is `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST`, and nothing otherwise. Add 10 when the submitting contact's `activist_flag` is true. Add 10 when `linked_prior_inquiry_id` is not null **and** the inquiry it names is itself in status `ESCALATED` — a follow-up to a routine inquiry earns nothing. Finally add a posture term read from the issuer's `disclosure_posture` as stored at intake: 20 when it is `BLACKOUT`, 10 when it is `RESTRICTED`, and 0 when it is `OPEN`. The sum of the base and the five terms is then capped at 100, so a score can never exceed that ceiling. All of this arithmetic is on integers and nothing is rounded.

The second derived field is `routing_queue`, one of `SHAREHOLDER_SERVICES`, `IR_DESK`, `DISCLOSURE_COMMITTEE` or `TRANSFER_AGENT_DESK`. Selection walks four clauses top to bottom and the first match wins. If the `topic_category` is `DIVIDEND` or `RECORD_DATE` and the frozen `sensitivity_score` is strictly below 50, the queue is `TRANSFER_AGENT_DESK`. Otherwise, if the score is at least 60, the queue is `DISCLOSURE_COMMITTEE`. Otherwise, if the submitting contact's `contact_type` is `RETAIL_HOLDER`, the queue is `SHAREHOLDER_SERVICES`. Otherwise the queue is `IR_DESK`. Note the asymmetry of the two thresholds: the 50 boundary is exclusive, so a score of exactly 50 does not qualify for the transfer-agent desk, while the 60 boundary is inclusive, so a score of exactly 60 does reach the disclosure committee.

The third derived field is `sla_due_at`, the response deadline. Each queue carries its own service-level allowance, and the deadline is the logical clock at the moment of the write plus that allowance: 48 hours for `DISCLOSURE_COMMITTEE`, three days (72 hours) for `IR_DESK`, four days (96 hours) for `TRANSFER_AGENT_DESK` and five days (120 hours) for `SHAREHOLDER_SERVICES`. The allowance used is always the one belonging to the queue in force **after** the write completes, not before it — which matters whenever a routing decision changes the queue in the same request. The deadline is recomputed on intake, on routing, and on every cascade link that returns an inquiry to `DRAFTING`.

Routing is where the score earns its keep, and it is an automatic fork on a single operation available to an `IRAnalyst` or a `DisclosureOfficer`. A ticket in `NEW` whose frozen `sensitivity_score` is strictly below 80 transitions `NEW` → `ROUTED`. A ticket in `NEW` whose score is at least 80 instead transitions `NEW` → `ESCALATED`, and the branches diverge in their side effects: the escalated branch overrides `routing_queue` to `DISCLOSURE_COMMITTEE` regardless of what the four selection clauses computed at intake, and recomputes `sla_due_at` against the disclosure committee's 48-hour allowance. The escalating branch also outranks the queue override described next: a `DisclosureOfficer` who supplies a `target_queue` on a `NEW` ticket scoring at least 80 is not refused, but the supplied value is discarded unused and the ticket still lands in `ESCALATED` on `DISCLOSURE_COMMITTEE` with the 48-hour deadline. Escalation cannot be steered around, only undone afterwards by a re-route. Escalation does not change `open_inquiry_count`, because `NEW` and `ESCALATED` are both open statuses — and in fact neither branch of this operation writes to the issuer row at all, so no recount is performed and the issuer's `version` does not move. The routed branch leaves `routing_queue` and `sla_due_at` alone unless a queue override was supplied. Either way the ticket's `version` increments by 1.

The override is `target_queue`, and it is restricted by role rather than by endpoint. A `DisclosureOfficer` may supply any member of the queue enumeration, in which case `routing_queue` is replaced with the supplied value and `sla_due_at` is recomputed against that queue's allowance. An `IRAnalyst` supplying `target_queue` is refused with `ERR_QUEUE_OVERRIDE_FORBIDDEN` even though the routing operation itself is open to that role. The same restriction is what makes an escalated ticket recoverable: a ticket in `ESCALATED` transitions `ESCALATED` → `ROUTED` only when a `DisclosureOfficer` supplies `target_queue`, taking the supplied queue and a freshly computed deadline; an attempt to re-route an escalated ticket without a `DisclosureOfficer` supplying `target_queue` is likewise refused with `ERR_QUEUE_OVERRIDE_FORBIDDEN`. Routing a ticket that is in neither `NEW` nor `ESCALATED` is refused with `ERR_INQUIRY_INVALID_STATE`.

Inquiry tickets may be listed with filters on `company_id`, `contact_id`, `linked_prior_inquiry_id`, `status`, `routing_queue` and `topic_category` — each an exact-equality match, and none of them existence-checked, so an identifier-valued filter naming no issuer, contact or prior ticket yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than that entity's not-found error. A single ticket read exposes its score, queue, deadline and current `version` — the last of which is precisely the value a reviewer must pin under §2.6.

### 3.6. Authoring, Reviewing and Dispatching the Answer

Answering an inquiry is a four-station assembly line — author, review, revise, dispatch — and the **ResponseDraft** row is the workpiece moving along it. Only an `IRAnalyst` may open one, against an inquiry named by `inquiry_id`, supplying `body_text` (non-empty, at most 4000 characters, and the text that will actually be sent to the contact) and a `disclosure_class` that declares what kind of information the answer contains: `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` for material already in the public record, `GUIDANCE_REPEAT` for a restatement of published guidance, or `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC` for anything beyond it. Optionally the author names the published disclosure the draft repeats through `cited_disclosure_id`, which defaults to null when the draft cites nothing. At the authoring station that identifier is checked for existence only: the row it names may be in any of the four disclosure statuses, and it need not even belong to the same issuer as the inquiry being answered. Nothing about the citation's *quality* is judged here — the demand that it be `PUBLISHED` first bites when a `GUIDANCE_REPEAT` draft is submitted for review, and again at approval and at dispatch — so citing a `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` disclosure produces a draft that opens cleanly and can never be approved. The issuer of the cited row is never compared to the issuer of the inquiry at any station of the line, so a cross-issuer citation that happens to be `PUBLISHED` travels all the way to `SENT`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stamped as `author_user_id` and becomes the only user permitted to move the draft forward; `reviewer_user_id`, `reviewer_note`, `pinned_disclosure_version` and `blocked_reason` all start null, `status` starts at `DRAFT`, `revision_round` starts at 1, and `version` starts at 1.

Two rules guard the opening of a draft. The parent inquiry must be in `ROUTED` or in `DRAFTING`; any other status is refused with `ERR_INQUIRY_INVALID_STATE`. `DRAFTING` is admitted on purpose, so that a fresh answer can be authored after a previous draft was force-blocked by a cascade — `BLOCKED` is not an active status. What `DRAFTING` must not admit is a second live draft alongside a first, so an inquiry may hold at most one draft whose status is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, and a second one is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_DRAFT_EXISTS`. Opening a draft against an inquiry in `ROUTED` transitions that inquiry `ROUTED` → `DRAFTING` and increments its `version` by 1; opening one against an inquiry already in `DRAFTING` leaves the status where it is but still increments the inquiry's `version` by 1, because the new draft row is itself the write. Drafts may be listed with filters on `inquiry_id`, `cited_disclosure_id`, `author_user_id`, `status` and `disclosure_class` — this listing is the only discovery channel for a draft that a cascade has quietly moved to `BLOCKED` — and addressing a draft that does not exist yields `ERR_RESPONSE_DRAFT_NOT_FOUND`. As with every other collection in this specification, those filters are exact-equality matches and are not existence-checked: an `inquiry_id` or `cited_disclosure_id` that names nothing, or an `author_user_id` that has never authored anything, returns an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a not-found error, and a `cited_disclosure_id` filter never matches the drafts that cite nothing at all.

Submitting for review moves the draft `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW` and is available only to the author: another user is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_AUTHOR_MISMATCH`, and a draft in any status other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_INVALID_STATE`. One content rule is enforced at this station: a draft whose `disclosure_class` is `GUIDANCE_REPEAT` must carry a non-null `cited_disclosure_id` and the disclosure it names must be in status `PUBLISHED` at that moment; a missing citation or an unpublished one is refused with `ERR_CITATION_REQUIRED`. Despite the order in which this paragraph happens to name them, the three checks are evaluated in the §2.10 order — the draft's own state precondition first, then the citation rule as cross-entity referential state, then the authorship rule — so a user who is not the author submitting a `GUIDANCE_REPEAT` draft that cites nothing is told `ERR_CITATION_REQUIRED` and not `ERR_DRAFT_AUTHOR_MISMATCH`. A successful submission increments the draft's `version` by 1 and drives the parent inquiry `DRAFTING` → `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, incrementing the inquiry's `version` by 1.

Review is the `DisclosureOfficer`'s station and the point at which the two branches of this lifecycle diverge. It takes a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, the mandatory `expected_inquiry_version` of §2.6, and an optional `reviewer_note`. Three preconditions are shared by both branches: the draft must be in `IN_REVIEW` (otherwise `ERR_DRAFT_INVALID_STATE`), the supplied inquiry version must match the parent inquiry's current `version` (otherwise `ERR_STALE_INQUIRY_VERSION`), and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the draft's `author_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Both branches stamp the acting user as `reviewer_user_id` and increment both the draft's `version` and the parent inquiry's `version` by 1. `reviewer_note` is optional on the `APPROVE` branch, and when it is supplied there it is stored on the draft exactly as the other branch stores it; when it is omitted on that branch the draft's existing `reviewer_note` is left as it stands rather than being written back to null.

The `APPROVE` branch carries three further preconditions of its own, all of them disclosure-boundary rules. The issuer's stored `disclosure_posture` must not be `BLACKOUT`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT`. The draft's `disclosure_class` must not be `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC` — such a draft can *never* be approved, regardless of posture, regardless of role, and the refusal is `ERR_SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED`. And if `cited_disclosure_id` is not null, the disclosure it names must still be in status `PUBLISHED`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_CITATION_REQUIRED` — a requirement that applies to every `disclosure_class`, not only to `GUIDANCE_REPEAT`, because it is the citation's existence on the row and not the class that triggers it. These three are not evaluated in the order this paragraph lists them, and one of them is not even evaluated after the shared preconditions. The citation check is cross-entity referential state and therefore sits at level 9 of §2.10, *ahead* of the shared segregation-of-duties wall: an officer who is also the draft's author, approving a draft whose cited disclosure has since been withdrawn, is told `ERR_CITATION_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The blackout and selective-disclosure refusals are the level-11 gates, evaluated after that wall, and between those two the blackout gate is evaluated first — so a `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC` draft belonging to an issuer whose stored posture is `BLACKOUT` is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT`, not `ERR_SELECTIVE_DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED`. An approval that clears all of these moves the draft `IN_REVIEW` → `APPROVED` and captures the cited disclosure's current `version` into `pinned_disclosure_version`; when the draft cites nothing, `pinned_disclosure_version` stays null. The parent inquiry deliberately stays in `AWAITING_APPROVAL` — its version advances, but its status does not, because the answer has not yet gone out.

The `REQUEST_CHANGES` branch has one further requirement instead: `reviewer_note` must be supplied and non-empty, or the review is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Although the requirement belongs to only one branch, it is a rule about the request rather than about the rows, and it is evaluated at the request-shape stage of §2.10 — before the draft named by the path has even been resolved. A `REQUEST_CHANGES` review that omits `reviewer_note`, or supplies it as `null` or as a whitespace-only string, therefore yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the addressed draft does not exist, even when `expected_inquiry_version` is stale, and even when the draft is not in `IN_REVIEW`. The branch moves the draft `IN_REVIEW` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, stores the supplied note as `reviewer_note`, and rewinds the parent inquiry `AWAITING_APPROVAL` → `DRAFTING` with `sla_due_at` recomputed from the logical clock and the inquiry's current queue allowance. Nothing is pinned on this branch.

Revising is the author's station on the way back round, and it closes the `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `DRAFT` cycle. It accepts a draft in `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or in `BLOCKED` — any other status is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_INVALID_STATE` — and only from the recorded `author_user_id`, with anyone else refused by `ERR_DRAFT_AUTHOR_MISMATCH`. The cycle is bounded: a revision is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` once `revision_round` has already reached 3, so a draft that has consumed all three authoring rounds is dead and the inquiry needs a fresh draft instead. A permitted revision returns the draft to `DRAFT`, increases `revision_round` by 1, clears `blocked_reason` back to null, clears `reviewer_note` back to null, clears `pinned_disclosure_version` back to null, and replaces `body_text`, `disclosure_class` and `cited_disclosure_id` with whichever of those three the author supplied, leaving the others as they were. Those three fields do not all read "supplied" the same way, and this operation is the single place in the system where the §2.10 equivalence of an explicit JSON `null` with an omission is deliberately broken. `body_text` and `disclosure_class` follow the ordinary rule: supplying either as `null` is indistinguishable from omitting it and leaves the stored value untouched, so neither field can be cleared here — and a `body_text` that is present but empty or whitespace-only, or longer than 4000 characters, or a `disclosure_class` outside the enumeration, is still a request-shape failure. `cited_disclosure_id` is judged instead on the **presence of the key** in the request body: omitting the key leaves the existing citation exactly as it is, supplying an identifier replaces it (and that identifier is existence-checked, yielding `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND` when it names nothing), and supplying an explicit `null` **clears the citation back to null**. That is the only mechanism in this system for detaching a citation from a draft, and it is what lets an author abandon the reference to a disclosure that was withdrawn out from under them. Clearing it does not by itself unblock the answer: a draft whose `disclosure_class` is still `GUIDANCE_REPEAT` and whose citation has just been cleared is refused with `ERR_CITATION_REQUIRED` at its next submission for review, so the author must normally supply a replacement citation or a new `disclosure_class` in the same revision. The parent inquiry remains in `DRAFTING` throughout — revision touches only the draft's own `version`, recomputes no `sla_due_at`, and writes nothing to the issuer.

Dispatch is the author's final station and moves the draft `APPROVED` → `SENT`. It is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_INVALID_STATE` on a draft that is not `APPROVED`, with `ERR_DRAFT_AUTHOR_MISMATCH` when anyone but the author attempts it, and with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT` when the issuer's stored `disclosure_posture` is `BLACKOUT` — an answer approved before the quiet period began still cannot leave the building during it. The last gate is the pinned-version check: if the draft carries a non-null `cited_disclosure_id`, then that disclosure must still be in status `PUBLISHED` **and** its current `version` must still equal the draft's `pinned_disclosure_version`, or dispatch is refused with `ERR_PINNED_DISCLOSURE_STALE`. This is the serialisation point between the disclosure record and the answer queue: any republication that superseded the cited row, or any other change that advanced its version after approval, stops the answer at the door. A successful dispatch drives the parent inquiry `AWAITING_APPROVAL` → `ANSWERED` with `answered_at` stamped from the logical clock and the inquiry's `version` incremented, and then recounts the issuer's `open_inquiry_count` — `ANSWERED` being the one status outside the open set — incrementing the issuer's `version` by 1 as well. That recount is a count and nothing more: exactly like inquiry intake, dispatch is not one of the four posture-re-deriving events of §2.7, so the issuer's `disclosure_posture` and `posture_recomputed_at` survive the write unchanged even though its `version` moves. An issuer whose scheduled blackout window the logical clock has silently entered will therefore still read `OPEN` immediately after an answer has gone out through this very gate.

Two automatic transitions reach the draft from outside the assembly line, and neither is requested by a client. A draft in `IN_REVIEW` becomes `BLOCKED` when the disclosure it cites is withdrawn, or when a posture re-derivation lands the issuer in `BLACKOUT` while the draft's `disclosure_class` is `MATERIAL_NONPUBLIC`; `blocked_reason` records which cause applied, as `CITED_DISCLOSURE_WITHDRAWN` or `POSTURE_BLACKOUT` respectively. A draft in `APPROVED` becomes `BLOCKED` only for the first of those two causes — a withdrawal of its cited disclosure — with `blocked_reason` set to `CITED_DISCLOSURE_WITHDRAWN`. In both cases the parent inquiry is rewound `AWAITING_APPROVAL` → `DRAFTING` with `sla_due_at` recomputed, and each affected row's `version` is incremented. `SENT` is terminal and is never blocked.

### 3.7. Consensus Estimates and the Guidance Framework

A covering analyst files a point estimate as an **EstimateSubmission**, and this is the one operation reserved to the `Investor` role alone. The filer supplies its own contact identity as `contact_id`, a `metric` of `REVENUE`, `EPS` or `FCF_MARGIN`, a `period_label` of at most 20 non-empty characters, a strictly positive `value`, and the `expected_contact_version` of §2.6. The `value` carries no prescribed scale of its own: it is stored and echoed back at exactly the precision the filer supplied, and rounding enters only where the consensus arithmetic below rounds a computed figure. Because the `Investor` identity is entity-backed, a filer whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the supplied `contact_id` is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. The issuer-consistency rule of §3.5 — that a referenced contact must belong to the same issuer as any other issuer-scoped row the request references, refused with `ERR_CONTACT_COMPANY_MISMATCH` — bites on two operations: inquiry intake and meeting-request creation. Estimate filing names no issuer-scoped row beyond the contact itself, so the rule is vacuous there and this operation never raises `ERR_CONTACT_COMPANY_MISMATCH`.

Two eligibility gates sit on the filer itself. The filing contact's `model_access_status` must be `GRANTED`, because an analyst who has not been given the model has nothing to build an estimate on; otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_MODEL_ACCESS_REQUIRED`. And the filing contact must be a `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST` whose `coverage_status` is exactly `INITIATED`; an analyst who has not initiated coverage, one whose coverage is `SUSPENDED` or `NOT_COVERING`, or a holder row attempting to file at all, is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_INITIATED`.

A successful filing is born with `status` `ACCEPTED` and `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and it supersedes the filer's own history: any earlier submission by the same contact for the same `metric` and the same `period_label` that is still `ACCEPTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`. One accepted estimate per analyst, metric and period is therefore the invariant. Filing also increments the analyst contact's `version` by 1 — which is why a client that files twice in a row must re-read the contact between filings to obtain a fresh `expected_contact_version`. The full history, including superseded rows, is readable as a sub-resource listing under the analyst, filterable by `metric`, `period_label` and `status`.

The consensus itself is a **computed read**, not a stored row: it takes a required `metric` and `period_label`, plus an optional `variant`, and returns freshly calculated numbers without incrementing any version. `metric` must be `REVENUE`, `EPS` or `FCF_MARGIN` — the disclosure-only sentinel `NONE` is not admissible here — `period_label` is matched as an exact, case-sensitive string, and `variant` is `SIMPLE` or `ANCHORED`; a value outside any of those enumerations fails the request-shape stage of §2.10. It is available to an `IRAdmin`, an `IRAnalyst` or a `DisclosureOfficer`, and it proceeds in five pinned steps.

**Step one — the eligible set.** A submission is eligible when all of the following hold together: its own `status` is `ACCEPTED`; the contact that filed it belongs to the issuer being read; that contact's `contact_type` is `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST`; that contact's `coverage_status` is `INITIATED`; that contact's `model_access_status` is anything other than `REVOKED`; its `metric` equals the requested metric; its `period_label` equals the requested period; and its `submitted_at` is at or after the logical clock's current instant minus 90 days, the lower bound being inclusive. There is no upper bound on `submitted_at`: after the clock has been moved backwards (§2.4), a submission stamped ahead of the clock's present position remains eligible. The order in which those filters are applied is irrelevant, since the predicate is a conjunction. The size of the surviving set is reported as `eligible_count`. If the set is empty the whole read fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ESTIMATES` and no partial payload is returned.

**Step two — the simple mean.** Sum the `value` of every eligible submission, divide by the number of eligible submissions, and round the quotient half up to 4 decimal places. That figure is reported as `simple_mean`.

**Step three — the band anchor.** Look for the issuer's published guidance for this metric and period: the disclosure row belonging to this issuer whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose `is_guidance` is true, and whose `metric` and `period_label` match the request. At most one such row can exist, because publishing a guidance row supersedes its predecessor. If that row exists, `guidance_anchored` is true, `band_low` is `guidance_low` multiplied by 0.85 and rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and `band_high` is `guidance_high` multiplied by 1.15 and rounded half up to 4 decimal places. If no such row exists, `guidance_anchored` is false and the same fifteen-percent margins are applied to `simple_mean` instead: `band_low` is `simple_mean` multiplied by 0.85 and `band_high` is `simple_mean` multiplied by 1.15, each rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The band is computed exactly once, at this step, from these inputs. Trimming the set in the next step will change the mean, and the band is **never** recomputed from that changed mean — the apparent circularity is broken by this pinned evaluation order, and there is no iteration.

**Step four — the trim.** Keep every eligible submission whose `value` lies within the band, both bounds inclusive: a value exactly equal to `band_low` or exactly equal to `band_high` is kept. The number of eligible submissions not kept is reported as `outlier_excluded_count`. If nothing survives the trim, the anchored mean falls back to `simple_mean` and `trimmed_applied` is reported as false. Otherwise the anchored mean is the sum of the kept values divided by their count, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and `trimmed_applied` is reported as true.

**Step five — the reported numbers.** The `variant` query parameter selects which mean is reported as `consensus_mean`. When `variant` is `SIMPLE`, `consensus_mean` is the untrimmed `simple_mean` computed over the whole eligible set — outliers are still counted and still reported through `outlier_excluded_count`, they simply do not move the reported number. When `variant` is `ANCHORED`, or when it is omitted entirely (`ANCHORED` being the default), `consensus_mean` is the anchored mean from step four; for identical inputs the two variants diverge whenever `outlier_excluded_count` is greater than zero. Finally, `dispersion_pct` is the spread of the kept values relative to the reported consensus: the largest kept value minus the smallest kept value, divided by `consensus_mean`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. It is reported as null when nothing survived the trim or when `consensus_mean` is zero. The response also echoes `company_id`, `metric`, `period_label` and the effective `variant`.

### 3.8. Conferences, Roadshows and the Allocation Waterfall

Management time is the scarcest resource on an IR desk, and a **RoadshowEvent** is how it is rationed. An `IRAdmin` schedules one against an issuer named by `company_id`, declaring an `event_type` of `CONFERENCE` for a sponsored investor conference or `NDR` for a non-deal roadshow, a `venue_city` of at most 80 non-empty characters that is printed on the outreach plan, and the shape of the meeting grid: `window_start` is the earliest instant any slot may begin and `window_end`, which must be strictly later, is the latest instant any slot may end. `slot_minutes` fixes the exact length every slot must have and must be 20, 30, 45 or 60 — no other duration is admissible. That admissible set behaves as an enumeration and not as a temporal rule: an inadmissible `slot_minutes` on event creation is a request-shape failure yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at level 3 of §2.10, decided before the issuer named by `company_id` is existence-checked, and never `ERR_SLOT_DURATION_INVALID`, which belongs to the meeting-request slot rule alone. `capacity_slots` is how many meetings the management team can actually host and must be at least 1. A new event is born in `PLANNING`, with `allocated_count`, `waitlisted_count`, `released_count`, `declined_count` and `request_ledger_total` all at zero and `version` at 1. Events may be listed by `company_id`, `event_type` and `status`, each filter matching by exact equality against the stored value and none of them existence-checked: a `company_id` naming no issuer is a well-formed filter that simply matches nothing, so the listing returns an empty page with a `total_count` of zero rather than `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`. Addressing an event that does not exist yields `ERR_ROADSHOW_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Opening the event for business transitions it `PLANNING` → `OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS` and is refused with `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE` from any other status. It takes no body, increments the event's `version` by 1, and returns the updated event row in full — the same projection the single-event read returns.

A **MeetingRequest** is one investor's bid for a slot, and either the `Investor` themselves or an `IRAdmin` on their behalf may lodge one, naming the event through `event_id`, the requester through `contact_id`, and the wanted slot through `requested_slot_start` and `requested_slot_end`. Five rules police the bid, and they are applied in exactly the order set out here and in the paragraph below, which is the §2.10 precedence order. The parent event must be in `OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS` — a request against an event still planning or already locked is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS`. The requesting contact must belong to the same issuer as the event, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_COMPANY_MISMATCH`. The slot must sit entirely inside the event's meeting window, meaning `requested_slot_start` is at or after `window_start` and `requested_slot_end` is at or before `window_end`; a slot poking out either side is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. And the slot's length in minutes — the end instant minus the start instant, the slot itself being half-open so the end instant is not part of it — must exactly equal the event's `slot_minutes`; anything longer or shorter is refused with `ERR_SLOT_DURATION_INVALID`.

The fifth rule is about the contact rather than the event. Nobody can be in two meetings at once, and management needs travel time between them, so a request collides with any *other* meeting request of the same contact whose status is `REQUESTED`, `ALLOCATED` or `WAITLISTED` when the two intervals overlap after the stored one is padded by half an hour on each side. The scan is deliberately unbounded by event: it considers every such request of that contact system-wide, whichever event it belongs to and whichever issuer that event names, because a person's calendar does not partition by conference. A stored request in `RELEASED` or `DECLINED` never collides, because its slot has been given up. Concretely, the collision test is that the new start is earlier than the stored end plus thirty minutes **and** the stored start minus thirty minutes is earlier than the new end. A gap of exactly thirty minutes between two slots therefore does not collide, while a gap of twenty-nine minutes does. A collision is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. A surviving request is born in `REQUESTED` with `final_priority_score` null, `allocated_at` null, `release_reason` null and `promoted_from_waitlist` false, and it increments the parent event's `request_ledger_total` by 1 along with the event's `version`. Requests may be listed by `event_id`, `contact_id` and `status` — the discovery channel for requests a cascade has promoted, released or declined. Each of those filters matches by exact equality against the stored value and none of them is existence-checked, so an `event_id` or `contact_id` naming nothing yields an empty page with a `total_count` of zero rather than a not-found error. The listing and the single-request read are not scoped by acting identity either: an `Investor` on the role list sees every meeting request the filters match, including requests lodged by other contacts, because the ownership rule of §2.1 binds only the write path's `contact_id` and the reads it enumerates there. Addressing a request that does not exist yields `ERR_MEETING_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`.

Priority is what breaks ties when demand exceeds `capacity_slots`, and it is computed by one scoring rule that runs in two variants. The additive part is built from the requesting contact as stored at the moment the variant runs. Start with tier points read from the contact's `target_tier`: 50 for `TIER_1`, 35 for `TIER_2`, 20 for `TIER_3` and 5 for `UNTIERED`. Add 8 when the contact's `nda_on_file` is true, and nothing when it is false. Add an engagement term equal to the contact's `ownership_pct` multiplied by 4, rounded half up to 2 decimal places and then capped at 20 — the rounding happening before the term is added, and the cap meaning that any holder above five percent of the register contributes exactly 20. Add the lead-time bonus described below. From that sum subtract an activist penalty of 15, applied only when the contact's `activist_flag` is true **and** its `threat_level` is `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL`; a flagged activist at `NONE` or `WATCH` pays nothing. Multiply the result by a type multiplier read from the contact's `contact_type`: 1.20 for an `INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDER`, 1.00 for a `SELL_SIDE_ANALYST` and 0.60 for a `RETAIL_HOLDER`. Take the greater of that product and zero, so the score can never be negative. Round that figure half up to 2 decimal places. The evaluation order is pinned and consequential: every additive term including the lead bonus is summed first, the floor at zero is applied to the product rather than to the sum, and the single final rounding to 2 decimal places happens last of all.

The two variants differ in the lead-time bonus and in when the contact snapshot is taken. The **provisional** variant runs at intake, is written to `provisional_priority_score`, uses the contact as it stands at intake, and its lead-time bonus is always zero. The **final** variant runs during the allocation waterfall, is written to `final_priority_score`, uses the contact as it stands at allocation time, and adds a genuine lead-time bonus: 5 when the lead time is at least 14 days, otherwise 2 when the lead time is at least 7 days, otherwise 0. Lead time in days is the interval from the logical clock's current instant to `requested_slot_start`, divided by 86,400 seconds and rounded down to a whole number of days. Because the snapshot moves as well as the bonus, a position update or a threat-level change between intake and allocation makes the final score diverge from the provisional one for reasons that have nothing to do with lead time — the two fields are both retained precisely so that divergence is visible.

The allocation waterfall is an `IRAdmin` operation and transitions the event `OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS` → `LOCKED`. Three preconditions guard it: the event must be in `OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS` (otherwise `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`), the issuer's stored `disclosure_posture` must not be `BLACKOUT` (otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_BLACKOUT`, since a quiet period is no time to be taking investor meetings), and at least one of the event's requests must be in `REQUESTED` (otherwise `ERR_NO_PENDING_REQUESTS`). The run then takes every request of this event in status `REQUESTED` as its candidate pool and scores each one with the final variant, storing the result in `final_priority_score`. Candidates whose score is at least 25 are eligible; candidates below 25 are **threshold-deferred** and are never allocated in this run, however much capacity remains. The eligible candidates are ranked by `final_priority_score` descending, then — for equal scores — by the requesting contact's `shares_held` descending, then by the request's own creation timestamp ascending, compared at the full precision of the stored instant rather than at the three-digit width it is rendered with. Identifiers are never used as a tiebreaker, and rows that tie on all three keys fall back to insertion order per §2.3. Remaining capacity is the event's `capacity_slots` minus its current `allocated_count`, and the first that many rows of the ranked eligible list transition `REQUESTED` → `ALLOCATED`. Every other eligible row, and every threshold-deferred row, transitions `REQUESTED` → `WAITLISTED`.

The two branches of that fork have deliberately different side effects. A request that lands on `ALLOCATED` stores its `final_priority_score`, stamps `allocated_at` with the logical clock, sets `promoted_from_waitlist` to false, increments its own `version` by 1, and increases the event's `allocated_count` by 1. A request that lands on `WAITLISTED` also stores its `final_priority_score` and increments its own `version` by 1, but `allocated_at` stays null — a waitlisted request has never been given a time — and it increases the event's `waitlisted_count` by 1 instead. The run finishes by recomputing the event's counters, setting `status` to `LOCKED` and incrementing the event's `version` by 1, and it reports back `event_id`, the resulting `event_status`, the `evaluated_count` of candidates it scored, the resulting `allocated_count` and `waitlisted_count`, and a `threshold_deferred_count` that isolates the requests waitlisted because their score fell below 25 rather than because capacity ran out.

Releasing an allocated slot is an `IRAdmin` operation requiring a non-empty `reason`, and it is the second deepest cascade in this specification. The request must be in `ALLOCATED`, or the release is refused with `ERR_MEETING_REQUEST_NOT_ALLOCATED`, and the parent event must be in `LOCKED`, or it is refused with `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. The first link transitions the request `ALLOCATED` → `RELEASED`, stores the supplied reason as `release_reason`, and increments the request's `version` by 1; it clears nothing else, so the released row keeps the `allocated_at` instant it was originally given and keeps whatever `promoted_from_waitlist` flag it carried, both retained as the audit record of the slot that was surrendered. The second link, caused by the first, decreases the event's `allocated_count` by 1, increases its `released_count` by 1, and increments the event's `version` by 1 — a slot is now free. The third link, caused by the second, fires only while that free slot exists: from the pool of this event's requests in status `WAITLISTED` whose `final_priority_score` is at least 25, the promotion selector picks the single highest-ranked row using the same ordering as the run — `final_priority_score` descending, then the contact's `shares_held` descending, then creation timestamp ascending, with insertion order as the last resort — and transitions it `WAITLISTED` → `ALLOCATED` with `promoted_from_waitlist` set to true and `allocated_at` stamped from the logical clock and its own `version` incremented by 1, then increases the event's `allocated_count` by 1 and decreases its `waitlisted_count` by 1. The two ranking inputs are read differently here, and the difference is observable: `final_priority_score` is the value the allocation run stored and is never recomputed at promotion time, so a lead-time bonus that has since lapsed or a contact attribute that has since changed does not move it, whereas the `shares_held` tiebreaker is read live from the contact as it stands at the moment of promotion. Exactly one row is promoted per release, however many slots happen to be free. If the pool is empty, or if the event's `allocated_count` is not below its `capacity_slots`, no promotion happens and the release simply leaves the slot free. A threshold-deferred request whose score is below 25 is never promoted, no matter how many slots open up. Whether or not a promotion fires, the response body is the **released** request in full, never the promoted one, which the client discovers through the request listing.

Completing the event is the last `IRAdmin` step and transitions it `LOCKED` → `COMPLETED`, refused with `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE` from any other status. The first link sets `status` to `COMPLETED` and increments the event's `version` by 1. The second link, caused by the first, transitions every one of that event's requests still in `WAITLISTED` to `DECLINED`, incrementing each of those requests' `version` by 1. The third link, caused by the second, sets the event's `waitlisted_count` to zero and increases its `declined_count` by exactly the number of requests declined in the second link; it does not increment the event's `version` a second time, so a completion moves the event's `version` by exactly 1 no matter how many requests it declines. Requests already in `ALLOCATED` or `RELEASED` are left entirely alone, and a declined row keeps its stored `final_priority_score` and its null `allocated_at`. Completion takes no body and returns the updated event row in full, with its recomputed counters already reflected.

Across all four of those operations — request creation, the allocation run, release and completion — the event's ledger obeys one conserved identity. The per-link increments and decrements described above state the arithmetic effect, not the mechanism: as with `open_inquiry_count` in §2.11, every one of the five counters is maintained by a **full recount** over the event's meeting requests at each trigger point, never by incremental adjustment, so the stored figures cannot drift from the rows even when a single call fires several links. `request_ledger_total` is recounted as the number of meeting requests currently on file against the event, which is also the count of every request ever created against it — requests are never deleted, only moved between statuses — and it is therefore monotonic: it never decreases. `allocated_count`, `waitlisted_count`, `released_count` and `declined_count` are the counts of that event's requests in the corresponding statuses, and `request_ledger_total` always equals the sum of those four counters plus the number of the event's requests still in `REQUESTED`. Separately, `allocated_count` never exceeds `capacity_slots` at any point in time.

### 3.9. Transfer Agent Coordination: Record Dates, Dividends and Reversal

The **TransferAgentInstruction** is the desk's channel to the registrar, and it carries three kinds of traffic selected by `instruction_type`: `RECORD_DATE_SET` fixes a holder-of-record date, `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT` instructs an actual cash distribution, and `RECORD_INQUIRY` forwards a shareholder record question using the free-text `notes` field, which is optional on every type and stored as null when omitted. `notes` is genuinely free text and carries no length ceiling, but it is still a string field under §2.10 step 3, so supplying it empty or whitespace-only is a request-shape failure rather than a way of writing a blank note. Either an `IRAdmin` or a `TransferAgentLiaison` may submit one against an issuer named by `company_id`, and the acting `X-User-ID` is stamped as `submitted_by_user_id` — the value against which the dividend segregation-of-duties wall of §2.8 will later be tested. A new instruction is born in `SUBMITTED` with `acknowledged_by_user_id`, `executed_by_user_id`, `snapshot_holder_count`, `snapshot_shares_total`, `payout_ledger`, `total_payout_cents`, `executed_at` and `decision_reason` all null, and `version` at 1. Instructions may be listed by `company_id`, `instruction_type` and `status`, each filter matching by exact equality against the stored value and none of them existence-checked, so a `company_id` naming no issuer returns an empty page with a `total_count` of zero rather than `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`. Addressing an instruction that does not exist yields `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_FOUND`.

Which of the remaining inputs are required depends on the type. `record_date` is the date on which the holder snapshot is taken and is required for both `RECORD_DATE_SET` and `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT`; it is compared against the logical clock at UTC calendar-date granularity, and a record date whose calendar date falls earlier than the clock's current calendar date is refused with `ERR_RECORD_DATE_IN_PAST`. `payable_date` is the date the dividend becomes payable and is required for `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT`; its calendar date must be at least ten days after the record date's calendar date, and a shorter lag is refused with `ERR_PAYABLE_DATE_TOO_EARLY`. The type table governs which fields are **required**, not which are permitted: both dates are accepted and stored on the types that do not require them, and each rule above fires on whatever value is present regardless of type. A `RECORD_INQUIRY` may therefore carry a `record_date` and is still refused with `ERR_RECORD_DATE_IN_PAST` if that date has gone by, and a `RECORD_DATE_SET` may carry a `payable_date` against which the ten-day lag is still enforced. Conversely a rule whose operands are not all present is simply skipped rather than failing: the lag check needs both dates, so an instruction carrying only one of them never trips `ERR_PAYABLE_DATE_TOO_EARLY`. Both fields are calendar dates rather than instants: each is carried and compared at UTC calendar-date granularity and, whenever it holds a value, is rendered in an instruction response as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, never reformatted into the three-fractional-digit timestamp form that §2.9 prescribes for this entity's timestamp fields such as `executed_at`. On input each accepts either a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date or a full ISO 8601 timestamp read under the §2.9 input rules, and in the latter case the instant is normalised to UTC and then only its UTC calendar date is ever compared or rendered — a supplied time of day is never rejected and never observable. `declared_rate_cents_per_share` is required for `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT`, must be strictly greater than zero, and carries up to four decimal places of cents per share; it too is accepted and stored on the other two types, where the same strictly-positive rule applies to any value supplied, and it stays null when omitted. `funding_mode` selects which payout variant will run and is either `DECLARED_RATE` or `RESERVE_CAPPED`, defaulting to `DECLARED_RATE` for a dividend instruction. Its default is type-conditional and it has no default at all on the other two types, which have no payout step to fund: a `RECORD_DATE_SET` or `RECORD_INQUIRY` submitted without `funding_mode` stores and reports it as null, while one that supplies the field explicitly keeps the supplied value inertly. The field is projected on every instruction response whatever the type, so `funding_mode` is a documented null rather than an absent key on a record-date or record-inquiry row.

An issuer may not run two overlapping dividend cycles. When a `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT` instruction is submitted, the system compares its cycle against every other `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT` instruction of that issuer whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `EXECUTED`. A cycle is the **closed** date interval running from `record_date` through `payable_date` inclusive, and two cycles overlap when the new record date is on or before the stored payable date **and** the stored record date is on or before the new payable date. A new record date falling exactly on a stored payable date therefore does collide — the opposite inclusivity from the half-open meeting-slot rule of §3.8, and a deliberate difference. A collision is refused with `ERR_DIVIDEND_CYCLE_OVERLAP`.

Acknowledgement is a `TransferAgentLiaison` operation and a two-branch fork on a single `decision` field, either `ACKNOWLEDGE` or `REJECT`. Both branches require the instruction to still be in `SUBMITTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_ALREADY_DECIDED` — both are barred to the user recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` by `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and both stamp the acting user as `acknowledged_by_user_id` and increment the instruction's `version` by 1.

The `ACKNOWLEDGE` branch transitions the instruction `SUBMITTED` → `ACKNOWLEDGED` and freezes the record-date position. The snapshot counts the issuer's contacts whose `contact_type` is `INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDER` or `RETAIL_HOLDER` and whose `shares_held` is strictly greater than zero, writing that count to `snapshot_holder_count`, and sums those same rows' `shares_held` into `snapshot_shares_total`. Analyst rows are excluded even when they happen to hold shares, and holders sitting at exactly zero shares are excluded because the boundary is strict. The snapshot is frozen at this instant and is never refreshed by later position changes. An acknowledgement writes nothing to `decision_reason`, which stays null: `rejection_reason` is meaningful only on the other branch, and supplying it alongside `ACKNOWLEDGE` is neither an error nor recorded anywhere — it is read, found irrelevant and discarded. The `REJECT` branch instead transitions the instruction `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED`, requires a non-empty `rejection_reason` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) which it stores as `decision_reason`, and deliberately takes **no** snapshot at all: `snapshot_holder_count` and `snapshot_shares_total` stay null on a rejected instruction.

Execution is where cash moves, and it transitions the instruction `ACKNOWLEDGED` → `EXECUTED`. Five preconditions guard it, and per §2.10 they are evaluated in the precedence order given there. The instruction must be in `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or execution is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The logical clock must have reached `payable_date`, compared inclusively at calendar-date granularity, or it is refused with `ERR_PAYABLE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`; when the instruction carries no `payable_date` at all this precondition has no operand and is skipped rather than failed. The `instruction_type` must be `DIVIDEND_PAYMENT`, or it is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_TYPE_NOT_PAYABLE` — a `RECORD_DATE_SET` or `RECORD_INQUIRY` instruction is complete at `ACKNOWLEDGED` and has no payout step at all. The ordering of those last two is observable and is not an accident of drafting: a `RECORD_DATE_SET` that was submitted carrying a future `payable_date` is refused with `ERR_PAYABLE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, not with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_TYPE_NOT_PAYABLE`, because the temporal check at level 13 precedes the fiscal check at level 17. And the payout total computed below must not exceed the issuer's `dividend_reserve_cents`, or it is refused with `ERR_DIVIDEND_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` with nothing whatsoever written.

The payout itself is built as `payout_ledger`, an array of one row per payee, each row carrying that holder's `contact_id`, the `shares_held` used, and the `amount_cents` credited. The payees are the issuer's contacts whose `contact_type` is `INSTITUTIONAL_HOLDER` or `RETAIL_HOLDER` and whose `shares_held` is strictly greater than zero **as of execution time** — not as of the acknowledgement snapshot — and the ledger rows are written in order of the payees' creation timestamps ascending, never by identifier. For each payee, the amount is that payee's `shares_held` multiplied by the effective rate and rounded half up to whole cents. `total_payout_cents` is then the sum of the rounded per-holder amounts. This rounding order is pinned and it matters: every holder's amount is rounded to whole cents **first** and the rounded amounts are added afterwards. Summing the share counts first and rounding the product once is explicitly not the rule and yields a different total. An issuer with no qualifying payee at execution time is a legitimate execution and not an error: the instruction still reaches `EXECUTED`, with `payout_ledger` written as an empty array — an empty array, never null — and `total_payout_cents` written as zero, and the issuer's and holders' balances left exactly where they stood. A payee whose per-holder amount rounds to zero cents is likewise kept as a ledger row carrying an `amount_cents` of 0 rather than being dropped from the ledger.

The two funding modes differ only in the effective rate. Under `DECLARED_RATE` the effective rate is exactly `declared_rate_cents_per_share` as submitted, and if the resulting total exceeds the reserve the execution is refused with `ERR_DIVIDEND_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` and nothing is written. Under `RESERVE_CAPPED` the declared rate is ignored entirely: the effective rate is the issuer's `dividend_reserve_cents` divided by the instruction's `snapshot_shares_total`, truncated downward to 4 decimal places — that is, multiply the quotient by 10,000, discard any fractional part toward zero, and divide by 10,000 again. The division is guarded rather than attempted: when the snapshot's `snapshot_shares_total` is zero, the effective rate is taken to be exactly zero and no division is performed, so the execution proceeds to an all-zero ledger instead of failing. Note the deliberate asymmetry inside this variant: the denominator is the acknowledgement snapshot's share total, while the per-holder amounts use execution-time positions, so a holder who bought more shares between acknowledgement and execution can still push the total above the reserve and trip `ERR_DIVIDEND_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`. The sufficiency check runs on the resulting total under both modes.

A successful execution writes `payout_ledger` and `total_payout_cents`, sets `status` to `EXECUTED`, stamps the acting user as `executed_by_user_id` and the logical clock as `executed_at`, and increments the instruction's `version` by 1. It then moves the money: the issuer's `dividend_reserve_cents` decreases by `total_payout_cents` while its `dividend_paid_cents` increases by the same figure, with the issuer's `version` incremented by 1. Finally each ledger row credits that holder's `dividend_received_cents` by the row's `amount_cents` and increments that contact's `version` by 1.

Reversal exists because dividends are occasionally instructed in error, and it transitions the instruction `EXECUTED` → `REVERSED`. It requires a non-empty `reason`, stored as `decision_reason`; an instruction that is not in `EXECUTED` is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_EXECUTED`; and the logical clock must be strictly earlier than `executed_at` plus 72 hours — three days after execution the window has closed and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, the boundary being exclusive so a clock sitting exactly on `executed_at` plus 72 hours is already too late. Reversal is the one instruction operation that carries **no** segregation-of-duties wall: any `TransferAgentLiaison` may reverse, including the very user recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` or as `executed_by_user_id`, because the §2.8 dividend wall names acknowledgement and execution only and unwinding a payment is a remedy rather than an authorisation. Reversal is a compensating transaction, not a recomputation: it replays the **stored** `payout_ledger` backwards. The issuer's `dividend_reserve_cents` increases by `total_payout_cents` while `dividend_paid_cents` decreases by the same figure, with the issuer's `version` incremented by 1, and each ledger row debits that holder's `dividend_received_cents` by the row's `amount_cents` with that contact's `version` incremented by 1. Both `payout_ledger` and `total_payout_cents` are retained on the reversed instruction for audit rather than being cleared, and so are `executed_at` and `executed_by_user_id` — the reversal records itself only in `status`, `decision_reason`, `updated_at` and `version`, overwriting whatever `decision_reason` previously held. Because the entry condition is `EXECUTED`, a `REVERSED` instruction can never be reversed a second time, and it is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_EXECUTED`; a reversed cycle also drops out of the overlap scan above, so reversing a dividend frees its date interval for a fresh instruction.

Replaying the stored ledger rather than recomputing from current positions is what makes the arithmetic exact, and it upholds two named invariants over the issuer's dividend cash. First, the total of `dividend_reserve_cents` plus `dividend_paid_cents` is identical before execution, after execution and after reversal — cash is only ever moved between the two accounts, never created or destroyed. Second, the sum of `dividend_received_cents` across the issuer's contacts always equals the issuer's `dividend_paid_cents`. Because the reversal replays the same rows with the same amounts, no rounding drift can appear even if holders' `shares_held` changed after execution.
