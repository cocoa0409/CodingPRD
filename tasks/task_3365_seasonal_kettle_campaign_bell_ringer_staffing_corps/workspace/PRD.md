# Kettle Campaign Corps Service Delivery API - Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The Kettle Campaign Corps Service Delivery API is the operational backbone of a charitable organization's seasonal fundraising and year-round community service work. It coordinates the retail host site permissions that make a kettle placement legal, the bell ringers who staff those kettles, the counting-room controls that turn a day's coins into a reconciled bank deposit, the digital gifts that arrive without a coin bucket at all, and the seasonal assistance and corps community programs that the same organization delivers to households and neighborhoods. The system exists because these activities are financially and legally entangled: a kettle may not stand without a valid, unexpired retailer permission, a shift may not be scheduled for a ringer whose background screening has lapsed, and no dollar may be reported as raised until it has cleared the counting room and the bank.

Two design postures run through the whole product. The first is posture-driven gating: every resource carries a lifecycle status, and writes to a resource are permitted only while its governing parents are in the posture that authorizes them — a campaign that is still in planning or already closed does not accept new kettle placements, shifts, collections, gifts or reallocation applications. The second is temporal windowing: permissions, placements, intake periods, shift intervals, rest buffers, curfews, appointment slots and campaign seasons are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that an auditor or a test harness can place the system at any instant and observe the rules that hold there.

All routes are mounted under the `/api` prefix. The remainder of this document specifies the shared contract that every route obeys, then narrates the business rules of each operational area, and finally lists the routes and the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: who may call the API, what a response looks like, how collections of resources are paged, and the cross-cutting principles — hierarchy, precision, validation order, concurrency control and the logical clock — that every workflow inherits.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting party, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that party acts. A request that omits either header, sends an empty value, or names a role the platform does not recognize is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the caller is identified, the declared role must appear among the roles authorized for the operation being attempted; a recognized role that is not authorized for that operation is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

Fourteen roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows and are case-sensitive: `PlatformAdmin`, `TerritorialCommander`, `DivisionalOfficer`, `CorpsOfficer`, `KettleCoordinator`, `VolunteerCoordinator`, `BellRinger`, `CountingRoomTeller`, `FinanceOfficer`, `AssistanceCaseworker`, `ProgramDirector`, `DisasterServicesCoordinator`, `DigitalGivingProcessor`, and `HostSiteManager`. The authoritative list of which roles may call which route appears in the endpoint table in §4; the notes below explain how an identifier is interpreted once a role is accepted.

Eleven roles are opaque operators: `PlatformAdmin`, `TerritorialCommander`, `DivisionalOfficer`, `KettleCoordinator`, `VolunteerCoordinator`, `CountingRoomTeller`, `FinanceOfficer`, `AssistanceCaseworker`, `ProgramDirector`, `DisasterServicesCoordinator`, and `DigitalGivingProcessor`. When `X-User-Role` is one of these, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it where a transition calls for an actor, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Three roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `CorpsOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Unit`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `Unit` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BellRinger`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BellRinger`. When `X-User-Role` is `HostSiteManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HostSite`.

Owner-scoped operations exist where a caller must be acting for a specific resource rather than merely holding a role. A caller who is authenticated and whose role is authorized but whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the addressed resource's owning foreign key is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This check governs the submission of a background screening request, the recording of a screening result, a ringer's self check-in at a shift, and the two single-resource reads that expose a host site and a kettle location to their respective entity-backed roles.

One capability is reserved to a single role: only `PlatformAdmin` may set the logical clock. `PlatformAdmin` may otherwise act on every route in the system.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List routes accept a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. The `limit` parameter caps how many items are returned and defaults to 10 when omitted; its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. The `offset` parameter states how many items to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a workflow says otherwise, list responses are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order, which is the stable ordering callers should rely on when paging through a result set.

The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in addition to the request identifiers described in §2.2, so a caller can determine both the size of the full result set and whether further pages remain.

### 2.4. The Organizational Hierarchy and Unit Oversight

Every campaign, kettle, ringer, program and deployment belongs to an organizational unit, and the units themselves form a supervision tree. A unit is created with a `name`, a `unit_type`, a `territory_code` of two letters such as "NT" or "ST", an optional `parent_unit_id` naming its supervising unit, and an optional `canteen_pool_size` that defaults to 0. The `unit_type` is one of `CORPS`, `DIVISION`, `AREA_COMMAND` or `TERRITORY`, and it expresses the unit's rank in that tree. Only a `TERRITORY` unit may stand at the root: the `parent_unit_id` of a territory is null, while every other unit must name a supervising unit. Units can be listed and filtered by `unit_type`, by `parent_unit_id` or by `territory_code`.

Two fields on a unit are derived rather than supplied. The `oversight_level` is the depth of the unit in the `parent_unit_id` chain, counting the `TERRITORY` level as zero, so a corps sitting under a division under a territory reports an oversight level of two. The `committed_canteen_count` is the sum of `assigned_canteen_count` across all of the unit's disaster deployments that are in `APPROVED`, `STAGED` or `DEPLOYED` status; it is the measure of how much of the unit's `canteen_pool_size` is already spoken for. Both derived values are recomputed whenever the events that feed them occur, and a recomputation is a mutation of the unit for the purposes of §2.5.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every top-level resource in this system — units, campaigns, host sites, kettle locations, bell ringers, shifts, kettle collections, digital givings, assistance programs, assistance applications, corps programs, disaster deployments and reallocation decisions — carries the same four system-managed properties alongside its own fields.

The `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning; it is the only value guaranteed unique across the whole system, and it is what path parameters refer to. The `created_at` property is an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with exactly three fractional digits, recording the instant the resource was created. The `updated_at` property has the same format and records the instant of the most recent modification. The `version` property is a positive integer that starts at its initial value when the resource is created and is incremented by one on every subsequent mutation of that resource.

The mutation rule is deliberately broad: a write that changes only a derived or computed field still counts as a mutation, so recomputing a derived total, cascading a parent's state change into a child, or stamping a timestamp all refresh `updated_at` and advance `version`. This is what makes the version values usable as the concurrency tokens described in §2.10.

### 2.6. Posture Gating

Lifecycle posture, not elapsed time alone, decides whether a write is allowed. The campaign is the outermost gate: a campaign begins in `PLANNING`, becomes `ACTIVE` when the territory activates it, and ends in `CLOSED`, and the campaign must be `ACTIVE` for a kettle location to be activated, for a shift to be created, for a kettle collection to be recorded, for a digital gift to be recorded, or for a reallocation decision to be applied. A write to a campaign-scoped resource while the campaign is in `PLANNING` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

Beneath the campaign sit two further gates that guard the physical kettle. A kettle location may only be activated, and a shift may only be scheduled or a digital gift attributed, when the host site that carries the kettle holds a permission in `APPROVED` status; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_HOST_SITE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 409). A shift may only be created, confirmed or rebooked, a collection may only be recorded, and a gift may only be attributed against a kettle location whose own `status` is `ACTIVE`; otherwise the refusal is `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

Seasonal assistance has its own posture gate: applications are accepted only while the assistance program's `status` is `OPEN`, and an intake attempt against a program that is not open is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). More generally, an operation that targets a resource whose current state does not permit it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), and an operation that targets a resource already sitting in one of its declared terminal states is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409) rather than being silently ignored.

### 2.7. Permission, Placement and Exclusivity Discipline

A retailer's permission is the legal instrument that allows a kettle to stand on private property, and the platform treats it as such. A negotiated permission is recorded with the window during which it is valid — `permission_starts_on` and `permission_ends_on` — and a kettle placement is recorded with its own occupancy window, `placement_starts_on` and `placement_ends_on`. Every window in this system is inclusive at both of its endpoints unless a rule explicitly says otherwise, so an instant that falls exactly on `permission_starts_on`, `permission_ends_on`, `placement_starts_on`, `placement_ends_on`, a campaign's `starts_on` or `ends_on`, or a program's `intake_opens_on` or `intake_closes_on` counts as inside the window. A placement may never outlive the permission that authorizes it: the placement window must be fully contained in the permission window, and a placement whose window reaches even one instant beyond the permission is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PERMISSION` (HTTP 422).

Host sites also carry commercial terms. The `exclusivity_scope` records whether the retailer granted `EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE` kettle rights for the window and defaults to `NON_EXCLUSIVE`; at an exclusive site the organization may not operate a second kettle, and an overlapping placement — including one whose window merely touches the existing placement's window at a single instant — is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The `max_kettles` field states how many kettle locations the site permits simultaneously and defaults to 1; an activation that would push the site's count of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` locations to or beyond that ceiling is refused with `ERR_KETTLE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Within a single campaign, the operator-facing `location_code` is unique, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOCATION_CODE` (HTTP 409); across a campaign, a digital gift's processor reference `external_ref` is likewise unique, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF` (HTTP 409).

### 2.8. Money, Minutes and the Rounding Rules

All monetary values in this system are integers expressed in cents; there is no fractional-cent quantity anywhere in the API, and no monetary field is ever returned as a string. Where a calculation produces a monetary result from a division or a rate, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent unless the rule below says otherwise, and where a calculation produces an intermediate count — rolls of coin, hours, percentages, scores — the intermediate is floored to an integer before it is used downstream. Floors and roundings are applied in the order each workflow states, never at the end of a chain, because two orderings of the same arithmetic can differ.

Two quantities are deliberately not integers. A shift's `paid_hours` is a number rounded half up to exactly two decimal places, and it is the only field in the system that carries fractional precision. Every other duration is handled in whole minutes. Percentages — the coverage percentage used to judge staffing and the disruption rate used to grade a location — are floored to whole integer percentages. Scores — the assistance eligibility score, the provisional intake score and the ringer reliability score — are integers, and where a score is derived by clamping it is clamped after the arithmetic rather than before.

One ordering rule deserves emphasis because it is easy to get subtly wrong: the handling fee charged on a kettle collection is computed separately for each denomination of coin and for currency and cheques, each component is floored to a whole cent on its own, and only then are the components summed. The sum itself is never re-rounded. Summing the un-rounded components first and flooring once can produce a different answer by several cents, and the per-component-then-sum ordering is the one this system requires.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval and the Order of Validation

When a route addresses a resource by an identifier in its path, the system resolves that identifier before it evaluates anything about the request body, and when more than one resource is named in a path the system resolves them from left to right, stopping at the first one that does not exist. A path identifier that does not resolve to a resource yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a parent resource named in a path that does not exist yields `ERR_PARENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A body foreign key that does not resolve to a row yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a body foreign key that resolves but belongs to a different campaign or unit scope than the resource being addressed yields `ERR_REFERENCE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

Beyond those existence rules, the platform evaluates its checks in a fixed precedence. The order matters because when two checks would both fail, only the earlier one is reported; a client that fixes the later problem without fixing the earlier one will still see the earlier error. The full order, from first to last, is:

1. Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401).
2. `X-User-Role` names a role the platform recognizes; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401).
3. The role is listed among the roles authorized for the addressed operation; otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).
4. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of an existing instance of the backing entity, and for an owner-scoped operation it also equals the addressed resource's owning foreign key; otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
5. The request body carries every required input declared for the operation and every supplied value matches its declared type. This check deliberately precedes resource checks, because a malformed body cannot be attributed to a resource; a failure is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. The resource addressed by the path identifier exists; otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
7. Every parent resource named in the path exists; otherwise `ERR_PARENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
8. Every body foreign key resolves to an existing row; otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
9. Every body foreign key belongs to the same campaign or unit scope as the target resource; otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).
10. The target resource is in a state that permits the requested operation; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).
11. The target resource is not already in a terminal state; otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).
12. The owning campaign is `ACTIVE` for a write to a campaign-scoped resource; otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
13. The host site referenced by the request holds an `APPROVED` permission; otherwise `ERR_HOST_SITE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 409).
14. The kettle location referenced by the request is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
15. The bell ringer referenced by the request has a `CLEARED` screening whose expiry is later than the shift start; otherwise `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409).
16. The bell ringer referenced by the request has `engagement_status` `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_RINGER_INACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
17. The assistance program referenced by the request has `status` `OPEN`; otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).
18. The shift duration does not exceed 480 minutes, where exactly 480 minutes is accepted and 481 is rejected; otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_TOO_LONG` (HTTP 422).
19. The gap between the ringer's consecutive shifts is at least 30 minutes, where a gap of exactly 30 minutes is accepted and 29 is rejected; otherwise `ERR_RINGER_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).
20. A ringer whose age at the shift start is under 18 is not scheduled to end after the 20:00 local curfew; otherwise `ERR_MINOR_CURFEW_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422).
21. A deployment does not commit more canteens than the unit's uncommitted pool; otherwise `ERR_CANTEEN_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).
22. No other shift for the same ringer overlaps the requested interval; otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).
23. The kettle placement window is fully contained in the host site permission window; otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PERMISSION` (HTTP 422).
24. An exclusive host site carries no other overlapping or touching placement; otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
25. The host site's count of `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` locations stays below its `max_kettles`; otherwise `ERR_KETTLE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
26. The `location_code` is unique within the campaign; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOCATION_CODE` (HTTP 409).
27. The `external_ref` is unique within the campaign; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF` (HTTP 409).
28. The program's accepted application count stays below its `household_cap`; otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_INTAKE_FULL` (HTTP 409).
29. The `household_size` is between 1 and 12 inclusive; otherwise `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_INVALID` (HTTP 422).
30. A corps program enrollment that overflows capacity is only rejected when waitlisting is disabled; otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_AT_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409).
31. The digital gift's `occurred_at` falls inside the campaign window, both endpoints inclusive; otherwise `ERR_GIVING_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
32. The current logical clock instant falls inside the program's intake window, both endpoints inclusive; otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_INTAKE_WINDOW` (HTTP 409).
33. The collection's `collected_on` falls inside the kettle location's placement window, both endpoints inclusive; otherwise `ERR_COLLECTION_OUTSIDE_PLACEMENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
34. A distribution appointment does not overlap another `SCHEDULED` appointment at the same distribution unit, where merely adjacent appointments are accepted; otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
35. The reallocation's `effective_on` falls inside the campaign window and is not earlier than the current logical clock instant at approval time; otherwise `ERR_REALLOCATION_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422).
36. A bank-confirmed amount differs from the deposited amount by no more than 500 cents, where a difference of exactly 500 cents is accepted and 501 is rejected; otherwise `ERR_VARIANCE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 409).
37. The declared deposit equals the counted total minus the handling fee; otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
38. Completing a paid seasonal shift does not push the campaign's labor commitment above its labor budget; otherwise `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
39. A digital gift amount is greater than 0 and at most 500000 cents; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_GIFT_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422).
40. The moved hours do not exceed the source location's remaining scheduled hours for the campaign; otherwise `ERR_REALLOCATION_HOURS_EXCEED_SOURCE` (HTTP 409).
41. The household's accumulated assistance value does not exceed the program's `per_household_limit_cents`; otherwise `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations carry a mandatory concurrency token, supplied as an input field named for the parent entity whose state the write depends on. The token is the client's last observed `version` of that parent entity, and the request is rejected when it no longer matches the parent's current version, which is the signal that another writer has moved the parent since the client read it. The rejection code is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) in all three cases, and the check is part of the ordered ladder in §2.9 rather than an afterthought: the version comparison is evaluated as part of the operation's state checks.

Creating a shift is the first such operation. A shift-scheduling request must carry `expected_kettle_location_version` holding the kettle location's current version; if the location's version has advanced since the scheduler last read it — because its posture, its placement window or its staffing expectations changed underneath the scheduler's view — the shift is not created and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Verifying a teller count is the second. A verification request must carry `expected_kettle_collection_version` holding the collection's current version; if the collection's version has advanced since the teller last read it, because a recount or a deposit has already moved the row, the verification is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Scheduling a distribution appointment is the third. A scheduling request must carry `expected_assistance_application_version` holding the application's current version; if the application's version has advanced since the caseworker last read it, because a prior screening or approval decision changed its posture, the appointment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

These three fields are mandatory and their absence is a request-shape failure. Three other routes accept an optional version field for the resource they act on — campaign activation accepts `expected_campaign_version`, host site approval accepts `expected_host_site_version`, and kettle location activation accepts `expected_kettle_location_version` — but those fields are optional there, and the mandatory-token rule above applies only to the three operations named in this subsection. Routes not described here do not accept a version field at all.

### 2.11. The Logical Clock

All time-dependent evaluation in this system runs against a server-held logical clock rather than the wall clock of the host machine. The clock starts at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts and never falls back to wall-clock time before it is first set, so a freshly started server behaves as though the current instant were that value. Only a `PlatformAdmin` may move it, by supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`; the call returns the resulting `current_utc` value, and every subsequent clock-dependent evaluation — window membership, expiry, curfew, rest buffers, check-in windows, campaign closure, deployment billing — uses the new instant.

Several rules are enforced by the passage of this clock rather than by a client request. A campaign that is `ACTIVE` when the clock has moved past its `ends_on` closes automatically, and this evaluation happens when the campaign is read. A host site permission in `APPROVED` status expires when the clock has moved past its `permission_ends_on`. A ringer's `CLEARED` screening expires when the clock has moved past `screening_expires_at`. A no-show may only be recorded once the clock has moved past the shift's `ends_at`. Each of these transitions is described in full in §3, and each is a real mutation of the affected resource: it refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` like any other write.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The workflows below are narrated by operational area rather than by route. Each subsection describes the rules that govern a resource's life, the postures that gate it, the arithmetic it triggers and the errors it raises. The routes that carry these operations are listed in §4, and every rule stated here applies to whichever route performs the operation.

### 3.1. The Kettle Network: Retail Permissions and Kettle Placements

A kettle can only stand where a retailer has agreed to let it stand. That agreement is recorded as a host site: a `KettleCoordinator` supplies the `campaign_id` it belongs to, the `retailer_name` and `store_number` that identify the store, the `city` and two-letter `state_code` where it sits, the negotiated window in `permission_starts_on` and `permission_ends_on`, and optionally the `exclusivity_scope` and `max_kettles` terms. Every new host site begins its life with a `permission_status` of `PENDING`, and the site's `active_kettle_count` tracks how many kettle locations currently sit there in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status. Host sites can be listed and filtered by `campaign_id`, by `permission_status`, by `retailer_name` and by `store_number`.

Approval is the moment the permission becomes usable. A `KettleCoordinator` moves a `PENDING` permission to `APPROVED`, and the transition requires that the window be coherent — `permission_ends_on` must be later than `permission_starts_on` — and that the owning campaign still be `ACTIVE` or still be `PLANNING`, so a permission cannot be granted for a campaign that has already closed. The same approval route carries a `decision` input: supplying `DENY` moves a `PENDING` permission to `DENIED`, which is terminal, and supplying `REVOKE` moves an `APPROVED` permission to `REVOKED`, which is also terminal and which a `TerritorialCommander` records when a retailer withdraws consent mid-season. Revocation is the harshest cascade in the network: every kettle location at that site, whether still in `DRAFT`, currently `ACTIVE` or temporarily `SUSPENDED`, is moved to `CLOSED` in the same transaction, because a kettle may not remain on premises without permission. The approval route accepts an optional `expected_host_site_version` alongside the decision.

Permissions also expire on their own. When the logical clock moves past an `APPROVED` site's `permission_ends_on`, the permission becomes `EXPIRED` without any client request. The cascade here is narrower than revocation but still substantial: every kettle location at the site that is currently `ACTIVE` becomes `SUSPENDED`, and for each of those locations every shift still in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status whose `starts_at` lies after the expiry instant is cancelled. A shift that had already begun before the permission lapsed is left alone.

An expired permission is not necessarily dead. A `KettleCoordinator` may renew it, moving the site from `EXPIRED` back to `APPROVED` on a fresh window. Renewal is refused when the supplied `permission_ends_on` is not later than the current logical clock instant, and it is also refused when another host site with the same `retailer_name` and `store_number` already holds an `APPROVED` permission whose window overlaps the one being requested — the platform will not let one store be double-booked. The renewed window begins at the current logical clock instant and runs to the supplied `permission_ends_on`. When the renewal lands, every kettle location at that site that is `SUSPENDED` returns to `ACTIVE`, but only if its placement window is still entirely inside the renewed permission window; a placement that would now stick out past the new permission stays suspended.

Beneath the permission sits the kettle location itself. A `KettleCoordinator` or `CorpsOfficer` records one with the `host_site_id` where it will stand, the `campaign_id` that owns it, an operator-facing `location_code`, a `placement` describing whether it is `INDOOR`, `OUTDOOR` or `DRIVE_THROUGH`, the occupancy window in `placement_starts_on` and `placement_ends_on`, and an optional `daily_required_minutes` stating how much bell ringing coverage the location is expected to staff each day, which defaults to 480 minutes — eight hours. A new location begins in `DRAFT` and is not yet a working kettle: only an `ACTIVE` location accepts shifts and collections.

Activation is where the placement rules bite. Moving a location from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` requires that the host site's permission be `APPROVED`, that the location's placement window be fully contained in that permission window, and that the owning campaign be `ACTIVE`. It also requires that the site not be over its kettle ceiling: the number of locations at that host site already in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status must remain below the site's `max_kettles`. Two of these rules also guard the moment of creation, so a placement that is doomed can be rejected before it is ever stored: the window-containment rule and the exclusivity rule are evaluated both when a location is created and when it is activated. The activation route accepts an optional `expected_kettle_location_version`. Successful activation recomputes the host site's `active_kettle_count` and the campaign's `active_location_count`, so both counters always reflect the live network.

A working kettle can be paused. Suspending an `ACTIVE` location moves it to `SUSPENDED` and cancels every shift at that location that is still in `SCHEDULED` status; for each cancelled shift the owning ringer's `scheduled_hours` is reduced by the shift's duration in hours, so the ringer's availability is freed at the same moment the kettle stops. Suspension recomputes the host site's `active_kettle_count`. A suspended location returns to `ACTIVE` through the same activation route, and that path requires only that the host site's permission be `APPROVED` and the campaign be `ACTIVE`; a resumed location recomputes the campaign's `active_location_count`.

Closing a kettle is final. A location may be closed from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED`, and in both cases the platform refuses to close it while any collection at that location is still in `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `VARIANCE_HOLD` status — money that has been counted but not yet banked must be resolved before the kettle disappears from the books. Closing from `ACTIVE` additionally cancels every shift at the location still in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status. Closing from `SUSPENDED` cancels nothing, because a suspended location has no live shifts left. Either way the host site's `active_kettle_count` is recomputed, and closing from `ACTIVE` also recomputes the campaign's `active_location_count`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

The counters that feed the network's reporting are derived from live rows. A location's `covered_minutes` is the sum of the durations in minutes of its `COMPLETED` shifts, and it rises when a shift completes and falls when a shift is cancelled. Its `location_total_cents` is the reconciled proceeds attributed to it, built from counted collections and attributed digital gifts as described in §3.4, §3.5 and §3.9, and its `cents_per_hour` and `performance_tier` are derived from those figures by the calculation in §3.9.

### 3.2. Campaign Governance and the Seasonal Clock

A campaign is the container that gives every other seasonal activity its season. A `TerritorialCommander` or platform administrator opens one with a `name` such as "2026 Christmas Kettle Campaign", a `season_year`, the window bounded by `starts_on` and `ends_on`, a territory-approved `goal_cents`, and an optional `labor_budget_cents` that defaults to 0. Every campaign is born in `PLANNING`, a posture in which host site permissions may be negotiated but no kettle may operate.

The transition to `ACTIVE` is a territorial decision and it is deliberately hard to make prematurely. Activation requires that at least one host site belonging to the campaign already hold an `APPROVED` permission — a campaign with nowhere to stand a kettle is not ready — and that the campaign's `ends_on` instant still lie in the future relative to the logical clock. The activation request carries `expected_campaign_version`. Once active, the campaign becomes the gate described in §2.6: kettle location activation, shift creation, collection recording, digital gift recording and the application of reallocation decisions all require it.

Closure can arrive in two ways. A campaign may be closed explicitly, and it also closes itself: when a campaign in `ACTIVE` status is read and the logical clock has moved past its `ends_on`, the system closes it as part of serving that read. The cascade that follows is the largest in the system. Every kettle location belonging to the campaign that is in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status is moved to `CLOSED`, and for each of those locations every shift still in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status is cancelled. The season is over in one transaction: no kettle remains open, no shift remains live, and `CLOSED` is terminal for the campaign. Campaigns may be listed and filtered by `status` and by `season_year`.

Three derived figures on a campaign are maintained as the season runs. The `active_location_count` is the number of the campaign's kettle locations currently in `ACTIVE` status. The `labor_committed_cents` is the sum of `paid_amount_cents` across the campaign's shifts that have reached `COMPLETED` status — it is the paid-labor cost the campaign has actually incurred, and it is the figure the labor budget check in §3.3 measures against `labor_budget_cents`. The `total_raised_cents` is the reconciled campaign total, assembled from counted collections and attributed digital gifts exactly as described in §3.9; it moves whenever a collection is deposited or reconciled, or a digital gift is attributed or reversed.

The campaign summary read exposes the season at a glance and returns the campaign's `campaign_id`, its `status`, its `goal_cents`, its `total_raised_cents`, its `collections_counted_cents` and its `digital_attributed_cents` as separate components, its `attainment_pct` progress against the goal, its `active_location_count`, and both sides of the labor picture in `labor_committed_cents` and `labor_budget_cents`.

### 3.3. Staffing the Kettles: Ringers, Screening and Shift Scheduling

The people who stand the kettles are registered as bell ringers. A `VolunteerCoordinator` records a ringer with a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique for the campaign season, a `ringer_type` of either `VOLUNTEER` or `PAID_SEASONAL`, the `corps_id` of the unit that recruited and supervises them, a `date_of_birth` that the minor-curfew rule depends on, and an optional `hourly_rate_cents` that defaults to 0 and is meaningful only for paid seasonal workers — a volunteer's rate is ignored and their pay is zero. Ringers can be listed and filtered by `corps_id`, by `screening_status`, by `engagement_status` and by `ringer_type`.

A ringer row carries two status dimensions that move independently of each other. The `screening_status` describes the background screening and is one of `NOT_STARTED`, `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `FAILED` or `EXPIRED`, beginning at `NOT_STARTED`. The `engagement_status` describes the working relationship and is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `SEPARATED`, beginning at `ACTIVE`. A ringer may be engaged and unscreened, or screened and suspended; neither dimension implies the other. Alongside them sit three derived values: `screening_expires_at`, which is stamped only when a screening clears; `reliability_score`, an integer between 0 and 100 that begins at 100; and `scheduled_hours`, the sum of the durations in hours of the ringer's shifts currently in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status.

Screening begins when a `VolunteerCoordinator` or `CorpsOfficer` submits a screening request carrying the screening `vendor` and the `package_level` to be run. The request moves a ringer from `NOT_STARTED` to `PENDING`, and it also moves a ringer whose screening has `EXPIRED` back to `PENDING` for a fresh check; in both cases the ringer's `engagement_status` must not already be `SEPARATED`, because a separated ringer cannot be rescreened. Note that a screening that has already `FAILED` has no onward transition: a failure is not re-run through this route.

The result comes back through a completion route carrying the `result` and the `completed_at` instant. A `CLEAR` result moves a `PENDING` screening to `CLEARED` and stamps `screening_expires_at` at exactly 365 days after the supplied `completed_at`; the completion instant may not lie in the future, so a result recorded ahead of the logical clock is refused as an invalid state for the operation. A `FAIL` result moves a `PENDING` screening to `FAILED` and has an immediate staffing consequence: the ringer's `engagement_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and every shift of that ringer still in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status is cancelled.

Clearance is not permanent. When the logical clock moves past a `CLEARED` ringer's `screening_expires_at`, the screening becomes `EXPIRED`, and every shift of that ringer still in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status whose `starts_at` lies after the expiry instant is cancelled. Shifts that begin before the expiry are untouched.

A suspended ringer can be reinstated, but only through the screening route: recording a `CLEAR` result while the ringer's `screening_status` is `PENDING` and their `engagement_status` is `SUSPENDED` returns the engagement to `ACTIVE` and sets the `reliability_score` to exactly 60 — reinstatement restores the ringer to the threshold of good standing rather than to a perfect record. Separately, a ringer who wishes to leave is separated: the transition to `SEPARATED` requires that `scheduled_hours` be exactly zero, so a ringer with live commitments must first have those shifts cancelled or completed. `SEPARATED` is terminal for the engagement dimension.

Reliability is the system's memory of how a ringer has behaved, and it is recomputed from the ringer's whole shift history rather than decremented in place. The score starts from a base of 100 and adds one point for every shift the ringer has completed, subtracts five points for every late cancellation, and subtracts fifteen points for every no-show; the result is then clamped to the range 0 to 100. A late cancellation is a shift that is currently `CANCELLED` and whose previous status was `CONFIRMED` — cancelling a shift that was still only `SCHEDULED` costs nothing — while a no-show is a shift in `NO_SHOW` status. Shifts still sitting in `SCHEDULED` and shifts cancelled directly out of `SCHEDULED` are excluded from the computation entirely, so they neither help nor hurt. The recomputation runs when a shift completes, when a `CONFIRMED` shift is cancelled, and when a shift is marked as a no-show.

Falling below 60 on that scale is a staffing event of its own. When a no-show recomputation leaves the ringer's `reliability_score` below 60, the engagement transitions from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` as a system consequence: every one of that ringer's shifts still in `SCHEDULED` status and starting after the current logical clock instant is cancelled, and for each cancelled shift the affected kettle location's `covered_minutes` is recomputed, since the coverage that location thought it had has just evaporated.

A shift is the unit of work that connects a ringer to a kettle for a bounded interval. Scheduling one requires a `kettle_location_id`, a `bell_ringer_id`, a `starts_at` and an `ends_at`, an optional `break_minutes` that defaults to 0, an optional `shift_kind` of `REGULAR`, `RELIEF` or `DOUBLE` that defaults to `REGULAR`, and the mandatory concurrency token `expected_kettle_location_version` described in §2.10. A new shift begins in `SCHEDULED` status with `checked_in_at` empty. Shifts can be listed and filtered by `kettle_location_id`, by `bell_ringer_id`, by `status` and by a `starts_at_from` lower bound.

Six gates stand between a request and a created shift. The kettle location must be `ACTIVE`; the host site behind it must hold an `APPROVED` permission; the owning campaign must be `ACTIVE`; the ringer's screening must be `CLEARED` with an expiry later than the shift's `starts_at`; the ringer's `engagement_status` must be `ACTIVE`; and the ringer must not already be committed to an overlapping interval. The last of these is checked against every shift of that ringer that is still in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` status, and any overlap of the requested interval with one of them is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Three further scheduling rules constrain the shape of the shift itself. Its duration, measured from `starts_at` to `ends_at`, may not exceed 480 minutes, and a duration of exactly 480 minutes is accepted while 481 is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_TOO_LONG` (HTTP 422). The gap between this shift and the ringer's nearest adjacent shift may not be shorter than 30 minutes, and a gap of exactly 30 minutes is accepted while 29 minutes is refused with `ERR_RINGER_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409); the platform reads this as a rest-buffer obligation and applies it to both sides of the shift. Finally, a ringer whose age at the shift's start is under 18 may not be scheduled to end after the 20:00 local curfew, and a request that would do so is refused with `ERR_MINOR_CURFEW_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). Creating a shift increases the ringer's `scheduled_hours` by the shift's duration in hours and advances the kettle location's `version` by one, which is precisely why the scheduling token has to match the location as the client last read it.

Confirmation is a deliberate second look. Moving a shift from `SCHEDULED` to `CONFIRMED` re-evaluates the ringer's screening — it must still be `CLEARED` with an expiry later than the shift start — the ringer's engagement, which must still be `ACTIVE`, and the kettle location, which must still be `ACTIVE`. This is why a ringer whose clearance lapses between scheduling and confirmation loses the shift rather than silently working it.

Check-in belongs to the ringer alone. A shift in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status moves to `CHECKED_IN` when the ringer themself calls the check-in route, and the request is accepted only when `X-User-ID` equals the shift's `bell_ringer_id` — anyone else is refused as not being the resource owner. The clock must also be inside the check-in window, which opens 15 minutes before `starts_at` and closes at `ends_at`; a check-in before the window opens or after the shift's end is refused as an invalid state for the operation. A successful check-in stamps `checked_in_at` with the current logical clock instant.

Completion is where a shift turns into money and coverage. A `CHECKED_IN` shift moves to `COMPLETED`, which is terminal. Completion recomputes the shift's `paid_hours` and `paid_amount_cents`, adds the shift's duration in minutes to its kettle location's `covered_minutes`, recomputes that location's `cents_per_hour` and `performance_tier` per §3.9, recomputes the ringer's `reliability_score` per the weighted formula above, and recomputes the campaign's `labor_committed_cents`. It also enforces the campaign's labor ceiling: completing a `PAID_SEASONAL` shift whose `paid_amount_cents` would push `labor_committed_cents` above the campaign's `labor_budget_cents` is refused with `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The arithmetic behind the payable amount is precise. The duration in minutes is the interval from `starts_at` to `ends_at`; the paid minutes are that duration less `break_minutes`, never below zero; of those paid minutes the first 420 — seven hours — are base minutes and anything beyond is overtime; the `paid_hours` field is the paid minutes divided by sixty, rounded half up to two decimal places; and `paid_amount_cents` is the base minutes divided by sixty, multiplied by the ringer's `hourly_rate_cents`, plus the overtime minutes divided by sixty, multiplied by `hourly_rate_cents` and then by one and a half, with the total rounded half up to a whole cent. For a volunteer the rate is zero, so the payable amount is zero even though the hours are recorded.

Cancellation releases the commitment. A shift in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status may be cancelled, optionally with a `reason` recorded by the operator, and cancellation reduces the ringer's `scheduled_hours` by the shift's duration in hours. The two paths are not equivalent: cancelling a shift that was still only `SCHEDULED` leaves the ringer's reliability untouched, while cancelling a shift that had already reached `CONFIRMED` is a late cancellation and costs the ringer five points on the next recomputation.

A no-show is recorded only after the fact. Marking a shift as a no-show requires that it be in `SCHEDULED` status and that the logical clock have moved past the shift's `ends_at`; the shift becomes `NO_SHOW`, the ringer's `scheduled_hours` is reduced by the shift's duration in hours, and the ringer's `reliability_score` is recomputed with the fifteen-point no-show penalty included. If that recomputation drops the score below 60, the suspension cascade described above fires in the same transaction, cancelling the ringer's future scheduled shifts and recomputing the affected locations' coverage.

Both `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` shifts can be brought back to life. Rebooking a `CANCELLED` shift returns it to `SCHEDULED` with a new `starts_at` and `ends_at`, and it requires the same staffing conditions as a fresh booking — a `CLEARED` screening that does not expire before the new start, an `ACTIVE` engagement, and an `ACTIVE` kettle location. Rebooking a `NO_SHOW` shift requires the new window and an `ACTIVE` engagement, but not the screening and location re-checks. Either rebooking increases the ringer's `scheduled_hours` by the shift's duration in hours. Both rebooking paths also enforce the shift-length ceiling, the rest buffer, the minor curfew and the overlap rule, since a rebooked window is a scheduling decision like any other.

Scheduling can also be delegated to the system. The auto-assign capability takes a `kettle_location_id`, a `starts_at`, an `ends_at` and an optional `break_minutes`, and selects the best eligible ringer for the window. The candidate pool is restricted to ringers whose `engagement_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `screening_status` is `CLEARED` with a `screening_expires_at` later than the requested start, who hold no shift in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` status overlapping the requested interval, and who hold no shift whose boundary lies within 30 minutes of the requested interval. Candidates are ranked by `reliability_score` descending; ties are broken by `scheduled_hours` ascending, and any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. The top-ranked ringer is scheduled and a shift is created. If the pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RINGER` (HTTP 422) and no shift is created.

### 3.4. Counting Room Controls: Variance, Handling Fees and Deposits

A kettle collection is the record of a kettle being pulled and its contents counted. A `CountingRoomTeller` records one with the `kettle_location_id` that was counted, the business date `collected_on`, the `declared_cents` the bell ringer wrote on the collection slip before the teller opened the kettle, and the teller's own counts of `coin_cents`, `currency_cents` and `check_cents`, each of which defaults to 0 when not supplied. The `collected_on` date must fall inside the kettle location's placement window, and a collection recorded for a date outside that window — even a single instant outside it — is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_OUTSIDE_PLACEMENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The collection's `counted_total_cents` is the sum of the coin, currency and cheque counts, and its `handling_fee_cents` is computed from those counts the moment the collection is recorded; a new collection begins in `SUBMITTED` status with a `deposited_cents` of 0 and no `deposit_reference`. Collections can be listed and filtered by `kettle_location_id`, by `deposit_status` and by `collected_on`.

Verification is the counting room's judgement on the teller's work, and it is where the first of the system's two variance figures is produced. The teller supplies the mandatory concurrency token `expected_kettle_collection_version` described in §2.10, and the platform computes the provisional variance as the counted total minus the ringer's declared slip amount. When the absolute value of that variance is 500 cents or less, the collection moves to `VERIFIED`. When it is greater than 500 cents, the collection moves to `VARIANCE_HOLD` instead — the money is not rejected, it is quarantined — and the kettle location's `performance_tier` is downgraded by one step, because a kettle that does not balance is a kettle whose reported performance cannot be trusted. Both paths recompute `counted_total_cents`, recompute `handling_fee_cents`, and record the provisional `variance_cents`; the 500-cent tolerance means that a variance of exactly 500 cents verifies and 501 cents does not.

A held collection can be recounted. The recount route accepts fresh `coin_cents`, `currency_cents` and `check_cents` values, each of which must be zero or greater, replaces the stored counts with them, and returns the collection to `SUBMITTED` so that it can be verified again from scratch. This is the only way out of `VARIANCE_HOLD` in the direction of deposit; the alternative is to remain held.

The handling fee is the cost of turning loose coin into bankable rolls, and it is charged on the counted denominations rather than on the total. The coin is split by denomination with the largest first: 40 percent of `coin_cents` is treated as quarters, 30 percent as dimes, 20 percent as nickels, and whatever remains as pennies. Each denomination's share is then divided by that denomination's roll value to give a roll count, floored: a roll of quarters is 1000 cents, a roll of dimes 500 cents, a roll of nickels 200 cents and a roll of pennies 50 cents. Each denomination's fee is then computed separately as the roll count multiplied by twelve mills and divided by ten, floored to a whole cent. Paper `currency_cents` is charged five parts per thousand of its value, floored to a whole cent, and `check_cents` is charged eight parts per thousand, also floored. The `handling_fee_cents` is the sum of those six separately floored components, and that sum is not rounded again. Because every component is floored before it is added, the total can be a cent or two lower than a naive single rounding would produce, and this per-component-then-sum order is the required one.

Deposit is the point at which counted money becomes banked money, and it is a finance function. A collection in `VERIFIED` status is deposited by a `FinanceOfficer` who supplies the `deposited_cents` the bank accepted. The declared amount must equal the counted total minus the handling fee exactly; a deposit that does not balance to the cent is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A successful deposit moves the collection to `DEPOSITED`, records the `deposited_cents`, mints a server-side `deposit_reference` for the bank transaction, and recomputes both the kettle location's `location_total_cents` and the campaign's `total_raised_cents`, because money that has reached the bank is now part of the reconciled total.

Reconciliation is the last step and it produces the second variance figure. A `FinanceOfficer` reconciles a `DEPOSITED` collection by reporting whether the bank statement matched, supplying `matched` and, when a figure is known, the `bank_confirmed_cents`. A bank-confirmed amount that differs from `deposited_cents` by more than 500 cents is refused outright with `ERR_VARIANCE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 409); a difference of exactly 500 cents is accepted, and 501 cents is not. Within the accepted band the outcome depends on the match report: a collection reported as matched, with the bank-confirmed amount equal to the deposited amount, moves to `RECONCILED`, which is terminal, and its final `variance_cents` is computed as the bank-confirmed deposit minus the counted total net of the handling fee; a collection reported as not matched, with a differing bank-confirmed amount inside the tolerance band, moves instead to `VARIANCE_HOLD` for investigation, and the location and campaign totals are recomputed on that path as well.

The two variance figures are deliberately different numbers computed from the same row. The provisional figure compares the teller's count against the ringer's slip and is produced at verification; the final figure compares the bank's confirmation against the teller's count net of the roll handling fee and is produced at reconciliation. A ringer who under-declares produces a positive provisional variance even when the bank deposit matches the count exactly, and because the final variant deducts the handling fee and reads a different comparison value, identical input data yields a different number in each variant. Neither figure is ever rounded: both are exact integer cents. One further note for the totals described in §3.9: the handling fee is a cost of doing business and is not deducted from the amount reported as raised.

### 3.5. Digital Giving and Attribution

Not every gift arrives in a kettle. A digital gift is captured by a payment channel and recorded by a `DigitalGivingProcessor` with the `campaign_id` it belongs to, the `unit_id` of the corps unit that is credited with it, the `amount_cents`, the `channel` through which it arrived — `QR`, `SMS`, `TERMINAL` or `ONLINE` — the `occurred_at` instant at which the channel captured it, and the processor's `external_ref`. The gift's `kettle_location_id` may be supplied when the donor scanned a code printed for a specific kettle, and is otherwise left empty until the gift is attributed. A new gift begins in `RECEIVED` status with an `attributed_cents` of 0, meaning that it is known to the organization but counted in no total yet.

Three rules guard intake. The amount must be greater than 0 and no greater than 500000 cents, and a gift outside that range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GIFT_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). The `occurred_at` instant must fall inside the owning campaign's window, with both endpoints inclusive, and a gift captured outside the season is refused with `ERR_GIVING_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). And the `external_ref` must be unique across the campaign, so that a processor replay does not double-count a gift; a repeated reference is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF` (HTTP 409). Gifts can be listed and filtered by `kettle_location_id`, by `campaign_id`, by `unit_id` and by `attribution_status`.

Attribution is what moves a gift from known to counted. A gift in `RECEIVED` or `UNATTRIBUTED` status is attributed to a kettle location whose own `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose campaign is the gift's campaign; a location that fails either test is not a valid target. Attribution stores the chosen `kettle_location_id` on the gift, sets `attributed_cents` to the gift's `amount_cents`, and recomputes both the location's `location_total_cents` and the campaign's `total_raised_cents`. A gift may also be attributed without the caller naming a location, in which case the system selects one from the eligible pool: the candidates are the campaign's kettle locations that are `ACTIVE`, whose host site holds an `APPROVED` permission, and whose host site belongs to the unit credited on the gift. Candidates are ranked by `covered_minutes` descending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by `id`, and the top-ranked location receives the gift. When the pool is empty the attribution is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOCATION` (HTTP 422) and the gift is left `UNATTRIBUTED` — not counted anywhere, but not lost.

Some gifts must be taken back. A gift in `ATTRIBUTED` status may be reversed by a `DigitalGivingProcessor` or `FinanceOfficer` who supplies a `reason`, which must be a non-empty string; a gift still sitting in `RECEIVED` may also be reversed directly. Reversal sets the gift's `attribution_status` to `REVERSED`, which is terminal, stores the supplied text in `reversed_reason`, and sets `attributed_cents` to 0. Reversing a gift that had been attributed also recomputes the location's and the campaign's totals, because money that was counted must stop being counted; a gift reversed straight out of `RECEIVED` never contributed to any total, so its reversal touches only its own row.

A gift can also become unattributable without anyone touching it. When the attribution pass runs and finds that the credited unit has no `ACTIVE` kettle location in that campaign at all, a `RECEIVED` gift moves to `UNATTRIBUTED` on its own. This is a parked state rather than a terminal one: a location can later become active and the gift can then be attributed normally.

### 3.6. Seasonal Assistance: Intake, Eligibility and Distribution

Alongside the kettles, the organization runs seasonal assistance programs that distribute toys, food, coats and hygiene supplies to households. An `AssistanceCaseworker` or `CorpsOfficer` creates a program with the `campaign_id` it belongs to, a `name` such as "Christmas Toy Distribution", a `program_type` of `TOY`, `FOOD`, `COAT` or `HYGIENE`, an intake window bounded by `intake_opens_on` and `intake_closes_on`, a `household_cap` limiting how many applications the program will accept, and an optional `per_household_limit_cents` capping the value any one household may receive, which defaults to 0. A program begins in `DRAFT` and accepts nothing until its intake is opened, at which point it moves to `OPEN`. Its `accepted_application_count` tracks the applications that currently hold a place in the program — those in `ELIGIBLE`, `APPROVED`, `DISTRIBUTION_SCHEDULED` or `DISTRIBUTED` status. Programs can be listed and filtered by `campaign_id`, by `status` and by `program_type`.

Intake is the front door. An application is recorded with the `program_id` it applies to, the `applicant_name` of the head of household, the `household_size`, the self-reported `annual_income_cents`, and optionally the `distribution_site_unit_id` of the corps unit that will host the household's appointment. Three gates apply at intake: the program must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409); the logical clock must fall inside the program's intake window with both endpoints inclusive, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_INTAKE_WINDOW` (HTTP 409); and the `household_size` must be between 1 and 12 inclusive, or the request is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A new application begins in `SUBMITTED` status with a `distribution_status` of `NOT_SCHEDULED`.

Intake also produces a first, provisional read on need. The self-reported income is divided by the household size, the quotient floored to whole cents, and that per-member figure is divided by 20000 cents — two hundred dollars — and floored again. The result is subtracted from 100 and the difference is clamped to the range 0 to 100 to give the `provisional_score`. Because this variant reads only what the household said about itself and applies no deductions, a household can score below the threshold at intake and above it later.

Screening replaces the household's word with a caseworker's verification. A `SUBMITTED` application moves to `SCREENING` when a caseworker records the `verified_income_cents`, and the screening route accepts applications whose program is `OPEN` or `CLOSED` — a program that has stopped taking new applications may still finish screening the ones it has. The final score is computed differently from the provisional one: an asset deduction of 150000 cents — one thousand five hundred dollars — is subtracted for each member of the household from the verified income, the result floored at zero so it can never go negative, the adjusted figure is divided by the household size and floored, and that per-member amount is divided by 20000 cents and floored again; the result is subtracted from 100 and clamped to the range 0 to 100 to give the `eligibility_score`. The same household can therefore score above the threshold at screening even when it scored below at intake, because verification replaces the self-report and the per-member asset deduction reduces the income that is measured.

Screening routes the application by its score and by the program's remaining room. An application scoring 65 or higher is eligible; an application scoring below 65 is `INELIGIBLE`, which is terminal. An eligible application is made `ELIGIBLE` when the program still has room — that is, when its accepted count is below the `household_cap` — and is placed on the waitlist as `WAITLISTED` when the program has already reached its cap. The capacity guard on this operation is reported as `ERR_PROGRAM_INTAKE_FULL` (HTTP 409), and an application that cannot be accepted outright is queued rather than discarded. Waitlisted households are not forgotten: when a seat later frees in the program — because another application leaves `ELIGIBLE`, `APPROVED`, `DISTRIBUTION_SCHEDULED` or `DISTRIBUTED` — the waitlisted application of that program with the earliest `created_at` is promoted to `ELIGIBLE`. The same earliest-first rule governs promotions triggered by a missed appointment, so the queue is strictly first-come, first-served.

Approval pins the household to a distribution site. An `ELIGIBLE` application is approved when a caseworker supplies a `distribution_site_unit_id` that references a unit whose `unit_type` is `CORPS`; the application moves to `APPROVED` and the supplied unit is stored on it. Approval also enforces the household ceiling: the accumulated assistance value recorded for the household may not exceed the program's `per_household_limit_cents`, and an approval that would breach the limit is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

An approved household then books an appointment. The scheduling route carries the mandatory concurrency token `expected_assistance_application_version` from §2.10, the `distribution_scheduled_at` instant, and an optional `distribution_slot_minutes` that defaults to 30. Two rules constrain the booking: the requested start may not lie before the current logical clock instant, and the appointment interval — from `distribution_scheduled_at` for `distribution_slot_minutes` — may not overlap another `SCHEDULED` appointment at the same `distribution_site_unit_id`. Appointments that merely touch at their boundary are accepted; overlapping ones are refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A successful booking moves the application to `DISTRIBUTION_SCHEDULED`, sets its `distribution_status` to `SCHEDULED`, stores the appointment instant, and mints a `pickup_code` of six characters that the household will present at the door.

Distribution day closes the loop. When the household presents its code and a caseworker records it, the application moves to `DISTRIBUTED` and its `distribution_status` to `COMPLETED`; the supplied `pickup_code` must equal the stored code or the request is refused as an invalid state for the operation. `DISTRIBUTED` is terminal. When a household does not appear, the caseworker may record the missed appointment once the logical clock has moved past the appointment's end — that is, past `distribution_scheduled_at` plus `distribution_slot_minutes`. A no-show moves the application back to `WAITLISTED`, sets its `distribution_status` to `NO_SHOW`, recomputes the program's accepted count, and immediately promotes the program's earliest `WAITLISTED` application to `ELIGIBLE`. Applications can be listed and filtered by `program_id`, by `eligibility_status` and by `distribution_site_unit_id`.

### 3.7. Corps Community Programs

The corps community centers run programs all year, not only at Christmas, and those programs have their own enrollment economy. A `ProgramDirector` creates one with the `unit_id` of the corps that delivers it, a `name` such as "Sunbeam Youth Club", a `category` of `YOUTH`, `SENIOR`, `WORSHIP` or `COMMUNITY`, a `capacity` stating how many participants may hold a seat, an optional `waitlist_enabled` flag that defaults to true, and a human-readable `meeting_pattern` such as "Tuesdays 16:00". A new program begins in `PLANNED` status, and its `enrolled_count` and `waitlist_count` both start at zero. Programs can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, by `category` and by `status`.

Enrollment is a queue with a deterministic order, not a set. Each enrollment appends an entry carrying the `participant_name` and the `participant_type` to the program's `enrollment_log`, and that log is ordered by the instant the entry was enrolled, ascending, with the participant's name ascending as the tie-break. That ordering is total — two distinct entries cannot tie on both keys — so there is never any ambiguity about who is next. The program's `enrolled_count` is the number of log entries up to the program's `capacity`, and its `waitlist_count` is the number of entries beyond it, never below zero. In other words the first entries up to the capacity hold seats and the remainder wait.

Two behaviors hang off that ordering. The first enrollment of a `PLANNED` program moves it to `ACTIVE`, and that transition requires the program's `capacity` to be at least 1 — a program that can hold nobody cannot start. When an enrollment arrives at a program that is already full, the outcome depends on the `waitlist_enabled` flag: with waitlisting enabled the participant is placed on the waitlist rather than rejected, and with waitlisting disabled the enrollment is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_AT_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409). Withdrawing is the mirror image: the named `participant_name`'s entry is removed from the log, and the earliest waitlisted entry — by the same enrolled-then-name ordering — is promoted into the freed seat, so the queue advances by exactly one.

A program can be paused, resumed and finished. Suspending an `ACTIVE` program moves it to `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended program returns to `ACTIVE` when it is resumed. Completion is the terminal state, and it is refused while anyone is still waiting: a program may only move from `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED` when its `waitlist_count` is exactly zero, because finishing a program with a queue still attached would abandon households the program had promised to serve.

### 3.8. Disaster Canteen Deployment

When a disaster strikes, the organization's canteens are committed through an incident command structure that mirrors the formal emergency-management hierarchy. A `DisasterServicesCoordinator` requests a deployment with the `unit_id` of the unit whose canteen assets are being committed, an `incident_name`, an `incident_command_level` of `TYPE_1`, `TYPE_2`, `TYPE_3`, `TYPE_4` or `TYPE_5` that expresses the incident's complexity, the `requested_canteens` count, and the `command_authority` — the incident commander or emergency operations center authority making the request. A new deployment begins in `REQUESTED` status with an `assigned_canteen_count` of 0. Deployments can be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, by `status` and by `incident_command_level`.

Approval is where the unit's asset pool is spent. A `TerritorialCommander` approves a requested deployment only when the unit's uncommitted pool is large enough: the unit's `canteen_pool_size` minus its `committed_canteen_count` must be at least the deployment's `requested_canteens`, and a request that would over-commit the pool is refused with `ERR_CANTEEN_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). Approval sets `assigned_canteen_count` to the requested count and recomputes the unit's `committed_canteen_count`, so the canteens are reserved from that moment. The same approval route carries a `decision` input, and supplying `DENY` moves the deployment to `DENIED`, which is terminal, without touching the pool.

An approved deployment moves through staging to the field. Staging moves it from `APPROVED` to `STAGED`. Deployment moves it from `STAGED` to `DEPLOYED` and requires that at least one canteen actually be assigned — a deployment with zero assigned canteens cannot take the field — and it stamps `deployed_at` with the current logical clock instant. An approved or staged deployment may instead be cancelled, optionally with a `reason`; cancellation moves it to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, sets `assigned_canteen_count` back to 0, and recomputes the unit's committed count so the assets return to the pool.

Demobilization ends a deployment and settles its bill. A `DEPLOYED` deployment is demobilized by a coordinator who supplies the `demobilized_at` instant, which must not be earlier than the deployment's `deployed_at`. Demobilization moves the deployment to `DEMOBILIZED`, which is terminal, stores the instant, releases the canteens by setting `assigned_canteen_count` to 0 and recomputing the unit's committed count, and computes the deployment's final `deployment_cost_cents`.

The cost of a deployment is billed per canteen per day, with a surge rate after the first two weeks. The billed days are the number of whole 24-hour periods between `deployed_at` and `demobilized_at`, rounded up — so any part of a day counts as a day — and for a deployment that has not yet been demobilized the same computation runs against the current logical clock instant instead. The daily base rate depends on the incident command level: 95000 cents for `TYPE_1`, 72000 for `TYPE_2`, 48000 for `TYPE_3`, 30000 for `TYPE_4` and 18000 for `TYPE_5`. The first 14 billed days are charged at that base rate; any billed days beyond the fourteenth are surge days and are charged at 60 percent of the base rate. The `deployment_cost_cents` is then the `assigned_canteen_count` multiplied by the sum of the regular days' charge and the surge days' charge, rounded half up to a whole cent. A deployment that was denied or cancelled costs zero. The cost is recomputed when a deployment is demobilized and again whenever a deployment is read, so a deployment still in the field reports its cost as of the current logical clock instant.

### 3.9. Location Performance, Reallocation and Campaign Totals

Every kettle is graded, and the grade is meant to drive where the organization's scarcest resource — bell ringing hours — is spent. The grade rests on three quantities. The location's `location_total_cents` is the reconciled proceeds attributed to it, built from the counted totals of its collections that have reached `DEPOSITED` or `RECONCILED` status plus the `attributed_cents` of the digital gifts attributed to it. Its `covered_minutes` is the total duration in minutes of its `COMPLETED` shifts. Its disruption record is the count of its shifts that ended in `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`, set against the count that reached `COMPLETED`.

The derived figures are computed in a fixed order. The covered hours are the covered minutes divided by sixty, floored to a whole hour. The `cents_per_hour` is the location total divided by the covered hours, floored to a whole cent, with the divisor never allowed below one so that a location with no covered hours does not divide by zero. The disruption count is the cancelled shifts plus the no-shows, and the disruption rate percentage is one hundred times that count divided by the greater of the completed plus disrupted count and one, floored to a whole percent. The `performance_index` is the `cents_per_hour` multiplied by one hundred minus the disruption rate percentage, that product divided by one hundred, and floored to a whole number. The `performance_tier` then follows from two tests applied in order. First, a location that has covered fewer than 8 hours is `UNRATED` regardless of its money. Otherwise the tier is set by the cents-per-hour bands: below 4000 cents per hour is `BRONZE`, below 8000 is `SILVER`, below 12000 is `GOLD`, and 12000 or more is `PLATINUM`. Second, a rated location whose `performance_index` falls below 2000 is demoted one step down the tier ladder, so a `PLATINUM` becomes `GOLD`, a `GOLD` becomes `SILVER`, a `SILVER` becomes `BRONZE`, and a `BRONZE` becomes `UNRATED`. The same one-step demotion is applied when a collection at the location is placed in `VARIANCE_HOLD`, as described in §3.4. The whole grading is recomputed when a shift completes, when a shift is cancelled, when a collection at the location is held for variance, and when a reallocation decision is applied.

The performance read exposes all of it for a single location, accepting an optional `on_date` and returning the location's `location_id`, its `location_total_cents`, its `covered_minutes`, its `covered_hours`, its `cents_per_hour`, its `performance_index`, its `performance_tier`, its `coverage_pct` and an `understaffed` flag. The coverage percentage answers a narrower question than the tier: for the evaluated date, it sums the durations in minutes of the location's `COMPLETED` shifts only — scheduled, confirmed, checked-in, cancelled and no-show shifts are all excluded — and divides that by the location's `daily_required_minutes`, floored to a whole percent, with the divisor never below one. A location is `understaffed` when that percentage is below 80; a coverage of exactly 80 percent is not understaffed.

Reallocation is the mechanism that acts on those grades. A `KettleCoordinator` or divisional officer proposes moving hours from a weak kettle to a strong one, supplying the `campaign_id`, the `from_location_id` that will lose hours, the `to_location_id` that will gain them, the `moved_hours`, the `effective_on` date on which the change takes effect, and a `rationale` recording the operator's justification. The proposal immediately carries an `expected_lift_cents` preview, computed as the moved hours multiplied by the target location's `cents_per_hour` minus the source location's `cents_per_hour`, where a negative difference is treated as zero so the lift is never negative, with the product rounded half up to a whole cent. Both cents-per-hour figures are read from the locations as they stand before any application, which is what keeps the preview from depending on the change it is previewing. The `effective_on` date must fall inside the campaign window and must not be earlier than the current logical clock instant at the moment of approval, or the request is refused with `ERR_REALLOCATION_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A proposal has a short life. A `TerritorialCommander` or divisional officer approves it, and approval requires that the source and target be different locations and that the target location be `ACTIVE`; approval freezes the `expected_lift_cents` at its previewed value, and that frozen figure is never recomputed by the application step even though the locations will have changed by then. A proposal may instead be rejected, optionally with a `reason`, which moves it to `REJECTED` and is terminal. An approved decision may be sent back to `PROPOSED` for revision when a coordinator supplies a new `moved_hours` that must be greater than zero, optionally with a new `effective_on`. Proposals can be listed and filtered by `campaign_id`, by `from_location_id`, by `to_location_id` and by `status`.

Applying a decision is what changes the kettles. An approved decision is applied when the logical clock has reached its `effective_on`, and the moved hours may not exceed the source location's remaining scheduled hours for the campaign; a decision that would move more hours than the source has left is refused with `ERR_REALLOCATION_HOURS_EXCEED_SOURCE` (HTTP 409). Application moves the decision to `APPLIED`, which is terminal, stamps `applied_at` with the current logical clock instant, reduces the source location's `daily_required_minutes` by the moved hours multiplied by sixty and increases the target location's by the same amount, and recomputes the `performance_tier` of both locations. From that point the two kettles are expected to staff different amounts of bell ringing each day, and the coverage measure in the performance read judges them against their new expectations.

Campaign totals are assembled from the same reconciled material, and the exclusions matter as much as the inclusions. The counted collections component sums the `counted_total_cents` of collections whose kettle location belongs to the campaign and whose `deposit_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `RECONCILED`; collections still in `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `VARIANCE_HOLD` are excluded, because money that has not cleared the counting room is not raised money. The digital component sums the `attributed_cents` of gifts belonging to the campaign whose `attribution_status` is `ATTRIBUTED`; gifts in `RECEIVED`, `UNATTRIBUTED` or `REVERSED` status are excluded. The campaign's `total_raised_cents` is the sum of the two components, and a single location's `location_total_cents` is the same construction scoped to that location. The roll handling fee described in §3.4 is a cost of banking the money and is not deducted from either figure.
