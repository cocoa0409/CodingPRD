# Youth Orchestra Audition & Seating API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A youth symphony organization runs on two scarce resources that it can never manufacture on demand: chairs in an orchestra and dollars in a financial-aid pool. Every autumn, several hundred young musicians audition behind a blind screen, a panel of adjudicators scores them against a rubric, and an artistic staff must then distribute a fixed number of seats across skill-tiered orchestras while keeping each orchestra's instrumentation in balance. At the same time a finance office assesses tuition, distributes a capped aid budget, and lends organization-owned instruments; a registrar tracks rehearsal attendance and decides whether a late excuse note is credible; and an artistic director programs concerts, picks soloists, and takes ensembles on tour. Today all of this lives in spreadsheets, and the organization's recurring failures are all failures of arithmetic and sequencing: a section is over-offered because two staff worked from the same stale roster, an aid award exceeds a family's income-band cap, a soloist is announced who is on attendance probation.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. The system is deliberately built around conserved quantities and observable orderings. Section fill counts, ensemble roster states and season phases move only through declared transitions; the aid budget is a closed pool whose committed and available halves always sum back to the season's authorization; chair numbers within a section always form a contiguous run starting at one; and the order in which the aid waterfall visits applicants is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Where a rule could plausibly be read two ways — whether a discount is rounded before or after subtraction, whether an exactly-fifteen-minute gap between two audition slots is legal — this document pins the reading.

The API is also written to be testable without waiting for real time to pass. Every deadline, expiry and window is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock** that an operator sets explicitly, so an offer expiry, a tuition due date and a forty-eight-hour excuse window can all be exercised deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Five roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly `ArtisticDirector`, `RegistrarStaff`, `FinanceOfficer`, `Panelist`, and `Guardian`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401). If the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint in §4, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

The organization's governance model is segregation of duties, and it shows up three ways. Artistic judgement belongs to the `ArtisticDirector`, who owns seasons, ensembles, instrumentation sections, adjudication, placements, chairs, concert programming and performance eligibility — and who never records attendance and never moves money. Attendance bookkeeping, the audition calendar, membership records and the operational offer-expiry sweep belong to `RegistrarStaff`, who never adjudicates and never moves money. Tuition finalization and the financial-aid workflow belong to `FinanceOfficer`, who cannot create members, cannot adjudicate, and — as §3.7 details — cannot fund an aid request they personally reviewed. A `Panelist` submits blind rubric sheets and nothing else; because reading a musician's `legal_name` would defeat blind evaluation, the `Panelist` role is the one role excluded from the musician-read and musician-list endpoints, and is authorized on every other read endpoint. A `Guardian` acts as a musician's household.

Three of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ArtisticDirector`, `RegistrarStaff`, or `FinanceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `aid_reviewed_by_user_id` or `aid_awarded_by_user_id` on a tuition account when a finance officer reviews or funds an aid request, or as `excuse_decided_by_user_id` on a participation row when a registrar decides an excuse — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed, and ownership is enforced. When `X-User-Role` is `Guardian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting musician: ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against the `member_id` reached from the target resource — an audition's `member_id`, a placement's `member_id`, a tuition account's `member_id`, or a participation row's `member_id` — and, when registering an audition, against the `member_id` supplied in the request body. When `X-User-Role` is `Panelist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting adjudicator, and it must equal the `panelist_id` supplied in the body of a rubric-sheet submission. A mismatch on either is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This ownership check guards exactly eight operations: registering an audition, submitting a rubric sheet, accepting a placement offer, declining a placement offer or waitlist claim, requesting financial aid, recording a payment, filing an excuse request, and submitting tour requirements.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).

Unless a list endpoint's description says otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Each list endpoint also accepts a small set of optional equality filters, named after the field they filter on, so that any foreign key declared in the data model can be walked in reverse. Ensembles filter on `season_id`, `roster_state`, and `tier`; section quotas on `ensemble_id`, `instrument`, and `balance_state`; musicians on `enrollment_status`, `standing_status`, and `primary_instrument`; adjudicators on `instrument_expertise` and `available_for_panels`; audition slots on `season_id`, `room_code`, `instrument_focus`, and `status`; auditions on `member_id`, `season_id`, `slot_id`, and `status`; rubric sheets on `audition_id`, `panelist_id`, and `revision_round`; placements on `audition_id`, `member_id`, `ensemble_id`, and `status`; chairs on `section_quota_id`, `placement_id`, `member_id`, `predecessor_assignment_id`, and `status`; tuition accounts on `member_id`, `season_id`, `billing_status`, and `aid_status`; calendar events on `ensemble_id`, `soloist_member_id`, `kind`, and `programming_state`; participation rows on `event_id`, `member_id`, `attendance_status`, `performance_eligibility`, and `tour_status`; and seasons on `status`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the system persists carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of the same shape refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative, such as a derived counter changing because a child row was created.

There is exactly one exception. A rubric sheet is immutable once submitted: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and nothing in the system ever mutates a sheet after it lands.

### 2.5. The Logical Season Clock

Every deadline, window and expiry rule in this system reads a single server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the machine's real time. Any of the five roles may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; the call mutates no entity, and its response echoes the `current_utc` now in effect. Subsequent requests evaluate offer expiry, tuition due dates, attendance recording windows, excuse windows and audition-window containment against the new instant.

### 2.6. Cascade Propagation Through the Roster Hierarchy

Because seats, sections, ensembles and seasons form a fill-count hierarchy, a single client request routinely mutates several rows. The system performs those propagations itself, immediately and synchronously, rather than waiting for a client to request them on each affected row. Accepting a placement offer, for example, seats a player and then walks upward: the section's fill count rises, which may flip the section's balance posture, which may flip the owning ensemble's roster posture, which may in turn advance the season's phase. Vacating a chair walks the same ladder downward and additionally reaches sideways into the waitlist. Denying an excuse walks from a participation row up to the musician's standing and then back down into every future performance the musician was slated for, and from there into the programming state of any event where that musician had been named soloist.

Every row touched by such a propagation is a mutation in the sense of §2.4: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented. A cascade that touches four entities therefore bumps four version counters. Derived counters — a season's `ensemble_count` and `seated_ensemble_count`, an ensemble's `seated_count` and `offered_count`, a section's `filled_count`, a slot's `booked_count`, an audition's `submitted_score_count`, an event's `participation_count`, a musician's `unexcused_absence_count`, `tardy_count` and `effective_unexcused_count` — are recomputed as part of the same request that caused them to change, never lazily.

### 2.7. Money, Rubric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are stored and exchanged as integers in whole cents; there is no fractional-cent representation anywhere in the API. Wherever a percentage or a multiplier produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Where a calculation subtracts several independently derived components, the order of rounding is pinned by the rule that defines it and is observable in the stored result; §3.7 states that order explicitly for tuition and for the aid cap.

Scores carry two decimal places. A panelist's `weighted_total`, an audition's `composite_provisional_score` and `composite_final_score`, and a participation row's `attendance_index` are all rounded half up to exactly two decimal places, and every threshold comparison against them is performed on the two-decimal value. Intermediate products inside a single formula are not rounded — only the final stored figure is. A musician's `aid_need_score` is a pure integer with no rounding at all, and chair numbers are integers that form a contiguous run.

### 2.8. Guarding a Roster or an Aid Pool Against Stale Reads

Three write operations act on arithmetic the client cannot see in the row it is writing, and each is guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* row's `version`.

Creating a placement requires a non-optional `expected_ensemble_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target ensemble. Running a chair rotation on a section likewise requires a non-optional `expected_ensemble_version` carrying the last observed `version` of the section's owning ensemble. In both cases, if the supplied value does not equal the ensemble's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENSEMBLE_VERSION` (412). The reason is that an ensemble's `version` advances whenever another writer legitimately consumes or re-arranges its capacity — creating an offer or waitlist row, accepting one, vacating a chair, running a rotation — so a client holding an older version is reasoning about `offered_count`, `seated_count` and section fill figures that no longer hold, and its own capacity arithmetic or chair map can no longer be trusted.

Executing the season's aid award run requires a non-optional `expected_season_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the season. If it does not equal the season's current `version`, the run is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` (412) — another award run, a rescission or a budget-affecting assessment will have moved `aid_committed_cents` and bumped the season's `version`, which means the officer's view of `aid_available_cents`, the very quantity the waterfall distributes, is stale.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_*_version` input and perform no concurrency check.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Reverse Discovery

Business collections in this API are peers: seasons, ensembles, section quotas, members, panelists, audition slots, auditions, panel scores, placements, seat assignments, tuition accounts, ensemble events and event participations are all addressed at the top level, and none is reached through another resource's path. Where an identifier appears in a path — `seasonId`, `ensembleId`, `quotaId`, `memberId`, `panelistId`, `slotId`, `auditionId`, `scoreId`, `placementId`, `seatId`, `accountId`, `eventId`, `participationId` — it is resolved before any body or business rule is considered, and when a path carries more than one identifier they are resolved strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier determines the error. Each resource family has exactly one not-found code: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENSEMBLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_QUOTA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDITION_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_SCORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TUITION_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENSEMBLE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

The same family codes report unresolvable foreign keys supplied in a request *body* — `season_id`, `ensemble_id`, `member_id`, `slot_id`, `audition_id`, `panelist_id`, any element of `panelist_ids`, `event_id`, or `soloist_member_id` — although body resolution happens after body schema validation, as §2.10 sets out.

Two record types are never created by a client request: chairs and tuition accounts both come into existence only as side effects of the placement workflow. They are discoverable exclusively through their filtered list endpoints, which is why those filters (§2.3) are part of the contract rather than a convenience.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once, and which error surfaces is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure decides the response.

The protocol band runs first, in this internal order: header presence and role validity (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`), role authorization for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), ownership for the two entity-backed roles (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), path identifier existence left to right (the family not-found codes), request body schema (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), foreign keys supplied in the body (the family not-found codes again), and finally pagination arguments on list endpoints (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`). Body schema validation covers missing required fields, enum values that are not declared members, integers outside the ranges stated in §3, datetimes that do not parse as ISO 8601 UTC, an `end_at` that is not strictly later than its `start_at`, array cardinality violations on `panelist_ids` and `excerpt_ids`, and a `quorum_required` larger than the number of entries in `panelist_ids`.

Next comes the state band, which asks whether the *target row itself* admits the requested action: season phase succession (`ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`), the generic per-row state gate (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`), the aid workflow's own state gate (`ERR_AID_REQUEST_NOT_ALLOWED_IN_STATE`), the musician's two-dimensional transition gate (`ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_TRANSITION`), the re-audition round cap (`ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_REACHED`), and event programmability (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_PROGRAMMABLE`).

The concurrency band follows, evaluating `expected_ensemble_version` and `expected_season_version` per §2.8. Then comes the cross-entity band, where a *different* entity's state gates the write: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_ACCEPTING_AUDITIONS`, `ERR_ENSEMBLE_NOT_ACCEPTING_PLACEMENTS`, `ERR_AUDITION_NOT_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_ENSEMBLE_NOT_SEATED`, `ERR_EVENT_KIND_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_DELINQUENT_ACCOUNT_BLOCKS_CLOSE`, `ERR_SEASON_PRECONDITION_UNMET`, and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_FINAL`.

After cross-entity state comes the safety, segregation-of-duties and blind-panel-integrity band, in this internal order: `ERR_PANEL_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ON_PANEL`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_SOLOIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SOLOIST`. Then the temporal band — window containment and clock-relative windows, reported as `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_AUDITION_WINDOW`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_STARTED` and `ERR_EXCUSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Then the referential, uniqueness and per-row-constraint band, in this internal order: `ERR_SLOT_INSTRUMENT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_REPERTOIRE_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`, the six uniqueness codes (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_SCORE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_QUOTA`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TUITION_ACCOUNT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTICIPATION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDITION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT`, which share one rank because each guards a different tuple), `ERR_TIER_ABOVE_RECOMMENDATION`, `ERR_NO_SECTION_FOR_INSTRUMENT`, `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_PERMITTED`, and `ERR_AUDITION_SLOT_FULL`.

Below that sits the interval-overlap band on shared resources — `ERR_ROOM_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, `ERR_MEMBER_EVENT_CONFLICT`, `ERR_PANELIST_UNAVAILABLE` — then the fiscal band in this internal order: `ERR_AID_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`, `ERR_NO_AID_BUDGET`, `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, `ERR_GUEST_ARTIST_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_TOUR_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. Last of all comes capacity and quota: `ERR_SECTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_SECTION_FULL_AT_ACCEPTANCE`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_IN_ENSEMBLE`.

Four cross-band pairings are worth calling out because they are easy to invert. An oversized aid request on an account that is already approved reports the aid state error, not the fiscal size error, because state outranks fiscal. Naming an ineligible soloist while also exceeding the guest-artist fee cap reports the soloist error, because safety outranks fiscal. An audition slot that falls outside the season's audition window *and* double-books its room reports the window error, because temporal outranks interval overlap. And scheduling an audition into a slot whose instrument does not match the musician *and* which is already full reports the instrument mismatch, because the referential check outranks the per-row capacity check. Likewise, within the safety and uniqueness bands, a conflicted panelist who is also submitting a second sheet for the same round reports the conflict of interest, because blind-panel integrity outranks the duplicate check.

### 2.11. The Conserved Aid Pool

Financial aid is a closed pool, and the system treats that as an invariant rather than a convention. A season authorizes `aid_budget_cents`; at every observable moment thereafter, `aid_committed_cents` plus `aid_available_cents` equals `aid_budget_cents` exactly. `aid_committed_cents` is the sum of `aid_awarded_cents` over that season's accounts whose `aid_status` is `APPROVED`, and `aid_available_cents` is the authorization minus the commitment. Every operation that touches aid — an award run granting money, a rescission returning it, a final assessment capping an award and refunding the surplus — moves the two halves in equal and opposite directions in the same request. No operation may drive `aid_available_cents` negative.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Season and Its Orchestras

An `ArtisticDirector` opens the artistic year by creating a **Season**, supplying a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, a `year_label` used on invoices such as "2026-27", an `audition_window_start` and an `audition_window_end` that bound when audition slots may be held, a `tuition_base_cents` figure of at least 1, a `tuition_due_at` instant, and an `aid_budget_cents` authorization of at least 0. The season is born in `PLANNING`, with `aid_committed_cents` at 0, `aid_available_cents` equal to `aid_budget_cents`, `ensemble_count` and `seated_ensemble_count` both 0, and `version` 1.

A season's `status` walks a single ladder: `PLANNING` → `AUDITIONS_OPEN` → `PLACEMENT` → `IN_SEASON` → `CLOSED`, where `CLOSED` is terminal. The director advances it one rung at a time by supplying a `to_status`; any `to_status` that is not the declared immediate successor of the current value — a skip ahead or any reversal — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. Each successful hop increments the season's `version`. Each rung also has its own admission criteria, reported as `ERR_SEASON_PRECONDITION_UNMET`: reaching `AUDITIONS_OPEN` requires `ensemble_count` of at least 1 and requires every ensemble of the season to own at least one instrumentation section; reaching `PLACEMENT` requires that no audition of the season sits in `SCORED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` (auditions still in `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` may remain — they simply can no longer be scored, because slot booking closes with the audition phase); and reaching `IN_SEASON` requires `seated_ensemble_count` to equal `ensemble_count`.

Closing a season additionally requires that no tuition account of that season carries `billing_status` `DELINQUENT`; a delinquent account refuses the close with `ERR_DELINQUENT_ACCOUNT_BLOCKS_CLOSE`. When the close does succeed, three cascading hops fire in order. First, every ensemble of the season whose `roster_state` is `FORMING` or `SEATED` moves to `CLOSED`. Second, because those ensembles closed, every musician holding an `ACTIVE` chair in them whose `graduation_year` equals the calendar year of the season's `tuition_due_at` and whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED` moves to `GRADUATED`. Third, because those musicians graduated, each of them whose `alumni_engagement_opt_in` is true moves on to `ALUMNI`. Every ensemble and every musician touched by these hops has its `version` incremented, as does the season itself.

Inside a season the director creates one **Ensemble** per skill tier, supplying the owning `season_id`, a `name` such as "Youth Symphony", a `tier` of `SYMPHONY`, `PHILHARMONIC` or `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA`, a `min_total_size` of at least 1, a `max_total_size` no smaller than `min_total_size`, and a `rehearsal_weekday` drawn from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU`, `FRI`, `SAT` and `SUN`. A new ensemble starts with `roster_state` `FORMING`, `seated_count` 0 and `offered_count` 0, and its creation increments the season's `ensemble_count` and `version`. The tier is not merely a label: it selects the tuition multiplier of §3.7 and bounds which audition results may be offered a chair, per §3.5.

Instrumentation balance is expressed as one **SectionQuota** row per instrument the ensemble seats. The director supplies the `ensemble_id`, the `instrument` — one of `VIOLIN`, `VIOLA`, `CELLO`, `BASS`, `FLUTE`, `OBOE`, `CLARINET`, `BASSOON`, `HORN`, `TRUMPET`, `TROMBONE`, `PERCUSSION` — a `min_count` of at least 0, a `target_count` that is at least `min_count` and at least 1, and a `max_count` no smaller than `target_count`. Two optional flags shape the chair policy: `principal_required`, which defaults to true and decides whether chair one of the section carries the principal designation, and `rotation_policy`, which defaults to `ROTATING` and may instead be `FIXED`. Only one section may exist per combination of `ensemble_id` and `instrument`; a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_QUOTA`. A fresh section starts with `filled_count` 0, `rotation_cycle_index` 0, and a `balance_state` of `UNDERFILLED` when `min_count` is greater than 0 and `BALANCED` when `min_count` is 0. Creating a section increments the owning ensemble's `version`.

Thereafter `balance_state` is derived purely from `filled_count`: it is `UNDERFILLED` while `filled_count` is below `min_count`, `BALANCED` while `filled_count` is at least `min_count` but still below `target_count`, and `FULL` once `filled_count` is at least `target_count`. Seat creation therefore drives `UNDERFILLED → BALANCED` and `BALANCED → FULL`, and — on a section configured with `min_count` equal to `target_count`, where no intermediate band exists — directly `UNDERFILLED → FULL`. Seat vacancy drives the mirror-image moves `FULL → BALANCED`, `BALANCED → UNDERFILLED`, and, on those same equal-bound sections, directly `FULL → UNDERFILLED`. Every one of these six moves increments the section's `version`, and each move that arrives at or departs from `FULL` re-evaluates the owning ensemble's roster posture.

An ensemble's `roster_state` is likewise derived. It advances `FORMING → SEATED` when every one of its sections has `balance_state` `FULL` and its `seated_count` — the sum of `filled_count` across its sections — is at least `min_total_size`; this sets the roster state, increments the ensemble's `version`, and re-evaluates the season's `seated_ensemble_count`, which may itself advance the season from `PLACEMENT` to `IN_SEASON`. It falls back `SEATED → FORMING` when at least one section is no longer `FULL` after a chair was vacated, which decrements the season's `seated_ensemble_count` and increments the ensemble's `version`. And it reaches the terminal `CLOSED` from either `FORMING` or `SEATED` when the season closes, incrementing the ensemble's `version`. An ensemble's `offered_count` tracks how many of its placements currently sit in `OFFERED`.

### 3.2. Musicians, Their Two Standings, and the Adjudicator Roster

`RegistrarStaff` registers each musician as a **Member**, supplying a `legal_name`, a `primary_instrument` drawn from the same twelve instruments a section may seat, a `grade_level` between 4 and 12, a `graduation_year` of 2026 or later, and a declared `household_income_band` of `BAND_A`, `BAND_B` or `BAND_C`. Three inputs are optional: `years_in_org`, the count of completed prior seasons, between 0 and 12 and defaulting to 0; `sibling_enrolled`, defaulting to false; and `alumni_engagement_opt_in`, defaulting to false. The server derives `instrument_class` from the instrument — `STRING` for `VIOLIN`, `VIOLA`, `CELLO` and `BASS`, `PERCUSSION` for `PERCUSSION`, and `WIND` for every other instrument — and that class later selects the loan fee. A new musician starts with `enrollment_status` `PROSPECT`, `standing_status` `CLEAR`, and `unexcused_absence_count`, `tardy_count`, `effective_unexcused_count` and `probation_baseline_count` all 0.

A musician carries two genuinely independent state dimensions on the same row, and moving one never implies anything about the other — an `ENROLLED` musician can sit on attendance probation, and a `PROSPECT` can be under a financial hold. The membership dimension, `enrollment_status`, runs over `PROSPECT`, `ENROLLED`, `GRADUATED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `ALUMNI`, of which `ALUMNI` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal. The good-standing dimension, `standing_status`, runs over `CLEAR`, `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` and `FINANCIAL_HOLD`; when both an attendance cause and a financial cause hold at once, the precedence is `FINANCIAL_HOLD` above `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` above `CLEAR`.

A registrar drives either dimension by naming a `dimension` of `ENROLLMENT` or `STANDING` together with a `to_status`. If the pair is not a declared transition out of the musician's current value on that dimension, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEMBER_TRANSITION`; that includes any attempt to reach `ALUMNI` from anything other than `GRADUATED`, and any attempt to reach `ALUMNI` for a musician whose `alumni_engagement_opt_in` is false. On the membership dimension the registrar may move `ENROLLED → GRADUATED`, which requires that no tuition account of that musician is `DELINQUENT` (otherwise `ERR_DELINQUENT_ACCOUNT_BLOCKS_CLOSE`) and which vacates every `ACTIVE` chair the musician holds, running the full vacate cascade of §3.5 on each; `GRADUATED → ALUMNI`, which requires `alumni_engagement_opt_in` to be true; `PROSPECT → WITHDRAWN`, which moves every placement of that musician sitting in `OFFERED` or `WAITLISTED` to `WITHDRAWN`; and `ENROLLED → WITHDRAWN`, which does the same to open placements *and* vacates every `ACTIVE` chair with the full vacate cascade. `PROSPECT → ENROLLED` is not a registrar action at all: the server performs it when a placement offer is accepted. Every one of these moves increments the musician's `version`.

On the standing dimension the only manual move is `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION → CLEAR`, which a registrar may perform only when no tuition account of that musician is `DELINQUENT`, again reported as `ERR_DELINQUENT_ACCOUNT_BLOCKS_CLOSE` when one is. Clearing probation pins `probation_baseline_count` to the musician's current `effective_unexcused_count`, which is the reset mechanism described in §3.8: the next probation requires three *further* effective absences rather than re-firing immediately. Every other standing move is the server's own. A musician moves `CLEAR → ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` when an attendance mutation raises the effective absence count past the threshold. A musician moves `CLEAR → FINANCIAL_HOLD`, or `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION → FINANCIAL_HOLD`, as soon as any of their tuition accounts becomes `DELINQUENT` — and in the second case the attendance cause is not forgotten, because it is remembered in `probation_baseline_count`. Once a payment or an aid award leaves no `DELINQUENT` account for that musician, the hold is released: to `CLEAR` when the effective absence count minus `probation_baseline_count` is below three, and to `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` when that difference is three or more, because the attendance cause still holds.

The `ArtisticDirector` separately maintains the adjudicator roster. Each **Panelist** is registered with a `display_name` and an `instrument_expertise` drawn from the instrument enumeration, plus three optional inputs: a `panel_role` of `ADJUDICATOR` (the default) or `CHAIR`, where a chair scores blind like everyone else and may additionally sign the panel sheet; `conflict_member_ids`, an array of musician identifiers the adjudicator privately teaches, defaulting to empty; and `available_for_panels`, defaulting to true, which controls whether the adjudicator may be seated on new panels.

### 3.3. The Audition Calendar: Rooms, Panels and Repertoire Requirements

`RegistrarStaff` opens each **AuditionSlot** while the season is in `AUDITIONS_OPEN`; attempting it in any other phase is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_ACCEPTING_AUDITIONS`. The slot is described by its `season_id`, a `room_code` of 1 to 16 characters, a `start_at` and an `end_at` that must be strictly later than the start, an array of `panelist_ids`, and an `instrument_focus` naming the one instrument the slot auditions. `panelist_ids` must hold between two and five distinct adjudicator identifiers. Three inputs are optional: `required_excerpt_codes`, an array of excerpt code strings that every audition booked into the slot must have prepared, defaulting to empty; `quorum_required`, the number of submitted sheets that flips an audition to scored, defaulting to 3, constrained to between two and five, and never larger than the number of entries in `panelist_ids`; and `capacity`, the number of auditions the slot may hold, defaulting to 1 and constrained to between one and eight.

Slot times must be contained in the season's audition window: `start_at` must be at or after `audition_window_start`, and `end_at` must be at or before `audition_window_end`. A slot that escapes either bound is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_AUDITION_WINDOW`, and per §2.10 that window check is evaluated before the room-occupancy check, so a slot that is both out of window and double-booked reports the window error.

Room occupancy is **buffered** on both sides by a fifteen-minute turnover allowance, because a room needs resetting between candidates. Two slots in the same `room_code` conflict when the first slot's `start_at` is earlier than the second slot's `end_at` advanced by fifteen minutes, and simultaneously the second slot's `start_at` is earlier than the first slot's `end_at` advanced by fifteen minutes. Both comparisons are strict, which pins the boundary precisely: two slots in the same room separated by a gap of exactly fifteen minutes do not conflict, while a gap of fourteen minutes does. A conflict is `ERR_ROOM_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. The same buffered comparison governs a panelist's own calendar: every identifier in `panelist_ids` must belong to an adjudicator whose `available_for_panels` is true, and no requested adjudicator may already be seated on another slot whose buffered interval overlaps this one — either failure is `ERR_PANELIST_UNAVAILABLE`.

A new slot starts `OPEN` with `booked_count` 0. `booked_count` counts the auditions pointing at the slot whose own status is `SCHEDULED`, `SCORED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `ADJUDICATED`, and the slot's `status` is derived from it: `OPEN` while `booked_count` is below `capacity`, `FULL` once `booked_count` reaches or exceeds `capacity`.

### 3.4. From Audition Request to Adjudicated Score

Either a `Guardian` acting for their own musician or `RegistrarStaff` acting on a family's behalf registers an **Audition**, supplying the `member_id`, the `season_id`, a `declared_program` of 1 to 200 characters naming the solo repertoire, an `excerpt_ids` array of between one and six distinct excerpt code strings the musician has prepared, and an optional `scale_attested` flag defaulting to false that records whether the required scale set was attested at registration. Registration is only accepted while the season is in `PLANNING` or `AUDITIONS_OPEN`; a later phase is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_ACCEPTING_AUDITIONS`. Only one non-terminal audition may exist per combination of `member_id` and `season_id`, and a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDITION`. The new row is created in `REQUESTED` with `revision_round` 0 and `slot_id` null, and the server mints a `blind_code` — an opaque panel-facing label such as "CELLO-014", built from the musician's `primary_instrument` plus a per-instrument sequence number — which is the only identity the panel ever sees.

Booking the audition into a slot advances it `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED`, and the same registrar action re-books a musician sent back for another round, advancing `REVISION_REQUESTED → SCHEDULED`; from any other status the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`. The caller supplies the chosen `slot_id`. Booking is gated on a chain of conditions. The season must be in `AUDITIONS_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_ACCEPTING_AUDITIONS`. The musician's `standing_status` must be `CLEAR` — both `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` and `FINANCIAL_HOLD` block booking, and a blocked booking is reported as the state conflict `ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`. The slot's `season_id` must equal the audition's `season_id` and the slot's `instrument_focus` must equal the musician's `primary_instrument`; either mismatch is `ERR_SLOT_INSTRUMENT_MISMATCH`. The audition's `excerpt_ids` must cover every code listed in the slot's `required_excerpt_codes`, and when that list is non-empty the audition's `scale_attested` must be true; either failure is `ERR_REPERTOIRE_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. The slot's own times must lie inside the season's audition window, and its `start_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock — booking a slot that has already begun is refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`. Finally the slot must still be `OPEN`, meaning its `booked_count` is below its `capacity`; a full slot is `ERR_AUDITION_SLOT_FULL`. A re-booking from `REVISION_REQUESTED` additionally requires `revision_round` to be no greater than two.

A successful booking sets the audition's `slot_id`, increments the target slot's `booked_count` by one, flips that slot's `status` to `FULL` when the incremented count reaches or exceeds `capacity`, and increments both the audition's and the slot's `version` by one each. When the booking is a re-booking onto a different slot, the previously held slot's `booked_count` is decremented and its `status` returns to `OPEN` once the count falls below `capacity`.

A `Panelist` submits one blind rubric sheet — a **PanelScore** — against a `SCHEDULED` audition, supplying their own `panelist_id` together with four rubric integers each between 0 and 25: `tone_score`, `rhythm_score`, `intonation_score` and `sight_reading_score`. Three integrity gates guard the submission, in the order §2.10 fixes. The submitting adjudicator's `conflict_member_ids` must not contain the audition's `member_id` — scoring a private student is a blind-panel integrity violation reported as `ERR_PANEL_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`, and it outranks the duplicate check below. The `panelist_id` must appear in the `panelist_ids` of the slot the audition currently occupies, and that adjudicator's `instrument_expertise` must equal the slot's `instrument_focus`; otherwise `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_ON_PANEL`. And only one sheet may exist per combination of `audition_id`, `panelist_id` and `revision_round`, a second being `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_SCORE`.

Each sheet's `weighted_total` is computed before the row is persisted, by weighting the four raw rubric integers and rounding the sum half up to two decimal places: tone counts 1.2 times its raw score, rhythm 1.0 times, intonation 1.0 times, and sight-reading 0.8 times. No intermediate product is rounded — only the final sum. Because the four weights total 4.0 and each rubric integer is capped at 25, a `weighted_total` always lies between 0.00 and 100.00. The sheet snapshots the audition's current `revision_round` so that sheets from superseded rounds can be excluded from later arithmetic, and its `blind` flag is always true, recording that the adjudicator saw only the `blind_code` and never the musician's `legal_name`. Sheets are immutable and carry no version counter, per §2.4.

The audition's `submitted_score_count` counts the sheets belonging to its *current* `revision_round`. When that count reaches the slot's `quorum_required`, the server advances the audition `SCHEDULED → SCORED` and stores `composite_provisional_score`: the plain arithmetic mean of the `weighted_total` values of every sheet in the current round, rounded half up to two decimal places, with no trimming whatever the number of sheets. The audition's `version` increments.

An `ArtisticDirector` closes a `SCORED` audition by adjudicating it, which requires the season to be in `AUDITIONS_OPEN` or `PLACEMENT` and requires `submitted_score_count` to still be at least the slot's `quorum_required`. Adjudication stores `composite_final_score` using a conditional symmetric trim over the same current-round sheet set. When the round holds four or more sheets, exactly one sheet holding the maximum `weighted_total` and exactly one sheet holding the minimum are discarded — and where several sheets tie on an extreme value, the one with the earliest `created_at` is the one discarded, never the one with the lowest `id` — after which the mean of the surviving sheets is rounded half up to two decimal places. When the round holds fewer than four sheets, nothing is discarded and the mean is taken over all of them. The two composites therefore diverge whenever the discarded extremes are not symmetric about the mean: four sheets scoring 81.80, 94.60, 66.60 and 82.40 yield a provisional composite of 81.35 and a final composite of 82.10.

Immediately after the final composite is stored, `tier_recommendation` is derived from it by three inclusive threshold comparisons on the two-decimal value: a composite of at least 85.00 recommends `SYMPHONY`; otherwise a composite of at least 75.00 recommends `PHILHARMONIC`; otherwise a composite of at least 62.50 recommends `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA`; and anything below that is `NOT_PLACED`. The inclusivity is observable at the boundary — 62.50 recommends `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA` while 62.49 recommends `NOT_PLACED`. The audition then reaches `ADJUDICATED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments.

The director may instead send a `SCORED` audition back for another hearing, advancing it `SCORED → REVISION_REQUESTED`. That requires the season to be in `AUDITIONS_OPEN` and requires `revision_round` to be strictly below two; a third revision request on the same audition is refused with `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_REACHED`. The revision increments `revision_round` by one, clears `composite_provisional_score` back to null, decrements the previously held slot's `booked_count` by one and returns that slot's `status` to `OPEN` when its count falls below `capacity`, and clears the audition's `slot_id` to null. Sheets from earlier rounds are retained in the database but permanently excluded from both composite variants, which is precisely what makes a revision observable in the arithmetic. Both the audition's `version` and the released slot's `version` increment by one each. The audition may then be re-booked, closing the cycle `SCORED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SCHEDULED → SCORED`.

### 3.5. Placement: Offers, the Waitlist, and Taking a Chair

Once the season stands in `PLACEMENT`, the `ArtisticDirector` distributes chairs by creating **Placement** rows. The request names the `audition_id` whose result justifies the offer, the `ensemble_id` being offered, an `offer_kind` of either `OFFER` or `WAITLIST`, and the `expected_ensemble_version` guard of §2.8. When the kind is `OFFER`, an `offer_expires_at` instant is required and must be strictly later than the logical clock; on the `WAITLIST` branch the field is ignored. The placement's `member_id` is never supplied by the caller — it is copied from the referenced audition.

Several gates apply to both branches. The season must be in `PLACEMENT` and the target ensemble's `roster_state` must be `FORMING`; a `SEATED` or `CLOSED` ensemble accepts no further offers or waitlist claims, and either failure is `ERR_ENSEMBLE_NOT_ACCEPTING_PLACEMENTS`. The referenced audition must be `ADJUDICATED`, must carry a `tier_recommendation` other than `NOT_PLACED`, and must belong to the same season as the ensemble; otherwise `ERR_AUDITION_NOT_ADJUDICATED`. The tier bands are ordered with `SYMPHONY` highest, then `PHILHARMONIC`, then `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA`, and the target ensemble's `tier` must be equal to or below the audition's `tier_recommendation` — a musician may always be offered a lower tier than recommended, never a higher one, and a higher one is `ERR_TIER_ABOVE_RECOMMENDATION`. The ensemble must own a section whose `instrument` equals the musician's `primary_instrument`, or the request is `ERR_NO_SECTION_FOR_INSTRUMENT`. No other placement for the same musician and ensemble may already sit in `OFFERED`, `WAITLISTED` or `ACCEPTED`, and at most one non-terminal placement may exist per combination of `audition_id` and `ensemble_id`; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT`.

Capacity is checked only on the `OFFER` branch, and it is checked twice: for the section, `filled_count` plus the number of placements already sitting in `OFFERED` on that same ensemble-and-instrument pairing must be strictly below the section's `max_count`; and for the ensemble, `seated_count` plus `offered_count` must be strictly below `max_total_size`. Either breach is `ERR_SECTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A `WAITLIST` row is exempt from both, because it reserves nothing.

The two branches then diverge sharply, and the divergence is the point of the fork. An `OFFER` is created in `OFFERED` with `waitlist_rank` null; it increments the ensemble's `offered_count` by one, thereby consuming section capacity; it increments the ensemble's `version`; and — when the musician has no tuition account for that season yet — it opens one, snapshotting the offered ensemble's tier into `ensemble_tier` and starting the account in `billing_status` `PENDING_ASSESSMENT` (a second account for the same `member_id` and `season_id` would be `ERR_DUPLICATE_TUITION_ACCOUNT`). A `WAITLIST` is created in `WAITLISTED` with `waitlist_rank` set to one more than the number of placements already `WAITLISTED` on that same ensemble-and-instrument pairing; it increments the ensemble's `version`; and it deliberately does *neither* of the offer branch's two commitments — `offered_count` is untouched and no tuition account is opened.

A `Guardian` accepts their own open offer, advancing it `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`. Acceptance requires the logical clock to be strictly earlier than `offer_expires_at`; requires the musician's `standing_status` to be anything other than `FINANCIAL_HOLD`; requires the musician's tuition account for that season to carry a `billing_status` other than `DELINQUENT`; requires the section's `filled_count` to still be below its `max_count`, so that an offer whose section filled up while it sat open is refused with `ERR_SECTION_FULL_AT_ACCEPTANCE`; and requires the ensemble's `seated_count` to still be below `max_total_size`. The only body input is the optional `instrument_loan_requested` flag, recording whether the family is taking an organization instrument; when it is true, `instrument_class_loaned` is set to the musician's own `instrument_class`, and when it is false or absent, `instrument_class_loaned` stays null.

Acceptance is the system's deepest cascade. `accepted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. A **SeatAssignment** is then created in `ACTIVE` status, carrying the `placement_id` that produced it, the `section_quota_id` of the matching section, the seat holder's `member_id`, a `predecessor_assignment_id` of null because it opens a fresh rotation chain, a `rotation_cycle_index` copied from the section's current `rotation_cycle_index`, and a `ranking_score` snapshotted from the audition's `composite_final_score`; the section's chair order is then recomputed per §3.6 and `filled_count` increments by one. Because `filled_count` reached `target_count`, the section's `balance_state` advances to `FULL`. Because every section of the ensemble is now `FULL` and `seated_count` is at least `min_total_size`, the ensemble's `roster_state` advances `FORMING → SEATED`. And because that was the season's last unseated ensemble, the season's `status` advances `PLACEMENT → IN_SEASON`. Alongside that four-hop climb, the musician's `enrollment_status` moves `PROSPECT → ENROLLED` if it was `PROSPECT`; the preliminary tuition assessment of §3.7 runs on the tuition account and moves its `billing_status` `PENDING_ASSESSMENT → CURRENT` once `assessed_cents` is above zero; the ensemble's `offered_count` decrements by one and its `version` increments; and every *other* placement of the same musician in the same season sitting in `OFFERED` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, while any `ACCEPTED` or already-terminal sibling row is left untouched.

Declining is also the `Guardian`'s action, and it too forks on the row's current state. Declining an `OFFERED` row moves it to `DECLINED`, decrements the ensemble's `offered_count`, increments the ensemble's `version`, and — because seat capacity has just been freed — runs the waitlist promotion described below. Declining a `WAITLISTED` row instead moves it to `WITHDRAWN`, densely renumbers the `waitlist_rank` of the remaining `WAITLISTED` rows for that ensemble-and-instrument pairing from 1 upward, and increments the ensemble's `version` — but runs *no* promotion, precisely because no seat capacity was freed. Neither branch has any precondition beyond the row being in the expected state.

`RegistrarStaff` runs the offer-expiry sweep as an operational checkpoint over a whole season. Every placement of that season sitting in `OFFERED` whose `offer_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, decrementing its ensemble's `offered_count`; the ensemble's `version` increments once per affected ensemble; and the waitlist promotion runs for each freed section. The sweep reports how many offers it expired as `expired_count` and how many waitlisted rows it promoted as `promoted_count`.

**Waitlist promotion** runs whenever a section loses a claim on a seat: an `OFFERED` placement leaving for `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`, or an `ACCEPTED` placement being `RELEASED` by a chair vacate. It operates on one section — the one matching the vacating placement's ensemble and the musician's instrument. The queue is every placement of that ensemble sitting in `WAITLISTED` whose musician's `primary_instrument` equals the section's `instrument`, ordered by the referenced audition's `composite_final_score` descending, then by the placement's own `created_at` ascending; the row's `id` is never a tie-break. Every row in the queue receives a fresh 1-based `waitlist_rank` in that order, and `WITHDRAWN` rows are excluded permanently. If the queue is non-empty and the section's `filled_count` plus the ensemble's `offered_count` is strictly below the section's `max_count`, the head of the queue is promoted: its status moves `WAITLISTED → OFFERED`, its `offer_expires_at` is set to the logical clock advanced by seventy-two hours, its `waitlist_rank` is cleared to null, the ensemble's `offered_count` increments by one, a tuition account is opened for that musician and season if none exists, and the rows behind it are re-ranked densely from 1.

Vacating a chair is the `ArtisticDirector`'s action and runs the mirror cascade. It requires the chair to be `ACTIVE` and its owning placement to be `ACCEPTED`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`. The chair moves `ACTIVE → VACATED` and its `version` increments; the owning placement moves `ACCEPTED → RELEASED`; the section's `filled_count` decrements by one and the chair order is recomputed over the remaining `ACTIVE` chairs so that chair numbers stay contiguous from 1; the section's `balance_state` and the ensemble's `roster_state` are re-evaluated, which may demote the section and flip the ensemble `SEATED → FORMING` with the season's `seated_ensemble_count` decremented; waitlist promotion runs on the freed section; and the ensemble's `version` increments. `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `RELEASED` are all terminal placement states.

### 3.6. Chairs, Principals and the Rotation Policy

Chair order within a section is not stored by the caller; it is derived and re-derived every time the section's set of `ACTIVE` chairs changes — on acceptance, on vacate, on waitlist promotion, and on rotation. The `ACTIVE` chairs of the one section are sorted by `ranking_score` descending, then by the seat holder's `years_in_org` descending, then by the chair row's own `created_at` ascending, with the row's `id` never used as a tie-break. Each chair's `chair_number` is then its 1-based position in that order, and `is_principal` is true exactly when `chair_number` is 1 and the section's `principal_required` is true. `ROTATED_OUT` and `VACATED` chairs keep whatever `chair_number` they held historically and take no part in the ordering. The resulting invariant is strict: the `chair_number` values over a section's `ACTIVE` chairs are always exactly the whole numbers from 1 through `filled_count`, with no gaps and no duplicates, so their sum is always the section size multiplied by one more than the section size, divided by two.

A rotation policy lets a section share the principal chair over a season. The `ArtisticDirector` runs one rotation cycle over a section, supplying the `expected_ensemble_version` guard of §2.8. Rotation is permitted only when the section's `rotation_policy` is `ROTATING` and its `filled_count` is at least 2 — a `FIXED` section, or a section holding fewer than two `ACTIVE` chairs, is refused with `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_PERMITTED` — and only when the owning ensemble's `roster_state` is `SEATED`, since a `FORMING` ensemble cannot rotate chairs even if the section itself is `FULL`, which is refused with `ERR_ENSEMBLE_NOT_SEATED`.

The rotation itself is a single-cycle permutation. Writing the section's `filled_count` as its size, every `ACTIVE` chair moves to `ROTATED_OUT` and a successor chair is created in `ACTIVE` status that copies the predecessor's `placement_id`, `section_quota_id`, `member_id` and `ranking_score`, sets `predecessor_assignment_id` to the predecessor row's `id`, sets `rotation_cycle_index` to one more than the predecessor's, and takes a `chair_number` equal to the section size when the predecessor sat in chair 1 and equal to the predecessor's `chair_number` minus one otherwise. In other words the principal steps back to the last chair and everyone else advances one chair forward. `is_principal` is recomputed on each successor from its new `chair_number` and the section's `principal_required`. The section's `rotation_cycle_index` increments by one, and because exactly one `ACTIVE` successor replaces each `ACTIVE` predecessor, both `filled_count` and the set of chair numbers are conserved — `balance_state` cannot move as a result of a rotation. The ensemble's `version` increments. One subtlety matters: the ranking-based chair order is **not** re-applied after a rotation. The rotated order is authoritative until the set of `ACTIVE` chairs changes again for some other reason.

### 3.7. Tuition, Financial Aid and the Instrument Loan Program

A **TuitionAccount** is never created by a client request. It comes into being as a side effect of a placement offer or a waitlist promotion, and it is unique per combination of `member_id` and `season_id`. It records the `ensemble_tier` snapshot taken from the ensemble that was offered, and it is discoverable only through its filtered list endpoint.

Tuition is assessed in two variants, and which one produced the current figure is recorded in `assessment_variant`. The tier multiplier is the same in both: `SYMPHONY` charges 1.25 times the season's `tuition_base_cents`, `PHILHARMONIC` charges 1.00 times, and `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA` charges 0.80 times, with that gross product rounded half up to whole cents.

The **PRELIMINARY** variant runs inside placement acceptance, while the family's discount evidence is still unverified. It forces both discount components to zero — `sibling_discount_cents` is 0 and `loyalty_credit_cents` is 0 — so `assessed_cents` equals the gross tier-adjusted figure exactly. The instrument loan program is priced at the same moment: when the acceptance requested a loan, `instrument_loan_fee_cents` is 4500 cents for a `STRING` instrument, 6500 cents for a `WIND` instrument and 9000 cents for `PERCUSSION`, selected by `instrument_class_loaned`; when no loan was requested it is 0. The loan fee is a charge, never a subsidy target: aid never offsets it. The account's `balance_cents` is then `assessed_cents`, less `aid_effective_cents`, plus `instrument_loan_fee_cents`, less `payments_total_cents`. With a positive `assessed_cents` the account's `billing_status` moves `PENDING_ASSESSMENT → CURRENT`.

The **FINAL** variant is a `FinanceOfficer` action once discounts are verified. It requires the account's `assessment_variant` to still be `PRELIMINARY` — the final assessment is computed exactly once — and requires the musician's placement to be `ACCEPTED`; either failure is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_FINAL`. It recomputes the two discount components against the same gross figure. `sibling_discount_cents` is ten percent of the gross, rounded half up to whole cents, when the musician's `sibling_enrolled` is true, and 0 otherwise. The loyalty component first caps the credited years at four: the credited year count is the lesser of the musician's `years_in_org` and 4, and when that count is at least 1, `loyalty_credit_cents` is one and a half percent of the gross multiplied by the credited year count, rounded half up to whole cents, and 0 when the credited year count is zero. `assessed_cents` is then the gross minus `sibling_discount_cents` minus `loyalty_credit_cents`.

The rounding order here is pinned and observable: each component is rounded to whole cents *first*, and only then are the rounded components subtracted from the gross. Summing the exact fractional components and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different stored figure. To make the difference concrete: a gross of 123223 cents for a musician with a sibling enrolled and one year in the organization gives exact components of 12322.30 and 1848.345, which round to 12322 and 1848 and leave `assessed_cents` at 109053 — whereas summing the exact components first would give 14170.645, round to 14171, and wrongly leave 109052.

The final assessment then applies the **income-band aid cap**. Until it runs, `aid_effective_cents` simply equals `aid_awarded_cents`, meaning an award is credited uncapped. The cap is a percentage of the freshly recomputed `assessed_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — sixty percent for `BAND_A`, forty percent for `BAND_B`, and twenty-five percent for `BAND_C`. `aid_effective_cents` becomes the lesser of `aid_awarded_cents` and that cap, and the surplus — the awarded amount minus the effective amount — is returned to the season pool: `aid_committed_cents` decreases by the surplus and `aid_available_cents` increases by the same surplus, preserving the invariant of §2.11. Continuing the example above, an `assessed_cents` of 109053 in `BAND_C` yields a cap of 27263 cents, from an exact 27263.25. `balance_cents` is then recomputed, which may move `billing_status` in either direction — to `CURRENT`, to `DELINQUENT`, or to `PAID_IN_FULL` — and may carry the musician's `standing_status` with it.

Billing posture is one of the account's two independent dimensions and moves as follows. `PENDING_ASSESSMENT → CURRENT` on the preliminary assessment, once `assessed_cents` exceeds 0. `CURRENT → DELINQUENT` whenever any balance recomputation — a final assessment, a payment, an aid award or an aid rescission — observes the logical clock at or after the season's `tuition_due_at` while `balance_cents` is above 0; that transition also sets the musician's `standing_status` to `FINANCIAL_HOLD`. `PAID_IN_FULL → CURRENT` when a final assessment or a rescission raises `balance_cents` above 0 while the clock is still strictly before `tuition_due_at`, and `PAID_IN_FULL → DELINQUENT` when the same thing happens at or after the due date, again setting `FINANCIAL_HOLD`. `DELINQUENT → CURRENT` when a recomputation leaves `balance_cents` above 0 with the clock strictly before `tuition_due_at`, which re-evaluates the musician's standing. Every one of these moves increments the account's `version`.

A `Guardian` records a payment against their own account by supplying `amount_cents`, which must be at least 1 and no greater than the account's current `balance_cents`; an overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` rather than credited. The payment raises `payments_total_cents` and lowers `balance_cents`. A payment that brings `balance_cents` to zero or below moves the account to `PAID_IN_FULL` from either `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT`, and clearing a delinquency re-evaluates the musician's `standing_status`, releasing `FINANCIAL_HOLD` to `CLEAR` when no other account of that musician is `DELINQUENT` and the attendance cause no longer holds, or dropping it back to `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` when the attendance cause does still hold.

The aid dimension, `aid_status`, is the account's second independent dimension and runs `NONE`, `REQUESTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `RESCINDED`. A `Guardian` opens or re-opens a request for their own account by supplying `aid_requested_cents`. The request is admissible only from `NONE`, `DENIED` or `RESCINDED`; from `REQUESTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` it is refused with `ERR_AID_REQUEST_NOT_ALLOWED_IN_STATE`, and because that state check outranks the fiscal band, an oversized request on an already-approved account reports the state error rather than the size error. The requested amount must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than the account's `assessed_cents`; otherwise `ERR_AID_REQUEST_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`. A first request from `NONE` additionally requires the season to be in `PLACEMENT` or `IN_SEASON`. Re-applying from `DENIED` resets `aid_denial_reason` to `NONE`; re-applying from `RESCINDED` clears both `aid_reviewed_by_user_id` and `aid_awarded_by_user_id` to null, which closes the cycle `APPROVED → RESCINDED → REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`.

A `FinanceOfficer` opens a `REQUESTED` account for funding, advancing it to `UNDER_REVIEW`. The season's `aid_budget_cents` must be greater than 0 — a season with no aid pool cannot open reviews, and the attempt is `ERR_NO_AID_BUDGET`. The review records `aid_reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, which becomes the segregation-of-duties anchor for the award run, and it computes the waterfall sort key `aid_need_score` as an integer with no rounding: the band contributes 40 points for `BAND_A`, 25 for `BAND_B` and 10 for `BAND_C`; an enrolled sibling adds 8 points; seniority adds 3 points for each year in the organization up to a ceiling of five counted years, so the seniority term is 3 multiplied by the lesser of `years_in_org` and 5; a requested instrument loan on the accepted placement adds 6 points; and the tier snapshot adds 5 points for `SYMPHONY`, 3 for `PHILHARMONIC` and 1 for `CONCERT_ORCHESTRA`.

The **aid award run** is a `FinanceOfficer` action over a whole season, guarded by `expected_season_version`. Segregation of duties bites first: the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal `aid_reviewed_by_user_id` on *any* account in the run queue, since the officer who reviewed a request may not be the officer who funds it, and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The queue is every account of that season whose `aid_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, ordered by `aid_need_score` descending, then by `assessed_cents` descending, then by `created_at` ascending; the account's `id` is never a tie-break. Accounts still merely `REQUESTED`, and accounts already `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `RESCINDED`, are skipped entirely — which is why a second run redistributes only what the first run left over.

Two figures bound each grant. A per-member ceiling equal to thirty percent of the season's `aid_budget_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, is computed once for the whole run. A running remainder starts at the season's current `aid_available_cents`. The queue is then walked strictly in order, one account at a time: the candidate grant is the smallest of the account's `aid_requested_cents`, the per-member ceiling, and the running remainder. If that candidate grant is at least 5000 cents, the account is approved — `aid_awarded_cents` is set to the grant, `aid_effective_cents` is set to the same grant (uncapped, because `assessment_variant` is still `PRELIMINARY`), `aid_awarded_by_user_id` records the acting officer, `aid_status` becomes `APPROVED`, the running remainder decreases by the grant, and the season's `aid_committed_cents` increases by the grant. If the candidate grant falls below 5000 cents, the account is denied instead — `aid_awarded_cents` is set to 0, `aid_status` becomes `DENIED`, and `aid_denial_reason` records `INSUFFICIENT_BUDGET` when the running remainder was the binding constraint by being itself below 5000 cents, and `BELOW_MIN_AWARD` otherwise. Crucially, a denial does **not** decrease the running remainder: the money stays in the pool for the applicants behind it, and clamping the grant *before* the minimum-award test, rather than committing and then reversing, is what makes the later applicants' outcomes depend observably on queue order. After the walk, `aid_available_cents` is set to `aid_budget_cents` minus `aid_committed_cents`.

A worked illustration: with an `aid_budget_cents` of 100000 the per-member ceiling is 30000, and requests of 30000, 40000, 38000, 3000 and 20000 in queue order produce awards of 30000, 30000 and 30000 to the first three applicants, a denial with reason `BELOW_MIN_AWARD` for the fourth because 3000 is below the 5000-cent floor and its request leaves the budget untouched, and then 10000 to the fifth applicant out of the surviving remainder — leaving `aid_committed_cents` at 100000 and `aid_available_cents` at 0. Each funded account's `balance_cents` is recomputed, which may flip its `billing_status` and the musician's `standing_status` with it. The run reports `approved_count`, `denied_count`, and the season's resulting `aid_committed_cents` and `aid_available_cents`, and it increments the season's `version`.

A `FinanceOfficer` may withdraw an award from an `APPROVED` account whose `aid_awarded_cents` is above 0, moving `aid_status` to `RESCINDED`. Three hops follow. First, `aid_awarded_cents` and `aid_effective_cents` are both set to 0; the season's `aid_committed_cents` decreases by the account's `aid_effective_cents` *as it stood immediately before the rescission* — that is, the amount actually committed to this account, which is the capped figure once the final assessment has run — and `aid_available_cents` increases by the same amount; and `balance_cents` is recomputed upward. Second, because `balance_cents` is now above 0 and the logical clock is at or past the season's `tuition_due_at`, `billing_status` advances to `DELINQUENT`. Third, because the account went delinquent, the musician's `standing_status` advances `CLEAR → FINANCIAL_HOLD`. Both the account's `version` and the season's `version` increment by one each.

### 3.8. The Rehearsal Calendar, Attendance and Performance Eligibility

The `ArtisticDirector` puts each **EnsembleEvent** on an ensemble's calendar, supplying the `ensemble_id`, a `kind` of `REHEARSAL`, `CONCERT`, `TOUR_PERFORMANCE` or `FESTIVAL`, a `start_at` and a strictly later `end_at`, and a `venue_code`. Six optional inputs shape the event: `is_dress_rehearsal`, defaulting to false, marks a rehearsal as its ensemble's dress rehearsal; `dress_rehearsal_required`, defaulting to false, declares on a performance event that missing the dress rehearsal disqualifies a musician; `eligibility_window_start` fixes the inclusive start of the rehearsal window the attendance index scans and is required for the three performance kinds; `soloist_selection_mode`, defaulting to `AUTO_SELECT` and otherwise `MANUAL` or `NONE`, decides who picks the soloist; `tour_cost_share_cents`, defaulting to 0, is the per-member cost share for tour and festival events; and `chaperone_required`, defaulting to false, makes a confirmed chaperone a tour-readiness precondition. A new event starts in `programming_state` `DRAFT` with `participation_count` 0.

`RegistrarStaff` rosters a musician onto an event by creating an **EventParticipation** row from an `event_id` and a `member_id`. The musician must hold an `ACTIVE` chair in some section of that event's ensemble; a non-member of the ensemble cannot be added to its calendar, and the attempt is `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_IN_ENSEMBLE`. Only one row may exist per combination of `event_id` and `member_id`, a second being `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTICIPATION`. A musician's own calendar uses plain half-open intervals with no buffer at all: the new row conflicts with an existing one when this event's `start_at` is earlier than the other event's `end_at` and the other event's `start_at` is earlier than this event's `end_at`, which is reported as `ERR_MEMBER_EVENT_CONFLICT`. Because the intervals are half-open, two back-to-back events where one ends at exactly the instant the next begins do not conflict. A new row starts with `attendance_status` `NOT_RECORDED`, `performance_eligibility` `UNREVIEWED`, and `tour_status` `PENDING` for `TOUR_PERFORMANCE` and `FESTIVAL` events but `NOT_APPLICABLE` for `REHEARSAL` and `CONCERT` events, and it increments the event's `participation_count`.

A participation row carries three independent state dimensions at once, and each moves on its own. `RegistrarStaff` drives the first by recording an `attendance_status` of `PRESENT`, `TARDY`, `ABSENCE_UNVERIFIED` or `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT`. Recording requires the row still to be `NOT_RECORDED`, so a second recording on the same row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_FOR_OPERATION`, and it requires the logical clock to be at or after the event's `start_at`; recording attendance strictly before the event begins is `ERR_EVENT_NOT_STARTED`.

The absence arithmetic that hangs off attendance is the organization's probation policy. A musician's `unexcused_absence_count` is the number of their participation rows sitting in `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` and their `tardy_count` is the number sitting in `TARDY`. The combined metric `effective_unexcused_count` is `unexcused_absence_count` plus `tardy_count` divided by three with the remainder discarded — so two tardies contribute nothing and the third tardy contributes one whole effective absence. A musician goes on probation when `effective_unexcused_count` minus `probation_baseline_count` reaches three, and `probation_baseline_count` is what makes probation re-fire fairly: clearing a musician's standing pins the baseline to the then-current effective count, so the next probation requires three *further* effective absences rather than triggering again immediately. Recording a `TARDY` therefore raises `tardy_count` by one, recomputes `effective_unexcused_count`, and may advance the musician `CLEAR → ATTENDANCE_PROBATION` if the recomputed figure crosses the threshold. Recording `PRESENT` or `ABSENCE_UNVERIFIED` changes no counter and merely increments the participation row's `version`.

Recording `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` fires a four-hop cascade. First, the musician's `unexcused_absence_count` increments by one and `effective_unexcused_count` is recomputed. Second, when `effective_unexcused_count` minus `probation_baseline_count` is at least three and the musician's standing was `CLEAR`, `standing_status` advances `CLEAR → ATTENDANCE_PROBATION`. Third, because the musician went on probation, every participation row of that musician on an event whose `kind` is `CONCERT`, `TOUR_PERFORMANCE` or `FESTIVAL`, whose event `start_at` is strictly later than the logical clock, and whose `performance_eligibility` is not already `INELIGIBLE`, moves to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` set to `MEMBER_ON_PROBATION`, and any of those rows whose `tour_status` was `READY` drops back to `PENDING`. Fourth, wherever one of those revoked rows belongs to an event whose `soloist_member_id` is that same musician, the event's `programming_state` advances `PROGRAMMED → SOLOIST_VACANT` and its `soloist_member_id` is cleared to null. Every row touched along the way has its `version` incremented.

An unverified absence is the family's opening to appeal. A `Guardian` files an excuse for their own musician, advancing the row `ABSENCE_UNVERIFIED → EXCUSE_PENDING`, and must do so while the logical clock is strictly before the event's `end_at` advanced by forty-eight hours; at or after that instant the window has closed and the request is `ERR_EXCUSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The filing records an `excuse_reason_code` of `NONE`, `ILLNESS`, `ACADEMIC_CONFLICT`, `FAMILY` or `TRANSPORT`, plus an optional `excuse_documentation_ref`.

`RegistrarStaff` then decides the pending excuse by supplying a `decision`, and the decision is segregated from the recording: the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the `X-User-ID` that recorded the absence in the earlier attendance call on the same row, and must not equal an `excuse_decided_by_user_id` left by an earlier decision; either coincidence is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A decision of `APPROVED` moves the row `EXCUSE_PENDING → EXCUSED_ABSENT`, records `excuse_decided_by_user_id`, and leaves every counter untouched. A decision of `DENIED` moves the row `EXCUSE_PENDING → UNEXCUSED_ABSENT`, records `excuse_decided_by_user_id`, and fires exactly the same four-hop cascade described above: the musician's `unexcused_absence_count` and `effective_unexcused_count` rise, the musician may advance `CLEAR → ATTENDANCE_PROBATION`, every future performance participation of that musician becomes `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` `MEMBER_ON_PROBATION` and any `READY` tour status drops to `PENDING`, and any event where that musician was the named soloist advances `PROGRAMMED → SOLOIST_VACANT` with `soloist_member_id` cleared to null. `EXCUSED_ABSENT` and `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` are both terminal attendance values.

The second dimension, `performance_eligibility`, is the `ArtisticDirector`'s judgement and applies only to performances: an eligibility review on a `REHEARSAL` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_KIND_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The review computes and stores `attendance_index`, a weighted rehearsal-attendance percentage. Its scope is the `REHEARSAL` events of the *same* ensemble whose `start_at` is at or after the performance's `eligibility_window_start` and strictly before the performance's own `start_at` — a half-open window. Within that scope, the counted rows are this musician's participation rows on those rehearsals whose `attendance_status` is anything other than `NOT_RECORDED`; an unrecorded rehearsal is excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. If no rows are counted at all, `attendance_index` is 0.00. Otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the sum of the counted rows' weights, divided by the number of counted rows, rounded half up to two decimal places. The weights are 1.0 for `PRESENT`, 0.75 for `EXCUSED_ABSENT`, 0.5 for `TARDY`, and 0.0 for each of `ABSENCE_UNVERIFIED`, `EXCUSE_PENDING` and `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` — so an excuse that has been filed but not yet decided still sits in the denominator with no credit, and therefore hurts.

Eligibility is granted only when three conditions all hold: `attendance_index` is at least 80.00; when the performance's `dress_rehearsal_required` is true, the musician's participation on the latest dress-rehearsal-flagged `REHEARSAL` of that ensemble inside the window carries `attendance_status` `PRESENT`; and the musician's `standing_status` is not `ATTENDANCE_PROBATION`. When all three hold the row moves `UNREVIEWED → ELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` set to `NONE` and the computed index stored. When at least one fails the row moves `UNREVIEWED → INELIGIBLE`, the index is still stored, and `ineligibility_reason` is set from the first failing condition in that same order — `ATTENDANCE_INDEX_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, then `DRESS_REHEARSAL_MISSED`, then `MEMBER_ON_PROBATION`. The threshold is inclusive and the boundary is observable: four `PRESENT` rows and one `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` row over five counted rows give exactly 80.00 and are eligible, while four `PRESENT`, one `EXCUSED_ABSENT` and one `UNEXCUSED_ABSENT` over six counted rows give 79.17 (from an exact 79.1666…) and are not. A row that a probation cascade dropped from `ELIGIBLE → INELIGIBLE` can be reviewed again once the musician's standing has been cleared, and when all three conditions now hold the row returns `INELIGIBLE → ELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` cleared to `NONE`, closing that cycle. An eligible result also makes the musician a soloist candidate; an ineligible result on a tour or festival drops `tour_status` from `READY` back to `PENDING`.

### 3.9. Programming Performances, Tours, and Life After Graduation

Programming turns a drafted performance into an announced one. The `ArtisticDirector` programs an event whose `programming_state` is `DRAFT` or `SOLOIST_VACANT` and whose `kind` is anything other than `REHEARSAL`; a rehearsal, or an event already sitting in `PROGRAMMED`, is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_PROGRAMMABLE`. The request may carry a `soloist_member_id`, a `guest_artist_name` and a `guest_artist_fee_cents`. The guest-artist figures are constrained: `guest_artist_fee_cents` may not exceed 500000 cents, and a `guest_artist_fee_cents` above 0 must be accompanied by a `guest_artist_name`; either breach is `ERR_GUEST_ARTIST_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

How the soloist is chosen depends on the event's `soloist_selection_mode`. Under `MANUAL` the caller must name the `soloist_member_id` explicitly, and that musician must hold an `ACTIVE` chair in this ensemble, must have `standing_status` `CLEAR`, and must have `performance_eligibility` `ELIGIBLE` on this very event; any of those failing is `ERR_SOLOIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and per §2.10 that check is evaluated before the guest-artist fee cap. Under `NONE` the event is programmed with no soloist and `soloist_member_id` stays null. Under `AUTO_SELECT` the server picks. The candidate pool is every musician who holds an `ACTIVE` chair in a section of this event's ensemble, whose `standing_status` is `CLEAR`, who has a participation row on this event with `performance_eligibility` `ELIGIBLE`, and who is not already the `soloist_member_id` of another event of the same ensemble whose `programming_state` is `PROGRAMMED`. An empty pool refuses the programming with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SOLOIST`. Otherwise the pool is ordered by the candidate's chair `is_principal` descending, so principals come first, then by that chair's `ranking_score` descending, then by the musician's `years_in_org` descending, then by that participation row's `attendance_index` descending, and finally by the chair row's `created_at` ascending; the first row wins. Every tie-break is a stored field, and the chair's `created_at` is the final fallback, so the winner is fully determined.

Successful programming sets `soloist_member_id` as chosen, records `guest_artist_name` and `guest_artist_fee_cents` when supplied, advances `programming_state` to `PROGRAMMED`, and increments the event's `version`. When a probation cascade later revokes the selected soloist's eligibility, the event advances `PROGRAMMED → SOLOIST_VACANT` and `soloist_member_id` is cleared to null; programming may then be re-run to pick a replacement, closing the cycle `PROGRAMMED → SOLOIST_VACANT → PROGRAMMED`. The exclusion clause carries the retry semantics: after a vacancy the previous winner is out of the pool only if they are still the soloist of some *other* `PROGRAMMED` event, so a cleared `soloist_member_id` makes that musician selectable again.

Tours and festivals carry a third participation dimension, `tour_status`, which is `NOT_APPLICABLE` for rehearsals and concerts and starts at `PENDING` for tour and festival rows. A `Guardian` works their own musician's row toward readiness by crediting fundraising and registering a chaperone; the attempt is refused with `ERR_EVENT_KIND_NOT_ELIGIBLE` on any event whose `kind` is not `TOUR_PERFORMANCE` or `FESTIVAL`. Three optional inputs are accepted: `fundraising_amount_cents`, which accumulates into `fundraising_credited_cents`; `chaperone_name`; and `chaperone_background_check_ref`. `fundraising_shortfall_cents` is then recomputed as the greater of 0 and the event's `tour_cost_share_cents` minus `fundraising_credited_cents`, and `chaperone_confirmed` is derived as true only when both `chaperone_name` and `chaperone_background_check_ref` are present. The row advances `PENDING → READY` only when `fundraising_shortfall_cents` is 0, when `chaperone_confirmed` is true in the case that the event's `chaperone_required` is true, and when `performance_eligibility` is `ELIGIBLE`; if any of those three does not hold, the request is refused with `ERR_TOUR_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. A `READY` row falls back to `PENDING` whenever a probation cascade revokes the musician's `performance_eligibility`.

Retention and graduation close the loop. A musician's `graduation_year` and `alumni_engagement_opt_in` are declared at registration and drive the end of the relationship two ways, described in §3.1 and §3.2: a registrar may graduate an `ENROLLED` musician explicitly once no tuition account of theirs is `DELINQUENT`, and the season close graduates every `ENROLLED` chair-holder whose `graduation_year` matches the closing year and then moves the opted-in graduates onward to `ALUMNI`. A registrar may also move a graduate to `ALUMNI` directly, provided `alumni_engagement_opt_in` is true. `years_in_org` is the fact that ties the two ends of a member's history together: it credits loyalty against tuition, adds weight to an aid application, breaks ties in the chair order, and breaks ties in the soloist selection.
