# Pro Desk Contractor Account API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A home-improvement retailer's Pro Desk is not a checkout lane with a bigger cart. The contractors it serves buy against negotiated job pricing rather than shelf prices, they buy on terms rather than on a card, they need a hundred and forty sheets of the same dye lot rather than whatever is on the rack, and they need it staged in a bay at six in the morning or craned over a fence on a Thursday afternoon. Today those relationships are held together by spreadsheets, faxed resale certificates and a credit manager's memory. The result is that a contractor whose trailing purchase volume has earned a deeper discount keeps paying the old rate, a quote that was negotiated three weeks ago gets re-negotiated because nobody recorded its term, and a job that was supposed to ship from one store arrives split across three with visibly different lots.

This document specifies the Pro Desk & Contractor Account API, the system of record that replaces those spreadsheets. It owns the whole commercial arc of a contractor relationship: tiering an account by its trailing twelve-month invoiced volume and applying the tier's discount, running a bounded negotiation loop on bulk job quotes and binding the resulting prices for a fixed term, extending and policing a commercial credit line, holding resale-exemption certificates per state, sourcing job lots across the stores of a region, staging will-call material in bay-limited windows, dispatching lift-gate-capable trucks to jobsites with awkward access, accruing and paying volume rebates, and valuing returns of job-quoted material under a restocking policy that distinguishes negotiated material from spot material. Six operational roles share the surface — the platform operator who provisions the network, the contractor's own buyer, the pro desk negotiator, the commercial credit manager, the store fulfillment crew, and the tax-compliance gatekeeper — and the design deliberately separates the desk that authors a discount from the manager who approves it.

Two technical constraints shape everything that follows. First, every resource lives under the `/pro` mount and every money amount in the system is an integer number of cents; there are no floating-point currency fields anywhere in the contract. Second, the server evaluates every temporal rule — quote terms, staging windows, the trailing-volume window, certificate expiry, the return window, rebate periods — against a **logical clock** that tests drive explicitly, never against wall-clock time. A deterministic clock is what makes a term-expiry rule or a ninety-day return boundary testable at all.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that holds across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the clock, identifiers, rounding, concurrency, resolution order, the tier ladder, cascade semantics, and the order in which rules are evaluated.

### 2.1. Identity, Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ContractorAdmin`, `ProDeskAssociate`, `CreditManager`, `StoreOperations`, and `TaxComplianceOfficer`. Each endpoint publishes the roles permitted to invoke it (see §4); a well-formed request from a role that is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

`ContractorAdmin` is the only role whose identity is bound to a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ProAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ProAccount` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. That comparison reaches through one hop where necessary: a job quote, a pro order, a tax-exempt certificate, a credit line and a rebate accrual each carry `pro_account_id` directly, while a quote line, an order line, a reservation, a will-call staging, a delivery appointment and a return request are owned by the account of the quote or order they hang from. A `ContractorAdmin` acting on a resource that resolves to a different account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). That rejection governs single-resource reads and writes; a `ContractorAdmin`'s *collection* reads are not rejected but silently narrowed — on every list endpoint the role may call, the result set is filtered down to the records resolving to its own account before the page window is applied, so `total_count` counts only those.

The remaining five roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ProDeskAssociate`, `CreditManager`, `StoreOperations` or `TaxComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a `ProDeskAssociate`'s identifier is stamped into `authored_by_user_id` when a counter-offer round is written and into `approved_by_user_id` on a return request when a return is approved, and a `CreditManager`'s identifier is stamped into `approved_by_user_id` on a job quote when a negotiated round is approved — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers exist to support one rule in particular: the associate who authored a negotiated round can never be the manager who approves it, and an approval attempt where the acting `CreditManager`'s `X-User-ID` equals the quote's `authored_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is read from the logical clock of §2.4, not from the host's wall clock, so it moves only when a client moves the clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints only. The one array response in this API that is not a list read — the sourcing call of §3.7, whose `data` is the reservations it just created — carries a `meta` holding just `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and must be a non-negative integer. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, a negative `offset`, or either parameter carrying a value that is not an integer at all, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An `offset` past the end of the matching set is not an error: the page comes back as an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is ordered by the record's `created_at` timestamp in ascending order — oldest first — and the same ordering is used wherever this document says lines or records are processed "in creation order". Because the clock is logical, many records legitimately share one `created_at` value; every such tie is broken by the order in which the server wrote the records, earliest write first, and that same tie-break settles any `created_at` comparison elsewhere in this document, including the sourcing tie-break of §3.7.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current window). Every filter a list endpoint publishes is an exact string match against the named field, filters supplied together are combined conjunctively, a filter value that nothing matches — including a value outside a declared enum — yields an empty page rather than an error, and a query parameter the endpoint does not publish is ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every temporal decision in this system reads a server-held logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. Before any client sets the clock, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is fixed and the server must never fall back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, the instant the clock should read; the response body is an object carrying a single `current_utc` string. Setting the clock advances time and nothing else — no entity is mutated, no staging expires, and no sweep runs implicitly as a side effect of the clock moving. Expiry is realised only when an operator runs the will-call sweep at a store, or when the relevant rule is next evaluated in the course of another request.

### 2.5. Resource Identifiers, Timestamps and Record Versions

All fifteen resource types in this system — stores, stock positions, delivery trucks, pro accounts, credit lines, tax-exempt certificates, job quotes, quote lines, pro orders, order lines, reservations, will-call stagings, delivery appointments, rebate accruals and return requests — carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-specific encoding; a caller must never construct one.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the record was first written. It is taken from the logical clock.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including recomputation of purely derived fields such as an account's tier or a credit line's exposure.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by one on every mutation. The increment is once per record per request: a record that a single request mutates several times — directly and then again through a cascade, or twice inside one cascade — rises by exactly one, and a record the request itself *created* stays at `version` 1 even when a later effect of that same request writes to it again, as the tier review that follows account creation and the exposure recomputation that follows opening a credit line both do. `updated_at` is refreshed on every one of those writes regardless. There is no resource in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Each resource type is addressed in URLs by its own identifier name: a store by `store_id`, a stock position by `store_stock_id`, a truck by `delivery_truck_id`, a contractor account by `pro_account_id`, a credit line by `credit_line_id`, a resale certificate by `tax_exempt_certificate_id`, a job quote by `job_quote_id`, a quote line by `quote_line_id`, a pro order by `pro_order_id`, an order line by `order_line_id`, a reservation by `reservation_id`, a will-call staging by `will_call_staging_id`, a delivery appointment by `delivery_appointment_id`, a rebate accrual by `rebate_accrual_id`, and a return request by `return_request_id`.

### 2.6. Money, Units, Rounding and Precision

Every monetary amount in this system is an **integer number of cents**. There are no decimal currency fields, no currency strings, and no per-line fractional cents carried between steps. Rates and percentages, by contrast, are decimal fractions: a tax rate of `0.0825` means 8.25 percent, and a tier discount of `0.08` means 8 percent. Unit counts — units on hand, units reserved, units ordered, units fulfilled, units returned — are plain integers and are never rounded.

Wherever a computation would produce a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. The system is deliberately explicit about *where* each rounding happens, because the same inputs produce different totals depending on the position of the rounding step:

- Tier-discounted order pricing rounds at the **unit** price, and only then multiplies by the ordered quantity (see §3.6).
- Order tax rounds **once**, on the summed tax base, never per line (see §3.6).
- A return's restocking fee rounds **once**, on the summed gross refund, never per return line; the return's tax refund then rounds once on the net-of-fee goods value (see §3.9).
- A credit line's freeze threshold rounds once, when the threshold is derived from the limit (see §3.3).
- Rebate accrual and clawback round **per booking event** — each individual delta is rounded as it is booked, and the running totals are never re-rounded (see §3.9).
- Quote line subtotals and negotiated totals are exact integer products and sums with no rounding at all; only a quote's `discount_depth_pct` is rounded, half up, to four decimal places (see §3.5).

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Counter-Offers and Certificate Filings

Two writes in this system legitimately *supersede* prior data, which means no state-machine guard can tell a deliberate replacement from an accidental one. Both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control against the parent record's `version`.

A counter-offer written on a job quote must carry `expected_job_quote_version`. The field is not optional: the request body must contain the client's last observed `version` of the target `JobQuote`. If that value does not equal the quote's current `version`, the counter-offer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product deliberately allows a fresh round to overwrite the previous round's negotiated prices, so the write is legitimate from a state standpoint; the stale version is the only signal that the associate priced against a round the contractor has since revised.

A resale certificate filing must carry `expected_pro_account_version`, likewise non-optional, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProAccount`. If it does not equal the account's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newer certificate legitimately supersedes the prior verified certificate for the same state, so no state precondition blocks the create; the stale version is what identifies a filing made against an outdated view of the account's exemption set.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Both rejections carry the same identifier, `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and the same HTTP 412 status.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Foreign-Key Resolution and Account Scoping

When a URL contains an identifier, the server resolves it before applying any business rule, and when a URL contains more than one identifier the resolution proceeds strictly left to right. A request for the lines of a job quote that does not exist fails on the quote, not on the lines. Each resource type has its own not-found identifier and all of them return HTTP 404: `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_STOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRO_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_EXEMPT_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRO_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WILL_CALL_STAGING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBATE_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RETURN_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`.

The same treatment applies to references carried in a request body, and the error is the one belonging to the referenced resource type, not to the resource being created. A `pro_account_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PRO_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`; a `home_store_id`, `originating_store_id` or `fulfillment_store_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`; a `job_quote_id` yields `ERR_JOB_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`; a `delivery_truck_id` yields `ERR_DELIVERY_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`; a `quote_line_id` named inside a counter-offer's `line_prices` that is not a line of the target quote yields `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`; an `order_line_id` named inside a return's `lines` that is not a line of the target order yields `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FOUND` carries one extra duty: it is also the rejection when an order that intends to pay on terms is submitted for an account that has no credit line at all.

Only once identifiers resolve does the server test account scope for a `ContractorAdmin`, as described in §2.1, rejecting a cross-account reach with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 2.9. The Pro Tier Ladder

Pro pricing is driven by a four-rung tier ladder. Each rung publishes the trailing volume at which it opens, the discount it grants off list price, the rebate rate it accrues on invoiced volume, and the longest quote term it permits:

- **`MEMBER`** opens at zero trailing volume. It grants no discount, accrues no rebate, and permits quote terms of at most 15 days.
- **`GOLD`** opens once trailing volume reaches fifty thousand dollars. It grants a 5 percent discount, accrues rebate at 1.0 percent of invoiced volume, and permits quote terms of at most 30 days.
- **`PLATINUM`** opens once trailing volume reaches two hundred thousand dollars. It grants an 8 percent discount, accrues rebate at 2.0 percent, and permits quote terms of at most 60 days.
- **`DIAMOND`** opens once trailing volume reaches five hundred thousand dollars. It grants a 12 percent discount, accrues rebate at 3.0 percent, and permits quote terms of at most 90 days.

Remember that trailing volume is stored in cents, so the `GOLD` opening figure is five million cents, `PLATINUM`'s is twenty million cents and `DIAMOND`'s is fifty million cents. Each opening figure is an **inclusive** lower bound: an account sitting exactly on a rung's opening volume qualifies for that rung. The account's tier is the qualifying rung with the greatest opening volume, and the account's discount is that rung's discount. The four opening figures are distinct by construction so no tie can arise; were two rungs ever to share an opening figure, the rung with the greater discount would win, and if the discounts also matched, the rung listed earlier above would win.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Parallel State Dimensions

A single write in this system routinely mutates several records. Whenever the server changes a record as part of a cascade — releasing a reservation, recomputing a stock position, freezing a credit line, putting an account on hold, re-tiering an account, booking a rebate delta — that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, exactly as if the change had been requested directly. The cascade is not an atomic invisible fix-up; it is a sequence of ordinary mutations, and the affected records read back changed.

A pro order is unusual in that it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `fulfillment_status` tracks the physical journey of the material through the values `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RESERVED`, `STAGED`, `FULFILLED` and `CANCELLED`, and `credit_status` tracks the commercial journey through `UNBILLED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID` and `VOIDED`. The two dimensions evolve independently. Reserving, staging and handing over material never move `credit_status`; invoicing and settling never move `fulfillment_status`. Only two operations touch both: submission, which moves `fulfillment_status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and `credit_status` from `UNBILLED` to `AUTHORIZED`, and cancellation, which drives `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED` and, when the order was authorized, `credit_status` from `AUTHORIZED` to `VOIDED`. There is a one-way dependency between them: invoicing requires the physical dimension to have reached `FULFILLED`, but nothing in the physical dimension ever waits on the commercial one.

Every write that reduces a record to a derived figure recomputes that figure from its inputs rather than adjusting it incrementally. A stock position's `reserved_units` is always the sum of the quantities held by that position's live reservations; a credit line's `exposure_cents` is always recomputed from the account's outstanding orders; an account's `trailing_volume_cents` is always recomputed from its in-window invoices. This matters when several effects land in one request: the recomputation reads the state left by the earlier effects of the same request.

### 2.11. Rule Evaluation Order

When a single request could fail several checks, the server must report the failure that comes first in the following ladder. The ladder is global: it holds across every endpoint, and where a rung applies to only some endpoints, its scope is stated.

1. **Authentication** — the headers are present and `X-User-Role` names a declared role, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies everywhere.
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's role list, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies everywhere.
3. **Path resolution** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, otherwise that resource type's not-found error. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
4. **Body reference resolution** — every foreign key in the body resolves (`pro_account_id`, `home_store_id`, `originating_store_id`, `fulfillment_store_id`, the `store_id` of a stock position, a truck or a staging, `job_quote_id`, `delivery_truck_id`, `pro_order_id`, each `quote_line_id` inside a counter-offer's `line_prices`, and each `order_line_id` inside a return's `lines`), otherwise that resource type's not-found error. A reference that is present but does not resolve is that 404 whatever its JSON type — a number or an object where an id was expected resolves to nothing and is reported as the referenced type's not-found error, not as a shape error — whereas a reference that is absent altogether is left to rung 5, which reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when the endpoint requires it and lets it pass when it is optional, as an order's `job_quote_id` is. Applies to every request body carrying a reference.
5. **Request shape** — required fields are present, enum-valued fields hold declared values, integers meet their declared minimums, ISO 8601 instants and ISO dates parse, and `lines` and `line_prices` arrays are non-empty; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Body fields an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Applies everywhere.
6. **Stale-view check** — the supplied `expected_job_quote_version` or `expected_pro_account_version` matches the parent's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Applies to the counter-offer write and the certificate filing.
7. **Account scope** — a `ContractorAdmin`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `pro_account_id` owning the target resource, otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Applies to every `ContractorAdmin` operation.
8. **Stock uniqueness** — no stock position already exists for that store and SKU pair, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_STOCK`. Applies to creating a stock position.
9. **Credit-line uniqueness** — the account does not already have a credit line, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_EXISTS`. Applies to opening a credit line.
10. **Credit line already active** — a reinstatement targets a line that is actually frozen, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FROZEN`. This is a specialization of the generic state guard at rung 13 and is evaluated ahead of it. Applies to credit-line reinstatement.
11. **Utilization still high** — a frozen line being reinstated has exposure strictly below its freeze threshold, otherwise `ERR_UTILIZATION_STILL_HIGH`. Applies to credit-line reinstatement.
12. **Order not reserved** — a will-call staging or a delivery appointment is being booked for an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RESERVED`. This too is a specialization evaluated ahead of rung 13, and it applies only to *booking* a staging or an appointment; marking a staging physically staged and confirming an appointment keep the generic guard.
13. **Generic state guard** — the target resource is in a state the requested transition is declared to leave from, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. This is what rejects a repeated trigger and any trigger issued from a terminal state. Applies to every transition endpoint. The three specializations at rungs 10 through 12 are evaluated before it.
14. **Account hold** — the owning `ProAccount` has `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`. Applies to five operations: submitting a job quote, revising a job quote, approving a job quote, accepting a job quote, and submitting a pro order.
15. **Credit line frozen** — the account's credit line has `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_FROZEN`. Applies to two operations: submitting an order whose `payment_method` is `CREDIT_LINE`, and reinstating an account.
16. **Order fulfilled** — the order's `fulfillment_status` is `FULFILLED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED`. Applies to two operations: invoicing an order, and creating a return request against it.
17. **Reservations allocated** — every live reservation of the order (one still in `HELD` or `ALLOCATED`) has reached `ALLOCATED` and at least one such reservation exists, otherwise `ERR_RESERVATIONS_NOT_ALLOCATED`. Applies to marking a will-call staging physically staged.
18. **Truck in service** — the truck's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Applies to two operations: booking a delivery appointment, and confirming one.
19. **Lift-gate capability** — when the order requires a lift gate, the assigned truck's `lift_gate_equipped` is true **and** the `lift_gate_available` of the store the truck is registered against is true, otherwise `ERR_LIFT_GATE_UNAVAILABLE`. Applies to booking a delivery appointment.
20. **Jobsite access capability** — the requested `jobsite_access_type` is one of the truck's `access_types_supported`, otherwise `ERR_JOBSITE_ACCESS_UNSUPPORTED`. Applies to booking a delivery appointment.
21. **Delivery window shape** — `window_end` is strictly later than `window_start`; a zero-length or inverted window is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DELIVERY_WINDOW`. Applies to booking a delivery appointment. Its will-call counterpart is the next rung.
22. **Staging window length** — `stage_hours` is no greater than the store's `max_stage_hours`, otherwise `ERR_STAGE_WINDOW_TOO_LONG`. Applies to booking a will-call staging.
23. **Truck window conflict** — the buffered window does not intersect another live appointment on the same truck, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Applies to booking a delivery appointment.
24. **Bay capacity** — the requested bay interval does not overlap as many live stagings as the store has bays, otherwise `ERR_WILL_CALL_CAPACITY_FULL`. Applies to booking a will-call staging.
25. **Channel match** — `delivery_mode` matches the scheduling channel: a `WILL_CALL` order cannot take a delivery appointment and a `DELIVERY` order cannot take a will-call staging, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_MISMATCH`. Applies to booking a will-call staging and to booking a delivery appointment.
26. **Order has lines** — the order has at least one order line, otherwise `ERR_NO_ORDER_LINES`. Applies to submitting an order, to the read-only pricing preview, and to the sourcing call.
27. **SKU stocked** — the `sku` has a stock position at the relevant store — the fulfillment store for order lines, the originating store for quote lines — otherwise `ERR_SKU_NOT_STOCKED`. Applies to five operations: adding an order line, adding a quote line, submitting a job quote, and the two order-pricing runs (submitting an order and the pricing preview), each of which must read a live list price from the fulfillment store's position for every line.
28. **Quote has lines** — the quote has at least one quote line, otherwise `ERR_NO_QUOTE_LINES`. Applies to submitting a job quote.
29. **Quote belongs to the account** — the order's `job_quote_id` names a quote of the same `pro_account_id` as the order, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. Applies to creating a pro order.
30. **Quote accepted** — the referenced quote's `status` is `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Applies to submitting an order.
31. **Purchase order coding** — `po_number` is present and non-empty when the account's `po_required` is true, otherwise `ERR_PO_NUMBER_REQUIRED`. Applies to submitting an order.
32. **Job-name coding** — `job_name` is present and non-empty when the account's `job_name_required` is true, otherwise `ERR_JOB_NAME_REQUIRED`. Applies to submitting an order.
33. **Resale exemption on file** — when `resale_purchase` is true, the fulfillment store's `state_code` is among the account's `tax_exempt_state_codes`, otherwise `ERR_NO_VALID_TAX_CERTIFICATE`. Applies to submitting an order.
34. **Quote term open** — a quote-bound order is submitted only while the clock is at or after `term_start` and at or before `term_end`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_TERM_EXPIRED`. Applies to submitting an order.
35. **Quote term closed** — a quote is expired only once the clock is strictly past `term_end`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_TERM_ACTIVE`. Applies to expiring a job quote.
36. **Certificate not lapsed** — the certificate's `expires_on` is strictly after the current clock date, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_MISMATCH`. Applies to verifying a certificate.
37. **Negotiation round budget** — the quote's `round_count` is strictly below three before a further counter-offer or revision, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_ROUND_LIMIT`. Applies to writing a counter-offer and to revising a quote.
38. **Complete round** — `line_prices` names every quote line of the quote, otherwise `ERR_INCOMPLETE_COUNTER_OFFER`. Applies to writing a counter-offer.
39. **Negotiated price range** — every `negotiated_unit_price_cents` is at least 1 cent and no greater than that line's snapshotted `list_unit_price_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_NEGOTIATED_PRICE`. Applies to writing a counter-offer.
40. **Segregation of duties** — the approving `CreditManager`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the quote's `authored_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to approving a job quote.
41. **Approvable discount depth** — the quote's `discount_depth_pct` is no greater than 30 percent, otherwise `ERR_DISCOUNT_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. Applies to approving a job quote.
42. **Sourcing candidates exist** — every line of the order has at least one candidate store, otherwise `ERR_NO_SOURCING_CANDIDATES`. Applies to reserving an order.
43. **Sourcing covers the order** — the waterfall covers every line's full quantity, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_JOB_LOT`. Applies to reserving an order.
44. **Job-lot integrity** — an order whose `job_lot_required` is true resolves to exactly one sourcing store, otherwise `ERR_JOB_LOT_SPLIT_NOT_ALLOWED`. Applies to reserving an order.
45. **Return window open** — the whole days elapsed since the order's `fulfilled_at` are no more than 90, otherwise `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Applies to approving a return request.
46. **Return quantity available** — each requested return quantity is no greater than that order line's `fulfilled_units` less its `returned_units`, otherwise `ERR_RETURN_QTY_EXCEEDS_FULFILLED`. Applies to creating a return request.
47. **Credit headroom** — for an order paying on terms, the order's `total_cents` is no greater than the credit line's `available_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Applies to submitting an order.
48. **Payment amount** — the supplied `amount_cents` equals the order's `total_cents` less its `credited_refund_cents`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Applies to settling an order.
49. **Rebate payable** — the accrual's `payable_cents` is strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_REBATE_PAYABLE`. Applies to paying out a rebate accrual.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out, because they are easy to get backwards. Because the account-hold rung (14) precedes the credit-line rung (15), an order submitted for an account on hold whose credit line is also frozen reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`. And because the credit-headroom rung (47) is nearly last, an order that is both over its credit headroom and missing its `po_number` reports `ERR_PO_NUMBER_REQUIRED`, not `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Provisioning the Store Network and Its Fleet

Nothing in this system can happen until a `PlatformAdmin` has described the physical network. A **store** is registered with a `store_code`, the human-readable code printed on the building, which is expected to be unique across all stores but is recorded exactly as supplied — the server runs no uniqueness test on it and never rejects a repeat. Two further codes place the store in the world: `region_code`, which defines the sourcing pool the store belongs to, and `state_code`, the two-letter state used to match resale-exemption certificates. Sourcing order is driven by `distance_rank`, a non-negative integer where a lower value means a nearer store; `will_call_bay_slots`, at least 1, is the number of staging bays that can be occupied at the same instant; `lift_gate_available` records whether the store dispatches lift-gate-capable equipment at all and, like `store_code`, `region_code`, `state_code`, `distance_rank`, `will_call_bay_slots` and `tax_rate_pct`, is required in the body with no default; `tax_rate_pct` is the sales tax rate applied to that store's taxable orders, expressed as a decimal fraction between 0 and 0.20 inclusive — a store charging eight and a quarter percent is registered as `0.0825`; and `max_stage_hours`, at least 1 and defaulting to 72 when omitted, caps how long a single will-call occupancy may run at that store. Stores can be listed filtered by `region_code`.

A **stock position** ties a `sku` to a store. It is opened with the owning `store_id`, the `sku` code, the `on_hand_units` physically present (which may legitimately be zero) and the `list_unit_price_cents` at which that store lists the item — at least 1 cent, undiscounted. A store may hold only one position per SKU: a second attempt at the same store-and-SKU pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_STOCK`. A freshly opened position starts with `reserved_units` at 0 and `available_units` equal to `on_hand_units`. Those two fields are never written by a client. `reserved_units` is always the sum of `qty_units` across the reservations at that store for that SKU whose status is `HELD` or `ALLOCATED`, and `available_units` is `on_hand_units` less `reserved_units`; both are recomputed whenever a reservation is created, released or consumed, and reads of a position always show live figures. Stock positions can be listed filtered by `store_id` and by `sku`.

`StoreOperations` owns the fleet. A **delivery truck** is registered against a home `store_id` with a `truck_code` expected to be unique within that store but, like a store's code, never actually tested for uniqueness, a required `lift_gate_equipped` flag recording whether it carries a hydraulic lift gate, and `access_types_supported` — a non-empty subset of `CURBSIDE`, `DRIVEWAY`, `NARROW_ALLEY` and `CRANE_REQUIRED` describing the jobsite approaches the vehicle can actually service. `turnaround_buffer_minutes`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 30, is the depot padding the scheduler applies on **both** sides of every appointment window when it tests whether the truck is already committed. A truck's `status` begins at `AVAILABLE` and takes only one other value, `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Taking a truck out of service is a mechanical hold requested with a non-empty `reason_code`, which the server requires in the body but does not persist anywhere on the truck; it flips `status` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and increments the truck's `version` — a truck that is already `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and there is no way back to `AVAILABLE` — but it deliberately leaves the truck's existing appointments in place, still occupying their windows, so that a dispatcher rescheduling around a breakdown does not silently double-book the replacement vehicle. Trucks can be listed filtered by `store_id` and by `status`.

### 3.2. Contractor Accounts and Trailing-Volume Tiering

A contractor relationship is opened by a `PlatformAdmin` as a **pro account**, carrying the `business_name` on the paperwork and the `home_store_id` of the pro desk that owns the relationship. Two coding switches are pinned at this moment: `po_required`, which when true forces every order submission for this account to carry a non-empty `po_number`, and `job_name_required`, which when true forces every order submission to carry a non-empty `job_name`. Both default to false. `baseline_volume_cents`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 0, is trailing volume carried over from the legacy system and is always counted in the trailing window regardless of dates. `tier_review_mode` selects which of two tier-determination variants prices this account's orders; it takes the values `PINNED_AT_REVIEW` and `ROLLING_365_INCLUSIVE` and defaults to `PINNED_AT_REVIEW`. An account's `status` begins at `ACTIVE` and takes only one other value, `HOLD`.

Opening the account immediately performs a **tier review**, so a newly created account already reports a `trailing_volume_cents`, a `tier_code`, a `tier_discount_pct` and a `tier_reviewed_at` stamp rather than nulls. Four events in total constitute a tier review: creating the account, booking a trailing-volume correction, invoicing an order, and crediting a return. Each of them recomputes all three tier figures and re-stamps `tier_reviewed_at` with the current logical clock instant.

The trailing volume itself is computed in two steps. First the window: it opens 365 days before the current clock instant and closes at the current clock instant, and **both ends are inclusive**. An order counts toward the window when its `credit_status` is either `INVOICED` or `PAID`, and its `invoiced_at` instant is at or after the window's opening instant and at or before the current clock instant. Orders in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNBILLED`, `AUTHORIZED` or `VOIDED` never contribute, which means an order that has been authorized against the credit line but not yet invoiced does not move the tier by a single cent. `trailing_volume_cents` is then the account's `baseline_volume_cents` plus the sum of `net_volume_cents` across the account's in-window orders, where an order's `net_volume_cents` is its `subtotal_cents` less its `credited_gross_refund_cents` — returns net out at their gross refund value, before any restocking fee. Second, the volume is matched against the tier ladder of §2.9 to yield `tier_code` and `tier_discount_pct`.

Which volume figure feeds that match depends on the account's `tier_review_mode`, and the two variants can price the identical order differently. Under `PINNED_AT_REVIEW`, order pricing uses the `tier_code` and `tier_discount_pct` already persisted on the account by the most recent tier review; the order being priced never influences its own tier, so an order that would by itself carry the account over a rung's opening volume is still priced at the old rung. Under `ROLLING_365_INCLUSIVE`, order pricing first re-runs the two-step trailing-volume computation described in the previous paragraph against the clock at the pricing instant — the window rolls forward with the clock rather than staying pinned to the figure the last tier review stored, so an invoice that has since aged out of the 365-day window no longer counts toward the volume this order is priced against — and adds the order's own `list_subtotal_cents` — the undiscounted line total, chosen precisely so the computation cannot be circular — to that recomputed volume, matching the ladder against that sum instead. That recomputation only prices the order: it is not a tier review, so it neither rewrites the account's `trailing_volume_cents`, `tier_code` or `tier_discount_pct` nor re-stamps `tier_reviewed_at`. For identical stored data the two variants can therefore select different rungs and produce different `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents` for the same order. The rung actually used to price an order is recorded on the order as `tier_code_applied`.

A `CreditManager` can book a **trailing-volume correction** against an account — a legacy migration figure, a goodwill credit, a reconciliation after a data fix. The correction carries `delta_cents`, the signed amount to apply — any integer, positive, negative or zero — and a non-empty `memo` recording why. The delta is added to `baseline_volume_cents`, which is what makes the correction permanent rather than window-bound; the addition is not floored, so a large negative correction may drive `baseline_volume_cents` and the resulting `trailing_volume_cents` below zero, and a negative trailing volume simply matches the `MEMBER` rung. The correction counts as a tier review event, the account's `version` increases by one, and the response body is the updated account. Accounts can be listed filtered by `home_store_id`, by `tier_code` and by `status`, and a read of one account reports its tier, its trailing volume, its exemption states in `tax_exempt_state_codes` and its rebate balance in `rebate_accrued_cents`.

### 3.3. Commercial Credit: Exposure, Authorization, Freezes and Holds

A `CreditManager` extends terms by opening a **credit line** for an account, naming the `pro_account_id`, the `limit_cents` ceiling (at least 1 cent) and the `terms_days` net payment terms (at least 1 day). An account may hold at most one credit line; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_EXISTS`. A new line opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `frozen_reason_code` null and its two money figures computed rather than assumed: opening the line immediately runs the exposure recomputation described in the next paragraph, so `exposure_cents` is 0 and `available_cents` equals `limit_cents` only when the account has no order already sitting in `AUTHORIZED` or `INVOICED` — a card order authorized before the line existed is in the exposure base from the instant the line opens. Credit lines can be listed filtered by `pro_account_id` and by `status`.

Exposure is a recomputed aggregate, never an incrementally adjusted balance. It is the sum of `total_cents` across the account's orders whose `credit_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `INVOICED`, less the sum of `credited_refund_cents` across those same orders. `available_cents` is `limit_cents` less `exposure_cents`. Orders in `PAID` or `VOIDED` leave the exposure base entirely, and orders that have never been submitted never enter it. The recomputation runs on five occasions: when an order is authorized at submission, when it is invoiced, when it is settled, when it is voided by cancellation, and when a return against it is credited.

At submission, exposure gates the order. When the order's `payment_method` is `CREDIT_LINE`, its `total_cents` must be no greater than the line's `available_cents`; the comparison is inclusive, so an order landing exactly on the remaining headroom is accepted, and one cent beyond it is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). An order whose `payment_method` is `CARD` bypasses the gate entirely — it neither consumes headroom nor is blocked by its absence, though it still enters the exposure base once authorized.

The **credit-utilization review** is the mechanism that protects the retailer from a contractor who is quietly filling the line. Its threshold is 90 percent of the limit: the freeze threshold in cents is `limit_cents` multiplied by nine tenths, rounded half up to a whole cent. When exposure reaches or exceeds that threshold the line freezes. Two points about the review are load-bearing and easy to get wrong. First, it is evaluated **only** when an order moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `INVOICED` — authorizing an order at submission recomputes exposure but runs no review, so a line can legitimately sit above its freeze threshold on authorized-only volume without freezing. Second, it looks at the freshly recomputed exposure, not the figure that was on the record before the invoice landed.

A freeze can also be commanded directly: a `CreditManager` freezing a line supplies a non-empty `reason_code` and the line records that supplied string verbatim as its `frozen_reason_code` — the client's own value, not a fixed `MANUAL` literal — whereas a freeze produced by the utilization review records `frozen_reason_code` as exactly `UTILIZATION_REVIEW`. Both paths do the same three things — they move `status` from `ACTIVE` to `FROZEN`, they increment the line's `version`, and they cascade into the owning account, driving its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `HOLD`. An account on `HOLD` is barred from five operations, each rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`: submitting a job quote, revising a job quote, approving a job quote, accepting a job quote, and submitting a pro order. Reading, listing, negotiating counter-offers, reserving, staging, delivering, invoicing and settling all continue to work; the hold stops new commercial commitments, not the completion of existing ones.

Recovery is deliberately two-handed. Reinstating the **credit line** moves it from `FROZEN` back to `ACTIVE`, clears `frozen_reason_code` and increments its `version`, but it is guarded twice: a line that is already `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FROZEN`, and a genuinely frozen line whose `exposure_cents` is still at or above the freeze threshold is rejected with `ERR_UTILIZATION_STILL_HIGH` — the manager must first bring exposure down, by settling invoices or crediting returns, before the line will come back. Reinstating the line does **not** by itself lift the account's hold. That is a separate act on the **account**, moving its `status` from `HOLD` to `ACTIVE` and incrementing its `version`, and it is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_FROZEN` while the account's credit line is still frozen — and with that same `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_FROZEN` when the account has no credit line at all, there being no active line to point to. An account that is not on `HOLD` is refused earlier, by the generic state guard, with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The cycle is genuinely repeatable: a reinstated line can be frozen again by the next invoice's review, and a reinstated account can go back on hold behind it.

### 3.4. Resale Exemption Certificates and Tax Treatment

Contractors buying material for resale do not pay sales tax on it, but only where they have a valid state-issued resale certificate on file. A `ContractorAdmin` files a **resale exemption certificate** naming the `pro_account_id`, the two-letter `state_code` the exemption covers, the `certificate_number` the state issued, and `expires_on`, the ISO calendar date (in `YYYY-MM-DD` form) on which the certificate lapses. Because a newer filing is designed to displace an older one for the same state, the filing also carries `expected_pro_account_version` under the stale-view rule of §2.7. A filing lands in `status` `PENDING` and, as a mutation of the account's exemption picture, increments the owning account's `version` by one. The filing step never tests `expires_on` against the clock — an already-lapsed date is accepted here and bites only at verification. Certificates can be listed filtered by `pro_account_id`, by `state_code` and by `status`.

Only a `TaxComplianceOfficer` may move a certificate onward. Verification is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_MISMATCH` unless `expires_on` is **strictly after** the current clock date — the comparison is strict, so a certificate whose `expires_on` falls on today's date is already lapsed and cannot be verified. A successful verification moves `status` from `PENDING` to `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the current clock instant, and increments the certificate's `version`. It also supersedes the incumbent: any other certificate of the same account and the same `state_code` that was in `VERIFIED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Finally, the account's `tax_exempt_state_codes` is recomputed; that recomputation is itself a mutation of the account, so a verification — and likewise a revocation below — refreshes the account's `updated_at` and increments its `version`. A certificate in any state other than `PENDING` is refused the verify transition by the generic state guard with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, which is evaluated before the lapse test. That derived array holds exactly those `state_code` values for which the account has a certificate whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `expires_on` is strictly after the current clock date — the same strict comparison, applied continuously, so a verified certificate silently drops out of the array on the day it expires without any transition being recorded against it.

Revocation is the other exit. A `TaxComplianceOfficer` revoking a certificate supplies a `reason_code`, which is stored as `revoke_reason_code`; the certificate moves from `VERIFIED` to `REVOKED`, its `version` increments, and `tax_exempt_state_codes` is recomputed without it. Revocation is available only from `VERIFIED`; from any other state the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Crucially, revocation is not retroactive: orders that were already priced keep the `tax_exempt_applied` flag and the `tax_cents` figure they were priced with, because the retailer has already told the contractor what the invoice will say. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `REVOKED` are final resting states — nothing moves a certificate out of them.

The exemption array is consumed in exactly two places. At order submission it gates the order: an order whose `resale_purchase` is true is rejected with `ERR_NO_VALID_TAX_CERTIFICATE` unless the fulfillment store's `state_code` appears in the account's `tax_exempt_state_codes`. And during pricing it sets the order's `tax_exempt_applied` flag, which is what suppresses the tax line (see §3.6).

### 3.5. Bulk Quotes and the Negotiation Cycle

Large jobs are not bought at shelf price; they are bid. A `ContractorAdmin` opens a **job quote** naming the `pro_account_id` it belongs to, the `originating_store_id` of the pro desk that will price it (whose list prices will be snapshotted, and which need not be the store that eventually fulfills anything), the `job_name` the quote is tied to, and `requested_term_days`, at least 1, being the length of price protection the contractor is asking for. A new quote opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `round_count` at 0 and `revision_count` at 0. Quotes can be listed filtered by `pro_account_id`, by `originating_store_id` and by `status`.

While the quote is in `DRAFT` the contractor builds it up out of **quote lines**, each naming a `sku` and a `qty_units` of at least 1. A quote may carry only one line per SKU — a second line naming a SKU the quote already carries is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a line added once the quote has left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a line whose `sku` has no stock position at the originating store is refused with `ERR_SKU_NOT_STOCKED`. A line records the `job_quote_id` of its owning quote and, at this stage, nothing about price: `list_unit_price_cents`, `negotiated_unit_price_cents`, `line_list_subtotal_cents` and `line_negotiated_subtotal_cents` all stay null until the relevant step fills them. Lines can be read one at a time by their own identifier — which is exactly the identifier a counter-offer must name — and listed either under their quote or filtered by `job_quote_id` and `sku`.

Submission takes the quote from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and is the moment list prices are frozen. It is refused if the quote has no lines at all (`ERR_NO_QUOTE_LINES`), if any line's `sku` has lost its stock position at the originating store (`ERR_SKU_NOT_STOCKED`), or if the owning account is on hold (`ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`). On success, every line's `list_unit_price_cents` is copied from the originating store's stock position for that SKU, every line's `line_list_subtotal_cents` is set to that unit price multiplied by the line's `qty_units`, the quote's `list_subtotal_cents` is set to the sum of those line figures, and the quote's `version` increments. These are exact integer products and sums; nothing is rounded.

The negotiation itself is a bounded loop between the desk and the contractor. A `ProDeskAssociate` writes a **counter-offer round**, taking the quote from `SUBMITTED` to `COUNTERED`. The round carries `line_prices`, an array whose entries each name a `quote_line_id` and the `negotiated_unit_price_cents` the desk is offering for it; a `term_days` figure the desk is proposing, required and at least 1; and the `expected_job_quote_version` required by §2.7. Four business checks guard the round beyond the stale-view test. The quote's `round_count` must be strictly below three, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_ROUND_LIMIT` — three counter-offers is the whole negotiation budget. Every `quote_line_id` named must belong to this quote, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. The array must name **every** line of the quote, because partial rounds are not a thing the desk may do; an omission is rejected with `ERR_INCOMPLETE_COUNTER_OFFER`. And every `negotiated_unit_price_cents` must be at least 1 cent and no greater than that line's snapshotted `list_unit_price_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_NEGOTIATED_PRICE` — the desk may discount, never mark up.

A successful round **supersedes** the previous one wholesale. Each named line's `negotiated_unit_price_cents` is overwritten and its `line_negotiated_subtotal_cents` is set to that price multiplied by the line's `qty_units`. The quote's `negotiated_total_cents` becomes the sum of those line figures, and its `discount_depth_pct` is recomputed as the amount given away divided by the list value — that is, `list_subtotal_cents` less `negotiated_total_cents`, divided by `list_subtotal_cents` — rounded half up to four decimal places. Both fields are null until the first counter-offer lands. The quote also records the round's proposed `term_days` as the candidate term, stamps `authored_by_user_id` with the acting associate's `X-User-ID`, increases `round_count` by one, and increments its `version`.

The contractor's answer to a round is either acceptance further down the chain or a **revision**, which is the cycle edge that closes the loop: a revision takes the quote from `COUNTERED` back to `SUBMITTED`, carries a `revision_note` explaining what the contractor wants changed, increases `revision_count` by one, and increments the quote's `version`. It is subject to the same three-round budget (`ERR_QUOTE_ROUND_LIMIT`) and the same account-hold bar (`ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`). One subtlety matters for pricing: a revision does **not** wipe the negotiated prices from the previous round. They stay on the quote lines until the next counter-offer overwrites them, so a quote sitting in `SUBMITTED` after a revision still reports the last round's `negotiated_total_cents` and `discount_depth_pct`. The full cycle is therefore `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → SUBMITTED → COUNTERED`, and it can turn until the round budget is spent.

Approval is the segregation-of-duties gate. A `CreditManager` moves the quote from `COUNTERED` to `APPROVED`, which stamps `approved_by_user_id` and increments the quote's `version`. Three conditions guard it: the acting manager's `X-User-ID` must differ from the quote's `authored_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the quote's `discount_depth_pct` must be no greater than 30 percent, otherwise `ERR_DISCOUNT_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, because a discount deeper than that needs an authority this API does not model; and the account must not be on hold, otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`.

Acceptance by the `ContractorAdmin` moves the quote from `APPROVED` to `ACCEPTED` and starts the clock on price protection. It too requires an account that is not on hold. The term the contractor actually gets is the **lesser** of what was asked and what the tier permits: `term_days` is set to the smaller of `requested_term_days` and the `max_quote_term_days` of the tier persisted on the account at the acceptance instant — 15 days at `MEMBER`, 30 at `GOLD`, 60 at `PLATINUM`, 90 at `DIAMOND`. `term_start` is set to the current clock instant and `term_end` to `term_start` advanced by `term_days` days. The negotiated prices bind while the clock is at or after `term_start` and at or before `term_end`; **both bounds are inclusive** everywhere downstream. The quote's `version` increments.

Two exits close the quote out. Once the clock is strictly past `term_end`, a `ProDeskAssociate` retires the quote by moving it from `ACCEPTED` to `EXPIRED`, incrementing its `version`; attempting this while the clock is still inside the term is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_TERM_ACTIVE`. Expiry is not retroactive — orders already priced under the quote keep the unit prices snapshotted onto their order lines and are never repriced. Separately, a `ContractorAdmin` may **withdraw** a quote that has not yet been accepted, supplying a `reason_code` that is recorded as `withdrawn_reason_code` and incrementing the quote's `version`. Withdrawal is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` and `APPROVED`, and from anywhere else — including from `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED` or an already-withdrawn quote — it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the quote's three terminal states.

### 3.6. Order Assembly, Pricing and Totals

A **pro order** is opened by a `ContractorAdmin` against a `pro_account_id` and a `fulfillment_store_id`. The fulfillment store is the order of record in three separate senses: its list prices are the ones used, its `tax_rate_pct` and `state_code` are the ones applied, and it heads the sourcing waterfall. The order declares its `payment_method`, either `CREDIT_LINE` or `CARD`, and its `delivery_mode`, either `WILL_CALL` or `DELIVERY`. It may optionally name a `job_quote_id` to bind negotiated prices — a quote belonging to a different account is refused at creation with `ERR_QUOTE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` — and may optionally carry a `po_number` and a `job_name` for coding. Three booleans, each defaulting to false, complete the picture: `lift_gate_required` records that the jobsite needs a lift gate, `job_lot_required` demands that every line be sourced from one single store for dye-lot consistency, and `resale_purchase` declares the material bought for resale and therefore tax-exempt. A new order sits at `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT` and `credit_status` `UNBILLED`, with `tier_code_applied`, every priced money field (`list_subtotal_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents`, `freight_surcharge_cents`, `lift_gate_fee_cents`, `tax_cents`, `total_cents`, `net_volume_cents`) and every stamp (`priced_at`, `invoiced_at`, `fulfilled_at`, `cancel_reason_code`) null until the relevant step fills them — with two exceptions that start at a value rather than null: `tax_exempt_applied` is `false`, and `credited_refund_cents` and `credited_gross_refund_cents` are 0. Orders can be listed filtered by `pro_account_id`, by `job_quote_id`, by `fulfillment_store_id`, by `fulfillment_status` and by `credit_status`.

**Order lines** are added while the order is in `DRAFT` — an addition attempted once the order has left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — each naming a `sku` and a `qty_units` of at least 1, with one line per SKU per order, a repeat SKU being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `sku` with no stock position at the fulfillment store is refused with `ERR_SKU_NOT_STOCKED`. Each line records its owning `pro_order_id` and, until pricing runs, leaves `price_source`, `list_unit_price_cents`, `effective_unit_price_cents` and `line_subtotal_cents` null and both counters — `fulfilled_units` and `returned_units` — at 0. Lines can be read individually by their own identifier, which is what a return request must name, and listed either under their order or filtered by `pro_order_id` and `sku`.

Before committing, the contractor or the desk can ask for a **read-only pricing preview**, which runs the whole pricing computation against the current clock and the current tier and persists nothing at all. The preview reports `tier_code_applied`, `list_subtotal_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents`, `freight_surcharge_cents`, `lift_gate_fee_cents`, `tax_exempt_applied`, `tax_cents` and `total_cents`. The preview's `data` is exactly those nine computed fields and nothing else — no `id`, no `version`, no state fields. Like submission, it refuses an order with no lines, reporting `ERR_NO_ORDER_LINES`, and like submission it reports `ERR_SKU_NOT_STOCKED` when some line's `sku` no longer has a stock position at the fulfillment store to take a list price from. Nothing else guards it: an account on hold, a frozen credit line, a lapsed quote term and an exhausted credit headroom are all irrelevant to a preview.

Submission is where the money is fixed. It drives `fulfillment_status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and in the same request drives `credit_status` from `UNBILLED` to `AUTHORIZED`. Eight conditions guard it, in the relative order set out in §2.11: the order must have at least one line (`ERR_NO_ORDER_LINES`); the account must not be on hold (`ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`); an order paying by `CREDIT_LINE` requires the account's credit line to exist (`ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`) and to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CREDIT_LINE_FROZEN`); `po_number` must be non-empty when the account's `po_required` is true (`ERR_PO_NUMBER_REQUIRED`); `job_name` must be non-empty when `job_name_required` is true (`ERR_JOB_NAME_REQUIRED`); a `resale_purchase` order requires the fulfillment store's `state_code` to be in the account's `tax_exempt_state_codes` (`ERR_NO_VALID_TAX_CERTIFICATE`); a quote-bound order requires that quote to be in `ACCEPTED` (`ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTED`) and the clock to be inside its inclusive term window (`ERR_QUOTE_TERM_EXPIRED`); and an order paying by `CREDIT_LINE` requires its `total_cents` to fit inside `available_cents` (`ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`). On success the server writes `tier_code_applied`, every order-line price field and every order money field, stamps `priced_at` with the current clock instant, sets `net_volume_cents` to `subtotal_cents` less `credited_gross_refund_cents`, recomputes the credit line's `exposure_cents` and `available_cents` — only for a `CREDIT_LINE` order, since a `CARD` submission touches no credit line at all even though the authorized order does enter the exposure base of any later recomputation — and increments the order's `version`.

Pricing a line proceeds in creation order across the order's lines. First the tier: the discount rate comes from the account's tier determination under whichever variant the account's `tier_review_mode` selects (§3.2), and the rung used is recorded on the order as `tier_code_applied`. Then, for each line, `list_unit_price_cents` is copied from the fulfillment store's stock position for that SKU — never from a sourcing store's position, however the units eventually get sourced. The line then takes one of two price sources. It is `QUOTED` when the order names a `job_quote_id`, that quote has a quote line for the same `sku`, that quote line's `negotiated_unit_price_cents` is not null, and the pricing instant falls at or after the quote's `term_start` and at or before its `term_end`; in that case `effective_unit_price_cents` is exactly the negotiated unit price. Otherwise the line is `TIER_DISCOUNTED` and `effective_unit_price_cents` is the line's `list_unit_price_cents` reduced by the tier discount and rounded half up to a whole cent. Either way, `line_subtotal_cents` is the `effective_unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `qty_units` as an exact integer product. Note that quote binding is decided per line: an order bound to a quote that covers only some of its SKUs will carry `QUOTED` lines and `TIER_DISCOUNTED` lines side by side.

**The position of the rounding step in tier-discounted pricing is pinned, and it changes the answer.** The discount is applied to the *unit* price and rounded there; only then is the rounded unit price multiplied by the quantity. Consider a line with a `list_unit_price_cents` of 1999, a tier discount of 8 percent, and a `qty_units` of 7. The discounted unit price is 1839.08 cents, which rounds half up to 1839, and the line subtotal is therefore 12873 cents. The alternative reading — multiply the list price by the quantity to get 13993, apply the 8 percent discount to the line to get 12873.56, and round that — yields 12874. Implementations must produce 12873.

Once all lines are priced, the order's own figures are assembled, and here too **the evaluation order is pinned**. The order's `subtotal_cents` is the sum of the lines' `line_subtotal_cents`; its `list_subtotal_cents` is the sum over lines of `list_unit_price_cents` multiplied by `qty_units`; and its `discount_cents` is `list_subtotal_cents` less `subtotal_cents`. Next comes freight. A `DELIVERY` order starts from a base freight surcharge of $89.00, and a `WILL_CALL` order starts from zero and stays there — `freight_surcharge_cents` is always 0 for will-call. The waiver test is then applied to the base freight, and it is keyed on `subtotal_cents` **only** — the post-discount line total, excluding the surcharge itself, the lift-gate fee and the tax — which is precisely what makes the apparent circularity (the total needs the surcharge, the surcharge seems to need a total) well defined. When a `DELIVERY` order's `subtotal_cents` reaches $2,500.00 the freight is waived to 0; the threshold is inclusive, so an order landing exactly on $2,500.00 pays no freight. Below it, `freight_surcharge_cents` is the full $89.00.

The lift-gate fee is next and it is **never** waived, not even on an order whose freight has been waived: `lift_gate_fee_cents` is $45.00 when the order's `delivery_mode` is `DELIVERY` and its `lift_gate_required` is true, and 0 otherwise. Then the exemption and the tax. The order's `tax_exempt_applied` is true when its `resale_purchase` is true **and** the fulfillment store's `state_code` is among the account's `tax_exempt_state_codes` evaluated at the pricing instant. The tax base is the sum of `subtotal_cents`, `freight_surcharge_cents` and `lift_gate_fee_cents` — freight and the lift-gate fee are taxable. When `tax_exempt_applied` is true, `tax_cents` is 0; otherwise `tax_cents` is the tax base multiplied by the fulfillment store's `tax_rate_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent in a single rounding on the summed base, never per line. Finally, `total_cents` is the sum of `subtotal_cents`, `freight_surcharge_cents`, `lift_gate_fee_cents` and `tax_cents`.

Cancellation is the order's escape hatch, available to a `ContractorAdmin` from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RESERVED` and `STAGED`, and rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` from `FULFILLED` or from an already-cancelled order. It carries a `reason_code` recorded as `cancel_reason_code`, drives `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, and increments the order's `version`. Its cascades depend on how far the order had travelled, and every one of them must be applied. An order that had been authorized has its `credit_status` driven from `AUTHORIZED` to `VOIDED`, after which the credit line's `exposure_cents` and `available_cents` are recomputed — this is the compensating leg that returns the authorized amount to the contractor's headroom. Every reservation of the order that is not already terminal is released, with `released_reason_code` set to `ORDER_CANCELLED`, and each release recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units` at the store that lost it. Any will-call staging of the order that was `SCHEDULED` or `STAGED` is driven to `EXPIRED`, and any delivery appointment of the order that was `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` is driven to `CANCELLED` with its own `cancel_reason_code` set to `ORDER_CANCELLED`, freeing the truck window for other work.

### 3.7. Multi-Store Sourcing and Job-Lot Reservations

A submitted order still has to find its units. A `ProDeskAssociate` runs the **reservation step**, which drives `fulfillment_status` from `SUBMITTED` to `RESERVED` and is the only place in this API where inventory is committed. The step plans the whole order and then either commits the whole plan or commits nothing.

Eligibility is an explicit predicate, not a judgement. For a given SKU, a store is a candidate when its `region_code` equals the `region_code` of the order's fulfillment store **and** it holds a stock position for that SKU whose `on_hand_units` less `reserved_units` is strictly greater than zero. Nothing else qualifies a store: not distance, not size, not whether it is the fulfillment store. If for any line of the order that candidate set is empty, the entire call is rejected with `ERR_NO_SOURCING_CANDIDATES`.

Candidates are then put in a strict order, per line, on four keys applied in sequence. First, the order's own fulfillment store sorts ahead of every other store — this is a two-valued key, the fulfillment store against everybody else, not a distance comparison. Second, `distance_rank` ascending, so nearer stores are drained before farther ones. Third, the candidate stock position's `available_units` descending, so among equidistant stores the deepest position is drawn down first. Fourth, and only as a final tie-break, the store's `created_at` ascending. Record identifiers are never consulted as a tie-break, at any level.

Allocation is a strict waterfall, and the lines are processed in their own `created_at` ascending order. For each line, the server tracks the units still to be found, walks the ranked candidates in order, takes at each store the smaller of the units still needed and that store's `available_units`, plans a reservation for that store when the take is positive, subtracts it from the remainder, and stops as soon as the remainder reaches zero. If any line finishes the walk with units still unfound, the entire call — every line, not just the short one — is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_JOB_LOT`. Then the job-lot rule is applied to the finished plan: if the order's `job_lot_required` is true and the plan draws on more than one distinct store, the entire call is rejected with `ERR_JOB_LOT_SPLIT_NOT_ALLOWED`. **Rejection is atomic in the strongest sense: no reservation row and no change to any store's `reserved_units` survives a failed reservation call.** A caller who retries after a rejection must see exactly the inventory picture that existed before.

On success the plan is persisted as one **reservation** per line-and-store pair. Each reservation is entirely server-derived: it records the `pro_order_id` it serves, the `order_line_id` it serves, the `store_id` holding the units, the `sku` copied from the order line, and the `qty_units` — at least 1 — held at that store for that line. Its `status` starts at `HELD` and its `released_reason_code` is null. Every sourcing store then has its `reserved_units` recomputed as the sum of `qty_units` across its own reservations in `HELD` or `ALLOCATED`, with `available_units` following. The order's `version` increments. The `201 Created` response body is the array of reservations this call created, in the order the waterfall planned them — the order's lines in `created_at` ascending order, and within a line the ranked sourcing stores — and, not being a list read, it carries no pagination fields in `meta`. Reservations can be read individually and listed filtered by `pro_order_id`, by `order_line_id`, by `store_id` and by `status`.

`StoreOperations` moves a reservation from `HELD` to `ALLOCATED` once the units are physically picked, which requires the owning order to be in `RESERVED` and otherwise fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Allocation increments the reservation's `version` and leaves `reserved_units` untouched: `HELD` and `ALLOCATED` both occupy units, so the store's committed figure does not move when a reservation crosses between them.

Release is the reverse leg and carries a `reason_code` — any non-empty string, stored verbatim, never validated against a fixed list — that is recorded as `released_reason_code`. Three values are used across the system: `ORDER_CANCELLED` when the release is a cascade of order cancellation, `STAGING_EXPIRED` when it is a cascade of a will-call sweep, and `MANUAL` when an operator releases the reservation directly. A `HELD` reservation may be released while its order is in `SUBMITTED`, `RESERVED` or `STAGED`; an `ALLOCATED` one only while its order is in `RESERVED` or `STAGED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every release recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units` at the store that lost the units. And every release carries a conditional cascade: **when the release leaves every reservation of the order in `RELEASED`, the order's `fulfillment_status` falls back to `SUBMITTED`** and the order's `version` increments. An order that reverts this way is fully re-reservable — the sourcing step may be run again and may well produce a different plan, because the inventory picture has moved on.

Reservations end in one of two terminal states. `RELEASED` is the abandonment path just described. `CONSUMED` is the fulfilment path: a will-call pickup consumes the order's reservations out of `ALLOCATED`, and a completed delivery consumes them out of `HELD`. Consumption is the only operation in this API that reduces a store's `on_hand_units`: it decrements that figure by the reservation's `qty_units` and then recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units` at that store, so the units leave both the on-hand base and the committed base in the same step.

### 3.8. Will-Call Staging and Jobsite Delivery

A reserved order has to leave the building, and the `delivery_mode` chosen at order creation decides through which channel. The two channels are strictly separated: a `WILL_CALL` order may not take a delivery appointment and a `DELIVERY` order may not take a will-call staging, and either mismatch is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_MISMATCH`. Both channels also insist that the order has actually been sourced — booking either against an order whose `fulfillment_status` is anything other than `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RESERVED`, which is the specialized form the two booking endpoints use in place of the generic state guard.

A **will-call staging** occupies a bay. It is created naming the `pro_order_id` being staged, the `store_id` whose bays are occupied, the `stage_start` instant at which occupancy begins, and `stage_hours`, an integer of at least 1. The server computes `expires_at` as `stage_start` advanced by `stage_hours` hours. Two limits guard the booking. The requested `stage_hours` may not exceed the store's `max_stage_hours`, otherwise `ERR_STAGE_WINDOW_TOO_LONG`. And the store's bays must not be oversubscribed: **the occupancy interval is half-open, `[stage_start, expires_at)`**, and the booking is refused with `ERR_WILL_CALL_CAPACITY_FULL` when the new interval overlaps that of the store's `will_call_bay_slots` figure or more existing stagings in `SCHEDULED` or `STAGED`. Because the interval is half-open, adjacency is not overlap: an existing staging whose `expires_at` is exactly the new staging's `stage_start` does not consume a bay against it, and back-to-back bookings pack perfectly. A new staging starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `picked_up_by` and `picked_up_at` null. Stagings can be listed filtered by `pro_order_id`, by `store_id` and by `status`.

`StoreOperations` moves the staging from `SCHEDULED` to `STAGED` when the material is physically in the bay. This requires the order to be in `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and requires **every live** reservation of the order — those still in `HELD` or `ALLOCATED`, with `RELEASED` and `CONSUMED` ones passed over rather than counted against it — to be in `ALLOCATED`, otherwise `ERR_RESERVATIONS_NOT_ALLOCATED`; an order left with no live reservation at all is refused the same way. A partially picked order cannot be staged. Staging drives the order's `fulfillment_status` from `RESERVED` to `STAGED` and increments both the staging's and the order's `version`.

Pickup moves the staging from `STAGED` to `PICKED_UP` and carries `picked_up_by`, the name of whoever collected the material, which is recorded along with `picked_up_at` set to the current clock instant. It is permitted only while the current clock is strictly before `expires_at`; an attempt at or after that instant is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because a lapsed bay must be swept rather than collected. Pickup then fulfils the order: every reservation of the order goes to `CONSUMED`, each consumption decrementing `on_hand_units` at its store and recomputing that store's `reserved_units` and `available_units`; every order line's `fulfilled_units` is set to its `qty_units`; the order's `fulfilled_at` is stamped with the current clock; and the order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `STAGED` to `FULFILLED`. `PICKED_UP` and `EXPIRED` are the staging's terminal states.

Lapsed bays are reclaimed by an explicit, per-store **will-call sweep**, run by `StoreOperations` against a `store_id`. Consistent with §2.4, nothing expires on its own: a staging whose `expires_at` has passed stays exactly as it is until a sweep of its store is invoked. The sweep drives every staging at that store whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock instant, and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `STAGED`, to `EXPIRED`. Each expiry then fires a three-hop cascade, and every hop must be applied. First, every reservation of the affected order in `HELD` or `ALLOCATED` is released with `released_reason_code` set to `STAGING_EXPIRED`. Second, each of those releases recomputes `reserved_units` and `available_units` at the store that held the units. Third, because every reservation of the order is now `RELEASED`, the order's `fulfillment_status` falls back from `RESERVED` or `STAGED` to `SUBMITTED` — the order survives, keeps its prices and its credit authorization, and can be sourced and staged again. The sweep's response body is a plain object counting what it did — `expired_staging_count`, `released_reservation_count` and `reverted_order_count` — and a sweep that finds nothing due reports all three as 0 rather than failing.

A **delivery appointment** occupies a truck instead of a bay. It is created naming the `pro_order_id` being scheduled, the `delivery_truck_id` that will run it, the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the promised window — both inclusive — and the `jobsite_access_type`, one of `CURBSIDE`, `DRIVEWAY`, `NARROW_ALLEY` or `CRANE_REQUIRED`. Five checks beyond the mode and order-state gates guard the booking, and they are evaluated in the relative order fixed by §2.11. The assigned truck must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. An order whose `lift_gate_required` is true demands both that the truck's `lift_gate_equipped` is true and that the `lift_gate_available` of the truck's own home store — the store the truck was registered against, never the order's fulfillment store — is true, both sides, the equipment and the yard capability, otherwise `ERR_LIFT_GATE_UNAVAILABLE`. The requested `jobsite_access_type` must be a member of the truck's `access_types_supported`, otherwise `ERR_JOBSITE_ACCESS_UNSUPPORTED`. The `window_end` must be strictly after the `window_start`, so a zero-length or inverted window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DELIVERY_WINDOW`. And the truck must be free, which is where the buffer bites: **the requested window is inflated by the truck's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` at both ends, and that inflated interval is closed at both endpoints** — the interval running from `window_start` less the buffer to `window_end` plus the buffer, inclusive of both. If that inflated interval intersects the window of any `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment of the same truck, the booking is refused with `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the inflated interval is closed, touching at a single instant counts as an intersection: two appointments on the same truck must be separated by strictly more than the buffer, and a second booking that begins exactly one buffer-length after the first one ends is a conflict. A new appointment starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `delivered_at` and `cancel_reason_code` null, and immediately occupies the buffered window against later bookings. Appointments can be listed filtered by `pro_order_id`, by `delivery_truck_id` and by `status`.

Confirmation moves the appointment from `SCHEDULED` to `CONFIRMED` and re-tests the truck: a truck that has since gone `OUT_OF_SERVICE` yields `ERR_TRUCK_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and an order no longer in `RESERVED` yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Completion moves the appointment from `CONFIRMED` to `DELIVERED`, requires the order still to be in `RESERVED` and at least one of its reservations still to be live in `HELD` or `ALLOCATED` — reservations already `RELEASED` are passed over rather than blocking the delivery, while an order left with no live reservation at all is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — sets `delivered_at` to the current clock, and fulfils the order by exactly the mechanism will-call pickup uses — reservations to `CONSUMED` with the `on_hand_units` decrements and store recomputations, `fulfilled_units` set to `qty_units` on every line, `fulfilled_at` stamped, and `fulfillment_status` moved from `RESERVED` straight to `FULFILLED`. Note the asymmetry between the channels: a delivery order never passes through `STAGED`. Cancellation moves the appointment from `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` to `CANCELLED`, recording the supplied `reason_code` as `cancel_reason_code` and freeing the truck's buffered window for later appointments; from `DELIVERED` or an already-cancelled appointment it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Cancelling an appointment does **not** release the order's reservations — the material stays committed and the order stays in `RESERVED`, ready to be rescheduled onto another truck. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are the appointment's terminal states.

### 3.9. Invoicing, Rebates, Settlement and Returns

Fulfilment ends the physical story; the credit dimension then runs on alone. A `CreditManager` **invoices** a fulfilled order, moving `credit_status` from `AUTHORIZED` to `INVOICED`. Two conditions guard it: the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `FULFILLED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED`, which is the one place the credit dimension reads the physical dimension; and the order's `payment_method` must be `CREDIT_LINE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since a card order is never invoiced against a credit line. Invoicing is the single most consequential write in this API, and it fires a cascade whose every hop must be applied.

The order itself gets `invoiced_at` stamped with the current clock and its `net_volume_cents` recomputed. Hop one recomputes the credit line's `exposure_cents` and `available_cents`. Hop two puts that freshly recomputed exposure through the **credit-utilization review**: when `exposure_cents` reaches or exceeds the limit multiplied by 90 percent — rounded half up to a whole cent — the credit line takes its own `ACTIVE` to `FROZEN` transition with `frozen_reason_code` set to `UTILIZATION_REVIEW`. Hop three follows from that freeze: the owning account's `status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `HOLD`. This review is pinned to the invoice transition and to nothing else — order submission recomputes exposure without ever running it, which is exactly why an account can sit above the freeze threshold in authorized-but-uninvoiced exposure and only be frozen when the first of those orders is invoiced. Invoicing also constitutes a **tier review event**, recomputing the account's `trailing_volume_cents`, `tier_code` and `tier_discount_pct` and stamping `tier_reviewed_at`. And it books the rebate accrual delta **last**, after that tier review has landed, so the rate the delta is struck at is the rung this very invoice has just persisted rather than the rung the account held a moment before.

The **rebate accrual** is the account's earned volume rebate for a period. A rebate accrual records the `pro_account_id` earning it, the `period_start` date on which the period opened and the `period_end` date 90 days later, both ISO dates; the running `accrued_cents` and `clawed_back_cents`, each starting at 0; the derived `payable_cents`; a `payout_status` starting at `ACCRUING`; and the settlement fields `closed_at`, `payout_reference` and `paid_amount_cents`, the last defaulting to 0. Accruals can be listed filtered by `pro_account_id` and by `payout_status`.

An invoice books its delta onto the account's accrual whose `payout_status` is `ACCRUING`; if the account has none, the invoice transition creates one, with `period_start` set to the current clock date and `period_end` set 90 days after it. The delta added to `accrued_cents` is the order's `subtotal_cents` — the post-discount goods figure, not the total, so freight, the lift-gate fee and tax earn no rebate — multiplied by the rebate rate of the tier persisted on the account at the triggering instant — which on the invoice leg means the tier as re-persisted by that same invoice's tier review, since the review runs first — rounded half up to a whole cent. The rates are 0.0 percent at `MEMBER`, 1.0 percent at `GOLD`, 2.0 percent at `PLATINUM` and 3.0 percent at `DIAMOND`. **Each booking event is rounded on its own delta; the running total is never re-rounded.** After every booking, `payable_cents` is set to `accrued_cents` less `clawed_back_cents`, floored at zero, and the account's `rebate_accrued_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `payable_cents` across the account's accruals whose `payout_status` is `ACCRUING` or `PAYABLE`. Only an `ACCRUING` accrual receives invoice deltas; once a period is `PAYABLE` or `PAID`, a later invoice opens a fresh `ACCRUING` accrual rather than reopening the old one.

A `CreditManager` **closes** an accrual, which stamps `closed_at` with the current clock, increments the accrual's `version`, and branches on the money: `payout_status` goes from `ACCRUING` to `PAYABLE` when `payable_cents` is strictly positive, and from `ACCRUING` to `VOIDED` when `payable_cents` is exactly zero, which happens when clawbacks have consumed the whole accrual. Voiding recomputes the account's `rebate_accrued_cents`, since a `VOIDED` accrual leaves the base. Pay-out then moves the accrual from `PAYABLE` to `PAID`, carrying a required non-empty `payout_reference` recorded as such, setting `paid_amount_cents` to the accrual's `payable_cents`, recomputing the account's `rebate_accrued_cents` — a `PAID` accrual also leaves the base — and incrementing the accrual's `version`. Pay-out requires `payable_cents` to be strictly positive at that instant, otherwise `ERR_NO_REBATE_PAYABLE`. `PAID` and `VOIDED` are the accrual's terminal states, and neither is ever touched again.

Settlement closes the credit dimension. A `CreditManager` records payment against an invoiced order, moving `credit_status` from `INVOICED` to `PAID`, and must supply an `amount_cents` that equals the order's `total_cents` less its `credited_refund_cents` — exactly, to the cent — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Partial payments and overpayments are both refused. Because refunds already credited are subtracted, an order that was invoiced for its full total and then had a return credited settles for less than it was invoiced, and the amount owed depends on the order of operations. Payment then recomputes the credit line's `exposure_cents` and `available_cents`, because `PAID` orders leave the exposure base. `PAID` and `VOIDED` are the credit dimension's terminal states.

Returns run against fulfilled orders. A `ContractorAdmin` opens a **return request** naming the `pro_order_id`, a `reason_code` that is one of `OVER_ORDERED`, `DAMAGED`, `JOB_CANCELLED` or `WRONG_ITEM`, and `lines`, an array whose entries each name an `order_line_id` and the `qty_units` being sent back. Three checks guard the request: the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `FULFILLED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED`; every `order_line_id` must belong to that order, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`; and each requested `qty_units` — which must itself be an integer of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — must be no greater than that line's `fulfilled_units` less its `returned_units`, otherwise `ERR_RETURN_QTY_EXCEEDS_FULFILLED`, which is what stops the same units from being returned twice. Every entry is measured on its own against that remaining figure and `lines` is stored exactly as submitted, so two entries of one request naming the same `order_line_id` are each compared with the same remainder and both are then valued and credited. The request opens at `status` `REQUESTED` with `valuation_mode`, every refund figure, `approved_by_user_id`, `denied_reason_code` and `credited_at` all null. Requests can be listed filtered by `pro_order_id` and by `status`.

A `ProDeskAssociate` either denies the request — moving it from `REQUESTED` to `DENIED`, recording the supplied `reason_code` as `denied_reason_code` and incrementing its `version` — or approves it, which moves it from `REQUESTED` to `APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the acting associate's `X-User-ID`, increments its `version`, and is where the whole refund is valued. Approval first checks the return window: the whole number of days elapsed since the order's `fulfilled_at`, computed by taking the floor of the elapsed time in whole days, must be no greater than 90, otherwise `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

**The valuation itself has two variants, and which one applies is decided once, at approval, and stored on the request as `valuation_mode`.** The mode is `QUOTED_TERM` when the order names a `job_quote_id` and the approval instant is at or before that quote's `term_end`; only `term_end` enters the test — neither the quote's current `status` nor its `term_start` is consulted, and a quote carrying a null `term_end` because it never reached acceptance yields `CURRENT_LIST`. Otherwise it is `CURRENT_LIST`. The two paths differ in one respect — the unit figure each returned line is refunded at — and everything downstream of that figure is identical.

Under `QUOTED_TERM`, each returned line is refunded at the `effective_unit_price_cents` recorded on its order line, which is exactly what the contractor paid, so a return inside a live quote term is a clean reversal of the original sale at the negotiated price. Under `CURRENT_LIST`, each returned line is instead revalued from scratch at today's price: the fulfillment store's current `list_unit_price_cents` for that line's `sku`, reduced by the account's currently persisted `tier_discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent; should that stock position have been removed altogether in the meantime, the unit figure falls back to the `list_unit_price_cents` snapshotted on the order line and is reduced the same way. The two variants therefore diverge whenever list prices have moved or the account's tier has moved since the order was priced, and the divergence propagates: identical return quantities yield a different gross refund, a different restocking fee and a different tax refund. `CURRENT_LIST` can even refund more than the contractor paid, when the negotiated price on the original order was deeper than the tier discount now available. Neither variant is a fallback for the other; each is the correct treatment for its own circumstance.

From there the arithmetic is common. Returned lines are processed in their order lines' `created_at` ascending order, and `gross_refund_cents` is the sum over them of the applicable unit figure multiplied by the returned `qty_units`. The restocking fee is then set by **two independent policies, of which the higher wins**. The age policy charges nothing when the elapsed whole days since `fulfilled_at` are within 30, and 10 percent beyond that. The job-quoted-material policy charges a floor of 15 percent whenever any returned line has a `price_source` of `QUOTED`, and nothing otherwise. The request's `restocking_fee_pct` is the greater of the two, so a quoted-material return sent back on day 3 still carries 15 percent, and a tier-priced return sent back on day 60 carries 10 percent, while a quoted-material return on day 60 carries 15 percent rather than the sum of the two. `restocking_fee_cents` is then that percentage applied to `gross_refund_cents` and rounded half up to a whole cent — **one single rounding, on the summed gross, never per line**, which is the deliberate opposite of the per-unit rounding used when order lines were priced. The goods refund net of the fee is `gross_refund_cents` less `restocking_fee_cents`. `tax_refund_cents` is 0 when the original order's `tax_exempt_applied` was true, and otherwise that net goods figure multiplied by the fulfillment store's `tax_rate_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent — note that the refunded tax is computed on the goods net of the restocking fee, not on the gross. Finally, `refund_total_cents` is the net goods figure plus `tax_refund_cents`.

Crediting is the money movement, performed by a `CreditManager`, which moves the request from `APPROVED` to `CREDITED` and requires the order's `credit_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`, `INVOICED` or `PAID`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — a voided order has nothing to credit against. The credit stamps `credited_at` with the current clock, adds each returned `qty_units` to its order line's `returned_units`, increments the request's `version`, and then fires the deepest cascade in the system, all four hops of which must be applied. Hop one recomputes the order's `credited_refund_cents`, `credited_gross_refund_cents` and `net_volume_cents`. Hop two recomputes the credit line's `exposure_cents` and `available_cents` from those order-level changes — this is the compensating leg of the exposure invariant, and it is what gives the contractor headroom back for returned material. Hop three takes the changed `net_volume_cents` and recomputes the account's `trailing_volume_cents`, `tier_code` and `tier_discount_pct`, stamping `tier_reviewed_at`: **a credited return is a tier review event and can demote an account**, which is why the `CURRENT_LIST` variant reads the tier persisted at the approval instant rather than after the credit. Hop four books the rebate clawback. The clawback delta is the request's `gross_refund_cents` — the pre-fee, pre-tax figure — multiplied by the rebate rate of the tier persisted at the triggering instant, which because hop four follows hop three is the possibly demoted rung that this very credit has just written, rounded half up to a whole cent, and added to `clawed_back_cents` on the account's **newest** accrual whose `payout_status` is `ACCRUING` or `PAYABLE`. Clawbacks reach one rung wider than accruals do: a period that has been closed but not yet paid is still clawable, and can be driven all the way down to a `payable_cents` of zero — which is precisely what makes a later pay-out attempt on it fail with `ERR_NO_REBATE_PAYABLE`. `PAID` and `VOIDED` accruals are immune. That booking recomputes `payable_cents` on the touched accrual and `rebate_accrued_cents` on the account. `DENIED` and `CREDITED` are the return request's terminal states.
