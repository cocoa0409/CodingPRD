# Liquidation Marketplace Bulk Lot Auctions API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retailers dispose of customer returns, shelf-pulls and excess inventory in bulk, and the economics of that disposal are brutal: a pallet of mixed apparel that cost the retailer real money at wholesale may recover only a fraction of its declared retail value, and every hour that inventory sits on a dock consumes space that new season goods need. This platform is the wholesale liquidation venue that sits between those retailers and the secondary-market resale businesses that buy from them. Sellers assemble **lots** — physical groupings of merchandise described by a line-by-line **manifest** — and offer them through one of three sale mechanisms: a timed ascending auction, a take-it-now fixed price, or a private negotiated ladder of offers and counter-offers. Buyers, who are resale businesses rather than consumers, must be qualified before they can commit money: their business identity is verified, their state resale certificate is checked for validity, and a credit ceiling is granted against which every open bid and unpaid order is charged.

Two failure modes dominate the trust economics of this market, and the API is shaped around both. The first is **manifest inaccuracy**: a buyer who paid for 400 units and received 320 has been overcharged, and the platform must be able to price that shortfall, credit the buyer, and hold the seller accountable without letting a single bad lot destroy an otherwise reliable seller's standing. The second is **manipulation of the sale mechanism**: shill bidding by a seller through an affiliated buyer account, last-second sniping that denies honest bidders a chance to respond, and resale of protected brands through channels the brand owner never authorised. Every one of these concerns lands in this specification as a hard, ordered validation rather than as an after-the-fact investigation.

Money in this system flows down a single pipeline that must be reproducible to the cent. A hammer price selects a buyer premium tier; the premium plus an estimated freight charge produces a provisional amount due; actual carrier freight replaces the estimate at pickup; a late fee may attach; an approved discrepancy claim credits the buyer; and on the seller's side a per-line commission and an optional de-branding deduction produce the payout that feeds the seller's recovery-rate analytics, which in turn reassign the seller's commission tier for the *next* settlement. Because that pipeline is order-sensitive by design, this document specifies not only each formula but the order in which its steps are evaluated and rounded. All endpoints described here are mounted under `/liquidation`, with a single test-driving clock endpoint outside that prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participants, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is exercising. If either header is missing or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other consideration — before the path is resolved, before the body is parsed, before any state is inspected. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Five roles exist, and their spellings are exact: `PlatformAdmin`, `SellerOps`, `Buyer`, `MarketplaceOps`, and `TrustSafety`.

A `PlatformAdmin` provisions the reference data the marketplace runs on — seller accounts, buyer accounts, warehouses, carriers, brand policies — and adjusts buyer credit ceilings. It never touches a lot, a bid or a claim. `SellerOps` acts on behalf of exactly one liquidating seller: it builds lots and manifests, creates and publishes listings, and takes the seller side of a negotiation. `Buyer` acts on behalf of exactly one qualified resale business: it bids, places proxy bids, opens and counters negotiations, pays, books freight and files manifest discrepancy claims. `MarketplaceOps` is the operations desk: it reviews submitted manifests, closes auctions, completes pickups, decides manifest discrepancy claims, and runs the two periodic sweeps. `TrustSafety` is the compliance desk: it qualifies, rejects, suspends and reinstates buyers, and it reverses claim decisions it did not itself make.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role, and this binding is what makes ownership checks possible. When `X-User-Role` is `SellerOps`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SellerAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `SellerAccount` compare the resource's seller reference to `X-User-ID`, so a lot-scoped write requires that the lot's `seller_id` equal `X-User-ID`, and a listing-scoped or negotiation-scoped write requires the same of the lot standing behind that listing or negotiation. When `X-User-Role` is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BuyerAccount`; ownership checks compare the `buyer_id` of the bid, negotiation, sale order, pickup appointment or claim being written to `X-User-ID`. A violation in either direction is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketplaceOps` or `TrustSafety`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule demands an audit trail — as `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` on a lot whose manifest was reviewed, as `decided_by_user_id` on a decided claim, and as `reversed_by_user_id` on a reversed claim — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced through those recorded identifiers. The `MarketplaceOps` operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as a lot's `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` must not be the `MarketplaceOps` operator who decides a manifest discrepancy claim whose manifest line belongs to that lot; and the `TrustSafety` operator reversing a claim must not be the operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as that claim's `decided_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and — as §2.5 makes precise — it is evaluated ahead of every claim state, evidence and amount check, so a reviewer who is disqualified learns that first rather than learning about the claim's condition.

Read endpoints are broadly shared. `PlatformAdmin` and `MarketplaceOps` may read every resource; `SellerOps`, `TrustSafety` and `Buyer` may read lots, manifest lines, listings, bids, negotiations, offers and sale orders, with appointment reads available to `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketplaceOps`, `SellerOps` and `Buyer`, and claim reads available to `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketplaceOps`, `SellerOps`, `TrustSafety` and `Buyer`. Recovery analytics may be read by `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketplaceOps` and `SellerOps`, but a `SellerOps` caller may only request analytics for its own seller — asking for another seller's numbers is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The logical-clock endpoint is open to all five roles because every role needs deterministic time in test scenarios. The exact role list for each endpoint appears in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless an endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise; the offer rounds of a negotiation, for instance, are returned in round order, which coincides with creation order because rounds are appended. For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when unreturned rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and clients never supply them on creation. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never modified afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every accepted mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that resource. There is one deliberate exception: an `Offer` is an immutable record of a single negotiation round. An `Offer` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because once written it is never touched again. Every other entity in this specification — `SellerAccount`, `BuyerAccount`, `Warehouse`, `Carrier`, `BrandPolicy`, `Lot`, `ManifestLine`, `Listing`, `Bid`, `Negotiation`, `SaleOrder`, `PickupAppointment` and `Claim` — carries the full set of four common properties.

Version increments matter well beyond bookkeeping, because two endpoints require clients to echo a version they have observed (see §2.7). Concretely: every accepted live bid, proxy bid, publication and closure on a listing increments that `Listing`'s `version` by exactly 1, and every accepted counter-offer and acceptance on a negotiation increments that `Negotiation`'s `version` by exactly 1. Anti-sniping extensions are recorded through the same bidding writes that grant them, so an extension is one of the ways a listing's version can advance under a client's feet.

All temporal reasoning in this system — offering windows, payment terms, pickup deadlines, offer expiry, claim windows, certificate expiry and both sweeps — is evaluated against a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's wall clock. A test-driving endpoint, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it; the call mutates no business row, and it returns the instant it set as `current_utc`. Every subsequent request evaluates every window, deadline, expiry and sweep against that instant.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence: Sixteen Ordered Bands

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once. The response is never ambiguous: the platform evaluates checks in sixteen numbered bands and returns the error belonging to the **lowest-numbered band** that fails, stopping there. Within the sequence, safety and identity questions come before time, time comes before physical capacity, and money comes last — so a request that is both unaffordable and mistimed is reported as mistimed, and a request that is both unaffordable and made by a buyer barred from the brand's territory is reported as a territorial violation.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** `X-User-Role` appears in the endpoint's permitted role list, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a stored row, checked from left to right, with the leftmost unresolved segment deciding the error: `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MANIFEST_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SELLER_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
4. **Resource ownership.** A `SellerOps` caller acts only on lots, manifest lines, listings and negotiations whose lot's `seller_id` equals `X-User-ID`; a `Buyer` caller acts only on bids, negotiations, sale orders, pickup appointments and claims whose `buyer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Violation is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every `SellerOps` and `Buyer` write endpoint, and to the recovery-analytics read when the caller is `SellerOps`.
5. **Segregation of duties.** For a claim decision, the deciding `MarketplaceOps` `X-User-ID` differs from the `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` of the lot owning that claim's manifest line; for a claim reversal, the reversing `TrustSafety` `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `decided_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This band sits deliberately ahead of every claim state, evidence and units check, so a disqualified operator is turned away regardless of the claim's condition.
6. **Request-body foreign keys.** Every foreign reference carried in the request body resolves to a stored row — `seller_id`, `warehouse_id`, `brand_policy_id`, `lot_id`, `listing_id`, `buyer_id`, `carrier_id` and `manifest_line_id` — reported as `ERR_SELLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAREHOUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BRAND_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_MANIFEST_LINE_NOT_FOUND` respectively, each HTTP 404.
7. **Request shape.** All required input fields present; every enum-valued field inside its declared value set; every integer inside its declared range (`unit_count` at least 1, `unit_retail_cents` at least 1, `dock_door_count` at least 1, `daily_pickup_capacity` at least 1, `bid_increment_cents` at least 1); `window_end_at` strictly later than `window_start_at`; and the format-conditional price field present for the chosen sale mechanism (`opening_bid_cents` for an auction, `ask_price_cents` for a fixed price, `min_offer_cents` for negotiated terms). Violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The same band and the same error cover out-of-bounds `limit` and `offset` on list endpoints.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_listing_version` equals the listing's current `version` on a proxy-bid submission, or `ERR_STALE_LISTING_VERSION` (412); `expected_negotiation_version` equals the negotiation's current `version` on a counter-offer, or `ERR_STALE_NEGOTIATION_VERSION` (412). This band precedes every amount, window and credit check, so a client working from a stale read is told to re-read rather than told its price is wrong.
9. **State of the target resource.** The entity being written is in a state from which the requested transition is legal: `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_LISTING_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_NOT_YOUR_TURN` (409), `ERR_ORDER_PAYMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_ORDER_LOGISTICS_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (409) or `ERR_BUYER_QUALIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409), according to the entity in question.
10. **State of a different entity.** Cross-entity guards: `ERR_LOT_MANIFEST_NOT_APPROVED` (409) and `ERR_SELLER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) at publication; `ERR_BUYER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (409) on every buy-side commitment; `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID` (409) when scheduling a dock window; `ERR_PAYMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) when paying an abandoned order; and `ERR_ORDER_LOGISTICS_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_LINE_VOIDED` (409) when filing a claim.
11. **Brand protection and geographic safety.** `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_DEBRANDING_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_BUYER_STATE_PROHIBITED` and `ERR_SHILL_BIDDING_PROHIBITED`, each HTTP 422.
12. **Temporal windows and deadlines.** `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_CLOSABLE`, `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, `ERR_PICKUP_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_STARTED`, `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_RESALE_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` and `ERR_BRAND_EMBARGO_ACTIVE`, each HTTP 422.
13. **Dock interval capacity.** At least one dock door at the requested warehouse is free for the requested window under that warehouse's turnaround buffer, or `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` (422).
14. **Carrier daily aggregate capacity.** The nominated carrier has room on the requested UTC calendar date, or `ERR_CARRIER_DAY_CAPACITY_FULL` (422). This band follows band 13, so a request that fails both the door search and the carrier cap reports the dock error.
15. **Remaining structural constraints.** `ERR_MANIFEST_EMPTY`, `ERR_LISTING_ALREADY_OPEN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEGOTIATION`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUEST_LIMIT`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_VERIFIED_UNITS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CLAIMED_UNITS_EXCEED_LINE`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_ON_ORDER_LOT`, `ERR_WAREHOUSE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BUSINESS_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCAC`, each HTTP 422.
16. **Money.** `ERR_RESERVE_BELOW_OPENING`, `ERR_BID_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_PROXY_MAX_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_FIXED_PRICE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, each HTTP 422.

The per-endpoint scope of each of these rules — which operations a given check actually guards — is stated in the workflow narratives of §3, next to the business rule that motivates it.

### 2.6. Money, Rates, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional-cent fields and no floating-point currency. Field names ending in `_cents` are always integer cents.

All commission and premium rates are integers denominated in **basis points**, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 2,000 basis points is 20 percent and 150 basis points is 1.5 percent. Applying a basis-point rate to an amount means multiplying the amount by the rate and dividing the product by 10,000. Whenever such an application must produce a monetary field, the quotient is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero — and it is rounded exactly once, at the point the formula says to round it. Where a formula rounds an intermediate quantity and then uses the rounded value in a later step, that order is normative and must not be optimised away; §3.6 and §3.9 each describe a case in which resequencing the rounding changes the answer by a cent.

Ratio-valued fields come in two precisions. The two recovery rates, `gross_recovery_rate` and `net_recovery_rate`, are decimal ratios carried to exactly four fractional digits, produced by dividing integer cents by integer cents and rounding half up once at the end. The two accuracy figures, a lot's `manifest_accuracy_score` and a seller's `manifest_accuracy_avg`, are decimal ratios carried to exactly three fractional digits, again rounded half up once at the end of their respective formulas.

Intervals and thresholds in this specification are stated with explicit inclusivity, and implementations must honour the stated direction exactly. A reserve is satisfied by a high bid **at or above** it. A recovery rate qualifies for the preferred commission tier **at or above** the threshold. A seller falls onto manifest probation only when the average is **strictly below** the probation threshold, so an average landing exactly on the threshold keeps the seller in good standing. A standing negotiation offer lapses **at** its expiry instant, so an action attempted at exactly that instant is too late. The pickup-deadline sweep abandons an order only when the clock is **strictly after** the deadline. These directions are repeated at each rule in §3; where the two statements meet, they agree.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Bidding and Negotiation

Two write operations in this system are meaningful only relative to a state the client has already observed, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control.

A proxy-bid submission — `POST /liquidation/listings/{id}/proxy-bids` — requires a non-optional `expected_listing_version` field in its request body, carrying the `version` the client last read from the target `Listing`. A proxy bid encodes a hidden ceiling relative to the auction state the buyer believed was current; if any other write has advanced that listing's `version` in the meantime — another buyer's live bid, another buyer's proxy bid, an anti-sniping extension, or the publication itself — the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LISTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no `Bid` row is created, no exposure is recomputed and no extension is granted. The buyer's remedy is to re-read the listing and resubmit with the version it then observes.

A counter-offer — `POST /liquidation/negotiations/{id}/counter-offers` — requires a non-optional `expected_negotiation_version` field carrying the `version` the client last read from the target `Negotiation`. A counter is only meaningful against the offer the caller actually saw; if the other side has already countered, advancing `round_number` and `version`, the stale counter-offer is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NEGOTIATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), no `Offer` row is created, `round_number` is unchanged and the negotiation's turn does not flip.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Live bids, publications, closures, acceptances, payments, appointment bookings, completions, cancellations, claim filings, evidence submissions, claim decisions, claim reversals and qualification decisions are all specified without an expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline: One Write, Many Rows

The entities in this domain form a cross-referencing mesh rather than a tree, and a great many of the writes described in §3 mutate more than one row. The governing principle is that a cascade is part of the same request: by the time a response is returned, every derived field the write is specified to touch has already been recomputed, and a subsequent read observes the fully settled result. There is no deferred or eventual reconciliation.

Three cascade chains recur often enough to name here. The **recovery chain** runs from a listing publication, an auction close, a fixed-price purchase, a negotiated acceptance or a pickup-deadline abandonment into the seller's retail and hammer aggregates, from there into `gross_recovery_rate`, and from there into `commission_tier` and `commission_rate_bps`; a pickup completion additionally drives `net_proceeds_cents` and `net_recovery_rate`. The **accuracy chain** runs from a claim decision or a claim reversal into `ManifestLine.verified_unit_count`, then `Lot.manifest_accuracy_score`, then the seller's `manifest_accuracy_avg` and `scored_lot_count`, then possibly `account_status`, and then `commission_tier` and `commission_rate_bps`. The **exposure chain** runs from any bid, proxy bid, negotiation opening, counter-offer, auction closure, payment or pickup-deadline sweep into the affected buyer's `outstanding_exposure_cents`.

Each mutated row's own `updated_at` is refreshed and its own `version` advanced when the write mutates that row directly. Cascade-created child rows are always discoverable through a list endpoint: a sale order produced by a closure, purchase or acceptance is found by listing sale orders filtered on `listing_id`; a pickup appointment produced by a booking is found by listing appointments filtered on `sale_order_id`; and the round-1 offer created with a negotiation is found through that negotiation's offers list.

Reversal is specified as true compensation rather than approximation. When a claim decision is reversed, every field the decision moved returns to the value it held before the decision — the order's claim credit, the buyer's wallet credit, the lot's accuracy score, the seller's average, scored lot count, account status and commission tier — with no residual drift.

### 2.9. Nested Paths and Sub-Resource Read Channels

Several endpoints nest one resource inside another: manifest lines under a lot, listings under a lot, bids and negotiations under a listing, offers under a negotiation, payments, appointments and claims under a sale order, and evidence items, decisions and reversals under a claim. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the 404 error returned — a request naming a nonexistent lot and a nonexistent manifest line reports the lot as missing, because the lot segment appears first. Only after every path segment resolves does the platform consider ownership, body references, shape, concurrency or state. The primary path parameter is spelled `{id}` on every endpoint.

Because a number of rows are created as cascades rather than as the direct output of the request that produced them, every such row has a read channel. Listing sale orders accepts `listing_id`, `buyer_id`, `payment_status`, `logistics_status` and `settlement_basis` filters; listing bids accepts `listing_id`, `buyer_id`, `bid_status` and `bid_type`; listing negotiations accepts `listing_id`, `buyer_id` and `negotiation_status`; listing offers accepts `negotiation_id` and `offer_side`; listing appointments accepts `sale_order_id`, `warehouse_id`, `carrier_id` and `appointment_status`; listing claims accepts `sale_order_id`, `manifest_line_id` and `claim_status`; listing manifest lines accepts `lot_id` and `line_status`; listing listings accepts `lot_id`, `listing_status`, `listing_format` and `resale_channel`; listing lots accepts `seller_id`, `warehouse_id`, `brand_policy_id`, `lot_status` and `category_code`; listing seller accounts accepts `account_status` and `commission_tier`; and listing buyer accounts accepts `qualification_status` and `state_code`. Every one of these filters is optional, and every one of these list endpoints also accepts `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.10. Reference Data and Uniqueness

Before any commerce can happen, a `PlatformAdmin` provisions the reference layer. A `SellerAccount` is registered with a `legal_name`, a `tax_id`, a two-letter `state_code` for its registered address and a `contact_email`. A `BuyerAccount` is registered with the same four fields plus, optionally, a `resale_certificate_number`, a `resale_certificate_expires_on` calendar date rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and a `requested_credit_limit_cents` figure that records what the applicant asked for and is advisory only — it never participates in any fiscal check. A `Warehouse` is registered with a `name`, a `state_code` and a `dock_door_count` of at least one door, plus an optional `turnaround_buffer_minutes` that defaults to 15 minutes and may not be negative. A `Carrier` is registered with a `name`, a `scac_code` standard carrier alpha code and a `daily_pickup_capacity` of at least one appointment per day. A `BrandPolicy` is registered with a `brand_code`, a non-empty `permitted_channels` allow-list, and optional `de_branding_required`, `debranding_fee_cents_per_unit`, `prohibited_buyer_state_codes`, `embargo_start_at` and `embargo_end_at` values.

Three uniqueness constraints are enforced at creation, all at band 15. A `tax_id` must be unique across seller accounts, and a `tax_id` must be unique across buyer accounts — the two namespaces are independent, so the same identifier may legitimately appear once on each side, which is exactly the affiliation the anti-shill rule of §3.4 detects. A collision within either namespace is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID` (422). A `scac_code` must be unique across carriers; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCAC` (422). A `brand_code` is likewise unique across brand policies.

Enumerated value sets used across the reference layer and the commerce layer are closed. A lot's `category_code` is one of `APPAREL`, `ELECTRONICS`, `HOME_GOODS` or `TOYS`. A lot's `condition_grade` is one of `NEW_OVERSTOCK`, `CUSTOMER_RETURNS` or `SALVAGE`. A manifest line's `declared_condition` is one of `NEW`, `LIKE_NEW`, `USED_GOOD`, `DAMAGED` or `SALVAGE`. A resale channel — whether it appears as a member of `permitted_channels` or as a listing's `resale_channel` — is one of `MARKETPLACE_RESALE`, `EXPORT_ONLY`, `DISCOUNT_RETAIL` or `SCRAP_ONLY`. A listing's `listing_format` is one of `AUCTION`, `FIXED_PRICE` or `NEGOTIATED`. A claim's `claim_type` is one of `UNIT_SHORTAGE`, `CONDITION_MISGRADE` or `MISSING_SKU`. A value outside the declared set is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) at band 7.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building a Lot and Getting Its Manifest Approved

A seller's working unit is the lot. When a `SellerOps` operator creates one, the payload names the owning seller through a `seller_id`, the origin dock through a `warehouse_id`, an optional `brand_policy_id` when the merchandise carries a protected brand, a human-readable `title`, a `category_code` that later slices recovery analytics, and a `condition_grade` describing the goods as a whole. Creation places the lot in `DRAFT` with `manifest_line_count`, `declared_unit_count` and `declared_retail_value_cents` all at zero, `manifest_accuracy_score` still null because no claim has ever been resolved against it, and `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` still null. One value is copied rather than computed: the lot's `de_branding_required` mirrors the linked brand policy's own `de_branding_required` at the instant of creation, and it is false when no `brand_policy_id` was supplied. That mirror is what later publications and settlements consult, so a brand policy edited after the lot exists does not retroactively change the lot's obligation.

The manifest is built one line at a time. Each appended `ManifestLine` names its owning lot through `lot_id` and carries a `sku`, a `unit_count` of at least one unit, a `unit_retail_cents` declared per-unit retail value of at least one cent, and a `declared_condition` grading that line specifically — a lot graded `CUSTOMER_RETURNS` overall may still contain lines declared `NEW` and lines declared `DAMAGED`. Every appended line begins with `line_status` set to `ACTIVE` and `verified_unit_count` null, since nothing has been physically verified yet. Appending a line recomputes the three lot aggregates over `ACTIVE` lines only: `manifest_line_count` becomes the number of such lines, `declared_unit_count` becomes the sum of their `unit_count` values, and `declared_retail_value_cents` becomes the sum, over those same lines, of each line's `unit_count` multiplied by its `unit_retail_cents`. The lot's `version` advances by one.

Voiding a line is the seller's correction mechanism. A voided line has its `line_status` set to `VOIDED` and is retained permanently for audit, but it leaves every aggregate immediately: the three lot aggregates are recomputed over the remaining `ACTIVE` lines, which restores the declared unit total to exactly what it would have been had the voided line never been appended. Both appending and voiding are only legal while the lot's `lot_status` is `DRAFT` or `MANIFEST_REJECTED`; any other state is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. When the lot was in `MANIFEST_REJECTED`, either edit returns it to `DRAFT` — the `MANIFEST_REJECTED → DRAFT` revision loop — which forces a fresh submission rather than allowing a quietly amended manifest to inherit an old review.

Submitting a manifest advances the lot `DRAFT → MANIFEST_IN_REVIEW`. The submission takes no payload; the lot must be in `DRAFT`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the lot's `seller_id`, and the lot must hold at least one line with `line_status` equal to `ACTIVE`. A submission on a lot with zero active lines — the zero-count boundary, which occurs naturally when a seller voids the last surviving line — is rejected with `ERR_MANIFEST_EMPTY`. An accepted submission advances the lot's `version` by one.

Review is a `MarketplaceOps` responsibility and is the segregation-of-duties anchor for everything that follows. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by a `review_note`. From `MANIFEST_IN_REVIEW`, an approval advances the lot to `MANIFEST_APPROVED` and a rejection advances it to `MANIFEST_REJECTED`; either way the reviewing operator's `X-User-ID` is written to `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` and the lot's `version` advances by one. That recorded identifier has consequences months later: it is precisely the identifier compared at claim-decision time, and it is compared before any other claim check, so the operator who signed off on a manifest can never also adjudicate a buyer's complaint that the manifest was wrong.

### 3.2. Qualifying a Buyer: Certificates, Verification and Credit Headroom

A newly registered buyer can read the marketplace but cannot commit to anything. Registration leaves `qualification_status` at `PENDING`, `business_verification_status` at `UNVERIFIED`, `approved_credit_limit_cents` at zero, `outstanding_exposure_cents` at zero and `wallet_credit_cents` at zero. Qualification is a `TrustSafety` act, driven by a `decision` of `QUALIFY`, `REJECT` or `SUSPEND`, accompanied where relevant by a `business_verified` boolean and an optional `approved_credit_limit_cents` figure that, when supplied, becomes the buyer's granted ceiling.

A `QUALIFY` decision from `PENDING` requires two things beyond the state itself. The buyer's `resale_certificate_number` must be present and non-empty, and the buyer's `resale_certificate_expires_on` date must fall strictly after the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock — a certificate lapsing today is already lapsed for this purpose, and the rejection is `ERR_RESALE_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`. The request must also carry `business_verified` set to true; without it the decision is rejected with `ERR_BUSINESS_NOT_VERIFIED`. When both hold, `qualification_status` advances `PENDING → QUALIFIED`, `business_verification_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, and the buyer may from that moment bid, negotiate and purchase.

The qualification machine is cyclic rather than linear. A `REJECT` decision from `PENDING` advances `PENDING → REJECTED` and sets `business_verification_status` to `REJECTED`; `REJECTED` is terminal and no later decision can move a buyer out of it. A `SUSPEND` decision from `QUALIFIED` advances `QUALIFIED → SUSPENDED`, after which every new buy-side commitment is refused with `ERR_BUYER_NOT_QUALIFIED` while sale orders the buyer already holds remain fully payable — suspension stops new exposure, it does not cancel obligations. A `QUALIFY` decision from `SUSPENDED` advances `SUSPENDED → QUALIFIED` and again sets `business_verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, subject to the same requirement that `resale_certificate_expires_on` fall strictly after the current logical clock's UTC date. Any decision requested from a state that does not admit it — a `SUSPEND` on a `PENDING` buyer, a `QUALIFY` on an already `QUALIFIED` buyer, anything at all on a `REJECTED` buyer — is `ERR_BUYER_QUALIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted decision advances the buyer's `version` by one.

Credit headroom is managed on a separate axis from qualification, by `PlatformAdmin` rather than `TrustSafety`, so a ceiling can be raised or lowered without disturbing the buyer's standing. That adjustment carries `approved_credit_limit_cents`, resets the buyer's granted ceiling, recomputes the derived `available_credit_cents` figure described below, and advances the buyer's `version` by one.

**Exposure and the credit ceiling.** A buyer's `outstanding_exposure_cents` is the total the buyer is currently on the hook for, and it is the sum of two parts. The first part covers live bidding commitments: over all of this buyer's bids whose `bid_status` is either `STANDING` or `WINNING`, sum each bid's `ceiling_cents` plus the buyer premium that ceiling would attract, where that premium is the ceiling multiplied by the premium rate its own amount selects from the tier table in §3.6, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent before being added into the sum. Bids whose `bid_status` is `SUPERSEDED`, `WON` or `LOST` drop out of this part entirely; a `WON` commitment is represented by its sale order instead. The second part covers unsettled orders: over all of this buyer's sale orders whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `logistics_status` is not `ABANDONED`, sum `balance_due_cents`. Adding the two parts gives `outstanding_exposure_cents`.

The buyer's ceiling is `approved_credit_limit_cents` plus `wallet_credit_cents`, and `available_credit_cents` is that ceiling less `outstanding_exposure_cents`. Note the direction carefully: refund credit sitting in `wallet_credit_cents` **expands headroom** rather than reducing exposure, so a buyer with a granted limit one cent short of a commitment and one cent of wallet credit is admitted. Every new commitment is admitted on the exposure the request *would* create: if that prospective exposure is less than or equal to the ceiling the commitment is allowed — the boundary is inclusive, so a prospective exposure exactly equal to the ceiling is admitted — and otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and nothing is created. Because a proxy bid's ceiling is its hidden maximum rather than the public price it currently produces, a proxy bid can consume far more headroom than the visible auction price suggests. This admission test guards three operations: placing a live bid, placing a proxy bid, and opening a negotiation. It is not re-run on a counter-offer.

### 3.3. Offering the Lot: Format Choice, Brand Protection and Publication

An approved lot is not yet for sale; a listing must be created on it, and a listing is created in `DRAFT` so the seller can assemble its terms before exposing them. The creation payload chooses the sale mechanism through `listing_format` and the channel the winning buyer will be licensed to resell through via `resale_channel`, then bounds the offering in time with `window_start_at` and a strictly later `window_end_at`. Price fields are conditional on the format, and band 7 requires the one that matches: an `AUCTION` listing must carry `opening_bid_cents`, a `FIXED_PRICE` listing must carry `ask_price_cents`, and a `NEGOTIATED` listing must carry `min_offer_cents`. Three further fields are optional. `reserve_price_cents` names the price an auction's high bid must reach for the lot to change hands, and leaving it out means the listing has no reserve at all. `bid_increment_cents` is the minimum raise over the current high bid and the step the proxy resolver uses; it must be at least one cent and it defaults to 100,000 cents when omitted. `de_branding_attested` is the seller's attestation that goods will have brand marks removed before resale, and it defaults to false.

One fiscal check runs at creation: when both `reserve_price_cents` and `opening_bid_cents` are supplied on an auction listing, the reserve must be at least the opening bid, since a reserve below the opening bid could never bind. A reserve below the opening bid is `ERR_RESERVE_BELOW_OPENING`. A listing's `listing_status` takes one of four values over its life — `DRAFT` before publication, `OPEN` while the offering is live, and then one of the two terminal outcomes, `CLOSED_SOLD` or `CLOSED_NO_SALE`. A freshly created listing has `listing_status` `DRAFT`, `effective_close_at` seeded to `window_end_at`, `extension_count` at zero, `bid_count` at zero, `current_high_bid_cents` and `current_high_bidder_id` both null, and `reserve_met` set by the reserve rule: it is true when `reserve_price_cents` is null, and otherwise false while there is no high bid. The listing's `min_next_bid_cents` is meaningful only on an auction — it is null on `FIXED_PRICE` and `NEGOTIATED` listings — and on an auction with no bids it equals `opening_bid_cents`.

**Publication is where brand protection bites.** Opening a `DRAFT` listing takes no payload and runs a gate whose checks are ordered by the band structure of §2.5, which matters because a listing can fail several of them at once. First, at band 9, the listing itself must be in `DRAFT`; anything else is `ERR_LISTING_STATE_INVALID`. Then, at band 10, two cross-entity conditions: the backing lot's `lot_status` must be `MANIFEST_APPROVED` or `RELIST_PENDING`, and a lot still in `DRAFT`, `MANIFEST_IN_REVIEW` or `MANIFEST_REJECTED` cannot back an open offering — `ERR_LOT_MANIFEST_NOT_APPROVED`; and the owning seller's `account_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so a seller sitting on `MANIFEST_PROBATION` is barred from publishing anything at all — `ERR_SELLER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Next, at band 11, the brand-protection pair: when the lot names a brand policy, the listing's `resale_channel` must appear in that policy's `permitted_channels` allow-list, and a channel absent from the list is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PERMITTED`; and whenever the lot's `de_branding_required` is true, the listing's `de_branding_attested` must be true, or the publication is refused with `ERR_DEBRANDING_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. Then, at band 12, the embargo test: the offering interval running from `window_start_at` to `window_end_at` must not intersect the brand policy's embargo interval running from `embargo_start_at` to `embargo_end_at`, with both intervals treated as inclusive at both ends, so an offering window that merely touches an embargo endpoint is still an intersection and is rejected with `ERR_BRAND_EMBARGO_ACTIVE`. A policy whose `embargo_start_at` and `embargo_end_at` are both null imposes no embargo. Finally, at band 15, at most one live offering per lot: if another listing on the same lot already has `listing_status` equal to `OPEN`, the publication is refused with `ERR_LISTING_ALREADY_OPEN`.

A publication that clears the whole gate advances the listing `DRAFT → OPEN`, sets `effective_close_at` to `window_end_at` and `extension_count` to zero, and advances the listing's `version` by one. The lot moves with it: `MANIFEST_APPROVED → LISTED` on a first offering, or `RELIST_PENDING → LISTED` when the lot is being offered again after a failed auction or an abandoned pickup. Publication also enters the lot into the seller's gross recovery denominator — the seller's `listed_retail_value_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `declared_retail_value_cents` over that seller's lots whose `lot_status` is `LISTED`, `SOLD` or `RELIST_PENDING`, which recomputes `gross_recovery_rate`, which reassigns `commission_tier` and `commission_rate_bps` as §3.9 specifies. Publishing a lot therefore lowers a seller's gross recovery rate before any sale raises it again, and that is intentional: the metric measures what the seller recovered on everything it put on the market, not merely on what sold.

### 3.4. The Auction Floor: Live Bids, Hidden Ceilings and Anti-Sniping

Bidding is open only to a `Buyer` whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`, and only on a listing whose `listing_status` is `OPEN`. A live bid payload names the bidding account through `buyer_id` and states an `amount_cents`; the resulting `Bid` row records `bid_type` as `LIVE`, sets `ceiling_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, leaves `max_amount_cents` null, stamps `placed_at` with the current logical clock instant, and starts with `bid_status` `STANDING` before resolution assigns leadership. A proxy bid payload names `buyer_id`, states a hidden `max_amount_cents` ceiling, and carries the `expected_listing_version` required by §2.7; the resulting `Bid` records `bid_type` as `PROXY`, sets `ceiling_cents` equal to `max_amount_cents`, and sets `amount_cents` to the resolved public price at the moment of placement rather than to the ceiling — that is, to the `current_high_bid_cents` the listing publishes once the resolution described below has run with this new proxy already in the pool, whether or not the new proxy ends up leading; that figure is a snapshot of the placement and is never rewritten by later bids or by the closure. In both cases the bid's `meets_reserve` flag starts false and becomes a snapshot of the listing's `reserve_met` at the moment that bid becomes `WINNING`; a bid that never leads keeps `meets_reserve` false forever.

Before a bid is recorded, four business gates run in band order. At band 10, the bidder's `qualification_status` must be `QUALIFIED`; a `PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `SUSPENDED` buyer is refused with `ERR_BUYER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. At band 11 come the two safety rules. The **geographic restriction**: the bidder's `state_code` must not appear in the lot's brand policy's `prohibited_buyer_state_codes`, or the attempt is `ERR_BUYER_STATE_PROHIBITED`; this rule guards four operations — placing a live bid, placing a proxy bid, opening a negotiation, and countering within a negotiation — and it is applied once more at auction close against the prospective winner. The **anti-shill control**: the bidding buyer's `tax_id` must differ from the `tax_id` of the lot's selling account, so a seller cannot inflate its own lot through an affiliated buyer registration; a match is `ERR_SHILL_BIDDING_PROHIBITED`, and this rule guards three operations — placing a live bid, placing a proxy bid, and opening a negotiation. At band 12 the offering window must be open: the current logical clock must be at or after `window_start_at`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, and strictly before `effective_close_at`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_CLOSED`; this window test guards live bids, proxy bids and negotiation openings alike. At band 16 the amount must clear the floor and the credit ceiling must not be breached: a live auction bid's `amount_cents` must be at least the listing's published `min_next_bid_cents`, with the comparison inclusive, or the bid is refused with `ERR_BID_BELOW_MINIMUM`; a proxy bid's `max_amount_cents` must likewise be at least `min_next_bid_cents`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PROXY_MAX_BELOW_MINIMUM`; and the exposure rule of §3.2 must admit the new commitment.

**Resolving the public price.** Every accepted bid on an auction listing re-derives the listing's public state from the whole pool of bids whose `bid_status` is `STANDING` or `WINNING`; bids that are `SUPERSEDED`, `WON` or `LOST` take no part. The resolution proceeds in five steps. First, the pool is collapsed to one candidate per distinct `buyer_id`: when a buyer places a new bid, that buyer's earlier `STANDING` or `WINNING` bid on the same listing is set to `SUPERSEDED`, so the pool naturally holds at most one entry per buyer. Second, candidates are ranked by `ceiling_cents` descending, and where two different buyers hold equal ceilings the earlier `created_at` ranks ahead — creation time is the sole tie-break, and the row identifier is never consulted. The top-ranked candidate is the leader and the second-ranked, if one exists, is the runner-up. Third, the public price is set. With an empty pool, `current_high_bid_cents` and `current_high_bidder_id` are both null. With exactly one candidate, `current_high_bidder_id` becomes that candidate's `buyer_id`, and `current_high_bid_cents` becomes the greater of the listing's `opening_bid_cents` and — only when that sole candidate is a `LIVE` bid — its own `amount_cents`; a sole `PROXY` candidate produces a public price of exactly `opening_bid_cents`, which is what keeps a hidden ceiling hidden. With two or more candidates, `current_high_bidder_id` becomes the leader's `buyer_id` and `current_high_bid_cents` becomes the smaller of the leader's own `ceiling_cents` and the runner-up's `ceiling_cents` plus the listing's `bid_increment_cents` — the leader pays only enough to beat the runner-up by one increment, never its full ceiling. Fourth, statuses are settled: the leader's `bid_status` becomes `WINNING` and every other candidate's `bid_status` remains `STANDING`, and the leader's `meets_reserve` is snapshotted from the newly computed reserve test. Fifth, the listing republishes `min_next_bid_cents`: it equals `opening_bid_cents` while `current_high_bid_cents` is null, and otherwise equals `current_high_bid_cents` plus `bid_increment_cents`. The listing's `bid_count` counts this listing's bids whose `bid_status` is `STANDING`, `WINNING`, `WON` or `LOST`.

The reserve test itself is a small, sharp rule: `reserve_met` is true whenever `reserve_price_cents` is null; it is false whenever `current_high_bid_cents` is null; and otherwise it is true exactly when `current_high_bid_cents` is greater than or equal to `reserve_price_cents`. The comparison is inclusive at the reserve, so a high bid landing exactly on the reserve satisfies it while a high bid one cent short does not.

**Anti-sniping.** A bid arriving in the last moments of an auction pushes the close outward so that honest bidders retain a chance to respond. The rule applies only to a listing whose `listing_format` is `AUCTION`, and it fires on both live bids and proxy bids. Compute the whole seconds remaining as the interval from the current logical clock to `effective_close_at`. An extension is granted when four conditions hold together: the format is `AUCTION`; the seconds remaining are not negative; the seconds remaining are at most two minutes, with the boundary inclusive, so a bid landing exactly 120 seconds before the effective close does extend the auction while one landing 121 seconds before does not; and `extension_count` is strictly below three. When granted, `effective_close_at` moves out by five minutes **measured from its own current value, not from the time of the bid**, and `extension_count` increases by one. The scheduled `window_end_at` is never modified; only `effective_close_at` moves, which is why the listing exposes both. Once three extensions have been granted, a fourth qualifying bid leaves `effective_close_at` and `extension_count` untouched — the bid itself is still accepted and still resolves normally.

Every accepted live bid and proxy bid recomputes the bidding buyer's `outstanding_exposure_cents` and advances the listing's `version` by one. For a proxy bid the exposure is computed on the hidden ceiling, not on the public price the bid produces.

### 3.5. Three Ways a Lot Changes Hands

All three sale mechanisms funnel into a single `SaleOrder`, and the order records which mechanism produced it in `sale_source`, alongside the winning price in `hammer_price_cents`. The three values of `sale_source` are `AUCTION_CLOSE`, `FIXED_PRICE_PURCHASE` and `NEGOTIATED_ACCEPTANCE`, and each has its own path to that hammer price.

**Closing an auction.** A `MarketplaceOps` operator closes an `AUCTION` listing that is `OPEN`. The close is refused with `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_CLOSABLE` while the current logical clock is strictly before `effective_close_at` — including when the clock has passed `window_end_at` but an anti-sniping extension has moved the effective close beyond it. Once the clock has reached the effective close, the outcome is binary and there is no partial result. If the qualifying-bid pool is empty, or if `reserve_met` is false, the outcome is no sale: the listing advances `OPEN → CLOSED_NO_SALE`, every bid whose `bid_status` was `STANDING` or `WINNING` becomes `LOST`, no order is created, and the lot advances `LISTED → RELIST_PENDING` so it may be offered again. Otherwise the outcome is a sale: the listing advances `OPEN → CLOSED_SOLD`, `hammer_price_cents` is pinned to the listing's `current_high_bid_cents`, the single bid whose `bid_status` was `WINNING` becomes `WON` while every other qualifying bid becomes `LOST`, the lot advances `LISTED → SOLD`, and exactly one sale order is created with `sale_source` `AUCTION_CLOSE`. Either outcome recomputes the affected buyers' `outstanding_exposure_cents` — for every bidder on a no-sale, for the winning buyer on a sale — advances the listing's `version` by one, and drives the seller recovery cascade of §3.9. On a no-sale the lot remains in the gross recovery denominator while contributing nothing to the numerator, which is precisely why a failed auction depresses the seller's gross recovery rate.

**Buying at a fixed price.** On a listing whose `listing_format` is `FIXED_PRICE`, a `Buyer` commits by placing a live bid whose `amount_cents` equals the listing's `ask_price_cents` exactly — not at least, exactly. Any other amount is `ERR_FIXED_PRICE_MISMATCH`. The purchase is admitted only while the current logical clock is at or after `window_start_at` and strictly before `effective_close_at`, and only when the same qualification, geographic, anti-shill and credit gates of §3.4 are satisfied. An accepted purchase creates a `Bid` with `bid_type` `LIVE` whose `bid_status` is `WON` immediately, advances the listing `OPEN → CLOSED_SOLD`, advances the lot `LISTED → SOLD`, and creates one sale order with `sale_source` `FIXED_PRICE_PURCHASE` and `hammer_price_cents` equal to `ask_price_cents`. No proxy resolution, no reserve test and no anti-sniping extension apply to a fixed-price listing.

**Negotiating terms.** On a listing whose `listing_format` is `NEGOTIATED` and whose `listing_status` is `OPEN`, a `Buyer` opens a negotiation by naming its `buyer_id` and stating an `opening_offer_cents`. That opening amount must be at least the listing's `min_offer_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OFFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`; the same floor applies to every subsequent counter-offer, and both operations report the same error. A buyer may hold only one live negotiation per listing: if that buyer already has a negotiation on this listing whose `negotiation_status` is `AWAITING_SELLER` or `AWAITING_BUYER`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEGOTIATION`. An accepted opening creates a `Negotiation` in `AWAITING_SELLER` with `round_number` at 1, `current_offer_cents` set to the opening amount, `current_offer_side` set to `BUYER`, `current_offer_expires_at` set to the current logical clock plus 48 hours, and `accepted_price_cents` still null. It simultaneously creates the round-1 `Offer`, which names its parent through `negotiation_id` and records `offer_side` `BUYER`, `amount_cents` equal to the opening amount, `round_number` 1, and an `expires_at` equal to the same clock-plus-48-hours instant. An offer's `offer_side` is exactly one of two values, `BUYER` when the offer was placed by the negotiation's buyer and `SELLER` when it was placed by the lot's seller, and the negotiation's `current_offer_side` mirrors the `offer_side` of the offer holding the highest `round_number`. Offers are append-only: an `Offer` row is never mutated after creation, which is why it carries no `version`. Opening a negotiation also recomputes the buyer's `outstanding_exposure_cents`.

The ladder then alternates. A counter-offer states an `amount_cents` and the `expected_negotiation_version` of §2.7, and the acting side is inferred from `X-User-Role`: `SellerOps` counters from `AWAITING_SELLER` and `Buyer` counters from `AWAITING_BUYER`. Four conditions govern a counter. The negotiation must be in `AWAITING_SELLER` or `AWAITING_BUYER` — a negotiation already `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED` yields `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STATE_INVALID` — and the acting side must be the side the negotiation is awaiting, or the attempt is `ERR_NOT_YOUR_TURN`. When the acting role is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` must additionally equal the negotiation's `buyer_id`. The current logical clock must be strictly before `current_offer_expires_at`; an action attempted at exactly the expiry instant is too late and is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. And the round cap must hold: the round the counter would create is the current `round_number` plus one, and that value must be no greater than four, or the counter is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` without creating any `Offer` and without changing `round_number`. Since the buyer's opening offer is itself round 1, at most three counter-offers can ever occur on a negotiation.

An accepted counter appends the next `Offer` with `offer_side` matching the acting side and `round_number` one higher than the previous round, then updates the negotiation: `round_number` to the new round, `current_offer_cents` to the countered amount, `current_offer_side` to the countering side, and `current_offer_expires_at` to the current logical clock plus 48 hours — each counter restarts the 48-hour clock rather than inheriting the previous deadline. The state flips `AWAITING_SELLER → AWAITING_BUYER` when the seller countered and `AWAITING_BUYER → AWAITING_SELLER` when the buyer countered, and the negotiation's `version` advances by one. Countering also recomputes the countering party's exposure where that party is the buyer.

Acceptance ends the ladder. Only the awaited side may accept — `SellerOps` when the negotiation is `AWAITING_SELLER`, `Buyer` when it is `AWAITING_BUYER` — which is to say the accepting side is never the side that made the standing offer, and an acceptance by the wrong side is `ERR_NOT_YOUR_TURN`. The clock must still be strictly before `current_offer_expires_at`, or the acceptance is `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. An accepted negotiation advances `AWAITING_SELLER → ACCEPTED` or `AWAITING_BUYER → ACCEPTED`, freezes `accepted_price_cents` at whatever `current_offer_cents` held at that instant, and advances the negotiation's `version` by one. The cascade is broad: the listing advances `OPEN → CLOSED_SOLD`, the lot advances `LISTED → SOLD`, one sale order is created with `sale_source` `NEGOTIATED_ACCEPTANCE` and `hammer_price_cents` equal to `accepted_price_cents`, the seller recovery aggregates and commission tier are recomputed, and **every other negotiation on the same listing whose `negotiation_status` was `AWAITING_SELLER` or `AWAITING_BUYER` is advanced to `DECLINED`**, each such sibling having its own `version` advanced by one. Sibling negotiations already `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED` are left untouched — only live siblings are declined.

### 3.6. The Money Waterfall: Premium, Freight, Payment and Late Fees

A sale order records the listing it came from through `listing_id` and the winning account through `buyer_id`, and it carries **two independent status dimensions**. `payment_status` tracks money and moves through `UNPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID` and `REFUND_ISSUED`. `logistics_status` tracks freight and moves through `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT`, `APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP` and `ABANDONED`. Neither dimension's transitions constrain the other's in general; a new order begins at `UNPAID` and `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT` simultaneously and the two evolve separately. Exactly two cross-dimension guards exist, and both are one-directional: an appointment may only be scheduled while `payment_status` is `PAID`, and a payment may not be accepted while `logistics_status` is `ABANDONED`. The pickup-deadline sweep, by contrast, abandons a qualifying order regardless of its `payment_status`.

**The buyer premium** is a tiered percentage of the hammer price, and the table is flat rather than marginal — the tier selected by the whole hammer price applies to the whole hammer price. A `hammer_price_cents` of at most 1,000,000 cents selects a `buyer_premium_bps` of 1,400 basis points, that is 14 percent. A hammer price above 1,000,000 cents and at most 2,500,000 cents selects 1,200 basis points, 12 percent. A hammer price above 2,500,000 cents selects 1,000 basis points, 10 percent. Both boundaries are inclusive at the top of their tier, so a hammer price of exactly 1,000,000 cents is charged 14 percent while a hammer price one cent higher is charged 12 percent **on the entire amount**, not merely on the excess. `buyer_premium_cents` is then the hammer price multiplied by the selected rate, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The selected `buyer_premium_bps` is snapshotted onto the order at creation and is never re-selected afterwards, so a later claim credit or freight correction never reprices the premium.

**Freight** is billed per whole pallet. `pallet_count` is the lot's `declared_unit_count` divided by 200 units per pallet and rounded **up** to a whole pallet, so 400 units occupy 2 pallets while 401 units occupy 3, and 445 units also occupy 3. `estimated_freight_cents` is that pallet count multiplied by 8,500 cents per pallet. Both are computed once, at order creation, from the lot's declared unit count as it stood at that instant.

**The two settlement variants.** An order's `settlement_basis` names which variant of the money waterfall is currently in force. It begins as `PROVISIONAL` at order creation, whichever of the three sale mechanisms produced the order, and it uses `estimated_freight_cents` as the freight term. It becomes `FINAL` when the order's pickup appointment is completed, at which point the freight term becomes the carrier-invoiced `actual_freight_cents` supplied at completion. The hammer and premium terms are identical across the two variants, so the entire difference between a provisional and a final settlement is the difference between estimated and actual freight. Consider an order with a hammer price of 2,700,000 cents on a 400-unit lot: the premium is 270,000 cents, estimated freight is 17,000 cents, and if the carrier ultimately invoices 19,000 cents the final settlement is exactly 2,000 cents higher than the provisional one.

The waterfall itself runs in a fixed order. First, `pre_fee_total_cents` is `hammer_price_cents` plus `buyer_premium_cents` plus the freight term of the active `settlement_basis`. Second, `late_fee_cents` is derived — from `pre_fee_total_cents`, never from `total_due_cents`. It is zero for an order paid in full at or before `payment_due_at`, and once the late-fee sweep observes a clock strictly past `payment_due_at` with a balance still outstanding it becomes `pre_fee_total_cents` multiplied by 150 basis points, that is 1.5 percent, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. Deriving the fee from the pre-fee total rather than from the amount due has two deliberate consequences: the fee is never charged on itself, and an approved claim credit never shrinks it. Third, `total_due_cents` is `pre_fee_total_cents` plus `late_fee_cents` minus `claim_credit_cents`. Fourth, `balance_due_cents` is `total_due_cents` minus `amount_paid_cents`, **clamped at zero** — it never goes negative. That clamp matters when a claim is approved against an already fully paid order: the amount due falls below the amount paid, and the buyer is made whole through `wallet_credit_cents` rather than through a negative balance. Finally, two deadlines are fixed from the order's `created_at`: `payment_due_at` is three days after creation and `pickup_deadline_at` is ten days after creation.

The late fee is assessed **at most once per order** and is frozen once assessed. Switching from `PROVISIONAL` to `FINAL` recomputes `pre_fee_total_cents`, `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, but it does not re-derive a late fee that has already been recorded. Concretely, an order whose provisional pre-fee total is 2,987,000 cents and which sits unpaid past its payment due date is assessed 44,805 cents, and that figure survives the switch to final settlement unchanged even though the final pre-fee total is larger.

**Paying.** A `Buyer` pays against the balance by supplying an `amount_cents`. The amount must be at least 1 cent and at most the order's current `balance_due_cents`; an amount outside that inclusive range — including any attempt to overpay — is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Payments are accepted only while `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; an order already `PAID` or `REFUND_ISSUED` refuses further payment with `ERR_ORDER_PAYMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Payment is also refused when `logistics_status` is `ABANDONED`, with `ERR_PAYMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` — once the pickup-deadline sweep has written off an order it can no longer be paid. And `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `buyer_id`.

An accepted payment increases `amount_paid_cents` by the paid amount and recomputes `balance_due_cents`. A payment smaller than the outstanding balance advances `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID`; a payment exactly equal to the outstanding balance advances `UNPAID → PAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`, drives `balance_due_cents` to zero and stamps `paid_at` with the logical clock instant at which the balance first reached zero. When a payment lands after `payment_due_at`, any late fee already recorded in `late_fee_cents` remains part of `total_due_cents` and must be covered — settling late does not retire the fee. Every accepted payment recomputes the buyer's `outstanding_exposure_cents` and advances the order's `version` by one.

**The late-fee sweep** is a `MarketplaceOps` capability that takes no payload and returns the orders it changed. It selects every order whose `balance_due_cents` is greater than zero, whose `late_fee_cents` is still zero, and whose `payment_due_at` is strictly before the current logical clock; for each, it computes the fee from `pre_fee_total_cents` as described above, then recomputes `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents`, and finally recomputes the affected buyers' `outstanding_exposure_cents`. Orders already carrying a non-zero late fee are skipped, which is what makes the sweep safe to run repeatedly.

### 3.7. Freight: Dock Windows, Doors, Completion and Abandonment

Once an order is fully paid the buyer arranges collection. Booking a dock window names the origin `warehouse_id`, the collecting `carrier_id`, the requested `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, and the `bol_number` bill-of-lading reference the carrier will present. The resulting `PickupAppointment` records its parent order through `sale_order_id`, begins with `appointment_status` `SCHEDULED`, `completed_at` null and `picked_up_unit_count` null, and carries the `dock_door_number` the platform assigns.

The gate runs in band order, and the sequence is worth stating explicitly because a bad booking often fails several checks. At band 9, the order's `logistics_status` must be `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT`, or the request is `ERR_ORDER_LOGISTICS_STATE_INVALID`. At band 10, the order's `payment_status` must be `PAID`, or the request is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PAID` — this is the one direction in which the freight dimension is gated on the money dimension. At band 12, the requested `window_end_at` must fall at or before the order's `pickup_deadline_at`, or the request is `ERR_PICKUP_DEADLINE_PASSED`; this deadline test runs **before** the door selector, so a request that could never be honoured in time is rejected on time rather than on capacity. At band 13 the door selector runs, and at band 14 the carrier's daily cap is checked. Only then, at band 15, is the requested `warehouse_id` compared against the lot's own `warehouse_id`: goods can only be collected from the dock they sit on, and a mismatch is `ERR_WAREHOUSE_MISMATCH`. That ordering means a request naming the wrong warehouse *and* colliding with an existing booking there reports the capacity failure first.

**Selecting a dock door.** A warehouse's doors are numbered from 1 through its `dock_door_count`, and each door must sit idle for the warehouse's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` between consecutive appointments. The occupation interval a booking claims is the requested window itself, treated as inclusive at `window_start_at` and exclusive at `window_end_at`, and it is padded by the buffer on both sides for the purpose of detecting collisions. Formally, an existing appointment conflicts with the request when both of the following hold: the requested `window_start_at` falls strictly before the existing appointment's `window_end_at` advanced by the buffer, and the existing appointment's `window_start_at` falls strictly before the requested `window_end_at` advanced by the buffer. Only appointments at the same warehouse whose `appointment_status` is `SCHEDULED` participate; `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` appointments release their door immediately and are ignored. The eligible doors are those numbers, from 1 through `dock_door_count`, on which no such conflict exists, and the platform assigns the **lowest-numbered** eligible door; no further tie-break is needed because door numbers are unique within a warehouse. When no door is eligible the booking is rejected with `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` and no appointment is created. To make the buffer arithmetic concrete: with a single door and a 15-minute buffer, an appointment already occupying 14:00 to 15:00 blocks a request starting at 15:00, because 15:00 falls strictly before 15:15, while a request starting at 15:15 is accepted because 15:15 does not fall strictly before 15:15. Cancelling the earlier appointment frees the interval immediately and the 15:00 request then succeeds.

**The carrier's daily cap** is an aggregate limit checked only after a door has been found. Count the appointments naming the requested carrier whose `appointment_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `window_start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the request; if that count plus one would exceed the carrier's `daily_pickup_capacity`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_CARRIER_DAY_CAPACITY_FULL`. The counter spans every warehouse — a carrier with a capacity of one appointment per day may take one booking on a given UTC date anywhere in the network and no more, but is free again on the next UTC date.

An accepted booking advances the order `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT → APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED` and advances the order's `version` by one. The new appointment is discoverable by listing pickup appointments filtered on the order.

**Completing the pickup.** A `MarketplaceOps` operator closes out the collection by supplying the carrier's invoiced `actual_freight_cents`, which must not be negative, together with the `picked_up_unit_count` physically loaded. The appointment must be `SCHEDULED`, or the request is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and the current logical clock must be at or after the appointment's `window_start_at`, or the request is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_STARTED`. Completion is the single busiest write in the system. The appointment advances `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped at the current logical clock and `picked_up_unit_count` recorded. The order advances `APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED → PICKED_UP`, stamps `picked_up_at`, receives the supplied `actual_freight_cents`, and sets `claim_window_closes_at` to five days after the completion instant — the window inside which the buyer may dispute the manifest. The order's `settlement_basis` flips `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`, which re-derives `pre_fee_total_cents`, `total_due_cents` and `balance_due_cents` on the actual freight while leaving any already-assessed `late_fee_cents` alone. The seller payout is computed and frozen in the same request, filling `seller_commission_rate_bps`, `seller_commission_cents`, `seller_debranding_fee_cents` and `seller_net_proceeds_cents` as §3.9 specifies, and the seller's `net_proceeds_cents` and `net_recovery_rate` are recomputed from there. The order's `version` advances by one.

**Cancelling an appointment.** A scheduled appointment may be cancelled, optionally with a `cancellation_reason`, by the owning `Buyer` or by `MarketplaceOps`. The appointment must be `SCHEDULED`. On the buyer-initiated path, `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `buyer_id` and the current logical clock must be strictly before the appointment's `window_start_at` — a buyer cannot cancel a window that has already opened. Cancellation advances the appointment `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, frees its dock-door interval so that exactly the same window becomes assignable again, and returns the order `APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED → AWAITING_APPOINTMENT`, leaving the order with no appointment in `SCHEDULED` and advancing the order's `version` by one.

**Abandonment.** The pickup-deadline sweep is a `MarketplaceOps` capability that takes no payload and returns the orders it changed. It selects every order whose `logistics_status` is `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT` or `APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED` and whose `pickup_deadline_at` is strictly before the current logical clock — an order sitting exactly on its deadline is not yet abandoned — and it does so regardless of `payment_status`. Each selected order advances `AWAITING_APPOINTMENT → ABANDONED` or `APPOINTMENT_SCHEDULED → ABANDONED` and has `abandoned_at` stamped. Where the order held an appointment in `SCHEDULED`, that appointment advances `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and frees its dock interval; where it held none, nothing is cancelled. The lot returns `SOLD → RELIST_PENDING` so the seller can offer it again, and the seller aggregates are recomputed: an abandoned order leaves the gross hammer numerator entirely, so `gross_hammer_cents` and `sold_retail_value_cents` fall, which recomputes `gross_recovery_rate`, which reassigns `commission_tier` and `commission_rate_bps`. The affected buyers' `outstanding_exposure_cents` are recomputed too, since an abandoned order leaves the exposure base. Orders already `PICKED_UP` or `ABANDONED` are skipped, which makes the sweep idempotent across repeated runs at the same clock.

### 3.8. Manifest Discrepancy Claims: Evidence, Refunds and Reversal

A buyer who opens the pallets and finds the manifest wrong files a claim. Filing names the disputed line through `manifest_line_id`, categorises the discrepancy through `claim_type` — `UNIT_SHORTAGE`, `CONDITION_MISGRADE` or `MISSING_SKU` — states the `claimed_units` in dispute, which must be at least one unit, and may attach an `evidence_urls` array of supporting photographs or documents. The claim records its parent order through `sale_order_id`, stamps `filed_at` with the current logical clock, sets `evidence_item_count` to the number of entries supplied in `evidence_urls`, sets `refund_cents` to zero, `verified_units` to null, `evidence_request_count` to zero, `decided_by_user_id` and `reversed_by_user_id` to null, and enters `claim_status` `SUBMITTED`. When the filing already carries at least two evidence items, the claim advances `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` in the same request, because it is immediately decidable.

Filing is gated as follows. At band 10, the order's `logistics_status` must be `PICKED_UP` — a buyer cannot dispute a manifest before taking possession — reported as `ERR_ORDER_LOGISTICS_STATE_INVALID`; and the referenced line's `line_status` must be `ACTIVE`, since a voided line was never sold, reported as `ERR_LINE_VOIDED`. At band 12, the current logical clock must be at or before the order's `claim_window_closes_at`, with the boundary inclusive, or the filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. At band 15, `claimed_units` must not exceed the line's own `unit_count`, or the filing is `ERR_CLAIMED_UNITS_EXCEED_LINE`, and the line's `lot_id` must identify the very lot behind the order's listing, or the filing is `ERR_LINE_NOT_ON_ORDER_LOT`.

**The evidence loop.** A buyer adds evidence one item at a time by supplying a single `evidence_url`, which increments `evidence_item_count` by one and advances the claim's `version` by one. Adding evidence is legal only while the claim is `SUBMITTED` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`; any other state is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and only the buyer of the claim's order may add it. From `SUBMITTED`, once `evidence_item_count` reaches at least two the claim advances `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; from `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, any evidence submission returns the claim `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, closing the review loop.

**Deciding.** A `MarketplaceOps` reviewer supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `PARTIALLY_APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_EVIDENCE`, together with `verified_units` where the decision requires it and an optional `decision_note`. The very first thing checked is the segregation rule of §2.1 and band 5: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `manifest_reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on the lot owning the claim's manifest line, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — checked ahead of the claim's own state, ahead of the evidence counters and ahead of the units arithmetic, so a disqualified reviewer never learns anything about the claim's condition. At band 9 the claim must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the decision is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. At band 15 the counters and the arithmetic are checked: a `REQUEST_EVIDENCE` decision requires `evidence_request_count` to be strictly below two, or it is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUEST_LIMIT`; every other decision requires `evidence_item_count` to be at least two, or it is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`; and `verified_units` must be consistent with the decision — exactly equal to `claimed_units` for `APPROVE`, strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `claimed_units` for `PARTIALLY_APPROVE`, and exactly zero for `DENY` — with any inconsistency reported as `ERR_VERIFIED_UNITS_MISMATCH`.

A `REQUEST_EVIDENCE` decision advances the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` and increments `evidence_request_count` by one; no money moves. Because at most two evidence requests are permitted per claim, at most two such round trips can occur before a substantive decision must be made. A `DENY` decision advances `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets `refund_cents` to zero and `verified_units` to zero, records `decided_by_user_id`, and touches no order, buyer or seller aggregate whatsoever. An `APPROVE` decision advances `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and a `PARTIALLY_APPROVE` decision advances `UNDER_REVIEW → PARTIALLY_APPROVED`; the two are otherwise identical in every side effect, the only difference being that a partial approval computes the refund on the smaller confirmed count. Both record `decided_by_user_id` and the confirmed `verified_units`, and both advance the claim's `version` by one.

**Pricing the shortfall.** A refund reimburses the buyer at the per-unit price the buyer effectively paid for that specific manifest line, plus the buyer premium that was charged on that share. Freight is never refunded, because the freight was incurred regardless of what was inside the pallets. The calculation proceeds in exactly this order, and each rounding step is normative. First, the line's extended value is its `unit_count` multiplied by its `unit_retail_cents`. Second, the line's share of the sale is the order's `hammer_price_cents` multiplied by that extended value, divided by the lot's `declared_retail_value_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Third, the per-unit recovery is that rounded line share divided by the line's `unit_count`, again rounded half up to a whole cent. Fourth, the base refund is that per-unit recovery multiplied by `verified_units` — a plain multiplication of an already-rounded figure, with no further rounding. Fifth, the premium portion of the refund is the base refund multiplied by the order's snapshotted `buyer_premium_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. Finally, `refund_cents` is the base refund plus that premium portion. Worked through on a lot whose `declared_retail_value_cents` is 10,000,000 with a line of 100 units at 40,000 cents each, an order with a hammer price of 2,700,000 cents and a premium rate of 1,000 basis points: the line share is 1,080,000 cents, the per-unit recovery is 10,800 cents, 80 confirmed units give a base refund of 864,000 cents, the premium portion is 86,400 cents, and `refund_cents` is 950,400 cents. A denial leaves `refund_cents` at zero and touches no ledger.

**The ledger effect.** An approval or partial approval increases the order's `claim_credit_cents` by the computed `refund_cents`, which lowers `total_due_cents` and recomputes `balance_due_cents` under the zero clamp of §3.6, and advances the order `PAID → REFUND_ISSUED`. It increases the buyer's `wallet_credit_cents` by the same amount, and — this is the crucial asymmetry — it does **not** reduce `amount_paid_cents`, because the cash genuinely changed hands. The net cash the buyer is out of pocket, being `amount_paid_cents` less `wallet_credit_cents`, therefore falls by exactly the refund and by nothing else. The decision also writes the line's `verified_unit_count` from the most recent non-reversed decision touching that line — that is, from the `verified_units` of the most recently decided claim against that line whose `claim_status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, and back to null when no such claim remains — then recomputes the lot's accuracy score, then the seller's average, as described next. A claim reversal re-derives all three the same way, which is why reversing the only claim ever resolved against a line leaves that line's `verified_unit_count` null again.

**Scoring the manifest.** A lot's `manifest_accuracy_score` stays null until the first claim against it is resolved, and is recomputed on every claim decision and every claim reversal thereafter; the score is null exactly while no claim against any of that lot's manifest lines stands `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, so a reversal that empties that population returns the score to null rather than to a recomputed 1.000. Each condition grade carries a shortfall tolerance expressed as a fraction of the declared unit count: a lot graded `NEW_OVERSTOCK` tolerates 1 percent, a lot graded `CUSTOMER_RETURNS` tolerates 3 percent, and a lot graded `SALVAGE` tolerates 8 percent. The allowance in units is the lot's `declared_unit_count` multiplied by that fraction and rounded **down** to a whole unit, so a 400-unit `CUSTOMER_RETURNS` lot absorbs exactly 12 units of shortfall for free while the same 400 units graded `NEW_OVERSTOCK` absorb only 4 and graded `SALVAGE` absorb 32. The total verified shortfall is the sum of `verified_units` over every claim against a manifest line of this lot whose `claim_status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`; claims that are `REVERSED`, `DENIED`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` contribute nothing. The excess is that total less the allowance, floored at zero. The score is then one minus the excess divided by `declared_unit_count`, rounded half up to three decimal places and clamped to the range from 0.000 through 1.000. On that 400-unit `CUSTOMER_RETURNS` lot, 80 verified units give an excess of 68 and a score of 0.830; 72 verified units give an excess of 60 and a score of exactly 0.850; and any shortfall inside the 12-unit allowance leaves the score at 1.000.

**Reversal.** A `TrustSafety` operator may reverse an approved or partially approved claim, supplying a mandatory `reversal_reason`. The claim's `claim_status` must be `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, or the request is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and the order's `payment_status` must be `REFUND_ISSUED`. Band 5 applies here too: the reversing operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `decided_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A reversal advances the claim `APPROVED → REVERSED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED → REVERSED`, records `reversed_by_user_id`, and advances the claim's `version` by one. It is a complete compensation of the decision: the order's `claim_credit_cents` and the buyer's `wallet_credit_cents` each fall by the reversed `refund_cents`, and when `claim_credit_cents` returns to zero the order returns `REFUND_ISSUED → PAID`; the buyer's net cash position returns to exactly its pre-claim value with no residual drift. The reversed claim leaves the scoring population, so the lot's `manifest_accuracy_score` returns to its pre-claim value, which restores the seller's `manifest_accuracy_avg` and `scored_lot_count`, which may return the seller's `account_status` to `ACTIVE` and reassign its `commission_tier`.

### 3.9. Seller Standing, Commission Tiers, Payouts and Recovery Analytics

A seller's standing is a rolling judgement on the accuracy of its manifests. Whenever a claim decision or a claim reversal changes any of a seller's lot scores, the seller's `manifest_accuracy_avg` is recomputed as the arithmetic mean of `manifest_accuracy_score` across that seller's lots whose score is not null, with the mean taken over the already-rounded per-lot scores and then rounded half up once to three decimal places. `scored_lot_count` is the number of lots in that population. A lot that has never been claimed against has no score and is excluded from the population entirely rather than being counted as a perfect 1.000, and when `scored_lot_count` is zero the average reads 1.000 by definition.

Probation follows from that average, in both directions, and the comparison is strict. When `manifest_accuracy_avg` falls strictly below 0.850, the seller advances `ACTIVE → MANIFEST_PROBATION`; an average landing exactly on 0.850 keeps the seller `ACTIVE`. Probation has two consequences: `commission_tier` is forced to `TIER_PROBATION` with `commission_rate_bps` of 2,500 basis points, overriding whatever the recovery-based tier would otherwise have been, and every publication attempt on any of that seller's lots is refused with `ERR_SELLER_NOT_ACTIVE`. When a reversal restores the lot scores and the recomputed average is at least 0.850, the seller returns `MANIFEST_PROBATION → ACTIVE` and the commission tier is reassigned from the recovery rate as if probation had never applied. A seller with a single scored lot at 0.830 is on probation; a seller with two scored lots at 0.830 and 1.000 has an average of 0.915 and remains `ACTIVE`, because one bad lot is diluted by a good one.

**Commission tiers** are otherwise assigned from the gross recovery rate, with probation as an absolute override. If `account_status` is `MANIFEST_PROBATION`, the tier is `TIER_PROBATION` at 2,500 basis points. Otherwise, if `gross_recovery_rate` is greater than or equal to 0.2500 the tier is `TIER_PREFERRED` at 1,500 basis points — the comparison is inclusive, so a rate of exactly 0.2500 earns the preferred tier while 0.2499 does not — and in every remaining case the tier is `TIER_STANDARD` at 2,000 basis points. A freshly registered seller starts `ACTIVE` on `TIER_STANDARD` at 2,000 basis points with every recovery aggregate at zero and `manifest_accuracy_avg` at 1.000. Reassignment is triggered by listing publication, auction close, fixed-price purchase, negotiated acceptance, the pickup-deadline sweep, claim decisions and claim reversals — that is, by every event that can move either the recovery rate or the accuracy average.

**The seller payout**, computed and frozen at pickup completion, is deliberately charged per manifest line rather than on the hammer price as a whole. First, the rate in force is read from the seller's `commission_rate_bps` at that instant — after the same request's recovery recomputation, and then frozen into the order's `seller_commission_rate_bps`, so a later tier change never reprices an already settled order. Then, walking the lot's manifest lines whose `line_status` is `ACTIVE` in `created_at` ascending order — the ordering is fixed so that the summation is reproducible, and the row identifier is never used for ordering — each line yields its extended value as `unit_count` multiplied by `unit_retail_cents`; its share of the sale as the order's `hammer_price_cents` multiplied by that extended value, divided by the lot's `declared_retail_value_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent; and its commission as that rounded share multiplied by the frozen rate, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `seller_commission_cents` is the sum of those per-line commissions — **rounded per line and only then summed**. This order is normative and must not be shortcut: on a lot whose three line shares are 704,283, 1,526,176 and 469,541 cents at a rate of 1,500 basis points, the per-line commissions of 105,642, 228,926 and 70,431 cents sum to 404,999 cents, whereas applying the rate directly to the 2,700,000-cent hammer price would yield 405,000 cents — a one-cent divergence that the per-line order resolves in the seller's favour.

`seller_debranding_fee_cents` is the lot's `declared_unit_count` multiplied by the brand policy's `debranding_fee_cents_per_unit` whenever the lot's `de_branding_required` is true, and zero otherwise. `seller_net_proceeds_cents` is then the order's `hammer_price_cents` less `seller_commission_cents` less `seller_debranding_fee_cents`. All four of these fields stay null until the order's `settlement_basis` becomes `FINAL`. Note what is absent: an approved claim never reduces `seller_net_proceeds_cents`. Claim refunds are funded out of the buyer premium the platform retained, and the seller's exposure to manifest inaccuracy runs entirely through probation and the commission tier instead.

**Recovery analytics** are dual-variant on the same underlying sales. The **gross** basis measures what the seller recovered against everything it put on the market: `gross_hammer_cents` is the sum of `hammer_price_cents` over that seller's orders whose `logistics_status` is not `ABANDONED`, `listed_retail_value_cents` is the sum of `declared_retail_value_cents` over that seller's lots whose `lot_status` is `LISTED`, `SOLD` or `RELIST_PENDING`, and `gross_recovery_rate` is the first divided by the second, rounded half up once to four decimal places, or exactly 0.0000 when the denominator is zero. The **net** basis measures post-commission payouts against what actually sold: `net_proceeds_cents` is the sum of `seller_net_proceeds_cents` over that seller's orders whose `settlement_basis` is `FINAL`, `sold_retail_value_cents` is the sum of `declared_retail_value_cents` over that seller's lots whose `lot_status` is `SOLD`, and `net_recovery_rate` is the first divided by the second, rounded half up once to four decimal places, or exactly 0.0000 when the denominator is zero.

The two variants move differently when a lot fails to sell, and that asymmetry is the point of carrying both. A published lot that closes with no bids stays in the gross denominator while contributing nothing to the gross numerator, so the gross rate falls; the same lot never entered either net term, so the net rate is untouched. Because a seller's commission rate is read and frozen at each order's pickup completion, the pipeline is order-sensitive: a seller whose second lot fails *after* the first lot is picked up keeps the preferred 1,500-basis-point rate on that first order, while a seller whose second lot fails *before* the first lot is picked up pays the standard 2,000 basis points on it. Both outcomes are correct; the sequence of events determines which one occurs.

A computed analytics read serves these figures for one seller without persisting anything. The caller names the `seller_id` and may supply a `group_by` of `NONE`, `CATEGORY` or `CHANNEL`, defaulting to `NONE`, along with optional `category_code` and `resale_channel` filters that narrow the lot population before either variant is computed. The response reports `seller_id`, the effective `group_by`, and both variants at the top level as `gross_hammer_cents`, `listed_retail_value_cents`, `gross_recovery_rate`, `net_proceeds_cents`, `sold_retail_value_cents` and `net_recovery_rate`, plus a `groups` array. With `group_by` of `NONE` the `groups` array is empty. With `CATEGORY` the lot population is grouped by each lot's `category_code`, and with `CHANNEL` it is grouped by the `resale_channel` of the listing that produced each order; in either case each entry carries a `key` naming the group and the same six aggregate figures recomputed within that group, and a group whose denominator is zero reports 0.0000 rather than being omitted. A `SellerOps` caller may only name its own `seller_id`.
