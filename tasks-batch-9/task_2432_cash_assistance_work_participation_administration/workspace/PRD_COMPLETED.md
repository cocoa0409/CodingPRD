# Cash Assistance & Work Participation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

State temporary assistance programs sit at the intersection of three pressures that pull in different directions. Families need a cash grant computed quickly and correctly from whatever income and property they actually have. Federal law caps how many months of assistance a family may receive over a lifetime, requires that adults participate in a narrow list of allowable work activities for a specified number of hours per week, and grades the state every quarter on the share of its caseload that met those hours. County staff, meanwhile, must impose graduated sanctions on families who do not participate, but only after a conciliation period, only when no good cause exists, and only through a supervisor who did not prepare the underlying determination. This API is the system of record that reconciles all three.

The service administers the complete arc of a case: an application is filed for a household, a caseworker records income sources and resource items and verifies them, a determination computes countable income, countable resources and a grant amount, and a supervisor either approves the application into an ongoing assistance case, denies it, or offers a one-time diversion lump sum in lieu of ongoing benefits. Once a case exists, each month of assistance issued writes a row into a time-limit ledger that feeds two separate clocks — a 24-month state clock and a 60-month federal clock — which deliberately diverge when a hardship extension is granted. Work activities are assigned against the case, participation hours are reported and verified with a credit factor that depends on the documentation type, and a shortfall in credited core hours opens a compliance episode that walks a conciliation-then-sanction ladder with escalating grant reductions. Supportive service money for transportation, child care and work expenses is drawn against per-service-type and per-case fiscal-year caps. Money paid in error is established as an overpayment claim and recovered through a signed ledger that supports reversals and fraud-driven re-sizing.

Two engineering properties matter more than anything else in this domain. First, **quantities are conserved**: the number of issued countable months, the funding headroom under each supportive service cap, and the outstanding balance of an overpayment claim must all reconcile exactly after every compensating action — after an extension is revoked, after an authorization is voided, after a recoupment is reversed. Second, **evaluation order is pinned**: the grant formula, the income-budgeting pipelines, the participation predicate and the quarterly rate calculation each specify a step sequence, and executing the steps in a different order produces different integers even though every individual step looks commutative. Implementations that treat either property as an implementation detail will produce numbers that are close but wrong, which in this domain means federal penalties.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock capability described in §2.5 must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the supplied role is not among the roles listed for the target endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `Applicant` — the head of an assistance unit. May read its own household and application record, file and withdraw an application, and accept or decline a diversion offer.
- `Caseworker` — county intake and maintenance worker. Registers households, records income sources and resource items, verifies income, runs the determination, issues monthly assistance, and requests hardship extensions. May not dispose of a determination it produced.
- `EligibilitySupervisor` — disposes of determinations and adverse actions: approves, denies, offers diversion, grants, denies and revokes extensions, grants good cause, and imposes sanctions.
- `WorkProgramSpecialist` — manages the work-requirement side: assigns and terminates activities, files and verifies participation, opens compliance episodes, runs conciliation and cures, and requests, authorizes, releases and voids supportive services.
- `FiscalOfficer` — owns the money ledgers: drafts and establishes overpayments, posts recovery and reversal entries, writes off uncollectible claims, and voids supportive service authorizations. May not write off a claim it established.
- `FraudInvestigator` — handles suspected client fraud: drafts and establishes claims, refers them for investigation, and records the disposition that re-sizes recoupment. Has no authority over benefit determination or work assignments.
- `StateReportingAnalyst` — prepares and certifies the federal work participation report. Opens quarters, runs the rate and caseload-reduction-credit calculation, and certifies a quarter calculated by a different analyst.

Identity binding differs by role. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Household` compare the resource's household foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, directly for household-scoped endpoints and transitively through the owning application's `household_id` for application-scoped and diversion-scoped endpoints. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_HOUSEHOLD_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That check guards exactly seven operations: reading one household, listing a household's applications, filing an application, reading one application, withdrawing an application, accepting a diversion grant, and declining a diversion grant.

When `X-User-Role` is `Caseworker`, `EligibilitySupervisor`, `WorkProgramSpecialist`, `FiscalOfficer`, `FraudInvestigator` or `StateReportingAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on the resources whose transitions capture an actor — `determined_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an application, `decided_by_user_id` on a hardship extension, `established_by_user_id` on an overpayment claim, and `calculated_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a federal reporting period — but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier recorded by the paired earlier step. Approving or denying an application requires an acting user different from the application's `determined_by_user_id`. Writing off an overpayment claim requires an acting user different from that claim's `established_by_user_id`. Certifying a reporting period requires an acting user different from that period's `calculated_by_user_id`. All four of those operations reject a same-user attempt with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The comparison is only made when the paired earlier step actually recorded an identifier: while that field is still null the check passes silently and evaluation falls through to the state rules beneath it, so approving a still-`RECEIVED` application — one that has no `determined_by_user_id` yet — yields `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` and never a segregation error.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as is either parameter supplied as anything other than a base-ten integer literal; an empty string for either parameter is treated as absent and falls back to its default.

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, rows are returned sorted by their creation timestamp in ascending order. The filter query parameters named for individual endpoints below are the one part of the query string that is *not* schema-checked: an empty filter value is treated as absent, and a syntactically or semantically unrecognised filter value — a status outside the enum, a malformed month — is compared literally against the stored field, so it simply matches no row and returns an empty page rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and the Audit Trail

Most resources in this system carry a uniform set of server-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request against that row.
- `version` — an integer that increases by one on every mutation of the row.

Three record types are append-only ledger rows and are deliberately thinner: a resource item attached to an application, a time-limit month written by an issuance, and a recovery transaction posted against an overpayment claim. Each of those three carries only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely. Every other record type — households, applications, income sources, assistance cases, hardship extensions, work assignments, participation reports, compliance episodes, supportive service authorizations, diversion grants, overpayment claims and federal reporting periods — carries the full four.

A recomputed derived value is a mutation *when the recomputation actually changes something*. When a cascade refreshes a household's clock rollups, a case's sanction rollup or benefit amount, an assignment's cumulative hours, or a claim's balance and at least one of those figures lands on a new value, the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, exactly as if a client had written to it directly; a recomputation that reproduces every figure identically is a no-op and leaves both `updated_at` and `version` alone. Version arithmetic is also idempotent within a single request: however many steps of a cascade reach the same row, and whether or not the request also wrote that row directly, the row's `version` advances by exactly one for that request.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Nothing in this service reads the wall clock. A single logical clock is the only source of "now", and it starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on every service startup. It advances only when an operator explicitly sets it through the test-driving capability mounted at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which takes a `to_iso8601` instant and returns the new `current_utc`. That endpoint is the one exception to §2.1: it requires no authentication headers and is open to every role.

The logical clock is what stamps `conciliation_start_date` on a compliance episode, what the conciliation deadline comparison is made against when a sanction is imposed, what supplies `sanction_imposed_month`, and what determines the "current" federal fiscal year used when a case's fiscal-year supportive service total is rolled up. Because it only ever moves on request, deadline and fiscal-year behaviour is fully deterministic.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages, Months and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; no monetary field is ever fractional. Every stored percentage or rate field — a sanction fraction, a participation rate, a caseload reduction credit — is a decimal carried to exactly two places.

Two rounding operations appear throughout the business rules and they are not interchangeable. **Rounding half up** means rounding to the nearest value with ties resolved away from zero: applied to a whole number of cents unless a two-decimal target is named explicitly, in which case the result carries exactly two decimal places. **Flooring** means truncating toward negative infinity, discarding any fraction. Wherever the rules below say a figure is floored, half-up rounding is wrong, and wherever they say half-up, truncation is wrong; the two differ by a cent or an hour in exactly the cases the program cares about.

Months are represented as `YYYY-MM` strings and dates as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. Month comparison and month ordering are performed on those strings as calendar months, so lexical comparison and calendar comparison agree. Advancing a month by a number of calendar months rolls the year over as expected. A malformed month, date or timestamp is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.7. Guarding Against Stale Reads

Three write operations sit downstream of state that another writer may have moved, and each of them requires the client to echo back the `version` it last observed on the relevant parent record. The check is not optional and there is no default: omitting the field is a schema violation.

Creating a supportive service authorization requires `expected_case_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AssistanceCase`. If it does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the serialization point for the fiscal-year cap ledger and for compliance posture: another authorization may already have consumed the headroom the requester was sizing against, or a cascade may already have pushed the case into `CONCILIATION_PENDING`. A successful create increments the case's `version` by one.

Creating a compliance episode likewise requires `expected_case_version` matching the target case's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new episode legitimately supersedes the prior compliance posture, so acting on a stale view could escalate a sanction the case has already cured. A successful create increments the case's `version` by one.

Posting a recovery transaction requires `expected_overpayment_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Overpayment`. If it does not equal the claim's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OVERPAYMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the balance the entry was sized against no longer holds. A successful post increments the claim's `version` by one.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a stale-version check.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned and it is always the violation with the lowest ladder position below. This ordering is part of the contract: a test that supplies both an unknown role and a nonexistent path identifier expects the role error, not the not-found error.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and non-empty; `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
2. **Authorization** — the presented role is listed for the endpoint; `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
3. **Path existence** — every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing row, checked left to right, with the leftmost failure winning. Each resource type has its own code, all HTTP 404: `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCOME_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPATION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIVERSION_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, checked in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint, reusing the same twelve not-found codes. This applies to application filing, the case-scoped creation endpoints, compliance episode creation, supportive service authorization creation and overpayment claim drafting.
5. **Schema** — the body parses as an object, all required fields are present, every field matches its declared type, enum domain, numeric bound and string format, and the pagination query parameters are within range. Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected, but they are never honoured. Violations return `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. **Household ownership** — the `Applicant` check described in §2.1; `ERR_NOT_HOUSEHOLD_OWNER`.
7. **Segregation of duties** — the different-actor checks described in §2.1; `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. **Stale parent version** — the checks in §2.7; `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_OVERPAYMENT_VERSION`. Evaluated after authentication, authorization, existence and schema, and before any state or business rule.
9. **Own-entity state** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. All HTTP 409: `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXTENSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DIVERSION_STATE`.
10. **Related-entity state** — `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_UNVERIFIED_REPORTS_IN_QUARTER`, in that relative order.
11. **Program safety rules** — in this relative order: `ERR_TIME_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`, then `ERR_CONCILIATION_NOT_COMPLETE`, then `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
12. **Temporal and uniqueness rules** — in this relative order: `ERR_DIVERSION_INELIGIBILITY_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_ISSUANCE_MONTH`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_MONTH`, then `ERR_REPORT_MONTH_OUTSIDE_ASSIGNMENT`.
13. **Capacity** — `ERR_SCHEDULED_HOURS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
14. **Business referential integrity** — in this relative order: `ERR_WORK_ASSIGNMENT_CASE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_PRIOR_EPISODE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_OVERPAID_MONTH_NOT_ISSUED`.
15. **Fiscal rules**, evaluated last and in this relative order: `ERR_RESOURCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ZERO_BENEFIT`, then `ERR_SUPPORTIVE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, then `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, then `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_RECOVERED`, then `ERR_DIVERSION_REQUIRES_BENEFIT`.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

Several endpoints address a child collection through its parent, and a few address a grandchild. Path identifiers are always resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. A request for the participation reports of an assignment that does not exist yields `ERR_WORK_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and the server never looks for reports. A request to issue a month against a case identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` before the body is examined for a benefit month. This left-to-right discipline holds for reads and writes alike.

### 2.10. Two Independent Case Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

An assistance case carries two state fields that evolve independently of each other on the same row. `benefit_status` is the payment dimension and takes the values `PENDING_ISSUANCE`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, of which `CLOSED` is terminal. `compliance_status` is the work-program dimension and takes the values `NOT_REQUIRED`, `EXEMPT`, `ASSIGNED`, `CONCILIATION_PENDING`, `SANCTIONED` and `CURE_PENDING`, none of which is terminal. A case can be `SUSPENDED` and `CURE_PENDING` simultaneously; neither dimension constrains the other's legal values, and every rule that depends on case posture names which dimension it reads.

Because both dimensions move by cascade as often as by direct request, three named cascades recur throughout §3 and each one runs to completion within the request that triggered it. Verifying a participation report can, in one request, credit hours on the report, refresh the assignment's cumulative hours and compliance flag, open a compliance episode, move the case's `compliance_status` and place supportive service authorizations on hold. Imposing a sanction can, in one request, set the episode's level and percentage, roll the level up to the case, recompute the case's grant, suspend or close the case, and pause active recoupment on the case's overpayment claims. Granting or revoking a hardship extension can, in one request, flip the countability of a span of time-limit months, recompute four household rollups, and close the case. Every row touched by such a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented per §2.4.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Three ledgers in this system have compensating actions, and in each case the compensation must restore the ledger exactly rather than approximately. These invariants are stated here once and referenced by the workflows that maintain them.

The **time-limit ledger** conserves issued months. For any household, the number of state-countable months beyond the carried-in prior state months, plus the number of months excluded because a granted extension covers them, equals the number of issued months on non-child-only cases, which in turn equals the number of federal-countable months beyond the carried-in prior federal months. This equality must hold after every issuance, after every extension grant, and after every extension revocation.

The **supportive service cap ledger** conserves funding headroom. For any combination of case, service type and federal fiscal year, the amount already authorized plus the amount still available equals that service type's fiscal-year cap. Authorizing consumes headroom and voiding returns it, and the sum must be identical before the authorize and after the matching void.

The **overpayment ledger** conserves debt. For any claim, the established amount equals the outstanding balance plus the total recovered, after every recovery entry and after every reversal.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Assistance Unit and Classifying the Family

Everything begins with a **household**, the assistance unit as the program understands it. A `Caseworker` registers one at intake by supplying `head_of_household_name`, which must be a string with at least one non-whitespace character, the administering county office code as `county_code` — exactly three uppercase letters — the `household_size` as the number of persons in the unit, which must be at least 1, the `adult_count` as the number of work-eligible adults, which may be 0, and `youngest_child_age_months`, the age in months of the youngest child, which may not be negative and is recorded as 0 when the unit contains no child. A boolean `has_subsidized_child_care` records whether the unit receives federally subsidized child care; it is optional on the request and defaults to `false`. The identifier returned for a new household is the value an `Applicant` must later present as `X-User-ID` to act on that household's behalf.

Family classification is derived immediately at registration rather than supplied, and it drives the weekly hour requirements a case will later inherit. The `family_type` field takes one of `CHILD_ONLY`, `SINGLE_PARENT_CHILD_UNDER_6`, `SINGLE_PARENT`, `TWO_PARENT_NO_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE` or `TWO_PARENT_WITH_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`. Five branches are tested in exactly this order and the first branch that matches wins. First, a unit with an adult count of zero is `CHILD_ONLY`. Second, a unit with two or more adults that receives federally subsidized child care is `TWO_PARENT_WITH_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`. Third, a unit with two or more adults that does not is `TWO_PARENT_NO_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`. Fourth, a unit whose youngest child is younger than 72 months — that is, strictly under six years of age — is `SINGLE_PARENT_CHILD_UNDER_6`. Fifth and otherwise, the unit is `SINGLE_PARENT`. The under-six exemption uses a strict comparison, so a youngest child recorded at exactly 72 months yields `SINGLE_PARENT`, not `SINGLE_PARENT_CHILD_UNDER_6`.

A freshly registered household starts with every time-limit rollup at zero and with no diversion ineligibility window: `prior_state_clock_months` and `prior_federal_clock_months` both default to 0, the four computed rollups `state_clock_months_used`, `federal_clock_months_used`, `extension_months_used` and `issued_countable_months` are all 0, and `diversion_ineligible_through_month` is null. Reading a household returns the derived `family_type` together with all four rollups, and an `Applicant` may read only its own household.

Households that arrive with history are handled by a separate staff-only capability that records prior assistance carried in from another jurisdiction or a legacy system. It accepts a `source_state_code` naming the originating jurisdiction, which like the head-of-household name may not be blank, a `state_clock_months` count and a `federal_clock_months` count, neither of which may be negative; those two counts are written to `prior_state_clock_months` and `prior_federal_clock_months` respectively, and both clock rollups are then recomputed from the two carried-in figures plus whatever months this system has itself issued. This is the supported way to place a household near or at the 24-month state limit without issuing two dozen months of benefits, and it is available to every staff role.

Listing a household's applications is available to every role, subject to the applicant ownership rule, and accepts an optional `status` filter. That list is the one deliberate exception to the ascending default sort of §2.3: applications of a household are returned newest first.

### 3.2. Building the Application Record and Running the Determination

An application is filed against a household by supplying that household's identifier as `household_id`, the `application_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and a `requested_program` of either `ONGOING_ASSISTANCE` or `DIVERSION_CONSIDERED`, which records whether the applicant is willing to consider a one-time diversion payment instead of ongoing benefits. An optional `budgeting_method` selects which income-budgeting pipeline the determination will use and takes either `PROSPECTIVE` or `RETROSPECTIVE`, defaulting to `PROSPECTIVE` when omitted. Both an `Applicant`, acting for its own household, and a `Caseworker` may file. A new application lands in `status` `RECEIVED`; the full lifecycle domain is `RECEIVED`, `DETERMINED`, `DIVERSION_OFFERED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `DIVERTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which the last four are terminal. Before the determination runs, `snapshot_family_type`, `countable_income_cents`, `countable_resources_cents`, `determined_benefit_cents`, `determined_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` are all null, `denial_reason` is `NONE`, and `withdrawal_reason` is null.

While the application is still `RECEIVED`, a `Caseworker` attaches the household's reported income. Each **income source** records a `source_type` of `EARNED`, `UNEARNED_CHILD_SUPPORT`, `UNEARNED_SSI` or `UNEARNED_UI`; an anticipated `gross_monthly_cents` figure, which may not be negative; three retrospective base-month figures `month_1_cents`, `month_2_cents` and `month_3_cents`, each optional, each defaulting to 0 and none of them bounded below — unlike the anticipated figure, a base month may be stated as a negative integer; and a boolean `is_recurring` recording whether the income is expected to continue. A new income source carries `verification_status` `UNVERIFIED`. Attaching income to an application that has already left `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. A `Caseworker` later moves an income source's `verification_status` to `VERIFIED` or `REFUSED` through a dedicated verification action whose body carries the chosen outcome in a `verification_status` field admitting only those two values; that action is deliberately *not* gated on the owning application's status, so a source may be verified or refused before, during or after the determination has run, and the source's own `verification_status` is likewise unconstrained — re-verifying an already `VERIFIED` or `REFUSED` source simply overwrites it. Income sources of one application can be listed with optional `source_type` and `verification_status` filters.

Property is recorded in parallel as **resource items**, each with an `item_type` of `LIQUID_ASSET`, `VEHICLE`, `REAL_PROPERTY` or `RETIREMENT`, a fair market `gross_value_cents` that may not be negative, an optional `encumbrance_cents` recording verified debt secured by the item, defaulting to 0 and carrying no lower bound of its own, and an `exclusion_type` of `NONE`, `PRIMARY_VEHICLE`, `HOMESTEAD` or `RETIREMENT_ACCOUNT` naming any statutory exclusion claimed. The item's `countable_value_cents` is derived on write: an item claiming any exclusion other than `NONE` counts zero, and an item claiming `NONE` counts its gross value less its encumbrance, floored at zero so that an underwater asset counts nothing rather than a negative amount. Resource items are the only application-scoped record without an audit trail, per §2.4, and they can be listed with an optional `item_type` filter.

Running the **determination** is a `Caseworker` action that advances the application `RECEIVED → DETERMINED`. It is refused from any other status with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, and it is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_INELIGIBILITY_ACTIVE` when the household is inside a diversion ineligibility window — that is, when `diversion_ineligible_through_month` is set and the application's `application_month` is not strictly later than it; the first month after the window passes. On success the determination freezes the household's current `family_type` into the application's `snapshot_family_type`, computes `countable_income_cents`, computes `countable_resources_cents`, computes `determined_benefit_cents` from the household's *current* `household_size` — the size is read live from the household row rather than from any earlier snapshot — with the sanction fraction held at 0.00, and records the acting user in `determined_by_user_id`. The application stays out of `decided_by_user_id`, which remains null until a supervisor disposes of it.

Countable income is computed by two genuinely distinct pipelines selected by `budgeting_method`; they differ both in which income sources participate and in how each participating source's monthly figure is derived. Under `PROSPECTIVE` budgeting, only income sources whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` **and** whose `is_recurring` flag is true participate, and each participating source contributes its `gross_monthly_cents` unchanged. Under `RETROSPECTIVE` budgeting, income sources whose `verification_status` is either `VERIFIED` or `UNVERIFIED` participate — only `REFUSED` sources are excluded, and the recurrence flag is ignored entirely — and each participating source contributes the sum of its three base-month figures divided by three and then floored. The rounding order here is pinned: each source is averaged and floored on its own first, and only then are the floored per-source figures added together. Summing all three months across all sources and dividing once at the end is wrong and can differ by a cent.

From that point both pipelines proceed identically. Gross earned income is the total monthly figure across participating sources whose type is `EARNED`. Gross unearned income is the total across participating sources of every other type. Child support income is the total across participating sources whose type is `UNEARNED_CHILD_SUPPORT`. Gross earned income is first reduced by a flat earned-income disregard of 12000 cents, floored at zero. Half of whatever earned income survives that flat disregard is countable, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. On the unearned side, child support is passed through up to 5000 cents — the countable unearned figure is gross unearned income less the smaller of the child support total and that 5000-cent passthrough. Countable income is the countable earned figure plus the countable unearned figure.

Countable resources for the application are simply the total of `countable_value_cents` across the application's resource items, and an application with no resource items has countable resources of zero.

The grant itself is produced by a six-step formula whose order is pinned, because the same five operations composed in a different order yield different integers. The formula consumes the household size, the countable income figure and a sanction fraction — 0.00 at determination time, and the case's current sanction fraction thereafter. Step one establishes the **payment standard** for the household size: 26000 cents for a unit of one, 39000 for two, 49000 for three, 59000 for four, 68000 for five and 76000 for six; for a unit larger than six, the standard is the six-person figure of 76000 cents plus 7000 cents for each person beyond the sixth. Step two takes the deficit as the payment standard less countable income, floored at zero. Step three applies the ratable reduction, multiplying the deficit by 0.85 and rounding half up to a whole cent. Step four caps that pre-sanction figure at a maximum grant of 90000 cents, taking whichever is smaller. Step five computes the sanction cut as the capped figure multiplied by the sanction fraction, rounded half up to a whole cent, and subtracts that cut from the capped figure. Step six applies the minimum-issuance floor: if the post-sanction figure is below 1000 cents the grant is zero, and otherwise the grant is the post-sanction figure. The floor is inclusive at its boundary — a post-sanction figure of exactly 1000 cents is payable.

### 3.3. Disposing of the Determination: Approval, Denial, Withdrawal and Diversion

A determined application waits for an `EligibilitySupervisor` to dispose of it, and the supervisor has three constructive paths plus withdrawal. Approval is the path that creates an ongoing assistance case, and it is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. The supervisor supplies a `certification_start_month` naming the first month of the certification period. Five conditions must hold, and they are evaluated in the relative order the rejection ladder in §2.8 establishes. The application must be `DETERMINED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The acting user must differ from the recorded `determined_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The household's `state_clock_months_used` must be below the 24-month state limit, unless a `GRANTED` hardship extension on any case of that household covers the supplied `certification_start_month`; failing both, the request is refused with `ERR_TIME_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. The application's `countable_resources_cents` must not exceed the program resource limit of 200000 cents — exactly at the limit is eligible, and strictly above it is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Finally the application's `determined_benefit_cents` must be greater than zero, or the request is refused with `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ZERO_BENEFIT`, which is what happens when countable income has consumed the ratably reduced standard entirely or when the surviving grant fell under the 1000-cent minimum-issuance floor.

When all five hold, approval advances the application `DETERMINED → APPROVED`, records the acting user in `decided_by_user_id`, and mints a new **assistance case**, which is the response body of the approval. The new case records the originating `household_id` and `application_id`, a human-readable `case_number` minted at approval as the literal `CASE-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded sequence number — the counter starts at `000001` on each service startup and advances by one for every case created, so the first case of a fresh process is `CASE-000001` — and the supplied `certification_start_month`. It opens with `benefit_status` `PENDING_ISSUANCE` and `closure_reason` `NONE`. Three values are frozen out of the household and application at this moment and never re-read afterwards: the case's own `family_type`, its `household_size`, and its `countable_income_cents`. Its `benefit_amount_cents` starts equal to the application's `determined_benefit_cents`, its `sanction_level` starts at 0 and its `sanction_pct` at 0.00, and its `supportive_fy_authorized_cents` starts at 0. The case's `compliance_status` opens at `NOT_REQUIRED` when the frozen family type is `CHILD_ONLY`, and at `ASSIGNED` for every other family type.

The four hour requirements are derived from the frozen family type at the same moment. Weekly total hours are 0 for `CHILD_ONLY`, 20 for `SINGLE_PARENT_CHILD_UNDER_6`, 30 for `SINGLE_PARENT`, 35 for `TWO_PARENT_NO_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE` and 55 for `TWO_PARENT_WITH_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`; those figures land in `required_weekly_hours`. Weekly core hours are 0 for `CHILD_ONLY`, 20 for `SINGLE_PARENT_CHILD_UNDER_6`, 20 for `SINGLE_PARENT`, 30 for `TWO_PARENT_NO_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE` and 50 for `TWO_PARENT_WITH_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`; those land in `required_core_weekly_hours`. The two monthly figures are the weekly figures scaled by a reporting month of four weeks: `required_monthly_hours` is four times the weekly total and `required_core_monthly_hours` is four times the weekly core figure. A `CHILD_ONLY` case therefore carries zero in all four and sits in `NOT_REQUIRED`.

Denial is the mirror image and is far simpler. A supervisor different from the determiner moves the application `DETERMINED → DENIED` and supplies a `denial_reason`, which is one of `NONE`, `RESOURCES_OVER_LIMIT`, `ZERO_BENEFIT`, `TIME_LIMIT`, `FAILURE_TO_COOPERATE` or `OTHER`. Denial also records the acting supervisor in `decided_by_user_id`, exactly as approval does; beyond the status, the reason and that actor stamp nothing else changes and no case is created.

Withdrawal is available to the applicant itself and to a `Caseworker`, and is the only disposition reachable from more than one status: an application may be withdrawn while it is `RECEIVED`, `DETERMINED` or `DIVERSION_OFFERED`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The request carries a free-text `withdrawal_reason`, which is stored on the application. Withdrawal also cleans up any outstanding offer: every diversion grant of the application still sitting in `OFFERED` is moved to `DECLINED` as part of the same request.

**Diversion assistance** is the third constructive path — a single lump sum in place of an ongoing grant, offered when a short-term cash injection is expected to resolve the family's crisis. An `EligibilitySupervisor` makes the offer, advancing the application `DETERMINED → DIVERSION_OFFERED` and creating a **diversion grant** in `status` `OFFERED`. Because the transition is declared only for applications filed with `requested_program` `DIVERSION_CONSIDERED`, an offer against an application filed as `ONGOING_ASSISTANCE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The offer additionally requires a positive determined benefit to size the payment: an application whose `determined_benefit_cents` is zero or null is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_REQUIRES_BENEFIT`. Making an offer is not a disposition in the segregation-of-duties sense and takes no body of its own; it leaves the application's `decided_by_user_id` null, and the response body is the new diversion grant rather than the application.

The grant freezes the application's `determined_benefit_cents` into `monthly_benefit_basis_cents` at the moment the offer is made, and three figures follow from that basis. The `lump_sum_cents` is three times the basis, capped at 200000 cents, taking whichever is smaller. The `ineligibility_months` count is the lump sum divided by the basis and rounded up to a whole month, capped at 12 months, again taking whichever is smaller. The `ineligibility_end_month` is the application's `application_month` advanced by one month fewer than the ineligibility month count, so a one-month window ends in the application month itself and the recorded end month is inclusive. A newly created grant carries `decline_reason` null.

The applicant, and only the applicant, disposes of its own offer. Accepting it moves the grant `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and the application `DIVERSION_OFFERED → DIVERTED`, and writes the grant's `ineligibility_end_month` onto the household as `diversion_ineligible_through_month`. No assistance case is created, no time-limit month is consumed, and the household is barred from a further determination for any month at or before that recorded end month — a determination for a month inside the window is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_INELIGIBILITY_ACTIVE`, while the first month strictly after it proceeds normally. Declining instead moves the grant `OFFERED → DECLINED`, stores the supplied free-text `decline_reason`, and cycles the application `DIVERSION_OFFERED → DETERMINED` so that ongoing assistance can still be approved from the same determination; no ineligibility window is created by a decline. Accepting or declining a grant that is not `OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DIVERSION_STATE`. Diversion grants of one application can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.4. Monthly Issuance, the Two Time-Limit Clocks and Hardship Extensions

Once a case exists it must be read before it can be acted on, and reading one returns both state dimensions, the sanction rollup, the current `benefit_amount_cents`, all four hour requirements and `supportive_fy_authorized_cents`. Its `version` is the value a client echoes back as `expected_case_version` on the two guarded writes of §2.7. Staff can also search cases with optional `household_id`, `application_id`, `benefit_status` and `compliance_status` filters.

Issuing a month of assistance is a `Caseworker` action carrying a single `benefit_month`. It is permitted while the case's `benefit_status` is `PENDING_ISSUANCE` or `ACTIVE`, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` when the case is `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`. A second issuance for a benefit month the case has already issued is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ISSUANCE_MONTH` — the pair of case and benefit month is unique. The first successful issuance advances the case `PENDING_ISSUANCE → ACTIVE`; every subsequent issuance leaves it `ACTIVE`.

Each issuance writes one **time-limit month** row, always with `assistance_issued` set to true, and that row is where the program's two lifetime clocks are evaluated. The row is an append-only ledger entry and carries no `updated_at` or `version` per §2.4. It records the `case_id` it belongs to, the `benefit_month` issued, and three derived countability fields. The two clocks apply *different* exclusion sets to the same row, which is the single most consequential divergence in the system.

For the **state clock**, which is the 24-month limit, a month counts when assistance was issued, **and** the case's frozen `family_type` is anything other than `CHILD_ONLY`, **and** no `GRANTED` hardship extension of that case covers the benefit month — where "covers" means the benefit month falls at or after the extension's `first_effective_month` and at or before its `last_effective_month`. The result lands in `state_clock_countable`, and when the month is not state-countable the reason is recorded in `non_countable_reason` as `CHILD_ONLY_CASE` when the case is child-only, otherwise as `GRANTED_EXTENSION` when an extension shields it, and as `NONE` when the month is in fact countable.

For the **federal clock**, which is the 60-month limit, a month counts when assistance was issued **and** the case's frozen `family_type` is anything other than `CHILD_ONLY`. Hardship extension months are state-funded and therefore do not stop the federal clock at all. That is the divergence: a month shielded by a granted extension is federal-countable but not state-countable, and `federal_clock_countable` will read true on a row whose `state_clock_countable` reads false.

Four household rollups are recomputed after every issuance, every extension grant and every extension revocation, always across every time-limit month of every case belonging to that household. `issued_countable_months` is the number of rows with assistance issued whose case is not child-only. `extension_months_used` is the number of those rows whose non-countable reason is `GRANTED_EXTENSION`. `state_clock_months_used` is `prior_state_clock_months` plus the number of state-countable rows. `federal_clock_months_used` is `prior_federal_clock_months` plus the number of federal-countable rows. The conserved identity of §2.11 links them: state-countable months beyond the carried-in prior state figure, plus extension months used, equals the issued countable months, which equals federal-countable months beyond the carried-in prior federal figure.

At the end of a successful issuance the system re-evaluates the time limit. If the recomputed `state_clock_months_used` has reached or passed 24 months and no `GRANTED` extension on the case covers the month just issued, the case closes on the spot: `benefit_status` moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, `closure_reason` becomes `TIME_LIMIT`, every work assignment of the case still in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` `CASE_CLOSED` — and because that is the same adverse termination §3.5 describes, every supportive service authorization of each of those assignments still in `REQUESTED`, `HELD` or `AUTHORIZED` is voided with `void_reason` `ASSIGNMENT_TERMINATED` and its `authorized_amount_cents` returned to 0, after which the case's `supportive_fy_authorized_cents` is recomputed — and every overpayment claim of the case whose `recoupment_status` is `ACTIVE` is moved to `PAUSED`. Whether or not that closure fires, the response body of a successful issuance is the newly written time-limit month row, not the case. The month-by-month ledger for a case can be listed with an optional `benefit_month` filter, and each row exposes both countability flags and its non-countable reason so the two clocks can be compared row by row.

A family approaching the state limit may ask for a **hardship extension**. A `Caseworker` or a `WorkProgramSpecialist` files the request against the case with an `extension_type` of `HARDSHIP_DOMESTIC_VIOLENCE`, `HARDSHIP_DISABILITY`, `HARDSHIP_LOCAL_LABOR_MARKET` or `HARDSHIP_CARETAKER`, a `months_requested` count between 1 and 12 inclusive, and a `first_effective_month` naming the first month the extension would shield. The new request opens in `status` `REQUESTED` with `months_granted` at 0, `last_effective_month` null, and `denial_reason`, `revocation_reason` and `decided_by_user_id` all null. Filing against a `CLOSED` case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Extensions of a case can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

Granting is reserved to an `EligibilitySupervisor` and carries the `months_granted` figure actually approved. The extension must be `REQUESTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EXTENSION_STATE`; the owning case's `benefit_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and a `PENDING_ISSUANCE` or `CLOSED` case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Two arithmetic guards then apply and both surface as `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`: the granted figure must be at least 1 and no greater than the `months_requested` figure, and the household's existing `extension_months_used` plus the granted figure must not exceed the lifetime hardship allowance of 12 months. On success the extension moves `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, stores the granted count, derives `last_effective_month` as the first effective month advanced by one fewer than the granted count so that the span is inclusive at both ends, and records the acting supervisor in `decided_by_user_id`.

The grant then cascades. Every time-limit month of that case whose benefit month falls within the inclusive span from `first_effective_month` through `last_effective_month` is flipped to state-non-countable with `non_countable_reason` `GRANTED_EXTENSION`, and its federal countability is left exactly as it was. That flip in turn drives a recomputation of the household's `state_clock_months_used` and `extension_months_used`.

Denial is the simple branch: an `EligibilitySupervisor` moves a `REQUESTED` extension to `DENIED` with a free-text `denial_reason` and is recorded in `decided_by_user_id` just as a granting supervisor is, and no countability flag and no household rollup changes.

Revocation is the compensating action for a grant, and it is where the conserved month identity earns its keep. An `EligibilitySupervisor` moves a `GRANTED` extension to `REVOKED` — any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EXTENSION_STATE` — supplying a free-text `revocation_reason`. The extension's `months_granted` returns to 0, its `last_effective_month` returns to null, and the revoking supervisor overwrites `decided_by_user_id`. Every time-limit month this extension had shielded is restored to state-countable with `non_countable_reason` `NONE`, *unless* another still-`GRANTED` extension of the case also covers that month, in which case it stays shielded and keeps its `GRANTED_EXTENSION` reason. The household's `state_clock_months_used` and `extension_months_used` are recomputed, and the conserved identity must hold again exactly. If the restored state clock has reached or passed 24 months, the case closes as a consequence: `benefit_status` moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED` with `closure_reason` `TIME_LIMIT`. That closure is deliberately narrower than the one an issuance triggers — it changes only those two fields on the case, and unlike the issuance path it terminates no work assignment, voids no supportive service authorization and pauses no recoupment. The revocation also does not re-check whether a still-`GRANTED` sibling extension shields the months in question before closing; only the recomputed state clock and a `benefit_status` of `ACTIVE` are consulted, and the response body is the revoked extension.

### 3.5. Assigning Allowable Work Activities

A `WorkProgramSpecialist` structures a family's work obligation as one or more **work assignments** against the case. Each assignment names an `activity_category` drawn from the ten allowable federal categories: `UNSUBSIDIZED_EMPLOYMENT`, `SUBSIDIZED_EMPLOYMENT`, `WORK_EXPERIENCE`, `ON_THE_JOB_TRAINING`, `JOB_SEARCH_READINESS`, `COMMUNITY_SERVICE`, `VOCATIONAL_EDUCATION`, `JOB_SKILLS_TRAINING_DIRECT`, `EDUCATION_RELATED_TO_EMPLOYMENT` and `SECONDARY_SCHOOL`. It carries `scheduled_weekly_hours` between 1 and 60 inclusive, a `start_date` and an `end_date`, both well-formed calendar dates but otherwise unconstrained relative to each other: no rule requires `end_date` to fall after `start_date`, and an inverted or degenerate interval is accepted at face value (it simply overlaps nothing and intersects no report month). Creation is equally indifferent to case posture — neither the case's `benefit_status` nor its `compliance_status` is consulted, so an activity may be proposed against a case that is still `PENDING_ISSUANCE`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, `NOT_REQUIRED` or `SANCTIONED`; those postures bite only at activation. A new assignment opens in `status` `PROPOSED` with `termination_reason` `NONE`, `cumulative_verified_hours` at 0 and `compliance_flag` at `NONE`.

Federal law divides those categories into core and non-core, and the split matters enormously to whether a family counts as participating. The `is_core` flag is derived from the category on write: the first seven categories in the list above — unsubsidized employment, subsidized employment, work experience, on-the-job training, job search and readiness, community service, and vocational education — are core, and the remaining three — job skills training directed at employment, education related to employment, and secondary school — are non-core. A specialist cannot set the flag; it follows the category.

Assignment date ranges are **half-open**: the interval runs from the `start_date` inclusive up to but not including the `end_date`. Two assignments overlap when each one's start date falls strictly before the other one's end date. The practical consequence is that an assignment ending on 2026-10-01 and another starting on 2026-10-01 do *not* overlap, and both may carry a full schedule.

Scheduling capacity is checked twice — once when the assignment is created and again when it is activated — and it is the same check both times. For the candidate assignment, the system totals the candidate's own `scheduled_weekly_hours` with the `scheduled_weekly_hours` of every *other* work assignment of the same case that is in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` status and that overlaps the candidate's interval. If that total exceeds 60 hours per week the operation is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULED_HOURS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a total of exactly 60 is allowed. Assignments in `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` status contribute nothing, so terminating one activity frees its hours for a replacement.

Activation moves an assignment `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and is where the case's two dimensions both gate the operation. The assignment must be `PROPOSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. The owning case's `benefit_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a case sitting in `PENDING_ISSUANCE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The case's `compliance_status` must be `ASSIGNED`, `CONCILIATION_PENDING` or `CURE_PENDING`, and a case in `NOT_REQUIRED`, `EXEMPT` or `SANCTIONED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` — a family under sanction is not put back to work until the sanction is resolved, and a family with no work requirement is not assigned at all. Then the capacity total is re-evaluated, because sibling assignments may have been added since the candidate was proposed.

Termination is a fork, and the `termination_reason` supplied decides which branch runs. Either branch may be entered from `PROPOSED` or from `ACTIVE`; an assignment already in `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. The benign branch is `COMPLETED_ACTIVITY`: the assignment lands in `status` `COMPLETED`, its `cumulative_verified_hours` is frozen at whatever it had accumulated, and the assignment's supportive service authorizations are left completely untouched — a family that finished its activity keeps the child care it was awarded for the period. The adverse branch covers `NONCOMPLIANCE`, `CASE_CLOSED` and `PLAN_CHANGE`: the assignment lands in `status` `TERMINATED`, and every supportive service authorization of *that assignment* in `REQUESTED`, `HELD` or `AUTHORIZED` status is voided — its `void_reason` is recorded as `ASSIGNMENT_TERMINATED`, its `authorized_amount_cents` returns to 0, and the case's `supportive_fy_authorized_cents` is recomputed so that the freed fiscal-year headroom is restored exactly, as §2.11 requires. Both branches store the supplied reason on the assignment. Assignments of a case can be listed with optional `status` and `activity_category` filters, and a single assignment read on its own exposes its core flag, cumulative hours and compliance flag.

### 3.6. Reporting and Verifying Participation Hours

Hours are claimed one month at a time against one assignment. Either a `Caseworker` or a `WorkProgramSpecialist` files a **participation report** carrying a `report_month`, a `reported_hours` figure between 0 and 744 inclusive, and a `verification_source` of `EMPLOYER_ATTESTATION`, `TIMESHEET`, `THIRD_PARTY` or `SELF_REPORT`. The report opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `verified_hours` null, `core_hours_credited` and `non_core_hours_credited` both 0, and `rejection_reason` null. Filing requires the owning case's `benefit_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and a case that is `PENDING_ISSUANCE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. A report duplicating an existing `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED` report for the same assignment and the same reported month is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_MONTH`; a previously `REJECTED` report for that month does not block a replacement. The assignment's own status is not a filing guard at all — hours may be claimed against an assignment that is still `PROPOSED`, or that is already `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` — because the assignment-status rule belongs to verification rather than to filing.

The reported month must actually intersect the assignment's half-open interval, and the test is specific: the first calendar day of the reported month must fall strictly before the assignment's `end_date`, and the last calendar day of the reported month must fall on or after the assignment's `start_date`. A month failing either half is refused with `ERR_REPORT_MONTH_OUTSIDE_ASSIGNMENT`.

Verification is reserved to a `WorkProgramSpecialist` and carries the `verified_hours` figure the specialist accepts. Three conditions apply, and because the band check is a schema violation it sits at ladder position 5 and is therefore evaluated *first*, before either state check: the accepted figure must be at least 0 and no greater than the report's own `reported_hours`, and a figure outside that band is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the report or the assignment is also in the wrong state. Then the report must be `SUBMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`. Then the owning assignment must be `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` — hours can still be verified against an activity that has finished, but not against one that was proposed and never activated or that was adversely terminated — and any other assignment status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.

Crediting then applies a documentation discount. A report whose `verification_source` is `SELF_REPORT` is credited at half its accepted figure; every other documentation type is credited in full. The credited figure is the accepted figure multiplied by that factor and then floored to a whole hour, so 45 self-reported hours credit 22 and not 23. That floored figure is stored as `verified_hours`. It is then routed by the assignment's core flag: a core assignment puts the whole credited figure into `core_hours_credited` and leaves `non_core_hours_credited` at 0, while a non-core assignment puts the whole credited figure into `non_core_hours_credited` and leaves `core_hours_credited` at 0.

The assignment's rollup follows immediately: `cumulative_verified_hours` becomes the total of `verified_hours` across that assignment's `VERIFIED` reports.

The system then evaluates the **case-month participation predicate**, and it does so across the whole case rather than the single assignment, because a family satisfies its obligation with hours from any combination of activities. For the report's month, the system totals `core_hours_credited` across every `VERIFIED` participation report belonging to any work assignment of the case, and separately totals `non_core_hours_credited` the same way. The steps are ordered. First, if the core total is at least the case's `required_core_monthly_hours`, the non-core total is countable; otherwise the countable non-core figure is zero. The family participated for the month when the core total is at least `required_core_monthly_hours` **and** the core total plus the countable non-core figure is at least `required_monthly_hours`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so hitting the requirement exactly counts as meeting it. Non-core hours are forfeited *entirely*, not proportionally, when the core threshold is missed — a `SINGLE_PARENT` case needing 80 core hours and 120 total hours that credits 79 core hours and 60 non-core hours has 79 countable hours for the month, not 139.

The predicate drives the assignment's `compliance_flag`, which becomes `SHORTFALL` when the predicate is false for the reported month and `NONE` when it is true. When the flag becomes `SHORTFALL` *and* the case's `compliance_status` is `ASSIGNED` at that moment, the same request opens a **compliance episode** on the case in `status` `ALLEGED`, with `allegation_reason` `HOURS_SHORTFALL`, `origin` `SYSTEM_SHORTFALL` and `prior_episode_id` null — a system-opened episode is never chained to a predecessor, so it always begins the sanction ladder at level 1 — and moves the case's `compliance_status` to `CONCILIATION_PENDING`. Because that move happened, the request continues one step further: every supportive service authorization of the case still in `REQUESTED` status is put on hold, moving to `HELD`. A family under a fresh noncompliance allegation does not draw new supportive service money until the allegation is settled.

Rejection is the alternative disposition. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` moves a `SUBMITTED` report to `REJECTED` with a free-text `rejection_reason`, and the report's `verified_hours`, `core_hours_credited` and `non_core_hours_credited` are all set to 0. A rejected report never enters the participation predicate and never enters the federal rate numerator. Reports of an assignment can be listed with optional `report_month` and `status` filters.

### 3.7. Noncompliance: Conciliation, Good Cause, Sanction Escalation and Cure

A **compliance episode** is the container for one noncompliance event and its resolution. Episodes arrive by two routes, recorded in `origin`. The system creates them with `origin` `SYSTEM_SHORTFALL` when a verification leaves the case-month predicate false, as §3.6 describes. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` creates them directly with `origin` `CASEWORKER_REFERRAL`, supplying the `case_id`, an `allegation_reason` of `HOURS_SHORTFALL`, `REFUSED_ASSIGNMENT`, `QUIT_EMPLOYMENT` or `MISSED_APPOINTMENT`, the `expected_case_version` required by §2.7, and optionally a `prior_episode_id`. A referral is permitted only while the case's `compliance_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `EXEMPT` or `CURE_PENDING`; a case already in `CONCILIATION_PENDING`, `SANCTIONED`, or with no work requirement at all in `NOT_REQUIRED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A successful referral puts the case's `compliance_status` into `CONCILIATION_PENDING` and increments the case's `version` by one.

The optional `prior_episode_id` is what makes the sanction ladder cumulative across separate events. It must reference a compliance episode of the *same* case whose own `status` is `SANCTION_IMPOSED` or `CURE_PENDING`; a reference to another case's episode, or to an episode in any other status, is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_EPISODE_MISMATCH`. When it is omitted the episode is treated as a first occurrence. A referral filed with a prior episode while the case sits in `CURE_PENDING` is the cross-episode escalation loop: the case moves `CURE_PENDING → CONCILIATION_PENDING` and the new episode will start one rung higher than its predecessor.

A new episode opens in `status` `ALLEGED` with `conciliation_start_date` and `conciliation_deadline_date` null, `sanction_level` 0, `sanction_pct` 0.00, `sanction_imposed_month` null, `good_cause_reason` `NONE` and `cure_plan_note` null. The full episode lifecycle domain is `ALLEGED`, `CONCILIATION`, `SANCTION_IMPOSED`, `CURE_PENDING`, `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT` and `GOOD_CAUSE_GRANTED`, of which the last two are terminal; every other transition attempted from a status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. Episodes can be read individually or searched with optional `case_id`, `prior_episode_id` and `status` filters.

**Conciliation** is the mandatory cooling-off period before any adverse action. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` starts it, advancing the episode `ALLEGED → CONCILIATION`. The date portion of the logical clock at that moment is stamped into `conciliation_start_date`, and `conciliation_deadline_date` is set to 30 days after that start date. No sanction may be imposed before the deadline arrives.

Conciliation has three exits. An `EligibilitySupervisor` may **grant good cause**, supplying a `good_cause_reason` of `DOMESTIC_VIOLENCE`, `NO_CHILD_CARE_AVAILABLE`, `ILLNESS` or `TRANSPORTATION_UNAVAILABLE` (the field's fourth legal value, `NONE`, is its unset default rather than a grantable reason). The episode moves `CONCILIATION → GOOD_CAUSE_GRANTED`, its own `sanction_level` returns to 0 and its `sanction_pct` to 0.00, the case's `compliance_status` becomes `EXEMPT`, and the case's sanction rollup and `benefit_amount_cents` are recomputed. The good-cause grant stops there on the payment side: unlike a compliant resolution it does *not* move a `SUSPENDED` case back to `ACTIVE` and does *not* return any `PAUSED` overpayment claim to `ACTIVE`, even when the rollup it just ran brought the case sanction level back to 0. Once good cause has been recorded on an episode, any later attempt to sanction that episode is refused with `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_ACTIVE` — and that refusal outranks the episode-state check, so a `GOOD_CAUSE_GRANTED` episode yields `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_ACTIVE` rather than `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`.

A `WorkProgramSpecialist` may instead **resolve the episode as compliant**, which is reachable from `CONCILIATION` and also from `CURE_PENDING`. The episode moves to `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT`, its `sanction_level` returns to 0 and its `sanction_pct` to 0.00, and the case's `compliance_status` returns to `ASSIGNED`. The case's sanction rollup and `benefit_amount_cents` are recomputed, and two further consequences follow when the recomputed case-level sanction returns to 0: a case sitting in `benefit_status` `SUSPENDED` moves back to `ACTIVE`, and once the case is `ACTIVE` again every overpayment claim of the case whose `recoupment_status` is `PAUSED` returns to `ACTIVE`. Supportive service authorizations that were placed on `HELD` by a shortfall cascade are *not* released automatically by this resolution; they stay `HELD` until a specialist releases them explicitly.

The third exit is the adverse one. An `EligibilitySupervisor` **imposes a sanction**, which is reachable from `CONCILIATION` and, when a cure fails, again from `CURE_PENDING`. Two guards apply before anything is written: the episode's `good_cause_reason` must still be `NONE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_ACTIVE`; and the current logical clock date must have reached or passed the episode's `conciliation_deadline_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONCILIATION_NOT_COMPLETE`. The comparison is inclusive — the deadline date itself is a valid day to sanction.

The level the episode lands on depends on which status it came from. Coming from `CONCILIATION`, an episode with no `prior_episode_id` starts at level 1, and an episode chained to a prior episode starts one level above that prior episode's `sanction_level`; either way the result is capped at level 3. Coming from `CURE_PENDING` — the same-episode escalation that follows a failed cure — the episode's own `sanction_level` is raised by one, again capped at 3. In both branches `sanction_pct` is then read from the level: level 0 is 0.00, level 1 is 0.25, level 2 is 0.50 and level 3 is 1.00, meaning a quarter, a half, and the whole grant respectively. The logical clock's month is stamped into `sanction_imposed_month` on the episode's first imposition, and that stamp is what the federal sanction disregard in §3.10 reads.

The case-level rollup follows from the episode change. The case's `sanction_level` becomes the highest `sanction_level` among that case's episodes whose `status` is `SANCTION_IMPOSED` or `CURE_PENDING`, or 0 when the case has no episode in either status; the case's `sanction_pct` is read from that level using the same four-entry table. The case's `benefit_amount_cents` is then recomputed by the six-step grant formula of §3.2, using the case's frozen `household_size` and `countable_income_cents` together with the new sanction fraction. That same rollup runs after every episode transition, not only after impositions, which is how a resolution or a good-cause grant restores the family's full grant.

The rollup result then drives the payment dimension. When the recomputed case `sanction_level` is 2, an `ACTIVE` case moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. When it is 3, the case closes as a full-family sanction: `benefit_status` moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED` or `SUSPENDED → CLOSED`, `closure_reason` becomes `FULL_FAMILY_SANCTION`, `benefit_amount_cents` is set to 0, and every work assignment of the case still in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` `CASE_CLOSED`, dragging each of those assignments' `REQUESTED`, `HELD` and `AUTHORIZED` supportive service authorizations to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `ASSIGNMENT_TERMINATED` and `authorized_amount_cents` 0 and prompting a recompute of `supportive_fy_authorized_cents`, exactly as an adverse termination does in §3.5. Either outcome, suspension or closure, then pauses money recovery: every overpayment claim of the case whose `recoupment_status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `PAUSED`. Both the payment move and the pause are conditional on the transition actually happening — a level-2 rollup on a case that is already `SUSPENDED`, or a level-3 rollup on a case that is already `CLOSED`, changes neither the `benefit_status` nor any claim's `recoupment_status` — and a rollup landing on level 0 or 1 leaves the payment dimension entirely alone. Independently of all of that, the case's `compliance_status` becomes `SANCTIONED`.

A sanctioned family gets a route back. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` **begins a cure**, moving the episode `SANCTION_IMPOSED → CURE_PENDING` with a free-text `cure_plan_note`, and the case's `compliance_status` becomes `CURE_PENDING` to match. The sanction percentage stays in force throughout the cure period — the reduced grant is not restored on the strength of a plan — which is precisely why a failed cure escalates the same episode by one level rather than restarting it at level 1. The cycle `SANCTION_IMPOSED → CURE_PENDING → SANCTION_IMPOSED` can therefore run repeatedly, with the level ratcheting up to its cap of 3 and never back down except through a compliant resolution or a good-cause grant.

### 3.8. Supportive Services for Transportation, Child Care and Work Expenses

Work requirements are unenforceable without money for bus fare, child care and boots, so the program authorizes **supportive services** against a specific work activity. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` requests one by supplying the `case_id`, the `work_assignment_id` of the activity the service supports, a `service_type` of `TRANSPORTATION`, `CHILD_CARE` or `WORK_EXPENSE`, a `requested_amount_cents` of at least 1, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` of the service period, and the `expected_case_version` required by §2.7. The referenced assignment must belong to the referenced case; a mismatch between the assignment's own case and the `case_id` supplied is refused with `ERR_WORK_ASSIGNMENT_CASE_MISMATCH`. The request may be filed against an assignment that is still only `PROPOSED` — the activity does not have to be running before its support is lined up.

A new authorization opens in `status` `REQUESTED` with `authorized_amount_cents` at 0 and `void_reason` null. Its `fiscal_year` is derived from `period_start` on the federal calendar: the federal fiscal year is the calendar year of the start date, except that a start date in October, November or December belongs to the *following* fiscal year. A period starting 2026-10-01 therefore belongs to fiscal year 2027, while one starting 2026-09-30 belongs to fiscal year 2026.

Unlike work assignment intervals, service periods are **closed and inclusive** at both ends. Two service periods overlap when each one's start date falls on or before the other one's end date, which means two periods that merely share a single endpoint day *do* overlap. At create time the candidate period must not overlap any other authorization of the same case with the same `service_type` whose status is `REQUESTED`, `HELD` or `AUTHORIZED`; an overlap is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The `period_end` must also fall on or after the `period_start`, and an inverted period is treated as part of the same period rule rather than as a schema violation: it too is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, and it is checked immediately before the overlap scan so an inverted period is rejected whether or not it collides with anything. Voided authorizations do not block a replacement period.

Authorizing the money is a separate step and is where the caps bite. The authorization must be `REQUESTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`, and the owning work assignment must by then be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The awarded amount is then computed by a three-way waterfall whose steps run in a pinned order. First, the amount already committed under that service type is the total of `authorized_amount_cents` across the case's `AUTHORIZED` authorizations sharing the same `service_type` and the same `fiscal_year`; the remaining service-type headroom is that service type's fiscal-year cap less the committed amount. The per-service-type caps are 60000 cents for transportation, 240000 cents for child care and 30000 cents for work expenses. Second, the amount already committed across all service types is the total of `authorized_amount_cents` across the case's `AUTHORIZED` authorizations sharing the same `fiscal_year`; the remaining case headroom is the overall per-case fiscal-year cap of 300000 cents less that committed amount. Third, `authorized_amount_cents` becomes the smallest of three figures: the `requested_amount_cents`, the remaining service-type headroom, and the remaining case headroom.

A **partial award is normal and expected**. When the waterfall lands strictly between zero and the requested amount, the authorization still moves `REQUESTED → AUTHORIZED` and carries the smaller figure. Only a waterfall result of exactly zero is a failure: it is refused with `ERR_SUPPORTIVE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, the authorization stays `REQUESTED`, and no cap is consumed. On a successful authorization the case's `supportive_fy_authorized_cents` is recomputed as the total of `authorized_amount_cents` across the case's `AUTHORIZED` authorizations whose fiscal year matches the *current* federal fiscal year derived from the logical clock — note that this rollup tracks the current year, while the waterfall itself is evaluated against the candidate authorization's own fiscal year.

Voiding is the compensating action, available to a `WorkProgramSpecialist` or a `FiscalOfficer`, and reachable from `REQUESTED`, `HELD` or `AUTHORIZED`; an already `VOIDED` row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`. The row moves to `VOIDED`, stores the supplied free-text `void_reason`, returns `authorized_amount_cents` to 0, and the case's `supportive_fy_authorized_cents` is recomputed — again over the `AUTHORIZED` rows whose fiscal year matches the *current* federal fiscal year of the logical clock, not the voided row's own fiscal year — so that the freed per-service-type and per-case headroom is restored *exactly*. The conserved cap identity of §2.11 must hold before the authorize and after the matching void: for any case, service type and fiscal year, committed plus remaining equals the cap.

Holds are the one status change no client requests directly. As §3.6 describes, a shortfall cascade puts every `REQUESTED` authorization of a case into `HELD`. A `WorkProgramSpecialist` releases one by moving it `HELD → REQUESTED`, which is permitted only once the case's `compliance_status` has returned to `ASSIGNED`, `EXEMPT` or `NOT_REQUIRED`; a case still in `CONCILIATION_PENDING`, `SANCTIONED` or `CURE_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a row that is not `HELD` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`. The pair `REQUESTED → HELD → REQUESTED` is therefore a genuine cycle, and a released row can go on to be authorized normally. Authorizations can be read individually or searched with optional `case_id`, `work_assignment_id`, `service_type` and `status` filters.

### 3.9. Overpayments: Establishment, the Recovery Ledger and Fraud Referral

When a family has been paid more than it was entitled to, the money is tracked as an **overpayment claim**. Either a `FiscalOfficer` or a `FraudInvestigator` drafts one against a case, supplying the `case_id`, a `claim_type` of `AGENCY_ERROR`, `CLIENT_ERROR` or `SUSPECTED_FRAUD`, a non-empty `months_covered` list of `YYYY-MM` months the claim spans, a `gross_amount_cents` of at least 1 recording the overissued amount before any offset, a `computation_method` of `GROSS` or `NET`, and an optional `offsetting_underpayment_cents` recording underpaid money available to offset, defaulting to 0 and carrying no lower bound of its own. The draft opens in `status` `DRAFT` with every ledger figure zeroed — `established_amount_cents`, `total_recovered_cents`, `balance_cents` and `monthly_recoupment_cents` all 0 — with `recoupment_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `fraud_referral_status` `NOT_REFERRED`, and `established_by_user_id`, `write_off_reason` and `referral_note` all null.

Establishing the claim turns it into collectible debt and is refused from any status other than `DRAFT` with `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`. Every month in the `months_covered` list must correspond to a time-limit month row on that case with assistance actually issued; a month that was never issued is refused with `ERR_OVERPAID_MONTH_NOT_ISSUED`. The established amount is then computed by whichever of two variants `computation_method` selects, and the two variants consume different inputs rather than merely different constants. Under `GROSS`, the established amount is the `gross_amount_cents` figure and the `offsetting_underpayment_cents` figure is ignored entirely. Under `NET`, the established amount is the gross figure less the offsetting underpayment, floored at zero so that an offset larger than the overissuance establishes nothing rather than a credit.

Recoupment sizing runs the same way under both variants. The applicable percentage is 5 percent for an `AGENCY_ERROR` claim, 10 percent for a `CLIENT_ERROR` claim and 20 percent for a `SUSPECTED_FRAUD` claim — unless the claim's `fraud_referral_status` has already reached `SUBSTANTIATED`, in which case the percentage is 25 percent regardless of claim type. The `monthly_recoupment_cents` figure is that percentage of the case's *current* `benefit_amount_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then raised to a floor of 1000 cents if it came out lower — a family with a tiny grant still repays at least ten dollars a month. Establishment also sets `balance_cents` to the established amount less the total recovered, records the acting user in `established_by_user_id`, sets `recoupment_status` to `NOT_STARTED`, and moves the claim `DRAFT → ESTABLISHED`.

Recovery is a signed ledger. A `FiscalOfficer` posts a **recovery transaction** with a `method` of `BENEFIT_RECOUPMENT`, `CASH_PAYMENT`, `TAX_INTERCEPT` or `RECOUPMENT_REVERSAL`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1 stated as an absolute value, an `applied_month` in `YYYY-MM` form recording the month the entry books to, and the `expected_overpayment_version` required by §2.7. Entries are accepted while the claim is `ESTABLISHED`, `IN_RECOVERY` or `SATISFIED`; a claim still in `DRAFT` or already `WRITTEN_OFF` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`. A `BENEFIT_RECOUPMENT` entry additionally requires the owning case's `benefit_status` to be `ACTIVE`, since it withholds money from a live grant, and a case that is `PENDING_ISSUANCE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two amount guards then apply: any entry whose method is not `RECOUPMENT_REVERSAL` may not exceed the claim's current `balance_cents`, and one that does is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`; a `RECOUPMENT_REVERSAL` may not exceed the claim's current `total_recovered_cents`, and one that does is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_RECOVERED`.

Each entry's `signed_amount_cents` is the negative of its stated amount when the method is `RECOUPMENT_REVERSAL` and the positive of its stated amount otherwise — a reversal credits the participant back. Each entry also records the `overpayment_id` of the claim whose ledger it belongs to, taken from the path rather than the body. Recovery transactions are append-only ledger rows with no `updated_at` and no `version`, per §2.4. After each entry the claim's `total_recovered_cents` becomes the sum of `signed_amount_cents` across all of that claim's entries, and `balance_cents` becomes the established amount less that total, which keeps the conserved debt identity of §2.11 true at every point. The claim's `recoupment_status` becomes `ACTIVE` and the claim moves `ESTABLISHED → IN_RECOVERY`, or stays `IN_RECOVERY` on a subsequent entry, and each posting increments the claim's `version` by one.

Two ledger-driven status moves close the cycle. When the balance reaches exactly zero after an entry, the claim moves `IN_RECOVERY → SATISFIED` and its `recoupment_status` returns to `NOT_STARTED`. When a `RECOUPMENT_REVERSAL` posted against a satisfied claim leaves the balance above zero again, the claim moves `SATISFIED → IN_RECOVERY` and its `recoupment_status` returns to `ACTIVE`. That is why the recovery status is a separate dimension from the claim status: recoupment can be running, paused by a case suspension or closure as §3.4 and §3.7 describe, resumed when a compliant resolution restores the case, or simply not started.

Writing off an uncollectible claim is reserved to a `FiscalOfficer` and reachable from `ESTABLISHED` or `IN_RECOVERY`; any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`. It requires an acting user different from the recorded `established_by_user_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The claim moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, stores the free-text `write_off_reason`, and its `recoupment_status` returns to `NOT_STARTED`.

Fraud handling is a second, independent dimension on the same claim, and it moves without regard to where the recovery status stands. A `FraudInvestigator` refers a claim by supplying a free-text `referral_note`, moving `fraud_referral_status` `NOT_REFERRED → REFERRED`; referral requires the claim's own status to be `ESTABLISHED`, `IN_RECOVERY` or `SATISFIED`, and a referral of an already-referred claim or of a claim in another status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`. The investigator later records the outcome by supplying a `disposition`, moving `REFERRED → SUBSTANTIATED` or `REFERRED → UNSUBSTANTIATED`; the only gate on that step is the fraud dimension itself — a claim whose `fraud_referral_status` is not `REFERRED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OVERPAYMENT_STATE`, while the claim's own `status` is not consulted, so a disposition may be recorded even against a claim that has since been written off. A substantiated finding re-sizes `monthly_recoupment_cents` at 25 percent of the case's current `benefit_amount_cents`, rounded half up and floored at 1000 cents, replacing whatever the claim-type percentage had produced. An unsubstantiated finding leaves the monthly recoupment figure exactly as it was. Neither disposition touches the established amount, the total recovered or the balance. Claims can be read individually, listed per case with optional `status` and `recoupment_status` filters, and their ledger listed with optional `method` and `applied_month` filters, each entry exposing its signed amount.

### 3.10. Federal Reporting: Participation Rates and the Caseload Reduction Credit

Every quarter the state must report to the federal government what share of its work-eligible caseload actually participated, and it is graded against a statutory target that can be reduced by proving its caseload has shrunk. A `StateReportingAnalyst` opens a **federal reporting period** by supplying the `fiscal_year`, the `quarter` as an integer from 1 through 4, the `prior_year_caseload` as the average monthly caseload of the comparison year, and the `eligibility_change_caseload_effect` as the percentage points of caseload decline attributable to eligibility-rule changes, which federal rules forbid crediting. Neither of the two caseload inputs may be negative, and both are accepted as decimals rather than whole numbers — an average caseload of 120.5 is legal — while `fiscal_year` is any integer, unbounded in either direction. The period opens in `status` `OPEN` with `current_caseload`, `wpr_all_families_pct`, `wpr_two_parent_pct` and `caseload_reduction_credit_pct` all 0.00, `adjusted_target_all_families_pct` at 50.00, `target_met` false, and both `calculated_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` null.

The quarter identifier resolves to three specific calendar months on the federal fiscal calendar. Quarter 1 is October, November and December of the calendar year *preceding* the fiscal year. Quarter 2 is January, February and March of the fiscal year. Quarter 3 is April, May and June of the fiscal year. Quarter 4 is July, August and September of the fiscal year.

Running the calculation advances the period `OPEN → CALCULATED`, and it may be run again on an already-`CALCULATED` period to refresh every computed figure from current data; a `CERTIFIED` period may not be recalculated and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. Each run records the acting analyst in `calculated_by_user_id`. The calculation itself is five stages, each consuming the previous stage's output, and two different denominators are in play at once — deriving `current_caseload` from the adjusted denominator instead of the raw one is the classic way to get every downstream number wrong.

**Stage one — raw monthly denominators.** For each of the quarter's three months, the raw denominator is the set of assistance cases that have a time-limit month row for that month with assistance issued and whose frozen `family_type` is anything other than `CHILD_ONLY`. Child-only cases carry no work requirement and never appear in any denominator.

**Stage two — the sanction disregard, applied per case per fiscal year.** A case-month counts as sanctioned when the case has a compliance episode whose `sanction_imposed_month` is at or before that month and whose `status` is `SANCTION_IMPOSED` or `CURE_PENDING`. For each case, its sanctioned months are walked chronologically starting from October, the first month of the fiscal year, and moving forward. The first three such months are removed from their raw denominators; the fourth sanctioned month and every later one *remains* in its denominator. The surviving sets are the adjusted monthly denominators. The three-month allowance is per case and per fiscal year, so a case sanctioned in October, November and December has all three disregarded, and a case sanctioned from October through February has October, November and December disregarded while January and February stay in the count.

**Stage three — the numerator.** For each month, the numerator is the subset of that month's *adjusted* denominator for which the case-month participation predicate of §3.6 evaluates true for that month.

**Stage four — the two rates.** The all-families denominator is the total size of the adjusted denominators across the quarter's own three months, and the all-families numerator is the total size of the numerators across the same three months. When the denominator is zero, `wpr_all_families_pct` is 0.00; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, expressed as a percentage, rounded half up to two decimal places. The two-parent rate is computed identically but with both the adjusted denominators and the numerators restricted to cases whose frozen `family_type` is `TWO_PARENT_NO_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE` or `TWO_PARENT_WITH_SUBSIDIZED_CHILD_CARE`; when that restricted denominator is zero, `wpr_two_parent_pct` is 0.00, and otherwise it is the restricted numerator over the restricted denominator as a percentage, rounded half up to two decimal places. Federal law sets the two-parent statutory target at 90.00 percent, which the report carries for reference; only the all-families rate participates in the credit arithmetic below.

**Stage five — the caseload reduction credit.** The `current_caseload` figure is the total size of the *unadjusted* raw denominators from stage one across the quarter's three months, divided by three, rounded half up to two decimal places. This is the stage where the raw denominators are used and the adjusted ones must not be. The raw decline is 0.00 when `prior_year_caseload` is zero; otherwise it is the prior-year caseload less the current caseload, divided by the prior-year caseload, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places. The `caseload_reduction_credit_pct` is that raw decline less the `eligibility_change_caseload_effect`, rounded half up to two decimal places, then floored at 0.00 and capped at 50.00. The `adjusted_target_all_families_pct` is the statutory all-families target of 50.00 percent less the credit, floored at 0.00. Finally `target_met` is true when `wpr_all_families_pct` is at or above the adjusted target, using an inclusive comparison, and false otherwise.

A quarter with no issued months at all is well defined rather than an error: both rates come out 0.00, `current_caseload` comes out 0.00, the raw decline comes out 100.00 whenever the prior-year caseload is positive, and `target_met` is still evaluated with the same inclusive comparison against the adjusted target.

Certification is the final act and is deliberately hard to rush. It advances the period `CALCULATED → CERTIFIED`; a period still `OPEN` or already `CERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. The acting analyst must differ from the recorded `calculated_by_user_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And no participation report may still be sitting in `SUBMITTED` with a `report_month` inside the period's three months, because an unverified report could still change the numerator; such a period is refused with `ERR_UNVERIFIED_REPORTS_IN_QUARTER`. On success the acting analyst is recorded in `certified_by_user_id`, and the period's figures are frozen.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new stand-alone resource or add a new resource to a collection — registering a household, filing an application, attaching an income source or resource item, issuing a month, requesting an extension, assigning an activity, filing a participation report, opening a compliance episode, requesting a supportive service, drafting an overpayment claim, posting a recovery entry, opening a reporting quarter, and approving an application, which mints a new assistance case — return `201 Created`. Every other operation, including every state transition on an existing resource and every read, returns `200 OK`. In the table below, "Any role" means all seven roles named in §2.1, and "Any role except `Applicant`" means every staff role. The request and response payloads are not restated here; §3 is the normative source for what each operation accepts and what it changes.
