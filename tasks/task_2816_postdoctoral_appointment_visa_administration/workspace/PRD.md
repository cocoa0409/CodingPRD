# Postdoc Appointment & Visa Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university postdoctoral affairs office sits at the intersection of four regimes that rarely agree with one another: payroll title policy, sponsor award terms, immigration sponsorship timelines, and research-security law. An offer letter that is perfectly correct on the compensation side can be unlawful because the petition has not been adjudicated; a term that satisfies the visa office can breach the institution's total-years-in-rank ceiling; a bench seat that the mentor considers routine can be an export-control exposure that no amount of later paperwork can undo. Today these judgements are made by hand, in email threads, against spreadsheets that are stale by the time they are read.

This document specifies the API that replaces that manual coordination. The service owns the full arc of a postdoctoral appointment — registering the scholar, the mentor, the award and the laboratory space; drafting an appointment and pricing it against a policy salary floor; encumbering the sponsor award; selecting and tracking the visa category; collecting the funder-mandated mentoring plan and individual development plan; screening the appointment for research-security risk before any physical access is granted; running the reappointment review with its statutory non-renewal notice entitlement and grievance channel; and closing the appointment out through intellectual-property assignment and separation. Every one of those steps writes to a shared, immediately consistent record, so that the compensation officer, the international scholar advisor, the export-control reviewer and the mentor all read the same numbers at the same instant.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **research security outranks business convenience**: a compliance regression suspends physical access immediately and a denial terminates the appointment, because laboratory exposure cannot be reversed after the fact, whereas money can be. Second, **every derived number is a function of stored inputs, never of when it happened to be asked for**: rank months, salary floors, encumbrances, projected adjudication dates and notice deadlines are all recomputed deterministically from the record, and the server reads a controllable logical clock rather than wall-clock time so that deadline behaviour is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold everywhere in the API. Where a rule in section 3 appears to contradict this section, this section governs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they act. Six roles exist, and the spelling is exact: `PostdocAffairsOfficer`, `HRCompensationApprover`, `ExportControlReviewer`, `InternationalScholarAdvisor`, `FacultyMentor`, and `Postdoc`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those six strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). If the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

The six roles divide into two identity families, and the distinction matters because it determines whether `X-User-ID` is compared against stored data.

- When `X-User-Role` is `FacultyMentor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMentor` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMentor` compare the resource's mentor foreign key to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Postdoc`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Postdoc` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Postdoc` compare the resource's postdoc foreign key to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PostdocAffairsOfficer`, `HRCompensationApprover`, `ExportControlReviewer`, or `InternationalScholarAdvisor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow step demands an audit anchor — as `prepared_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on the appointment and review records, as `salary_exception_approved_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `reviewer_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on the records those operators act upon — but it never compares such an identifier to any row's `id`.

Ownership is enforced as a distinct check from role membership. Six operations are ownership-guarded and are rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) when the acting identifier does not match: offer acceptance, development-document submission, bench-access grant creation, the mentor's reappointment recommendation, grievance filing, and the intellectual-property assignment signature. A `Postdoc` acting on an appointment, a grievance or an exit record must have an `X-User-ID` equal to the governing appointment's `postdoc_id`; a `FacultyMentor` acting on a development document, a bench-access grant or a reappointment review must have an `X-User-ID` equal to the governing appointment's `mentor_id`.

Read access is deliberately broad: every listed retrieval operation, and the logical-clock capability described in section 2.4, is available to all six roles. Write access is narrow and is spelled out per operation in the table in section 4.

The role assignment of the four opaque roles reflects their office function. A `PostdocAffairsOfficer` maintains the registries, drafts appointments, issues offers, activates and withdraws them, reviews development documents, opens and finalizes reappointment reviews, resolves grievances, and initiates exit processing. An `HRCompensationApprover` verifies and suspends awards and approves salary-minimum exceptions, and never prepares an appointment. An `ExportControlReviewer` records screening findings. An `InternationalScholarAdvisor` files and re-files petitions and records adjudications. A `FacultyMentor` submits development documents, requests bench access, and records the reappointment recommendation. A `Postdoc` accepts the offer, acknowledges development documents, files grievances, and signs the intellectual-property assignment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List operations are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific operation states otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the research-security screening history for an appointment, which is returned ascending by `cycle_index`.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters, where an operation offers them, narrow the matched set before `total_count` is computed.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record in this system — scholars, mentors, awards, laboratories, appointments, visa cases, development documents, screening reviews, bench-access grants, reappointment reviews, grievances and exit records alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. There is no type prefix and no domain-flavoured encoding; clients must treat it as opaque.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the record was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the record.

No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` and `version`; a mutation performed by a cascade counts as a mutation for both fields, exactly as a client-initiated write does. Recomputing a derived field — a rank ledger total, a salary floor, an encumbrance, a projected date — is a mutation of the record that holds it.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability, available to all six roles and intended for test driving, sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, after which every logical-clock read returns that instant. Every stamped instant in the system — `verified_at`, `filed_at`, `decided_at`, `approved_at`, `notice_issued_at`, `initiated_at`, `ip_assignment_signed_at`, and the `filed_at` of a grievance — is the logical clock's value at the moment of the write, and every deadline comparison (award expiry, grievance window, notice timeliness) is made against the same source.

### 2.5. The Two Independent State Dimensions of an Appointment

An appointment is unusual in this system because it carries **two state machines on the same row**, and they advance independently. `appointment_status` is the offer-and-employment dimension, with the values `DRAFT`, `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `NON_RENEWED` and `TERMINATED`; it starts at `DRAFT`, and `COMPLETED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `TERMINATED` are terminal. `compliance_status` is the research-security dimension, with the values `PENDING_REVIEW`, `CLEARED`, `RESTRICTED` and `BLOCKED`; it starts at `PENDING_REVIEW` and has no terminal value at all, because a cleared appointment can be pulled back into review at any renewal.

Neither dimension gates the other's progress except where a transition explicitly says so. An appointment may reach `OFFER_ACCEPTED` while `compliance_status` is still `PENDING_REVIEW`. Conversely `compliance_status` may regress from `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED` back to `PENDING_REVIEW`, or drop to `BLOCKED`, while `appointment_status` remains `ACTIVE`. The employment dimension is also not monotonic: `NON_RENEWED` returns to `ACTIVE` when a grievance is sustained, closing a cycle that most workflows never see.

The compliance dimension is driven exclusively by research-security screening outcomes and never by a direct client request. A screening review that clears drives the appointment to `CLEARED`; one that clears with conditions drives it to `RESTRICTED`; one that is denied drives it to `BLOCKED`. In each case the appointment's compliance value may only be recomputed once no screening review for that appointment remains in `QUEUED`, so an appointment with two open screening cycles keeps its `PENDING_REVIEW` posture until the last of them is decided.

### 2.6. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Actions

Most of the interesting behaviour in this system is not the primary write but the chain of consequences it sets off, and those chains run to a depth of five. The rules below govern all of them.

A cascade is **immediate and complete within the triggering request**. There is no deferred reconciliation and no background sweep: by the time a response is returned, every downstream record the chain touches has already been written, including records the client never named and cannot see in the response body. A client that needs to observe a cascade-created child — a visa case, a mentoring plan, a screening cycle, an exit record — discovers it through the reverse-lookup reads described in section 2.7.

Cascades are **ordered where order is observable**. When a chain both reverses a ledger entry and restores a stored value that the ledger entry was computed from, the reversal is applied against the pre-restoration value, so the ledger returns to exactly the figure it held before. The reinstatement chain in section 3.8 states the three-step order normatively; implementations that reorder those steps will drift the rank ledger.

Every release of a financial commitment is a **compensating action of the write that created it**, and the two must be exact inverses. Issuing an offer adds this appointment's `encumbrance_usd` to the award's `encumbered_usd`; every path out of a held state — withdrawal, completion, or compliance termination — subtracts the same figure again, sets the appointment's own `encumbrance_usd` to 0.00, and recomputes `available_balance_usd`. A release is never partial and never applied twice.

Finally, cascades **do not double-post**. Rank months are posted to a scholar's ledger exactly once per appointment term, at the moment the appointment leaves the held population for a terminal or non-renewed state. If an appointment reached a terminal state through one path, a later chain that would ordinarily post its months posts nothing, and the exit record's `final_months_posted` records 0 to make that explicit.

### 2.7. Reverse-Lookup Reads and Left-to-Right Identifier Validation

The resource topology is flat: scholars, mentors, awards, laboratories, appointments, bench-access grants and reappointment reviews are all root-level collections joined by foreign keys, not nested under one another. Sub-resource read paths exist for exactly one reason — to make cascade-created children reachable. The visa case, the mentoring plan and individual development plan, the screening cycles and the exit record are all created by cascade and never appear in the response of the request that created them, so each has a dedicated reverse-lookup read keyed on the appointment, and grievances have one keyed on the review they contest. Appointments themselves are discoverable by any of their four foreign keys through filter parameters, and bench-access grants by either of theirs.

When a path contains more than one identifier, the server validates them **strictly left to right** and returns the 404 belonging to the first identifier that does not resolve, before evaluating anything further to the right and before touching the request body. Body-borne foreign keys are validated after all path identifiers, in the order in which the fields are declared for the entity being written. The 404 codes are entity-specific: `ERR_POSTDOC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABORATORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND` (which also covers an unresolvable `predecessor_appointment_id`), `ERR_VISA_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVELOPMENT_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPORT_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REAPPOINTMENT_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_EXIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. Money, Months and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are decimal quantities carried to exactly **2 decimal places**, and every rounding operation in this specification is **round half up**. Amounts are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, never as strings. Internal arithmetic must be performed with decimal semantics; binary floating-point accumulation that shifts a cent is a defect.

Where a calculation prescribes an intermediate rounding, that rounding is part of the contract and may not be optimised away. The encumbrance computation in section 3.3 is the sharpest case: the prorated base is rounded to cents, the fringe amount is rounded to cents, and only then are the two added. Multiplying an unrounded prorated salary by one plus the fringe rate and rounding once is a different calculation and can differ by a cent.

Two other precision regimes apply. Rank and term quantities — `term_months`, `months_in_rank_at_start`, `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end`, `total_months_in_rank`, `service_months_at_review`, `notice_days_required`, `term_extension_days` — are whole integers with no rounding anywhere; they are produced by the pinned month arithmetic in section 3.2, never by dividing a day count. The research-security `risk_score` is the one quantity carried to **1 decimal place**: its individual weighted components are each rounded to 2 decimal places, the components are then summed, and only the sum is rounded to one decimal place.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on the Appointment Record

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the appointment they target, and in both the guard is a required field rather than an optional one.

`POST /appointments/{id}/salary-exception` requires `expected_appointment_version` in its body, carrying the `version` of the appointment as the approver last read it. If that value does not equal the appointment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check cannot be replaced by a state precondition, because a later exception legitimately supersedes an earlier one: a fresh approval that carries the current `version` succeeds and overwrites `salary_exception_floor_usd`. The guard exists solely to guarantee that the approver priced the exception against the salary, the computed floor and the funding posture they actually observed.

`POST /lab-access-grants` likewise requires `expected_appointment_version` in its body, carrying the `version` of the appointment named by `appointment_id`. A mismatch with that appointment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple grants against one appointment are entirely legitimate, so a second create carrying the current `version` succeeds; the guard exists because the appointment's `compliance_status` may have regressed between the mentor's read and the mentor's write, and the mentor must re-observe the posture that authorises bench access. A successful grant creation is itself a mutation of the appointment and increments its `version` by 1, so a mentor requesting a second grant must re-read the appointment first.

No other operation accepts a version field, and no other operation performs a version check. Where an operation is not named above, omitting a version is not merely permitted — there is no such field to send.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four separations are enforced, all of them on `X-User-ID` rather than on `X-User-Role`, because in each case both parties hold the same role. All four are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

- The `HRCompensationApprover` who approves a salary-minimum exception must not be the appointment's `prepared_by_user_id`.
- The `PostdocAffairsOfficer` who finalizes a reappointment review must not be its `recommended_by_user_id`.
- The `PostdocAffairsOfficer` who resolves a grievance must not be the contested review's `finalized_by_user_id`.
- The `ExportControlReviewer` who decides a screening review must be neither the appointment's `prepared_by_user_id` nor its `mentor_id`.

### 2.11. The Validation Precedence Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the **highest-precedence** rule that the request violates. Precedence is a total order, listed below from strongest to weakest. The shape of the ladder encodes the two design commitments from section 1 — research security sits above every business check because laboratory exposure is irreversible, and fiscal checks sit at the bottom because money is recoverable.

1. Authentication: both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every operation.
2. Role authorisation: the acting role appears in the operation's authorised list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403), on every operation.
3. Existence of every referenced identifier, path identifiers first in path order and then body foreign keys in declaration order — the entity-specific 404 codes listed in section 2.7, on every operation that carries a path identifier or a foreign-key body field.
4. Ownership for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), on offer acceptance, development-document submission, bench-access grant creation, the mentor's reappointment recommendation, grievance filing, and the intellectual-property assignment.
5. Request shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every write operation. This covers a missing required field, an enum field whose value is not a declared member, a date that is not ISO 8601, a negative numeric field, a `term_end_date` that is not strictly after `term_start_date`, an `access_end_date` that is not strictly after `access_start_date`, and a `bench_seats` value below 1.
6. State precondition: the target record is in a from-state that declares the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), on every action operation. A terminal state accepts nothing, and re-issuing an already-applied trigger is refused by this rule.
7. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_appointment_version` equals the appointment's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (412), on salary-exception approval and bench-access grant creation.
8. Research-security admissibility of bench access: the appointment's `compliance_status` is `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED` — `ERR_EXPORT_CONTROL_BLOCKED` (422), on bench-access grant creation. A `compliance_status` of `PENDING_REVIEW` or `BLOCKED` is refused by this rule and by this rule alone. The additional limits that a `RESTRICTED` posture imposes — biosafety level and seat count — are deliberately **not** part of this check; they sit one rung lower, so a `RESTRICTED` appointment asking for a BSL2 bench passes rule 8 and fails rule 9.
9. Restricted-posture limits: a `RESTRICTED` appointment may not take seats in a `BSL2` or `BSL3` laboratory and may not take more than one bench seat anywhere — `ERR_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTION_VIOLATED` (422), on bench-access grant creation.
10. Laboratory availability: the target laboratory's `status` is `OPEN` — `ERR_LAB_CLOSED` (409), on bench-access grant creation.
11. Funding posture at offer time: the award's `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` **and** the appointment's `term_start_date` falls on or after the award's `award_start_date` — `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_VERIFIED` (422), on offer issuance.
12. Class-to-award pairing: `employment_class` of `FELLOWSHIP` is permitted only on an award whose `funding_type` is `EXTERNAL_FELLOWSHIP` or `INSTITUTIONAL_FELLOWSHIP`, and `EMPLOYEE` only on an award whose `funding_type` is `FEDERAL_GRANT` or `FOUNDATION_GRANT` — `ERR_FUNDING_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422), on appointment drafting.
13. Title-to-class pairing: `POSTDOC_SCHOLAR_EMPLOYEE` and `POSTDOC_ASSOCIATE` require `employment_class` `EMPLOYEE`, and `POSTDOC_FELLOW` requires `FELLOWSHIP` — `ERR_TITLE_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422), on appointment drafting.
14. Mentor training currency: the mentor's `mentor_training_valid_through` is on or after the appointment's `term_start_date` — `ERR_MENTOR_TRAINING_LAPSED` (422), on appointment drafting and on bench-access grant creation, and also on activation, where the same requirement is an activation precondition carrying the same code.
15. Total-years-in-rank ceiling: `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end` must not exceed 60 months; the boundary value of exactly 60 is permitted and 61 is not — `ERR_RANK_YEARS_EXCEEDED` (422), on appointment drafting and on reappointment finalization.
16. Term length: `term_months` must lie between 12 and 36 months inclusive — `ERR_TERM_LENGTH_INVALID` (422), on appointment drafting and on activation, where the value tested at activation is the term length as recomputed after any visa-driven deferral.
17. Premium processing availability: a `processing_track` of `PREMIUM` is available only when the case's `visa_category` is `H1B` or `O1` — `ERR_PREMIUM_TRACK_UNAVAILABLE` (422), on petition filing.
18. Request-for-evidence rounds: `rfe_round_count` must be strictly below 2 before another request for evidence may be recorded — `ERR_RFE_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on visa adjudication.
19. Document revision rounds: `revision_round` must be strictly below 3 before another return-for-revision may be recorded — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on development-document review.
20. Funder document completeness at activation: a `MENTORING_PLAN` must be `APPROVED` when the award sets `requires_mentoring_plan`, and an `IDP` must be `APPROVED` when the award sets `requires_idp` — `ERR_DEVELOPMENT_DOCS_INCOMPLETE` (422), on activation.
21. Scholar co-signature: an `IDP` may reach `APPROVED` only while `postdoc_acknowledged` is true — `ERR_IDP_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (422), on development-document review.
22. Visa approval at activation: a sponsorship-requiring appointment may be activated only when its latest visa case is `APPROVED` — `ERR_VISA_NOT_APPROVED` (422), on activation.
23. Segregation of duties as set out in section 2.10 — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on salary-exception approval, reappointment finalization, grievance resolution and screening decisions.
24. Lawful start: for a sponsorship-requiring appointment, `term_start_date` must be on or after the latest visa case's `earliest_lawful_start_date` — `ERR_START_BEFORE_LAWFUL_DATE` (422), on activation.
25. Term overlap: the drafted closed interval from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` must not overlap any other appointment of the same scholar whose `appointment_status` is `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`; touching endpoints, where the new start equals an existing end, count as an overlap — `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409), on appointment drafting.
26. Grievance window: a grievance may be filed only at or before the review's `grievance_window_end`, inclusive to the millisecond; a review whose `grievance_window_end` is null — which is every review status other than `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED` — has no open window and is refused by this same rule — `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422), on grievance filing.
27. Decontamination buffer collision: the requested grant's buffered leading edge must not collide with an incumbent grant that the unbuffered access window would have left disjoint — `ERR_LAB_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409), on bench-access grant creation.
28. Aggregate bench capacity across the requested effective interval — `ERR_LAB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on bench-access grant creation.
29. Mentor load: the mentor's `active_postdoc_count` must be strictly below `max_concurrent_postdocs` at the instant the offer is issued — `ERR_MENTOR_LOAD_EXCEEDED` (409), on offer issuance, and also on activation, where the same requirement is an activation precondition carrying the same code.
30. Salary floor: `annual_salary_usd` must be greater than or equal to the effective floor, which is `salary_exception_floor_usd` when one has been approved and `salary_floor_usd` otherwise; exact equality passes — `ERR_SALARY_BELOW_FLOOR` (422), on offer issuance.
31. Award budget: the new encumbrance must not drive the award's `encumbered_usd` above its `annual_budget_usd`; exact equality passes — `ERR_FUNDING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422), on offer issuance.
32. Singleton children: at most one non-terminal reappointment review may exist per appointment — `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_OPEN` (409) on opening a review — and at most one exit record may exist per appointment — `ERR_EXIT_ALREADY_INITIATED` (409) on initiating exit.
33. Visa eligibility: category selection must yield a non-empty candidate set for a sponsorship-requiring scholar — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VISA_CATEGORY` (422), on offer acceptance.
34. Award currency at verification: an award may be verified only while its `award_end_date` is on or after the logical-clock date — `ERR_AWARD_EXPIRED` (422), on award verification.
35. Award-term overlap: the appointment term must overlap the award window by at least one inclusive day, without which no encumbrance can be computed — `ERR_AWARD_TERM_NO_OVERLAP` (422), on offer issuance.
36. Exception floor bounds: `exception_floor_usd` must be at least 54000.00 and strictly below the appointment's computed `salary_floor_usd` — `ERR_EXCEPTION_FLOOR_INVALID` (422), on salary-exception approval.

Three collisions are common enough in practice to be worth stating explicitly, and in each the higher-precedence rule is the only error reported. On offer issuance, an unverified award combined with a salary below the floor yields `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_VERIFIED`, not `ERR_SALARY_BELOW_FLOOR`. On bench-access grant creation, an appointment still in `PENDING_REVIEW` asking for more seats than the laboratory has yields `ERR_EXPORT_CONTROL_BLOCKED`, not `ERR_LAB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On appointment drafting, a term that both breaches the rank ceiling and overlaps an existing held appointment yields `ERR_RANK_YEARS_EXCEEDED`, not `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the Registries: Scholars, Mentors, Awards and Laboratory Space

Nothing can be appointed until four registries exist, and all four are maintained by a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`.

A **scholar record** is registered with the person's `full_name` — non-empty and no longer than 120 characters — an institutional `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all scholar records, a `citizenship_country` given as exactly two uppercase letters in the ISO-3166 alpha-2 style, a `requires_sponsorship` flag that is true when the scholar needs institutional visa sponsorship in order to work, and the `phd_award_date` on which the doctorate was conferred as an ISO 8601 calendar date. Four further inputs are optional and each defaults to a value that assumes the least complicated case. `prior_external_months_in_rank` records months already served in postdoctoral rank at other institutions; it defaults to 0, may not be negative, and may not exceed 60 — the same ceiling that governs the institution's own rank policy. `o1_endorsement_on_file` defaults to false and is set true when an extraordinary-ability endorsement packet has been filed; it is the sole gate on one visa category. `j1_two_year_bar_flag` defaults to false and is set true when the scholar is subject to the two-year home-residency bar, which disqualifies a different category outright. `restricted_party_flag` defaults to false and records whether the scholar appeared on a restricted-party screening list at intake. On registration the scholar's `status` is set to `PROSPECTIVE` and the realized rank ledger `total_months_in_rank` is seeded to whatever `prior_external_months_in_rank` was supplied. The `status` values are `PROSPECTIVE`, `ACTIVE` and `SEPARATED`, and `SEPARATED` is terminal.

A **mentor record** carries the mentor's `full_name`, the `department` that owns the mentor appointment, and a `mentor_training_valid_through` date through which mandatory mentor training remains current. `max_concurrent_postdocs` is the institutional ceiling on simultaneously active appointments this mentor may hold; it defaults to 3 and must be at least 1 and no more than 12. Against it the server maintains `active_postdoc_count`, which is the number of appointments whose `mentor_id` is this mentor and whose `appointment_status` is exactly `ACTIVE`. It begins at 0 on registration, is incremented when an appointment activates, and is decremented when an appointment completes or is terminated for compliance.

An **award record** describes the money. It is registered with the `sponsor_name` of the awarding sponsor or fellowship programme, a `funding_type` of `FEDERAL_GRANT`, `FOUNDATION_GRANT`, `EXTERNAL_FELLOWSHIP` or `INSTITUTIONAL_FELLOWSHIP`, a `salary_floor_policy` of either `NIH_SCALE` or `INSTITUTIONAL_MIN` that selects which floor table the compensation calculation reads, an `award_start_date` as the first day the award may be charged, an `award_end_date` as the last such day, and an `annual_budget_usd` giving salary-and-fringe budget authority for the award year — strictly greater than zero and carried to two decimal places. Two optional booleans record funder mandates and both default to false: `requires_mentoring_plan` when the funder demands a mentoring plan, and `requires_idp` when it demands an individual development plan. At registration the award's `verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED`, its `encumbered_usd` is 0.00, and its `available_balance_usd` equals `annual_budget_usd`.

The award's verification posture is the `HRCompensationApprover`'s to set, through a single operation that takes a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `SUSPEND`. Moving `UNVERIFIED` to `VERIFIED`, or `SUSPENDED` back to `VERIFIED`, requires a `decision` of `VERIFY` and is permitted only while the award's `award_end_date` is on or after the logical-clock date; an expired award is refused with `ERR_AWARD_EXPIRED`. A successful verification stamps `verified_at` with the logical-clock instant and `verified_by_user_id` with the approver's `X-User-ID`; both are null until the first verification. Moving `VERIFIED` to `SUSPENDED` requires a `decision` of `SUSPEND` and carries no date test at all — a suspension may be recorded against an award that has already expired. Suspension bars any new offer from being issued against the award but leaves every existing encumbrance untouched: `encumbered_usd` and `available_balance_usd` are not disturbed. Any other combination of current posture and `decision` — a `VERIFY` against an already `VERIFIED` award, or a `SUSPEND` against an `UNVERIFIED` one — is refused as an invalid transition.

A **laboratory record** describes physical space. It carries a `name` identifying the space, a `biosafety_level` of `BSL1`, `BSL2` or `BSL3` describing its containment level, a `bench_capacity` of between 1 and 40 giving the number of seats that may be simultaneously occupied, an optional `decontamination_buffer_days` between 0 and 30 that defaults to 0 and is applied to the leading edge of every access interval in the space, and an optional `status` of `OPEN` or `CLOSED` that defaults to `OPEN` and determines whether the space accepts new access grants at all.

Because the topology is flat, the registries are also the discovery surface. Appointments may be listed filtered by `postdoc_id`, `mentor_id`, `funding_source_id`, `predecessor_appointment_id`, `appointment_status` and `compliance_status` in any combination; bench-access grants by `appointment_id`, `laboratory_id` and `status`; development documents for an appointment by `doc_type` and `status`; screening cycles for an appointment and grievances against a review by `status`; and reappointment reviews by `appointment_id` and `status`.

### 3.2. Drafting an Appointment: the Rank Ledger, Term Length and Classification Pairing

A `PostdocAffairsOfficer` drafts an appointment by naming the three parties and the shape of the term: `postdoc_id` for the scholar being appointed, `mentor_id` for the supervising mentor of record, `funding_source_id` for the award that will carry salary and fringe, a `title_classification` of `POSTDOC_FELLOW`, `POSTDOC_SCHOLAR_EMPLOYEE` or `POSTDOC_ASSOCIATE`, an `employment_class` of either `EMPLOYEE` or `FELLOWSHIP`, the `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` bounding the term as ISO 8601 dates, and the `annual_salary_usd` twelve-month salary or stipend rate being offered, strictly greater than zero and carried to two decimal places. One optional field, `predecessor_appointment_id`, names the prior term that this appointment continues; it defaults to null for a first term and, when supplied, must reference an appointment that already exists. The draft is created with `appointment_status` `DRAFT`, `compliance_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the drafting officer's `X-User-ID` — the value that anchors every segregation-of-duties check later in the appointment's life.

Two pairing rules are checked at drafting time and nowhere else. The `employment_class` must agree with the award's `funding_type`: `FELLOWSHIP` is permitted only on an `EXTERNAL_FELLOWSHIP` or `INSTITUTIONAL_FELLOWSHIP` award, and `EMPLOYEE` only on a `FEDERAL_GRANT` or `FOUNDATION_GRANT` award; a mismatch is `ERR_FUNDING_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The `title_classification` must in turn agree with the `employment_class`: `POSTDOC_SCHOLAR_EMPLOYEE` and `POSTDOC_ASSOCIATE` both require `EMPLOYEE`, while `POSTDOC_FELLOW` requires `FELLOWSHIP`; a mismatch is `ERR_TITLE_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Drafting also requires the named mentor's `mentor_training_valid_through` to fall on or after the appointment's `term_start_date`, failing which the draft is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_TRAINING_LAPSED`.

**Month arithmetic is pinned**, because every rank and notice figure in the system depends on it and a day-count approximation drifts. The number of whole months between a start date and an end date is computed as follows: multiply the end date's year by twelve and add its month number; do the same for the start date; subtract the start figure from the end figure; then add one further month when the end date's day-of-month is greater than or equal to the start date's day-of-month, and add nothing otherwise. That single rule produces `term_months` from the appointment's own `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, and it is reused unchanged wherever else a month count is needed.

The scholar's standing in rank when this term begins is `months_in_rank_at_start`, and it is assembled by walking the predecessor chain. Starting from this appointment's `predecessor_appointment_id`, the server visits that appointment, then that appointment's own `predecessor_appointment_id`, and so on until it reaches a null link. Because a predecessor may only be named at creation and must already exist, the chain is finite and cannot contain a cycle; because the link is single-valued, the walk is in linked-list order and no tie-break arises. Each appointment visited contributes the month count between its own `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, but only when its `appointment_status` is `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED`, `NON_RENEWED` or `TERMINATED`; a predecessor still in `DRAFT`, `OFFER_ISSUED` or `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, or one that ended in `WITHDRAWN`, contributes zero. `months_in_rank_at_start` is then the scholar's `prior_external_months_in_rank` plus that sum, and `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end` is `months_in_rank_at_start` plus this appointment's own `term_months`.

The scholar's own `total_months_in_rank` is a different quantity, and the difference is deliberate. It is the **realized** ledger: the scholar's `prior_external_months_in_rank` plus the sum of `term_months` over every appointment of that scholar whose `appointment_status` is `COMPLETED`, `NON_RENEWED` or `TERMINATED`. An appointment that is currently `ACTIVE` therefore counts toward a successor's `months_in_rank_at_start` but not toward the scholar's `total_months_in_rank`; the two metrics diverge for exactly as long as a predecessor is in flight. The realized ledger is maintained by cascade rather than recomputed on read — it is credited when an appointment reaches `COMPLETED`, `NON_RENEWED` or `TERMINATED`, and debited again when a sustained grievance returns a `NON_RENEWED` appointment to `ACTIVE`.

Two ceilings bound the draft. `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end` may not exceed 60 months — five years in rank across all institutions — and the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 60 is accepted and 61 is refused with `ERR_RANK_YEARS_EXCEEDED`. Independently, `term_months` must be at least 12 months and at most 36 months, both inclusive; anything shorter or longer is refused with `ERR_TERM_LENGTH_INVALID`. A drafted term also may not collide with the scholar's other commitments: the closed interval from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` must not overlap the closed term interval of any other appointment of the same scholar whose `appointment_status` is `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, and a shared endpoint counts as an overlap — a new term starting on the exact day an existing term ends is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. Where a single draft breaches both the rank ceiling and the overlap rule, the rank ceiling is the error reported.

Drafting is the first point at which the compensation figures are computed. The rank quantities above are produced first because the salary floor depends on them; the policy floor and the benefits determination described in the next section are then computed and stored on the draft, before any offer exists.

### 3.3. Compensation Policy: the Salary Floor, Exceptions, Benefits and the Award Encumbrance

The institution does not police salaries directly; it polices a **floor**, recomputed from policy tables every time the appointment's rank standing or classification could have moved. The stored result is `salary_floor_usd`, and it is derived in four steps.

First the appointment's experience band is fixed: divide `months_in_rank_at_start` by twelve and discard any remainder, then take whichever is smaller, that quotient or four. The band is therefore an integer from 0 through 4, and a scholar with 48 or more months already in rank sits in band 4 however long the prior service actually was. Second, a policy base is read from the table the award nominates through `salary_floor_policy`. Under `NIH_SCALE` the five bands read 61008, 61428, 62124, 64536 and 67284 US dollars in band order. Under `INSTITUTIONAL_MIN` they read 58000, 60000, 62000, 62000 and 62000 — note that the institutional table is flat from band 2 upward, so two scholars three years apart in experience can share a floor. Third, the base is adjusted for classification and title: it is multiplied by 1.00 when `employment_class` is `EMPLOYEE` and by 0.95 when it is `FELLOWSHIP`, and a title uplift of 3000 US dollars is then added when and only when `title_classification` is `POSTDOC_SCHOLAR_EMPLOYEE`, and nothing is added for the other two titles. That product-plus-uplift figure is rounded half up to two decimal places. Fourth, an absolute institutional stipend floor of 54000.00 US dollars is applied as a lower bound: `salary_floor_usd` is whichever is larger, the rounded figure from step three or 54000.00.

The floor that is actually **enforced** may be lower than the computed one, because an `HRCompensationApprover` may approve a documented exception. That approval carries three body fields: `expected_appointment_version` as described in section 2.9, an `exception_floor_usd` naming the substitute floor, and a `justification` recording the rationale in writing. The substitute floor must be at least 54000.00 US dollars and must be strictly below the appointment's computed `salary_floor_usd` — an "exception" at or above the policy floor is meaningless and is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_FLOOR_INVALID`. On approval the server writes `salary_exception_floor_usd` from the supplied `exception_floor_usd` and stamps `salary_exception_approved_by_user_id` with the approver's `X-User-ID`; both fields are null until an exception has been approved. The approver may not be the officer recorded in `prepared_by_user_id`. Wherever this document speaks of the **effective floor**, it means `salary_exception_floor_usd` when that field is not null and `salary_floor_usd` otherwise.

Benefits eligibility is a pure function of classification, term length and award family, and it is stored on the appointment as `benefits_package`. An `EMPLOYEE` appointment whose `term_months` is 12 or more is `EMPLOYEE_FULL`; an `EMPLOYEE` appointment whose `term_months` is fewer than 12 is `EMPLOYEE_LIMITED`. A `FELLOWSHIP` appointment on an award whose `funding_type` is `EXTERNAL_FELLOWSHIP` is `FELLOWSHIP_STIPEND_ONLY`; a `FELLOWSHIP` appointment on an award whose `funding_type` is `INSTITUTIONAL_FELLOWSHIP` is `FELLOWSHIP_INSTITUTIONAL`. In every other combination the value is `NONE`. That fallback is unreachable while the class-to-award pairing rule holds, and it exists as the declared answer for a `FELLOWSHIP` classification sitting on a grant-type award. `benefits_package` is recomputed whenever `term_months` or `employment_class` changes, which in practice means every recompute triggered by a term deferral, a notice extension, or a reinstatement.

The **encumbrance** is what the appointment actually costs the award, and it is computed in a fixed order that must not be rearranged.

Step one prorates the salary over the window in which the term and the award actually coincide. The overlap begins on the later of `term_start_date` and the award's `award_start_date` and ends on the earlier of `term_end_date` and the award's `award_end_date`. Both endpoints are inclusive, so the day count is the number of days between them plus one, and a single shared day counts as one day. A count of zero or less means the term and the award window share no day at all, in which case no encumbrance can be computed and offer issuance is refused with `ERR_AWARD_TERM_NO_OVERLAP`. Otherwise the prorated base is `annual_salary_usd` multiplied by that inclusive day count, divided by 365, and rounded half up to two decimal places.

Step two selects the fringe rate, and **the order here is the substance of the rule**. A `FELLOWSHIP` appointment takes a flat fringe rate of 0.075 regardless of amount. Otherwise the rate is banded on the prorated base from step one: 0.28 while the base is below 40000.00 US dollars, 0.31 while the base is below 65000.00, and 0.335 at 65000.00 or above. The band is keyed on the **prorated base**, never on the eventual total, even though the band thresholds superficially read like total-cost thresholds; keying them on the total would make the definition circular. The selected rate is stored as `fringe_rate`.

Step three rounds the fringe amount before it is added to anything: multiply the already-rounded base by `fringe_rate` and round the product half up to two decimal places. Step four adds the already-rounded base to the already-rounded fringe amount and rounds the sum half up to two decimal places, giving the appointment's `encumbrance_usd`. Multiplying the unrounded prorated salary by one plus the fringe rate and rounding a single time is a different calculation and is not permitted.

Step five rolls the appointment figures up onto the award. The award's `encumbered_usd` is the sum of `encumbrance_usd` over the appointments charged to that award whose `appointment_status` is `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `NON_RENEWED`, rounded half up to two decimal places; appointments in `DRAFT`, `WITHDRAWN`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` contribute 0.00. Because every addend was already rounded to cents in step four, the sum has no ordering sensitivity. The award's `available_balance_usd` is then `annual_budget_usd` less `encumbered_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

**Issuing the offer** is the moment all of this becomes binding, and it is a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`'s action on an appointment whose `appointment_status` is `DRAFT`, advancing it `DRAFT` → `OFFER_ISSUED`. The operation takes no request body. Five preconditions guard it. The award must be `VERIFIED` and the appointment's `term_start_date` must fall on or after the award's `award_start_date`, failing either of which the answer is `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_VERIFIED`. The term and the award window must share at least one inclusive day, else `ERR_AWARD_TERM_NO_OVERLAP`. The mentor's `active_postdoc_count` must be strictly below their `max_concurrent_postdocs` at that instant, else `ERR_MENTOR_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. `annual_salary_usd` must be greater than or equal to the effective floor, with exact equality accepted, else `ERR_SALARY_BELOW_FLOOR`. And the award must have room: the newly computed encumbrance must not drive `encumbered_usd` above `annual_budget_usd`, with exact equality accepted, else `ERR_FUNDING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Where the award is unverified and the salary is also below the floor, the funding answer is the one returned.

Issuance then recomputes and writes, in this order: `salary_floor_usd`, `term_months`, `months_in_rank_at_start`, `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end`, `benefits_package`, `fringe_rate` and `encumbrance_usd`. The award's `encumbered_usd` is increased by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd` and its `available_balance_usd` is recomputed from `annual_budget_usd`. Two further cascades fire in the same request. A research-security screening cycle is created for the appointment with `cycle_index` 1 and `status` `QUEUED`. And the funder's development-document mandates are materialised: a document with `doc_type` `MENTORING_PLAN` and `status` `DRAFT` is created when the award's `requires_mentoring_plan` is true, and a document with `doc_type` `IDP` and `status` `DRAFT` is created when the award's `requires_idp` is true. An award that sets neither flag produces no documents; an award that sets both produces two. The appointment's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.4. Acceptance, Visa Category Selection and the Sponsorship Pipeline

Acceptance is the scholar's own act. A `Postdoc` whose `X-User-ID` equals the appointment's `postdoc_id` accepts an appointment sitting in `OFFER_ISSUED`, advancing it `OFFER_ISSUED` → `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, and the appointment's `version` increments by 1. The only body field is an optional `processing_track`, which is either `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM` and defaults to `STANDARD` when omitted. For a scholar whose `requires_sponsorship` is false, acceptance does nothing further: **no visa case row is created at all**, and every later sponsorship gate simply does not apply to that appointment.

For a scholar whose `requires_sponsorship` is true, acceptance runs **category selection** and creates the visa case. Selection is a filter followed by a ranking. Four candidate categories are tested independently, and each is admitted to the candidate set only if its own predicate holds:

- `TN` is admitted when the scholar's `citizenship_country` is `CA` or `MX` and the appointment's `employment_class` is `EMPLOYEE`.
- `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR` is admitted when the scholar's `j1_two_year_bar_flag` is false and the appointment's `term_months` is 60 or fewer.
- `H1B` is admitted when the appointment's `employment_class` is `EMPLOYEE` and its `term_months` is 12 or more.
- `O1` is admitted when the scholar's `o1_endorsement_on_file` is true.

Among the admitted candidates the server selects the one with the lowest priority number, where `TN` is 1, `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR` is 2, `H1B` is 3 and `O1` is 4. Because those four priority numbers are distinct integers, the ranking cannot tie; there is no secondary key and record identifiers are never consulted. The selected value is written to the case as `visa_category`, which is a server-computed field and is never accepted from the client. If no candidate is admitted, acceptance itself is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VISA_CATEGORY` — consider a scholar from a country other than Canada or Mexico, subject to the two-year bar, with no extraordinary-ability endorsement, appointed on a `FELLOWSHIP` basis: every predicate fails and there is nothing to sponsor. Additionally, when the accepting scholar asks for the `PREMIUM` track, the selected category must be one of the two that premium processing supports, `H1B` or `O1`; asking for `PREMIUM` on a `TN` or `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR` selection is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_TRACK_UNAVAILABLE`.

The created case records the appointment through `appointment_id`, carries the selected `visa_category` and the requested `processing_track`, and starts in `status` `DOCS_PENDING`. Its pipeline values are `DOCS_PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `RFE_RECEIVED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `VOIDED`, of which the last three are terminal. `filed_at` is null while the case is `DOCS_PENDING`, `rfe_round_count` starts at 0, `projection_variant` starts null, and `decided_at` is null until an adjudication lands.

**Filing the petition** is an `InternationalScholarAdvisor`'s action, and the same capability serves both the first filing and every re-filing after a request for evidence. It may be exercised only while the governing appointment's `appointment_status` is `OFFER_ACCEPTED`; at any other appointment state it is refused as an invalid transition. An optional `processing_track` in the body switches the track; omitting it leaves the current track unchanged. Filing stamps `filed_at` with the logical-clock instant and recomputes the two projected dates.

Which projection applies depends on the state the case was in when it was filed, and the answer is recorded in `projection_variant`. A filing that moves the case `DOCS_PENDING` → `SUBMITTED` sets `projection_variant` to `INITIAL_FILING`. A filing that moves it `RFE_RECEIVED` → `SUBMITTED` sets `projection_variant` to `POST_RFE_REFILING` and additionally increases `rfe_round_count` by 1; it is permitted only while `rfe_round_count` is strictly below 2, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_RFE_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`.

The projection itself begins from a base processing duration keyed on the category and the track. On the `STANDARD` track the base is 10 days for `TN`, 21 days for `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR`, 120 days for `H1B` and 75 days for `O1`. On the `PREMIUM` track it is 15 days for both `H1B` and `O1`, the only two categories the premium track serves. Under the `INITIAL_FILING` variant, `projected_decision_date` is the calendar date of `filed_at` plus that base number of days, with no extension applied. Under the `POST_RFE_REFILING` variant, the base is first multiplied by 1.5 and rounded **up** to a whole number of days, and that extended count is added to the calendar date of `filed_at`. Processing time that already elapsed before the request for evidence is **discarded, not credited**: for the same category and track the two variants project different dates from an identical filing instant — a `STANDARD` `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR` case projects 21 days out on an initial filing and 32 days out on a post-evidence re-filing. In both variants `earliest_lawful_start_date` is `projected_decision_date` plus a further 10 days of activation lead time.

**Recording the adjudication** is also the advisor's action and takes an `outcome` of `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `RFE`, plus an optional `decision_note`. An `RFE` outcome moves the case `SUBMITTED` → `RFE_RECEIVED` and requires `rfe_round_count` to be strictly below 2, failing which the answer is `ERR_RFE_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. Recording the request for evidence deliberately leaves `projected_decision_date` and `earliest_lawful_start_date` untouched; they move only when the response is actually filed.

An `APPROVED` outcome moves the case `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and sets off the longest cascade in the system. `decided_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant. The projection is then **replaced by the realized decision**: `earliest_lawful_start_date` is recomputed as the calendar date of `decided_at` plus 10 days of activation lead time, discarding whatever the projection had said. If that recomputed date falls after the appointment's `term_start_date`, the appointment's `term_start_date` is deferred to equal it. Because the term start moved, the appointment's `term_months`, `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end`, `benefits_package`, `fringe_rate` and `encumbrance_usd` are all recomputed; because `encumbrance_usd` changed, the award's `encumbered_usd` and `available_balance_usd` are recomputed in turn. And if the deferral has shortened the term below the 12-month minimum, the appointment cannot proceed: it advances `OFFER_ACCEPTED` → `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` set to `VISA_DELAY_TERM_TOO_SHORT`, the award's `encumbered_usd` is reduced by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd` which is itself set to 0.00 with `available_balance_usd` recomputed, every development document for the appointment in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` becomes `VOIDED`, and every screening cycle for the appointment still in `QUEUED` becomes `VOIDED`. A deferral that leaves the term at 12 months or longer stops the chain one link earlier and the appointment remains `OFFER_ACCEPTED`.

A `DENIED` outcome moves the case `SUBMITTED` → `DENIED`, stamps `decided_at`, and withdraws the appointment: `appointment_status` advances `OFFER_ACCEPTED` → `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` set to `VISA_DENIED`, the award's `encumbered_usd` is reduced by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd` which is set to 0.00 with `available_balance_usd` recomputed, every development document in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` becomes `VOIDED`, and every `QUEUED` screening cycle becomes `VOIDED`. The denied case itself stays `DENIED`; it is not voided.

A case reaches `VOIDED` only by cascade, when the appointment it belongs to is withdrawn by an officer while the case is in `DOCS_PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `RFE_RECEIVED`. Voiding a case has no further consequences of its own.

### 3.5. Funder Mandates: the Mentoring Plan and the Individual Development Plan

Development documents are never created by a client. They appear only as a consequence of offer issuance, one row per mandate the award declares, each carrying the `appointment_id` of the appointment it belongs to and a `doc_type` of either `MENTORING_PLAN` or `IDP`. Like `visa_category`, `doc_type` is server-assigned and is not accepted from a request. A document's `status` runs through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, `APPROVED` and `VOIDED`, with `APPROVED` and `VOIDED` terminal, and it starts at `DRAFT`.

Authoring is the mentor's responsibility. A `FacultyMentor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the governing appointment's `mentor_id` submits the document, moving it `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`, and may do so only while that appointment's `appointment_status` is `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`. The submission body carries `content_summary`, the mentor-authored summary of the plan being put forward for review; a submission may additionally carry `postdoc_acknowledged` to record the scholar's co-signature, in which case the supplied value is stored, and when that field is absent the existing value stands. Submission also stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the submitting mentor's `X-User-ID`; that field is null while the document is in `DRAFT`.

The scholar's co-signature has its own capability. A `Postdoc` acknowledging a document sets its `postdoc_acknowledged` to true and **does not change the document's `status`** — acknowledgement is orthogonal to the review pipeline and may be recorded before or after submission. The field defaults to false on creation.

Review belongs to a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`, who acts on a document in `SUBMITTED` with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN` and an optional `reviewer_note`. A `RETURN` moves the document `SUBMITTED` → `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` and increases `revision_round` by 1; it is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly below 3, and a further return beyond that point is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. An `APPROVE` moves the document `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and stamps `approved_at` with the logical-clock instant; `approved_at` is null until then. One extra condition attaches to approval and only to documents whose `doc_type` is `IDP`: the individual development plan may reach `APPROVED` only while `postdoc_acknowledged` is true, and an attempt to approve an unacknowledged `IDP` is refused with `ERR_IDP_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. A `MENTORING_PLAN` carries no such requirement and may be approved regardless of the acknowledgement flag.

The revision loop closes through the same submission capability the mentor used for the first draft: a document in `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` returns to `SUBMITTED` when the mentor re-submits, again writing `content_summary`, again honouring a supplied `postdoc_acknowledged`, again stamping `submitted_by_user_id`, and again requiring the mentor's `X-User-ID` to match the appointment's `mentor_id`. Re-submission is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly below 3. A document reaches `VOIDED` only by cascade, when the appointment is withdrawn while the document sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`; an already `APPROVED` document is left alone.

### 3.6. Research Security: Screening, Risk Scoring and Bench Access

A screening cycle is created by cascade, never by a client: once at offer issuance with `cycle_index` 1, and again at every renewal finalization with a `cycle_index` one greater than the highest already recorded for that appointment. Each cycle carries the `appointment_id` under review and starts in `status` `QUEUED`, from which it forks into exactly one of `CLEARED`, `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, `DENIED` or `VOIDED`, all four terminal. A cycle reaches `VOIDED` only through the withdrawal cascade, while it is still `QUEUED`.

An `ExportControlReviewer` decides a `QUEUED` cycle by attesting to two optional findings: `restricted_party_hit`, the reviewer-attested restricted-party screening result, and `controlled_technology_flag`, the reviewer-attested presence of export-controlled technology in the research plan. Both default to false when omitted. The reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from both the appointment's `prepared_by_user_id` and its `mentor_id`; a reviewer who is either of those parties is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On a valid decision the server computes the tier, the score and the licence determination, stamps `reviewer_user_id` with the reviewer's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` with the logical-clock instant — both null while the cycle is `QUEUED` — and derives the outcome. `screening_tier`, `risk_score` and `deemed_export_license_required` all start null or false and are written by this decision.

Two country and funding factors feed the score. The country factor is read from the scholar's `citizenship_country`, where `US` scores 0.0, `CA` and `MX` each score 0.5, `IN` scores 1.5, `CN` scores 2.5 and `IR` scores 4.0; any country not named in that table scores the default of 1.0. The funding factor is read from the award's `funding_type`, where `FEDERAL_GRANT` scores 1.0, `FOUNDATION_GRANT` scores 0.5, `EXTERNAL_FELLOWSHIP` scores 1.6 and `INSTITUTIONAL_FELLOWSHIP` scores 0.0.

The **screening tier** decides which of two scoring variants applies, and it is written to `screening_tier`. The tier is `TIER_2_ENHANCED` when any one of three conditions holds: the cycle's `cycle_index` is 2 or greater, or `restricted_party_hit` is true, or `controlled_technology_flag` is true and the scholar's country factor is 3.0 or greater. Otherwise the tier is `TIER_1_STANDARD`. The two variants differ in exactly two respects: the country component is weighted 12.5 under `TIER_1_STANDARD` and 15.0 under `TIER_2_ENHANCED`, and the term-duration component is omitted entirely under `TIER_1_STANDARD` while it is added under `TIER_2_ENHANCED`. For identical reviewer findings the two variants can therefore return different scores and even different outcomes.

Five components are computed, **each rounded half up to two decimal places on its own**, and only their sum is rounded — half up, to one decimal place — to give `risk_score`. The country component is the country factor multiplied by the tier's country weight, 12.5 or 15.0. The technology component is 18.75 when `controlled_technology_flag` is true and 0.00 otherwise. The restricted-party component is 40.00 when `restricted_party_hit` is true and 0.00 otherwise. The funding component is the funding factor multiplied by 6.25. The duration component, present only under `TIER_2_ENHANCED`, is the appointment's `term_months` divided by twelve and then multiplied by 3.125; under `TIER_1_STANDARD` it is 0.00.

Independently of the score, `deemed_export_license_required` is set true when `controlled_technology_flag` is true **and** the scholar's country factor is 3.0 or greater, and false in every other case. It is a determination, not a gate: it does not by itself change the outcome.

The outcome is then derived in a fixed order of tests. A `restricted_party_hit` of true is `DENIED` outright, whatever the score. Otherwise a `risk_score` of 60.0 or above is `DENIED`. Otherwise a `risk_score` of 35.0 or above is `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS`. Otherwise the cycle is `CLEARED`. Each outcome then drives the appointment's compliance dimension, but only once no cycle for that appointment remains `QUEUED`. A `CLEARED` cycle drives `compliance_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` → `CLEARED`, after which bench access is admissible in a laboratory of any `biosafety_level`. A `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS` cycle drives `PENDING_REVIEW` → `RESTRICTED`, after which bench access is admissible only in a `BSL1` laboratory and only for a single bench seat. When a re-screen cycle clears, the same `PENDING_REVIEW` → `CLEARED` transition additionally restores access: every bench-access grant for the appointment sitting in `SUSPENDED` with a `suspension_reason` of `COMPLIANCE_REGRESSION` returns to `PROVISIONED` with its `suspension_reason` reset to `NONE`.

A `DENIED` cycle is the system's most consequential single write. It drives `compliance_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` → `BLOCKED`; every bench-access grant for the appointment still in `PROVISIONED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `COMPLIANCE_REGRESSION`; and if the appointment's `appointment_status` is `ACTIVE` at that moment, it advances `ACTIVE` → `TERMINATED`. That termination in turn posts the appointment's `term_months` to the scholar's `total_months_in_rank`, decreases the mentor's `active_postdoc_count` by 1, reduces the award's `encumbered_usd` by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd` while setting that `encumbrance_usd` to 0.00 and recomputing `available_balance_usd`, creates an exit record for the appointment with `exit_reason` `COMPLIANCE_TERMINATION` and `status` `INITIATED` unless one already exists, and moves every bench-access grant for the appointment in `PROVISIONED` or `SUSPENDED` to `REVOKED` with `suspension_reason` `EXIT_PROCESSING`. An appointment that was not `ACTIVE` when the denial landed stops at the `BLOCKED` posture and the grant suspension.

**Bench access** is requested by a `FacultyMentor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the appointment's `mentor_id`. The request names the `appointment_id` whose compliance posture authorises the access and the `laboratory_id` whose seats will be occupied, asks for a `bench_seats` count of at least 1 and at most 10, bounds the access with an `access_start_date` and an `access_end_date`, and carries the `expected_appointment_version` guard described in section 2.9. A created grant starts with `status` `PROVISIONED`, a `suspension_reason` of `NONE`, and `requested_by_user_id` stamped with the requesting mentor's `X-User-ID`. Its `status` values are `PROVISIONED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED`, of which only `REVOKED` is terminal, and both `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED` are reached exclusively by cascade — no client can suspend or revoke a grant directly. `suspension_reason` takes `NONE`, `COMPLIANCE_REGRESSION` or `EXIT_PROCESSING`. The mentor's `mentor_training_valid_through` must still be current relative to the appointment's `term_start_date`, else `ERR_MENTOR_TRAINING_LAPSED`; the target laboratory's `status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_LAB_CLOSED`; the appointment's `compliance_status` must be `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED`, else `ERR_EXPORT_CONTROL_BLOCKED`; and a `RESTRICTED` appointment may take neither a `BSL2` nor a `BSL3` bench, nor more than one seat in any laboratory, else `ERR_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTION_VIOLATED`.

Capacity is tested per calendar day, not pairwise between grants, and the test is run on the **effective occupancy interval** rather than the requested one. A grant's effective interval begins at its `access_start_date` less the laboratory's `decontamination_buffer_days` and ends at its `access_end_date`; only the leading edge is expanded, the trailing edge is not, and both ends are inclusive. The incumbents considered are the grants on the same `laboratory_id` whose `status` is `PROVISIONED` or `SUSPENDED`; a `REVOKED` grant contributes nothing on any day and frees its seats immediately. For each calendar day inside the requested grant's effective interval, the server sums `bench_seats` over every incumbent whose own effective interval contains that day; if that occupied total plus the requested `bench_seats` would exceed the laboratory's `bench_capacity` on any single day, the request is refused. The refusal is `ERR_LAB_BUFFER_CONFLICT` when the collision exists only because of the buffer — that is, when the incumbent and the requested access windows would have been disjoint had the leading edge not been expanded, the clearest case being an incumbent whose access ends on the requested grant's buffered effective start date. Otherwise the refusal is `ERR_LAB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Where an appointment's compliance posture is also inadmissible, the compliance refusal is the one returned and the capacity test is never reached.

### 3.7. Activation and Withdrawal

Activation is the gate at which every parallel thread of preparation must have converged, and it is a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`'s action on an appointment in `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, advancing it `OFFER_ACCEPTED` → `ACTIVE` with no request body. The appointment's `compliance_status` must already be `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED`; an appointment still in `PENDING_REVIEW`, or one in `BLOCKED`, offers no activation transition at all and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every development document that exists for the appointment must be `APPROVED` — in practice a `MENTORING_PLAN` when the award set `requires_mentoring_plan` and an `IDP` when it set `requires_idp` — and a document still short of approval yields `ERR_DEVELOPMENT_DOCS_INCOMPLETE`. The mentor must still be able to supervise: `mentor_training_valid_through` on or after `term_start_date`, else `ERR_MENTOR_TRAINING_LAPSED`, and `active_postdoc_count` strictly below `max_concurrent_postdocs`, else `ERR_MENTOR_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. The term must still be legal after any deferral the visa cascade applied: `term_months` between 12 and 36 inclusive, else `ERR_TERM_LENGTH_INVALID`.

Two further conditions apply only when the scholar's `requires_sponsorship` is true, and they are checked in the order given. The appointment's visa case must have reached `APPROVED`; a case still in `DOCS_PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `RFE_RECEIVED`, or one that ended `DENIED` or `VOIDED`, yields `ERR_VISA_NOT_APPROVED`. And the appointment's `term_start_date` must fall on or after the case's `earliest_lawful_start_date`; an earlier start yields `ERR_START_BEFORE_LAWFUL_DATE`. Because the approval cascade already deferred `term_start_date` to the lawful date whenever the lawful date was later, this second condition normally holds by construction — it exists to catch an appointment whose term was set up in a way the cascade did not reconcile. For a scholar who needs no sponsorship neither condition is evaluated and the absence of a visa case is not an obstacle.

Upon activation the scholar's own record follows: a `Postdoc` whose `status` is `PROSPECTIVE` advances `PROSPECTIVE` → `ACTIVE`. The mentor's `active_postdoc_count` increases by 1, which is what makes the mentor-load ceiling meaningful across a portfolio of appointments. The appointment's `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal is the officer's escape hatch before employment begins, and it is available from exactly three states — `DRAFT`, `OFFER_ISSUED` and `OFFER_ACCEPTED`. It is never available from `ACTIVE`, `NON_RENEWED`, or any terminal state; an appointment that has begun ends through exit processing or compliance termination, not through withdrawal. The body carries `withdraw_reason`, one of `VISA_DENIED`, `VISA_DELAY_TERM_TOO_SHORT`, `CANDIDATE_DECLINED` or `FUNDING_LOST`, and the supplied value is stored verbatim on the appointment. The field is null on any appointment whose `appointment_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`.

What the withdrawal unwinds depends on how far the appointment had travelled, and the three branches are deliberately different. From `DRAFT` there is nothing to unwind: the `withdraw_reason` is recorded, the `version` increments by 1, and no ledger or child record is touched, because a draft never encumbered the award and never spawned children. From `OFFER_ISSUED` the encumbrance is released as the exact compensating action of issuance — the award's `encumbered_usd` is reduced by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd`, that `encumbrance_usd` is set to 0.00, and `available_balance_usd` is recomputed from `annual_budget_usd` — and the children issuance created are voided: every development document in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` becomes `VOIDED`, and every screening cycle still in `QUEUED` becomes `VOIDED`. From `OFFER_ACCEPTED` the same release and the same two voidings apply, and in addition the appointment's visa case is voided if it sits in `DOCS_PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `RFE_RECEIVED`. In all three branches the `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Reappointment Review, the Non-Renewal Notice and the Grievance Channel

A reappointment review is opened by a `PostdocAffairsOfficer` against an appointment, with no request body. Its `review_cycle` is assigned as one greater than the highest `review_cycle` already recorded for that appointment, so the first review is cycle 1. At most one review per appointment may be open at a time: if a review exists for the appointment whose `status` is neither `FINALIZED_RENEWED` nor `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_OPEN`. A new review starts in `status` `OPENED`; the pipeline runs `OPENED`, then a fork into `RECOMMENDED_RENEW` or `RECOMMENDED_NON_RENEW`, then a join into `FINALIZED_RENEWED` or `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED`.

The mentor recommends first. A `FacultyMentor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the appointment's `mentor_id` records a `recommendation` of either `RENEW` or `NON_RENEW` together with a `mentor_recommendation_note` capturing the written recommendation. `RENEW` moves the review `OPENED` → `RECOMMENDED_RENEW` and `NON_RENEW` moves it `OPENED` → `RECOMMENDED_NON_RENEW`; either way the note is stored and `recommended_by_user_id` is stamped with the recommending mentor's `X-User-ID`, a field that is null while the review is `OPENED`.

Finalization is a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`'s action carrying a `decision` of `RENEW` or `NON_RENEW`, and the officer may not be the same person recorded in `recommended_by_user_id`. The `decision` must agree with the recommendation: a `RENEW` decision is available only from `RECOMMENDED_RENEW` and a `NON_RENEW` decision only from `RECOMMENDED_NON_RENEW`. In both branches the governing appointment's `appointment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, `finalized_by_user_id` is stamped with the finalizing officer's `X-User-ID`, `service_months_at_review` is computed, and the appointment's `version` increments by 1. Beyond that the two branches have **entirely disjoint consequences**, and the asymmetry is intentional.

`service_months_at_review` measures rank service as of the finalization instant rather than as of the term end. It is the appointment's `months_in_rank_at_start` plus the month count — computed with the pinned month arithmetic from section 3.2 — between the appointment's `term_start_date` and the calendar date of the logical-clock instant at which finalization occurs.

On the **renewal branch**, `RECOMMENDED_RENEW` → `FINALIZED_RENEWED`, the review touches no notice field, no term field and no ledger figure at all: `notice_days_required`, `notice_deadline_date`, `notice_issued_at`, `notice_timely`, `grievance_window_end` and `pre_notice_term_end_date` all remain null and `term_extension_days` remains 0. What renewal does instead is re-open research security. A new screening cycle is created for the appointment with a `cycle_index` one greater than the highest already recorded and a `status` of `QUEUED`. Because a `QUEUED` cycle now exists, the appointment's `compliance_status` regresses to `PENDING_REVIEW` — from `CLEARED` or from `RESTRICTED`, whichever it held. And because the compliance posture has left the admissible pair, every bench-access grant for the appointment in `PROVISIONED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `COMPLIANCE_REGRESSION`. The scholar keeps the appointment and loses the bench until the re-screen clears, at which point the grants return to `PROVISIONED` as described in section 3.6. Note that the re-screen is a second-or-later cycle, so it is scored under the enhanced tier regardless of the reviewer's findings.

On the **non-renewal branch**, `RECOMMENDED_NON_RENEW` → `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED`, the notice policy is computed and the appointment ends. `notice_issued_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant of finalization. The notice entitlement `notice_days_required` is banded on `service_months_at_review`: 30 days when service is 12 months or fewer, 60 days when service is more than 12 but 36 months or fewer, and 90 days beyond that. `notice_deadline_date` is the appointment's `term_end_date` less `notice_days_required` days. `notice_timely` is true when the calendar date of `notice_issued_at` falls on or before `notice_deadline_date`, and false otherwise. Before anything is changed, `pre_notice_term_end_date` captures the appointment's `term_end_date` exactly as it stood, so that a later reinstatement can restore it.

When the notice is timely, `term_extension_days` is 0 and the term is left alone. When the notice is **untimely**, the institution cures the shortfall by extending the term rather than by paying in lieu: the required end date is the calendar date of `notice_issued_at` plus `notice_days_required` days, `term_extension_days` is the number of days between the appointment's current `term_end_date` and that required end date, and the appointment's `term_end_date` is moved out to the required end date. Because the term end moved, the appointment's `term_months`, `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end`, `benefits_package`, `fringe_rate` and `encumbrance_usd` are recomputed, and then the award's `encumbered_usd` and `available_balance_usd` are recomputed in turn. An extension that would drive the recomputed `available_balance_usd` below zero is not permitted: the finalization is refused rather than the award overdrawn. The rank ceiling is also re-tested on finalization, so an extension that would push `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end` past 60 months is refused with `ERR_RANK_YEARS_EXCEEDED`.

The non-renewal branch then ends the appointment: `appointment_status` advances `ACTIVE` → `NON_RENEWED`, and the appointment's `term_months` — the value after any extension recompute, not before — is added to the scholar's `total_months_in_rank`. Note that `NON_RENEWED` is still a **held** state for encumbrance purposes: the appointment continues to contribute its `encumbrance_usd` to the award's `encumbered_usd` until it completes or is terminated.

Finally, `grievance_window_end` is computed as the calendar date of `notice_issued_at` plus 21 days, clamped to the instant `23:59:59.999Z` on that date — three fractional-second digits exactly, so that a grievance filed at the last representable millisecond of the twenty-first day is still inside the window.

A `Postdoc` files a grievance by naming the `reappointment_review_id` of the non-renewal decision being contested, a `category` of `PROCEDURAL`, `NOTICE_UNTIMELY` or `RETALIATION` describing the channel chosen, and a `narrative` giving the scholar's written statement. The filing scholar's `X-User-ID` must equal the `postdoc_id` of the appointment the review governs. The grievance is created in `status` `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock and `filed_by_user_id` stamped with the filer's `X-User-ID`; `resolution_note` and `resolved_by_user_id` are null until it is resolved. Filing is permitted only at or before the review's `grievance_window_end`, inclusive to the millisecond; a filing after that instant, or against a review whose `grievance_window_end` is null because the review never reached `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED`, is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Resolution is a `PostdocAffairsOfficer`'s action on a grievance in `FILED`, carrying an `outcome` of `DENY` or `SUSTAIN` and a `resolution_note` giving the officer's written rationale. The resolving officer must not be the review's `finalized_by_user_id`. A `DENY` moves the grievance `FILED` → `RESOLVED_DENIED`, stores the `resolution_note`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id`, and changes nothing else — the non-renewal stands.

A `SUSTAIN` moves the grievance `FILED` → `RESOLVED_SUSTAINED` and **undoes the non-renewal**, which is what makes the review pipeline non-monotonic: a state that reads as terminal is reversed through the grievance channel. The review advances `FINALIZED_NON_RENEWED` → `FINALIZED_RENEWED`, and its `notice_issued_at`, `notice_timely` and `grievance_window_end` are all set back to null. The appointment then advances `NON_RENEWED` → `ACTIVE`, and **the three steps of that reversal run in this normative order**. Step one debits the scholar's `total_months_in_rank` by the appointment's `term_months` — the exact value that was posted when the appointment became `NON_RENEWED`, which is why the debit must happen before the term is restored. Step two restores the appointment's `term_end_date` from the review's `pre_notice_term_end_date` and resets the review's `term_extension_days` to 0. Step three recomputes the appointment's `term_months`, `cumulative_months_in_rank_at_end`, `benefits_package`, `fringe_rate` and `encumbrance_usd`, and then recomputes the award's `encumbered_usd` and `available_balance_usd`. Performed in that order the rank ledger returns to exactly its pre-notice value; performed in any other order it drifts. The appointment's `reinstated_count`, which starts at 0, increases by 1 to record that a sustained grievance returned this appointment from `NON_RENEWED` to `ACTIVE`. A sustain that would drive the recomputed `available_balance_usd` below zero is not permitted and the resolution is refused.

### 3.9. Exit Processing, Intellectual-Property Assignment and Separation

Exit processing is opened by a `PostdocAffairsOfficer` against an appointment, with a single body field `exit_reason` recording why the appointment is ending: `TERM_COMPLETION`, `NON_RENEWAL`, `RESIGNATION` or `COMPLIANCE_TERMINATION`. Only one exit record may exist per appointment, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_EXIT_ALREADY_INITIATED` — including against an appointment whose exit record was created by the compliance-termination cascade rather than by an officer. The record is created with `status` `INITIATED`, `initiated_at` stamped from the logical clock, `ip_disclosure_count` at 0, `ip_assignment_signed_at` null, `lab_access_cleared` false and `final_months_posted` at 0. Its `status` values are `INITIATED`, `IP_ASSIGNMENT_SIGNED` and `CLOSED`, and `CLOSED` is terminal and is never reached by a client request.

Initiating exit immediately strips physical access: every bench-access grant for the appointment sitting in `PROVISIONED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `REVOKED` with `suspension_reason` `EXIT_PROCESSING`. Revocation is final for that grant — there is no path back out of `REVOKED` — and a revoked grant stops contributing bench seats on every calendar day of its former interval, freeing that capacity for other scholars at once.

The scholar closes the record. A `Postdoc` whose `X-User-ID` equals the appointment's `postdoc_id` signs the intellectual-property assignment on a record in `INITIATED`, supplying `ip_disclosure_count` as the number of invention disclosures being assigned to the institution at signature. The signature moves the record `INITIATED` → `IP_ASSIGNMENT_SIGNED`, stamps `ip_assignment_signed_at` with the logical-clock instant, stores the supplied `ip_disclosure_count`, and recomputes `lab_access_cleared`, which is true exactly when no bench-access grant for this appointment remains in `PROVISIONED`.

Auto-close is then evaluated **immediately, inside the same request**. If `lab_access_cleared` is true, the record advances `IP_ASSIGNMENT_SIGNED` → `CLOSED` without any further client action; if it is false, the record waits in `IP_ASSIGNMENT_SIGNED` until the outstanding grants are resolved. Closure sets off the final cascade. When the appointment's `appointment_status` is `ACTIVE` it advances `ACTIVE` → `COMPLETED`. Because the appointment completed, the appointment's `term_months` is added to the scholar's `total_months_in_rank` and the same figure is recorded on the exit record as `final_months_posted`; the mentor's `active_postdoc_count` decreases by 1; and the award's `encumbered_usd` is reduced by this appointment's `encumbrance_usd`, which is itself set to 0.00, with `available_balance_usd` recomputed from `annual_budget_usd`. Where the appointment had already reached a terminal state before the closure — most commonly `TERMINATED` by the research-security denial cascade, which posted the rank months and released the encumbrance at that time — none of those postings is repeated and `final_months_posted` stays 0, so no month is ever counted twice.

Separation is the last link. Once the appointment has left the held population, the server checks whether the scholar has any remaining appointment whose `appointment_status` is `DRAFT`, `OFFER_ISSUED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `NON_RENEWED`. If none remains, the scholar's own `status` advances `ACTIVE` → `SEPARATED`, which is terminal for the person record. A scholar who still holds another appointment in any of those five states stays `ACTIVE`.

Error responses carry the status code listed in section 5.
