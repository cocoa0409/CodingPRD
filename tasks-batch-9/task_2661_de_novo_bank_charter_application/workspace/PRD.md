# De Novo Bank Charter Application API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Chartering a brand-new bank is one of the few commercial projects where the product does not exist until a regulator says it may. A group of organizers must assemble itself, prove that each of its members is fit to hold a banking licence, write a three-year business plan that survives adverse economic overlays, raise real money from real investors and park it in escrow where nobody can touch it, and then persuade two independent authorities — the chartering authority and the deposit insurer — to approve the same project on two separate tracks. Every one of those workstreams produces evidence that the others depend on, and any one of them can regress: an investor fails screening, a policy is pulled back by an examiner, a field visit comes back deficient. The organizing group's readiness is therefore never a fact that is established once; it is a value that must be recomputed every time anything underneath it moves.

This document specifies the De Novo Bank Charter Application service, mounted under `/de-novo`, which models that project end to end. The service tracks the organizing group and its members, the business plan and its stress overlays, the capital raise and the individual subscriptions inside escrow, the two parallel regulatory filings and the conditions of approval each authority attaches, the four required policy domains, the vendor contracts and core-conversion windows, the pre-opening readiness examination, and — after opening day — three full years of heightened supervision during which any material departure from the pinned plan must be reported and, above a threshold, approved.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as constraints on every requirement that follows. First, **derived state is never trusted from the client**: counts, scores, escrow balances, leverage paths and verdicts are computed by the server from the rows that exist at that instant, and they are recomputed on every event that could change them, cascading upward through parents. Second, **regression is a first-class outcome**: a group that has reached the pre-opening stage can be pulled back out of it, a satisfied condition of approval can be reopened, a locked escrow can fall back open, and a pinned business plan can be un-pinned. Implementations that treat forward progress as monotonic will fail this specification.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Chartering Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is declared but is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before anything else in the request, in that order.

The seven roles are `CharterCounsel`, `LeadOrganizer`, `IndependentDirector`, `CharteringExaminer`, `InsuranceExaminer`, `EscrowAgent` and `PlatformAdmin`. No role is a superset of another. `CharterCounsel` is outside counsel and project manager for the organizing side: it opens the group file, seats organizers, drafts business plans and their stress overlays, structures the capital raise and opens it for subscription, prepares and files both applications, registers and advances vendor contracts, reserves and confirms core-conversion windows, and drives every stage advance of the group. `LeadOrganizer` is an organizer acting in a board-officer capacity: it submits the business plan for review, drafts and adopts board policies, submits remediation evidence against conditions of approval, and files business-plan deviation notices during heightened supervision. `IndependentDirector` is an independent organizer seated on the proposed board; it has no operation of its own and may only draft and adopt board policies and read. `CharteringExaminer` is the chartering authority's examination staff: it records background results, grants qualification waivers, decides the chartering authority's filing, attaches and adjudicates conditions of approval, reviews adopted policies, schedules and resolves the pre-opening readiness examination, and decides deviation requests. `InsuranceExaminer` is the deposit insurer's examination staff: it decides only the deposit insurer's filing and works that filing's conditions, and it has no reach into the charter decision, the readiness examination or the deviation regime. `EscrowAgent` is the independent escrow agent and is the only role that may record subscriptions, move subscription money, record investor-screening outcomes, or lock and release escrow. `PlatformAdmin` reads everything and owns the logical clock, and performs no business write of any kind.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against persisted rows. When `X-User-Role` is `LeadOrganizer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Organizer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Organizer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `IndependentDirector`, the same rule applies: `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Organizer`, and ownership checks compare the resource's organizer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for either of these two roles the supplied `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of an existing `Organizer` row whose `group_id` is the group in scope for the request — the group named in the path, or the group of the parent entity named in the path, or the `group_id` carried in the request body. A mismatch is `ERR_ORGANIZER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), and this check runs immediately after the endpoint role check and before request-body schema validation. When `X-User-Role` is `CharterCounsel`, `CharteringExaminer`, `InsuranceExaminer`, `EscrowAgent` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it in an audit context but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two additional authorization boundaries are role-sensitive rather than endpoint-sensitive. `CharteringExaminer` and `InsuranceExaminer` are peers on condition attachment and condition adjudication, and both appear in the filing-decision endpoint's role list, but each may only act on its own authority's filing: a `CharteringExaminer` may act only where the addressed filing's `authority` is `CHARTERING_AUTHORITY`, and an `InsuranceExaminer` only where it is `DEPOSIT_INSURER`. Because the role genuinely is in the endpoint's role list, a cross-authority attempt is `ERR_EXAMINER_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) and never `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. This check applies to recording a filing decision, to attaching a condition of approval to a filing, and to adjudicating a condition (evaluated through the condition's parent filing). Separately, policy adoption is guarded by a segregation-of-duties comparison on `X-User-ID` rather than on the role name, so an `IndependentDirector` who happens to be the drafter is refused exactly as a `LeadOrganizer` in the same position would be.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint is paginated with two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending; this default ordering is not configurable. For list responses, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Alongside pagination, each collection endpoint accepts a set of optional equality filters, and every filter is genuinely optional — a bare list request with no query string at all is always legal and returns the whole collection page by page. The filters are the channel through which each foreign key becomes reverse-reachable: organizers may be narrowed by `group_id`, `role_on_board`, `qualification_status` and `is_independent`; business plans by `group_id`, `supersedes_plan_id` and `status`; stress scenarios by `business_plan_id`, `scenario_code` and `governing_variant`; capital raises by `group_id` and `status`; subscriptions by `capital_raise_id`, `organizer_id`, `funding_state`, `clearance_state` and `counts_toward_escrow`; regulatory filings by `group_id`, `pinned_business_plan_id`, `authority` and `status`; approval conditions by `filing_id`, `category`, `status` and `is_pre_opening_blocking`; policy documents by `group_id`, `drafted_by_organizer_id`, `adopted_by_organizer_id`, `domain` and `status`; vendor contracts by `group_id`, `service_domain`, `criticality` and `status`; conversion windows by `vendor_contract_id` and `status`; readiness exams by `group_id`, `exam_scope` and `status`; and deviation requests by `group_id`, `baseline_plan_id`, `metric`, `status` and `requires_regulator_approval`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every entity in this service — `OrganizingGroup`, `Organizer`, `BusinessPlan`, `StressScenario`, `CapitalRaise`, `Subscription`, `RegulatoryFiling`, `ApprovalCondition`, `PolicyDocument`, `VendorContract`, `ConversionWindow`, `ReadinessExam` and `DeviationRequest` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded when the row is first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only change a derived field.
- `version` (integer): begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation of the row that owns it.

There are no entities in this service that omit `updated_at` or `version`; all thirteen carry the full set. A consequence worth stating plainly is that derived-field refreshes are mutations: when an organizer's background result changes the parent group's `composition_status` or `management_composite_score`, the group's `version` increments and its `updated_at` moves even though no client field on the group was touched. Where a single request touches several rows, each touched row's own `version` increments independently.

Time in this service is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to the host's real clock for any comparison. Every deadline test, exam-window test, conversion-window test and supervision-year computation reads the logical clock. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock forward by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response carries the new value as `current_utc`. The clock moves forward only: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the clock's current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), while a value equal to the current instant is accepted. Advancing the clock modifies no entity row; supervision-year derivations are refreshed lazily when the owning group is next read.

### 2.5. Numeric Representation, Precision and Rounding

Money is carried in **integer cents** throughout this service, never as a decimal or a floating-point figure. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a whole number of United States cents, and arithmetic on those fields is exact integer arithmetic wherever the specification does not explicitly call for a rounding step. Ratios and rates are carried in **integer basis points**, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so 10000 basis points is 100 percent and 800 basis points is 8 percent. Field names ending in `_bps` are always whole numbers of basis points.

Three rounding disciplines apply, and they are not interchangeable:

- **Round half up to whole cents.** Used wherever a rate is struck against a cent amount: a stress scenario's per-year credit loss and net-interest-margin hit, and the organizational-expense reimbursement cap. In every case the rounding happens *before* the resulting figure participates in any further arithmetic, so an unrounded intermediate never propagates.
- **Floor toward negative infinity to whole basis points.** Used for every ratio: a stress year's leverage ratio, the year-three loan concentration, and a deviation's magnitude. Flooring, not rounding, means a ratio of 799.9 basis points is 799 and fails a floor test set at 800.
- **Round half up to a fixed number of decimal places.** An organizer's `qualification_subscore` is rounded half up to zero decimal places and persisted as an integer. The group's `management_composite_score` is rounded half up to two decimal places. A conversion window's `duration_hours` is rounded half up to two decimal places.

Where a computation both averages and rounds, the order is pinned and must be followed exactly, because the two orders produce different cent values. The specific pinned order for the management composite score is given in §3.1: each organizer's subscore is rounded to an integer first and the persisted integer is what enters the sum; the average of those integers is then rounded to two decimal places. Averaging unrounded raw subscores and rounding only at the end is a different — and wrong — computation.

### 2.6. Derived Fields and the Recompute Discipline

A large share of every resource's payload is derived. The organizing group's `composition_status`, `organizer_count`, `independent_organizer_count`, `max_affiliation_count`, `management_composite_score` and `supervision_year_index` are derived; an organizer's `qualification_subscore` and `qualification_status` are derived; a business plan's `plan_revision_index`, `stress_scenario_count`, `breach_scenario_count` and `worst_governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` are derived; a stress scenario's six result fields and its two governing selections are derived; a capital raise's `subscribed_shares`, `pledged_gross_cents`, `funded_gross_cents`, `cleared_escrow_cents`, `refunded_gross_cents`, `projected_reimbursement_cents` and `projected_net_release_cents` are derived; a subscription's `amount_cents` and `counts_toward_escrow` are derived; a filing's `condition_total_count`, `open_blocking_condition_count`, `settled_condition_count` and `pre_opening_clearance` are derived; a vendor contract's `active_window_count` is derived; a conversion window's `duration_hours` is derived; a readiness exam's `buffer_end_utc` is derived; and a deviation request's `supervision_year_index_at_request`, `baseline_value`, `deviation_bps` and `requires_regulator_approval` are derived.

Derived fields are never accepted from a client. If a request body contains a key that names a derived field, that key is ignored and the server's own computation stands. Derived fields are recomputed **eagerly and immediately** at the end of every request that could change one of their inputs, before the response is serialized, so a response always shows a self-consistent snapshot. Two derived values are deliberate exceptions in that they are *frozen* rather than continuously live: a subscription's `amount_cents` is fixed from the raise's offering price at the moment the subscription is created and is never recomputed if the raise is later edited, and a deviation request's `supervision_year_index_at_request`, `baseline_value` and `deviation_bps` are fixed at the moment the request is created. A third field, the capital raise's `released_net_cents`, is a snapshot: it is 0 until escrow is released and thereafter holds the value `projected_net_release_cents` had at the instant of release.

### 2.7. Cascade Propagation and the Reversibility of Progress

When one operation changes more than one row, every downstream change is part of the same request and must be visible by the time the response is written. Cascades in this service are *transitive*: a link fires because the previous link fired, not because the original caller asked for it. The deepest chain in the specification has five links and starts when an examiner rejects a policy document that had already been accepted — the policy's rejection re-fails the satisfied policy-category conditions on the chartering authority's filing, the re-failed conditions raise that filing's open blocking count, the raised count pulls the filing out of conditional approval and consumes an information round, the filing leaving conditional approval pulls the group out of `PRE_OPENING`, and the group leaving `PRE_OPENING` cancels every scheduled readiness exam. Each of those five rows has its own `version` incremented.

Progress is reversible in four specific places, and implementations must support all four. A `RegulatoryFiling` can move from `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` back to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED`. An `OrganizingGroup` can move from `PRE_OPENING` back to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, and from `UNDER_REVIEW` back to `ORGANIZING`. A `CapitalRaise` can move from `ESCROW_LOCKED` back to `OPEN`. An `ApprovalCondition` can move from `SATISFIED` back to `OPEN`. In every case, the reverse move re-derives everything that depended on the forward move, and the group's `opening_blocked_reason` records why opening is currently barred: `FILING_INFO_REQUESTED` when a filing left conditional approval, `EXAM_DEFICIENT` when a readiness exam came back deficient, `ESCROW_SHORTFALL` when cleared escrow fell back below the raise's minimum, and `NONE` when nothing is blocking. `opening_blocked_reason` returns to `NONE` when an advance lands the group in `FILING_READY`, `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` or `PRE_OPENING`.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Group and Vendor Writes

Exactly two write endpoints in this service take a client-supplied version expectation, and on those two the field is a required input, not an optional hint.

Creating a business plan — `POST /de-novo/business-plans` — requires `expected_group_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the target `OrganizingGroup`. If that value does not equal the group's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GROUP_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The protection matters because a plan create legitimately supersedes the group's prior plan, so a writer holding an outdated view of the group — its composition, its background results, or a newer plan somebody else already created — must re-read the group before writing.

Reserving a core-conversion window — `POST /de-novo/conversion-windows` — requires `expected_vendor_contract_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the target `VendorContract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Reservations consume the contract's finite concurrent-conversion capacity, so a writer whose view of `active_window_count` is outdated must re-read the contract before reserving.

No other endpoint accepts a version expectation. Requests to any other endpoint neither require nor honour such a field, and the concurrency checks above run after existence resolution and before any state-machine check, so a stale version on a row that also happens to be in the wrong state reports the stale-version error.

### 2.9. Path Resolution for Nested Charter Records

Most collections in this service are addressed at the top level and narrowed with foreign-key filters, but seven creation paths and every action path are nested under the row they act on. Where a path contains more than one identifier — for example a stress scenario created under a business plan, or a readiness exam scheduled under an organizing group — identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing terminates the request with that identifier's own 404. A readiness exam requested under a group id that names no group therefore reports `ERR_GROUP_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the exam logic at all. The thirteen not-found codes are `ERR_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORGANIZER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUSINESS_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STRESS_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAPITAL_RAISE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONVERSION_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXAM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DEVIATION_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

The path identifiers themselves are named consistently across the service, one per entity: `group_id` for an organizing group, `organizer_id` for an organizer, `plan_id` for a business plan, `scenario_id` for a stress scenario, `raise_id` for a capital raise, `subscription_id` for a subscription, `filing_id` for a regulatory filing, `condition_id` for a condition of approval, `policy_id` for a policy document, `contract_id` for a vendor contract, `window_id` for a conversion window, `exam_id` for a readiness examination and `deviation_id` for a deviation request. Note that the path identifier for a business plan is `plan_id` while the body-supplied foreign key that points at the same entity is `business_plan_id`, and that the path identifier for a capital raise is `raise_id` while the body key is `capital_raise_id`; both spellings resolve the same rows and report the same 404 codes.

The same codes serve foreign keys supplied in a request body, which are resolved after every path identifier has resolved. The body-supplied keys that must name existing rows are `group_id`, `capital_raise_id`, `organizer_id`, `business_plan_id`, `supersedes_plan_id`, `pinned_business_plan_id`, `drafted_by_organizer_id`, `vendor_contract_id` and `baseline_plan_id`; a value naming no row reports the 404 belonging to the entity that key points at. Whether the row that *does* exist is also *eligible* — the right group, the right status — is a separate and later check with its own conflict code, described with the workflow it guards.

### 2.10. The Validation Ladder

This is a prudential-supervision domain, and when a single request violates several rules at once the reported error must be the one a supervisor would raise first. Checks are therefore evaluated in a fixed ladder of bands, and the first failing check in the earliest band wins; later bands are not evaluated at all. The bands, in order, are:

1. **Authentication and authorization** — headers present and role declared (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then role permitted on the endpoint (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`), then organizer identity for the two entity-backed roles (`ERR_ORGANIZER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`).
2. **Request schema** — required fields present and well-typed, enum members declared, numeric ranges and string lengths respected (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
3. **Existence** — path identifiers left to right, then body foreign keys (the thirteen not-found codes).
4. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_group_version` and `expected_vendor_contract_version` (`ERR_STALE_GROUP_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_CONTRACT_VERSION`).
5. **The target row's own state** — whether the addressed row's current status permits what is being asked of it.
6. **Another entity's state** — cross-entity preconditions, such as the parent raise being open or the owning group being in the right stage.
7. **Safety and prudential compliance** — segregation of duties, examiner authority, qualification bars, composition and management-score floors, coverage requirements, waiver bars.
8. **Referential eligibility beyond mere existence** — a referenced row exists but is in the wrong group or the wrong status.
9. **Constraint satisfaction, uniqueness and capacity** — uniqueness keys, role slots, monotonic projections, interval capacity.
10. **Temporal** — deadlines, window bounds, clock monotonicity, supervision-window expiry.
11. **Fiscal** — capital minimums, offering capacity, oversubscription, escrow minimums, stress floors, the capital-adequacy verdict, net-release sufficiency.

Placing fiscal checks last is deliberate: a request that is *both* fiscally impossible *and* mis-staged reports the staging error, never the money error. Two consequences of the ladder are called out explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. First, on policy adoption, the acting organizer's qualification bar (`ERR_ORGANIZER_NOT_QUALIFIED`, band 7) sits above the domain-uniqueness constraint (`ERR_POLICY_DOMAIN_ALREADY_ADOPTED`, band 9), so a request made by an unqualified organizer against an already-adopted domain must report `ERR_ORGANIZER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. Second, on escrow release, the state check that the raise must be locked (`ERR_ESCROW_NOT_LOCKED`, band 5) sits above the net-release sufficiency check (`ERR_NET_RELEASE_BELOW_PLAN_CAPITAL`, band 11), so a release attempted on a raise that is still `OPEN` and whose net release would also be short must report `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_LOCKED`.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities in this service are conserved, and the invariants are exact equalities rather than tolerances. They are assertions about every reachable state, not merely about happy paths.

**Cents of escrowed capital.** For every capital raise, at every point in time, `cleared_escrow_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over exactly those subscriptions of the raise whose `counts_toward_escrow` is true, and `pledged_gross_cents` plus `refunded_gross_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over every subscription of the raise. A funding event followed by a refund of the same subscription must restore `cleared_escrow_cents` to its exact pre-funding value, with no partial-cent residue and no double subtraction in the case where the subscription was also rejected in screening. A screening rejection of a subscription that was both funded and cleared must lower `cleared_escrow_cents` by exactly that subscription's `amount_cents`, and must move exactly that amount out of `funded_gross_cents` and into `refunded_gross_cents`, leaving the sum of `funded_gross_cents` and `refunded_gross_cents` unchanged by the rejection itself.

**The count of conditions on a filing.** For every regulatory filing, at every point in time, `condition_total_count` equals the number of approval conditions attached to that filing. No adjudication of any kind ever changes `condition_total_count`; only attaching a new condition changes it. A round trip in which a satisfied condition is reopened, has evidence resubmitted, and is satisfied again must restore both `settled_condition_count` and `open_blocking_condition_count` to their exact values before the reopen, while leaving `adjudication_count` strictly higher than before — the audit trail grows even though the counts return to where they started.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Seating the Organizing Group and Assessing Management Fitness

A charter project begins when a `CharterCounsel` opens a new organizing file. The payload is short: `proposed_bank_name`, the proposed legal name of the de novo bank, between 1 and 120 characters and not blank; `home_state_code`, the two-letter code of the state where the main office will sit, which must be exactly two uppercase letters; `charter_type`, which is one of `NATIONAL`, `STATE_MEMBER` or `STATE_NONMEMBER` and which later selects the base leverage requirement the plan is judged against; and `target_opening_date`, the planned first day of business as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. The target opening date must fall strictly after the current logical clock — a date equal to or earlier than the clock is rejected with `ERR_TARGET_OPENING_DATE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). A newly opened file starts at `stage` `ORGANIZING` with `composition_status` `FORMING`, with `organizer_count`, `independent_organizer_count`, `max_affiliation_count` and `supervision_year_index` all at 0, `management_composite_score` at 0.00, `opening_blocked_reason` at `NONE`, `supervision_started_at` null, and `version` at 1.

Organizers are seated one at a time, under the group, and only while the group's `stage` is `ORGANIZING` or `FILING_READY`; once the group is under review or beyond, seating is refused with `ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`. Each organizer is described by `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), `role_on_board`, `is_independent`, `affiliation_code`, `banking_years` and `regulatory_experience_score`. `role_on_board` is one of `PROPOSED_CEO`, `PROPOSED_CFO`, `PROPOSED_CRO`, `PROPOSED_CCO`, `DIRECTOR` or `INVESTOR_ONLY`; the four key officer roles are single-occupancy within a group, so seating a second `PROPOSED_CFO` alongside an existing one is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_ROLE_ALREADY_FILLED`, while `DIRECTOR` and `INVESTOR_ONLY` may be held by any number of organizers. `is_independent` is true when the organizer has no employment or control relationship with any other organizer of the group. `affiliation_code` is an opaque affiliation identifier of 1 to 32 non-blank characters, used by nothing except the concentration test below. `banking_years` counts years of regulated banking experience and must be between 0 and 60 inclusive; `regulatory_experience_score` is an examiner-assigned rating between 0 and 100 inclusive. The new row's `group_id` is taken from the group it is seated under. Both background dimensions start at `PENDING` — `fingerprint_state` at `PENDING` and `credit_state` at `PENDING` — with `adverse_finding_type` at `NONE`, `waiver_granted` false and `waiver_rationale` null.

An organizer's **qualification subscore** is a weighted rating out of 100, recomputed on creation and again on every background result. Three components are added together. The experience component takes the organizer's `banking_years` capped at twenty years, divides that capped figure by twenty, multiplies the result by one hundred, and weights it at forty percent. The regulatory component weights `regulatory_experience_score` at thirty-five percent. The integrity component contributes a weighted twenty-five percent of one hundred — that is, twenty-five points — when and only when `fingerprint_state` is `CLEARED`, and contributes nothing otherwise. The sum of the three components is rounded half up to zero decimal places and persisted in `qualification_subscore` as an integer; the unrounded sum is never persisted anywhere. Note carefully what does *not* enter this figure: `credit_state` has no effect on `qualification_subscore` at all, and reaches the organizer's standing only through the status ladder below.

`qualification_status` is then derived by a top-down ladder in which the **first** matching clause wins and no later clause is consulted. If `waiver_granted` is true, the status is `WAIVER_GRANTED`. Otherwise, if `fingerprint_state` is `FLAGGED` or `credit_state` is `ADVERSE`, the status is `DISQUALIFIED`. Otherwise, if either `fingerprint_state` or `credit_state` is still `PENDING`, the status is `PENDING`. Otherwise, if `qualification_subscore` is below 50, the status is `DISQUALIFIED`. In every remaining case the status is `QUALIFIED`. The ordering has a visible consequence: an organizer with an adverse finding is `DISQUALIFIED` even if the other dimension has not yet been resolved, and a waiver overrides an adverse finding without erasing it.

The two background dimensions are resolved independently and in either order, each by a `CharteringExaminer` recording a result on that organizer. The request names the `dimension` — `FINGERPRINT` or `CREDIT` — and the `outcome`. For the fingerprint dimension the outcome is `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED`, moving `fingerprint_state` from `PENDING` to that value; for the credit dimension the outcome is `CLEARED` or `ADVERSE`, moving `credit_state` from `PENDING` to that value. The addressed dimension must still be `PENDING`: a second result on an already-resolved dimension is refused with `ERR_BACKGROUND_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, and the sibling dimension's state is irrelevant to that check. An adverse outcome — `FLAGGED` on the fingerprint dimension or `ADVERSE` on the credit dimension — must be accompanied by an `adverse_finding_type` that is supplied and is not `NONE`; omitting it, or passing `NONE`, is rejected with `ERR_ADVERSE_FINDING_TYPE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The permitted categories are `NONE`, `FINANCIAL_CRIME`, `REGULATORY_ENFORCEMENT` and `PERSONAL_BANKRUPTCY`, and the supplied category is recorded on the organizer's `adverse_finding_type`. Recording any background result re-derives that organizer's `qualification_subscore` and `qualification_status`, then re-derives the parent group's `composition_status`, `organizer_count`, `independent_organizer_count`, `max_affiliation_count` and `management_composite_score`; the organizer's `version` increments by one and the group's `version` increments by one.

A disqualified organizer may be rehabilitated by a **management-qualification waiver**, granted by a `CharteringExaminer` with a `waiver_rationale` of at least 20 characters. The waiver is available only when the organizer's `qualification_status` is `DISQUALIFIED` and `waiver_granted` is still false; on any other standing, or on a second waiver attempt, the request is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_APPLICABLE`. One finding category is non-waivable: when `adverse_finding_type` is `FINANCIAL_CRIME`, the waiver is barred with `ERR_WAIVER_BARRED_BY_FINDING` regardless of how high the subscore is. These two checks are ordered — the applicability check sits in the target-row-state band and the finding bar sits in the safety band, so an organizer who is not disqualified at all and who also carries a financial-crime finding reports `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_APPLICABLE`. A granted waiver sets `waiver_granted` to true, stores the supplied `waiver_rationale`, and moves `qualification_status` to `WAIVER_GRANTED`. It deliberately does **not** alter `fingerprint_state` or `credit_state`: both dimensions keep their adverse values, and the waiver is visible only in the status and the rationale. Granting a waiver re-derives the parent group's `composition_status` and `management_composite_score`, increments the organizer's `version` by one and the group's `version` by one.

Group-level composition is reassessed on every organizer creation, every background result and every waiver, and it reads **every** organizer row of the group regardless of qualification standing. `organizer_count` is the number of organizer rows with that group's id. `independent_organizer_count` is the number of those rows whose `is_independent` is true. `max_affiliation_count` is the largest number of the group's organizers sharing a single `affiliation_code`, and is 0 when the group has no organizers. Two further quantities are computed but not persisted: the set of distinct key officer roles among `PROPOSED_CEO`, `PROPOSED_CFO`, `PROPOSED_CRO` and `PROPOSED_CCO` that are held by an organizer whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `WAIVER_GRANTED`, and the number of organizers whose `qualification_status` is `DISQUALIFIED`. From these, `composition_status` is set as follows. If `organizer_count` is below five, the status is `FORMING`. Otherwise, if `independent_organizer_count` is at least three, **and** all four key officer roles are present in that qualified-roles set, **and** no organizer of the group is `DISQUALIFIED`, **and** `max_affiliation_count` is at most the group's `organizer_count` divided by two and rounded down to a whole number, the status is `COMPOSITION_MET`. In every other case the status is `COMPOSITION_DEFICIENT`. The affiliation ceiling is therefore 2 for a five- or six-organizer group, 3 for a seven- or eight-organizer group, and so on.

The group's **management composite score** is the average subscore of its non-passive members, and the rounding order is pinned. The eligible population is every organizer of the group whose `role_on_board` is anything other than `INVESTOR_ONLY`, taken in ascending `created_at` order; investors who hold no board seat never affect management quality. When the eligible population is empty, `management_composite_score` is 0.00. Otherwise, each eligible organizer's already-rounded integer `qualification_subscore` is what enters the sum — the integer rounding has already happened at the organizer level and the persisted integer is authoritative — the sum is divided by the number of eligible organizers, and only that quotient is rounded, half up, to two decimal places. Averaging unrounded raw subscores and rounding once at the end is a different computation and produces a different cent value; it is not the specified behaviour. Qualification standing does not filter this population: a `DISQUALIFIED` or `PENDING` organizer with a board role still drags the average down, which is why a group can hold a `COMPOSITION_DEFICIENT` verdict and a low score simultaneously and must fix both.

Both group-level figures are gates further along. Advancing the group out of `ORGANIZING` requires `composition_status` to be exactly `COMPOSITION_MET`, failing which the advance is refused with `ERR_GROUP_COMPOSITION_INCOMPLETE`, and requires `management_composite_score` to be at least 70.00, failing which it is refused with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The same two floors guard business-plan submission, and in both places the composition check is evaluated before the score check.

### 3.2. The Three-Year Business Plan and Its Stress Overlays

A business plan is the document the regulators actually judge, and a group may hold several of them over the life of the project. A plan is created by a `CharterCounsel` against a group named by `group_id` in the payload, together with `expected_group_version` as described in §2.8, and it may only be created while the group's `stage` is neither `OPEN` nor `ABANDONED`; otherwise the create is refused with `ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`. The plan's substance is `opening_tier1_capital_cents` — the Tier 1 capital the bank expects to hold on its first day, which the eventual escrow release must be able to deliver — plus twelve projection fields covering three years: `projected_assets_y1_cents`, `projected_assets_y2_cents` and `projected_assets_y3_cents`; `projected_loans_y1_cents`, `projected_loans_y2_cents` and `projected_loans_y3_cents`; `projected_deposits_y1_cents`, `projected_deposits_y2_cents` and `projected_deposits_y3_cents`; and `projected_net_income_y1_cents`, `projected_net_income_y2_cents` and `projected_net_income_y3_cents`. A thirteenth figure, `projected_branch_count`, is the number of branches planned by the end of year three and must be at least 1. Net income figures may be negative — customary de novo loss years are expected and are not an error. Projected assets and projected deposits must each be non-decreasing across the three years, so year one may not exceed year two and year two may not exceed year three; a decrease in either series is rejected with `ERR_PROJECTION_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). For each of the three years, projected loans may not exceed projected assets in the same year; a violation in any year is rejected with `ERR_LOANS_EXCEED_ASSETS` (HTTP 422). Opening Tier 1 capital must be at least 2,000,000,000 cents — twenty million United States dollars — and a lower figure is rejected with `ERR_OPENING_CAPITAL_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Every projected asset, loan and deposit figure must be greater than or equal to zero, and each projected assets figure must be strictly greater than zero.

Plans supersede one another rather than being edited. The optional `supersedes_plan_id` names the plan this revision replaces, and it is null for a group's first plan. When it is supplied, the named plan must belong to the same group **and** be in `DRAFT` status; anything else — a plan of another group, or a plan that has moved on — is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A successful create moves the superseded plan from `DRAFT` to `SUPERSEDED` and increments that plan's `version` by one. `plan_revision_index` is 1 when `supersedes_plan_id` is omitted, and otherwise one more than the superseded plan's own `plan_revision_index`, so a chain of revisions is self-numbering. A plan that has been pinned by a regulator can never be superseded: because `PINNED` is not `DRAFT`, such a create falls to the same `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_NOT_ELIGIBLE` refusal, and the pinned plan is left untouched. A new plan begins in `DRAFT` with `stress_scenario_count`, `breach_scenario_count` and `worst_governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` all 0, and creating it increments the parent group's `version` by one.

The plan's lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `PINNED`, with `PINNED` reverting to `SUBMITTED` if the chartering authority later denies the filing that pinned it, and with `SUPERSEDED` as the terminal state reachable from either `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`. `PINNED` is written only by the chartering authority's conditional approval and is never requested directly. While a plan is anything other than `DRAFT`, no stress scenario may be attached to it and it may not be submitted again; both are refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE`.

**Stress scenarios** are the overlays that decide whether the plan's capital story holds up. A `CharterCounsel` attaches each one to a `DRAFT` plan with a `scenario_code` — `BASELINE`, `ADVERSE` or `SEVERELY_ADVERSE` — plus `credit_loss_rate_bps`, an annual credit-loss rate between 0 and 5000 basis points struck against projected loans; `nim_compression_bps`, a net-interest-margin compression between 0 and 2000 basis points struck against projected assets; and `expense_overrun_cents`, a one-off pre-opening expense overrun of zero or more cents charged against capital in stress year one only. A scenario code may appear at most once per plan; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_SCENARIO_CODE_DUPLICATE`. The new row's `business_plan_id` comes from the plan it is attached under.

Every scenario is run through the leverage path **twice**, and both results are stored. The path walks stress years one, two and three in that ascending order, carrying capital forward. Capital starts at the plan's `opening_tier1_capital_cents`. For each year, the year's assets figure and loans figure are selected according to the variant being run. Under the `DYNAMIC_GROWTH` variant, the assets figure is that year's projected assets and the loans figure is that year's projected loans, so the balance sheet follows the plan year by year. Under the `STATIC_BALANCE_SHEET` variant, the assets figure is `projected_assets_y1_cents` and the loans figure is `projected_loans_y1_cents` for all three years, so the balance sheet is frozen at the year-one projection, losses do not scale with growth, and the ratio denominator never grows. With those two figures in hand, the year's credit loss is the loans figure multiplied by `credit_loss_rate_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents; the year's margin hit is the assets figure multiplied by `nim_compression_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents; and the year's overrun is `expense_overrun_cents` in year one and zero in years two and three. The year's stressed net income is that year's projected net income minus the year's credit loss, minus the year's margin hit, minus the year's overrun — each of the three subtracted figures having already been rounded to whole cents before the subtraction. Capital is then increased by that stressed net income, which for a loss year lowers it. The year's leverage ratio in basis points is the carried-forward capital multiplied by 10000, divided by that year's assets figure, and floored toward negative infinity to a whole number of basis points.

Each run yields two outputs. The minimum leverage ratio is the smallest of the three years' ratios. The breach year index is the smallest year among one, two and three whose ratio is below 800 basis points, and is 0 when no year breaches. The `STATIC_BALANCE_SHEET` run's outputs are stored in `static_min_leverage_ratio_bps` and `static_breach_year_index`; the `DYNAMIC_GROWTH` run's outputs are stored in `dynamic_min_leverage_ratio_bps` and `dynamic_breach_year_index`. Which run **governs** is decided purely by the scenario code: `governing_variant` is `STATIC_BALANCE_SHEET` when `scenario_code` is `BASELINE`, and `DYNAMIC_GROWTH` when `scenario_code` is `ADVERSE` or `SEVERELY_ADVERSE`. `governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` and `governing_breach_year_index` are copies of whichever pair `governing_variant` selects. Both pairs remain visible on the scenario, so the non-governing path is auditable even though it does not decide anything. A scenario's own path reads only its own three inputs and the twelve projection fields of its parent plan; no sibling scenario participates.

Attaching a scenario then re-derives three aggregates on the parent plan: `stress_scenario_count` is the number of scenarios attached to that plan; `breach_scenario_count` is the number of those scenarios whose `governing_breach_year_index` is anything other than 0; and `worst_governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` is the smallest `governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` across that plan's scenarios, or 0 when the plan has no scenario at all. The plan's `version` increments by one.

### 3.3. The Capital Adequacy Verdict and Plan Submission

Whether a plan holds enough capital is not a single comparison; it is a five-step derivation in which four independent inputs — the charter type's tier, the number of breaching scenarios, the year-three loan concentration, and the worst governing stress ratio — interact through a nested minimum and maximum. The steps are as follows.

First, **concentration**. The year-three loan concentration in basis points is `projected_loans_y3_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `projected_assets_y3_cents`, floored to a whole number of basis points. It is exposed as `concentration_bps`.

Second, the **concentration add-on**. When `concentration_bps` is at or below the trip point of 6500 basis points — a loan book at sixty-five percent of assets — the add-on is zero. Above the trip point, the add-on charges five basis points for each *full* ten basis points of excess: subtract 6500 from `concentration_bps`, divide by ten and floor to a whole number, then multiply by five. The result is exposed as `concentration_add_on_bps`, so a concentration of 6519 basis points yields an add-on of 5 and a concentration of 6520 yields an add-on of 10.

Third, the **risk add-on**. Each stress scenario of the plan whose governing path breaches the floor adds 25 basis points, so multiply the plan's `breach_scenario_count` by 25 and add the concentration add-on. The result is exposed as `risk_add_on_bps`.

Fourth, the **required leverage**. The charter type sets a base tier: 800 basis points when the group's `charter_type` is `NATIONAL`, 850 basis points when it is `STATE_MEMBER`, and 900 basis points when it is `STATE_NONMEMBER`. That figure is exposed as `base_required_bps`. Separately, the risk-adjusted floor is the de novo floor of 800 basis points plus `risk_add_on_bps`. The requirement is the **larger** of the base tier and the risk-adjusted floor, and then the **smaller** of that result and the ceiling of 1200 basis points. The final figure is exposed as `required_leverage_bps`, and the ceiling means no combination of concentration and breaches can push the requirement above twelve percent.

Fifth, the **verdict**. When the plan's `worst_governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` is greater than or equal to `required_leverage_bps`, the verdict is `ADEQUATE`; otherwise it is `DEFICIENT`. Equality passes. Because the charter type enters the requirement, an identical set of projections and scenarios can be `ADEQUATE` under a `NATIONAL` charter and `DEFICIENT` under a `STATE_NONMEMBER` charter with no change to any number in the plan.

The whole derivation is also available as a read-only computation on the group, which returns every intermediate term so it can be reconciled: `business_plan_id`, `charter_type`, `base_required_bps`, `concentration_bps`, `concentration_add_on_bps`, `breach_scenario_count`, `risk_add_on_bps`, `required_leverage_bps`, `worst_governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` and `verdict`. By default this runs against the group's governing plan, which is the plan pinned by the group's chartering-authority filing, or — when no filing has pinned one yet — the group's most recently created plan in `SUBMITTED` status. An optional `business_plan_id` query parameter directs the computation at a specific plan instead. This read mutates nothing.

Submission is the moment the plan stops being an internal draft, and it is performed by a `LeadOrganizer` on a `DRAFT` plan, moving `status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and incrementing the plan's `version` by one. Six gates are evaluated, and they are evaluated in this order. The plan must be in `DRAFT` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE`). The group's `composition_status` must be `COMPOSITION_MET` (`ERR_GROUP_COMPOSITION_INCOMPLETE`). The group's `management_composite_score` must be at least 70.00 (`ERR_MANAGEMENT_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`). The plan's `stress_scenario_count` must be at least 1, so a plan with no overlay at all cannot be submitted (`ERR_STRESS_SCENARIO_REQUIRED`). Every stress scenario of the plan must have a `governing_min_leverage_ratio_bps` of at least 800 basis points — exactly 800 passes and 799 fails (`ERR_STRESS_CAPITAL_SHORTFALL`). Finally, the capital adequacy verdict for the plan must evaluate to `ADEQUATE` (`ERR_CAPITAL_INADEQUATE`). The ordering matters in practice: a plan with no scenarios *and* an inadequate verdict reports the missing-scenario error, and a group whose composition is deficient reports that before any capital arithmetic is attempted. A `DEFICIENT` verdict rejects the request and changes nothing — no status moves, no version increments.

### 3.4. The Capital Raise, Investor Screening and Escrow

Subscription money must sit untouched until the charter is approved, so the raise is modelled as a container with two independent balances and a strict release waterfall. A `CharterCounsel` structures the raise against a group named by `group_id`, and may do so only while the group's `stage` is neither `OPEN` nor `ABANDONED` (`ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`). Exactly one raise per group may be live at a time: if the group already has a raise in `STRUCTURING`, `OPEN` or `ESCROW_LOCKED`, the create is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_CAPITAL_RAISE_EXISTS`, while a raise that has reached `RELEASED` or `REFUNDED` no longer blocks a new one. The offering is described by `target_gross_cents`, the gross offering target, which must be greater than zero; `minimum_gross_cents`, the minimum gross proceeds required before escrow may be locked, which must be greater than zero and may not exceed the target — a minimum above the target is rejected with `ERR_MINIMUM_EXCEEDS_TARGET` (HTTP 422); `offering_price_cents_per_share`, the price per share in cents, greater than zero; `max_shares`, the ceiling on shares that may be outstanding across live subscriptions, greater than zero; and `organizational_expense_claim_cents`, the organizational expenses the organizers claim back out of the eventual release, which must be zero or more. The offering must be capable of raising its own target: `max_shares` multiplied by `offering_price_cents_per_share` must be at least `target_gross_cents`, and a structurally under-capacity offering is rejected with `ERR_OFFERING_CAPACITY_BELOW_TARGET` (HTTP 422). A new raise starts in `STRUCTURING` with `subscribed_shares`, `pledged_gross_cents`, `funded_gross_cents`, `cleared_escrow_cents`, `refunded_gross_cents` and `released_net_cents` all 0; the release waterfall is run once against that empty escrow, so `projected_reimbursement_cents` and `projected_net_release_cents` are also 0. The parent group's `version` increments by one.

Opening the offering for subscription is a `CharterCounsel` action that moves the raise from `STRUCTURING` to `OPEN`. A raise in any other status is refused with `ERR_RAISE_NOT_OPENABLE`. The group must still be in one of `ORGANIZING`, `FILING_READY`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`; a group that has already reached `PRE_OPENING`, `OPEN` or `ABANDONED` cannot have a fresh offering opened, and that refusal carries `ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`. Opening increments the raise's `version` by one.

Individual **subscriptions** are recorded by the `EscrowAgent` and by nobody else. The payload names `capital_raise_id`, `subscriber_name` — the legal name of the subscriber as it appears on the subscription agreement, 1 to 120 non-blank characters — and `shares`, which must be at least 1. `organizer_id` is optional and identifies a subscribing organizer; when omitted the subscriber is an outside investor, and when supplied it must name an `Organizer` whose group is the same group as the raise's, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_ORGANIZER_MISMATCH`. The parent raise must be `OPEN`: a subscription against a raise in any other status is refused with `ERR_RAISE_NOT_OPEN`. `amount_cents` is derived as `shares` multiplied by the raise's `offering_price_cents_per_share` — an exact integer multiplication with no rounding possible — and the price is read from the raise once, at creation, and pinned into the row; if the raise's price is later changed, existing `amount_cents` values do not move. Any client-supplied amount is ignored. The offering's share ceiling is an **aggregate** check: the raise's current `subscribed_shares` plus the requested `shares` must not exceed `max_shares`, so each individual subscription can be perfectly valid while the collection overflows, and the overflowing one is refused with `ERR_OFFERING_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. A new subscription starts with `funding_state` `PLEDGED`, `clearance_state` `UNSCREENED` and `counts_toward_escrow` false, then the raise's aggregates and its release waterfall are re-derived and the raise's `version` increments by one.

A subscription carries **two independent state dimensions** on the same row, and this is the most consequential structural feature of the escrow model. `funding_state` tracks the money and runs `PLEDGED` → `FUNDED` → `REFUNDED`. `clearance_state` tracks investor screening and runs `UNSCREENED` → `SCREENING` → `CLEARED`, with `REJECTED` reachable from any of `UNSCREENED`, `SCREENING` or `CLEARED`. The two dimensions advance in either order and impose no ordering on each other; `counts_toward_escrow` is true for exactly those rows where `funding_state` is `FUNDED` **and** `clearance_state` is `CLEARED`.

Money moves through a funding event named by `funding_event`. `FUND` moves `funding_state` from `PLEDGED` to `FUNDED` and requires the parent raise to be `OPEN` (`ERR_RAISE_NOT_OPEN`); a subscription that is not `PLEDGED` is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. `REFUND` moves `funding_state` from `FUNDED` to `REFUNDED` and requires the parent raise to be `OPEN` or `ESCROW_LOCKED`; a subscription that is not `FUNDED` is likewise refused with `ERR_FUNDING_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Once the raise has reached `RELEASED`, no refund is possible at all: released escrow cannot be clawed back through this surface, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_ALREADY_RELEASED`. Either funding event re-derives `counts_toward_escrow` on the row and the raise's aggregates and waterfall, and increments both the subscription's `version` and the raise's `version` by one.

Screening moves through an `outcome`. `SCREENING` is accepted only from `UNSCREENED` and simply parks the row mid-review. `CLEARED` is accepted from `UNSCREENED` or `SCREENING`. `REJECTED` is accepted from `UNSCREENED`, `SCREENING` or `CLEARED`, and is terminal for that dimension. Any outcome the current `clearance_state` does not permit — a second `SCREENING`, or anything at all once the row is `REJECTED` — is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A rejection after the raise has reached `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_ALREADY_RELEASED`. Clearing a row re-derives `counts_toward_escrow`, `cleared_escrow_cents`, `projected_reimbursement_cents` and `projected_net_release_cents`.

A **rejection is the unwinding event**, and its cascade is long. The row's `clearance_state` becomes `REJECTED` and `counts_toward_escrow` becomes false. If the row was `FUNDED`, its `funding_state` moves to `REFUNDED` in the same operation — a rejected investor's money goes back. Because the row stopped counting, the raise's `cleared_escrow_cents`, `funded_gross_cents`, `pledged_gross_cents`, `subscribed_shares`, `refunded_gross_cents`, `projected_reimbursement_cents` and `projected_net_release_cents` are all re-derived. If the recomputed `cleared_escrow_cents` is below the raise's `minimum_gross_cents` while the raise is `ESCROW_LOCKED`, the raise reverts from `ESCROW_LOCKED` to `OPEN` and the group's `opening_blocked_reason` becomes `ESCROW_SHORTFALL`. Because the raise left `ESCROW_LOCKED`, every approval condition on either of the group's filings whose `category` is `CAPITAL` and whose `status` is `SATISFIED` is returned to `OPEN` and has its `adjudication_count` incremented by one. Because those conditions returned to `OPEN`, each affected filing's `open_blocking_condition_count` and `settled_condition_count` are re-derived. And if that re-derivation raises a filing's `open_blocking_condition_count` from zero to a positive number while that filing is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, the filing itself cascades from `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` with `info_request_round` incremented by one — which in turn pulls the group back out of `PRE_OPENING`. A refund follows the same reversion chain whenever it is the refund that drops cleared escrow below the minimum.

The raise's aggregates are computed over all of its subscriptions and fall into **two bases** that must never be confused. The **subscription basis** counts every subscription that has not been refunded, whether or not it has been funded and whether or not screening has cleared: `subscribed_shares` is the sum of `shares` over rows whose `funding_state` is `PLEDGED` or `FUNDED`, and `pledged_gross_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the same rows. It is the marketing view of the raise and is always greater than or equal to the cleared basis; it is what the oversubscription guard measures against `max_shares`. The **cleared basis** is `cleared_escrow_cents`, the sum of `amount_cents` over exactly those rows whose `counts_toward_escrow` is true. Alongside them, `funded_gross_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over rows whose `funding_state` is `FUNDED` regardless of screening, and `refunded_gross_cents` is the sum over rows whose `funding_state` is `REFUNDED`; refunded rows feed neither of the two bases. The cleared basis is the **only** basis the escrow gate accepts — it governs the escrow lock, the minimum-proceeds test, the automatic reversion out of `ESCROW_LOCKED`, and the release waterfall. A subscription that is funded but whose investor screening was later rejected sits inside the subscription basis and outside the cleared basis, so the two diverge by exactly that subscription's `amount_cents` and a raise can look fully subscribed while being impossible to lock. All of these sums are exact cent integers and never round, which is why a rejected screening must remove that subscription's whole amount rather than a rounded share of it.

**Locking escrow** is an `EscrowAgent` action that moves the raise from `OPEN` to `ESCROW_LOCKED`. Three gates apply in this order: the raise must be `OPEN` (`ERR_RAISE_NOT_LOCKABLE`); at least one subscription of the raise must have `counts_toward_escrow` true, so an empty cleared set cannot be locked even in the degenerate case where `minimum_gross_cents` happens to be zero (`ERR_NO_CLEARED_SUBSCRIPTIONS`); and `cleared_escrow_cents` must be at least `minimum_gross_cents`, measured on the cleared basis only — `pledged_gross_cents` is never accepted here (`ERR_ESCROW_BELOW_MINIMUM`). Locking re-derives the aggregates and the waterfall so `projected_reimbursement_cents` and `projected_net_release_cents` are current, and increments the raise's `version` by one.

The **release waterfall** has two buckets and is re-derived on every escrow-affecting change as well as one final time at release. The gross figure is `cleared_escrow_cents` — never the pledged figure. Bucket one is the organizational-expense reimbursement, capped at five percent of that **gross** figure: five percent of gross cleared escrow is rounded half up to whole cents to form the cap, and `projected_reimbursement_cents` is the smaller of `organizational_expense_claim_cents` and that cap. Bucket two is everything left over: `projected_net_release_cents` is the gross figure minus `projected_reimbursement_cents`, an exact subtraction, so gross always reconciles to reimbursement plus net. The cap base is gross cleared escrow and *not* escrow net of the reimbursement; the alternative reading, under which the reimbursement may not exceed five percent of the capital actually released, is a self-referential fixed point and yields a different answer whenever the claim is above the cap. When the claim is below the cap the two readings coincide, so the divergence is only observable with an over-cap claim — and only the gross-based reading is correct.

**Releasing escrow** is the `EscrowAgent`'s last act and moves the raise from `ESCROW_LOCKED` to `RELEASED`. The raise must be `ESCROW_LOCKED`, and a release attempted on any other status is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_LOCKED`. The group must be in `PRE_OPENING`, failing which the release is refused with `ERR_GROUP_NOT_IN_PRE_OPENING`. Both of the group's filings must have `pre_opening_clearance` true, failing which the release is refused with `ERR_PRE_OPENING_CONDITIONS_OPEN`. And the money must actually be enough to open the bank: `projected_net_release_cents` must be at least the `opening_tier1_capital_cents` of the business plan pinned on the group's chartering-authority filing, failing which the release is refused with `ERR_NET_RELEASE_BELOW_PLAN_CAPITAL`. That last check sits at the very bottom of the ladder, so a release attempted on a raise that is still `OPEN` *and* whose net release would also be short reports `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_LOCKED` — the state error, not the money error. On success the waterfall is run one final time and `released_net_cents` is set to the value of `projected_net_release_cents` at that instant, freezing it; the raise's `version` increments by one.

Abandonment unwinds everything. When the group is moved to `ABANDONED`, every subscription of the group's raise whose `funding_state` is `FUNDED` becomes `REFUNDED`; because those subscriptions left `FUNDED`, the raise's `cleared_escrow_cents`, `funded_gross_cents`, `pledged_gross_cents`, `subscribed_shares` and `refunded_gross_cents` are re-derived; and because cleared escrow has fallen to zero, the raise's own `status` becomes `REFUNDED`, with its `version` incremented by one. A raise in `STRUCTURING`, `OPEN` or `ESCROW_LOCKED` all follow this same path to `REFUNDED`.

### 3.5. Two Filings, Two Authorities, One Project

A de novo bank needs two independent blessings, and the service models them as two rows rather than two fields on one row, because they advance at different speeds and either can regress while the other holds. A `CharterCounsel` prepares each filing by naming the `group_id`, the `authority` receiving it — `CHARTERING_AUTHORITY` for the charter application, `DEPOSIT_INSURER` for the deposit-insurance application — and the `pinned_business_plan_id`, which is the plan version the filing will be judged against. Exactly one filing per authority per group is permitted, so a second filing to the same authority is refused with `ERR_FILING_AUTHORITY_DUPLICATE`. The pinned plan must belong to the same group as the filing and must be in `SUBMITTED` or `PINNED` status: a `DRAFT` plan that has never been submitted, or a `SUPERSEDED` plan that has been replaced, cannot be put in front of a regulator, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PINNED_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`. A new filing begins in `DRAFT` with `info_request_round` 0, `filed_at` null and `decision_at` null; the condition posture is run against its empty condition set, so `condition_total_count`, `open_blocking_condition_count` and `settled_condition_count` are all 0 and `pre_opening_clearance` is false. The parent group's `version` increments by one.

Filing is a `CharterCounsel` action, and it is the same action for a first submission and for a re-submission after an information request. The filing must be in `DRAFT` or `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED`; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_FILING_NOT_FILEABLE`. The group must be in `FILING_READY` or `UNDER_REVIEW`. On success `status` becomes `FILED` and `filed_at` is set to the current logical clock — on a re-filing, `filed_at` is overwritten with the newer instant, so it always names the most recent submission and never the first. When this is the first of the group's filings to reach `FILED`, the group moves from `FILING_READY` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and the group's `version` increments by one; a second filing reaching `FILED` while the group is already `UNDER_REVIEW` leaves the group untouched. The filing's `version` increments by one.

An examiner records a `decision`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `decision_note`. Only the examiner of that filing's own authority may decide it, and every decision sets `decision_at` to the current logical clock and increments the filing's `version` by one. Four decisions exist, and each is legal only from specific statuses; a decision the current status does not permit is refused with `ERR_FILING_DECISION_NOT_ALLOWED`.

`ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` is legal only from `FILED` and starts the **information-request cycle**, which is the one genuinely cyclic path in this service: the filing goes back to counsel, counsel re-files, the examiner may request information again. The cycle is bounded by a ceiling of three rounds, and the accounting is precise. When the filing's `info_request_round` is currently less than 3, the decision increments `info_request_round` by one and moves `status` to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED`. When `info_request_round` is already exactly 3, the very same request does **not** produce a fourth information round and is **not** rejected — the ceiling converts it into an outright denial, so `status` becomes `DENIED` with the full denial cascade below. Rounds one, two and three are therefore information requests; the fourth attempt closes the file. `info_request_round` is never reset, so a filing that consumed rounds early carries that history for the rest of the project, and a system-initiated return to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` caused by a reopened condition consumes exactly the same budget as an examiner-initiated one.

`CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` is legal only from `FILED` and is the pivot of the whole project. When the filing's `authority` is `CHARTERING_AUTHORITY`, its pinned business plan moves from `SUBMITTED` to `PINNED` and that plan's `version` increments by one — this is the moment the plan becomes the fixed benchmark for heightened supervision. The filing's condition posture is recomputed. And because the group's stage is compound, the group advances only when **both** filings are `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` at the same instant: on that second approval the group moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `opening_blocked_reason` reset to `NONE` and its `version` incremented by one. The first approval on its own moves nothing at the group level.

`DENIED` is legal only from `FILED` and unwinds the project. The group reverts from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ORGANIZING` with `opening_blocked_reason` set to `FILING_INFO_REQUESTED`, every business plan of the group whose `status` is `PINNED` reverts to `SUBMITTED` with a version bump, and the group's `version` increments by one. One authority's denial therefore un-pins the plan that the *other* authority may still be relying on, which is exactly the coupling the two-filing model is meant to expose.

`FINAL_APPROVED` is legal only from `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` and is the last regulatory act before opening. Three further gates apply. The filing's `open_blocking_condition_count` must be 0, and a filing with any unsettled blocking condition is refused with `ERR_PRE_OPENING_CONDITIONS_OPEN`. The group must be in `PRE_OPENING`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_GROUP_NOT_IN_PRE_OPENING`. And at least one readiness examination of the group must have `status` `PASSED`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_READINESS_EXAM_NOT_PASSED`. `DENIED` and `FINAL_APPROVED` are terminal: no decision, and no cascade, can move a filing out of either.

One further transition into `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` is driven by the system rather than by an examiner, and it is the mechanism by which condition rework pulls a project backwards. When a filing that is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` sees its `open_blocking_condition_count` rise from 0 to a positive number — because a settled blocking condition was reopened or failed — the filing itself moves from `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED`, `info_request_round` increments by one, `pre_opening_clearance` becomes false, and the filing's `version` increments by one. Because that filing has left `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, a group standing in `PRE_OPENING` falls back to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `opening_blocked_reason` set to `FILING_INFO_REQUESTED`, every readiness examination of that group whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED`, and the group's `version` increments by one.

### 3.6. Conditions of Approval and the Rework Loop

Conditions of approval are how an examiner grants approval before the work is finished, and they are the only place in the service where settled work can be deliberately unsettled. Conditions hang off a filing and are attached by that filing's own examiner, so `CharteringExaminer` may attach only to a `CHARTERING_AUTHORITY` filing and `InsuranceExaminer` only to a `DEPOSIT_INSURER` filing. The filing must be in `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`; attaching to a filing in any other status — including a `FILED` filing that has not yet been decided, and a `FINAL_APPROVED` filing that is already closed — is refused with `ERR_CONDITIONS_NOT_ATTACHABLE`. The payload carries `condition_code`, the authority's short code, 1 to 40 non-blank characters and unique among that filing's conditions, with a duplicate refused by `ERR_CONDITION_CODE_DUPLICATE`; `category`, one of `CAPITAL`, `MANAGEMENT`, `POLICY`, `VENDOR` or `INSURANCE`, which determines which downstream regression re-fails the condition; `is_pre_opening_blocking`, true when the condition must be settled before the group may enter pre-opening; and `deadline_utc`, the deadline for evidence, which must be strictly after the current logical clock — a deadline equal to the clock is already too late and is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_DEADLINE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). A new condition begins with `status` `OPEN`, `evidence_reference` null and `adjudication_count` 0, then the parent filing's four posture fields are recomputed and the filing's `version` increments by one.

Remediation evidence is submitted by `CharterCounsel` or by a `LeadOrganizer` and carries a single `evidence_reference` string, which must be at least 20 characters long — a bare ticket number is not an audit trail. The condition must be in `OPEN` or `FAILED` — the second of those is what makes rework possible, since a failed condition can be re-evidenced without being reopened — and any other status is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_OPEN`. The current logical clock must be strictly before `deadline_utc`; a submission at exactly the deadline instant is late and is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 409). On success `status` becomes `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` and `evidence_reference` is overwritten with the supplied value, so it always reflects the most recent submission. The parent filing's posture is recomputed and both the condition's and the filing's `version` increment by one. Note that `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` is itself a blocking status: submitting evidence does not reduce `open_blocking_condition_count`, it merely moves the condition sideways within the blocking set.

Adjudication is the examiner's disposition of a condition, supplied as `disposition`, and every adjudication — including one that pulls a condition backwards — increments `adjudication_count` by one, recomputes the parent filing's `condition_total_count`, `open_blocking_condition_count`, `settled_condition_count` and `pre_opening_clearance`, and increments both versions by one. Four dispositions exist, each legal only from specific statuses; a disposition the current status does not permit is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_DISPOSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.

`SATISFIED` is legal only from `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED`: the examiner has reviewed the evidence and accepted it. `FAILED` is also legal only from `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` and rejects the evidence, leaving the condition in the blocking set and open to a fresh submission. `WAIVED` is legal from `OPEN`, `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` or `FAILED` and is terminal — a waived condition can never be reopened — but it is barred outright for any condition whose `is_pre_opening_blocking` is true: an examiner may not waive away a pre-opening blocker, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_CONDITION_NOT_WAIVABLE`. `REOPENED` is legal only from `SATISFIED` and is the compensating half of the rework loop: it returns the condition's `status` to `OPEN`, so it must restore `open_blocking_condition_count` to exactly the value it held before the condition was satisfied. `REOPENED` is additionally refused when the parent filing has already reached `FINAL_APPROVED`, with `ERR_FILING_ALREADY_FINAL` — final approval closes the file for good.

Both `FAILED` and `REOPENED` can therefore push a settled blocking condition back into the blocking set, and when that raises the filing's `open_blocking_condition_count` from 0 while the filing is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, the filing cascades to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` with `info_request_round` incremented by one, which in turn pulls a `PRE_OPENING` group back to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `opening_blocked_reason` `FILING_INFO_REQUESTED` and cancels the group's `SCHEDULED` examinations. A single adjudication of one condition can thus move four rows.

The filing's four posture fields are derived from that filing's conditions and nothing else. `condition_total_count` is the number of conditions attached to the filing in any status whatsoever. `settled_condition_count` is the number whose `status` is `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED`. `open_blocking_condition_count` is the number whose `is_pre_opening_blocking` is true **and** whose `status` is `OPEN`, `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` or `FAILED`; a non-blocking condition is counted in the total and, once settled, in the settled count, but it can never hold `open_blocking_condition_count` above zero no matter how long it stays open. `pre_opening_clearance` is true exactly when the filing's own `status` is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` or `FINAL_APPROVED` **and** `open_blocking_condition_count` is 0; a filing sitting in `FILED` with no conditions at all has zero open blockers and still has `pre_opening_clearance` false, because clearance is a statement about an approved filing, not about an empty condition list. Two identities must hold after every adjudication and after every cascade that touches a condition: `condition_total_count` always equals the number of conditions actually attached to the filing, and `settled_condition_count` plus the number of unsettled conditions always equals `condition_total_count`. `REOPENED` conserves the total and moves one unit from the settled side to the open blocking side; nothing in the service may ever change `condition_total_count` other than attaching a new condition.

### 3.7. The Operational Build-Out: Board Policies, Vendors and Core Conversion

While the applications are under review the organizers must build an actual bank, and three tracks run in parallel: the board must adopt policies, the vendors must be contracted, and the core system must be converted inside a booked window.

**Board policies** exist in four required domains — `LENDING`, `COMPLIANCE_BSA_AML`, `INFORMATION_SECURITY` and `RISK_MANAGEMENT` — and all four must be accepted by the chartering examiner before the group may enter pre-opening. A policy is drafted by a `LeadOrganizer` or an `IndependentDirector` and carries `group_id`, `domain`, `drafted_by_organizer_id` naming the organizer who authored it, and `document_title`, 1 to 160 non-blank characters. The named drafter must be an organizer of the same group as the policy, and a drafter from another group is refused with `ERR_DRAFTER_NOT_IN_GROUP`. Drafting is refused entirely once the group's `stage` is `OPEN` or `ABANDONED` (`ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`). A new policy begins in `DRAFT` with `adopted_by_organizer_id`, `adopted_at` and `finding_note` all null, and the parent group's `version` increments by one.

Board adoption is where segregation of duties bites. Adoption is performed by a `LeadOrganizer` or `IndependentDirector`, and the policy must be in `DRAFT`; any other status is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ADOPTABLE`. Four further gates apply, and their relative order is fixed. The acting principal must be an organizer of the policy's own group. The acting principal must be **qualified**: the organizer identified by `X-User-ID` must have a `qualification_status` of `QUALIFIED` or `WAIVER_GRANTED`, and a `PENDING` or `DISQUALIFIED` organizer may not adopt anything, which is refused with `ERR_ORGANIZER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The acting principal may not be the drafter: `X-User-ID` must differ from the policy's `drafted_by_organizer_id`, and a self-adoption is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` regardless of which of the two adopting roles is used. And no other policy of the same group and the same `domain` may already be in `BOARD_ADOPTED` or `EXAMINER_ACCEPTED`, with a duplicate refused by `ERR_POLICY_DOMAIN_ALREADY_ADOPTED`. The qualification gate is evaluated *before* the domain-uniqueness gate, so an unqualified organizer trying to adopt a second policy in an already-adopted domain is told about the qualification problem and never about the domain collision. On success `status` becomes `BOARD_ADOPTED`, `adopted_by_organizer_id` is set from `X-User-ID`, `adopted_at` is set to the current logical clock, and the policy's `version` increments by one.

The chartering examiner then reviews an adopted policy, supplying an `outcome` and an optional `finding_note` which is stored on the row and overwrites any earlier note. The policy must be in `BOARD_ADOPTED` or `EXAMINER_ACCEPTED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REVIEWABLE`. An `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` moves `BOARD_ADOPTED` to `EXAMINER_ACCEPTED`. An `outcome` of `REJECTED` is accepted from either `BOARD_ADOPTED` or `EXAMINER_ACCEPTED` and moves the policy to `REJECTED`, which is terminal: a rejected policy can never be revived, and remediation means drafting a brand-new policy for the same domain — which the domain-uniqueness gate now permits, precisely because a `REJECTED` policy no longer counts as adopted.

Rejecting a policy that had already been accepted is the **deepest cascade in the service**, and each link is caused by the previous one rather than by the original call. First, the policy's `status` becomes `REJECTED` and its `finding_note` is stored. Second, because the policy became `REJECTED`, every approval condition whose `category` is `POLICY` and whose `status` is `SATISFIED` on the group's `CHARTERING_AUTHORITY` filing becomes `FAILED`, with `adjudication_count` incremented by one on each. Third, because those conditions became `FAILED`, that filing's `open_blocking_condition_count` and `settled_condition_count` are recomputed and `pre_opening_clearance` becomes false. Fourth, because `open_blocking_condition_count` rose from 0 while the filing was `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, the filing moves to `ADDITIONAL_INFO_REQUESTED` and `info_request_round` increments by one — consuming a round from the same three-round budget an examiner would have used. Fifth, because that filing left `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, a group standing in `PRE_OPENING` falls back to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `opening_blocked_reason` set to `FILING_INFO_REQUESTED`. Sixth, because the group left `PRE_OPENING`, every readiness examination of the group whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED`. The policy's `version` increments by one, as does every other row the cascade touched. Note that the cascade reaches only the chartering authority's filing and only `POLICY`-category conditions; the deposit insurer's filing is untouched by a policy rejection, and a `POLICY` condition that was merely `OPEN` rather than `SATISFIED` is left where it is.

**Vendor contracts** are registered by a `CharterCounsel` with `group_id`, `service_domain` — one of `CORE_PROCESSING`, `DIGITAL_BANKING`, `BSA_MONITORING` or `ITSM` — `vendor_name` (1 to 120 non-blank characters), `annual_cost_cents` (zero or more), `contract_term_months` (from 1 to 120 months, so a term of ten years is the longest permitted), `due_diligence_score` (an integer rating from 0 to 100) and `criticality`, one of `CRITICAL`, `SIGNIFICANT` or `ROUTINE`. Registration is refused once the group's `stage` is `OPEN` or `ABANDONED` (`ERR_GROUP_STAGE_LOCKED`). A new contract begins in `EVALUATING` with `active_window_count` 0, and the group's `version` increments by one.

A contract is moved along by naming a `target_status`, and a target the current status cannot reach is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_STATUS_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. `SELECTED` is reachable only from `EVALUATING`, and carries two extra gates: no other contract of the same group and the same `service_domain` may already be `SELECTED` or `SIGNED` — a second core provider is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_DOMAIN_ALREADY_SELECTED` — and a contract whose `criticality` is `CRITICAL` must have a `due_diligence_score` of at least 70, with a score of 69 on a critical vendor refused by `ERR_DUE_DILIGENCE_INSUFFICIENT` while the same score on a `SIGNIFICANT` or `ROUTINE` vendor passes untouched. `SIGNED` is reachable only from `SELECTED`. `TERMINATED` is reachable from `EVALUATING`, `SELECTED` or `SIGNED`, and is terminal, but it is barred once the group's `stage` is `OPEN`: a live bank may not drop a vendor through this surface, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_TERMINATION_BLOCKED_AFTER_OPENING`. Termination cascades: every conversion window of that contract whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED` with its own `version` bumped by one, and because those windows released their intervals the contract's `active_window_count` is recomputed to 0. Every advance increments the contract's `version` by one.

**Conversion windows** book the core-migration outage against a contract. Reservation is a `CharterCounsel` action naming `vendor_contract_id`, the concurrency guard `expected_vendor_contract_version`, and the half-open interval `window_start_utc` (inclusive) and `window_end_utc` (exclusive). The parent contract must be `SELECTED` or `SIGNED` — an `EVALUATING` or `TERMINATED` contract cannot be booked against, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_CONVERTIBLE`. The end must be strictly after the start, so a zero-length window is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_END_NOT_AFTER_START` (HTTP 422), and the start must be at or after the current logical clock, so a window starting exactly now is fine but one starting a second ago is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_START_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). `duration_hours` is derived as the elapsed seconds between the two instants divided by 3600 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and it may not exceed 72.00 hours — a three-day outage is the ceiling, so exactly 72.00 hours is accepted and 72.01 is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_TOO_LONG` (HTTP 422).

Capacity on a contract is an **aggregate of two**, not a pairwise exclusion: the vendor can run two migrations at once. The occupants are that contract's own windows whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`; `CANCELLED` windows release their interval entirely, and windows booked against a sibling contract are irrelevant even when both contracts belong to the same group. An occupant overlaps the requested interval when the requested start is strictly before the occupant's end **and** the occupant's start is strictly before the requested end. The reservation is refused with `ERR_CONVERSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when the number of overlapping occupants is 2 or more; one overlapping occupant is therefore accepted and becomes the second concurrent window, and a third simultaneous booking is the one that fails. Because the interval is half-open with no buffer at all, a window starting at exactly the instant another ends is adjacent rather than overlapping and consumes no capacity. On success the window begins in `RESERVED`, the contract's `active_window_count` is recomputed as the number of its `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` windows — counting all occupants, not merely the overlapping ones — and the contract's `version` increments by one.

Confirming a window is a separate `CharterCounsel` act. The window must be in `RESERVED`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_CONFIRMABLE`. The parent contract must be `SIGNED`: being `SELECTED` is enough to *reserve* a window but not to *confirm* one, and confirming against a merely selected contract is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED`. On success `status` becomes `CONFIRMED` and the window's `version` increments by one; because both `RESERVED` and `CONFIRMED` count as occupants, confirmation does not change `active_window_count`.

### 3.8. Pre-Opening Readiness and Opening Day

The **readiness examination** is the regulator's on-site field visit, and it is scheduled by a `CharteringExaminer` against the group with `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc` and `exam_scope`, which is either `FULL_SCOPE` for the main visit or `TARGETED_FOLLOW_UP` for a return trip. The group must be in `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` or `PRE_OPENING`; scheduling against a group at any other stage is refused with `ERR_GROUP_NOT_EXAM_READY`. As with conversion windows, the end must be strictly after the start (`ERR_WINDOW_END_NOT_AFTER_START`, HTTP 422) and the start may not precede the current logical clock (`ERR_WINDOW_START_IN_PAST`, HTTP 422). Only one `FULL_SCOPE` examination may be live at a time: if the group already has a `FULL_SCOPE` examination in `SCHEDULED` or `PASSED` status, a second one is refused with `ERR_FULL_SCOPE_EXAM_EXISTS` — note that a `FULL_SCOPE` examination that came back `DEFICIENT`, or one that was `CANCELLED`, does *not* block a replacement. Conversely a `TARGETED_FOLLOW_UP` may only be booked as a genuine follow-up: at least one prior examination of the group must have `status` `DEFICIENT`, and a follow-up with nothing to follow up on is refused with `ERR_NO_DEFICIENT_EXAM`.

Examiners also need time to write the report, so every examination occupies **more than its own window**. Each examination's `buffer_end_utc` is derived as its `window_end_utc` plus 48 hours — two full days of report-writing time — and the interval it occupies runs from `window_start_utc` inclusive to `buffer_end_utc` exclusive. Note that the buffer is added to the end only and never to the start. The occupants of the group's calendar are its examinations whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DEFICIENT` or `PASSED`; only a `CANCELLED` examination releases its interval, so a deficient visit continues to hold its slot as a matter of record. Capacity is exactly one — the examination team is modelled per organizing group, and examinations of other groups never conflict. A conflict exists when the proposed start is strictly before an occupant's `buffer_end_utc` **and** that occupant's `window_start_utc` is strictly before the proposed `buffer_end_utc`; the request is then refused with `ERR_EXAM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the interval is half-open, a new examination starting at exactly an existing examination's `buffer_end_utc` is permitted, while one starting a single second earlier conflicts. Both windows are buffer-extended before the comparison, so two examinations must be separated by the full 48 hours in whichever order they fall. A new examination begins in `SCHEDULED` with `finding_count` 0, and the group's `version` increments by one.

Recording the result is a `CharteringExaminer` action carrying an `outcome` and a `finding_count`. The examination must be in `SCHEDULED`, and any other status — including one already resolved — is refused with `ERR_EXAM_NOT_RESOLVABLE`. The group must be in `PRE_OPENING` at the moment of resolution, which is a stricter requirement than for scheduling, and a group that has fallen back is refused with `ERR_GROUP_NOT_IN_PRE_OPENING`. The current logical clock must be at or after the examination's `window_start_utc`, so a visit cannot be reported before it has begun; a result recorded at exactly the start instant is accepted, while one recorded a second earlier is refused with `ERR_EXAM_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`. An `outcome` of `PASSED` additionally requires the supplied `finding_count` to be no more than 3: exactly three findings can still be recorded as a pass, a fourth cannot, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FINDINGS_EXCEED_PASS_LIMIT`. There is no such limit on a `DEFICIENT` result. In both cases `finding_count` is stored on the row and the examination's `version` increments by one; `PASSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

A `DEFICIENT` result knocks the project backwards. The group falls from `PRE_OPENING` to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `opening_blocked_reason` set to `EXAM_DEFICIENT` — distinguishing this regression from a filing-driven one — every *other* examination of the group whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` with its version bumped, and the group's `version` increments by one. The deficient examination itself is left untouched as the audit record. Recovery is a new `TARGETED_FOLLOW_UP` examination scheduled against the now-`CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` group, and the group may cycle from deficient to a fresh examination to passed any number of times; each cycle needs a window that clears every prior examination's buffer-extended interval, since the deficient ones never release theirs.

The group's `stage` is advanced by a `CharterCounsel` naming a `target_stage`, and this single operation is where every stage gate lives. A `target_stage` the current stage cannot reach at all is refused with `ERR_STAGE_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, which is evaluated before any gate specific to the target. Two stages are never a legal `target_stage` because they are entered only by cascade: `UNDER_REVIEW`, which the group enters when the first of its filings is filed, and `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, which the group enters when the second of its filings is conditionally approved or when it falls back out of pre-opening.

Advancing `ORGANIZING` to `FILING_READY` requires the group's `composition_status` to be `COMPOSITION_MET` (`ERR_GROUP_COMPOSITION_INCOMPLETE`), then its `management_composite_score` to be at least 70.00 (`ERR_MANAGEMENT_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`), then at least one business plan of the group in `SUBMITTED` status (`ERR_NO_SUBMITTED_BUSINESS_PLAN`) — in that order. On success `opening_blocked_reason` is reset to `NONE`.

Advancing `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` to `PRE_OPENING` requires four things, evaluated in this order. Both of the group's filings must be in `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` (`ERR_FILINGS_NOT_CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`). The group's capital raise must be in `ESCROW_LOCKED` or `RELEASED`, since escrow must be committed before pre-opening (`ERR_ESCROW_NOT_LOCKED`). There must be an `EXAMINER_ACCEPTED` policy document in every one of the four domains — all four, not merely the ones the group considers relevant (`ERR_POLICY_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`). And every vendor contract of the group whose `criticality` is `CRITICAL` must be in `SIGNED` status, with at least one such critical contract having `service_domain` `CORE_PROCESSING`; a group with no critical contract at all therefore fails this gate, as does one whose critical contracts are all in non-core domains (`ERR_CRITICAL_VENDOR_NOT_SIGNED`). On success `opening_blocked_reason` is reset to `NONE`.

Advancing `PRE_OPENING` to `OPEN` is opening day. No filing may have an unsettled blocking condition, and one that does is refused with `ERR_PRE_OPENING_CONDITIONS_OPEN`. Then four conditions are checked together: at least one readiness examination of the group must have `status` `PASSED`, both filings must be in `FINAL_APPROVED`, the capital raise must be in `RELEASED`, and `opening_blocked_reason` must be `NONE`; a failure of any of them is reported as `ERR_READINESS_EXAM_NOT_PASSED`. On success `stage` becomes `OPEN`, `supervision_started_at` is set to the current logical clock, and `supervision_year_index` is recomputed — which, at zero elapsed days, makes it 1. `OPEN` is terminal.

Advancing to `ABANDONED` is permitted from `ORGANIZING`, `FILING_READY`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` and from nowhere else, so a group that has reached pre-opening or has opened cannot be abandoned through this surface. Abandonment carries no other gate, and its cascade returns the investors' money as described for the capital raise. `ABANDONED` is terminal.

One cascade is shared by every departure from pre-opening regardless of cause: leaving `PRE_OPENING` for any target stage cancels every `SCHEDULED` readiness examination of the group and bumps each cancelled examination's `version` by one. Every successful advance increments the group's `version` by one.

### 3.9. Three Years of Heightened Supervision

A newly chartered bank is supervised more closely for its first three years, and the service measures that period from the logical clock rather than from a calendar. While `supervision_started_at` is null — that is, at every stage before the group opens — `supervision_year_index` is 0. Once it is set, the elapsed time from `supervision_started_at` to the current logical clock is taken in whole seconds and divided by 86400 with the remainder discarded, giving whole elapsed days; that day count is divided by 365 with the remainder discarded and one is added, giving the raw supervision year. If the raw year is 1, 2 or 3 the index takes that value; if it is 4 or more the group has graduated and `supervision_year_index` becomes 0 again. The boundaries are therefore exact: elapsed day 0 through day 364 is year 1, day 365 is the first day of year 2, day 730 is the first day of year 3, and day 1095 is the first day outside the supervision window, at which point the index returns to 0. Because a zero index means both "not yet open" and "graduated", it is `supervision_started_at` that distinguishes the two. Moving the logical clock modifies no row of its own accord; the index is instead recomputed lazily whenever a single organizing group is read, and again as the first step of creating a deviation request, so the index a client sees always reflects the clock at the moment of the read and not the clock at the moment of opening.

Inside that window, a `LeadOrganizer` reports any departure from the pinned plan as a **deviation request** carrying `group_id`; `baseline_plan_id`, the pinned plan the deviation is measured against; `metric`, one of `ASSET_GROWTH`, `LOAN_GROWTH`, `DEPOSIT_GROWTH` or `BRANCH_COUNT`; `actual_value`, the observed figure, which is in cents for the three financial metrics and a plain count for `BRANCH_COUNT` and must be zero or more; and `narrative`, the organizer's explanation, which must be at least 20 characters long and at most 500. The group must be in `OPEN`, so a deviation cannot be filed before opening day (`ERR_GROUP_NOT_OPEN`). The named baseline plan must belong to that group and must be in `PINNED` status — a merely `SUBMITTED` plan is not a supervisory benchmark, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_PLAN_NOT_PINNED`. Only one deviation may be pending per metric per supervision year: if the group already has a deviation request in `SUBMITTED` status whose `metric` is the same **and** whose `supervision_year_index_at_request` equals the group's current `supervision_year_index`, a second is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_ALREADY_PENDING`. A pending request on a different metric does not block, a pending request on the same metric from an earlier supervision year does not block, and requests that have reached `AUTO_ACCEPTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `ESCALATED` never block. And the supervision window must still be open: the group's `supervision_year_index` must be between 1 and 3 inclusive, so a group whose index has fallen back to 0 after three years is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — a check that applies equally to submitting a deviation and to deciding one, so a request left pending until the window lapses can no longer be decided at all. Creation recomputes the group's supervision year first and then the magnitude, and increments the group's `version` by one.

The **magnitude** of a deviation is computed at creation and frozen, because the plan year it is measured against must not drift as the clock advances. Three fields are frozen on the row: `supervision_year_index_at_request`, a copy of the group's `supervision_year_index` at that instant; `baseline_value`, the plan figure that index selected; and `deviation_bps`. The baseline is chosen by **two** independent selectors at once — the `metric` picks which family of plan fields to read, and the frozen supervision year picks which year within that family. For `ASSET_GROWTH` the baseline is the plan's projected assets for that year; for `LOAN_GROWTH`, its projected loans for that year; for `DEPOSIT_GROWTH`, its projected deposits for that year. So a request filed in supervision year 2 with metric `LOAN_GROWTH` reads `projected_loans_y2_cents`, and the same request filed a year later reads `projected_loans_y3_cents` instead. `BRANCH_COUNT` is the exception: it has no per-year variants and always reads `projected_branch_count` whatever the supervision year.

From there the deviation is a relative distance in basis points. When the selected baseline is zero the ordinary division is undefined, so a special rule applies: `deviation_bps` is 0 when `actual_value` is also 0 — no plan, no deviation — and otherwise 2500, which is exactly the escalation threshold and therefore treats any activity at all against a zero baseline as a maximal departure. When the baseline is non-zero, take the absolute difference between `actual_value` and the baseline, multiply by 10000, divide by the absolute value of the baseline, and floor the result to a whole number of basis points. The measure is symmetric: overshooting the plan by a given proportion and undershooting it by the same proportion produce identical values. `requires_regulator_approval` is then true exactly when `deviation_bps` is greater than or equal to 1000 basis points — a ten percent departure.

That flag decides whether the request needs a human at all, and the decision is applied **at creation**, in the same operation. When `requires_regulator_approval` is false the request self-clears: its `status` is `AUTO_ACCEPTED` immediately, `decision_note` stays null, and no examiner ever sees it. When the flag is true the request is left in `SUBMITTED` awaiting a supervisory decision. A deviation of exactly 1000 basis points therefore requires approval, while 999 does not.

A `CharteringExaminer` disposes of a pending request with a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`, which is stored on the row. The request must be in `SUBMITTED`; anything else, including a request that already self-cleared as `AUTO_ACCEPTED`, is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_NOT_DECIDABLE`. `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are always available. `ESCALATED` is reserved for the most serious departures and requires `deviation_bps` to be at least 2500 basis points — a twenty-five percent departure — so escalating a smaller deviation is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, and a request at exactly 2500 may be escalated while one at 2499 may not. All four of `AUTO_ACCEPTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `ESCALATED` are terminal; a decision increments the request's `version` by one and changes nothing on the group.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted under the `/de-novo` prefix and all of them exchange JSON in the envelope described in section 2.2. Status codes follow two rules. An endpoint that brings a **new resource** into existence returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`. Every other successful call — reads, and the action endpoints that move an existing resource through its lifecycle — returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource in `data`, or with the computed result object in the case of the two computed endpoints.
