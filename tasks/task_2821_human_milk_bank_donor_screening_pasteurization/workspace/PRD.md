# Human Milk Bank Screening Pasteurization API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An HMBANA-member human milk bank sits between two populations that never meet: lactating donors who express and freeze surplus milk at home, and hospitalized preterm infants for whom donor milk is a prescribed therapy rather than a convenience. Everything between those two populations is a chain of custody, and every link in that chain has to be reconstructable months later — which donor's milk went into which pool, which pasteurizer held that pool and for how many seconds above 62.5 °C, which culture cleared it, which lot was bottled from it, and which infant received which bottle. Today that chain is kept in a mixture of paper batch sheets, freezer logs and spreadsheet trackers, and when a donor discloses a deferrable condition after the fact, the bank cannot answer the only question that matters — *what is still on the shelf that traces back to her?* — without an all-day manual reconciliation.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those artifacts. The service owns donor qualification (health-history scoring, serologic testing and prescribing-physician approval), the reimbursed cold-chain shipment leg, pooling across donors for nutritional balance, pasteurization run control with temperature/time verification and post-pasteurization culture, bottling with deterministic lot identity and expiration dating, and the dispensing of prescriptions against a NICU-first allocation policy with outpatient waitlists, processing-fee billing and charitable subsidy accounting. It also owns the unhappy path: donor deferral events with retrospective lookback recall that walks forward through pools, lots, fills and the subsidy ledger in a single request.

Two design constraints shape the whole specification. First, the bank is a regulated compliance environment: no operation is permitted to leave the record in a state that a later auditor could not explain, which is why nearly every write in this API is either rejected outright or applied together with the full set of derived consequences it implies. Second, several people work the same batch sheet at the same time — an intake clerk logging containers while a medical director re-dispositions the same cooler, a technician sizing an allocation while a colleague adds a component to the same pool — so the service is explicit about which writes require the client to prove that it read the current state of a shared parent before it decided what to write. The API is mounted under the `/milk-bank` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Donor-Scoped Access

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight roles below, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the named role is real but is not authorized for the endpoint being addressed — the authorized set for each endpoint is the one given in the §4 table — the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `Donor`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `MedicalDirector`, `ProcessingTechnician`, `QualityAssuranceOfficer`, `DispensingPharmacist`, `Prescriber` and `MilkBankAdmin`. Their remits divide the bank as follows. A `Donor` is a milk donor acting on her own record. An `IntakeCoordinator` enrolls donors, takes health histories, and receives and logs incoming coolers. A `MedicalDirector` owns clinical eligibility — serology, physician approvals, qualification, requalification, deferrals and the disposition of a quarantined cooler. A `ProcessingTechnician` builds pools, schedules and drives the pasteurizer, and bottles lots. A `QualityAssuranceOfficer` records post-pasteurization cultures and releases lots to inventory. A `DispensingPharmacist` fills prescriptions from released lots, voids incorrect fills and runs allocation cycles. A `Prescriber` is the hospital NICU or outpatient clinician who submits and cancels prescriptions and who cannot dispense. A `MilkBankAdmin` is the operational administrator who owns subsidy programs, allocation cycles and the logical clock, and who holds broad read access but no clinical or processing authority.

When `X-User-Role` is `Donor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Donor` row itself; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's donor reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Donor` may read her own donor record and her own questionnaire, serology, physician-approval and deferral histories, may register her own coolers, and may read her own coolers — the comparison is against `Donor.id` for the donor record itself and against the `donor_id` carried by a `Shipment`, a `Donation`, a `HealthHistoryQuestionnaire`, a `SerologyPanel`, a `PhysicianApproval` or a `DeferralEvent` for everything else. A `Donor` who addresses a resource belonging to another donor is rejected with `ERR_DONOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403); the one exception is the donor-scoped list channels, which are silently filtered down to the acting donor's own rows rather than rejected. Registering a cooler is likewise scoped: a `Donor` may only submit a `donor_id` equal to her own `X-User-ID`.

When `X-User-Role` is any of `IntakeCoordinator`, `MedicalDirector`, `ProcessingTechnician`, `QualityAssuranceOfficer`, `DispensingPharmacist`, `Prescriber` or `MilkBankAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record these identifiers where the specification says so — `qualified_by_user_id` on a `Donor`, `recorded_by_user_id` on a `SerologyPanel`, a `PhysicianApproval` or a `DeferralEvent`, `received_by_user_id` on a `Shipment`, `committed_by_user_id` on a `Pool`, `recorded_by_user_id` on a `TemperatureReading`, `completed_by_user_id` and `culture_recorded_by_user_id` on a `PasteurizationRun`, `released_by_user_id` on a `MilkLot`, `prescriber_user_id` on a `Prescription` and `dispensed_by_user_id` on a `Dispense` — and it compares those recorded identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties (§2.9), but it never resolves them to entities.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination controls in the query string. `limit` bounds the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the zero-based index of the first row returned; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Each list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window) inside the `meta` object, and carries `response_type` of `"array"`.

List ordering is a property of the collection being read, and each ordering below is total — a deterministic tie-break always follows the primary key so that two identical requests never disagree about page boundaries:

- Donors: `created_at` descending, then `donor_code` ascending.
- A donor's health-history questionnaires: `created_at` descending, then `submitted_at` descending.
- A donor's serology panels: `collected_on` descending, then `created_at` descending.
- A donor's physician approvals: `approved_on` descending, then `created_at` descending.
- A donor's deferral events: `effective_from` descending, then `created_at` descending.
- Shipments: `created_at` descending, then `tracking_code` ascending.
- Donations: `created_at` descending, then `container_label` ascending.
- Pools: `created_at` descending, then `pool_code` ascending.
- Pool components, whether listed by pool or by donation: `created_at` ascending.
- Pasteurization runs: `scheduled_start_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
- A run's temperature readings: `offset_seconds` ascending.
- Milk lots: `expiration_date` ascending, then `lot_code` ascending.
- Dispenses, whether listed by lot or by prescription: `created_at` ascending.
- Prescriptions: `priority_tier` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, then `prescription_code` ascending.

Filters are optional and combine conjunctively. The donor list accepts `status` and `zone_code`. A donor's questionnaire, physician-approval and deferral lists accept `status`; the deferral list additionally accepts `deferral_type`, and the serology list accepts `panel_status` instead. The shipment list accepts `donor_id` and `status`. The donation list accepts `donor_id`, `shipment_id`, `status` and `milk_class`. The pool list accepts `status` and `target_milk_class`; component lists accept `status`. The run list accepts `pool_id`, `run_status` and `pasteurizer_id`; the reading list accepts `qualifies`. The lot list accepts `run_id`, `release_status` and `milk_class`. The prescription list accepts `subsidy_program_id`, `status` and `priority_tier`; dispense lists accept `status`. These filters double as the reverse-reachability channels by which a client walks from a parent to its children, since the bank exposes no nested ownership URLs.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API creates is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and never derived from any business code. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, stamped once when the row is written and never changed thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only change a derived field. The resources that carry the full four-field set are `Donor`, `Shipment`, `Donation`, `Pool`, `PasteurizationRun`, `MilkLot`, `SubsidyProgram`, `Prescription` and `Dispense`. Six resources are immutable-by-design records of a single event and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations: `HealthHistoryQuestionnaire`, `SerologyPanel`, `PhysicianApproval`, `PoolComponent`, `TemperatureReading` and `DeferralEvent`. Those six do change their own `status` field when a later event supersedes, withdraws, expires or consumes them, but they expose no `version` and no `updated_at` with which to observe it.

All date-and-time reasoning in the bank runs off a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the clock is monotonic, so a requested instant earlier than the current one is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 409). Fields described as "the clock instant" (`qualified_at`, `submitted_at`, `received_at`, `committed_at`, `completed_at`, `released_at`) take the clock's current value at the moment of the write; fields described as "the clock date" take the calendar date component of that instant. Day arithmetic on dates is exact calendar addition, so leap days count, and all dates exchanged over the API use the `YYYY-MM-DD` form.

### 2.5. Ordered Rejection: How a Request Is Evaluated

Validation is strictly ordered, not parallel. The service evaluates the applicable checks in the sequence below, and the **first** check that fails determines the response; no later check runs, and no partial write survives a rejection. This matters because many endpoints are guarded by half a dozen rules at once, and the tests of this system distinguish implementations by which error a doubly-invalid request reports.

The global sequence is: (1) both auth headers present and non-empty; (2) the role is one of the eight declared roles; (3) the role is authorized for this endpoint; (4) a `Donor` acts only within her own scope; (5) every referenced id exists; (6) the request body is shape-valid; (7) numeric and date inputs are inside their declared ranges; (8) business codes declared unique are not already taken; (9) the logical clock does not move backwards; (10) the addressed entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared; (11) the optimistic-concurrency token matches the parent's current `version`; (12) clinical-safety and compliance rules; (13) temporal rules; (14) capacity and quantity constraints; (15) fiscal rules about subsidy capacity.

Existence resolution in step 5 runs **left to right**: path parameters first, in the order they appear in the path, and then body foreign keys in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. The leftmost missing reference wins, and the rejection is always `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) regardless of which reference was missing. A request for a component of a pool that does not exist therefore reports the missing pool, never the missing donation.

Shape validation in step 6 rejects a missing required field, a field of the wrong JSON type, a string that violates its declared pattern, or an enum value outside its declared set, all with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Fields the client is not permitted to write — every computed or system-maintained field — are **ignored** when they appear in a request body, never rejected, and so are unrecognized keys. Step 7 then checks declared numeric and date ranges and reports `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422); it applies to write requests only. Step 8 reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409) when `donor_code`, `tracking_code`, `pool_code`, `program_code` or `prescription_code` is already present anywhere in the bank, or when `container_label` repeats inside the same cooler.

Because the state gate at step 10 precedes the concurrency token at step 11, a request that is both stale and aimed at a wrongly-stated parent reports the state error, not the stale-version error. And because the state gate precedes every safety, temporal and quantity rule, an operation attempted against a terminal row never reaches the interesting rules at all.

### 2.6. Money, Millilitres and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of **whole cents**; there are no fractional-cent representations anywhere in the API, and no monetary field is ever returned as a string. Wherever a rounding step is called for, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero. Where a rounding sequence matters, this document states the order explicitly, because rounding a per-unit amount before multiplying and rounding a total afterwards are genuinely different numbers, and the bank's ledgers are reconciled against the former.

Volumes are whole millilitres throughout. Caloric density (`kcal_per_oz` on a donation, `component_kcal_per_oz` on a component, `projected_kcal_per_oz` on a pool, `kcal_per_oz` on a lot) is a decimal number; assayed input densities are recorded to one decimal place, and the pool's weighted projection is rounded half up to two decimal places at the single final division and at no earlier point. Temperatures are decimals to one decimal place. `coverage_pct` on a subsidy program is a fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive, expressed to two decimal places, and `tier_discount_pct` on a fill is one of the fractions `0.00`, `0.05` or `0.12`.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Shared Parents

Three write operations in the bank act on a parent row that a colleague may legitimately have advanced between the moment the client read the screen and the moment it submitted. None of those three can be protected by a state precondition, because in each case the second write is a perfectly legal operation on a parent in exactly the same state as the first. They are therefore protected by an optimistic-concurrency token: the client must send back the `version` of the parent it last observed, as a **non-optional** field in the request body, and the write is refused if that value does not equal the parent's current `version` at the moment of evaluation.

- Logging a container against a cooler requires `expected_shipment_version` to equal the parent `Shipment`'s current `version`. If it does not — because another container was logged against the same cooler, or because a `MedicalDirector` re-dispositioned it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which is how the intake clerk learns that the cooler's manifest has changed since the screen was loaded.
- Adding a component to a pool requires `expected_pool_version` to equal the parent `Pool`'s current `version`, and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This protects the technician who sized an allocation from a `projected_kcal_per_oz` and `distinct_donor_count` snapshot that another component write has already invalidated.
- Creating a fill against a prescription requires `expected_lot_version` to equal the current `version` of the `MilkLot` named by `lot_id` — note that the concurrency parent here is the lot, not the prescription — and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This prevents a pharmacist from committing a volume decision taken against an `available_ml` snapshot that a prior fill, a void or an allocation cycle has already changed.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a concurrency check. Where a version field is required, it is checked after the relevant state preconditions, as §2.5 sets out.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Propagation

The bank's most consequential operations are not the ones that write a single row. A reactive serology result, a deferral with a lookback window, a failed post-pasteurization culture and a scrapped pool each set off a chain in which every hop is caused by the hop before it and all hops complete inside the originating request. Two rules govern them all. First, **every row a cascade touches is mutated properly**: its own `status` field moves, its derived aggregates are recomputed from their definitions rather than patched incrementally, and — for the nine resources that carry them — its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1. A parent whose child changed and whose own derived totals therefore changed is itself a mutated row and takes a `version` increment too. Second, a cascade is **all-or-nothing**: if any hop cannot complete, the whole request is rejected and nothing is written.

Two bubble-up rules run at the end of every request that could satisfy them, rather than being triggered by any particular endpoint. A `Shipment` in `ACCEPTED` that has at least one `Donation` and whose every `Donation` has reached `PASTEURIZED`, `RECALLED` or `DISCARDED` moves to `CLOSED`, taking a `version` increment. A `MilkLot` in `RELEASED` whose recomputed `available_ml` has reached 0 moves to `EXHAUSTED`, taking a `version` increment; conversely a lot in `EXHAUSTED` whose recomputed `available_ml` rises above 0 returns to `RELEASED`, provided its `expiration_date` is on or after the current clock date, again taking a `version` increment.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties Across the Processing Chain

The bank's accreditation rests on the principle that no single operator can both produce a batch and certify it. Two checks enforce it, and both compare recorded opaque operator identifiers rather than roles. At lot release, the releasing `QualityAssuranceOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `completed_by_user_id` **and** from the run's `culture_recorded_by_user_id`; a match with either one is refused with `ERR_SOD_RELEASE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403). At fill time, the dispensing `DispensingPharmacist`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the prescription's `prescriber_user_id`; a match is refused with `ERR_SOD_DISPENSE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403). Both checks are unconditional: no override parameter exists, and no role bypasses them.

### 2.10. Per-Operation Check Ordering

The global sequence of §2.5 fixes the relative order of the check families. Within the families, the following operation-specific orderings are normative, because each of these operations is guarded by several rules that a single malformed request can trip at once.

**Qualifying a donor** enforces five checks in this order: the donor's own state gate first (`ERR_DONOR_STATE_INVALID`), then the accepted-questionnaire requirement (`ERR_HEALTH_HISTORY_INCOMPLETE`), then unexpired non-reactive serology (`ERR_SEROLOGY_MISSING_OR_EXPIRED`), then an active physician approval (`ERR_PHYSICIAN_APPROVAL_MISSING`), and only then the recomputed surveillance score ceiling (`ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`). A donor with neither serology nor approval on file therefore reports the serology failure.

**Adding a component to a pool** enforces, after existence and shape: the donation's own eligibility (`ERR_DONATION_STATE_INVALID`), then the pool's state gate (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`), then the concurrency token (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`), then the donor's qualification (`ERR_DONOR_NOT_QUALIFIED`), then the deferral-window overlap (`ERR_DONATION_IN_DEFERRAL_WINDOW`), then the raw-milk age ceiling (`ERR_DONOR_MILK_AGE_EXCEEDED`), then nutritional-class compatibility (`ERR_MILK_CLASS_MISMATCH`), then the single-pool exclusivity rule (`ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_ALLOCATED`), then the donation's unallocated volume (`ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_DONATION_REMAINING`), and last the pool's donor ceiling (`ERR_POOL_DONOR_CAP_EXCEEDED`).

**Committing a pool** enforces its state gate first, and then the four balance constraints in this exact order: emptiness (`ERR_POOL_EMPTY`) before donor count (`ERR_POOL_DONOR_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`) before nutritional range (`ERR_POOL_NUTRITION_OUT_OF_RANGE`) before total volume (`ERR_POOL_VOLUME_OUT_OF_RANGE`). The emptiness rule deliberately runs first so that a pool with no components reports emptiness rather than a nutrition range failure against an absent projection.

**Logging a container** enforces the duplicate-label check before the cooler's state gate (`ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`), which precedes the concurrency token, which precedes the donor's qualification.

**Filling a prescription** is the most heavily guarded operation in the bank. After existence and shape, the order is: the lot's state gate (`ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`), the prescription's state gate (`ERR_PRESCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID`), the concurrency token (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SOD_DISPENSE_CONFLICT`), nutritional suitability (`ERR_LOT_MILK_CLASS_UNSUITABLE`), the lot's expiration date (`ERR_LOT_EXPIRED`), the prescription's needed-by date (`ERR_PRESCRIPTION_PAST_NEEDED_BY`), the prescription's outstanding volume (`ERR_DISPENSE_EXCEEDS_PRESCRIPTION_REMAINING`), the lot's available volume (`ERR_DISPENSE_EXCEEDS_LOT_AVAILABLE`), the bank-wide NICU reserve (`ERR_NICU_RESERVE_PROTECTED`), and finally the two fiscal rules — subsidy programme availability (`ERR_SUBSIDY_PROGRAM_EXHAUSTED`) before subsidy headroom (`ERR_SUBSIDY_CAP_EXCEEDED`).

**Releasing a lot** enforces the lot's state gate, then the run's release eligibility (`ERR_RUN_NOT_RELEASE_ELIGIBLE`), then segregation of duties (`ERR_SOD_RELEASE_CONFLICT`). **Appending a reading** enforces the input ranges, then the run's state gate (`ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`), then the strictly-increasing offset rule (`ERR_READING_SEQUENCE_INVALID`). **Completing a run** enforces the run's state gate before the minimum-readings rule (`ERR_RUN_READINGS_INSUFFICIENT`). **Bottling a lot** enforces the run's state gate — which is where a thermally failed run is refused — before the pool-volume ceiling (`ERR_BOTTLING_EXCEEDS_POOL_VOLUME`). **Scheduling a run** enforces the pool's committed state (`ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`) before the pasteurizer slot check (`ERR_PASTEURIZER_SLOT_CONFLICT`). **Requalifying a donor** enforces the donor's state gate before the elapsed-deferral rule (`ERR_DEFERRAL_STILL_ACTIVE`). **Recording a deferral** enforces the `deferral_days` / `deferral_type` consistency rule as part of range validation, therefore before the donor's state gate.

### 2.11. Addressing Model: Flat Collections and Reverse Reachability

The bank exposes a **flat** resource topology. Every entity is addressed through its own top-level collection beneath `/milk-bank`, and parent-child links travel as identifier fields in request bodies rather than as nested URL ownership chains. A pool component is created by posting to the pool it belongs to while naming its `donation_id` in the body; a fill is created by posting to the prescription it satisfies while naming its `lot_id` in the body. Every path parameter in this API is spelled `{id}`, and where a path contains only one, that single identifier is resolved first as §2.5 requires.

The sub-resource list channels that do exist — a donor's questionnaires, serology panels, physician approvals and deferrals; a shipment's donations by filter; a donation's components; a pool's components; a run's readings; a lot's fills; a prescription's fills — exist purely as reverse-reachability read channels so that a client can walk from a parent to its children. They confer no ownership semantics and impose no additional validation beyond the resolution of the identifier in the path.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling a Donor and Triaging Her Health History

An `IntakeCoordinator` (or a `MilkBankAdmin`) enrolls a prospective donor by supplying four pieces of information: a bank-assigned `donor_code` written in the form `DNR-` followed by exactly four digits and unique across every donor the bank has ever enrolled; a `full_name` of between 1 and 120 characters; an `email` address, which must contain an `@` character and is the channel through which home-collection instructions are sent; and a `zone_code`, which is the courier reimbursement zone of her home address and must be one of `Z1`, `Z2` or `Z3`. The new donor begins her qualification lifecycle in `APPLICANT` with both risk scores at 0, a `reimbursement_balance_cents` ledger of 0, a `lifetime_accepted_volume_ml` of 0, a `temporary_deferral_count` of 0, a null `qualified_at`, a null `qualified_by_user_id`, a null `active_deferral_until_date` and `version` 1. The `zone_code` recorded here is durable in a specific sense: it is copied onto every cooler she subsequently ships, and it is the cooler's copy — not the donor's live value — that prices the reimbursement.

The first substantive step is the health-history questionnaire, which only an `IntakeCoordinator` may take. A submission carries five disclosures: `medication_flag_count`, the number of flagged medications, between 0 and 10; `travel_risk_flag`, true when the donor disclosed travel to a deferral-listed region in the previous twelve months; `tobacco_use`, true for any tobacco or nicotine use; `alcohol_units_per_week`, self-reported and between 0 and 40; and `months_postpartum`, the months elapsed since delivery, between 0 and 48. The submission is stamped with `submitted_at`, the clock instant at which it was taken. Wherever this specification refers to a donor's *newest* questionnaire, it means the one that sorts first by `created_at` descending with `submitted_at` descending as the tie-break.

Every questionnaire produces a **shared base score**, persisted on the questionnaire as `base_risk_points`, from five additive terms. Each flagged medication is worth 8 points. Disclosed travel adds a flat 25 points. Disclosed tobacco or nicotine use adds a flat 40 points. Alcohol is charged in tiers read off `alcohol_units_per_week`: exactly 0 units contributes nothing, 1 through 3 units contributes 5 points, 4 through 7 units contributes 15 points, and 8 units or more contributes 45 points. Postpartum time is charged only beyond the first twelve months: subtract 12 from `months_postpartum`, treat any negative result as 0, multiply what remains by 3 points per month, and cap the resulting term at 30 points. The five terms are summed to give `base_risk_points`.

Two different risk figures are then derived from that base, and they deliberately disagree. The first, a **pessimistic screening score**, is what the triage bands read. It adds a flat 20-point pending-serology penalty whenever the donor has no `SerologyPanel` whose `panel_status` is `NON_REACTIVE` and whose `expires_on` falls on or after the current clock date, and it charges 15 points for **each** of the donor's temporary deferral events whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, at full weight and regardless of how long ago they occurred. The sum of base, serology penalty and deferral history is then capped at 100. This figure is written both to the questionnaire's own `screening_risk_score` and to the donor's `screening_risk_score`.

The second figure, the **time-discounted surveillance score**, is what qualification reads. It never adds the serology penalty at all — serology is a hard precondition at qualification rather than a score term — and it discounts deferral history by age: each of the donor's temporary deferral events whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` contributes 15 points multiplied by one half raised to the power of (the whole number of calendar days from that event's `effective_from` to the current clock date, divided by 180). Those discounted contributions are summed at full real-valued precision, added to the base, rounded half up once to a whole number, and capped at 100. The result is stored on the donor as `surveillance_risk_score`. Because the surveillance variant omits the pending-serology penalty entirely and discounts every deferral contribution by a factor of at most one, `screening_risk_score` is never lower than `surveillance_risk_score` for the same stored data; both remain integers between 0 and 100. Only non-superseded questionnaire data feeds either figure — the newest questionnaire is the sole questionnaire input to both.

Triage then assigns the questionnaire's own `status` from the pessimistic screening score against two bands. A score of 60 or less accepts the questionnaire as `ACCEPTED`. A score above 60 but not above 85 marks it `INFO_REQUESTED`, meaning the coordinator must go back to the donor for clarification. A score above 85 marks it `AUTO_DEFERRED`. Whenever a donor submits a questionnaire while she already has one on file, that previous newest questionnaire's `status` becomes `SUPERSEDED` in the same request — a questionnaire is never deleted, and a superseded questionnaire no longer feeds any calculation.

The donor's own `status` moves with the band. A questionnaire that lands `ACCEPTED` advances the donor to `SCREENING`, whether she was in `APPLICANT`, `SCREENING` or `INFO_REQUESTED` when it was taken; the `INFO_REQUESTED → SCREENING` direction is what makes health-history revision a genuine loop rather than a one-way funnel. A questionnaire that lands `INFO_REQUESTED` puts (or keeps) the donor in `INFO_REQUESTED`, so `APPLICANT → INFO_REQUESTED`, `SCREENING → INFO_REQUESTED` and the `INFO_REQUESTED → INFO_REQUESTED` self-loop are all declared transitions and a donor may bounce between `INFO_REQUESTED` and `SCREENING` any number of times. Every one of these submissions refreshes both of the donor's stored scores and increments her `version` by 1.

A questionnaire that lands `AUTO_DEFERRED` sets off a three-hop cascade inside the single request. The questionnaire is written with `status` `AUTO_DEFERRED`; that in turn creates a system-authored `DeferralEvent` whose `reason_code` is `AUTO_HIGH_RISK`, whose `deferral_type` is `TEMPORARY`, whose `effective_from` is the current clock date, whose `deferral_days` is 90, whose `lookback_days` is 0, whose `recall_scope` is `NONE` and whose `recorded_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`; that new event in turn increments the donor's `temporary_deferral_count` by 1 and sets her `active_deferral_until_date` to 90 days after its `effective_from`; and because the deferral count has changed, her `surveillance_risk_score` is recomputed once more before the request completes. The donor lands in `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`, and her `version` increases by 1 for the request as a whole. Note that with `recall_scope` of `NONE` and a `lookback_days` of 0 this automatic deferral recalls nothing — it is forward-looking only.

A donor in `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` accepts no operation at all: that state is terminal. Health-history submission is admitted only from `APPLICANT`, `SCREENING` or `INFO_REQUESTED`; qualification only from `SCREENING`; requalification only from `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`; deferral recording from `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `QUALIFIED` or `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`. Any request that addresses a donor outside the admitted set for the operation is rejected with `ERR_DONOR_STATE_INVALID`, and that same state gate also guards the recording of serology and of physician approvals.

### 3.2. Clinical Clearance: Serology, Physician Approval and Qualification

A `MedicalDirector` records the donor's five-marker serologic panel. The submission carries `collected_on`, the draw date of the specimen, and five results — `hiv_result` for HIV-1/2, `htlv_result` for HTLV-I/II, `hbv_result` for hepatitis B, `hcv_result` for hepatitis C and `syphilis_result` — each one of `NON_REACTIVE`, `REACTIVE` or `INDETERMINATE`. From those five the server derives the panel's `panel_status` by a strict precedence: `REACTIVE` if **any** marker is `REACTIVE`; otherwise `INDETERMINATE` if **any** marker is `INDETERMINATE`; otherwise `NON_REACTIVE`. The panel's `expires_on` is 180 days after `collected_on`, and the acting director's identifier is kept as `recorded_by_user_id`.

The prescribing physician's sign-off is a separate record, also authored by a `MedicalDirector`. It carries a `physician_npi` — the National Provider Identifier, exactly ten digits — and `approved_on`, the date the physician signed. Its `valid_until` is 365 days after `approved_on`, and its `status` begins at `ACTIVE`. An approval leaves `ACTIVE` in exactly two ways: the clock sweep sets it to `EXPIRED` once its `valid_until` falls before the clock date, and a deferral recorded with `reason_code` `PHYSICIAN_WITHDRAWAL` sets every one of that donor's `ACTIVE` approvals to `WITHDRAWN`. Its acting director is likewise kept as `recorded_by_user_id`.

Qualification is the `MedicalDirector`'s decision to admit the donor's milk to the processing stream, and it advances the donor `SCREENING → QUALIFIED`. It requires four conditions beyond the state gate, checked in the order §2.10 fixes. The donor's newest questionnaire must have `status` `ACCEPTED` — an `INFO_REQUESTED` or `AUTO_DEFERRED` questionnaire as the current one is disqualifying, and having no questionnaire at all is equally disqualifying, both reported as `ERR_HEALTH_HISTORY_INCOMPLETE`. There must exist at least one `SerologyPanel` for her with `panel_status` `NON_REACTIVE` and `expires_on` on or after the current clock date, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEROLOGY_MISSING_OR_EXPIRED`. There must exist at least one `PhysicianApproval` for her with `status` `ACTIVE` and `valid_until` on or after the current clock date, failing which the answer is `ERR_PHYSICIAN_APPROVAL_MISSING`. And her `surveillance_risk_score`, recomputed fresh at this moment rather than read from the last write, must be 60 or less, failing which the answer is `ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`. On success the recomputed surveillance score is stored, `qualified_at` is stamped with the clock instant, `qualified_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting director's `X-User-ID`, and the donor's `version` increases by 1.

A `REACTIVE` panel is the one clinical result that acts on its own authority. Recording it permanently defers the donor — she moves to `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` from `APPLICANT`, `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `QUALIFIED` alike — and the server authors a `DeferralEvent` on her behalf with `reason_code` `REACTIVE_SEROLOGY`, `deferral_type` `PERMANENT`, `effective_from` set to the current clock date, `deferral_days` 0, `lookback_days` 365, `recall_scope` `FULL_CHAIN` and `recorded_by_user_id` the literal string `system`. That event then executes the full retrospective recall described in §3.3, stamping its five cascade counters, and the donor's `version` increases by 1. `REACTIVE_SEROLOGY` and `AUTO_HIGH_RISK` are the two `reason_code` values that only ever appear on system-authored events; a human director records `MEDICATION`, `ILLNESS`, `TRAVEL`, `DONOR_REQUEST` or `PHYSICIAN_WITHDRAWAL`.

### 3.3. Deferral Events, Lookback Recall and the Road Back to Screening

A deferral is how the bank records that a donor's milk is, for a period or forever, unsuitable. A `MedicalDirector` records one by supplying a `reason_code` from the seven values named above; a `deferral_type` of either `TEMPORARY` or `PERMANENT`; an `effective_from` date on which the deferring condition began; a `deferral_days` count between 0 and 3650; a `lookback_days` window between 0 and 1095; and a `recall_scope` of `NONE`, `DONATIONS_ONLY` or `FULL_CHAIN`. The two duration fields are constrained against the type: `deferral_days` must be exactly 0 when `deferral_type` is `PERMANENT`, and must be at least 1 when it is `TEMPORARY`. A mismatch is a range violation reported as `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`, evaluated before the donor's state gate. Every deferral event is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, and its `recorded_by_user_id` is the acting director's identifier — or the literal string `system` for the two events the server authors on its own.

A `TEMPORARY` deferral moves the donor to `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` from `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `QUALIFIED`, and is also admitted as a `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED → TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED` self-loop against a donor who is already deferred, in which case her `status` simply does not change. In every one of those cases her `temporary_deferral_count` rises by 1, her `surveillance_risk_score` is recomputed against the changed deferral history, her `active_deferral_until_date` is recomputed, any recall implied by `recall_scope` runs, and her `version` increases by 1. A `PERMANENT` deferral moves her to `PERMANENTLY_DEFERRED` from `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `QUALIFIED` or `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED`, runs the same recall, and likewise increments her `version`. When the `reason_code` is `PHYSICIAN_WITHDRAWAL`, every `PhysicianApproval` of that donor whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `WITHDRAWN` in the same request.

The `active_deferral_until_date` deserves careful reading, because it is **not** the latest end date on file. It is the end date of the single **most recently created** `ACTIVE` `TEMPORARY` deferral event of that donor — ordered by `created_at` descending, with the row `id` never used as a tie-break — computed as that event's `effective_from` plus its `deferral_days`, and it is null whenever the donor has no `ACTIVE` `TEMPORARY` deferral at all. It is recomputed on every deferral write, at requalification, and on every clock sweep. The consequence is deliberate: if a long-dated deferral was recorded first and a short one afterwards, the short one governs the requalification gate, so the donor can return to screening and even to `QUALIFIED` while the long-dated event remains `ACTIVE` — and that still-`ACTIVE` event will keep refusing her milk at pooling time through the lookback-window rule of §3.6.

A `recall_scope` of `NONE` recalls nothing; the deferral is purely forward-looking. `DONATIONS_ONLY` executes the first hop and stops. `FULL_CHAIN` executes all five. The hops are as follows, and each is caused by the one before it rather than by the original request.

The first hop looks at the deferred donor's `Donation` rows. Every donation of hers whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `ALLOCATED` or `PASTEURIZED` and whose `expressed_on` falls inside the **closed** interval beginning `lookback_days` before `effective_from` and ending on `effective_from` itself moves to `RECALLED`, taking a `version` increment. The number of donations moved is stamped on the event as `recalled_donation_count`.

The second hop follows those recalled donations to their `PoolComponent` rows. A component whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` becomes `VOIDED`, and its `allocated_volume_ml` returns to its donation's `remaining_ml`; if the pool holding it is in `DRAFT`, `BALANCED` or `COMMITTED`, that pool moves to `SCRAPPED` and its `distinct_donor_count`, `total_volume_ml` and `projected_kcal_per_oz` are recomputed. A component that is already `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED` is left exactly where it is — its milk is already in bottles, so there is nothing to return — and its pool keeps its `PASTEURIZED` status. Only the pools actually moved to `SCRAPPED` are counted in the event's `quarantined_pool_count`.

The third hop follows the implicated components to bottled inventory. Every `MilkLot` bottled from a `PasteurizationRun` whose pool holds an implicated component, and whose `release_status` is `HOLD`, `RELEASED` or `EXHAUSTED`, moves to `RECALLED` with a `version` increment. The count is stamped as `recalled_lot_count`.

The fourth hop follows those recalled lots to the fills drawn from them. Every `Dispense` against such a lot whose `status` is `DISPENSED` moves to `RECALLED`. Its `subsidy_cents` is credited back to its programme's `consumed_cents`, which may flip that programme from `EXHAUSTED` back to `OPEN`; the same amount accumulates into the event's `reversed_subsidy_cents`. Its `volume_ml` is subtracted from its prescription's `cumulative_dispensed_ml`, raising `remaining_ml` accordingly. Crucially, the volume is **not** credited back to the lot's `dispensed_ml`: recalled milk is destroyed rather than returned to inventory, so `available_ml` on a recalled lot does not recover. The number of fills moved is stamped as `recalled_dispense_count`.

The fifth hop follows those reversals back to the waitlist. Any `Prescription` whose `cumulative_dispensed_ml` has dropped below its `prescribed_ml` leaves `FILLED` and returns to the queue — to `QUEUED` when its `cumulative_dispensed_ml` has reached 0 and to `PARTIALLY_FILLED` otherwise — with its `waitlist_position` recomputed along with every other position in its priority bucket, and with a `version` increment.

Requalification is the compensating door back in, and only a `MedicalDirector` may open it. It advances the donor `TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED → SCREENING`, and its single additional precondition is that the current clock date be **strictly later** than her `active_deferral_until_date`; on the last deferred day itself the answer is still `ERR_DEFERRAL_STILL_ACTIVE`. A null `active_deferral_until_date` satisfies this check vacuously, since it means a clock sweep has already expired her last active temporary deferral. On success, every `ACTIVE` `TEMPORARY` deferral event of hers whose `effective_from` plus `deferral_days` falls before the current clock date is set to `EXPIRED`; her `active_deferral_until_date` is then recomputed from whatever `ACTIVE` `TEMPORARY` events remain, becoming null when none do; her `surveillance_risk_score` is recomputed against the now further-decayed history; and her `version` increases by 1. This closes the qualification cycle: a donor may traverse `QUALIFIED → TEMPORARILY_DEFERRED → SCREENING → QUALIFIED` as many times as her history requires.

### 3.4. The Cold Chain: Dispatch, Receipt and Courier Reimbursement

Once a donor is `QUALIFIED`, she may pack and ship a cooler; either the donor herself or an `IntakeCoordinator` registers it. The registration carries the `donor_id` of the shipping donor, a `tracking_code` written as `TRK-` followed by exactly four digits and unique across every cooler the bank has ever seen, a `container_count` between 1 and 8 insulated containers, a `uses_dry_ice` flag recording whether she packed dry ice, a `declared_volume_ml` between 50 and 9600 taken from her home-collection form, and `shipped_on`, the date she handed the cooler to the courier. The donor's `zone_code` is copied onto the cooler at this moment and never changes afterwards. If the owning donor is not `QUALIFIED` at the instant of registration, the cooler is refused with `ERR_DONOR_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The new cooler begins `IN_TRANSIT` at `version` 1, with `final_reimbursement_cents`, `credited_reimbursement_cents`, `excursion_minutes`, `arrival_max_temp_celsius`, `verified_volume_ml`, `received_at` and `received_by_user_id` all null.

Reimbursement is calculated twice from one shared arithmetic skeleton, and both passes read only the cooler's own `zone_code`, `container_count` and `uses_dry_ice` — plus, on the second pass, its `excursion_minutes`. The cooler's contents are explicitly **not** an input: the bank reimburses the courier leg, not the milk, so an accepted cooler with no containers logged against it still reimburses in full. The skeleton runs as follows. Start from the zone base, which is 1200 cents for `Z1`, 1850 cents for `Z2` and 2600 cents for `Z3`. Divide that base by the `container_count` to obtain each container's share of it, and — only when `uses_dry_ice` is true — multiply that share by 1.15. Add a flat 275 cents of per-container handling. Multiply the result by one minus the excursion penalty rate. **Then round that per-container amount half up to a whole cent, and only afterwards multiply by `container_count`.** The order of those last two operations is normative: summing the unrounded per-container amounts and rounding the total once at the end yields a different figure whenever the zone base does not divide evenly by the container count, and the ledgers are reconciled against the per-container rounding.

The two passes differ in one term only. At dispatch the data logger has not been read and no excursion is known, so the excursion penalty rate is pinned to 0 and the result is stamped on the cooler as `estimated_reimbursement_cents`; nothing is credited to the donor at this point. At receipt the logger has been downloaded, so the excursion penalty rate becomes the **lesser** of 0.60 and 0.02 multiplied by `excursion_minutes` — meaning each excursion minute costs two percent of the pre-penalty amount, and the penalty stops growing once it has consumed sixty percent of it. The receipt-pass result is stamped as `final_reimbursement_cents`, and is strictly lower than the dispatch estimate whenever `excursion_minutes` is 1 or more.

Receiving is an `IntakeCoordinator`'s act and reports three logger figures: `excursion_minutes`, the number of minutes the cooler spent above −8.0 °C; `arrival_max_temp_celsius`, the peak temperature the logger recorded; and `verified_volume_ml`, the volume actually measured at the bench, which is recorded alongside — and may differ from — the donor's `declared_volume_ml`. All three are stamped onto the cooler along with `received_at`, the clock instant of the call, and `received_by_user_id`. The cooler's disposition then forks three ways, evaluated in this order. If the peak temperature exceeded 4.0 °C, or the excursion exceeded 120 minutes, the cooler is `REJECTED`. Otherwise, if the peak temperature was above −8.0 °C, or the excursion was above 30 minutes, the cooler is `QUARANTINED`. Otherwise — peak temperature at or below −8.0 °C and excursion at or below 30 minutes — it is `ACCEPTED`.

What the donor is actually paid follows the fork. `credited_reimbursement_cents` equals the full `final_reimbursement_cents` for an `ACCEPTED` cooler; equals `final_reimbursement_cents` multiplied by one half and rounded half up to a whole cent for a `QUARANTINED` cooler; and equals 0 for a `REJECTED` one. Whatever that figure is, it is added to the donor's `reimbursement_balance_cents` — which is by definition the sum of `credited_reimbursement_cents` across her coolers in `ACCEPTED`, `QUARANTINED` or `CLOSED` — and her `version` increases by 1 alongside the cooler's. A `REJECTED` cooler credits nothing and leaves her ledger untouched.

A `QUARANTINED` cooler waits for a `MedicalDirector`, who supplies a `disposition` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. `ACCEPT` rejoins the accepted path — `QUARANTINED → ACCEPTED` — by raising `credited_reimbursement_cents` to the full `final_reimbursement_cents` and crediting the donor's ledger with **only the difference** between the full amount and what was already credited; the ledger is topped up, never double-credited. `REJECT` moves the cooler `QUARANTINED → REJECTED`, debits the donor's `reimbursement_balance_cents` by the partial amount previously credited, and sets `credited_reimbursement_cents` to 0. Either way both the donor's and the cooler's `version` increase by 1. On `REJECT`, every `Donation` of the cooler still in `ELIGIBLE` is moved to `DISCARDED`; in practice that set is always empty, because container logging is admitted only against an `ACCEPTED` cooler and a cooler can never return to `ACCEPTED` once dispositioned to `REJECTED`.

Containers are logged one at a time by an `IntakeCoordinator`, and only against an `ACCEPTED` cooler — any other cooler state is `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Each log carries a `container_label` of 1 to 16 characters, unique within that cooler; `expressed_on`, the date the milk was expressed; a `volume_ml` between 50 and 1200; a `kcal_per_oz` assay between 14.0 and 26.0 recorded to one decimal place; a `milk_class` of either `PRETERM` or `TERM`; and the `expected_shipment_version` concurrency token described in §2.7. The donor who owns the cooler must still be `QUALIFIED` at this moment, or the log is refused with `ERR_DONOR_NOT_QUALIFIED` — milk from a donor deferred since dispatch does not enter the stream. The new `Donation` starts `ELIGIBLE` with its `remaining_ml` equal to its `volume_ml` and with its own `donor_id` copied from the cooler's, never to be changed. That donation's volume raises the donor's `lifetime_accepted_volume_ml`, which is the sum of `volume_ml` over her donations in `ELIGIBLE`, `ALLOCATED` or `PASTEURIZED`; both the donor's and the cooler's `version` increase by 1. A donation's `remaining_ml` is thereafter always its `volume_ml` less the sum of `allocated_volume_ml` across its non-`VOIDED` components, and never goes below 0.

A cooler in `ACCEPTED` closes itself, per §2.8, as soon as it has at least one container logged and every one of those containers has reached `PASTEURIZED`, `RECALLED` or `DISCARDED`. `REJECTED` and `CLOSED` are terminal cooler states.

### 3.5. Building a Pool: Donor Spread and Nutritional Balance

No infant receives one donor's milk. A `ProcessingTechnician` opens a **pool** by supplying a `pool_code` written as `POOL-` followed by exactly four digits and unique across every pool, and a `target_milk_class` of `PRETERM`, `TERM` or `MIXED`. The server assigns the pool a `pool_sequence`, a monotonic 1-based counter equal to 1 plus the number of pools that already exist — it is assigned in pool-creation order, is never derived from the pool's `id`, and later reappears in the middle segment of every lot code bottled from this pool. A new pool is `DRAFT` at `version` 1 with `distinct_donor_count` 0, `total_volume_ml` 0, `projected_kcal_per_oz` null, `bottled_volume_ml` 0, and null `committed_at` and `committed_by_user_id`.

Milk enters the pool one draw at a time. The technician names the `donation_id` to draw from and the `allocated_volume_ml` to draw, between 50 and 1200 millilitres, together with the `expected_pool_version` token of §2.7. The resulting component freezes two values from the donation at the moment it is written: `component_kcal_per_oz`, copied from the donation's `kcal_per_oz`, and `component_donor_id`, copied from the donation's `donor_id`. Freezing the assay is what makes a committed pool's nutrition immune to a later correction on the donation, and freezing the donor reference is what the pool's donor-spread count reads. Every component is born `ALLOCATED`.

A draw is guarded by eight business rules beyond the shape checks, in the order §2.10 fixes. The donation must be `ELIGIBLE`. The pool must be `DRAFT` or `BALANCED`. The donation's owning donor must still be `QUALIFIED`, or the draw is `ERR_DONOR_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The donation's `expressed_on` must **not** fall inside the closed lookback interval — from `lookback_days` before `effective_from` through `effective_from` inclusive — of **any** `ACTIVE` `DeferralEvent` of its donor, or the draw is `ERR_DONATION_IN_DEFERRAL_WINDOW`; this is the rule that a still-`ACTIVE` long-dated deferral keeps enforcing even after its donor has been requalified. The donation's `expressed_on` must also be on or after the date exactly 365 days before the current clock date, because raw milk more than a year old cannot be pooled; a violation is `ERR_DONOR_MILK_AGE_EXCEEDED`. The donation's `milk_class` must be compatible with the pool's `target_milk_class`: a `PRETERM` pool accepts only `PRETERM` donations, a `TERM` pool only `TERM` donations, and a `MIXED` pool accepts either, with a mismatch reported as `ERR_MILK_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The donation must have no existing `ALLOCATED` component in any **different** pool, since a container cannot be committed to two pools at once — that is `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_ALLOCATED`, and it does not prevent a second draw from the same donation into the *same* pool. The `allocated_volume_ml` must not exceed the donation's current `remaining_ml`, or the draw is `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_DONATION_REMAINING`. And the draw must not push the pool's `distinct_donor_count` above 5, reported as `ERR_POOL_DONOR_CAP_EXCEEDED`; a second draw from a donor already represented in the pool does not raise that count and so is never refused by this rule.

On success the drawn volume is debited from the donation's `remaining_ml` and the donation's `version` increases by 1. The donation advances `ELIGIBLE → ALLOCATED` **only** when the debit brings its `remaining_ml` to exactly 0; a partially drawn container stays `ELIGIBLE` and remains available for a further draw.

The pool's three aggregates are then recomputed from scratch — never patched incrementally — and this recomputation happens on exactly three occasions: when a component is added, when the pool is scrapped, and whenever a recall cascade voids one of its components. At each of those moments only components whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` are counted; `VOIDED`, `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED` and `CONSUMED_LOST` components are excluded, which is why a scrapped-and-rebuilt pool re-derives its nutrition from scratch instead of accumulating history. When no such component remains, `projected_kcal_per_oz` becomes null, `total_volume_ml` becomes 0 and `distinct_donor_count` becomes 0. Otherwise `total_volume_ml` is the sum of `allocated_volume_ml` across the counted components; `distinct_donor_count` is the number of distinct `component_donor_id` values among them; and `projected_kcal_per_oz` is the volume-weighted average caloric density — multiply each counted component's `allocated_volume_ml` by its `component_kcal_per_oz`, sum those products at exact decimal precision with no intermediate rounding, divide that sum by `total_volume_ml`, and round the single quotient half up to two decimal places. Since a component only ever leaves `ALLOCATED` for `VOIDED` (which always triggers a recomputation) or for a consumed status (which never does), these three figures stand thereafter as a record of the pool's non-`VOIDED` composition.

Balance is a derived verdict, not a stored flag, and it is re-evaluated immediately after every component write. A pool is balanced when all three conditions hold at once: its `distinct_donor_count` is at least 3, its `projected_kcal_per_oz` lies inside the **closed** range from 19.00 through 21.00, and its `total_volume_ml` lies inside the **closed** range from 1500 through 6000 millilitres. A `DRAFT` pool that satisfies all three moves `DRAFT → BALANCED`; a `BALANCED` pool whose newest component pushes any one of the three back outside its range moves `BALANCED → DRAFT`. The pool oscillates between the two as freely as the technician adds milk, so eligibility to commit is never monotonic.

Committing freezes the pool for pasteurization and is the technician's declaration that the batch sheet is final. The state gate admits both `DRAFT` and `BALANCED` — deliberately, so that an unbalanced pool reports which specific constraint it failed rather than a generic state error. The four constraints are then evaluated in the fixed order of §2.10: the pool must hold at least one `ALLOCATED` component (`ERR_POOL_EMPTY`); its `distinct_donor_count` must be at least 3 (`ERR_POOL_DONOR_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`) and, as the add-time cap already guarantees, no more than 5; its `projected_kcal_per_oz` must lie in the closed range 19.00 through 21.00, where exactly 19.00 and exactly 21.00 both pass (`ERR_POOL_NUTRITION_OUT_OF_RANGE`); and its `total_volume_ml` must lie in the closed range 1500 through 6000 (`ERR_POOL_VOLUME_OUT_OF_RANGE`). A `DRAFT` pool always fails at least one of those four and therefore never reaches `COMMITTED`. On success the pool moves `BALANCED → COMMITTED`, `committed_at` is stamped with the clock instant, `committed_by_user_id` with the acting technician's `X-User-ID`, and the pool's `version` increases by 1.

Scrapping is the compensating action, available to a `ProcessingTechnician` or a `QualityAssuranceOfficer` against a pool in `DRAFT`, `BALANCED` or `COMMITTED`. Every component still `ALLOCATED` becomes `VOIDED`; each affected donation has the voided `allocated_volume_ml` credited back to its `remaining_ml` and, unless it has been recalled, returns from `ALLOCATED` to `ELIGIBLE`, so a container can be allocated, released and re-allocated to a different pool without any volume drift. The pool's three aggregates are recomputed, with `projected_kcal_per_oz` falling to null when no component remains. Any `PasteurizationRun` of that pool whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `VOIDED`, which frees its occupied interval on the pasteurizer so a later run may take the slot. The pool lands in `SCRAPPED` — a terminal state, alongside `PASTEURIZED` and `SCRAPPED_CULTURE` — and its `version` increases by 1.

### 3.6. Pasteurization Run Control: Three Dimensions on One Row

A committed pool is loaded into a physical pasteurizer, and the `ProcessingTechnician` schedules the run by naming the `pool_id`, the `pasteurizer_id` of the machine — written as `PAST-` followed by exactly two digits — the `scheduled_start_at` instant at which the cycle begins, and the `cycle_minutes` length of the heating cycle, between 30 and 120 minutes. The pool must be `COMMITTED`; any other pool state is refused as `ERR_POOL_STATE_INVALID`. A run occupies its machine for its cycle **plus** a fifteen-minute cleaning buffer, so its occupied interval begins at `scheduled_start_at` and ends `cycle_minutes` plus 15 minutes later. That interval is **half-open**: the ending instant itself is not occupied, so a run that starts exactly when a previous run's buffer ends is permitted. A new run whose interval overlaps the interval of any other run on the same `pasteurizer_id` whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `BOTTLED` is refused with `ERR_PASTEURIZER_SLOT_CONFLICT`; a `VOIDED` run occupies nothing, which is precisely why scrapping a pool frees the slot.

A new run is born in `SCHEDULED` at `version` 1, with `thermal_status` `PENDING`, `culture_status` `NOT_COLLECTED`, `release_eligibility` `BLOCKED`, `reading_count` 0, and null `longest_hold_seconds`, `peak_celsius`, `completed_at`, `completed_by_user_id`, `culture_cfu_per_ml`, `culture_organism` and `culture_recorded_by_user_id`. Those first four state fields are the run's **three independent dimensions plus their derived verdict**: `run_status` tracks physical progress, `thermal_status` tracks temperature/time verification, `culture_status` tracks microbiology, and each evolves on its own schedule. `release_eligibility` is `ELIGIBLE` if and only if `thermal_status` is `PASSED` **and** `culture_status` is `PASSED`, and `BLOCKED` in every other combination — including the very common case of a thermally passed run whose culture is still `NOT_COLLECTED`. It is recomputed immediately after any change to either of the two dimensions it depends on.

Datalogger samples arrive one at a time from the technician, each carrying an `offset_seconds` between 0 and 14400 measured from the start of the heating cycle and a `celsius` bath temperature between 0.0 and 90.0 recorded to one decimal place, with the acting technician kept as `recorded_by_user_id`. Each sample's `qualifies` flag is derived at write time and is true exactly when its `celsius` lies inside the **closed** holding band from 62.5 through 65.0 degrees. A sample's `offset_seconds` must be strictly greater than the `offset_seconds` of **every** sample already recorded for that run; an equal or lower offset is refused with `ERR_READING_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The first sample moves the run `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`; every later sample is admitted as the `IN_PROGRESS → IN_PROGRESS` self-loop. Each sample raises the run's `reading_count` by 1, refreshes `peak_celsius` to the maximum `celsius` across the run's samples, and increments the run's `version` by 1.

Completing the run closes the thermal phase. It requires `run_status` `IN_PROGRESS` and at least 2 samples on file, failing which the answer is `ERR_RUN_READINGS_INSUFFICIENT`. The verification then measures the **longest contiguous certified hold** across the temperature curve, as follows. Take the run's samples in ascending `offset_seconds` order — offsets are strictly increasing within a run, so that ordering is total and needs no tie-break. Walk them in that order carrying two pieces of state: the offset at which the current in-band segment opened (initially unset) and the longest hold found so far (initially 0). For each sample in turn: if the sample does not qualify, the current segment is discarded and no segment is open. Otherwise, if no segment is open, this sample's `offset_seconds` opens one. Otherwise, if this sample's `offset_seconds` minus the `offset_seconds` of the sample **immediately preceding it in the walk** is greater than 600 seconds, the segment is restarted at this sample's offset — an unmonitored gap of more than ten minutes cannot be certified even when the samples on both sides of it qualify — and the longest hold is not updated in that step. Otherwise, the longest hold becomes the greater of its current value and this sample's `offset_seconds` minus the open segment's start offset. Note that the "immediately preceding sample" is the previous sample in the walk whether or not it qualified. After the last sample, the surviving longest hold is stored as `longest_hold_seconds`, a whole number of seconds; the longest hold is therefore a property of the worst dip in the curve, not of the total time spent in band.

The verdict follows directly: `thermal_status` becomes `PASSED` when `longest_hold_seconds` is 1800 seconds or more, and `FAILED` otherwise. Either way the run advances `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` — physical progress does not depend on the thermal verdict — `peak_celsius` reflects the maximum `celsius` over the run's samples, `completed_at` is stamped with the clock instant, `completed_by_user_id` with the acting technician's `X-User-ID`, `release_eligibility` is recomputed, and the run's `version` increases by 1.

The post-pasteurization culture is a `QualityAssuranceOfficer`'s record and is admitted while the run's `run_status` is `COMPLETED` or `BOTTLED`, provided its `culture_status` is still `NOT_COLLECTED`; a second culture on the same run is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The submission carries `culture_cfu_per_ml`, the colony-forming units per millilitre from the plate, and `culture_organism`, the organism named on the report or the literal string `NONE` for a no-growth result. Both are stamped on the run along with `culture_recorded_by_user_id`, and the verdict is a single threshold: `culture_status` becomes `FAILED` when `culture_cfu_per_ml` is 10 or more, and `PASSED` otherwise. `release_eligibility` is recomputed from the pair of dimensions, and the run's `version` increases by 1. The run's `run_status` is untouched by the culture, which is why this is a `COMPLETED → COMPLETED` (or `BOTTLED → BOTTLED`) transition on the physical dimension.

A `FAILED` culture is the bank's most destructive single event, and it runs a four-hop cascade in which each hop is caused by the one before it. The first hop is the verdict itself: `culture_status` becomes `FAILED`, which forces `release_eligibility` to `BLOCKED`. Because eligibility became blocked, the second hop moves every `MilkLot` of that run whose `release_status` is `HOLD`, `RELEASED` or `EXHAUSTED` to `DISCARDED`, freezing each lot's `available_ml` at whatever value it then held and incrementing each lot's `version`. Because every lot of the run is now `DISCARDED`, the third hop moves the run's `Pool` — from `COMMITTED` or `PASTEURIZED` — to `SCRAPPED_CULTURE` and moves every one of that pool's components whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` or `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED` to `CONSUMED_LOST`, with the pool's `version` incremented. `SCRAPPED_CULTURE` is emphatically **not** the same outcome as `SCRAPPED`: no volume is returned to any donation, because the milk has been heat-treated and then found contaminated. Because those components became `CONSUMED_LOST`, the fourth hop moves each of their donations whose `status` is not already `RECALLED` to `DISCARDED` and sets that donation's `remaining_ml` to 0, with a `version` increment — after which any cooler all of whose containers are now terminal bubbles up to `CLOSED` per §2.8.

### 3.7. Bottling, Lot Identity, Shelf Life and QA Release

Bottling is the technician's act of turning a heat-treated pool into addressable inventory, and it is admitted only while the run's `thermal_status` is `PASSED`; a thermally failed run is refused at the state gate with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` and never reaches the volume arithmetic. The submission is two numbers: a `bottle_count` between 1 and 200 and a `bottle_volume_ml` fill volume between 20 and 250. Their product is the lot's `total_volume_ml`, and it may not exceed the pool's `total_volume_ml` less the pool's `bottled_volume_ml` — that is, the milk the pool still holds after accounting for whatever earlier lots of the same run already took. Exceeding it is `ERR_BOTTLING_EXCEEDS_POOL_VOLUME`. The pool's `bottled_volume_ml` is by definition the sum of `total_volume_ml` over the lots bottled from that pool's run whose `release_status` is anything other than `DISCARDED`, and it is recomputed on every bottling.

Each new lot is born in `HOLD` with `dispensed_ml` 0 and `available_ml` equal to its `total_volume_ml`, and it freezes three values from the pool and run that produced it: `kcal_per_oz` copied from the pool's `projected_kcal_per_oz`, `milk_class` copied from the pool's `target_milk_class`, and `pasteurization_date` taken as the calendar date component of the run's `completed_at`. Its `lot_sequence` is 1 plus the number of lots the run has already produced, counted in bottling order. Its `lot_code` is a deterministic string assembled from three segments joined by hyphens: the run's pasteurization date rendered as eight digits in year-month-day order, then the pool's `pool_sequence` zero-padded to three digits, then the `lot_sequence` zero-padded to two digits. No part of a lot code is ever derived from any `id`, and every lot code is unique across the bank. Because every lot of one run shares that run's `completed_at`, sibling lots of the same run differ only in the trailing two-digit segment.

Shelf life is the earliest of three independent ceilings, and all three are measured over the same **contributing set**: the components of the run's pool whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` or `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED`, which is to say every component that survived to bottling, including those an earlier lot of the same run has already marked consumed. `VOIDED` and `CONSUMED_LOST` components are excluded. The first ceiling is the pasteurization ceiling: the calendar date of the run's `completed_at` plus 180 days. The second is the raw-age ceiling: the **earliest** `expressed_on` among the donations behind the contributing components, plus 365 days. The third is the receipt ceiling: the **earliest** calendar date of `received_at` among the coolers those donations arrived in, plus 240 days. The lot's `expiration_date` is the earliest of those three dates. Because the contributing set does not shrink as a run is bottled repeatedly, a run's second lot inherits exactly the same three ceilings as its first — and a single very old container in the pool short-dates every lot of the run no matter how fresh the pasteurization was.

The first lot of a run drives a chain of consequences. The run itself advances `COMPLETED → BOTTLED`. The pool advances `COMMITTED → PASTEURIZED`, and every one of its components still `ALLOCATED` becomes `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED`, which in turn advances each donation `ALLOCATED → PASTEURIZED` once all of that donation's components are `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED` and its `remaining_ml` has reached 0. The pool's `bottled_volume_ml` is recomputed, and the run's, the pool's and each touched donation's `version` all increase by 1. A second or later lot of the same run is admitted as the `BOTTLED → BOTTLED` self-loop: it creates a further lot with the next `lot_sequence`, recomputes the pool's `bottled_volume_ml` and increments the pool's `version`, but performs no pool or component state change at all, because the pool is already `PASTEURIZED` and its components are already `CONSUMED_PASTEURIZED`.

A lot in `HOLD` is not inventory. A `QualityAssuranceOfficer` releases it, moving it `HOLD → RELEASED`, and the release is guarded across an entity boundary: the lot's own run must sit at `release_eligibility` `ELIGIBLE`, meaning both `thermal_status` and `culture_status` are `PASSED`. A run whose culture is still `NOT_COLLECTED` is not eligible, and the refusal is `ERR_RUN_NOT_RELEASE_ELIGIBLE`. The segregation-of-duties rule of §2.9 then applies. On success `released_at` is stamped with the clock instant, `released_by_user_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, and the lot's `version` increases by 1.

Thereafter a lot's `dispensed_ml` is the sum of `volume_ml` across the fills against it whose `status` is `DISPENSED`, and its `available_ml` is its `total_volume_ml` less that figure. A lot moves `RELEASED → EXHAUSTED` when `available_ml` reaches 0 and back `EXHAUSTED → RELEASED` when returned volume raises it above 0, both per §2.8. `DISCARDED`, `RECALLED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. The `EXPIRED` state is reached only by the clock sweep and only from `HOLD`: a lot that was never released and whose `expiration_date` has fallen behind the clock date is written off. A lot already in `RELEASED` or `EXHAUSTED` keeps its status past its expiration date and is instead stopped at fill time, so the sweep and the fill-time date check never shadow each other.

### 3.8. Demand: Prescriptions, Priority Tiers, the Waitlist and Subsidy Programs

Charitable funding is modelled first, because a prescription may point at it. A `MilkBankAdmin` opens a subsidy program with a `program_code` matching the literal `SUB-` followed by exactly four digits and unique across the bank, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `coverage_pct` in the closed range 0.00 to 1.00 expressed to two decimal places, and an `annual_cap_cents` integer from 1 to 100000000. The program is born with `consumed_cents` 0, `status` `OPEN` and `version` 1. Thereafter `consumed_cents` is by definition the sum of `subsidy_cents` over every fill with `status` `DISPENSED` whose prescription names this program, and `status` is `OPEN` while `consumed_cents` is strictly below `annual_cap_cents` and `EXHAUSTED` once it reaches or passes it. `EXHAUSTED` is not terminal: a voided fill that frees capacity returns the program to `OPEN`.

A `Prescriber` submits an order carrying a `prescription_code` matching the literal `RX-` followed by exactly four digits, unique across the bank and used as the final deterministic tie-break wherever prescriptions are ranked; an optional `subsidy_program_id` naming an existing program, which when omitted means no subsidy applies at all; a de-identified `patient_reference` of 1 to 40 characters; a `patient_type` of either `NICU_INPATIENT` or `OUTPATIENT`; a `clinical_acuity` of either `CRITICAL` or `STANDARD`; an optional `requires_preterm_milk` flag defaulting to false; an optional `subsidy_required` flag defaulting to false; a `prescribed_ml` volume from 50 to 12000; and a `needed_by_date` calendar date.

The `priority_tier` is derived, not supplied, from the pair of enumerations: tier 1 for a `NICU_INPATIENT` patient at `CRITICAL` acuity, tier 2 for a `NICU_INPATIENT` patient at `STANDARD` acuity, and tier 3 for any `OUTPATIENT` patient, whose acuity is therefore irrelevant to rank. A critical outpatient is a tier-3 order and stands behind every routine inpatient. The order is born `QUEUED` with `cumulative_dispensed_ml` 0, `remaining_ml` equal to `prescribed_ml`, `prescriber_user_id` stamped from the submitting operator's `X-User-ID`, and `version` 1.

`waitlist_position` is a bucket-local rank, not a bank-wide one: within the set of prescriptions sharing this order's `priority_tier` whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending, then by `remaining_ml` descending, then by `prescription_code` ascending, and each is given its 1-based position in that order. Two tier-1 orders and two tier-3 orders therefore both produce positions 1 and 2 — the number alone says nothing about how soon milk arrives. A prescription in any terminal status carries `waitlist_position` null. The whole affected bucket is renumbered whenever a prescription is created, whenever a fill changes a `remaining_ml` or closes an order, whenever an order is cancelled or expired, and once at the end of every allocation cycle. Because `remaining_ml` descending sits above `prescription_code` in the ordering, a partial fill can move an order down its own bucket.

A `Prescriber` may cancel an order whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, moving it to `CANCELLED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_PRESCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation clears `waitlist_position` to null, renumbers the bucket and increments `version` by 1; beyond the role gate no further identity comparison is imposed on it. The clock sweep independently moves any `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` order whose `needed_by_date` has fallen strictly behind the new clock date to `EXPIRED`, with the same waitlist and version consequences. `FILLED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` are the declared terminal states, but `FILLED` is reachable in both directions: a void returns a filled order to the queue, as §3.9 describes, and it may make that round trip any number of times.

### 3.9. Filling a Prescription: Nine-Step Pricing, Safety Gates and Reversal

A `DispensingPharmacist` fills an order by naming a `lot_id`, a `volume_ml` from 20 to 12000, and the `expected_lot_version` optimistic-concurrency token for that lot. The gauntlet the request runs is ordered, and every step is observable, so a request that violates several rules at once reports only the earliest. The lot must be `RELEASED` — a lot in `HOLD`, or one already `EXHAUSTED`, `DISCARDED`, `RECALLED` or `EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`; note that a lot whose `expiration_date` has already passed but whose `release_status` is still `RELEASED` passes this step and is caught further down. Then the prescription's `status` must be `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, else `ERR_PRESCRIPTION_STATE_INVALID`. Then the OCC token is compared, per §2.7. Then segregation of duties: the acting pharmacist's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `prescriber_user_id`, else `ERR_SOD_DISPENSE_CONFLICT`. Then suitability: when the order's `requires_preterm_milk` is true the lot's `milk_class` must be exactly `PRETERM`, and a `TERM` or `MIXED` lot is refused with `ERR_LOT_MILK_CLASS_UNSUITABLE`; when the flag is false any class is acceptable. Then two date checks in fixed order — the lot's `expiration_date` must be on or after the clock date (`ERR_LOT_EXPIRED`), and only then the order's `needed_by_date` must be on or after the clock date (`ERR_PRESCRIPTION_PAST_NEEDED_BY`). Then two volume ceilings, again in fixed order: `volume_ml` may not exceed the order's `remaining_ml` (`ERR_DISPENSE_EXCEEDS_PRESCRIPTION_REMAINING`), and then may not exceed the lot's `available_ml` (`ERR_DISPENSE_EXCEEDS_LOT_AVAILABLE`).

The next gate is the neonatal reserve. When the order's `priority_tier` is 3, the bank must still hold at least 3000 millilitres of `available_ml` summed across **all** `RELEASED` lots after this fill would be applied; otherwise the fill is refused with `ERR_NICU_RESERVE_PROTECTED`. Because the reserve is a bank-wide aggregate rather than a per-lot floor, a twenty-millilitre outpatient fill against a lot holding two litres is still refused when the bank as a whole is close to the floor, and no tier-1 or tier-2 fill is ever subject to the check.

The two fiscal gates apply only when the order's `subsidy_required` flag is true, and only then. The linked program must exist and be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SUBSIDY_PROGRAM_EXHAUSTED`; and the program's remaining headroom, its `annual_cap_cents` less its `consumed_cents`, must be at least the full uncapped subsidy this fill would generate, else `ERR_SUBSIDY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Both are reported with HTTP 402. For such an order a partial subsidy is a rejection rather than a discount. When `subsidy_required` is false the same shortfall is not an error at all: the fill proceeds and the subsidy is simply clipped to the headroom.

Pricing is a nine-step sequence, and the sequence itself is normative because two of its steps are order-sensitive. **Step one:** the billable unit count is `volume_ml` divided by 100 and rounded **up** to the next whole unit, so 450 millilitres is 5 units and 500 millilitres is exactly 5. **Step two:** the discount tier is read from the order's `cumulative_dispensed_ml` **as it stands before this fill** — 0.00 when that prior volume is strictly below 2000 millilitres, 0.05 when it is strictly below 5000, and 0.12 otherwise — and the chosen fraction is stored on the fill as `tier_discount_pct`. Reading the tier after adding the current volume would produce a different and incorrect fee. **Step three:** the base charge is the unit count multiplied by 480 cents. **Step four:** a handling charge of 350 cents is added only when the order's `patient_type` is `OUTPATIENT`, and is zero for `NICU_INPATIENT`. **Step five:** `gross_fee_cents` is the sum of base and handling multiplied by one minus the tier fraction, rounded half up to whole cents. The volume discount is therefore applied to the whole charge, handling included, and before any subsidy is considered. **Step six:** the coverage fraction is the program's `coverage_pct` when a program is linked and its `status` is `OPEN`, and zero in every other case — no program linked, or a linked program that is `EXHAUSTED`. **Step seven:** the uncapped subsidy is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by that coverage fraction, rounded half up to whole cents; this is the second and last rounding in the algorithm, and it is independent of the one at step five. **Step eight:** `subsidy_cents` is the lesser of the uncapped subsidy and the program's headroom, so a program near its cap pays only what it has left. **Step nine:** `payer_due_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` less `subsidy_cents`. The invariant that `gross_fee_cents` equals `subsidy_cents` plus `payer_due_cents` holds after every fill by construction.

A successful fill is created with `status` `DISPENSED`, its `prescription_id` set to the order it was addressed to and its `lot_id` to the lot named in the body, its `dispensed_by_user_id` stamped, its `allocation_cycle_sequence` null because it was created by hand rather than by a cycle, and the four priced figures frozen onto it. It then rewrites three other ledgers. The lot's `dispensed_ml` rises by `volume_ml` and its `available_ml` falls correspondingly, moving it `RELEASED → EXHAUSTED` when `available_ml` reaches exactly 0, with a `version` increment. The program's `consumed_cents` rises by `subsidy_cents`, flipping it `OPEN → EXHAUSTED` once the cap is reached or passed, with a `version` increment. The order's `cumulative_dispensed_ml` rises and its `remaining_ml` falls; it moves `QUEUED → PARTIALLY_FILLED` when the fill leaves volume outstanding and `PARTIALLY_FILLED → FILLED` when `volume_ml` exactly equals the `remaining_ml` that stood before the fill, in which case `waitlist_position` is cleared to null. Either way its tier bucket is renumbered and its `version` increments by 1.

Voiding is the compensating action, available to a `DispensingPharmacist` while the fill's `status` is `DISPENSED`; a fill already `VOIDED` or `RECALLED` cannot be voided again and is refused with `ERR_DISPENSE_STATE_INVALID`, and a fill whose lot has been recalled cannot be voided at all, because the recall cascade has already accounted for it. The void moves the fill `DISPENSED → VOIDED` and reverses all three ledgers: the lot's `dispensed_ml` is credited back by `volume_ml`, raising `available_ml` and returning the lot `EXHAUSTED → RELEASED` when volume comes back; the program's `consumed_cents` is credited back by `subsidy_cents`, returning it `EXHAUSTED → OPEN` once it falls strictly below the cap; and the order's `cumulative_dispensed_ml` is lowered, its `remaining_ml` raised, and its status recomputed to `QUEUED` when `cumulative_dispensed_ml` has returned to exactly 0 and to `PARTIALLY_FILLED` otherwise — which is how a `FILLED` order re-enters the waitlist. The bucket is renumbered and every touched row's `version` increments. Crucially, the void does **not** zero `tier_discount_pct`, `gross_fee_cents`, `subsidy_cents` or `payer_due_cents`: those figures are retained on the voided row as the audit record of what was once charged. This is the difference between a void and a recall: the recall path of §3.3 also credits the subsidy and re-opens the order, but deliberately withholds the volume credit to the lot, because recalled milk is destroyed rather than returned to inventory.

### 3.10. The Allocation Cycle and Time-Driven Expiry

Manual fills serve individual clinical decisions; the allocation cycle serves the waitlist. A `DispensingPharmacist` or a `MilkBankAdmin` runs one cycle with no request body, and it drains demand against supply in a strictly deterministic waterfall whose every ordering key is a stored field, never an `id` and never insertion luck.

Demand consists of the prescriptions whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` and whose `needed_by_date` is on or after the clock date; `FILLED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` orders and orders already past their needed-by date are excluded outright. Those prescriptions form three buckets by `priority_tier`, and the buckets are drained strictly in order — tier 1 first, then tier 2, then tier 3 — with no bucket touched until the bucket above it can absorb no further milk. Within a bucket the demand order is `created_at` ascending, then `remaining_ml` descending, then `prescription_code` ascending.

Supply for a given prescription consists of the lots whose `release_status` is `RELEASED`, whose `available_ml` is strictly greater than 0, whose `expiration_date` is on or after the clock date, and — only when that prescription's `requires_preterm_milk` is true — whose `milk_class` is `PRETERM`. Lots in `HOLD`, `EXHAUSTED`, `DISCARDED`, `RECALLED` or `EXPIRED` never participate. Those lots are consumed first-expiring-first-out: `expiration_date` ascending, then `available_ml` ascending, then `lot_code` ascending. The secondary key means that among two lots dated the same day the emptier one is drained first, which retires nearly-empty lots rather than fragmenting fresh ones.

For each prescription in turn the cycle walks its eligible lots in that order, stopping as soon as the order's `remaining_ml` reaches 0 or as soon as it has drawn from three distinct lots — three is the hard per-prescription, per-cycle lot cap, and an order needing more milk than three lots can give simply waits for the next cycle. The draw from a lot is the lesser of the order's outstanding `remaining_ml` and the volume the bucket is permitted to take from that lot. For tier 1 and tier 2 that permitted volume is the lot's whole `available_ml`. For tier 3 it is additionally clipped by the bank-wide neonatal reserve: before **every** individual draw the cycle re-measures the total `available_ml` across every lot whose `release_status` is `RELEASED` — including lots this prescription itself cannot use — subtracts 3000 millilitres, floors the result at 0, and takes the lesser of that spare volume and the lot's own `available_ml`. Because the total is re-measured before each draw rather than once per cycle, an earlier tier-1 draw inside the same cycle can shrink or eliminate the spare that a later tier-3 order sees. A computed draw of 0 is not an error inside a cycle: that lot is skipped and the walk continues.

Every draw creates a fill exactly as a manual fill would, priced independently through the nine steps of §3.9 and applying the same ledger consequences to the lot, the subsidy program and the prescription. Two consequences of that independence matter. First, because each fill reads the tier from the prescription's pre-fill `cumulative_dispensed_ml`, the second fill of one cycle already sees the volume the first fill of the same cycle added, so a single cycle can straddle a discount boundary. Second, each created fill carries `allocation_cycle_sequence` stamped with the cycle's monotonic counter, which is what distinguishes machine-allocated volume from hand-dispensed volume in the audit trail. Waitlist positions are **not** renumbered after each individual fill during a cycle; they are recomputed exactly once, at the end of the cycle, across every prescription still `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, using the same bucket and within-bucket ordering.

Time is the last actor in the system. Advancing the logical clock, subject to the monotonicity rule of §2.4, sweeps four families of rows in one pass. Every physician approval whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `valid_until` has fallen strictly behind the new clock date becomes `EXPIRED`, which is how a qualified donor silently loses her clinical cover. Every milk lot whose `release_status` is `HOLD` and whose `expiration_date` has fallen strictly behind the new clock date becomes `EXPIRED`, while `RELEASED` and `EXHAUSTED` lots are deliberately left alone and refused at fill time instead. Every prescription whose `status` is `QUEUED` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` and whose `needed_by_date` has fallen strictly behind the new clock date becomes `EXPIRED`, with `waitlist_position` cleared and the affected buckets renumbered. And every `ACTIVE` deferral event of `deferral_type` `TEMPORARY` whose `effective_from` plus `deferral_days` has fallen strictly behind the new clock date becomes `EXPIRED`, after which each affected donor's `active_deferral_until_date` is recomputed from whatever `ACTIVE` `TEMPORARY` deferrals she has left — becoming null when none remain. A `PERMANENT` deferral is never swept; nothing in the passage of time undoes it.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is rooted at the `/milk-bank` prefix. Creations answer with HTTP 201 and the created resource; modifications, transitions and reads answer with HTTP 200. Two operations return no resource — running an allocation cycle and setting the logical clock — and their `data` field is an empty object, as §2.2 requires. The Authorized Role(s) column lists exactly the values of `X-Role` the operation admits; every other recognized role is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and a `Donor` reading a collection or a resource is additionally confined to her own records per §2.1.
