# AI Governance & Model Approval API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An enterprise AI governance office sits between the business units that want to ship model-driven features and the regulators, customers and employees who bear the consequences when one of those features misbehaves. Today that office works out of spreadsheets: a risk questionnaire in one tab, a legal sign-off thread in an inbox, a vendor contract summary in a shared drive, a bias test report attached to a ticket that nobody can find six months later. When a model quietly ships without review — a "shadow deployment" discovered through an expense report or a network scan — there is no systematic way to establish whether it was ever assessed, which vendor terms govern its outputs, or who is accountable for tearing it down.

This document specifies the API for a governance system that replaces those spreadsheets with a single auditable record. Every AI use case enters through an intake that declares who it affects, how much of the decision the model makes on its own, and how sensitive the data is; the system scores that declaration into a provisional risk tier and, from the tier alone, decides which domain reviews are mandatory. Legal, privacy, security and business reviewers each own exactly one gate and clear it against evidence produced elsewhere in the system — an accepted vendor terms assessment, a passed red-team run, a published statutory notice. Only when every demanded gate is cleared may a governance officer sign the deployment approval, and at that moment the risk tier is deliberately recomputed a second time, now crediting the controls the first tier demanded and penalising weak vendor terms. The final tier, not the provisional one, determines how often the approval must be revisited and how fast an incident must be contained.

Two properties are non-negotiable for the office's audit obligations. First, **segregation of duties**: the person who filed an intake may never clear a gate on it or sign its approval, and the officer who signs may not be a reviewer who cleared one of its gates. Second, **conservation**: an append-only governance ledger records every movement of deployed headcount reach and of accumulated review control debt, and the ledger balances must always reconcile against the live inventory — a clearance followed by a reopening must leave the debt total at exactly the cent value it held before, and an approval followed by its revocation must leave the reach total where it started.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All paths in this document are mounted at the server root; there is no additional API prefix.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or blank, or if `X-User-Role` is a string outside the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Requester`, `LegalReviewer`, `PrivacyReviewer`, `SecurityReviewer`, `BusinessReviewer` and `GovernanceOfficer`.

- A `Requester` is the business-unit owner who files an AI use case intake, keeps its intake data current, files the human-oversight design and publishes statutory notices.
- A `LegalReviewer` owns the `LEGAL` review gate and files third-party vendor assessments covering training-data provenance and output rights.
- A `PrivacyReviewer` owns the `PRIVACY` review gate and cannot clear it until the required notices are published in every jurisdiction the use case declares.
- A `SecurityReviewer` owns the `SECURITY` review gate, runs the testing programme and reports unapproved deployments found in the estate.
- A `BusinessReviewer` owns the `BUSINESS` review gate — the only gate every non-prohibited risk tier demands.
- A `GovernanceOfficer` is the governance-office operator who runs classification, the model registry, vendor decisions, oversight decisions, model bindings, gate reopenings, approvals, revocations, reassessments, incident resolution, the shadow-deployment programme, the inventory reconciliation read and the periodic sweep.

**Operator identity is opaque for all six roles.** When `X-User-Role` is `Requester`, `LegalReviewer`, `PrivacyReviewer`, `SecurityReviewer`, `BusinessReviewer` or `GovernanceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any record's `id`. The server does record it where a transition says so — as `requester_user_id` on a `UseCase` at intake, as `assigned_reviewer_user_id` and later `cleared_by_user_id` on a `ReviewGate`, as `approved_by_user_id` on a `UseCase` at approval, as `decided_by_user_id` on a `VendorModelAssessment` or an `OversightDesign`, and as `actor_user_id` on every `GovernanceLedgerEntry` — and the segregation-of-duties rules compare those recorded strings to each other and to the acting `X-User-ID`, never to an entity identifier. Where a cascade appends a ledger entry without a human request behind it, `actor_user_id` carries the literal string `system`; the same literal is written to `decided_by_user_id` when a newly filed oversight design supersedes an earlier draft.

Two role-scoped restrictions cut across the whole system. **Gate ownership**: each gate type belongs to exactly one reviewer role — `LegalReviewer` owns `LEGAL`, `PrivacyReviewer` owns `PRIVACY`, `SecurityReviewer` owns `SECURITY`, `BusinessReviewer` owns `BUSINESS`. A reviewer acting on a gate whose `gate_type` belongs to a different role is rejected with `ERR_GATE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403); this applies when taking up a gate for review, when issuing conditions on it, and when clearing it. No `GovernanceOfficer` may clear a domain gate, and no reviewer role may sign an approval. **Segregation of duties**: the reviewer clearing a gate must not be the use case's requester; the officer signing an approval must be neither the requester nor any reviewer who cleared a gate on that use case; and the officer completing a periodic reassessment must not be the requester. Any of these violations is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), which guards gate clearance, approval and reassessment completion.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are sorted by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise. This ordering is load-bearing in one place in particular: because the review gates a classification creates are created in the canonical order `LEGAL`, `PRIVACY`, `SECURITY`, `BUSINESS`, listing a use case's gates by `created_at` ascending yields that same canonical order and is therefore index-stable across runs.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true when records remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters offered by individual list endpoints — for example `status`, `risk_tier`, `final_risk_tier`, `business_unit`, `predecessor_use_case_id`, `provider`, `is_third_party`, `gate_type`, `variant`, `use_case_id`, `model_binding_id`, `model_asset_id`, `claimed_use_case_id`, `eval_type`, `jurisdiction`, `notice_type`, `severity`, `environment` and `entry_type` — are combined conjunctively and are applied before pagination.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every record the system mints carries an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every record also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the instant of creation. That rendering is uniform across the whole API rather than special to `created_at`: every instant-valued field any response carries — `created_at`, `updated_at`, the append-only event stamps, every deadline, effective-window bound, test-window bound and decision stamp, the `current_utc` the clock setter echoes back, and any instant the client itself supplied in the request body — is emitted in UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, whatever form the client wrote it in, with a finer-grained input truncated to whole milliseconds. A client that supplies `2026-09-01T06:00:00Z` therefore reads `2026-09-01T06:00:00.000Z` back off the record; the two denote the same instant and every comparison the system makes is on the instant, not on the rendering.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the logical clock's current reading on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations a cascade performs on the row rather than mutations a client requested directly, subject to the two narrower disciplines §2.10 sets out for `UseCase` and `ModelAsset`. `UseCase`, `ReviewGate`, `ModelAsset`, `ModelBinding`, `VendorModelAssessment`, `TestEvaluation`, `OversightDesign`, `DisclosureNotice`, `MonitoringIncident` and `ShadowDeployment` all behave this way.

Two record kinds are append-only events and are the exception: a `ClassificationSnapshot` and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, because neither is ever mutated after it is appended. Both carry their own explicit event timestamps instead — `computed_at` on a snapshot and `recorded_at` on a ledger entry — each holding the logical-clock instant of the event.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, effective window and grace period in this system is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to the host's wall clock under any circumstance. Any of the six roles may move the clock forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response echoes back `current_utc` as the clock's new reading. Moving the clock performs no state transitions of its own — nothing expires, suspends or enforces merely because the clock advanced. Those consequences are produced only by the periodic governance sweep described in §3.9, or by the next request that reads a deadline.

Day arithmetic (reassessment intervals, disclosure quiet buffers, shadow grace periods) is exact calendar-day addition in UTC. Hour arithmetic (incident containment) is exact hour addition in UTC. Instant comparisons are exact to the second in UTC. No rounding, truncation or timezone conversion is applied anywhere in temporal arithmetic.

### 2.6. Version Pinning and Stale-Version Rejection

Four write operations require the client to prove that its picture of the parent `UseCase` is current, by supplying `expected_use_case_version` in the request body carrying the `version` value of that use case the client last observed. Those four are running classification, creating a model binding, filing an oversight design, and signing the approval. On any of them, if `expected_use_case_version` does not equal the use case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_USE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The field is mandatory on all four — it is never optional and never defaulted. **Endpoints not named here do not accept a version field at all**, and supplying one has no effect.

Two of the four deserve particular emphasis, because on both of them a repeated request that observes identical state would legitimately succeed, so the pinned version is the only signal that a client's view is out of date:

- Creating a `ModelBinding` on a use case supersedes whatever binding that use case previously held, retiring it. A client that read the use case, then had another writer advance the use case's `version` behind its back, would be superseding a binding it never saw. Supplying an `expected_use_case_version` that no longer matches the use case's current `version` is therefore rejected with `ERR_STALE_USE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Filing an `OversightDesign` on a use case supersedes any prior design on that use case still in `DRAFT`, moving the prior row to `REJECTED`. The risk tier the new design must clear may have changed since the filer last read the use case. Supplying an `expected_use_case_version` that no longer matches the use case's current `version` is therefore rejected with `ERR_STALE_USE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A second, independent form of version pinning applies to model assets and is *not* an OCC check on a client-supplied field. A `ModelBinding` records `pinned_model_version`, the `ModelAsset.version` value that was current at the moment the binding was created. Because every revision or re-approval of an asset increments `ModelAsset.version`, a binding whose `pinned_model_version` has fallen behind its asset's current `version` is, by definition, pinned to a model the enterprise no longer runs. Such a binding is moved to `STALE`, and an approval attempt that finds its `PINNED` binding pinned to an outdated asset version is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_STALE` (HTTP 409).

### 2.7. Scoring Precision and Rounding Discipline

Three precision rules govern every number this system computes.

**Risk scores are integers.** A risk score — provisional or final — is rounded half up to zero decimal places and then clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100. A raw score below 0 becomes 0; a raw score above 100 becomes 100.

**Control debt carries two decimal places and is rounded per gate, never per portfolio.** The control debt a single gate carries is rounded half up to two decimal places at the gate, and the portfolio total is the plain sum of those already-rounded per-gate values. Summing the unrounded products first and rounding the sum once at the end is incorrect and yields a different cent value. To make the ordering unambiguous: for a `HIGH`-tier use case whose `LEGAL` gate holds two open conditions and whose `PRIVACY`, `SECURITY` and `BUSINESS` gates each hold one, the mandated order yields per-gate values of 1.35, 0.63, 0.58 and 0.38 and a total of 2.94, whereas summing first and rounding once would yield 2.93. The mandated total is 2.94.

**Everything else is exact.** Vendor terms risk points, oversight adequacy scores and point contributions are integer-valued because every contributing constant is an integer; no rounding step is required or permitted. Evaluation metrics are compared at the four decimal places in which they were supplied, with no rounding before the comparison. Containment hours are produced by rounding up to the next whole hour. Headcount reach quantities are exact integers. Because control debt totals are sums of values already rounded to two decimals, the portfolio total is itself exact at two decimals.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost unresolvable segment decides the error. On any path of the shape `/use-cases/{id}/...`, an `{id}` that resolves to no `UseCase` row yields `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). On any path of the shape `/model-assets/{id}/...`, an unresolvable `{id}` yields `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). On the four gate actions — taking up review, issuing conditions, clearing and reopening — an unresolvable gate `{id}` yields `ERR_REVIEW_GATE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). For every other addressed record — a vendor assessment being decided, an oversight design being decided, a test evaluation being read or completed, a disclosure notice being withdrawn, a monitoring incident being read, contained or resolved, a shadow deployment being read, linked, granted grace or retired — an unresolvable `{id}` yields `ERR_GOVERNANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved in the order the fields appear in that endpoint's input list, and the first one that fails decides the error. A `use_case_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; a `model_asset_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`; a `predecessor_use_case_id` or `claimed_use_case_id` that resolves to no use case yields `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; and a `model_binding_id` that resolves to no binding yields `ERR_GOVERNANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. Body-key resolution of this kind applies when filing an intake, scheduling a test evaluation, reporting a monitoring incident, recording a shadow deployment, creating a model binding and linking a shadow deployment to a use case.

A record whose parent use case has reached a terminal status is frozen. If the addressed use case — or the parent use case of the addressed child record — is `REJECTED` or `REVOKED`, every mutating request against it is rejected with `ERR_USE_CASE_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). Reads are unaffected.

### 2.9. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

Many operations in this system are guarded by several checks at once, and a request that violates more than one must fail with a specific, predictable error. The order below is normative: checks are evaluated top to bottom and the **first** one that fails determines the response. A later check never masks an earlier one.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. The `{id}` of a `/use-cases/{id}...` path resolves — `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every such path.
4. The `{id}` of a `/model-assets/{id}...` path resolves — `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every such path.
5. The gate `{id}` resolves — `ERR_REVIEW_GATE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on gate take-up, issue-conditions, clear and reopen.
6. The addressed vendor assessment, oversight design, test evaluation, disclosure notice, monitoring incident or shadow deployment resolves — `ERR_GOVERNANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404), on vendor-assessment decide, oversight-design decide, the evaluation read and complete actions, notice withdrawal, and the incident and shadow-deployment reads and actions.
7. Body foreign keys resolve, left to right in input order — the error is `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_GOVERNANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404) according to which key failed. This applies to intake filing, evaluation scheduling, incident reporting, shadow-deployment recording, binding creation and shadow linking.
8. Request-body shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint that accepts a body. This covers a missing required field, a wrong type, an enum value outside its declared set, `window_end_at` not strictly after `window_start_at`, `effective_to` not strictly after `effective_from`, `sample_pct` outside 0 through 100 inclusive, `escalation_sla_hours` outside 0 through 240 inclusive, `deployed_headcount_reach` below 0, `locale_count` below 1, a `jurisdictions` array containing duplicates, and a revision request that supplies no updatable field at all.
9. `expected_use_case_version` equals the use case's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_USE_CASE_VERSION` (412), on classification, binding creation, oversight-design filing and approval.
10. The addressed use case, or the parent use case of the addressed child, is not `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` — `ERR_USE_CASE_TERMINAL` (409), on every mutating endpoint whose target resolves to a use case or one of its children.
11. The use case is in `DRAFT` — `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_IN_DRAFT` (409), on classification; the same error and status also answer an intake revision attempted from a status that is neither `DRAFT` nor `CHANGES_REQUESTED`.
12. The addressed gate is in a status the requested action has a transition out of: take-up needs `PENDING` or `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, issuing conditions needs `IN_REVIEW`, clearing needs `IN_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, reopening needs `CLEARED` — `ERR_GATE_NOT_IN_REVIEWABLE_STATE` (409), on all four gate actions.
13. The use case is in `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` — `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_READY_FOR_APPROVAL` (409), on approval.
14. The use case is in `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` — `ERR_REASSESSMENT_NOT_DUE` (409), on reassessment completion.
15. The addressed vendor assessment is `SUBMITTED`, or the addressed oversight design is `DRAFT` — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED` (409), on vendor-assessment decide and oversight-design decide.
16. The addressed test evaluation is `PLANNED` — `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_PLANNED` (409), on evaluation completion.
17. The addressed incident is in a status the requested action has a transition out of: containment needs `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, resolution needs `CONTAINED` — `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (409), on incident containment and resolution.
18. The addressed shadow deployment is neither `RETIRED` nor `ENFORCED_BLOCK`, and granting grace additionally requires `DISCOVERED` — `ERR_SHADOW_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (409), on shadow linking, grace granting and retirement.
19. The addressed disclosure notice is `PUBLISHED` — `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN` (409), on notice withdrawal.
20. The addressed model asset is in a status the requested action has a transition out of: approving for use needs `REGISTERED` or `RESTRICTED`, and a `RESTRICTED` asset additionally needs `enforced_shadow_count` equal to 0, while revising an asset in place needs `APPROVED_FOR_USE` because the revise transition is declared only on that state — `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_USE` (409), on asset approve-for-use and asset revision.
21. The use case's `risk_tier` is not `PROHIBITED` — `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_CASE` (422). This guards three operations: approval, oversight-design decide, and binding creation.
22. The use case's `safety_flag` is false — `ERR_BIAS_EVALUATION_FAILED` (422), on approval.
23. Required evaluation evidence is present — `ERR_REQUIRED_EVALUATION_MISSING` (422). This guards two operations, and **the two consult different evidence sets**. On approval, every `eval_type` the use case's provisional `risk_tier` demands must have a `PASSED` evaluation on that use case. On gate clearance the tier's evaluation list is not consulted at all; the only rule is that clearing a gate whose `gate_type` is `SECURITY` requires a `PASSED` `SECURITY_REDTEAM` evaluation on the use case, while clearing a `PRIVACY` or `BUSINESS` gate requires no evaluation evidence whatsoever.
24. An `OversightDesign` with status `APPROVED` exists on the use case — `ERR_OVERSIGHT_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED` (422), on approval.
25. When the decision is to approve a design, its `adequacy_score` is at least its `required_adequacy_score` — `ERR_OVERSIGHT_DESIGN_INADEQUATE` (422), on oversight-design decide.
26. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This guards three operations: gate clearance (the clearing reviewer must not be the requester), approval (the signing officer must be neither the requester nor any reviewer who cleared a gate on this use case), and reassessment completion (the officer must not be the requester).
27. The acting role owns the gate type — `ERR_GATE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403). This guards three of the four gate actions: take-up, issuing conditions and clearing. Reopening is a `GovernanceOfficer` action and is not gate-type scoped.
28. A `PINNED` `ModelBinding` exists on the use case — `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_MISSING` (422). This guards two operations: approval, and clearance of a `LEGAL` gate only.
29. The `PINNED` binding's `pinned_model_version` equals its asset's current `version` — `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_STALE` (409), on approval.
30. The referenced asset has status `APPROVED_FOR_USE` — `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_USE` (422), on binding creation.
31. A third-party asset has an `ACCEPTED` `VendorModelAssessment` whose `terms_grade` is `A`, `B` or `C` — `ERR_VENDOR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422). This guards two operations: approving an asset for use, and clearance of a `LEGAL` gate only.
32. A decision taken under the `DOCUMENTED_TERMS` variant requires the assessment's `retention_days` to be non-null — `ERR_RETENTION_TERMS_UNDOCUMENTED` (422), on vendor-assessment decide.
33. The link target use case is `APPROVED` and owns a `PINNED` binding whose `model_asset_id` equals the shadow deployment's `model_asset_id` — `ERR_SHADOW_LINK_MISMATCH` (422), on shadow linking.
34. Every jurisdiction in the use case's `jurisdictions` has a `PUBLISHED` notice in force at the current logical instant — `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (422). This guards two operations: approval, and clearance of a `PRIVACY` gate only.
35. The new notice's closed effective window neither intersects nor starts inside the quiet buffer following any `PUBLISHED` notice on the same use case with the same `notice_type` and `jurisdiction` — `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on notice publication.
36. The new evaluation's half-open test window does not intersect the window of any `PLANNED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED` evaluation reachable from the same asset — `ERR_EVALUATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on evaluation scheduling.
37. A `SEV1` incident on a use case whose `affects_individuals` is true has `reported_to_regulator` true — `ERR_REGULATOR_REPORT_REQUIRED` (422). This guards containment out of `ESCALATED` and resolution.
38. The current logical instant is at or before the shadow deployment's `grace_deadline_at` — `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_ELAPSED` (409), on grace granting.
39. `grace_days` is at least 1 and at most 30 — `ERR_GRACE_EXTENSION_EXCEEDED` (422), on grace granting.
40. The current logical instant is at or before the use case's `approval_expires_at` — `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED` (409), on reassessment completion. The comparison is inclusive: because approval sets `reassessment_due_at` to the same instant as `approval_expires_at`, a reassessment can only ever be opened at an instant already at or past the expiry, so a strict comparison would make both reassessment outcomes unreachable. The error therefore fires only strictly after the expiry instant.
41. When — and only when — the finalization has produced a `final_risk_tier` of `HIGH`, the portfolio's high-tier reach after this approval must not exceed its cap — `ERR_PORTFOLIO_REACH_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422), on approval. This is the last check in the ladder and is evaluated after the finalization arithmetic has run. A use case whose final tier is `LIMITED` or `MINIMAL` never consults the cap at all.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

When one operation changes several rows, every affected row is changed in the same request. A row a cascade moves to a new `status` — a voided gate, a staled or retired binding, a voided evaluation, an expired assessment, a withdrawn notice, a superseded oversight design, a restricted asset — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. Recomputing a derived counter is not by itself such a mutation, and neither is a parent status bubble-up that a child's own transition drives, so two record kinds carry a narrower version discipline than the rest:

- A `UseCase`'s `updated_at` and `version` move on exactly the operations addressed to the use case itself — intake revision, classification, model-binding creation, oversight-design filing, approval, revocation, reassessment closure and the sweep's opening of a due reassessment — plus the suspension and the reinstatement cascades. The four gate actions of §3.3, the evaluation outcomes of §3.4 and a monitoring-posture re-derivation all leave the use case's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they stood, even when they rewrite `open_conditions_count`, `cleared_gate_count`, `monitoring_posture` or `safety_flag`, and even when they bubble the use case's own `status`. A client that needs the use case's current `version` — to pin it on one of the four operations of §2.6 — reads it back off the use case rather than predicting it.
- A `ModelAsset`'s `updated_at` and `version` move on revision in place, on clearance for use, on the move to `RESTRICTED` and on the sweep's `enforced_shadow_count` increment. A request whose only effect on the asset is a changed `active_binding_count` or a changed `vendor_terms_grade` leaves the asset's `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they stood — which matters because `ModelAsset.version` is what bindings pin against, so a binding does not go `STALE` merely because a sibling binding was retired or a vendor assessment was accepted.

Every other versioned record kind bumps its `updated_at` and `version` whenever any field on it changes at all. There is no deferred or eventually-consistent settlement anywhere in this system — a read issued immediately after a write observes every consequence of that write.

Several counters exist purely as materialisations of facts stored elsewhere, and each must be recomputed whenever anything it summarises moves:

- `open_conditions_count` on a `UseCase` is the sum of `open_condition_count` over that use case's gates whose status is `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`.
- `cleared_gate_count` on a `UseCase` is the number of that use case's gates whose status is `CLEARED`.
- `active_binding_count` on a `ModelAsset` is the number of bindings on that asset whose status is `PINNED` or `STALE`.
- `enforced_shadow_count` on a `ModelAsset` is the number of shadow deployments on that asset whose status is `ENFORCED_BLOCK`.
- `open_condition_count` on a `ReviewGate` is the number of entries in that gate's `conditions` array while the gate is `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, and 0 in every other status.
- `control_debt_index` on a `ReviewGate` is 0.00 whenever the gate is not `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`.
- `monitoring_posture` on a `UseCase` is derived from that use case's unresolved incidents, as described in §3.8.
- `vendor_terms_grade` on a `ModelAsset` is the `terms_grade` of the most recently `ACCEPTED` assessment on that asset, and null when no assessment has been accepted or when acceptance has since been expired.

Parent state is derived from the aggregate state of its children in two places, and both are automatic rather than client-driven. A use case bubbles up to `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` the moment every one of its non-`VOIDED` gates is `CLEARED`; a use case is pushed back to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` the moment any gate issues conditions or any evaluation on it fails.

### 2.11. The Governance Ledger and Conserved Quantities

Every governance event of consequence appends a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` against the use case whose position it moves. An entry records its `entry_type` — one of `APPROVAL_GRANTED`, `APPROVAL_REVOKED`, `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED`, `APPROVAL_REINSTATED`, `GATE_CLEARED`, `GATE_REOPENED`, `REASSESSMENT_COMPLETED`, `BINDING_RETIRED`, `VENDOR_ASSESSMENT_EXPIRED`, `SHADOW_ENFORCED`, `CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED` or `DISCLOSURE_WITHDRAWN` — together with a signed `reach_delta`, a signed `debt_delta` carried to two decimals, the `actor_user_id` behind the request (or the literal `system` for sweep-produced entries), the `recorded_at` instant, its `use_case_id`, and a machine-generated `note` explaining the entry. Events that move neither quantity record `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00. The ledger is append-only: entries are never edited or deleted.

The ledger exists to make two quantities reconcilable at all times.

**Conserved quantity 1 — inventory headcount reach.** The three statuses `APPROVED`, `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` and `SUSPENDED` constitute the *inventory statuses*: a use case in any of them is live in the governance inventory. `portfolio_approved_reach` is the sum of `deployed_headcount_reach` over every use case in an inventory status, and `portfolio_approved_reach_high_tier` is that same sum restricted to use cases whose `final_risk_tier` is `HIGH`. The sum of `reach_delta` over all ledger entries — `ledger_reach_balance` — must equal `portfolio_approved_reach` at all times. This holds because an approval books plus the use case's `deployed_headcount_reach` and its compensating revocation books minus the same figure, while suspension and reinstatement book 0 on both sides: `SUSPENDED` is itself an inventory status, so posture churn cannot drift the reach total.

**Conserved quantity 2 — review control debt.** `control_debt_index_total` is the sum of `control_debt_index` over every gate whose status is `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` or `CLEARED`; `VOIDED` gates are excluded entirely. The sum of `debt_delta` over all ledger entries is `ledger_debt_balance`. For any gate, clearing and then reopening is debt-neutral by construction: clearance books a `debt_delta` of minus the debt the gate carried and reopening books plus exactly that same figure, so `control_debt_index_total` returns to its exact pre-clearance cent value and the ledger debt balance for that use case returns to 0.00. Voiding a gate is deliberately **not** compensating — it removes that gate's contribution permanently and appends no ledger entry at all.

A reconciliation read exposes both quantities alongside their ledger balances: `inventory_count`, `portfolio_approved_reach`, `portfolio_approved_reach_high_tier`, `control_debt_index_total`, `ledger_reach_balance`, `ledger_debt_balance`, `open_incident_count` and `unapproved_shadow_count`. It is a pure computation and writes nothing. It accepts optional `business_unit` and `final_risk_tier` filters.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Model Asset Registry and Vendor Terms Grading

Nothing may be bound to a use case until it exists in the registry. A `GovernanceOfficer` registers a **Model Asset** by declaring the `provider` that supplies it, the `model_name` of the family, the vendor-declared `model_version` label, the `hosting` arrangement — one of `INTERNAL`, `VENDOR_API` or `ON_PREM` — the `modality`, which is one of `TEXT`, `VISION`, `MULTIMODAL` or `TABULAR`, and the boolean `is_third_party`, which is true when an outside party supplies the model and therefore when a vendor assessment becomes mandatory. A freshly registered asset lands in status `REGISTERED` with `version` 1, `active_binding_count` 0, `enforced_shadow_count` 0 and `vendor_terms_grade` null. Assets may be listed with optional `status`, `provider` and `is_third_party` filters, and read individually — the individual read is how a client discovers the asset `version` that a binding will pin against.

Clearing an asset for use moves it from `REGISTERED` to `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, and this is the gate at which third-party supply is tested: if `is_third_party` is true, the asset must already carry a `VendorModelAssessment` with status `ACCEPTED` whose `terms_grade` is `A`, `B` or `C`, and without one the attempt is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. A grade of `D` is never sufficient. The same clearing action also brings an asset back from `RESTRICTED` to `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, with one extra precondition: the asset's `enforced_shadow_count` must be 0, because an asset with an enforced shadow block outstanding cannot be rehabilitated. In both directions, clearing increments the asset's `version`, and that increment is exactly what makes previously pinned bindings stale — every binding on the asset whose status is `PINNED` and whose `pinned_model_version` is below the new asset version is moved to `STALE` with `superseded_reason` set to `ASSET_REAPPROVED`.

An `APPROVED_FOR_USE` asset may also be revised in place. A `GovernanceOfficer` may overwrite any combination of `model_version`, `hosting` and `modality`, and at least one of those three must be supplied. This is the ordinary way a vendor shipping a new model version reaches the governance record, and it carries the same consequence: the asset's `version` increments, every `PINNED` binding pinned to an older asset version drops to `STALE` with `superseded_reason` `ASSET_REAPPROVED`, and every `TestEvaluation` still in `PLANNED` on a binding that just went stale is moved to `VOIDED`, freeing the test window it had reserved. Revision in place is declared only on the `APPROVED_FOR_USE` state; attempting it against an asset in any other status is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_USE`.

An asset can also fall the other way, from `APPROVED_FOR_USE` to `RESTRICTED`, and this is never a client action — it happens as a consequence of either of two facts becoming true: the asset's `active_binding_count` has fallen to 0 while `is_third_party` is true, or its `enforced_shadow_count` has risen to at least 1. When an asset becomes `RESTRICTED`, every `VendorModelAssessment` on it with status `ACCEPTED` is moved to `EXPIRED`, the asset's `vendor_terms_grade` is set back to null, and the asset's `version` increments.

Vendor due diligence is recorded as a **Vendor Model Assessment**, filed against a third-party asset by either a `LegalReviewer` or a `GovernanceOfficer`. The filing declares the `vendor_name` on the contract, the `training_data_provenance` — one of `DOCUMENTED_LICENSED`, `DOCUMENTED_MIXED` or `UNDISCLOSED` — the `output_rights` the enterprise obtains, which is one of `FULL_ASSIGNMENT`, `LICENSE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` or `VENDOR_RETAINS`, an optional `retention_days` figure giving the contractual retention period for prompts and outputs (omitted when the contract is silent, and never negative when supplied), the boolean `retention_opt_out` recording whether the enterprise can contractually opt out of vendor retention for training, and the boolean `subprocessors_disclosed` recording whether the vendor discloses its full subprocessor list. A new assessment lands in status `SUBMITTED` with `terms_variant` set to `CONSERVATIVE_LEGAL`, and it is graded immediately under that variant. Assessments on an asset may be listed with an optional `status` filter.

Grading turns the declared terms into a `terms_risk_points` total and bands that total into a `terms_grade`. Five contributions are added. Provenance contributes nothing when it is `DOCUMENTED_LICENSED`, 8 points when it is `DOCUMENTED_MIXED`, and 20 points when it is `UNDISCLOSED`; under the conservative variant an `UNDISCLOSED` provenance carries a further surcharge of 10 points on top of that 20. Output rights contribute nothing for `FULL_ASSIGNMENT`, 6 points for `LICENSE_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` and 16 points for `VENDOR_RETAINS`. Retention contributes according to the `effective_retention_days` the variant scored: nothing when that figure is at most 30 days, 5 points when it is above 30 and at most 90, 10 points when it is above 90 and at most 365, and 18 points when it exceeds 365. A further 6 points are added when `retention_opt_out` is false, and nothing when it is true. A final 5 points are added when `subprocessors_disclosed` is false, and nothing when it is true. The total is banded into a grade: at most 8 points is `A`, above 8 and at most 20 is `B`, above 20 and at most 34 is `C`, and anything above 34 is `D`. Only the single assessment row being scored participates; no other row contributes.

The two grading variants differ in exactly one input and one surcharge, and the difference is deliberate — the same stored row can grade two bands better once the contract is actually documented. Under `CONSERVATIVE_LEGAL`, `effective_retention_days` is taken as a worst-case assumption of 3650 days whenever `retention_days` is null **or** whenever `retention_opt_out` is false, and only otherwise is the declared `retention_days` used; the `UNDISCLOSED` provenance surcharge of 10 points also applies. Under `DOCUMENTED_TERMS`, `effective_retention_days` is the declared `retention_days` verbatim and no provenance surcharge is added at all. The conservative variant is always the one applied at submission time.

A `GovernanceOfficer` then decides the assessment, supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, the `terms_variant` the decision stands on, and an optional `decision_rationale`. Whichever way the decision falls, the grading is re-run under the requested variant and `effective_retention_days`, `terms_risk_points` and `terms_grade` are all overwritten, and `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are recorded. Deciding under `DOCUMENTED_TERMS` requires the assessment's `retention_days` to be non-null; a null figure makes the documented variant unavailable and the decision is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_TERMS_UNDOCUMENTED`. The two branches then diverge in one specific way: accepting moves the assessment `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` **and** copies its `terms_grade` onto the parent asset's `vendor_terms_grade`, whereas rejecting moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and leaves the asset's `vendor_terms_grade` untouched. An assessment that is not `SUBMITTED` cannot be decided again and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

### 3.2. Intake, Provisional Classification and the Revision Loop

A `Requester` opens the governance conversation by filing a **Use Case** intake. The filing declares a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, the owning `business_unit` code of 1 to 40 characters, a `description` of 1 to 2000 characters narrating what the system does and how its outputs are used, the boolean `affects_individuals` (true when outputs are applied to identifiable natural persons), the `decision_autonomy` level, the `data_sensitivity` class, the `individual_impact` severity, a `jurisdictions` array, a non-negative `deployed_headcount_reach` giving the number of individuals the deployment can touch per year, and optionally a `predecessor_use_case_id` naming the prior-generation use case this intake supersedes. A first-generation intake omits `predecessor_use_case_id`, which then holds null.

Four enumerations carry the whole weight of the risk model. `decision_autonomy` is one of `ADVISORY`, `HUMAN_IN_LOOP`, `HUMAN_ON_LOOP` or `FULLY_AUTOMATED`. `data_sensitivity` is one of `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `SPECIAL_CATEGORY`. `individual_impact` is one of `NONE`, `LOW`, `MATERIAL` or `CONSEQUENTIAL`. `jurisdictions` is an array whose members are drawn from `EU`, `UK`, `US_CA`, `US_CO` and `US_FED`; the array may be empty but may not contain the same jurisdiction twice.

A newly filed intake lands in status `DRAFT` with `version` 1 and `requester_user_id` set to the filer's `X-User-ID`. It starts with `classification_stale` false, `classification_cycle_index` 0, `risk_score` null, `risk_tier` null, `required_gate_types` empty, `final_risk_score` null, `final_risk_tier` null, `control_credit` null, `vendor_penalty` null, `open_conditions_count` 0, `cleared_gate_count` 0, `monitoring_posture` `HEALTHY`, `safety_flag` false, `approved_at` null, `approved_by_user_id` null, `approval_expires_at` null, `reassessment_interval_days` null, `reassessment_due_at` null, `reassessment_cycle_index` 0, `reassessment_status` `NOT_SCHEDULED`, `rejection_reason` null, `revoked_at` null and `revocation_reason` null. Its lifecycle statuses are `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `UNDER_REASSESSMENT`, `SUSPENDED`, `REJECTED` and `REVOKED`; `REJECTED` and `REVOKED` are terminal. Use cases may be listed with optional `status`, `risk_tier`, `final_risk_tier`, `business_unit` and `predecessor_use_case_id` filters.

Intake data stays editable, but only by the `Requester` whose `X-User-ID` matches the use case's own `requester_user_id`, and only from `DRAFT` or from `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. A revision attempted from any other status is rejected with `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_IN_DRAFT` (HTTP 409) — the same error classification raises for the same reason; because that check sits at step 11 of the §2.9 ladder, a `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` use case never reaches it and answers `ERR_USE_CASE_TERMINAL` at step 10 instead. A revision may overwrite any subset of `title`, `description`, `affects_individuals`, `decision_autonomy`, `data_sensitivity`, `individual_impact`, `jurisdictions` and `deployed_headcount_reach`, and at least one of those must be supplied — an empty revision body is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Neither `business_unit` nor `predecessor_use_case_id` may be changed after filing. A revision from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` lands the use case back in `DRAFT`; a revision from `DRAFT` leaves it in `DRAFT`. Either way the consequences are the same and they are severe by design: `classification_stale` is set to true, every `ReviewGate` on the use case whose status is `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` or `CLEARED` is moved to `VOIDED` with its `control_debt_index` zeroed to 0.00 and its `open_condition_count` zeroed, both `open_conditions_count` and `cleared_gate_count` on the use case are recomputed and therefore become 0, and the use case's `version` increments. Editing the declaration invalidates the classification and every gate that was pinned to it, because the reviews that gate the intake were scoped to facts that have just changed.

Classification is a `GovernanceOfficer` action, is permitted only from `DRAFT` — any other status is rejected with `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_IN_DRAFT` — and requires `expected_use_case_version` to match the use case's current `version`. It computes a provisional score from five point contributions, all read from the use case's own declared intake fields. Individual impact contributes nothing for `NONE`, 10 points for `LOW`, 25 points for `MATERIAL` and 40 points for `CONSEQUENTIAL`; these land on the snapshot as `impact_points`. Decision autonomy contributes nothing for `ADVISORY`, 8 points for `HUMAN_IN_LOOP`, 18 points for `HUMAN_ON_LOOP` and 30 points for `FULLY_AUTOMATED`, recorded as `autonomy_points`. Data sensitivity contributes nothing for `PUBLIC`, 6 points for `INTERNAL`, 14 points for `CONFIDENTIAL` and 24 points for `SPECIAL_CATEGORY`, recorded as `sensitivity_points`. Reach contributes by band, taking the first band that matches `deployed_headcount_reach`: 6 points at one hundred thousand individuals a year or more, otherwise 4 points at ten thousand or more, otherwise 2 points at one thousand or more, otherwise nothing; this is `reach_points`. Finally, `jurisdiction_points` is 3 points for each declared jurisdiction that is one of the three high-scrutiny jurisdictions `EU`, `UK` and `US_CO` — a use case declaring all three of those plus `US_FED` therefore earns 9 points, not 12. The five contributions add up to `base_score`.

From `base_score` the score is adjusted by adding the applicable `vendor_penalty` and subtracting the applicable `control_credit`, and the result is rounded half up to a whole number and clamped into 0 through 100 inclusive to give `risk_score`. At classification time both adjustments are nil by definition — the provisional pass scores the *declaration*, not the controls, so `vendor_penalty` is 0 and `control_credit` is 0, and the snapshot records `cleared_gate_count` and `passed_eval_count` as 0 even in the unusual case where such rows already exist. Banding then follows: a `risk_score` of 85 or more is `PROHIBITED`, 70 or more is `HIGH`, 35 or more is `LIMITED`, and anything below 35 is `MINIMAL`.

One override takes precedence over the bands entirely. When `affects_individuals` is true **and** `individual_impact` is `CONSEQUENTIAL` **and** `decision_autonomy` is `FULLY_AUTOMATED` **and** `data_sensitivity` is `SPECIAL_CATEGORY`, the combination is prohibited outright: `forced_prohibited` on the snapshot is set to true and `risk_tier` becomes `PROHIBITED` regardless of what the score came to. All four conditions must hold simultaneously; three out of four leaves the ordinary bands in charge.

Classification therefore forks into two branches from one action, and their side effects diverge sharply. Both branches increment `classification_cycle_index` by 1, append a `ClassificationSnapshot` for that cycle, write `risk_score` and `risk_tier` onto the use case, clear `classification_stale` to false, and increment the use case's `version`.

- **On the ordinary branch**, where the prohibited combination does not hold, the use case moves `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`. `required_gate_types` is set from the provisional tier in the canonical order `LEGAL`, `PRIVACY`, `SECURITY`, `BUSINESS`: a `MINIMAL` tier demands only the `BUSINESS` gate, a `LIMITED` tier demands `PRIVACY` and `BUSINESS`, a `HIGH` tier demands all four, and a `PROHIBITED` tier demands none. Every pre-existing gate on the use case that is not already `VOIDED` is moved to `VOIDED` with `control_debt_index` zeroed to 0.00, and then one fresh `ReviewGate` is created per entry of `required_gate_types`, in that canonical creation order, each starting in status `PENDING`, each with `pinned_risk_tier` frozen to the tier just computed and `pinned_classification_index` frozen to the new `classification_cycle_index`. Both `open_conditions_count` and `cleared_gate_count` are recomputed to 0.
- **On the prohibited branch**, where the combination holds, the use case moves `DRAFT → REJECTED`, which is terminal. The snapshot records `forced_prohibited` true and `risk_tier` `PROHIBITED`; `required_gate_types` is set to empty and **no gate rows are created at all**; `rejection_reason` is recorded as the literal string `PROHIBITED_COMBINATION`; and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `CLASSIFICATION_REJECTED` is appended with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00.

Every classification leaves behind an immutable **Classification Snapshot** recording, for that `cycle_index` and `variant`, the `use_case_id`, all five contributions (`impact_points`, `autonomy_points`, `sensitivity_points`, `reach_points`, `jurisdiction_points`), the `base_score`, the `vendor_penalty` and `control_credit` the variant applied, the frozen `cleared_gate_count` and `passed_eval_count` behind that credit, the resulting `risk_score` and `risk_tier`, the `forced_prohibited` flag, and the `computed_at` instant. The `variant` is `INTAKE_PROVISIONAL` for a classification run and `DEPLOYMENT_FINAL` for the one the approval writes. Snapshots for a use case can be listed, optionally filtered by `variant`, and the currently effective provisional snapshot is the `INTAKE_PROVISIONAL` row with the highest `cycle_index`.

### 3.3. Gate Review by the Four Domain Reviewers

A **Review Gate** is the unit of domain review. It carries its `use_case_id`, its `gate_type`, its `status` — one of `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, `CLEARED` or the terminal `VOIDED` — the `pinned_risk_tier` and `pinned_classification_index` frozen onto it at creation, the `assigned_reviewer_user_id` of whoever most recently took it up, its `conditions` array, its derived `open_condition_count`, its `control_debt_index`, the `debt_before_clear` figure it stashes at clearance, the `cleared_by_user_id` and `cleared_at` of its clearance, and the `decision_note` a reviewer recorded on the latest decision. A use case's gates can be listed with optional `status` and `gate_type` filters, and because the creation order is canonical, that listing is index-stable.

Taking up a gate moves it `PENDING → IN_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONS_ISSUED → IN_REVIEW`, sets `assigned_reviewer_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and is permitted only while the parent use case is in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Coming out of `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` it additionally wipes the slate: `conditions` becomes empty, `open_condition_count` drops to 0 and `control_debt_index` drops to 0.00. The use case's `open_conditions_count` is then recomputed, and if it reaches 0 the use case bubbles `CHANGES_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; while any other gate still holds open conditions the use case stays in `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. That the take-up action also accepts a gate sitting in `PENDING` matters more than it looks: a failed test evaluation pushes a use case to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` without any gate ever having issued a condition, and in that situation a reviewer picking up a hitherto untouched gate is the only route back to `UNDER_REVIEW`.

Issuing conditions moves a gate `IN_REVIEW → CONDITIONS_ISSUED`. The reviewer supplies a `conditions` array of between 1 and 20 remediation strings, and optionally a `decision_note`. `open_condition_count` becomes the number of entries supplied, `control_debt_index` is recomputed for the gate, the use case's `open_conditions_count` is recomputed, and the use case bubbles `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` — any single gate issuing conditions pushes the whole intake back to its requester.

Control debt is the mechanism by which outstanding conditions are made visible as a portfolio quantity. A gate's `control_debt_index` is its gate-type weight, multiplied by its `open_condition_count` divided by 3, multiplied by the tier multiplier of its own `pinned_risk_tier`, with the product rounded half up to two decimal places. The gate-type weights are 1.35 for `LEGAL`, 1.25 for `PRIVACY`, 1.15 for `SECURITY` and 0.75 for `BUSINESS`. The tier multipliers are 0.5 for `MINIMAL`, 1.0 for `LIMITED`, 1.5 for `HIGH` and 2.0 for `PROHIBITED`. Because `open_condition_count` is 0 in every status other than `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, a gate that is not currently holding conditions contributes exactly 0.00. Gates in `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` and `CLEARED` all participate in the portfolio total; `VOIDED` gates are excluded outright, and the rounding order set out in §2.7 — per gate first, then sum — is mandatory.

Clearing a gate is the densest reviewer action, and it runs an ordered battery of preconditions. The gate must be in `IN_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` (a reviewer may clear straight out of `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` when the requester satisfied the conditions out of band); the acting role must own the `gate_type`, or the request fails with `ERR_GATE_ROLE_MISMATCH`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must not be the use case's `requester_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The gate's `pinned_classification_index` must still equal the use case's `classification_cycle_index` and `classification_stale` must still be false — but a gate whose pin has fallen behind was voided by the very reclassification or intake revision that advanced the index, so in practice that gate is already `VOIDED` and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_GATE_NOT_IN_REVIEWABLE_STATE`.

Three of the four gate types then demand cross-aggregate evidence, and the fourth demands none:

- A `LEGAL` gate requires the use case to hold a `PINNED` `ModelBinding` — with none, the clearance fails with `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_MISSING` — and requires the asset behind that binding to be acceptable on vendor terms: either the asset's `is_third_party` is false, or it carries a `VendorModelAssessment` with status `ACCEPTED` whose `terms_grade` is `A`, `B` or `C`. Otherwise the clearance fails with `ERR_VENDOR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`.
- A `SECURITY` gate requires a `TestEvaluation` on the use case whose `eval_type` is `SECURITY_REDTEAM` and whose status is `PASSED`; without one the clearance fails with `ERR_REQUIRED_EVALUATION_MISSING`. Note carefully that the gate does **not** consult the tier's required-evaluation list — only the red-team run.
- A `PRIVACY` gate requires that for every jurisdiction in the use case's `jurisdictions` array there exists a `PUBLISHED` `DisclosureNotice` on that use case for that jurisdiction; a jurisdiction without one fails the clearance with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PUBLISHED`.
- A `BUSINESS` gate requires no evaluation evidence, no vendor terms and no notices — only the ordinary status, role and segregation checks.

Once those pass, the clearance captures `debt_before_clear` from the gate's current `control_debt_index` (this is what makes the compensating reopen exact), zeroes `control_debt_index` to 0.00 and `open_condition_count` to 0, records `cleared_by_user_id` and `cleared_at`, and appends a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `GATE_CLEARED` with `reach_delta` 0 and a `debt_delta` of minus the captured `debt_before_clear`. The use case's `cleared_gate_count` is recomputed. When, after this clearance, every non-`VOIDED` gate on the use case is `CLEARED` and `cleared_gate_count` equals the number of entries in `required_gate_types`, the use case bubbles `UNDER_REVIEW → READY_FOR_APPROVAL`.

Reopening is the compensating action, reserved to a `GovernanceOfficer`, and it exists so that a premature clearance can be undone without drift. It moves a gate `CLEARED → IN_REVIEW`, and is permitted only while the parent use case is in `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`. It restores `control_debt_index` verbatim from `debt_before_clear`, sets `open_condition_count` to 0, nulls both `cleared_by_user_id` and `cleared_at`, optionally records a `decision_note`, and appends a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `GATE_REOPENED` with `reach_delta` 0 and a `debt_delta` of plus that same `debt_before_clear`. Re-supplying the conditions is not required — the restored `open_condition_count` is whatever the restored debt implies. The use case's `cleared_gate_count` and `open_conditions_count` are recomputed and the use case is pushed `READY_FOR_APPROVAL → UNDER_REVIEW`. Because the clearance booked minus that figure and the reopen books plus the identical figure, the pair sums to zero and the portfolio debt total returns to its exact pre-clearance cent value.

Gates also die quietly. Whenever the parent use case is reclassified or its intake fields are revised, every gate in `PENDING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` or `CLEARED` is moved to `VOIDED`, its `control_debt_index` set to 0.00 and `open_condition_count` set to 0, and the use case's `open_conditions_count` and `cleared_gate_count` recomputed accordingly. Each voided gate's own `version` increments. Voiding a gate out of `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` permanently removes its contribution from the portfolio debt total, and — unlike clearance and reopening — it appends no ledger entry, so voiding is deliberately not a compensating event.

### 3.4. Binding a Model and Reserving Exclusive Test Windows

A **Model Binding** is the junction between a use case and the specific model asset version it will run, and a `GovernanceOfficer` creates one by naming the `model_asset_id` and supplying `expected_use_case_version`. The referenced asset must be `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, otherwise the creation is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_USE`, and the use case's `risk_tier` must not be `PROHIBITED`, otherwise it is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_CASE`. The new binding lands in status `PINNED` with `pinned_model_version` set to the asset's `version` at that instant and `pinned_at` set to the current logical instant.

Creating a binding supersedes whatever came before it on that use case. Every prior binding of the use case whose status is `PINNED` or `STALE` is moved to `RETIRED` with `superseded_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_BINDING`; for each such retirement the corresponding asset's `active_binding_count` is decremented by 1; every `TestEvaluation` still in `PLANNED` on a retired binding is moved to `VOIDED`; the newly bound asset's `active_binding_count` is incremented by 1; and the use case's `version` increments. A binding's `status` is one of `PINNED`, `STALE` or the terminal `RETIRED`, and its `superseded_reason`, null while the binding is current, is one of `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_BINDING`, `USE_CASE_REVOKED`, `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` or `ASSET_REAPPROVED` once it has left `PINNED`. Bindings can be listed per use case (current and historical) and per asset (across every use case), both with an optional `status` filter.

A binding leaves `PINNED` for `STALE` in exactly two circumstances: its `pinned_model_version` has fallen below the asset's current `version`, in which case `superseded_reason` is `ASSET_REAPPROVED`; or its parent use case has left `APPROVED` for `SUSPENDED`, in which case `superseded_reason` is `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED`. Either way, every `PLANNED` evaluation on the binding is voided along with it. The reverse edge exists only for the suspension case: when a suspended use case is reinstated, a binding whose `superseded_reason` is `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` and whose `pinned_model_version` still matches its asset's current `version` returns to `PINNED` with `superseded_reason` cleared to null. A binding stale for the `ASSET_REAPPROVED` reason never returns; a new binding must be created.

The testing programme runs on bindings. A `SecurityReviewer` or a `GovernanceOfficer` schedules a **Test Evaluation** by naming the `use_case_id` whose tier fixes the thresholds, the `model_binding_id` under test, the `eval_type` — one of `BIAS`, `ROBUSTNESS`, `HALLUCINATION` or `SECURITY_REDTEAM` — and the pair `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` bounding the exclusive test window the run reserves on the bound asset. `window_end_at` must be strictly after `window_start_at`. The new row lands in status `PLANNED`, and its statuses thereafter are `PASSED`, `FAILED` and `VOIDED`, all terminal. Evaluations can be listed with optional `use_case_id`, `model_binding_id`, `eval_type` and `status` filters, and read individually.

Test capacity on an asset is exclusive, and the window semantics are **half-open**: a window covers its `window_start_at` inclusively and stops short of its `window_end_at`. The candidate set for a conflict is every evaluation whose `model_binding_id` points at *any* binding of the same asset — not merely the same binding — whose status is `PLANNED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED`. Two windows conflict when the new window starts strictly before the existing one ends and the existing one starts strictly before the new one ends. Because both comparisons are strict, windows that merely touch do not conflict: an existing window ending at some instant and a new window beginning at that exact same instant are both accepted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Evaluations in `VOIDED` are excluded from the candidate set and therefore free the window they had reserved.

At the moment of scheduling, and never again, the evaluation freezes a threshold profile from the parent use case's provisional `risk_tier`. That profile is exactly three fields — `required_metric_name`, `required_metric_threshold` and `required_sample_size` — and nothing more: the tier that produced them is consumed at scheduling time and is not itself recorded on the row, so a `TestEvaluation` carries no `pinned_risk_tier` and no tier field of any name (`pinned_risk_tier` belongs to a `ReviewGate`). `required_metric_name` is the field the profile will score: `worst_group_parity_ratio` for a `BIAS` run, `adversarial_pass_rate` for a `ROBUSTNESS` run, `grounded_accuracy` for a `HALLUCINATION` run, and `adversarial_pass_rate` again for a `SECURITY_REDTEAM` run. `required_metric_threshold` is drawn from the tier: at `HIGH` the thresholds are 0.80 for `BIAS`, 0.90 for `ROBUSTNESS`, 0.95 for `HALLUCINATION` and 0.95 for `SECURITY_REDTEAM`; at `LIMITED` they are 0.70, 0.80, 0.85 and 0.85 respectively; at `MINIMAL` they are 0.60, 0.70, 0.75 and 0.75; and at `PROHIBITED` every threshold is 1.00. `required_sample_size` is likewise drawn from the tier: 100 scored examples at `MINIMAL`, 300 at `LIMITED`, and 1000 at both `HIGH` and `PROHIBITED`.

Completing a run records its results and lets the frozen profile decide the outcome. The submitter supplies `sample_size`, the number of scored examples in the run, optionally an `evidence_uri` pointing at the stored artefact, and the metric the profile named — `worst_group_parity_ratio` is the lowest subgroup selection-rate ratio observed, `adversarial_pass_rate` is the fraction of adversarial probes the model withstood, and `grounded_accuracy` is the fraction of outputs traceable to the supplied context. Each is a value between 0 and 1 carried to four decimal places. The body must contain whichever of the three `required_metric_name` designates; a body that omits it is malformed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The value supplied under that name is stored as `observed_metric_value`, and `completed_at` is set to the current logical instant. The run lands in `PASSED` when `observed_metric_value` is at least `required_metric_threshold` **and** `sample_size` is at least `required_sample_size`; a value exactly equal to the threshold passes, because both comparisons are inclusive. If either comparison fails, the run lands in `FAILED`. Metrics are compared at the four decimal places supplied with no rounding beforehand. A run that is not `PLANNED` cannot be completed and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_PLANNED`.

The two outcome branches carry deliberately different consequences. A `PASSED` run of any type sets `observed_metric_value` and `completed_at`, and if its `eval_type` is `BIAS` it additionally clears the use case's `safety_flag` to false. A `FAILED` run also sets `observed_metric_value` and `completed_at`, but if its `eval_type` is `BIAS` it raises the use case's `safety_flag` to true, and — whatever its type — if the use case is currently in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` it pushes the use case to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. So a failed bias run both raises the safety flag and pushes the intake back, while a failed robustness run only pushes it back. The `safety_flag` therefore reads true precisely when some `BIAS` evaluation on the use case has ended `FAILED` and no later `BIAS` evaluation has ended `PASSED`.

### 3.5. Designing Human Oversight

Consequential decisions need a human in the loop, and the shape of that human involvement is filed as an **Oversight Design** by the use case's `Requester`, together with `expected_use_case_version` proving the filer's view of the use case is current. The filing declares the `review_mode` — one of `NONE`, `SAMPLED`, `PRE_DECISION_REVIEW` or `DUAL_REVIEW` — the `sample_pct` percentage of decisions a human reviews (an integer from 0 through 100 inclusive, scored only when the mode is `SAMPLED`), the `escalation_sla_hours` within which an escalated consequential decision must reach a human decision-maker (an integer from 0 through 240 inclusive), the `reviewer_role` that staffs the oversight queue, which must be one of `LegalReviewer`, `PrivacyReviewer`, `SecurityReviewer` or `BusinessReviewer`, and the boolean `override_capability` recording whether the human reviewer can overturn a model output before it takes effect. The design lands in status `DRAFT`; its other statuses, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, are both terminal. Designs on a use case can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

Filing a design supersedes any earlier one: every prior `OversightDesign` on the same use case still in `DRAFT` is moved to `REJECTED`, with `decided_by_user_id` recorded as the literal string `system` and `decided_at` set to the current logical instant. The use case's `version` increments on the filing.

Adequacy is scored at filing time and re-evaluated when the design is decided. Four components add together. The mode base contributes nothing for `NONE`, 30 points for `SAMPLED`, 70 points for `PRE_DECISION_REVIEW` and 90 points for `DUAL_REVIEW`. The sample component applies only when `review_mode` is `SAMPLED`, and is `sample_pct` multiplied by three tenths, taken down to the next whole number, then capped at 30 — so sampling every decision earns the full 30 while sampling half of them earns 15; for every other review mode the sample component is 0 regardless of what `sample_pct` says. The SLA component starts from 20 points and subtracts one point for every whole 6 hours of `escalation_sla_hours`, with the result floored at 0 so it can never go negative — an SLA of 6 hours costs 1 point, an SLA of 240 hours costs the full 20 and leaves nothing. The override component is 10 points when `override_capability` is true and nothing when it is false. `adequacy_score` is the sum of the four, capped at 100. Both flooring operations happen inside their own components, before the outer capping and flooring are applied — never afterwards.

The bar the score must clear, `required_adequacy_score`, is selected two-dimensionally from the use case's provisional `risk_tier` and its `decision_autonomy`, and only the two fields participate. At a `HIGH` tier the requirement is 60 for `ADVISORY`, 75 for `HUMAN_IN_LOOP`, 85 for `HUMAN_ON_LOOP` and 95 for `FULLY_AUTOMATED`. At a `LIMITED` tier it is 45, 60, 70 and 80 for those same four autonomy levels. At a `MINIMAL` tier it is a flat 30 whatever the autonomy level. At a `PROHIBITED` tier it is a flat 100 — attainable only by a design that maxes every component. Because the threshold selection reads the tier, a design cannot be decided while the use case's `risk_tier` is still null.

A `GovernanceOfficer` decides the design by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`; both branches record `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`. Approving moves the design `DRAFT → APPROVED` and requires `adequacy_score` to be at least `required_adequacy_score`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_OVERSIGHT_DESIGN_INADEQUATE`. Rejecting moves it `DRAFT → REJECTED` and applies no adequacy test at all. Either decision is refused with `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_CASE` when the use case's `risk_tier` is `PROHIBITED`, and a design that has already left `DRAFT` cannot be decided again — that attempt is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

### 3.6. Statutory Disclosure Notices

Different jurisdictions demand different notices, and the system tracks each publication as a **Disclosure Notice** on the use case. Either the `Requester` or a `GovernanceOfficer` publishes one, declaring the `notice_type` it discharges — one of `USER_FACING_AI_DISCLOSURE`, `AUTOMATED_DECISION_NOTICE`, `TRAINING_DATA_NOTICE` or `EMPLOYMENT_SCREENING_NOTICE` — the `jurisdiction` whose law requires it, drawn from `EU`, `UK`, `US_CA`, `US_CO` and `US_FED`, the inclusive `effective_from` instant at which it comes into force, the inclusive `effective_to` instant at which it ceases (which must be strictly after `effective_from`), and the `locale_count` giving the number of locales it was published in, which must be at least 1. A published notice lands in status `PUBLISHED`; its only other status is the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. Notices on a use case can be listed with optional `status`, `jurisdiction` and `notice_type` filters.

A notice window is **closed** at both ends — it includes both `effective_from` and `effective_to` — and it additionally reserves a quiet buffer of 7 days after it ends. Uniqueness is scoped to the triple of use case, `notice_type` and `jurisdiction`, and only `PUBLISHED` rows on that triple are candidates. A new notice conflicts with an existing one when the new window's `effective_from` falls at or before 7 days after the existing window's `effective_to` **and** the existing window's `effective_from` falls at or before 7 days after the new window's `effective_to`. Because the comparisons are inclusive, touching windows do conflict, and so does a new window that begins anywhere inside the 7-day buffer following an existing one — for an existing window ending at `2026-09-30T00:00:00Z`, the earliest start the system will accept for a successor is `2026-10-08T00:00:00Z`. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A `WITHDRAWN` notice is excluded from the candidate set and therefore frees its slot immediately.

Withdrawal moves a notice `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN` and stamps `withdrawn_at` with the current logical instant, releasing the jurisdiction slot for a fresh publication. Either a `GovernanceOfficer` or the use case's own `Requester` may withdraw a notice; when the actor is a `Requester`, their `X-User-ID` must match the use case's `requester_user_id`. Withdrawal also happens without any client request as part of the revocation cascade. A notice already `WITHDRAWN` cannot be withdrawn again and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN`.

Two separate rules consume these notices, and they are not the same rule. Clearing a `PRIVACY` gate requires merely that a `PUBLISHED` notice exists on the use case for each declared jurisdiction. Signing the approval demands more: for each declared jurisdiction there must be a `PUBLISHED` notice **in force at the current logical instant** — that is, one whose `effective_from` is at or before now and whose `effective_to` is at or after now. Either failure is reported as `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A use case with an empty `jurisdictions` array trivially satisfies both.

### 3.7. Signing the Deployment Approval

Approval is the single densest operation in the system. A `GovernanceOfficer` signs it against a use case in `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, supplying `expected_use_case_version`; anything else in that field is rejected with `ERR_STALE_USE_CASE_VERSION`, and a use case in any other status is rejected with `ERR_USE_CASE_NOT_READY_FOR_APPROVAL`. The signature reads gates, bindings, assets, vendor terms, evaluations, the oversight design and the published notices, and then rewrites the very tier it was gated on.

Beyond status and version, the following must all hold. `classification_stale` must be false. The provisional `risk_tier` must not be `PROHIBITED`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_CASE`. `safety_flag` must be false, or it fails with `ERR_BIAS_EVALUATION_FAILED`. Every `eval_type` the provisional tier demands must have a `PASSED` evaluation on the use case, or it fails with `ERR_REQUIRED_EVALUATION_MISSING` — and the tier's demands are specific: a `MINIMAL` tier demands no evaluations at all, a `LIMITED` tier demands a passed `BIAS` run, a `HIGH` tier demands passed `BIAS`, `ROBUSTNESS` and `HALLUCINATION` runs, and a `PROHIBITED` tier demands none (it can never be approved anyway). Note that `SECURITY_REDTEAM` is not on any tier's approval list; it is demanded only by the `SECURITY` gate. An `OversightDesign` with status `APPROVED` must exist on the use case, or the attempt fails with `ERR_OVERSIGHT_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`. A `ModelBinding` with status `PINNED` must exist, or it fails with `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_MISSING`, and that binding's `pinned_model_version` must still equal its asset's current `version`, or it fails with `ERR_MODEL_BINDING_STALE`. For every jurisdiction in `jurisdictions` a `PUBLISHED` notice must be in force at the current logical instant, or it fails with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. And segregation of duties must hold: the signing `X-User-ID` must differ from the use case's `requester_user_id` and must differ from the `cleared_by_user_id` of every gate on the use case whose status is `CLEARED`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Once the preconditions pass, finalization runs in a **pinned six-step order**. The order is not a stylistic preference: the dependency graph looks circular — `required_gate_types` came from the provisional tier, the control credit comes from the gates that tier demanded, the final tier comes from the control credit, and the reassessment interval comes from the final tier — and the value is well defined only because the steps run in this sequence.

1. **Freeze the evidence.** Count the use case's gates whose status is `CLEARED` into `cleared_gate_count`, and count the use case's evaluations whose status is `PASSED` into `passed_eval_count`. Gates and evaluations in any other status are excluded.
2. **Compute the control credit.** `control_credit` is 3 points for each cleared gate plus 2 points for each passed evaluation, capped at 15 — so a `HIGH`-tier use case with all four gates cleared and three evaluations passed earns 12 plus 6, which the cap trims to 15.
3. **Compute the vendor penalty.** Take the use case's `PINNED` binding and the asset it names. If that asset's `is_third_party` is false, `vendor_penalty` is 0. Otherwise it is drawn from the asset's `vendor_terms_grade`: nothing for grade `A`, 5 for grade `B`, 12 for grade `C` and 25 for grade `D`. Where more than one assessment has ever been accepted on the asset, the asset's own current `vendor_terms_grade` field is authoritative.
4. **Recompute the score under the deployment-final variant.** The five declared point contributions are computed exactly as they were at classification, summed into `base_score`, then the `vendor_penalty` from step 3 is added and the `control_credit` from step 2 is subtracted; the result is rounded half up to a whole number and clamped into 0 through 100 to give `final_risk_score`. `final_risk_tier` is banded from that score by the same bands as before — 85 or more is `PROHIBITED`, 70 or more is `HIGH`, 35 or more is `LIMITED`, below 35 is `MINIMAL` — and the same prohibited-combination override still takes precedence over the bands. Because control evidence now counts against the score while the declaration has not changed, an identical declaration yields a final score that is lower than or equal to its provisional score whenever any control evidence exists, and it can land in a lower band.
5. **Select the reassessment interval.** `reassessment_interval_days` is 730 days (two years) for a `MINIMAL` final tier, 365 days (one year) for a `LIMITED` final tier, and 180 days (half a year) for a `HIGH` final tier.
6. **Stamp the approval clock.** `approved_at` is the current logical instant, `approval_expires_at` is that instant plus `reassessment_interval_days` days, `reassessment_due_at` is set to the very same instant as `approval_expires_at`, `reassessment_cycle_index` becomes 1 and `reassessment_status` becomes `SCHEDULED`.

Only after step 4 has produced `final_risk_tier`, and only when that tier is `HIGH`, does the portfolio limit apply. The sum of `deployed_headcount_reach` over every use case already in an inventory status whose `final_risk_tier` is `HIGH`, plus this use case's own `deployed_headcount_reach`, must not exceed 250,000 individuals; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_REACH_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A use case whose final tier comes out `LIMITED` or `MINIMAL` never consults the cap at all — which means the cap can be dodged legitimately by earning enough control credit to drop out of the `HIGH` band.

On success the use case moves `READY_FOR_APPROVAL → APPROVED`. A `ClassificationSnapshot` with `variant` `DEPLOYMENT_FINAL` is appended under the current `classification_cycle_index`, recording the frozen `cleared_gate_count` and `passed_eval_count`, the `control_credit` and `vendor_penalty` applied, and the resulting score and tier. `control_credit`, `vendor_penalty`, `final_risk_score` and `final_risk_tier` are written onto the use case, `approved_by_user_id` records the signing `X-User-ID`, `monitoring_posture` is set to `HEALTHY`, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `APPROVAL_GRANTED` is appended with a `reach_delta` of plus the use case's `deployed_headcount_reach` and a `debt_delta` of 0.00. The use case's `version` increments.

Approval also closes out the generation it replaces. When `predecessor_use_case_id` is non-null and the use case it names is itself in status `APPROVED`, that predecessor is revoked with `revocation_reason` recorded as `SUPERSEDED` — and that revocation is a real revocation, running the full cascade described in §3.8: the predecessor's `PINNED` bindings are retired, which decrements the `active_binding_count` of each asset they named, which can in turn restrict a third-party asset and expire its accepted assessments. A predecessor in any status other than `APPROVED` is left alone.

### 3.8. Post-Deployment Monitoring, Suspension, Reinstatement and Revocation

Once a use case is live, failures observed in production are filed as **Monitoring Incidents**. A `GovernanceOfficer`, `SecurityReviewer`, `PrivacyReviewer` or `Requester` reports one by naming the `use_case_id` it concerns, the `severity` — one of `SEV1`, `SEV2` or `SEV3` — the `category` of failure observed, which is one of `HARMFUL_OUTPUT`, `DRIFT`, `PRIVACY_LEAK` or `OVERSIGHT_FAILURE`, the `detected_at` instant at which the failure was first observed (this is where the containment clock starts, not the moment of reporting), a `summary` of 1 to 1000 characters describing what happened and who was affected, and optionally the boolean `reported_to_regulator` recording whether the mandatory regulator notification has already been filed, which defaults to false. A new incident lands in status `OPEN`; its other statuses are `CONTAINED`, `ESCALATED` and the terminal `RESOLVED`. Incidents can be listed with optional `use_case_id`, `status` and `severity` filters, and read individually.

The containment clock is derived at reporting time. The effective tier is the use case's `final_risk_tier` when that is non-null, and its provisional `risk_tier` otherwise. `containment_hours` is the base allowance for the severity — 4 hours for `SEV1`, 24 hours for `SEV2`, 72 hours for `SEV3` — multiplied by a tier speed factor of 1.5 for `MINIMAL`, 1.0 for `LIMITED`, 0.5 for `HIGH` and 0.5 for `PROHIBITED`, with the product rounded up to the next whole hour. `containment_deadline_at` is `detected_at` plus that many hours. So a `SEV1` on a `HIGH`-tier deployment must be contained within 2 hours of detection, while the same severity on a `MINIMAL`-tier deployment gets 6.

Posture is derived from unresolved incidents each time an incident is reported, contained or resolved, and it considers only incidents on that use case whose status is `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`; `CONTAINED` and `RESOLVED` incidents are excluded. If any unresolved incident carries `SEV1`, `monitoring_posture` becomes `IMPAIRED`. Otherwise, if there is at least one unresolved incident of any severity, it becomes `WATCH`. With none outstanding, it becomes `HEALTHY`. The derivation is order-independent, so no tie-break is needed.

An `IMPAIRED` posture is not merely informational — it suspends the deployment. When posture re-derivation leaves a use case that is in `APPROVED` or `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` at `IMPAIRED`, that use case moves to `SUSPENDED`, every `ModelBinding` of the use case in `PINNED` drops to `STALE` with `superseded_reason` `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED`, every `TestEvaluation` in `PLANNED` on a binding that just went stale is `VOIDED`, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` is appended with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00. The reach delta is zero precisely because `SUSPENDED` remains an inventory status — a suspension does not take reach out of the portfolio.

Recording containment forks on the deadline. When the incident is `OPEN` and the current logical instant is at or before `containment_deadline_at`, it moves `OPEN → CONTAINED` and `contained_at` is stamped with the current instant. When the incident is `OPEN` and the current instant is strictly after `containment_deadline_at`, the very same action instead moves it `OPEN → ESCALATED`, and `contained_at` stays null — an escalated incident still counts as unresolved for posture purposes, so the posture does not improve. Either branch re-derives the use case's `monitoring_posture`. Containment out of `OPEN` may be recorded by a `GovernanceOfficer` or a `SecurityReviewer`; the escalated case is tighter, and only a `GovernanceOfficer` may bring an incident `ESCALATED → CONTAINED`. That last transition also carries a reporting duty: when the incident's `severity` is `SEV1` and the use case's `affects_individuals` is true, `reported_to_regulator` must be true, otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REGULATOR_REPORT_REQUIRED`. The containment action accepts an optional `reported_to_regulator` update so the filing can be recorded in the same breath. Note that when a posture returns to `HEALTHY` on containment, a suspended use case is *not* reinstated here — reinstatement happens only on resolution.

Resolution is a `GovernanceOfficer` action on a `CONTAINED` incident, optionally updating `reported_to_regulator`, and it carries the same `SEV1`-plus-`affects_individuals` reporting duty enforced by `ERR_REGULATOR_REPORT_REQUIRED`. It moves the incident `CONTAINED → RESOLVED`, stamps `resolved_at`, and re-derives the posture. If the posture now comes out `HEALTHY`, the use case is currently `SUSPENDED`, and the current logical instant is strictly before `approval_expires_at`, then the use case is reinstated `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`; every `ModelBinding` of the use case whose status is `STALE`, whose `superseded_reason` is `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` and whose `pinned_model_version` still equals its asset's current `version` returns to `PINNED` with `superseded_reason` cleared to null; and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `APPROVAL_REINSTATED` is appended with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00, keeping the reach total conserved across the whole suspend-and-reinstate round trip. If the approval has already expired, the resolution still succeeds — `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED` is **not** raised here — but the reinstatement is simply skipped and the use case stays `SUSPENDED`.

Revocation is the terminal exit, available to a `GovernanceOfficer` against a use case in `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` or `SUSPENDED`, and it takes a free-text `revocation_reason`. That reason is a mandatory string on the request body, with no length bound and no default: a revocation request that omits it, sends it as null or sends a non-string is malformed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at step 8 of the §2.9 ladder. It runs a four-hop transitive cascade in which each hop is caused by the one before it:

- **Hop 1.** The use case moves to `REVOKED`, `revoked_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, `revocation_reason` records the supplied reason, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `APPROVAL_REVOKED` is appended. Its `reach_delta` is minus the use case's `deployed_headcount_reach` when the use case was in an inventory status — `APPROVED`, `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` or `SUSPENDED` — and 0 when it was merely `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` and therefore never counted toward the portfolio. Its `debt_delta` is 0.00.
- **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1. Every `ModelBinding` of the use case whose status is `PINNED` or `STALE` moves to `RETIRED` with `superseded_reason` `USE_CASE_REVOKED`, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `BINDING_RETIRED` is appended for each retired binding.
- **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2. Each retired binding decrements the `active_binding_count` of the asset it named by 1. When that count reaches 0 while the asset's `is_third_party` is true and its status is `APPROVED_FOR_USE`, the asset moves to `RESTRICTED`.
- **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3. On each asset that just became `RESTRICTED`, every `VendorModelAssessment` with status `ACCEPTED` moves to `EXPIRED`, the asset's `vendor_terms_grade` is set to null, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `VENDOR_ASSESSMENT_EXPIRED` is appended against the use case whose revocation started the cascade.

Alongside those hops, every `TestEvaluation` in `PLANNED` on a retired binding moves to `VOIDED`, and every `DisclosureNotice` of the use case in `PUBLISHED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_at` stamped, each withdrawal appending a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `DISCLOSURE_WITHDRAWN`. The use case's own `version` increments. Because hop 1's negative reach delta exactly offsets the positive one the approval booked, the sum of `reach_delta` for that use case returns to 0 and the portfolio reach returns to its pre-approval value.

### 3.9. Periodic Reassessment, Shadow Deployments and the Governance Sweep

An approval is never permanent. When the logical clock reaches a use case's `reassessment_due_at`, the periodic sweep opens a reassessment cycle: the use case moves `APPROVED → UNDER_REASSESSMENT` and `reassessment_status` becomes `IN_PROGRESS`, or `OVERDUE` when the clock is already more than 30 days past `reassessment_due_at`. A `GovernanceOfficer` then closes the cycle by supplying an `outcome`, and the two outcomes diverge completely. The use case must be in `UNDER_REASSESSMENT`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_NOT_DUE`, and the closing officer must not be the use case's `requester_user_id`, or it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

- An outcome of `CONFIRMED` requires the use case's `monitoring_posture` to be `HEALTHY` and moves it `UNDER_REASSESSMENT → APPROVED`. `reassessment_cycle_index` increments by 1, `reassessment_due_at` is pushed out to the current logical instant plus `reassessment_interval_days` days, `approval_expires_at` is set to that same instant, `reassessment_status` returns to `SCHEDULED`, and a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `REASSESSMENT_COMPLETED` is appended with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00 — the reach total is untouched because the use case never left the inventory statuses.
- An outcome of `SUSPEND` moves it `UNDER_REASSESSMENT → SUSPENDED`, sets `reassessment_status` to `SCHEDULED` and pushes `reassessment_due_at` out to the current instant plus `reassessment_interval_days` days, drops every `ModelBinding` of the use case in `PINNED` to `STALE` with `superseded_reason` `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED`, and appends a `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00.

Either way the use case's `version` increments. Closing a reassessment strictly after the use case's `approval_expires_at` is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`; at exactly that instant the call still succeeds, for the reason set out in §2.9.

Unapproved deployments are the other half of inventory hygiene. A `GovernanceOfficer` or `SecurityReviewer` records a **Shadow Deployment** by naming the `model_asset_id` found running without approval, optionally the `claimed_use_case_id` the finder believes it belongs to (null when the deployment is unattributed), the `environment` it was found in — one of `SANDBOX`, `INTERNAL_PILOT` or `PRODUCTION` — the `discovered_source` that brought it to the office's attention, which is one of `NETWORK_SCAN`, `EXPENSE_REPORT`, `SELF_REPORT` or `VENDOR_INVOICE`, and the `discovered_at` instant at which the grace clock starts. The record lands in status `DISCOVERED` with `grace_days_granted` 0, and `grace_deadline_at` computed as `discovered_at` plus the environment's base grace plus `grace_days_granted` days. Base grace is generous where the risk is low and short where it is not: a month (30 days) in `SANDBOX`, a fortnight (14 days) in `INTERNAL_PILOT`, and one week (7 days) in `PRODUCTION`. Shadow deployments can be listed with optional `model_asset_id`, `claimed_use_case_id`, `status` and `environment` filters, and read individually. Their statuses are `DISCOVERED`, `LINKED`, `GRACE_GRANTED`, and the terminal pair `RETIRED` and `ENFORCED_BLOCK`.

One discovery has three divergent dispositions, all `GovernanceOfficer` actions. **Linking** claims that the deployment turns out to be covered by an existing approval: the officer supplies a `use_case_id`, the record moves to `LINKED` from either `DISCOVERED` or `GRACE_GRANTED`, and `claimed_use_case_id` is set to the supplied value. The link target must be a use case in status `APPROVED` that holds a `ModelBinding` in `PINNED` whose `model_asset_id` equals the shadow deployment's own `model_asset_id`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_SHADOW_LINK_MISMATCH`. **Granting grace** gives the business a bounded window to either link or tear the deployment down: it is available only from `DISCOVERED`, only while the current logical instant is at or before `grace_deadline_at` (later is rejected with `ERR_GRACE_PERIOD_ELAPSED`), and only for a `grace_days` figure of at least 1 and at most 30 (outside that range is rejected with `ERR_GRACE_EXTENSION_EXCEEDED`). It moves the record to `GRACE_GRANTED`, sets `grace_days_granted` to the supplied `grace_days` — **overwriting** any previous grant rather than accumulating on top of it — and recomputes `grace_deadline_at` as `discovered_at` plus base grace plus the new `grace_days_granted` days. **Retiring** records that the unapproved deployment has been torn down: it is available from `DISCOVERED`, `GRACE_GRANTED` or `LINKED`, moves the record to `RETIRED` and stamps `retired_at` with the current logical instant.

The **governance sweep** is a `GovernanceOfficer` capability that drives every deadline in the system, in a pinned three-step order, with each step processing its candidates in `created_at` ascending order. It reports back how much work it did: `reassessments_opened`, `approvals_suspended`, `shadows_enforced`, `assets_restricted` and `assessments_expired`.

**Step 1** opens due reassessments: every use case in `APPROVED` whose `reassessment_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant moves to `UNDER_REASSESSMENT`, with `reassessment_status` set to `IN_PROGRESS` or, when the clock is more than 30 days past due, to `OVERDUE`.

**Step 2** suspends expired approvals: every use case in `APPROVED` or `UNDER_REASSESSMENT` whose `approval_expires_at` is strictly before the current logical instant moves to `SUSPENDED`, dropping its `PINNED` bindings to `STALE` with `superseded_reason` `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED`, voiding the `PLANNED` evaluations on those bindings, and appending an `APPROVAL_SUSPENDED` ledger entry with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00. The asymmetry between the two steps is deliberate and load-bearing: step 1 compares at-or-after while step 2 compares strictly-after, so a sweep run at exactly the expiry instant opens the reassessment without suspending it. Since approval sets `reassessment_due_at` and `approval_expires_at` to the same instant, that asymmetry is the only way a use case is ever observable in `UNDER_REASSESSMENT`, and the only window in which a reassessment closure can be attempted late enough to raise `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`. Step ordering matters in the same direction: a use case whose reassessment opens in step 1 can still be suspended by step 2 within the same sweep, but never the other way round.

**Step 3** enforces elapsed shadow deployments, and it drives a three-hop cascade of its own. Every shadow deployment in `DISCOVERED` or `GRACE_GRANTED` whose `grace_deadline_at` is strictly before the current logical instant moves to `ENFORCED_BLOCK` (hop 1). Caused by that, the named asset's `enforced_shadow_count` increments by 1, and once that count is at least 1 an asset in `APPROVED_FOR_USE` moves to `RESTRICTED` (hop 2). Caused by that, every `VendorModelAssessment` on the asset with status `ACCEPTED` moves to `EXPIRED` and the asset's `vendor_terms_grade` is set to null (hop 3). A `GovernanceLedgerEntry` of type `SHADOW_ENFORCED` is appended with `reach_delta` 0 and `debt_delta` 0.00 against the shadow deployment's `claimed_use_case_id` when that field is non-null; an unattributed enforcement books no ledger entry because there is no use case to book it against.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint that mints a new record responds with HTTP 201 on success; every other successful call — reads, revisions, state transitions, the reconciliation read, the clock setter and the sweep — responds with HTTP 200. Where the Authorized Role(s) column reads "All six roles" it means `Requester`, `LegalReviewer`, `PrivacyReviewer`, `SecurityReviewer`, `BusinessReviewer` and `GovernanceOfficer`.
