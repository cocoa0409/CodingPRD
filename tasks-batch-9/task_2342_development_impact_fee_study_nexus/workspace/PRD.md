# Development Impact Fee Nexus API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a city charges new development for its share of parks, roads, fire stations and libraries, the charge only survives legal challenge if the jurisdiction can show its work. Statute requires a documented chain: an adopted facility standard, a growth projection that converts building permits into service demand units, a cost allocation that ties the fee to the impact the development actually causes, credit for what the developer has already built or already paid in taxes, a published fee schedule with land use categories and conversion factors, a notice and protest period, an effective date that is not immediate, collection at a defined trigger, and — years later — proof that the money was spent on the facilities it was collected for or refunded to the payer.

Today that chain lives in spreadsheets, council packets and a finance system that does not know why any given dollar arrived. Nothing connects a 2026 permit to the 2031 deadline for spending its parks fee, and nothing stops a reviewer from approving a credit against a fee total that was recomputed an hour earlier. This API is the system of record for that chain. It owns the nexus study through its statutory lifecycle, prices every application against whichever study was in effect at the counter that day, and keeps a vintage-dated fund ledger whose oldest dollars are always the ones spent first.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, every technical study is priced under **both** cost-allocation methodologies and both figures are retained, so the record shows the alternative the council did not adopt. Second, nothing is ever silently recomputed behind a decision-maker's back: revising a growth projection visibly decertifies the study that depended on it, and the writers who could otherwise act on stale money are stopped by explicit version checks and by duty-separation rules that compare acting user identities, not merely role names.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section holds across the entire surface. Where a rule below and a workflow narrative in §3 describe the same behaviour, they are reinforcing each other, not competing.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. A request missing either header, or carrying a blank value for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The sole exception is the logical-clock control endpoint described in §2.4, which is reachable without authentication headers.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdministrator`, `NexusAnalyst`, `CouncilClerk`, `PermitOfficer`, `Developer`, and `FinanceOfficer`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of these six, or that is one of these six but is not authorized for the requested operation per the table in §4, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). All six roles may call every read endpoint and the clock control endpoint; write authority is partitioned as follows. A `ProgramAdministrator` owns the program's statutory constants and its facility standards and drives the statutory clock — effective-date activation and the deadline sweep are exclusively theirs. A `NexusAnalyst` builds the technical study: opening a study, adding and revising components, running allocations, adopting schedule lines, certifying the nexus findings, and reviewing credit claims. A `CouncilClerk` runs the legislative process: notice publication, the protest record, closing the protest window, and adoption. A `PermitOfficer` applies the adopted schedule at the counter: assessment, deferral decisions, collection, and certificate issuance. A `Developer` is the fee payer, filing applications, claiming credits and requesting deferrals. A `FinanceOfficer` is custodian of the fee funds: expenditures, statutory refunds and the annual report.

Identity semantics differ for exactly one role. When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is the opaque identifier of the fee payer and it is persisted as `submitted_by_user_id` on the `DevelopmentApplication` the developer files; on the two application sub-resource writes — filing a credit claim and requesting a deferral agreement — the server compares that application's `submitted_by_user_id` to the caller's `X-User-ID` and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_NOT_APPLICATION_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `NexusAnalyst`, `CouncilClerk`, `PermitOfficer` or `FinanceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition says to (as `certified_by_user_id` and `adopted_by_user_id` on a `FeeStudy`, as `decided_by_user_id` on a `CreditClaim` or a `DeferralAgreement`, as `authorized_by_user_id` on a `FundLedgerEntry`, and as `last_report_filed_by_user_id` on a `FeeProgram`) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded operator identifiers are compared against each other by the two duty-separation rules in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three collections deliberately depart from that default and their ordering is part of their contract: the program's facility standards come back ordered by `code` ascending, a component's fee schedule lines come back ordered by `land_use_category` ascending, and the fund ledger comes back ordered by `vintage_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — the same order the expenditure sweep consumes money in.

Every list response includes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether records remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every entity in this service — `FeeProgram`, `FacilityCategory`, `FeeStudy`, `NexusComponent`, `FeeScheduleItem`, `ProtestFiling`, `DevelopmentApplication`, `CreditClaim`, `DeferralAgreement` and `FundLedgerEntry` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the record is first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation, including mutations of purely derived fields.

That last point is load-bearding for the rest of this document: when a workflow says a category's `fund_balance_cents` or an application's `net_due_cents` was recomputed, the owning record's `version` has advanced and its `updated_at` has been refreshed, whether or not the client asked for that record to change.

All timestamps that this service writes — `certified_at`, `notice_published_at`, `protest_window_ends_at`, `adopted_at`, `effective_at`, `activated_at`, `filed_at`, `allocated_at`, `assessed_at`, `reassessed_at`, `co_issued_at`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`, `satisfied_at`, `defaulted_at`, `due_at`, `vintage_at`, `deadline_at`, `last_report_filed_at` and `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` — read from a single **logical clock** rather than from wall time. Statutory windows, protest periods, effective dates, expenditure deadlines and report grace periods all compare against that clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A test-only control capability at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; it echoes back `current_utc` and is the only endpoint exempt from the authentication headers.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Money is carried in whole cents as integers throughout; no field in this service holds a fractional cent, and no monetary value is ever returned as a string. Every rate, share and threshold that a jurisdiction can tune is expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of one percent — so `admin_charge_bp` of 300 means three percent, `credit_cap_bp` of 5000 means fifty percent, `protest_threshold_bp` of 5000 means half of the affected-property weight, and `planned_growth_share_bp` of 10000 means the entire planned cost. Converting a basis-point figure into a multiplier always means dividing it by ten thousand.

Wherever a computed money figure needs rounding, the method is **round half up** and the target is whole cents: a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. Round-half-even is explicitly not the rule here, and the difference is observable — a schedule line whose unrounded fee is 254,167.5 cents stores 254,168, and a sibling line whose unrounded fee is 183,000.6 cents stores 183,001. Two families of non-monetary numbers have their own precision: service demand units and level-of-service ratios are carried to **4 decimal places**, also round half up, which covers `existing_los_per_demand_unit`, `planned_los_per_demand_unit`, `existing_deficiency_demand_units`, `demand_units_per_dwelling`, `demand_units_per_1000_nonres_sqft`, `planned_capacity_demand_units`, `conversion_factor`, `growth_demand_units`, `committed_demand_units`, `nexus_ratio` and each assessed line's `demand_units`.

The point at which rounding happens matters as much as its direction, and three of those points are pinned deliberately. A schedule line's per-unit fee is rounded once, and the rounded figure is the only one carried into an assessment — the unrounded product is never multiplied by an application's billable units. An administrative charge is computed and rounded once per assessed line and the rounded per-line charges are then summed, never computed on an aggregate. And a credit cap is taken against the gross assessed fee, before the administrative charge exists. Each of those choices differs from its plausible alternative by a cent or two on realistic figures, which is exactly why they are stated rather than left to the implementer.

### 2.6. The Statutory Constants of a Fee Program

A `FeeProgram` is the root of everything: a jurisdiction's impact fee program, identified by a legal `name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters and a `jurisdiction_code` of 2 to 10 uppercase letters and digits that must be unique across all programs. Everything else on the program is a statutory constant with a default, so a program can be created from a minimal body and tuned later.

`fiscal_year_start_month` is the calendar month, 1 through 12, on whose first day the program's fiscal year opens; it defaults to 7. `statutory_expenditure_deadline_years`, in the range 1 through 20 and defaulting to 5, is how long a collected fee may sit unexpended before it becomes refundable. `notice_min_days`, 1 through 365 and defaulting to 14, is the shortest notice period the program will accept. `protest_threshold_bp`, 1 through 10000 and defaulting to 5000, is the affected-property weight at or above which a protest is sustained. `effective_delay_days`, 0 through 365 and defaulting to 60, is the statutory gap between adoption and the earliest permissible effective date. `admin_charge_bp`, 0 through 2000 and defaulting to 300, is the administrative charge added to each assessed line. `credit_cap_bp`, 0 through 10000 and defaulting to 5000, caps approved credits as a share of the gross assessed fee. `los_policy` is an enum of `EXISTING_DEFICIENCY_EXCLUDED` (the default) or `PLANNED_STANDARD_ALLOWED` and decides whether the standard-based methodology may price the planned level of service or is held to the existing one. `discount_rate_bp`, 1 through 5000 and defaulting to 400, is the annual discount rate the present-value credit variant uses. `max_deferral_days`, 1 through 3650 and defaulting to 730, caps how far out a deferral's due date may fall from execution. `default_penalty_bp`, 0 through 10000 and defaulting to 1000, is the penalty charged when an executed deferral defaults. `at_risk_window_days`, 1 through 3650 and defaulting to 365, is the look-ahead the deadline sweep uses to flag a fund before its earliest deadline. `report_grace_days`, 1 through 730 and defaulting to 180, is how long after a fiscal year closes its annual report remains on time.

Five program properties are derived rather than supplied. `effective_fee_study_id` is not a stored foreign key at all: it resolves at read time to the `id` of the single study of this program whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, and is null when there is none. `last_report_fiscal_year`, `last_report_filed_at` and `last_report_filed_by_user_id` describe the most recent annual report filing and are null before the first filing. `last_report_status` is an enum of `NOT_FILED`, `FILED_ON_TIME` or `FILED_LATE` and starts at `NOT_FILED`; note that the annual report read's own `report_status` uses the spelling `UNFILED` for the same unfiled condition, because it describes one requested fiscal year rather than the program's filing history.

### 2.7. Reading Nested and Reverse-Reachable Collections

Resource collections in this service are flat peers, not ownership chains: studies, components, schedule lines, applications, claims, agreements and ledger rows are each addressed at the top level by their own `id`, and the sub-paths that do appear are either action verbs or reverse-reachability reads. Where a path does carry an identifier, that identifier is resolved before any body or business rule is examined, and the 404 that comes back names the resource type that failed to resolve: `ERR_FEE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEXUS_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVELOPMENT_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFERRAL_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTEST_FILING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FUND_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Where more than one identifier appears in a path, they are validated left to right and the first one that fails determines the error.

Identifiers arriving in a **request body** are checked separately and later, because they are references rather than addresses. A `fee_program_id`, `fee_study_id` or `facility_category_id` in a body that does not resolve yields `ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE` (HTTP 404), and this applies when creating a facility category, when opening a fee study and when adding a nexus component. Once both references resolve on a component create, they must additionally belong to the same program; a component whose study and facility category come from different programs is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Several reads exist purely to traverse the graph backwards. Filtering fee studies by `superseded_by_fee_study_id` reaches the studies that a given activation retired; filtering development applications by `pinned_fee_study_id` reaches every project a given study priced; filtering ledger rows by `refunded_entry_id` reaches the refund that reversed a given collection, and filtering them by `development_application_id` reaches everything one project paid. The remaining list filters are straightforward equality matches: `fund_posture` on the program's facility standards; `fee_program_id`, `status`, `methodology` on studies; `status` on protest filings; `fee_study_id`, `facility_category_id`, `allocation_status` on components; `schedule_status` on schedule lines; `fee_program_id`, `assessment_status`, `collection_status`, `reassessment_required` on applications; `status` on credit claims and on deferral agreements; and `facility_category_id`, `kind`, `entry_status` on ledger rows. A `fiscal_year` is required, not optional, on the annual report read.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations touch a child record while depending on facts that live on a parent record, and each of them requires the client to prove it has seen the parent's current state. In all three cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one, and its absence is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Opening a fee study at `POST /fee-studies` requires `expected_fee_program_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `FeeProgram`. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A study prices the program's whole facility-standard roster and its statutory constants, and multiple `DRAFT` studies per program are perfectly legitimate, so nothing in the lifecycle would otherwise stop an analyst from drafting against a roster or a constant set that has since been replaced.

Deciding a credit claim at `POST /credit-claims/{id}/decide` requires `expected_development_application_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the claim's `DevelopmentApplication`. A mismatch against the application's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every approval legitimately re-prices the application and moves `credit_applied_cents` against the program's credit cap, so a reviewer working from a `net_due_cents` that has since changed would allow a credit the cap no longer has room for.

Recording an expenditure at `POST /facility-categories/{id}/expenditures` requires `expected_facility_category_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `FacilityCategory`. A mismatch against the category's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CATEGORY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two expenditures are each individually valid while the balance covers them, so the fund's own state cannot distinguish a finance officer who has seen the current balance, posture and vintage list from one who has not.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and none may be given one.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two rules in this service compare acting user identities rather than role names, and role separation alone satisfies neither.

The user identity that certified a study's nexus findings may not adopt that study. At adoption, the caller's `X-User-ID` is compared to the study's `certified_by_user_id`, and an equal value is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Because certification belongs to `NexusAnalyst` and adoption to `CouncilClerk`, this bites only when one human holds both roles — which is precisely the case the statute is worried about.

The user identity that authorized any expenditure inside a fiscal year may not file that fiscal year's annual report. At filing, the caller's `X-User-ID` is compared to the `authorized_by_user_id` of every `EXPENDITURE` ledger row created inside the reported year's window, and a match anywhere in that set is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_PREPARER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Both operations belong to `FinanceOfficer`, so the role matrix cannot enforce this at all: the same finance officer may spend or report for a given year, never both.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence and Error Selection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in the following order, and the first violated check is the only error reported.

1. Authentication — the two headers are present and non-blank (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401), on every endpoint except the clock control endpoint.
2. Authorization — the declared role is one of the six and is listed for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403), on every endpoint.
3. Path identifier resolution, in the order the resource types are listed in §2.7 — programs first, then facility categories, fee studies, nexus components, development applications, credit claims, deferral agreements, protest filings, and finally fund ledger entries.
4. Schema — required fields present, types correct, enum values inside their declared sets, integers inside their declared ranges, decimals within their declared scale, timestamps ISO-8601 UTC, `limit` between 1 and 100, `offset` at least 0, `dwelling_units` and `nonres_sqft` each at least 0 and not both zero, `conversion_factor` greater than 0, and a partial credit approval carrying an `approved_cents` strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than the claim's `claimed_credit_cents` (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422).
5. Body reference resolution (`ERR_INVALID_REFERENCE`, 404), then program-scope agreement between a component's study and category (`ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, 422).
6. Developer ownership of the application (`ERR_NOT_APPLICATION_OWNER`, 403).
7. The three parent-version checks of §2.8, in the order program, application, category.
8. The two duty-separation checks of §2.9 — study adoption first, then annual report filing.
9. State checks, in this order: fee study status (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`), component allocation currency (`ERR_COMPONENT_ALLOCATION_STALE`), a study having any components at all (`ERR_STUDY_HAS_NO_COMPONENTS`), protest filing state (`ERR_PROTEST_INVALID_STATE`), the existence of exactly one effective study (`ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STUDY`), application state (`ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`), the pending-reassessment block (`ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`), credit claim state (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`), deferral agreement state (`ERR_DEFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`), the fund's refund obligation (`ERR_FUND_REFUND_OBLIGATION_PENDING`), ledger row state (`ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_INVALID_STATE`), the certificate-of-occupancy payment block (`ERR_CO_BLOCKED_UNPAID`), and a report already filed for the year (`ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_FILED`).
10. Uniqueness and structural business constraints, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEXUS_COMPONENT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCHEDULE_ITEM`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`, `ERR_UNIT_BASIS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_HORIZON_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
11. Temporal checks, in this order: `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_DEFERRAL_DUE_DATE_INVALID`, `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_NOT_COMPLETE`.
12. Remaining substantive business constraints, in this order: `ERR_CREDIT_CATEGORY_NOT_IN_STUDY`, `ERR_ZERO_DEMAND_UNITS`, `ERR_NEXUS_RATIO_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_FEE_SCHEDULE`.
13. Fiscal checks last, in this order: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECURITY`, `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE`, `ERR_REFUND_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Two of those orderings are counter-intuitive enough to call out. A fund carrying an outstanding statutory refund obligation reports `ERR_FUND_REFUND_OBLIGATION_PENDING` even when the requested expenditure would also have exceeded its expendable balance — the refund obligation is checked well above the fiscal test, so `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE` never surfaces in that situation. Likewise, a notice published while another study of the same program still occupies its notice window reports `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` even when the submitted notice period is also below the statutory minimum; `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` is only reachable once no window conflict exists.

### 2.11. Derived Field Reconciliation and Cascade Discipline

A large fraction of this system's state is derived, and none of it is cached stale. Consistency is immediate: by the time a write returns, every derived field its effects reach has been recomputed and every record whose derived fields changed has had its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

On a `FeeStudy`, `protest_weight_bp` is the sum of `weight_bp` over that study's filings whose `status` is `RECORDED`, and `protest_count` is the number of such filings. On a `NexusComponent`, `growth_demand_units` follows the growth projection and `nexus_ratio`, `plan_based_cost_per_unit_cents`, `standard_based_cost_per_unit_cents` and `adopted_cost_per_unit_cents` follow the cost allocation. On a `FacilityCategory`, `fund_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over its `COLLECTION` ledger rows less the sum over its `EXPENDITURE` rows less the sum over its `REFUND` rows; `refund_due_cents` is the sum of `unexpended_cents` over its rows whose `entry_status` is `REFUND_DUE`; `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` is the earliest `vintage_at` among its `COLLECTION` rows whose `entry_status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED` or `REFUND_DUE`, and is null when no such row exists; `committed_demand_units` is the sum of `demand_units` over the assessed lines naming this category on applications whose `assessment_status` is `ASSESSED` or `REASSESSED`; and `deferred_receivable_cents` is the sum of `line_net_cents` for this category over applications whose `collection_status` is `DEFERRED` or `DELINQUENT`. On a `DevelopmentApplication`, `paid_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over its `COLLECTION` ledger rows less the sum over its `REFUND` rows.

Cascades in this service always run in the same direction — a child's change reaches back up to invalidate the parent that relied on it — and the longest of them is four hops deep. Revising a component that had been successfully allocated (hop 1) invalidates that component's schedule lines (hop 2), which remands the parent study out of its certified or noticed position back to `DRAFT` (hop 3), and the remand — not the revision request itself — voids the study's protest tally when the study had been noticed (hop 4). Activating a study likewise cascades: the study becoming effective (hop 1) supersedes the study it replaces (hop 2), and that supersession flags every unpaid application priced by the retired study for reassessment (hop 3). Both cascades are described in full, with their exact side effects, in §3.5 and §3.4 respectively.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program and Adopting Facility Standards

A `ProgramAdministrator` begins by creating the fee program itself, supplying at minimum a `name` and a `jurisdiction_code`; every statutory constant enumerated in §2.6 may be supplied in the same body or left to its default. The program comes back at `version` 1 with no effective study and with its report history empty. A second program attempting to reuse an existing `jurisdiction_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`.

The same administrator then adopts the facility standards. Each `FacilityCategory` is created against a `fee_program_id` and carries a `code` of 2 to 12 uppercase letters and underscores that must be unique inside its program — a collision returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE` — plus a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `demand_unit_label` of 1 to 40 characters naming the service demand unit the category is measured in, such as a resident or a PM peak-hour trip. The standard itself is two numbers: `existing_los_per_demand_unit`, the adopted existing level of service in facility units per demand unit, and `planned_los_per_demand_unit`, the adopted planned level of service, both strictly greater than zero and carried to four decimals. `unit_cost_cents` is the cost of one facility unit — one acre of park, one lane mile of arterial — and must be strictly positive. `existing_deficiency_demand_units` records demand units already served below the existing standard; it defaults to 0, may not be negative, and exists so the record shows plainly that those units are never charged to new development.

A newly adopted standard is born with `fund_posture` `COMPLIANT` and every derived money figure at zero: `fund_balance_cents`, `refund_due_cents`, `committed_demand_units` and `deferred_receivable_cents` all start at 0 and `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` starts null. The category's fund does not exist as a separate resource; it is the set of ledger rows pointing at the category, and everything §3.9 says about vintages and postures happens through those rows.

### 3.2. Drafting a Study: The Growth Projection and Two Readings of the Same Facts

A `NexusAnalyst` opens the technical study, supplying the `fee_program_id`, a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, and the `methodology` the study adopts as its fee basis — either `PLAN_BASED` or `STANDARD_BASED`. The create also requires `expected_fee_program_version` under the rule in §2.8. A new study is born in status `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, a protest tally of zero on both counters, and every notice, adoption and activation timestamp null; the create additionally increments the parent program's `version`, because the program's roster of studies has changed.

Inside a `DRAFT` study the analyst adds one `NexusComponent` per facility category being priced, naming the `fee_study_id` and the `facility_category_id`. A study may price a given facility category at most once; a second component for the same category returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEXUS_COMPONENT`. Components may only be added while the study is `DRAFT` — any other status returns `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`.

A component carries the growth projection and the planned capital programme. The horizon is `horizon_start_year` through `horizon_end_year`, each between 1900 and 2200 inclusive, with the end year at or after the start year. The projection itself is `projected_new_dwelling_units` and `projected_new_nonres_sqft`, each a non-negative integer, together with the two intensity factors `demand_units_per_dwelling` and `demand_units_per_1000_nonres_sqft`, each a non-negative four-decimal number. From these, **growth demand units** are computed immediately on create and again on every revision: multiply the projected new dwelling units by the demand units generated per dwelling, multiply the projected new non-residential square feet divided by one thousand by the demand units generated per thousand non-residential square feet, add those two products at full precision, and round the single sum half up to four decimal places. A projection of 1,200 dwellings at 1.0100 demand units each plus 200,000 non-residential square feet at 3.7500 demand units per thousand yields 1,212 plus 750, or 1962.0000 demand units. The projection is deliberately independent of cost: it is recomputed on every revision even when that revision invalidates the component's allocation, because how much demand new development creates does not depend on what the facilities cost.

The planned capital programme is `planned_facility_cost_cents`, the gross capital cost of the planned facilities in this category and at least zero; `planned_other_funding_cents`, the grants and non-fee money netted out before allocation, defaulting to 0 and at least zero; `planned_growth_share_bp`, the basis points of that gross cost attributable to new growth, between 0 and 10000 and defaulting to 10000; and `planned_capacity_demand_units`, the demand units of capacity the programme actually buys, at least zero and carried to four decimals. `existing_tax_credit_per_unit_cents` is a per-demand-unit credit for existing tax contributions; it defaults to 0, may not be negative, and is subtracted from whichever cost basis the study's methodology selects. Until the component is allocated, its `allocation_status` is `PENDING`, `allocated_at` is null, and all four computed cost figures are null.

Running the allocation is a separate, explicit act by the `NexusAnalyst` and it is only permitted while the parent study is `DRAFT`; attempting it against a study in any other status returns `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`. It also requires that there be something to allocate against: if the component's `growth_demand_units` is not strictly greater than zero, or if the smaller of `growth_demand_units` and `planned_capacity_demand_units` is not strictly greater than zero, the request is refused with `ERR_ZERO_DEMAND_UNITS`. A component with no projected growth has no rational nexus to allocate cost across.

The allocation prices the same statutory facts twice, and **both results are always stored** so the record shows the alternative the council did not adopt.

The **incremental-expansion reading**, stored as `plan_based_cost_per_unit_cents`, recovers only the growth share of the planned programme, net of committed outside money, spread over the capacity the programme genuinely delivers. Multiply `planned_facility_cost_cents` by `planned_growth_share_bp` divided by ten thousand and round that product half up to whole cents; subtract `planned_other_funding_cents` from it to get the net growth cost. Divide that net growth cost by the **smaller** of `growth_demand_units` and `planned_capacity_demand_units`, and round the quotient half up to whole cents. A programme costing 500,000,000 cents with an 8000-basis-point growth share and 50,000,000 cents of grants nets to 350,000,000 cents; divided by the smaller of 3000 projected demand units and 3500 units of planned capacity, that is 350,000,000 over 3000, or 116,666.66 recurring, stored as 116,667.

The **level-of-service reading**, stored as `standard_based_cost_per_unit_cents`, says the fee buys the adopted standard at today's unit cost, so the planned programme's price tag never enters the arithmetic at all. Multiply the facility category's `unit_cost_cents` by the applicable level of service and round half up to whole cents. Which standard is applicable is decided by the program's `los_policy`: under `EXISTING_DEFICIENCY_EXCLUDED` it is `existing_los_per_demand_unit`, and under `PLANNED_STANDARD_ALLOWED` it is the greater of `existing_los_per_demand_unit` and `planned_los_per_demand_unit`. A category with a unit cost of 45,000,000 cents, an existing standard of 0.0030 and a planned standard of 0.0040 therefore prices at 135,000 cents under the default policy and at 180,000 cents once the planned standard is allowed; where the planned standard sits *below* the existing one — 0.0006 against 0.0008 — taking the greater of the two keeps the existing standard and the policy flag changes nothing.

`adopted_cost_per_unit_cents` is then the study's chosen reading less the existing-tax credit, floored at zero: take `plan_based_cost_per_unit_cents` when the study's `methodology` is `PLAN_BASED` and `standard_based_cost_per_unit_cents` when it is `STANDARD_BASED`, subtract `existing_tax_credit_per_unit_cents`, and use zero instead if the subtraction would go negative. A plan-based selection of 116,667 with a 15,000-cent existing-tax credit adopts 101,667; a standard-based selection of 135,000 with the same credit adopts 120,000.

Proportionality evidence is stored alongside the price as `nexus_ratio`: `planned_capacity_demand_units` divided by `growth_demand_units`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and null when `growth_demand_units` is zero. 3500 units of capacity against 3000 units of projected growth gives 1.1667; 2000 against 1962 gives 1.0194. Because the ratio measures capacity rather than price, it is identical under both methodologies. Note that the two readings bite in opposite directions on purpose: raising `planned_capacity_demand_units` above `growth_demand_units` cannot change the plan-based figure, because the smaller of the two is the divisor, while flipping `los_policy` cannot change the plan-based figure at all.

A successful allocation stamps `allocated_at` from the logical clock, sets `allocation_status` to `VALID`, and increments the component's `version`. Re-running the allocation on a component that is already `VALID` is permitted and simply recomputes everything, leaving the record unchanged apart from the version bump when no input has changed — the `VALID` state is re-entered, not left. Running it on a `STALE` component is the repair path described in §3.5, and it does more than recompute the component.

### 3.3. Converting Cost Per Demand Unit Into a Published Fee Schedule

The study's cost allocation prices a demand unit; the fee schedule prices a building. A `NexusAnalyst` adopts one `FeeScheduleItem` per land use class on each component, supplying the `land_use_category`, the `unit_basis` and the `conversion_factor`; the line records the component it prices for as `nexus_component_id`. `land_use_category` is one of `SINGLE_FAMILY`, `MULTI_FAMILY`, `RETAIL`, `OFFICE` or `INDUSTRIAL`, and a component may carry at most one line per land use class — a second attempt returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCHEDULE_ITEM`. Lines are adopted only while the parent study is `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`, and a line can only be created while its component's `allocation_status` is `VALID` — which is why a schedule line is always born with `schedule_status` `VALID`, mirroring its parent component's allocation state for that land use.

`unit_basis` is either `PER_DWELLING` or `PER_1000_SQFT`, and it is not a free choice. Residential classes must be charged per dwelling: `SINGLE_FAMILY` and `MULTI_FAMILY` require `PER_DWELLING`. Non-residential classes must be charged per thousand square feet: `RETAIL`, `OFFICE` and `INDUSTRIAL` require `PER_1000_SQFT`. Beyond that, the parent component must actually carry a demand factor for the chosen basis — `demand_units_per_dwelling` strictly greater than zero for a per-dwelling line, `demand_units_per_1000_nonres_sqft` strictly greater than zero for a per-thousand-square-feet line. A mismatch on either count is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_BASIS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

`conversion_factor` must be strictly greater than zero and is carried to four decimals; it is the number of demand units one charging unit of that land use generates. The line's `fee_per_unit_cents` is the component's `adopted_cost_per_unit_cents` multiplied by the `conversion_factor`, rounded half up to whole cents, and rounded exactly once. A parks component adopting 101,667 cents per demand unit with a single-family conversion factor of 2.5000 stores 254,168 cents per dwelling — the unrounded 254,167.5 rounds up — and with a multi-family factor of 1.8000 stores 183,001 cents. A roads component adopting 247,197 cents per demand unit stores 249,669 cents for a single-family factor of 1.0100 and 1,606,781 cents for a retail factor of 6.5000. That rounded per-unit figure is the only number carried forward into an assessment; the unrounded product is discarded and is never multiplied by an application's billable units. `fee_per_unit_cents` is null while the parent component is not `VALID`.

### 3.4. Certification, Notice, Protest, Adoption and Effect

**Certification** is the analyst's formal record that the rational nexus and rough-proportionality tests are met for every category the study prices, and it moves the study `DRAFT → NEXUS_CERTIFIED`. It is refused unless the study is in `DRAFT` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`), unless the study has at least one component (`ERR_STUDY_HAS_NO_COMPONENTS`), unless every component of the study has `allocation_status` `VALID` **and** carries at least one schedule line (`ERR_COMPONENT_ALLOCATION_STALE` in either case), and unless every component's `nexus_ratio` is at least 1.0 (`ERR_NEXUS_RATIO_INSUFFICIENT`, HTTP 409) — a component whose planned capacity falls short of its projected growth demand would be charging new development for capacity the programme never buys. Certification writes `certified_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` from the logical clock, and increments the study's `version`.

**Notice publication** by a `CouncilClerk` moves the study `NEXUS_CERTIFIED → NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, and requires the study to be in `NEXUS_CERTIFIED` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`). The clerk supplies `notice_method` — one of `NEWSPAPER`, `WEBSITE` or `MAILED` — and `notice_days`, an integer of at least 1 that is both how long the notice stays published and the length of the protest window. Two guards apply, and their relative order matters. First, no other study of the same program may be inside its own statutory notice window; that window is the **half-open** timestamp interval running from a study's `notice_published_at` up to but not including its `protest_window_ends_at`, occupied by any study whose status is `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, `PROTEST_CLEARED` or `ADOPTED`. Publishing exactly at another study's `protest_window_ends_at` is therefore accepted — for timestamps, adjacency is not overlap — while publishing anywhere strictly inside returns `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Second, and only once no window conflict exists, `notice_days` must be at least the program's `notice_min_days`; a shorter period returns `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Publication stores the notice method and days, stamps `notice_published_at` from the logical clock, sets `protest_window_ends_at` to that instant plus `notice_days` days, and increments the study's `version`.

While a study is `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, the clerk records each record-owner objection as a `ProtestFiling` against the study, supplying a `protester_name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `parcel_count` of at least 1, and a `weight_bp` between 1 and 10000 representing the affected-property weight the filing carries. A filing recorded against a study in any other status returns `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`. Each filing is born with `status` `RECORDED` and `filed_at` from the logical clock, adds its `weight_bp` to the study's `protest_weight_bp`, adds one to `protest_count`, and increments the study's `version`. A recorded filing may be withdrawn, which moves it `RECORDED → WITHDRAWN`, subtracts its `weight_bp` from the study's tally, subtracts one from `protest_count`, and again increments the study's `version`; withdrawal requires the filing to be `RECORDED` and its study to still be `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, and otherwise returns `ERR_PROTEST_INVALID_STATE`.

Closing the protest window is a single clerk action with two divergent outcomes, and both require the same two preconditions: the study is `NOTICE_PUBLISHED` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`) and the logical clock has reached `protest_window_ends_at`, inclusively — closing exactly at that instant is allowed, and closing before it returns `ERR_PROTEST_WINDOW_OPEN`. The branch is then decided by the tally against the program's `protest_threshold_bp`:

- When `protest_weight_bp` is **strictly below** `protest_threshold_bp`, the protest fails and the study moves `NOTICE_PUBLISHED → PROTEST_CLEARED`. The study's `version` increments. Every `RECORDED` filing is left exactly as it is, so the tally that the council relied on stays on the record.
- When `protest_weight_bp` is **at or above** `protest_threshold_bp`, the protest is sustained and the study is remanded `NOTICE_PUBLISHED → DRAFT`. `revision_round` increments by one. `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are both cleared to null. All four notice fields — `notice_method`, `notice_days`, `notice_published_at` and `protest_window_ends_at` — are cleared to null. Every filing of that study whose `status` was `RECORDED` moves to `VOIDED`, and `protest_weight_bp` and `protest_count` are both reset to 0, because the next notice starts a fresh protest period and the old filings may not be carried forward. The study's `version` increments.

**Adoption** by a `CouncilClerk` moves the study `PROTEST_CLEARED → ADOPTED` and requires the study to be in `PROTEST_CLEARED` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`) and the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `certified_by_user_id` per §2.9 (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). It stamps `adopted_at` from the logical clock, records `adopted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, fixes `effective_at` at `adopted_at` plus the program's `effective_delay_days` days, and increments the study's `version`.

**Activation** is reserved to a `ProgramAdministrator` and moves the study `ADOPTED → EFFECTIVE`. The study must be in `ADOPTED` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`) and the logical clock must have reached `effective_at`, inclusively — activating exactly at that instant is allowed, and activating earlier returns `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and increments the study's `version`, and then cascades in two further hops. The program's prior study in status `EFFECTIVE`, of which there is at most one, moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_fee_study_id` pointing at the activating study and its own `version` incremented; `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and a superseded study's schedule remains readable so that assessments pinned to it stay auditable. Because that study became superseded, every development application whose `pinned_fee_study_id` names it, whose `assessment_status` is `ASSESSED` or `REASSESSED`, and whose `collection_status` is `NOT_DUE`, `DUE`, `DEFERRED` or `DELINQUENT` has `reassessment_required` set to true and its own `version` incremented — an application already `PAID` is left alone. Finally the program's own `version` increments, because its derived `effective_fee_study_id` now resolves to a different study.

### 3.5. Revision and the Decertification Cascade

A study rarely survives review unchanged, and revising it is where this system is most opinionated. A `NexusAnalyst` may patch any of a component's projection or cost inputs — `horizon_start_year`, `horizon_end_year`, `projected_new_dwelling_units`, `projected_new_nonres_sqft`, `demand_units_per_dwelling`, `demand_units_per_1000_nonres_sqft`, `planned_facility_cost_cents`, `planned_growth_share_bp`, `planned_capacity_demand_units`, `planned_other_funding_cents` and `existing_tax_credit_per_unit_cents` — and at least one of them must be present in the body, or the request is a schema violation. Revision is permitted while the parent study is `DRAFT`, `NEXUS_CERTIFIED` or `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`; a study that has already cleared protest, been adopted, gone effective or been superseded may not have its components revised at all, and the attempt returns `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`.

On a component that has never been allocated — `allocation_status` `PENDING` — the revision is quiet. The patched inputs are stored, `growth_demand_units` is recomputed, the component's `version` increments, and the state re-enters `PENDING`. Nothing cascades, because a never-allocated component has nothing to invalidate.

On a component whose `allocation_status` is `VALID`, the same request is the head of a four-hop cascade, and every hop is mandatory:

1. The patched inputs are stored and `growth_demand_units` is recomputed immediately. The component moves `VALID → STALE`, and `plan_based_cost_per_unit_cents`, `standard_based_cost_per_unit_cents`, `adopted_cost_per_unit_cents` and `nexus_ratio` are all set to null. The component's `version` increments.
2. Because the component went stale, every schedule line belonging to it moves `VALID → STALE` and its `fee_per_unit_cents` is set to null.
3. Because a child schedule line went stale, the parent study is remanded to `DRAFT` if it was sitting in `NEXUS_CERTIFIED` or `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`. In both cases `revision_round` increments by one, `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are cleared to null, and the study's `version` increments. It is the component's flip to `STALE` that causes this, not the operator's request: the remand happens even though the request named the component.
4. When the study being remanded was in `NOTICE_PUBLISHED` rather than `NEXUS_CERTIFIED`, the remand additionally clears `notice_method`, `notice_days`, `notice_published_at` and `protest_window_ends_at` to null, moves every `RECORDED` filing of that study to `VOIDED`, and resets `protest_weight_bp` and `protest_count` to 0. This hop belongs to the remand, not to the revision: the study left `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, therefore its notice and its protest record no longer stand.

One structural constraint is checked on both component creation and component revision. A component's horizon may not overlap the horizon of the component pricing the **same facility category** inside the program's currently `EFFECTIVE` study, because new development may not be charged twice for the same category over the same years. That interval is **closed and inclusive on both ends** and its endpoints are whole years, so a proposed horizon of 2030 through 2035 overlaps an effective horizon of 2026 through 2030 on the year 2030 and is rejected with `ERR_HORIZON_OVERLAP`, while 2031 through 2035 is accepted. Components belonging to studies in `DRAFT`, `NEXUS_CERTIFIED`, `NOTICE_PUBLISHED`, `PROTEST_CLEARED`, `ADOPTED` or `SUPERSEDED` are ignored by this check, so competing drafts covering the same years may legitimately coexist. Contrast this with the notice-window rule of §3.4: that one is a half-open timestamp interval where touching endpoints do not conflict, and this one is a closed year interval where they do.

Repair closes the loop. Running the allocation again on a `STALE` component requires the parent study to be back in `DRAFT` (`ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE`) and `growth_demand_units` to be strictly positive (`ERR_ZERO_DEMAND_UNITS`), and it moves the component `STALE → VALID` with the same recomputation the first allocation performed. It also does one thing the first allocation had no occasion to do: every schedule line of that component returns to `schedule_status` `VALID` with its `fee_per_unit_cents` recomputed from the freshly adopted per-unit cost and its own conversion factor. The component's `version` increments. The full revision loop is therefore `PENDING` or `STALE → VALID → STALE → VALID`, with certification available again once every component is back to `VALID`.

### 3.6. Pricing a Development Application

A `Developer` files a `DevelopmentApplication` against a `fee_program_id`, supplying a `permit_number` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique inside its program — a collision returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER` — and the project's `land_use_category`, again one of `SINGLE_FAMILY`, `MULTI_FAMILY`, `RETAIL`, `OFFICE` or `INDUSTRIAL`. The project's size is `dwelling_units` and `nonres_sqft`, each defaulting to 0, each at least 0, and not both zero. `collection_trigger` declares whether the fee falls due when the permit is assessed or when the certificate of occupancy is requested; it is `PERMIT` or `CERTIFICATE_OF_OCCUPANCY` and defaults to `PERMIT`. On creation the server records `submitted_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, sets `assessment_status` to `UNASSESSED` and `collection_status` to `NOT_DUE`, sets `reassessment_required` to false, leaves `assessed_lines` empty, and zeroes `gross_assessed_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, `admin_charge_cents`, `net_due_cents`, `paid_cents` and `default_penalty_cents`, with `assessed_at`, `reassessed_at` and `co_issued_at` all null.

An application carries **two independent status dimensions**, and this is the single most important structural fact about it. `assessment_status` walks `UNASSESSED → ASSESSED → REASSESSED` and describes whether and how recently the project has been priced. `collection_status` walks `NOT_DUE`, `DUE`, `DEFERRED`, `DELINQUENT` and `PAID` and describes where the money is. Neither dimension constrains the other's vocabulary, and a single operation may move one, both, or neither.

Assessment is a `PermitOfficer` action and it prices the project against whatever schedule is in effect at that moment. Three preconditions guard it. The program must have exactly one study in status `EFFECTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STUDY`. Every component of that effective study must have `allocation_status` `VALID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_ALLOCATION_STALE`. And at least one schedule line inside that study must apply to the application's `land_use_category`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_FEE_SCHEDULE`. On a first assessment the application moves `UNASSESSED → ASSESSED`; on a re-run it moves `ASSESSED → REASSESSED` or re-enters `REASSESSED`, and a re-run additionally requires that `collection_status` is not `PAID` and that `co_issued_at` is still null, either failure returning `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. Assessment records `pinned_fee_study_id` and `pinned_fee_study_version` from the effective study, stamps `assessed_at` on a first assessment and `reassessed_at` on a re-run, sets `reassessment_required` to false, and increments the application's `version`.

The pricing itself runs in seven steps, and the order is not negotiable.

**Step one, the applicable line set.** Take every schedule line whose component belongs to the study named by `pinned_fee_study_id`, whose `land_use_category` equals the application's `land_use_category`, and whose `schedule_status` is `VALID`. Order them by the owning facility category's `code` ascending. A component whose facility category has no line for this land use contributes nothing, and an empty set is the `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_FEE_SCHEDULE` case above.

**Step two, per-line billable units and gross fee.** A line's billable charging units are the application's `dwelling_units` when the line's `unit_basis` is `PER_DWELLING`, and the application's `nonres_sqft` divided by one thousand when it is `PER_1000_SQFT`. The line's `demand_units` are those billable units multiplied by the line's `conversion_factor`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The line's `line_gross_cents` are the line's `fee_per_unit_cents` multiplied by those billable units, rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_assessed_cents` is the sum of `line_gross_cents` across all lines. A ten-dwelling single-family project against a parks line of 254,168 cents and a roads line of 249,669 cents therefore grosses 2,541,680 plus 2,496,690, or 5,038,370 cents — and note that this differs from the rejected alternative of multiplying unrounded per-unit costs by ten and rounding at the end, which would have produced 5,038,365 cents.

**Step three, raw approved credit per category.** For each line, sum the `approved_cents` of every credit claim on this application whose `facility_category_code` matches the line's facility category `code` and whose `status` is `APPROVED_FULL` or `APPROVED_PARTIAL`. Claims that are still `SUBMITTED`, and claims that were `DENIED`, contribute nothing.

**Step four, the aggregate cap on the gross fee.** The credit cap is `gross_assessed_cents` multiplied by the program's `credit_cap_bp` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. `credit_applied_cents` is the **smaller** of the total raw approved credit across all lines and that cap. The cap is taken on the gross fee, before any administrative charge exists — never on the post-administrative total. On a gross of 5,038,370 cents with a `credit_cap_bp` of 5000, the cap is 2,519,185 cents, so 1,000,000 cents of approved parks credit applies in full while 3,000,000 cents of approved parks credit is cut back to 2,519,185.

**Step five, the waterfall back onto the lines.** Walk the lines in facility category `code` ascending order with the capped total in hand. Each line's `line_credit_cents` is the smallest of that line's own raw approved credit, the amount still remaining from the capped total, and the line's own `line_gross_cents` — a line's credit may never exceed its own gross fee. Subtract the amount actually assigned from the remaining total and carry the rest to the next line, so any surplus a clamp leaves behind stays available downstream. The first bucket fills before the second: with a 2,519,185-cent capped total, raw parks credit of 2,500,000 and raw roads credit of 2,400,000, parks takes 2,500,000 and roads takes the remaining 19,185. Because each line's credit is clamped to its own gross fee, in the ordinary case the per-line credits sum exactly to `credit_applied_cents`.

**Step six, the per-line administrative charge.** For each line, subtract `line_credit_cents` from `line_gross_cents` to get that line's net before administration. `line_admin_cents` is that net multiplied by the program's `admin_charge_bp` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents **once, per line**. `line_net_cents` is the line's net before administration plus its `line_admin_cents`. `admin_charge_cents` on the application is the sum of the per-line `line_admin_cents` values. This per-line rounding is observably different from rounding an aggregate: at a 300-basis-point charge, a parks net of 41,680 rounds 1,250.4 to 1,250 and a roads net of 2,477,505 rounds 74,325.15 to 74,325, summing to 75,575, whereas charging the combined 2,519,185 remainder in one operation would have produced 75,576 — one cent apart, which is exactly the discrimination the per-line rule exists to make.

**Step seven, the application totals.** `net_due_cents` is `gross_assessed_cents` less `credit_applied_cents` plus `admin_charge_cents` plus `default_penalty_cents`. `assessed_lines` is the ordered array of per-line objects, in facility category `code` ascending order, each object carrying `facility_category_id`, `facility_category_code`, `demand_units`, `line_gross_cents`, `line_credit_cents`, `line_admin_cents` and `line_net_cents`.

Assessment also updates the facility standards it touched. For each assessed line, that line's `demand_units` are added to the owning category's `committed_demand_units` and that category's `version` is incremented. On a re-run, the **previously** assessed lines' demand units are first subtracted from their categories before the new lines are added, so a re-run with unchanged inputs leaves `committed_demand_units` exactly where it was.

The collection dimension moves only conditionally on assessment. When `collection_trigger` is `PERMIT`, `collection_status` moves `NOT_DUE → DUE` in the same call. When `collection_trigger` is `CERTIFICATE_OF_OCCUPANCY`, `collection_status` stays `NOT_DUE` — the fee is not due until the certificate is sought. A re-assessment never moves the collection dimension at all, in either direction.

### 3.7. Credit Claims: Valuation, Review and the Overpayment They Can Create

A `Developer` claims credit against its own application — and only its own, per §2.1 — for work already delivered or taxes already paid. The claim names a `facility_category_code`, 2 to 12 uppercase letters and underscores, which is resolved to the facility category of the application's program bearing that code; the resolved category must be priced by a component of the application's effective study, and otherwise the claim is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_CATEGORY_NOT_IN_STUDY` (HTTP 422). A claim may not be filed once the application's certificate has issued, which surfaces as `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`; filing after payment is entirely legitimate and is the origin of the overpayment path below.

`credit_type` states the basis of the claim: `DEVELOPER_IMPROVEMENT` for a developer-provided improvement, in which case `improvement_value_cents` is required and must be strictly positive, or `EXISTING_TAX_CONTRIBUTION` for existing tax contributions, in which case `annual_tax_contribution_cents` is required and must be strictly positive. `contribution_years`, an integer from 0 to 50 defaulting to 0, means years of contribution for the tax basis and years of deferral for the improvement basis. `valuation_method` selects between two readings, and `claimed_credit_cents` is computed at filing time from whichever is chosen; an amount field that is absent counts as zero.

Under `NOMINAL`, contributions are added at face value: `claimed_credit_cents` is `improvement_value_cents` plus `annual_tax_contribution_cents` multiplied by `contribution_years`. Ten years of a 120,000-cent annual tax contribution is therefore 1,200,000 cents.

Under `PRESENT_VALUE`, a dollar of future property tax is worth less than a dollar of improvement delivered today. Let the annual rate be the program's `discount_rate_bp` divided by ten thousand. The annuity factor is the sum, over each whole year from one through `contribution_years`, of one divided by one plus that rate raised to the power of that year; the annuity factor itself is not rounded. The present value of the tax stream is `annual_tax_contribution_cents` multiplied by that annuity factor. A developer-built improvement is treated as delivered at the end of the same horizon when `contribution_years` is greater than zero, so its present value is `improvement_value_cents` divided by one plus the rate raised to the power of `contribution_years`; with `contribution_years` at zero it is undiscounted. `claimed_credit_cents` is the sum of those two present values, rounded half up to whole cents once at the end. At a `discount_rate_bp` of 400, ten years of a 120,000-cent annual contribution has an annuity factor of 8.110895779 and a present value of 973,307 cents, against 1,200,000 nominal; a 1,000,000-cent improvement over three years discounts to 888,996 cents. This per-claim credit and the per-demand-unit `existing_tax_credit_per_unit_cents` inside the cost allocation are the program-wide and project-specific halves of the same statutory idea, and they are deliberately not the same computation.

A claim is born `SUBMITTED` with `approved_cents` at 0, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` null, and no effect whatsoever on the application's money fields. A `NexusAnalyst` then decides it, supplying a `decision` and — mandatorily, per §2.8 — `expected_development_application_version`. Every decision requires the claim to still be `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`), the application's `assessment_status` to be `ASSESSED` or `REASSESSED`, and the application's `co_issued_at` to still be null (either of the latter two returning `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`). Each decision stamps `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock and increments the claim's `version`. The three branches then diverge sharply, and the divergence is the point:

- **`APPROVE_FULL`** moves the claim `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_FULL` and sets `approved_cents` to the claim's `claimed_credit_cents`.
- **`APPROVE_PARTIAL`** moves the claim `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_PARTIAL` and sets `approved_cents` to the value supplied in the body. That value must be present and strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than `claimed_credit_cents`; a value at or outside either bound is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
- **`DENY`** moves the claim `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and sets `approved_cents` to 0, where it stays forever. Denial is the only decision that leaves the application row completely untouched: no re-assessment fires, no money field changes, and the application's `version` does not move.

Both approval branches carry identical consequences beyond the claim row. The system re-assesses the application on its own initiative — `assessment_status` becomes `REASSESSED` and `assessed_lines`, `credit_applied_cents`, `admin_charge_cents` and `net_due_cents` are all recomputed under the credit cap by the seven-step procedure of §3.6 — and the application's `version` increments. Because an approval can only reduce `net_due_cents`, an application that had already reached `collection_status` `PAID` may now have `paid_cents` greater than `net_due_cents`; that surplus is a refundable overpayment, discharged through the ledger as described in §3.9. All three status values `APPROVED_FULL`, `APPROVED_PARTIAL` and `DENIED` are terminal for the claim.

### 3.8. Collection: Permit, Certificate of Occupancy, Deferral and Default

Collection is a single fee payer action recorded by a `PermitOfficer`, and it must settle the balance exactly. The payment body carries `amount_cents` and an optional `payment_reference`. `amount_cents` must equal `net_due_cents` minus `paid_cents` to the cent; anything else is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Four state preconditions guard the payment: `assessment_status` must be `ASSESSED` or `REASSESSED`; `reassessment_required` must be false, or the payment is refused with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` because the pinned schedule has been retired under the project's feet; `collection_status` must not already be `PAID`; and `collection_status` must not be `NOT_DUE` unless `collection_trigger` is `CERTIFICATE_OF_OCCUPANCY`, which is the one trigger under which a fee is paid straight out of `NOT_DUE`. The three state failures other than the reassessment block all surface as `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. A successful payment moves `collection_status` from `NOT_DUE` (certificate-triggered projects only), `DUE`, `DEFERRED` or `DELINQUENT` to `PAID`, recomputes `paid_cents`, and increments the application's `version`.

A payment is also where money enters the funds. One `COLLECTION` ledger row is created per assessed line whose `line_net_cents` is greater than zero, each carrying that line's `facility_category_id`, the application's id as its `development_application_id`, an `amount_cents` equal to the line's `line_net_cents`, a `vintage_at` stamped from the logical clock, a `deadline_at` set to that instant plus the program's `statutory_expenditure_deadline_years` years, an `unexpended_cents` equal to its full `amount_cents`, and an `entry_status` of `OPEN`. Because those rows now exist, each affected facility category has its `fund_balance_cents` increased by its line amount, its `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` recomputed, its `fund_posture` recomputed per §3.9, and its `version` incremented.

**Deferral** exists for projects that cannot pay at permit issuance. A `Developer` requests one on its own application, supplying a `requested_security_type` of `CASH_BOND`, `LETTER_OF_CREDIT` or `LIEN`, a strictly positive `security_amount_cents` naming the face amount of the offered instrument, and a `requested_due_at`. The requested due date must be strictly after the logical clock, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DEFERRAL_DUE_DATE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A deferral may only be requested while the application is assessed, its certificate has not issued, its `collection_trigger` is `PERMIT` and its `collection_status` is `DUE`; other states return `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. The agreement is born `REQUESTED` with `due_at`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `executed_at`, `satisfied_at` and `defaulted_at` all null, and the application's `collection_status` unchanged.

A `PermitOfficer` then decides the agreement with a `decision`, and here too the two branches are asymmetric. Both require the agreement to be `REQUESTED` and the application to be `PERMIT`-triggered with `collection_status` `DUE`, failures returning `ERR_DEFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`, and both stamp `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` and increment the agreement's `version`.

- **`EXECUTE`** additionally requires `security_amount_cents` to be at least the application's `net_due_cents`; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SECURITY` (HTTP 422). On success the agreement moves `REQUESTED → EXECUTED`, `executed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `due_at` is set to the **earlier** of `requested_due_at` and `executed_at` plus the program's `max_deferral_days` days. The application's `collection_status` moves `DUE → DEFERRED`, and for every assessed line that line's `line_net_cents` is added to the owning facility category's `deferred_receivable_cents` with each such category's `version` incremented. The application's `version` increments.
- **`DENY`** moves the agreement `REQUESTED → DENIED` and touches nothing else at all: the application's `collection_status` stays `DUE`, no receivable is booked, and the application's `version` does not move. `DENIED` is terminal.

An executed agreement that comes due unpaid **defaults**, and only the deadline sweep of §3.9 can cause that. When the sweep finds an `EXECUTED` agreement whose `due_at` is at or before the logical clock on an application whose `collection_status` is `DEFERRED`, the agreement moves `EXECUTED → DEFAULTED` with `defaulted_at` stamped and its `version` incremented, and the application moves `collection_status` `DEFERRED → DELINQUENT`. Because the agreement defaulted, the application's `default_penalty_cents` is set to its `net_due_cents` multiplied by the program's `default_penalty_bp` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents, and that penalty is added to `net_due_cents`; the application's `version` increments.

Both `EXECUTED` and `DEFAULTED` converge on `SATISFIED` when the fee is finally paid, and `SATISFIED` is terminal. Paying out of `EXECUTED` requires the payment to bring `paid_cents` up to `net_due_cents`; paying out of `DEFAULTED` requires the payment to equal `net_due_cents` minus `paid_cents` with the penalty already inside that figure — that is the only branch where the paid amount includes a penalty. Either way `satisfied_at` is stamped, the agreement's `version` increments, and every facility category that carried this application's lines has its `deferred_receivable_cents` reduced back by the same line amounts.

The **certificate of occupancy** is the last gate. A `PermitOfficer` issues it only when `assessment_status` is `ASSESSED` or `REASSESSED`, `reassessment_required` is false (otherwise `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`), `collection_status` is `PAID`, no agreement of the application is left in `REQUESTED`, `EXECUTED` or `DEFAULTED`, and `co_issued_at` is still null. An unpaid fee or an open deferral obligation both surface as `ERR_CO_BLOCKED_UNPAID`; an already-issued certificate surfaces as `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. Issuance stamps `co_issued_at` from the logical clock and increments the application's `version`, and from that moment the application can no longer be re-assessed and no further credit claim may be filed or decided against it.

### 3.9. The Fee Fund: Vintages, Expenditure, Statutory Refunds and Annual Reporting

Every dollar in a facility category's fund knows when it arrived and when it must be gone. That is what a `FundLedgerEntry` records, and one ledger table carries all three row shapes, discriminated by `kind`. A `COLLECTION` row is money in from a project: it names both a `facility_category_id` and a `development_application_id`, carries a `vintage_at` set to the collection instant, a `deadline_at` set to `vintage_at` plus the program's `statutory_expenditure_deadline_years` years, an `unexpended_cents` that starts equal to its `amount_cents`, and an `entry_status` that walks the consumption states. An `EXPENDITURE` row is money out to a capital project: its `development_application_id` is null, its `project_name` names the project charged (1 to 120 characters), its `deadline_at` is null and its `unexpended_cents` is 0. A `REFUND` row is money out to a payer: its `refunded_entry_id` points at the `COLLECTION` row it reverses, and it too carries no deadline and no unexpended balance. Both `EXPENDITURE` and `REFUND` rows record `authorized_by_user_id` from the authorizing officer's `X-User-ID` — the field the duty-separation rule of §2.9 reads — and that field is null on `COLLECTION` rows. Every row's `amount_cents` is a strictly positive absolute value; direction is carried by `kind`, never by sign.

`EXPENDITURE` and `REFUND` rows are born `POSTED` and never move again; `POSTED` is terminal, and only `COLLECTION` rows use the consumption states `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`, `FULLY_EXPENDED`, `REFUND_DUE` and `REFUNDED`.

**Spending** is a `FinanceOfficer` action against one facility category, supplying `amount_cents`, a `project_name` and — mandatorily, per §2.8 — `expected_facility_category_version`. Two guards precede the arithmetic, in this order. First, the category's `fund_posture` must not be `REFUND_DUE`: an outstanding statutory refund obligation must be discharged before any further expenditure from that fund, and the attempt is otherwise refused with `ERR_FUND_REFUND_OBLIGATION_PENDING`. Second, the amount must be affordable, which is deliberately narrower than the fund's balance. Eligible money is the `unexpended_cents` of the category's `COLLECTION` rows whose `entry_status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`, ordered by `vintage_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; rows that are `REFUND_DUE`, `REFUNDED` or `FULLY_EXPENDED` are excluded, because money that has already breached its statutory deadline may not be spent out from under the refund obligation it created. If the requested `amount_cents` exceeds the sum of eligible `unexpended_cents`, the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE` (HTTP 422) and **nothing** is written — consumption is all-or-nothing, never partial.

Consumption itself is strict first-in, first-out on vintage. Walk the eligible rows in that order and take from each the smaller of its remaining `unexpended_cents` and the amount still to be funded; reduce that row's `unexpended_cents` by what was taken, and stop as soon as the amount is fully funded. A row taken down to zero moves to `FULLY_EXPENDED` — from `OPEN` if this expenditure consumed its whole remainder, or from `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED` if an earlier expenditure had already bitten into it. A row taken down to a positive remainder moves `OPEN → PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`. Each touched row's `version` increments. Consider a parks fund holding three collections: 2,617,930 cents vintaged 10 May 2026, then 1,000,000 cents and 500,000 cents both vintaged 1 June 2026 with the 500,000 row created second. An expenditure of 3,000,000 cents consumes the May row entirely, takes 382,070 cents from the first June row leaving it at 617,930 and `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`, and leaves the second June row untouched and `OPEN`; `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` becomes 1 June 2026. An expenditure of 4,200,000 cents against that same starting state is rejected outright, because only 4,117,930 cents is eligible. Reversing the creation order of the two June rows changes only their order relative to each other; both are consumed after the May row either way. The expenditure then creates its own `EXPENDITURE` row `POSTED`, reduces the category's `fund_balance_cents` by the full `amount_cents`, recomputes `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` over the rows that remain eligible (null when none do), recomputes `fund_posture`, and increments the category's `version`.

**Fund posture** is the derived compliance verdict that turns a statutory deadline into an operational block rather than a report footnote, and it is recomputed after every ledger mutation — a payment, an expenditure, a refund — and by the deadline sweep. Only the sweep may convert a breach into an obligation: for every `COLLECTION` row of the category whose `entry_status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`, the sweep sets `entry_status` to `REFUND_DUE` when the logical clock has reached that row's `deadline_at`, incrementing the row's `version`. After that, the derived money figures are recomputed from the ledger as described in §2.11, and `fund_posture` is decided in exactly this order: it is `REFUND_DUE` when `refund_due_cents` is greater than zero; otherwise it is `AT_RISK` when `refund_due_cents` is zero and at least one `COLLECTION` row with `entry_status` `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED` has a `deadline_at` at or before the logical clock plus the program's `at_risk_window_days` days; otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. Both comparisons are inclusive at the boundary, and that boundary is pinned: with a five-year deadline and a 365-day at-risk window, a collection vintaged 10 May 2026 has a deadline of 10 May 2031, so a sweep on 9 May 2030 leaves the fund `COMPLIANT`, a sweep on 10 May 2030 makes it `AT_RISK` because the deadline falls exactly one window out, a sweep on 9 May 2031 leaves it `AT_RISK`, and a sweep on 10 May 2031 breaches the row exactly on its deadline, turning it `REFUND_DUE` and blocking further expenditure from that fund.

Every posture change increments the category's `version`, and the posture moves in both directions. `COMPLIANT → AT_RISK` and `COMPLIANT → REFUND_DUE` and `AT_RISK → REFUND_DUE` all follow from a recomputation — the first can follow any ledger mutation or a sweep, the two that reach `REFUND_DUE` require a sweep, since only the sweep creates refund obligations. Recovery runs the other way: after a refund clears the last obligation, the category moves `REFUND_DUE → COMPLIANT` when no eligible `COLLECTION` row remains inside the at-risk window, or `REFUND_DUE → AT_RISK` when one still does. After an expenditure carries the last near-deadline vintage away, the category moves `AT_RISK → COMPLIANT`, provided `refund_due_cents` is zero and no eligible row's `deadline_at` remains inside the window.

**Refunds** are a `FinanceOfficer` action against one ledger row, with an optional `reason`, and the variant is selected by the source row's own state rather than by any request field. The row must be a `COLLECTION` row in a refundable state, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_INVALID_STATE`.

- A row whose `entry_status` is `REFUND_DUE` is always a **statutory deadline refund**: the refunded amount is that row's full remaining `unexpended_cents`. The row moves `REFUND_DUE → REFUNDED` with `unexpended_cents` set to 0.
- A row whose `entry_status` is `OPEN` is only ever an **overpayment refund**, and it requires the row's application to have `paid_cents` strictly greater than `net_due_cents`; without that surplus the request is refused with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). The refunded amount is the smaller of the row's `unexpended_cents` and the application's overpayment, that overpayment being `paid_cents` minus `net_due_cents`. The row's `unexpended_cents` is reduced by the refunded amount; it becomes `REFUNDED` if that reduction reaches zero, and otherwise it stays `OPEN` with the smaller remainder — `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED` is deliberately not used on this path, because nothing was spent.

Either way a `REFUND` row is created `POSTED` for the refunded amount with `refunded_entry_id` pointing at the source row and `authorized_by_user_id` from the caller, the source row's `version` increments, the category's `fund_balance_cents` drops by the refunded amount while `refund_due_cents`, `oldest_unexpended_vintage_at` and `fund_posture` are recomputed and the category's `version` increments, and the paying application's `paid_cents` is recomputed with its `version` incremented. Because a refund can never exceed the source row's remaining unexpended balance, refunding the same row a second time finds nothing eligible and is rejected. Continuing the earlier example: a parks row of 2,617,930 cents fully unexpended and `REFUND_DUE` refunds all of it and returns `refund_due_cents` to zero, while an application that paid 5,189,521 cents and was then re-assessed to 4,159,521 by an approved credit has overpaid by 1,030,000, so a refund against a parks row holding 2,617,930 unexpended returns 1,030,000 and leaves that row `OPEN` with 1,587,930.

**The deadline sweep** is the `ProgramAdministrator`'s statutory clock run over an entire program, and it does three things in one pass: it converts every `COLLECTION` row whose `deadline_at` the clock has reached into `REFUND_DUE`; it defaults every `EXECUTED` deferral agreement whose `due_at` the clock has reached, moving its application to `DELINQUENT` and adding the default penalty to `net_due_cents` as described in §3.8; and it recomputes every category's `refund_due_cents` and `fund_posture`. It reports what it did as `refund_due_entry_ids` and `refund_due_entry_count`, `defaulted_agreement_ids`, `delinquent_application_ids`, `at_risk_category_ids` and `refund_due_category_ids`.

**Annual reporting** closes the statutory loop, and it is the one read in this service that mixes windowed flows with cumulative balances. A fiscal year is a **half-open** window: `window_start` is the first instant of the program's `fiscal_year_start_month` in the requested `fiscal_year`, and `window_end` is exactly one year later and is exclusive. An entry stamped precisely at `window_start` is inside the year; an entry stamped precisely at `window_end` belongs to the next one. With a `fiscal_year_start_month` of 7 and a requested `fiscal_year` of 2025, the window runs from 1 July 2025 up to but not including 1 July 2026, so a collection vintaged 10 May 2026 is in fiscal 2025 while one vintaged exactly 1 July 2026 is not.

Per facility category of the program, the report computes six figures over that category's ledger rows. `collections_cents` sums `amount_cents` over `COLLECTION` rows whose `vintage_at` falls inside the window. `expenditures_cents` sums `amount_cents` over `EXPENDITURE` rows whose `created_at` falls inside the window, and `refunds_cents` does the same over `REFUND` rows — outflows are dated by when they were recorded, not by the vintage they consumed. The remaining three are **cumulative, not windowed**, because the statutory disclosure is the balance at the close of the year including money collected in earlier years: `unexpended_cents` sums `unexpended_cents` over every `COLLECTION` row whose `vintage_at` is before `window_end`, `refund_obligation_cents` sums `unexpended_cents` over those same rows restricted to `entry_status` `REFUND_DUE`, and `earliest_deadline_at` is the earliest `deadline_at` among those same rows restricted to `entry_status` `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_EXPENDED`. Program totals are the sums of the per-category figures, and the per-category rows come back in facility category `code` ascending order, each carrying `facility_category_id`, `code`, `existing_deficiency_demand_units`, `collections_cents`, `expenditures_cents`, `refunds_cents`, `unexpended_cents`, `refund_obligation_cents` and `earliest_deadline_at`. The read exposes `fiscal_year`, `window_start`, `window_end`, the six program-level figures, the `categories` array, `filed_late` and `report_status`, where `report_status` reads `UNFILED` until the year has actually been filed.

Filing that report is a `FinanceOfficer` action naming a `fiscal_year`. The year's window must have closed — the logical clock must have reached `window_end` — or the filing is refused with `ERR_FISCAL_YEAR_NOT_COMPLETE`. No report may already exist for that program and year, or the filing is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_FILED`. And the filer must not have authorized any expenditure inside the reported year, per §2.9, or the filing is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PREPARER_CONFLICT`. `filed_late` is decided by a **strict** comparison: the filing is late only when the logical clock is strictly past `window_end` plus the program's `report_grace_days` days, so filing exactly on the grace deadline is on time. With a July fiscal start, a 2025 fiscal year and 180 grace days, the grace deadline is 28 December 2026: a filing stamped exactly at that instant is on time, and a filing one second later is late. The filing writes `last_report_fiscal_year`, `last_report_filed_at`, `last_report_filed_by_user_id` and `last_report_status` — `FILED_ON_TIME` or `FILED_LATE` — onto the program and increments the program's `version`. It returns the `fiscal_year`, `filed_at`, `filed_late`, `report_status` and the five program-level money figures `collections_cents`, `expenditures_cents`, `refunds_cents`, `unexpended_cents` and `refund_obligation_cents`.

"Any" above means all six roles: `ProgramAdministrator`, `NexusAnalyst`, `CouncilClerk`, `PermitOfficer`, `Developer` and `FinanceOfficer`.
