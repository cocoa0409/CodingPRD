# Contact Center Quality Monitoring & Coaching API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Quality programs in a large contact centre fail for a boringly predictable reason: the scorecard, the sample, the coach and the auditor each keep their own version of the truth. A supervisor coaches against a score the agent has already disputed; an evaluator scores a call whose recording consent was withdrawn an hour earlier; a calibration meeting rewrites the scoring guidance and nobody notices that half the open monitoring period was scored against the old wording. This API exists to make those divergences impossible to commit rather than merely discouraged. It is the system of record for evaluation form design, interaction sampling, evaluator calibration, agent feedback, dispute adjudication, coaching intervention and recognition, and it treats every one of those activities as a state machine with explicit, auditable preconditions.

The product is organised around a single spine. A **quality manager** designs an evaluation form out of weighted behaviours, marks some of them compliance-critical and some of them automatic-fail triggers, and publishes it. Interactions flow in from the telephony and messaging platforms; a compliance auditor rules on recording consent and disclosure capture; a speech-and-text analytics engine attaches screening results that rank contacts for manual review. The manager then opens a monitoring period, pulls interactions into a review pool under a declared per-agent volume and source mix, and reserves review windows for named evaluators. Evaluators score, deliver, and — when an agent contests the outcome — hand the dispute to a *different* evaluator, because the author of a score may never adjudicate its challenge. Coaching plans are sized from the agent's rolling quality index, escalate when they run past their due date, and are voided outright when the finding that justified them is overturned.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. The first is **pinning**: an evaluation captures the form identity and the scoring-guidance revision it was opened against, and it may only be submitted while that pinned revision is still current, so a calibration session that moves the standard blocks stale in-flight work instead of silently rescoring it. The second is **conserved accounting**: sampling quotas, evaluator load, compliance findings and coaching state are all derived quantities that must return to their exact prior values when the event that moved them is reversed — an excluded selection returns its slot, an overturned compliance failure clears its hold, and a rejected dispute restores the agent's remaining dispute window untouched. Every collection in this API is mounted at the root of the service; parent references travel in request bodies rather than in nested path segments.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply to every endpoint in the service: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and timestamp conventions, precision, concurrency control, and the fixed order in which the system rejects a bad request.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including reads and including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint (see the Authorized Role(s) column of §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `QualityManager`, `QualityEvaluator`, `Agent`, `Supervisor` and `ComplianceAuditor`.

- The `QualityManager` owns the quality programme itself: form and behaviour authoring, form activation and retirement, agent and evaluator registration, sampling plans and selections, calibration sessions, recognition awards and coaching escalation.
- The `QualityEvaluator` scores sampled interactions, delivers feedback and adjudicates *other* evaluators' disputes.
- The `Agent` is the coached contact-centre agent: they acknowledge delivered feedback, file disputes and supply additional rationale when a reviewer asks for it.
- The `Supervisor` is the agent's line manager and owns coaching plan assignment, session logging and completion. Supervisors may also deliver feedback.
- The `ComplianceAuditor` records recording-consent outcomes and disclosure capture, may record interactions and attach analytics scans, and holds read access across the whole quality record for audit purposes.

Two of these roles are **entity-backed**, which means `X-User-ID` is compared against stored data. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityEvaluator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Evaluator`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Evaluator` compare the resource's evaluator reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: an evaluator may only open or submit an evaluation on a selection whose `assigned_evaluator_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, may only resolve a dispute whose `reviewer_evaluator_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, and their calibration submissions are recorded with `evaluator_id` taken from `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Agent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Agent`; an agent may only acknowledge an evaluation, file a dispute, or respond to a dispute where the resource's `agent_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these ownership checks is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it in audit context but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true when `X-User-Role` is `Supervisor` — note in particular that a coaching plan's `assigned_supervisor_id` is a free-form opaque operator identifier supplied in the request body and is never resolved against a stored row, so a coaching plan is never rejected because its supervisor identifier is unknown. Likewise, when `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque auditor identifier that is not matched against any entity `id`.

Segregation of duties sits on top of role authorisation. A `QualityEvaluator` who authored an evaluation may never review or resolve the dispute filed against that evaluation; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Similarly, recording-consent outcomes are the exclusive province of the `ComplianceAuditor` and may never be set by the `QualityManager` who owns sampling.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to `10`, may not be smaller than `1`, and may not be larger than `100`. `offset` controls the starting position in the result set: it defaults to `0` and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds, or a non-integer value, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints override that default because their ordering is part of their purpose: the behaviour listing of a form is ordered by behaviour `code` ascending, and the analytics-scan listing is ordered by `risk_score` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, so that the highest-risk unscreened contacts sit at the top of the manual-review work queue.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters *before* the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. All list filters described in §3 combine conjunctively: supplying two filters narrows the result to rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored row is addressable by a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix. Alongside it, twelve of the thirteen resource types carry the standard system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): the UUID primary key, generated by the server; a client-supplied value is ignored.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent mutation of the row. Refreshing a computed field — a recomputed quota map, a recomputed quality index, a recomputed assignment count — is a mutation and refreshes `updated_at`.
- `version` (integer): an integer that starts at `1` on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request.

The one exception is the calibration submission. A calibration submission is immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. There is no update endpoint for it, and the fields the session close writes onto it are the only post-creation change the system makes.

All deadlines, windows and system-written timestamps in this domain read a **logical clock** rather than the host wall clock. The clock starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to wall-clock time, so an unset clock is a deterministic earliest instant. A test-driving capability, available to all five roles, moves the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant — forwards or backwards — and answers with the resulting `current_utc` value. Moving the clock mutates no entity: no version is bumped, no deadline is recomputed retroactively, and no cascade fires.

### 2.5. Flat Topology and Reference Resolution Order

Every resource collection in this service is a peer at the root of the URL space; there are no nested collection paths. A child row therefore names its parent through a foreign-key field in the request body — `form_id`, `interaction_id`, `plan_id`, `selection_id`, `session_id`, `evaluation_id`, `agent_id`, `source_evaluation_id` — rather than through a path segment. The only path suffixes beyond a resource `id` are action verbs and two derived reads: the behaviour listing of a form and the quality summary of an agent.

Identifier resolution is strictly ordered, and the first unresolvable identifier decides the answer. Identifiers appearing in the request **path** are resolved first, left to right; only then are foreign keys in the request **body** resolved, in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. Creating a sampling plan therefore resolves `form_id`; creating a selection resolves `plan_id` and then `interaction_id`; creating a coaching plan resolves `agent_id` and then `source_evaluation_id`; creating an analytics scan resolves `interaction_id`; creating an evaluation resolves `selection_id`; assigning a selection resolves the path selection and then the body's `assigned_evaluator_id`; creating a calibration session resolves `form_id` and then `interaction_id`; creating a calibration submission resolves `session_id`; filing a dispute resolves `evaluation_id`. Each miss returns HTTP 404 with the error identifying the *type* that could not be found: `ERR_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COACHING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Scoring Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every score, percentage, deviation and index in this domain is a decimal number carried to exactly **two decimal places**, and every rounding step uses **round half up**. Rounding is not applied once at the end; the specification pins *where* each rounding happens, because the pinned order changes the answer.

Two rules matter everywhere. First, the weighted score of an evaluation rounds **per behaviour before summation** and then rounds **again after renormalisation** — never a single rounding on an unrounded sum. Second, band comparisons are performed on the already-rounded value, so a deviation of exactly `7.00` lands in the `MINOR_VARIANCE` band rather than `MAJOR_VARIANCE`, and a deviation of exactly `3.00` lands in `ALIGNED`.

Counts — participant counts, session counts, selection counts, assignment counts, quota caps and quota remainders — are integers throughout, and quota caps are computed with floor division on integer percentages. Where a decimal input is accepted, at most two fractional digits are permitted; a third fractional digit is a malformed request.

### 2.7. Guarding Against Stale Reads: Optimistic Concurrency

Three create operations in this API legitimately supersede something the client believes it has already seen, and each is therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the *parent* row's `version`. In each case the version field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Endpoints not listed here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them is not part of their contract.

- **Creating an analytics scan** (`POST /analytics-scans`) requires `expected_interaction_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Interaction`. A new scan supersedes that interaction's prior `CURRENT` scan, and the interaction's consent posture may have moved since the client read it, so if `expected_interaction_version` does not equal the interaction's current `version` the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTERACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful scan itself increments the interaction's `version`, so two consecutive screenings must each re-read.
- **Creating a coaching plan** (`POST /coaching-plans`) requires `expected_agent_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Agent`. A new plan voids the agent's prior live plan and sizes itself from the agent's `quality_index_rolling`, so a stale agent view would both void the wrong plan and size the new one from an outdated index; a mismatch with the agent's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Filing a dispute** (`POST /disputes`) requires `expected_evaluation_version` carrying the agent's last observed `version` of the parent `Evaluation`. The delivered score being contested may already have been re-scored and re-delivered after an earlier upheld dispute, so a mismatch with the evaluation's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Consent as a Precondition for Monitoring

Recording consent is the gate on the entire monitoring pipeline, not a decoration on the interaction record. An interaction may only enter the review pool, be assigned, be scored, or serve as a calibration reference while its `recording_consent_status` is `VERIFIED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`. A `PENDING` or `DENIED` interaction is rejected from all five of those operations — pulling it into a plan, assigning it to an evaluator, opening an evaluation on it, submitting that evaluation, and creating a calibration session on it — with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 422). Separately, a `DENIED` interaction is frozen against further consent review and against new analytics screening: both are rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_DENIED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.9. Evaluator Standing and Segregation of Duties

An evaluator's `calibration_status` is a licence to work. A `SUSPENDED` evaluator may not receive an assignment, may not submit a score, and may not pick up a dispute; all three are rejected with `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CALIBRATED` (HTTP 409). `PROBATION` is a warning band and does not block work. Standing is never set by hand: it is moved only by the variance arithmetic of a closing calibration session, as described in §3.7.

Duty separation is enforced at two distinct points on the dispute path, and they answer with different errors. When an evaluator picks up or resolves a dispute whose underlying evaluation they authored, the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. When an evaluator resolves a dispute that some *other* evaluator picked up — that is, when the acting `X-User-ID` is not the dispute's recorded `reviewer_evaluator_id` — the answer is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Several operations in this domain mutate rows the caller never named. Those cascades follow four house rules.

**Chaining is causal.** Where a cascade is described as a sequence of hops, each hop fires *only because* the previous hop changed something. If the first hop's condition is not met, no later hop fires, and no error is raised for the missing work.

**Cascades are conditional over non-terminal children only.** A sweep that excludes selections skips any selection that has already reached a terminal status, and a release that returns an assigned selection to the pool skips any selection that already carries an evaluation row, so in-flight scoring is never orphaned.

**Cascades bump versions.** Every row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, exactly as if the client had written it directly. This is what makes the optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.7 meaningful: a compliance cascade that touches an agent invalidates any `expected_agent_version` a client captured beforehand.

**Cascade results are discoverable.** Rows moved by a cascade are not announced in the triggering response body; they are read back through the corresponding list endpoints, which is why the selection listing, the coaching-plan listing and the behaviour listing are part of the contract.

### 2.11. The Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed by the following precedence ladder. Lower-numbered stages always win. This ordering is part of the contract: two rules that would both reject a request must be evaluated in this sequence, never in the order that happens to be convenient.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request shape** — on every write endpoint: the body parses, every required input field is present and non-null, every field carries its declared JSON type, every enum-typed field carries a declared member, integers are integers, decimals carry at most two fractional digits, datetimes are RFC 3339 UTC with a trailing `Z`, and every declared numeric range holds (`weight_pct` from `0.01` to `100.00`, `silence_pct` from `0.00` to `100.00`, `sentiment_delta` from `-1.00` to `1.00`, `csat_score` from `1` to `5`, `max_points` one of `3`, `4` or `5`, `per_agent_target_volume` from `1` to `50`, `target_score` above `0.00` and at most `100.00`, recognition `points` from `1` to `500`). Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, else the matching not-found error (HTTP 404).
5. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, in declaration order, else the matching not-found error (HTTP 404). This stage covers `POST /analytics-scans`, `POST /sample-plans`, `POST /sample-selections`, `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`, `POST /evaluations`, `POST /calibration-sessions`, `POST /calibration-submissions`, `POST /disputes` and `POST /coaching-plans`.
6. **Ownership** — for an entity-backed role, the acting `X-User-ID` owns the target: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This stage guards `POST /evaluations`, `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`, `POST /evaluations/{id}/acknowledge`, `POST /disputes` and `POST /disputes/{id}/respond`.
7. **Segregation of duties** — on `POST /disputes/{id}/review` and `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`, the acting evaluator did not author the disputed evaluation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied `expected_interaction_version`, `expected_agent_version` or `expected_evaluation_version` equals the parent's current `version`, else the matching stale-version error (HTTP 412), on `POST /analytics-scans`, `POST /coaching-plans` and `POST /disputes` respectively.
9. **State preconditions**, evaluated in this order, each on the endpoints named:
   1. `ERR_FORM_NOT_DRAFT` — the form is `DRAFT` on `POST /evaluation-forms/{id}/activate`.
   2. `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE` — the referenced form is `ACTIVE` on `POST /sample-plans`, `POST /sample-plans/{id}/open` and `POST /calibration-sessions`.
   3. `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT` — the plan is `DRAFT` on `POST /sample-plans/{id}/open`.
   4. `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN` — the referenced plan is `OPEN` on `POST /sample-selections`, `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`, `POST /sample-selections/{id}/exclude` and `POST /sample-plans/{id}/close`.
   5. `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_ASSIGNABLE` — the selection is `SELECTED` on `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`, and `ASSIGNED` on `POST /evaluations`.
   6. `ERR_SELECTION_TERMINAL` — the selection is not yet `EVALUATED` or `EXCLUDED` on `POST /sample-selections/{id}/exclude`.
   7. `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_EDITABLE` — the evaluation's `review_status` is `DRAFT` or `REOPENED` on `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`.
   8. `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_DELIVERABLE` — the evaluation's `review_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `FINALIZED` on `POST /evaluations/{id}/deliver`.
   9. `ERR_FEEDBACK_ALREADY_DELIVERED` — the evaluation's `feedback_status` is still `PENDING` on `POST /evaluations/{id}/deliver`.
   10. `ERR_FEEDBACK_NOT_DELIVERED` — the evaluation's `feedback_status` is `DELIVERED` on `POST /evaluations/{id}/acknowledge`, and is one of `DELIVERED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED` on `POST /disputes`. A `DISPUTED` row is deliberately allowed through this stage so that the more specific duplicate-dispute rule at stage 11.11 owns the answer.
   11. `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_DENIED` — the interaction's `recording_consent_status` is not already `DENIED` on `POST /interactions/{id}/consent-review` and `POST /analytics-scans`.
   12. `ERR_SESSION_NOT_OPEN` — the session is `OPEN` on `POST /calibration-sessions/{id}/start-scoring`.
   13. `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCORING` — the referenced session is `SCORING` on `POST /calibration-submissions` and `POST /calibration-sessions/{id}/close`.
   14. `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_REVIEWABLE` — the dispute is `SUBMITTED` on `POST /disputes/{id}/review`.
   15. `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE` — the dispute is `UNDER_REVIEW` on `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`.
   16. `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESPONDABLE` — the dispute is `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` on `POST /disputes/{id}/respond`.
   17. `ERR_COACHING_PLAN_TERMINAL` — the plan is not `COMPLETED`, `ESCALATED` or `VOIDED` on `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/log-session`, `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/complete` and `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/escalate`.
10. **Safety and compliance gates**, in this order:
    1. `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_VERIFIED` — the consent gate of §2.8, on `POST /sample-selections`, `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`, `POST /evaluations`, `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit` and `POST /calibration-sessions`.
    2. `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CALIBRATED` — the evaluator receiving work is not `SUSPENDED`, on `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`, `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit` and `POST /disputes/{id}/review`.
    3. `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_OPEN` — the agent's `open_compliance_findings` is `0`, on `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/complete` and `POST /agents/{id}/recognition`.
11. **Referential and business constraints**, in this order:
    1. `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH` — every key of the submitted score map is a behaviour code of the referenced form, on `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`, `POST /calibration-submissions`, `POST /calibration-sessions` and `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`. This stage deliberately precedes the completeness check below, so an unknown code answers `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_INCOMPLETE`.
    2. `ERR_GUIDANCE_VERSION_STALE` — the plan's pinned guidance revision still equals the form's current one, on `POST /evaluations` and `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`.
    3. `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_100` — on `POST /evaluation-forms`.
    4. `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_BEHAVIOR` — on `POST /evaluation-forms`.
    5. `ERR_DUPLICATE_BEHAVIOR_CODE` — on `POST /evaluation-forms`.
    6. `ERR_SOURCE_MIX_INVALID` — on `POST /sample-plans`.
    7. `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_INCOMPLETE` — on `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`, `POST /calibration-submissions` and `POST /calibration-sessions`.
    8. `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE` — on `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`, `POST /calibration-submissions`, `POST /calibration-sessions` and `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`.
    9. `ERR_NA_NOT_ALLOWED` — on `POST /evaluations/{id}/submit`, `POST /calibration-submissions`, `POST /calibration-sessions` and `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`.

    11. `ERR_SESSION_PARTICIPANT_DUPLICATE` — on `POST /calibration-submissions`.
    12. `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` — on `POST /disputes`.
    13. `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` — on `POST /disputes/{id}/resolve`.
    14. `ERR_SESSIONS_INCOMPLETE` — on `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/complete`.
    15. `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATION_HISTORY` — on `POST /agents/{id}/recognition` and `POST /coaching-plans`.
12. **Temporal constraints**, in this order: `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE` (on `POST /interactions`, `POST /sample-plans`, `POST /calibration-sessions` and `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`), then `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (on `POST /sample-plans` and `POST /sample-plans/{id}/open`), then `ERR_EVALUATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (on `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`), then `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (on `POST /disputes`), then `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE` (on `POST /coaching-plans/{id}/escalate`).
13. **Capacity, quota and award budget**, in this order: `ERR_AGENT_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` then `ERR_SOURCE_MIX_EXCEEDED` (both on `POST /sample-selections`), then `ERR_EVALUATOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (on `POST /sample-selections/{id}/assign`), then `ERR_RECOGNITION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (on `POST /agents/{id}/recognition`).

Only after every applicable stage passes does the operation apply its side effects.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing the Scorecard: Forms, Weighted Behaviours and Guidance

Everything downstream depends on the scorecard, so a `QualityManager` builds one as a single atomic authoring act rather than assembling it piece by piece. The form itself carries a human `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters and an author-assigned `version_label` of one to twenty characters; the label must be unique among forms sharing the same `name`, so the same scorecard can be revised repeatedly without ambiguity. The manager also declares `auto_fail_policy`, which is either `ZERO_TOTAL` or `COMPLIANCE_SECTION_ZERO` and decides how an automatic-fail violation reshapes the weighted score, and may optionally supply `min_compliance_item_score` — the minimum number of points a compliance-critical behaviour must earn for the evaluation to be judged compliant. When `min_compliance_item_score` is omitted it defaults to `3`, and it may never be less than `1`.

The behaviours themselves arrive in the same payload as a `behaviors` array, and each element becomes a stored behaviour row whose `form_id` is the newly minted form. An element carries a stable `code`, a display `label`, a `weight_pct`, and a `guidance_text` giving the evaluator the wording of the standard. A `code` is matched against the pattern of two to twenty-four characters drawn only from upper-case letters and the underscore, and it is the key by which submitted scores are later addressed; a code that does not match that shape is a malformed request. `weight_pct` must be greater than `0.00`, at most `100.00`, and carry exactly two fractional digits. Four optional flags round out an element: `compliance_critical` (default `false`) marks the behaviour as one whose under-scoring forces a compliance failure; `auto_fail_on_violation` (default `false`) marks the behaviour as one whose zero score triggers the form's automatic-fail policy; `max_points` (default `5`, and restricted to `3`, `4` or `5`) sets the scale ceiling for the behaviour; and `allows_not_applicable` (default `false`) permits an evaluator to mark the behaviour as not applicable. Each stored behaviour is born with a per-behaviour `guidance_version` of `1`.

Three constraints are checked on the submitted behaviour set. The `weight_pct` values must sum to exactly `100.00`, or the form is rejected with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_100`. At least one behaviour must have `compliance_critical` set to `true`, or the form is rejected with `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_BEHAVIOR` — a scorecard with no compliance-critical item cannot express a compliance verdict. And behaviour codes must be unique within the form, compared case-sensitively, or the form is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BEHAVIOR_CODE`.

A freshly created form is born in `DRAFT` with `behavior_weight_total` set to the sum of its behaviours' `weight_pct` values carried to two decimals, `compliance_behavior_count` set to the number of behaviours flagged compliance-critical, a form-level `guidance_version` of `1`, and both `activated_at` and `retired_at` null. A form with no behaviours would therefore report a `behavior_weight_total` of `0.00` and a `compliance_behavior_count` of `0`, which is precisely why activation re-checks both.

Publishing the form moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. Four preconditions guard that transition and are checked in this order: the form must currently be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_FORM_NOT_DRAFT`; `behavior_weight_total` must equal exactly `100.00`, else `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_100`; `compliance_behavior_count` must be at least `1`, else `ERR_NO_COMPLIANCE_BEHAVIOR`; and `min_compliance_item_score` must be no greater than the smallest `max_points` found among the compliance-critical behaviours, else the request is malformed and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a form that demands four points from a behaviour scored out of three could never be passed. Activation stamps `activated_at` with the current logical-clock instant, sets the status, and increments the form's `version`. From the moment a form leaves `DRAFT` its behaviour set is frozen: no behaviour may be added, removed or re-weighted, and only a calibration session may rewrite a behaviour's `guidance_text`.

Retirement moves an `ACTIVE` form to `RETIRED`, stamps `retired_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and increments `version`; attempting it on a form that is not `ACTIVE` answers `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Retirement is deliberately gentle on work already in flight. Sampling plans already `OPEN` on the retired form keep running against the guidance revision they pinned when they opened, and evaluations under them continue to be scorable. What retirement forecloses is new commitment: a `DRAFT` plan on that form stays `DRAFT` but can never be opened, a new plan cannot name it, and a new calibration session cannot name it — all three answer `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Two reads expose the scorecard. A single-form read returns the computed `behavior_weight_total`, `compliance_behavior_count` and the current form-level `guidance_version`, and is available to the `QualityManager`, `QualityEvaluator`, `Supervisor` and `ComplianceAuditor`. Because behaviour rows are created as a cascade of form creation and are never addressable by their own creation call, the behaviour listing of a form is the only discovery channel for them; it is paginated with `limit` and `offset` and ordered by `code` ascending. The form collection listing accepts a `status` filter.

### 3.2. Registering the Population: Agents, Evaluators and Recorded Contacts

Agent registration is the `QualityManager`'s bookkeeping act. An agent carries an `external_ref` — the workforce-system identifier, which must be unique across all agents — a display `full_name`, a `team_code` naming the team they belong to, and a `hire_date` given as an ISO 8601 UTC instant that is used only for tenure reporting and never enters any calculation. A new agent starts with `quality_index_rolling` and `quality_index_period` both null, `open_compliance_findings` at `0`, `consecutive_passing_evaluations` at `0`, `coaching_state` at `NONE`, and `recognition_points` at `0`. Over the agent's life, `coaching_state` moves among `NONE`, `REQUIRED`, `ACTIVE` and `CLEARED` under the events described in §3.5, §3.6 and §3.8 — it is never set directly by any caller. Agents are readable individually by all five roles, and the agent collection listing — restricted to the `QualityManager` and the `Supervisor` — accepts `team_code` and `coaching_state` filters.

Evaluator registration is similarly light. An evaluator carries a `full_name` and an optional `max_open_assignments` capping how many selections they may hold concurrently in the assigned state; it defaults to `3` and must be at least `1` and at most `20`. A new evaluator begins with `calibration_status` at `CALIBRATED`, `open_assignment_count` at `0`, and `last_deviation_pct` null. `open_assignment_count` is always the number of selections whose `assigned_evaluator_id` names this evaluator and whose status is `ASSIGNED` — selections that are merely `SELECTED`, or that have reached `EVALUATED` or `EXCLUDED`, cost the evaluator nothing. `last_deviation_pct` is a copy, carried to two decimals, of the `deviation_pct` of this evaluator's most recent calibration submission ordered by `created_at` descending, and stays null until their first submission is scored. Both values are recomputed whenever a selection is assigned, released, evaluated or excluded. The evaluator collection listing accepts a `calibration_status` filter.

Recorded contacts enter the system as interactions, and either a `QualityManager` or a `ComplianceAuditor` may record one. The payload names the handling agent through `agent_id`, declares the `channel` as `VOICE`, `CHAT` or `EMAIL`, and supplies `started_at` and `ended_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants; `ended_at` must be strictly after `started_at`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`. From that interval the server computes `duration_seconds` as the number of whole seconds between the two instants, discarding any fractional remainder — this value later becomes the contact's weight in the duration-weighted quality index. Four optional inputs colour the record: `required_disclosures` is an array of disclosure codes the channel or jurisdiction demands on this contact, drawn from `RECORDING`, `MINI_MIRANDA`, `RATE_CHANGE` and `THIRD_PARTY`, and defaults to the empty array; `customer_flagged` (default `false`) records that the customer asked for the contact to be reviewed; `csat_score` is the post-contact satisfaction rating, an integer from `1` to `5`, and is null when the customer did not respond; and `repeat_contact_within_72h` (default `false`) records that the same customer came back inside three days. The server writes the remaining fields itself: `recording_consent_status` starts at `PENDING` for every channel including `EMAIL`, `captured_disclosures` starts as the empty array, `compliance_hold` starts `false`, and `sampled_count` starts at `0`. `sampled_count` is always the number of selections referencing this interaction whose status is `SELECTED`, `ASSIGNED` or `EVALUATED`.

Consent adjudication belongs exclusively to the `ComplianceAuditor`, and it is the one place where `recording_consent_status` moves. The payload carries `consent_outcome`, which is `VERIFIED`, `DENIED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`, and an optional `captured_disclosures` array — the disclosure codes the auditor confirmed were actually delivered on the contact, drawn from the same four codes — which defaults to the empty array when omitted. Recording an outcome of `VERIFIED` requires the interaction's current posture to be `PENDING`, `NOT_REQUIRED` or `VERIFIED`, and requires the supplied `captured_disclosures` to contain every code listed in the interaction's `required_disclosures`; a captured set that is missing even one required code is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Recording `NOT_REQUIRED` likewise requires the current posture to be `PENDING`, `NOT_REQUIRED` or `VERIFIED`, and additionally requires the `channel` to be `CHAT` or `EMAIL`: a `VOICE` contact always requires consent, so `NOT_REQUIRED` on a voice contact answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both outcomes overwrite `captured_disclosures` with the payload value and increment the interaction's `version`.

Recording `DENIED` is the destructive outcome, and it may be applied from any posture except `DENIED` itself — a denied interaction is frozen, and a second denial answers `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_DENIED`. Denial sets `recording_consent_status` to `DENIED`, increments the interaction's `version`, and fires a three-hop withdrawal cascade. In the first hop, every selection of this interaction whose status is `SELECTED` or `ASSIGNED` moves to `EXCLUDED` with `exclusion_reason` set to `CONSENT_DENIED`; selections that have already reached `EVALUATED` are left exactly as they are, because the scoring work has already happened. In the second hop, precisely because those selections left the pool, each one releases its evaluator: `assigned_evaluator_id` becomes null, `review_window_start` and `review_window_end` become null, and the affected evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed downward, freeing that window for later assignments. In the third hop, precisely because those selections no longer consume slots, each affected plan recomputes `agent_volume_attainment`, `selections_count` and `excluded_count`, and this interaction's `sampled_count` falls to `0`. Denial additionally supersedes screening: every analytics scan of this interaction whose status is `CURRENT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Every row touched by any of these hops has its `version` incremented.

Interactions are readable individually — exposing the consent posture, both disclosure arrays, `compliance_hold` and `sampled_count` — and the collection listing accepts `agent_id`, `channel`, `customer_flagged`, `recording_consent_status` and `compliance_hold` filters, which together give the reverse path from an agent back to their contacts.

### 3.3. Analytics Screening and the Manual-Review Queue

Speech and text analytics do not decide who gets reviewed; they rank the queue. A `QualityManager` or `ComplianceAuditor` attaches a screening result to a contact by naming the `interaction_id`, supplying the engine's two continuous measurements, and passing the `expected_interaction_version` demanded by §2.7. `silence_pct` is the percentage of the contact the speech engine classified as dead air, from `0.00` to `100.00` with two decimals. `sentiment_delta` is the end-minus-start movement in customer sentiment, from `-1.00` to `1.00` with two decimals, where negative values mean the customer left unhappier than they arrived. The optional `flagged_categories` array carries the phrase-spotting categories the engine detected, drawn from `PROFANITY`, `NO_DISCLOSURE`, `ESCALATION_REQUEST` and `CONSENT_OBJECTION`, with no duplicates permitted; it defaults to the empty array.

From those inputs the server computes `risk_score`, a number from `0.00` to `100.00`. The computation proceeds in a fixed order. First an unrounded base figure is formed by adding three terms: the `silence_pct` multiplied by forty-five hundredths; the sentiment penalty, which is the negated `sentiment_delta` floored at zero — so a positive or zero `sentiment_delta` contributes nothing — multiplied by one hundred and then by thirty-five hundredths; and the sum of the category points of every entry in `flagged_categories`, where `PROFANITY` is worth twenty-five points, `NO_DISCLOSURE` thirty points, `ESCALATION_REQUEST` fifteen points and `CONSENT_OBJECTION` forty points. That base figure is capped at one hundred, still unrounded. Second, a disclosure-gap bonus of fifteen points applies when the interaction's `required_disclosures` is non-empty **and** at least one required code is missing from the interaction's `captured_disclosures`; otherwise the bonus is zero. Third, the capped base is multiplied by the channel multiplier — one and twenty hundredths for `VOICE`, exactly one for `CHAT`, and eighty hundredths for `EMAIL` — the disclosure-gap bonus is added to that product, the result is capped at `100.00`, and only then is a single rounding to two decimals applied half up. Both the inner cap and the channel multiplication are performed on unrounded values; `risk_score` is the only rounded figure.

`priority_tier` then bands the result: `P1` when `risk_score` is at least `70.00`, `P2` when `risk_score` is at least `40.00` but below `70.00`, and `P3` when `risk_score` is below `40.00`. The screening reads the interaction's `channel`, `required_disclosures` and `captured_disclosures` as they stand at the instant of the call and never re-reads them: a consent review that later fills in the captured disclosures does not retroactively lower an existing scan's `risk_score` or move its tier.

At most one scan per interaction is authoritative. A new scan is stored with `status` of `CURRENT`, and every earlier scan of the same interaction whose `status` was `CURRENT` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented; a successful create also increments the parent interaction's `version`, which is why a second screening of the same contact must re-read the interaction first. A single scan is readable by its own identifier, and the scan collection listing accepts `interaction_id`, `status` and `priority_tier` filters and is ordered as the work queue described in §2.3.

### 3.4. Building the Monitoring Sample: Periods, Quotas and Selections

A monitoring period is expressed as a sampling plan, drafted by the `QualityManager`. The plan names the scorecard through `form_id` — the form must be `ACTIVE` at drafting time, else `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE` — and fixes the period through `period_start` and `period_end`, both inclusive, with `period_end` strictly after `period_start` or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`. `per_agent_target_volume` declares how many interactions are to be reviewed per agent inside the period and must be at least `1` and at most `50`. `source_mix` is an object splitting that per-agent volume across the three sampling sources; it must carry exactly the three keys `random_pct`, `targeted_pct` and `customer_flagged_pct`, each a non-negative integer, and the three must sum to exactly `100`. A missing key, a negative percentage, or a sum other than one hundred is rejected with `ERR_SOURCE_MIX_INVALID`.

Periods on the same scorecard may not collide. Before a plan is drafted, and again when it is opened, the server compares its period against every other plan sharing the same `form_id` whose status is `DRAFT` or `OPEN`, using **closed-interval** semantics: two periods collide when they share even a single instant, so touching endpoints collide — a plan ending on 31 August collides with a plan starting on 31 August. A collision is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A drafted plan starts in `DRAFT` with `pinned_form_guidance_version` null, `selections_count` and `excluded_count` at `0`, `targeted_reserved_count` at `0`, and both `targeted_reserve_by_agent` and `agent_volume_attainment` as empty objects.

Opening the plan moves it `DRAFT → OPEN`, and the transition is where pinning happens: `pinned_form_guidance_version` captures the form's `guidance_version` at that instant, the overlap check is re-run against sibling plans, and the plan's `version` is incremented. A plan that is not `DRAFT` answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`; a plan whose form is no longer `ACTIVE` answers `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The quota arithmetic is the heart of the plan and is recomputed on every event that adds or removes a live selection: a selection create, a selection exclusion, a plan close, the consent-denial cascade of §3.2, and the compliance reservation cascade of §3.5. For each plan and each agent it works as follows. A base cap per source is the plan's `per_agent_target_volume` multiplied by that source's percentage from `source_mix`, divided by one hundred and rounded **down** to a whole number. Because three floors can lose units, a remainder is formed as the `per_agent_target_volume` minus the sum of the three base caps, and the whole remainder is added to the `RANDOM` cap — it is never split across sources and never lands on `TARGETED` or `CUSTOMER_FLAGGED`. The effective `TARGETED` cap is its base cap plus that agent's entry in `targeted_reserve_by_agent`, treating an absent key as zero. The effective `CUSTOMER_FLAGGED` cap is exactly its base cap.

Against those caps the server counts consumption. For each source, the consumed count is the number of selections in this plan for this agent charged to that source whose status is `SELECTED`, `ASSIGNED` or `EVALUATED`; excluded selections are historical and consume nothing. The agent's total `consumed` is the sum across the three sources, and `remaining` is the `per_agent_target_volume` plus that agent's targeted reserve, minus `consumed`. Those three figures are published per agent inside `agent_volume_attainment` as an object with `consumed`, `remaining` and a `by_source` breakdown keyed by `RANDOM`, `TARGETED` and `CUSTOMER_FLAGGED`. At plan level, `selections_count` is the number of selections of the plan in status `SELECTED`, `ASSIGNED` or `EVALUATED`; `excluded_count` is the number in status `EXCLUDED`; and `targeted_reserved_count` is the sum of all values in `targeted_reserve_by_agent`. The identity that must always hold is that for every plan and agent, `consumed` plus `remaining` equals `per_agent_target_volume` plus that agent's reserve — which is exactly why creating a selection and then excluding it returns both sides to their pre-create values.

Pulling a contact into the pool creates a selection. The `QualityManager` supplies `plan_id`, `interaction_id` and the `source` the pull is charged against, one of `RANDOM`, `TARGETED` or `CUSTOMER_FLAGGED`. The plan must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN`, and the interaction must satisfy the consent gate of §2.8. Source eligibility is then checked: `CUSTOMER_FLAGGED` requires the interaction's `customer_flagged` to be `true`; `TARGETED` requires the interaction to carry an analytics scan whose `status` is `CURRENT` and whose `priority_tier` is `P1` or `P2`; `RANDOM` carries no additional requirement. An ineligible pull is rejected with `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Next, the same interaction may not be held twice inside the same plan by a live selection — one whose status is `SELECTED`, `ASSIGNED` or `EVALUATED` — and a second attempt answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_INTERACTION_SELECTION`. Finally the two quota gates fire in order: the agent's `consumed` count for this plan must be strictly below `per_agent_target_volume` plus their targeted reserve, else `ERR_AGENT_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`; and the consumed count for the requested source must be strictly below that source's effective cap, else `ERR_SOURCE_MIX_EXCEEDED`. On success the selection is stored with `status` of `SELECTED`, with `agent_id` denormalised from the interaction's `agent_id` at creation and never updated afterwards even if the interaction is later re-attributed, with `assigned_evaluator_id`, `review_window_start`, `review_window_end` and `exclusion_reason` all null; the plan's `agent_volume_attainment` and `selections_count` and the interaction's `sampled_count` are recomputed, and the plan's `version` is incremented.

Reserving review capacity moves a selection `SELECTED → ASSIGNED`. The payload names `assigned_evaluator_id` and a review window given as `review_window_start` and `review_window_end`. Seven preconditions guard the transition: the selection must be `SELECTED`, else `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`; its plan must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN`; its interaction must satisfy the consent gate, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_VERIFIED`; the named evaluator's `calibration_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CALIBRATED`; `review_window_end` must be strictly after `review_window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`; the requested window must not intersect any window that evaluator already holds on a selection in status `ASSIGNED`, using **half-open** semantics where the start is included and the end excluded — so a window ending at 11:00 sits legally beside one starting at 11:00, while any genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; and the evaluator's `open_assignment_count`, measured *before* this assignment, must be strictly below their `max_open_assignments`, else `ERR_EVALUATOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note the deliberate asymmetry between the two interval conventions in this domain: plan periods are closed intervals whose endpoints collide, while evaluator review windows are half-open intervals whose endpoints do not. On success the selection records the evaluator and both window bounds, the evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed one higher, and the selection's `version` is incremented.

Dropping a live selection is the manual counterpart of the cascades. Excluding works from either `SELECTED` or `ASSIGNED`, and both source states behave identically. The payload carries `exclusion_reason`, one of `CONSENT_DENIED`, `DUPLICATE_INTERACTION`, `EVALUATOR_UNAVAILABLE` or `PLAN_CLOSED_SWEEP`. A selection that has already reached `EVALUATED` or `EXCLUDED` answers `ERR_SELECTION_TERMINAL`, and a selection whose plan is no longer `OPEN` answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN`. On success the selection moves to `EXCLUDED` with the supplied reason recorded, `assigned_evaluator_id` and both window bounds cleared, the plan's `agent_volume_attainment`, `selections_count` and `excluded_count` and the interaction's `sampled_count` recomputed so that the slot returns to the agent's `remaining` volume, and the selection's `version` incremented. When the selection had been `ASSIGNED`, the evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed downward as well and the released window immediately stops blocking that evaluator's other assignments.

Closing the plan moves it `OPEN → CLOSED` and sweeps the pool in a three-hop cascade; a plan that is not `OPEN` answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN`. In the first hop, every selection of the plan whose status is `SELECTED` or `ASSIGNED` is processed in `created_at` ascending order and moved to `EXCLUDED` with `exclusion_reason` set to `PLAN_CLOSED_SWEEP`; selections already `EVALUATED` keep their status, since the sweep is conditional over non-terminal children only. In the second hop, precisely because each swept selection left the pool, its `assigned_evaluator_id` and both window bounds are cleared and the affected evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed, freeing the window for later assignments. In the third hop, precisely because the swept selections no longer consume slots, `agent_volume_attainment`, `selections_count` and `excluded_count` are recomputed on the plan and `sampled_count` is recomputed on each swept interaction. The plan's `version` is incremented, as is the `version` of every selection the sweep touched.

A single plan read returns the whole attainment picture — `agent_volume_attainment`, `targeted_reserve_by_agent`, `selections_count`, `excluded_count` and `targeted_reserved_count` — and the plan collection listing accepts `form_id` and `status` filters — the latter matching `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED` — giving the reverse path from a scorecard to its periods. Selections are readable individually, and the selection collection listing accepts `plan_id`, `interaction_id`, `agent_id`, `assigned_evaluator_id`, `status` and `source` filters; it is also the discovery channel for any selection a cascade moved.

### 3.5. Scoring a Contact: Evaluations, Weighted Scores and the Compliance Cascade

An evaluation is opened by the `QualityEvaluator` who holds the assignment, and the only input is the `selection_id`. The selection must be in status `ASSIGNED`, else `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`; its `assigned_evaluator_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; its interaction must satisfy the consent gate of §2.8; and the plan's pinned guidance revision must still equal the form's current `guidance_version`, else `ERR_GUIDANCE_VERSION_STALE`. Opening the evaluation is the pinning moment for the scoring work: `evaluator_id` is copied from the selection's `assigned_evaluator_id`, `form_id` from the selection's plan's scorecard, `agent_id` from the selection's denormalised `agent_id`, and `pinned_guidance_version` from the plan's `pinned_form_guidance_version`. The row is born with `review_status` of `DRAFT` and `feedback_status` of `PENDING`, with `item_scores` an empty object, and with `weighted_score`, `raw_weighted_score`, `adjusted_weighted_score`, `submitted_at`, `feedback_delivered_at` and `agreement` all null, `auto_fail_triggered` `false`, and `compliance_result` at `NOT_EVALUATED`.

These two status fields form a **parallel state machine**: `review_status` moves through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `FINALIZED` and `REOPENED` under the evaluator's scoring actions, while `feedback_status` moves through `PENDING`, `DELIVERED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `DISPUTED` under the agent-facing actions of §3.6, and the two advance independently on the same row. A row may legitimately sit at `FINALIZED` on the review dimension while sitting at `DISPUTED` on the feedback dimension, or at `REOPENED` while back at `PENDING`. Neither dimension has a terminal state, because an upheld dispute can reopen a finalized row and a reopened row must be delivered again.

Submitting the score carries a single input, `item_scores`: an object keyed by behaviour `code`, whose value for each behaviour is either an integer from `0` to that behaviour's `max_points` or the literal string `"NA"` when the behaviour's `allows_not_applicable` is `true`. Nine preconditions guard the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` transition. The evaluation's `review_status` must be `DRAFT` or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_EDITABLE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the evaluation's `evaluator_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. That evaluator's `calibration_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CALIBRATED`. The underlying selection must be `ASSIGNED` when the evaluation is `DRAFT`, and `EVALUATED` when the evaluation is `REOPENED`. The interaction's `recording_consent_status` must be `VERIFIED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_VERIFIED`. The evaluation's `pinned_guidance_version` must still equal the form's current `guidance_version`, else `ERR_GUIDANCE_VERSION_STALE` — this is the guard that stops a calibration session's guidance rewrite from being applied retroactively to in-flight scoring. The evaluation's own `form_id` must agree with its selection's plan's `form_id`, else `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH`. Every key of `item_scores` must be a behaviour code of the pinned form, and an unknown key answers `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH` rather than an incompleteness error, because a key the form does not recognise means the client scored against a different form version. Every behaviour code of the form must then appear exactly once, with none omitted and none repeated, else `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_INCOMPLETE`; every numeric value must be an integer from `0` through that behaviour's `max_points`, else `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE`; and the literal `"NA"` may appear only against a behaviour whose `allows_not_applicable` is `true` and never against a `compliance_critical` behaviour, else `ERR_NA_NOT_ALLOWED`.

The weighted score is then computed twice, in two variants, from exactly the behaviour rows of the pinned scorecard iterated in `code` ascending order — behaviours of any other form are never read. The arithmetic has a pinned order of operations, and departing from it changes the answer.

**Step one, per-behaviour contribution, rounded once per behaviour before any summation.** For a behaviour marked `"NA"`, the renormalised contribution is *excluded* from the calculation entirely, while the fixed-denominator contribution is that behaviour's whole `weight_pct` rounded half up to two decimals — under the fixed-denominator reading a not-applicable behaviour earns full credit. For a behaviour carrying a number, the achievement fraction is that behaviour's points divided by its `max_points`, kept as an exact ratio and never rounded at this stage; the renormalised contribution is the behaviour's `weight_pct` multiplied by that fraction and rounded half up to two decimals, and the fixed-denominator contribution is identical to it.

**Step two, automatic-fail reshaping.** `auto_fail_triggered` is `true` when at least one behaviour whose `auto_fail_on_violation` is `true` was not marked `"NA"` and was scored exactly `0` points. When `auto_fail_triggered` is `true` and the form's `auto_fail_policy` is `COMPLIANCE_SECTION_ZERO`, every `compliance_critical` behaviour has its contribution set to `0.00` in both variants — in the renormalised variant only where the contribution was not excluded. Critically, the renormalisation denominator is **not** reduced by this zeroing: the zeroed compliance weight stays in the denominator, so the penalty is real rather than absorbed.

**Step three, aggregate and renormalise, then round again.** The applicable weight is the sum of `weight_pct` over every behaviour whose renormalised contribution was not excluded. `weighted_score` is the sum of the non-excluded renormalised contributions, multiplied by one hundred, divided by that applicable weight, and rounded half up to two decimals. `raw_weighted_score` is the sum of the fixed-denominator contributions across **all** behaviours, rounded half up to two decimals — its denominator is always `100.00`. For the same submitted `item_scores`, the two figures diverge whenever at least one behaviour was marked not applicable, because the renormalised figure scales the surviving weight back up to one hundred while the fixed-denominator figure hands the absent behaviour full credit.

**Step four, total zeroing.** When `auto_fail_triggered` is `true` and the form's `auto_fail_policy` is `ZERO_TOTAL`, both `weighted_score` and `raw_weighted_score` are set to `0.00`, overriding whatever step three produced.

**Step five, the compliance verdict.** `compliance_result` is `FAIL` when `auto_fail_triggered` is `true`, or when at least one `compliance_critical` behaviour was not marked `"NA"` and scored strictly fewer points than the form's `min_compliance_item_score`; otherwise it is `PASS`.

One edge deserves its own sentence. When every behaviour on the form is marked `"NA"`, the applicable weight is zero: `weighted_score` is null, `raw_weighted_score` is `100.00`, and `compliance_result` is `NOT_EVALUATED`. No division by zero occurs and no error is raised.

The submit operation then applies its side effects in a fixed order. **Step one:** `item_scores` is stored and `weighted_score`, `raw_weighted_score`, `auto_fail_triggered` and `compliance_result` take the values just computed. **Step two:** `review_status` becomes `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` takes the current logical-clock instant; `feedback_status` is untouched and stays `PENDING`. **Step three:** the underlying selection moves `ASSIGNED → EVALUATED` and the evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed downward, releasing capacity. **Step four:** the agent's `quality_index_rolling` and `quality_index_period` are recomputed as described in §3.9, and `consecutive_passing_evaluations` is recomputed.

The remaining three steps are the compliance cascade, and they are strictly chained. **Step five, hop one:** when `compliance_result` is `FAIL`, the interaction's `compliance_hold` is set to `true`. **Step six, hop two:** precisely because `compliance_hold` moved from `false` to `true`, the agent's `open_compliance_findings` is recomputed one higher — it is always the count of that agent's interactions carrying `compliance_hold` — and the agent's `coaching_state` becomes `REQUIRED` when it had been `NONE` or `CLEARED`. **Step seven, hop three:** precisely because `coaching_state` moved to `REQUIRED`, the reservation-target plan grants that agent one extra targeted follow-up slot: the agent's entry in `targeted_reserve_by_agent` is increased by one, and `targeted_reserved_count` and `agent_volume_attainment` are recomputed. The reservation-target plan is chosen deterministically: among every plan whose status is `OPEN` and whose closed period interval contains the current logical-clock instant, the candidates are ranked by `period_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the first is taken. When no such plan exists, hop three is simply a no-op and no error is raised. If `compliance_result` is `PASS`, none of the three hops fires. A successful submit increments the `version` of the evaluation, the selection, the agent and the interaction, and — when hop three fired — of the reservation-target plan as well.

Re-scoring after an upheld dispute runs the same machinery from a different origin. The `REOPENED → SUBMITTED` transition carries the identical precondition list, with the difference that the underlying selection is expected to be `EVALUATED` rather than `ASSIGNED`, and the identical ordered side effects, with two differences: step three is a no-op because the selection is already `EVALUATED`, and `adjusted_weighted_score` is reset to null because the fresh score supersedes the dispute's adjustment.

`consecutive_passing_evaluations` deserves a precise reading. It is the length of the trailing run of the agent's evaluations, ordered by `submitted_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, whose `compliance_result` is `PASS` and whose effective score is at least `85.00`; any evaluation with a `FAIL` verdict or a score below `85.00` resets it to `0`. It is recomputed on every submit, and it advances by one when the agent acknowledges an evaluation whose `compliance_result` is `PASS` and whose effective score is at least `85.00`.

A single evaluation read exposes both score variants, `adjusted_weighted_score`, `auto_fail_triggered`, `compliance_result` and both state dimensions, and is available to all five roles. The evaluation collection listing accepts `selection_id`, `evaluator_id`, `form_id`, `agent_id`, `review_status`, `feedback_status` and `compliance_result` filters.

### 3.6. Delivering Feedback and Adjudicating Disputes

Delivery is the moment the score becomes the agent's business, and either the scoring `QualityEvaluator` or the agent's `Supervisor` may perform it. Two preconditions apply: the evaluation's `review_status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `FINALIZED`, else `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_DELIVERABLE`, and its `feedback_status` must still be `PENDING`, else `ERR_FEEDBACK_ALREADY_DELIVERED` — delivery is deliberately **not** idempotent, because a second delivery would silently restart the dispute clock. On success `feedback_status` moves `PENDING → DELIVERED`, `feedback_delivered_at` takes the current logical-clock instant, and the evaluation's `version` is incremented. That instant is the start of the dispute window, which runs for seven days.

Acknowledgement belongs to the `Agent` alone. The payload carries `agreement`, which is either `AGREE` or `DISAGREE`, and records the agent's stance without changing the score. The evaluation's `feedback_status` must be `DELIVERED`, else `ERR_FEEDBACK_NOT_DELIVERED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the evaluation's `agent_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. On success `feedback_status` moves `DELIVERED → ACKNOWLEDGED` and `agreement` is stored. Acknowledgement is also the single point where the two dimensions of the parallel state machine are coupled: when `review_status` was `SUBMITTED` it moves `SUBMITTED → FINALIZED`, and when it was already `FINALIZED` or `REOPENED` it is left alone. The agent's `consecutive_passing_evaluations` advances by one when the acknowledged evaluation's `compliance_result` is `PASS` and its effective score is at least `85.00`. Both the evaluation's and the agent's `version` are incremented.

Filing a dispute is how an agent contests a delivered score, and it must happen inside the window. The payload names the `evaluation_id`, lists the contested behaviours in `disputed_behavior_codes`, gives a written `rationale`, and carries the `expected_evaluation_version` demanded by §2.7. `disputed_behavior_codes` must be non-empty, must contain no duplicates, and every code in it must exist on the evaluation's form. `rationale` must be between ten and one thousand characters. The evaluation's `agent_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The evaluation's `feedback_status` must be `DELIVERED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_FEEDBACK_NOT_DELIVERED` — note that an already-acknowledged evaluation may still be disputed, and lands in the same `DISPUTED` state from either origin, and that an already-`DISPUTED` evaluation is deliberately let through this check so that the more specific duplicate rule answers instead: an evaluation that already carries a dispute in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN`. Finally, the filing must occur no later than seven days after the evaluation's `feedback_delivered_at`, measured against the logical clock; a later filing is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

A filed dispute is stored with `agent_id` copied from the evaluation, `status` at `SUBMITTED`, `round_count` at `0`, and `reviewer_evaluator_id`, `adjusted_item_scores` and `outcome_note` all null. The evaluation's `feedback_status` moves to `DISPUTED` and its `version` is incremented.

Picking up a dispute moves it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and is the segregation-of-duties choke point. The dispute must be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_REVIEWABLE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must **differ** from the `evaluator_id` of the disputed evaluation, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the evaluator who produced a score may never adjudicate its challenge. The reviewing evaluator's `calibration_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_CALIBRATED`. And `round_count` must be below two, else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT`, because a dispute may be cycled back to the agent at most twice. On success `reviewer_evaluator_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID`, `round_count` increases by one, and the dispute's `version` is incremented.

Resolution carries `outcome`, which is `UPHELD`, `REJECTED` or `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`, plus an optional `outcome_note` recording the reviewer's written decision and an optional `adjusted_item_scores` map. The dispute must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the dispute's recorded `reviewer_evaluator_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; the segregation-of-duties check applies here as well.

An outcome of `NEEDS_MORE_INFO` sends the dispute back to the agent for more detail. It requires `adjusted_item_scores` to be absent — supplying scores alongside a request for information is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` when `round_count` has already reached two, since no further round may be opened. On success `status` becomes `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`, `outcome_note` is stored, and `reviewer_evaluator_id` is retained. The evaluation's `feedback_status` deliberately stays `DISPUTED`: the information loop does not hand feedback back to the agent as fresh feedback. The dispute's `version` is incremented.

An outcome of `REJECTED` also requires `adjusted_item_scores` to be absent, on pain of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Rejection is a pure compensation: `status` becomes `REJECTED`, `outcome_note` is stored, the evaluation's scores are left completely untouched, `feedback_status` returns `DISPUTED → DELIVERED` with `feedback_delivered_at` left at its original value — so the agent's remaining dispute window is exactly what it was before they filed — and `review_status` moves `SUBMITTED → FINALIZED` when it had been `SUBMITTED`. Every field the dispute touched returns to its pre-dispute value. Both the dispute's and the evaluation's `version` are incremented.

An outcome of `UPHELD` is the substantive one and demands a full replacement scorecard. `adjusted_item_scores` must be present and must cover every behaviour code of the evaluation's form exactly once, else `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_INCOMPLETE`; a key that is not a behaviour code of that form answers `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH`; every adjusted numeric value must be an integer from `0` through that behaviour's `max_points`, else `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE`; the literal `"NA"` obeys the same restrictions as at submit time, on pain of `ERR_NA_NOT_ALLOWED`; and the adjusted scores must differ from the evaluation's stored `item_scores` on at least one of the codes listed in `disputed_behavior_codes`, else the request is malformed and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — upholding a dispute that changes nothing is meaningless.

Upholding then runs a four-hop cascade whose hops are strictly chained. First, `status` becomes `UPHELD` and `outcome_note` is stored. **Hop one:** because the dispute was upheld, the evaluation's `adjusted_weighted_score` is computed as the renormalised weighted score over `adjusted_item_scores`, `compliance_result` is recomputed from those adjusted scores by the same step-five verdict rule of §3.5, `review_status` moves to `REOPENED`, `feedback_status` moves to `PENDING` and `feedback_delivered_at` is cleared to null — the adjusted score must be delivered again before it can be acknowledged or disputed. **Hop two:** precisely because `compliance_result` moved from `FAIL` to `PASS`, the interaction's `compliance_hold` is cleared to `false`. **Hop three:** precisely because `compliance_hold` moved from `true` to `false`, the agent's `open_compliance_findings` is recomputed one lower, and `quality_index_rolling` and `quality_index_period` are recomputed — the adjusted score now takes precedence over the original `weighted_score` in every aggregate. **Hop four:** precisely because `open_compliance_findings` reached `0`, every coaching plan of that agent whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `source_evaluation_id` is this evaluation moves to `VOIDED`, and the agent's `coaching_state` becomes `CLEARED` when it had been `REQUIRED` or `ACTIVE`. When the adjusted scores still leave `compliance_result` at `FAIL`, hops two, three and four do not fire at all and only hop one's fields change. A successful uphold increments the `version` of the dispute, the evaluation, the interaction, the agent and every coaching plan it voided.

Responding to an information request belongs to the `Agent` and moves the dispute `NEEDS_MORE_INFO → SUBMITTED`. The dispute must be `NEEDS_MORE_INFO`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESPONDABLE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the dispute's `agent_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The payload carries `additional_rationale`, which is appended to the stored `rationale` separated by a space, a vertical bar and a space, so the full argument history is preserved on one field. `reviewer_evaluator_id` is cleared to null, freeing the next round to be picked up by any other evaluator, and `round_count` is deliberately left unchanged by this transition — only a fresh pickup counts as a new round. The dispute's `version` is incremented.

A single dispute read exposes `round_count`, `reviewer_evaluator_id`, `adjusted_item_scores` and `outcome_note`, and is available to all five roles. The dispute collection listing accepts `evaluation_id`, `agent_id`, `reviewer_evaluator_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.7. Calibration: Measuring Evaluator Variance and Moving the Standard

Calibration is how the programme keeps its evaluators reading the same scorecard the same way. A `QualityManager` schedules a session against one scorecard, named through `form_id`, and one shared contact, named through `interaction_id`. The form must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the contact must satisfy the consent gate of §2.8 — a session may not be built on a contact whose recording consent is `PENDING` or `DENIED`. The meeting window is given as `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, both inclusive, with `scheduled_end` strictly after `scheduled_start` or the request answers `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`. The manager also supplies `benchmark_item_scores`, the calibration lead's master scores, in exactly the same shape as an evaluation's `item_scores` and validated by exactly the same rules: an unknown behaviour code answers `ERR_FORM_VERSION_MISMATCH`, an incomplete or repeated cover answers `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_INCOMPLETE`, a value outside `0` through that behaviour's `max_points` answers `ERR_ITEM_SCORE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and a misplaced `"NA"` answers `ERR_NA_NOT_ALLOWED`.

At creation the server computes `benchmark_weighted_score` as the renormalised weighted score of `benchmark_item_scores` against the named form, using the identical step-one-through-four arithmetic of §3.5. That figure is fixed at creation and is never re-derived when the session closes, so a later guidance rewrite cannot move the reference every participant was measured against. The session is born with `status` at `OPEN`, `participant_count` at `0`, `variance_pct` and `mean_abs_deviation` null, and `guidance_updates_applied` at `0`.

Opening the floor moves the session `OPEN → SCORING`; submissions are accepted only in `SCORING`. The session must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SESSION_NOT_OPEN`, and its form must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FORM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The session's `version` is incremented.

Each participating `QualityEvaluator` then submits their own reading. The payload names the `session_id` and carries `item_scores` in the same shape and under the same four validation rules as the benchmark. The session must be `SCORING`, else `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCORING`, and an evaluator who has already submitted to this session is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_PARTICIPANT_DUPLICATE` — one reading per evaluator per session. The stored row takes `evaluator_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `weighted_score` from the renormalised weighted score of the submitted map against the session's form; `deviation_pct` and `alignment` are deliberately left null until the session closes, because a deviation only means something once the session is settled. As noted in §2.4, a calibration submission is immutable: it carries no `version` and no `updated_at`, and there is no update endpoint for it. Creating one recomputes the session's `participant_count` and increments the session's `version`.

Closing the session is a single action that forks into three terminal states, and the fork is decided by participant count and measured variance. The session must be `SCORING`, else `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCORING`. The optional `guidance_updates` input is an array of objects, each carrying a behaviour `code` and a replacement `guidance_text`.

The variance arithmetic runs over every submission of the session, whatever the submitting evaluator's standing, iterated in `created_at` ascending order. Each submission's `deviation_pct` is its `weighted_score` minus the session's `benchmark_weighted_score`, rounded half up to two decimals and **signed** — a participant who scored more harshly than the benchmark carries a negative deviation. Each submission's `alignment` is then banded on the absolute value of that rounded deviation: `ALIGNED` when the absolute deviation is at most `3.00`, `MINOR_VARIANCE` when it is greater than `3.00` and at most `7.00`, and `MAJOR_VARIANCE` when it is greater than `7.00`. At session level, `participant_count` is the number of submissions, `variance_pct` is the largest absolute deviation rounded half up to two decimals, and `mean_abs_deviation` is the sum of the absolute deviations divided by the number of submissions, rounded half up to two decimals.

The branch is then chosen. Fewer than two submissions closes the session `SCORING → VOID`. Two or more submissions with a `variance_pct` of at most `7.00` closes it `SCORING → CLOSED_ALIGNED`. Two or more submissions with a `variance_pct` greater than `7.00` closes it `SCORING → CLOSED_VARIANT`.

On either non-void branch the close applies the same four ordered steps. **Step one:** every submission of the session receives its `deviation_pct` and `alignment`, and each participating evaluator's `last_deviation_pct` is refreshed from their submission. **Step two:** the session's `variance_pct` and `mean_abs_deviation` are written and its `status` is set to the branch's value. **Step three:** evaluator standing is moved, one submission at a time, in `created_at` ascending order, under four rules applied in this order — an absolute deviation greater than `15.00` sets that evaluator's `calibration_status` to `SUSPENDED` outright; otherwise an `alignment` of `MAJOR_VARIANCE` on an evaluator currently `CALIBRATED` sets them to `PROBATION`; an `alignment` of `MAJOR_VARIANCE` on an evaluator already `PROBATION` sets them to `SUSPENDED`; and an `alignment` of `ALIGNED` on an evaluator currently `PROBATION` restores them to `CALIBRATED`. Any other combination leaves standing unchanged. Note the consequence for the aligned branch: because a `variance_pct` of at most `7.00` means no participant reached `MAJOR_VARIANCE`, the only rule that can fire on a `CLOSED_ALIGNED` close is the restoration of a probationary evaluator to `CALIBRATED`. **Step four:** for each entry of `guidance_updates`, the named behaviour's `guidance_text` is replaced with the supplied text and that behaviour's own `guidance_version` increases by one; `guidance_updates_applied` is set to the number of entries; and when the list is non-empty the **form-level** `guidance_version` increases by exactly one, no matter how many behaviours were rewritten. That single form-level bump is what invalidates the pinned guidance revision of any in-flight evaluation, surfacing later as `ERR_GUIDANCE_VERSION_STALE`.

A `CLOSED_VARIANT` close carries two additional cascade hops, because suspending an evaluator strands their work. **Hop one:** precisely because those evaluators became `SUSPENDED`, each of their selections whose status is `ASSIGNED` and which carries no evaluation row moves `ASSIGNED → SELECTED` with `assigned_evaluator_id`, `review_window_start` and `review_window_end` all cleared; a selection that already carries an evaluation row, even a `DRAFT` one, is left `ASSIGNED` so the in-flight scoring is not orphaned. **Hop two:** precisely because those selections were released, each affected evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed and the freed review windows stop blocking future assignments in that time range. A non-void close increments the session's `version` and the `version` of every evaluator, selection, behaviour and form row it mutated.

A `VOID` close is the deliberate no-op, and its restraint is the point: a session that only one evaluator scored yields no comparable deviation, so it may not move the scoring standard. `status` becomes `VOID`, `variance_pct` and `mean_abs_deviation` are left null, and `guidance_updates_applied` is set to `0`. No evaluator's `calibration_status` changes and no `last_deviation_pct` is refreshed. No submission is scored: `deviation_pct` and `alignment` stay null on every row. No behaviour's `guidance_text` is rewritten and no `guidance_version` — behaviour-level or form-level — is bumped, **even when the payload carried a populated `guidance_updates` list**. Only the session's `participant_count` is persisted, and only the session's own `version` is incremented.

Sessions are readable individually, exposing `benchmark_weighted_score`, `participant_count`, `variance_pct` and `mean_abs_deviation`, and the session collection listing accepts `form_id`, `interaction_id` and `status` filters. Submissions are likewise readable individually, exposing their post-close `deviation_pct` and `alignment`, and the submission collection listing accepts `session_id`, `evaluator_id` and `alignment` filters.

### 3.8. Coaching Intervention, Escalation and Recognition

A coaching plan is a `Supervisor`'s intervention, sized from the agent's measured shortfall at the instant it is written. The payload names the coached `agent_id`, the `source_evaluation_id` of the evaluation that justified the plan, the `assigned_supervisor_id` of the supervising operator, and the `target_score` the agent must reach, a decimal greater than `0.00` and at most `100.00`; it also carries the `expected_agent_version` demanded by §2.7. The referenced evaluation must belong to the named agent — an evaluation whose own `agent_id` differs is a malformed request and answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and, as noted in §2.1, `assigned_supervisor_id` is an opaque operator identifier that is never resolved against stored data.

Sizing the plan requires history. The agent's `quality_index_rolling` must not be null, meaning they must have at least one scored evaluation; otherwise the plan is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATION_HISTORY`. From there the calculation runs in a strictly pinned order.

First, `deficit_pct` is the `target_score` minus the agent's current `quality_index_rolling`, rounded half up to two decimals. A `deficit_pct` of zero or less means there is no real gap to close, and the request is rejected as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Second, a coaching tier is selected **on the raw deficit**, and that selection is never revisited: tier `T1` when `deficit_pct` is at most `5.00`, tier `T2` when it is greater than `5.00` and at most `12.00`, and tier `T3` when it is greater than `12.00`.

Third, an adjusted deficit is formed by multiplying `deficit_pct` by the tier's severity multiplier — one for `T1`, one and twenty-five hundredths for `T2`, one and a half for `T3` — and rounding half up to two decimals.

Fourth, `required_session_count` is the adjusted deficit divided by the tier's expected gain per session — two and a half points for `T1`, two points for `T2`, one and a half points for `T3` — rounded **up** to the next whole number, then clamped to no fewer than one and no more than eight sessions. The evaluation order here is load-bearing: the multiplier is applied only *after* the tier is fixed, and even when the adjusted deficit crosses into the next tier's raw-deficit band, the tier is not re-selected and the per-session gain is not swapped.

Fifth, `due_at` is the current logical-clock instant plus the tier's allowance: thirty days for `T1`, forty-five days for `T2`, sixty days for `T3`.

Sixth and last, a compliance bump decides `escalation_tier`, and it is applied only after `required_session_count` is already final. When the agent's `open_compliance_findings` is at least one, `escalation_tier` is the selected tier bumped one level — `T1` becomes `T2`, `T2` becomes `T3`, and `T3` stays `T3`; otherwise `escalation_tier` is the selected tier unchanged. Neither `required_session_count` nor `due_at` is recomputed from the bumped tier. All of these inputs are read as they stand when the plan is created; later evaluations never resize an existing plan.

A newly created plan is stored with `status` at `ASSIGNED`, `completed_session_count` at `0` and `escalation_note` null. Creating it sets the agent's `coaching_state` to `ACTIVE`, and it supersedes any earlier plan of the same agent whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` by moving that plan to `VOIDED` — a superseded plan keeps its `completed_session_count` for audit. The agent's `version` and every superseded plan's `version` are incremented.

Logging a delivered session is the `Supervisor`'s routine act. The payload carries a `session_note` and the `session_at` instant at which the session took place; `session_at` may not be earlier than the plan's `created_at`, and an earlier instant answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The plan's `status` must be `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_COACHING_PLAN_TERMINAL`. The first logged session moves the plan `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`; each subsequent one is a self-loop that leaves the status at `IN_PROGRESS`. Either way `completed_session_count` increases by one and the plan's `version` is incremented.

Completing a plan moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and has three preconditions, checked in this order: the plan must not already be `COMPLETED`, `ESCALATED` or `VOIDED`, else `ERR_COACHING_PLAN_TERMINAL`; the agent's `open_compliance_findings` must be exactly `0`, else `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_OPEN`, because a coaching plan cannot be signed off while a compliance failure stands; and `completed_session_count` must be at least `required_session_count`, else `ERR_SESSIONS_INCOMPLETE`. A plan still sitting at `ASSIGNED` always fails that third check, since `required_session_count` is never less than one. On success the plan becomes `COMPLETED` and the agent's `coaching_state` becomes `CLEARED`; both `version` values are incremented.

Escalation to performance improvement is the `QualityManager`'s conditional lever, and it works from either `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. The payload carries an `escalation_note` recording the reason. Beyond the terminal-status check, escalation requires **either** that the logical clock has reached or passed the plan's `due_at`, **or** that the agent's `open_compliance_findings` is at least two — an agent with two or more standing findings may be escalated immediately, before the due date. Failing both conditions answers `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE`. On success `status` becomes `ESCALATED`, `escalation_note` is stored, and `escalation_tier` is forced to `T3` regardless of what the original sizing computed. The agent's `coaching_state` is deliberately left unchanged, because an escalated plan is still an active intervention rather than a resolved one. The plan's `version` is incremented.

Recognition is the mirror image of coaching and belongs to the `QualityManager`. The payload carries an `award_code` naming the recognition programme and a `points` value, an integer from `1` to `500`. Three gates apply in order: the agent must have at least two evaluations in `review_status` `SUBMITTED`, `FINALIZED` or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVALUATION_HISTORY`; the agent's `open_compliance_findings` must be `0`, else `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_OPEN`; and the agent's `quality_index_rolling` must be at least `92.00`, else `ERR_RECOGNITION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. On success the awarded `points` are added to the agent's `recognition_points` and the agent's `version` is incremented; the operation answers with the updated agent rather than a separate award record.

Plans are readable individually — exposing `deficit_pct`, `escalation_tier`, `required_session_count`, `completed_session_count` and `due_at` — by the `QualityManager`, `Supervisor`, `ComplianceAuditor` and the `Agent`. The coaching-plan collection listing, available to the `QualityManager`, `Supervisor` and `ComplianceAuditor`, accepts `agent_id`, `source_evaluation_id`, `status` and `escalation_tier` filters, and is the discovery channel for plans that a cascade moved to `VOIDED`.

### 3.9. Reading the Quality Record: Indices, Satisfaction Correlation and Repeat Contact

Two persisted indices summarise an agent's standing, and both are recomputed at exactly two moments: ordered step four of an evaluation submit, and hop three of an upheld dispute resolution. Both draw on the same population — every evaluation of that agent whose `review_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `FINALIZED` or `REOPENED` and whose `weighted_score` is not null. `DRAFT` evaluations never contribute, and an excluded selection contributes nothing because it never produces an evaluation at all. Within that population, an evaluation's **effective score** is its `adjusted_weighted_score` when that field is not null, and otherwise its `weighted_score` — an upheld dispute's adjustment always wins over the original.

`quality_index_rolling` is the unweighted mean of the four most recent effective scores. The population is ordered by `submitted_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break; the window is the first four rows, or every row when there are fewer than four; and the index is the sum of the window's effective scores divided by the number of rows in the window, rounded half up to two decimals. When the population is empty the index is null. This is the figure that sizes coaching plans and gates recognition, and because it caps the window at four evaluations it responds quickly to a change in performance regardless of how long each contact ran.

`quality_index_period` is the duration-weighted mean over the open monitoring period, so long contacts dominate it. The relevant plan is the one whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose closed period interval — from `period_start` through `period_end`, both endpoints included — contains the current logical-clock instant, with candidates ranked by `period_start` ascending and `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. When no such plan exists, `quality_index_period` is null. Otherwise the population is narrowed to those evaluations whose interaction's `started_at` falls inside that same closed period interval; when that subset is empty, or when the total of its interactions' `duration_seconds` is zero, the index is null. Otherwise it is the sum, over the subset, of each evaluation's effective score multiplied by its interaction's `duration_seconds`, divided by the total `duration_seconds` of the subset, rounded half up to two decimals. Because of the weighting the two indices legitimately disagree: two evaluations scoring `78.82` over a ten-minute contact and `52.00` over a half-hour contact read `58.71` on the duration-weighted index while reading `65.41` on the rolling one.

A per-agent quality summary is computed on demand for the `QualityManager`, `Supervisor` and `ComplianceAuditor`, and nothing it produces is persisted. It reports `quality_index_rolling` and `quality_index_period` as defined above, alongside five correlation figures computed over the same evaluation population, each evaluation paired with the interaction reached through its selection. `evaluations_considered` is the size of that population. `paired_sample_size` is the number of those evaluations whose interaction carries a non-null `csat_score`. `repeat_contact_rate` is the count of evaluations whose interaction has `repeat_contact_within_72h` set to `true`, divided by the size of the population, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimals.

The satisfaction gap is reported twice, under two different treatments of missing survey responses, and the two figures diverge in exactly the way the two treatments imply. The population is first split at a floor of `85.00` on effective score: an evaluation whose effective score is at least `85.00` is a high scorer, and one below `85.00` is a low scorer. Under the paired-only treatment, reported as `csat_gap_paired`, the mean `csat_score` of the high group is computed over only those high-group interactions whose `csat_score` is not null and rounded half up to two decimals; the same is done for the low group; and `csat_gap_paired` is the high mean minus the low mean, rounded half up to two decimals — each mean is rounded **before** the subtraction. `csat_gap_paired` is null whenever either side has no non-null `csat_score` at all. Under the imputed treatment, reported as `csat_gap_all`, the identical computation is performed with every null `csat_score` replaced by the imputed value `3`, so no interaction is dropped; `csat_gap_all` is null only when the high group or the low group is empty. For the same three evaluations the paired gap can read `3.00` while the imputed gap reads `2.50`.

One edge governs the whole summary: when the evaluation population is empty, `evaluations_considered` and `paired_sample_size` are both `0`, and `csat_gap_paired`, `csat_gap_all` and `repeat_contact_rate` are all null. The summary still answers `200 OK` and never divides by zero.

## 4. API Endpoint Specifications

Every endpoint below is mounted at the API root, exactly as written in the Path column, and every request must carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer `201 Created`; every other successful call — reads, state transitions, computed summaries and the clock control — answers `200 OK`. All responses, successful or not, use the envelope of §2.2, and list endpoints honour the pagination and ordering rules of §2.3. Optional query parameters and optional body fields are described in §3 alongside the workflow they belong to; a body field the workflow requires is mandatory, and its absence is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` per §2.11.
