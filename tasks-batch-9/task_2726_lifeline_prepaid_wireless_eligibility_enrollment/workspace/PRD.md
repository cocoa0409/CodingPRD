# Lifeline Prepaid Wireless Eligibility & Enrollment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The federal Lifeline program pays a monthly subsidy to eligible telecommunications carriers so that low-income households can obtain voice and broadband service at little or no cost. The program's integrity rests almost entirely on three promises that are difficult to keep at scale: that a household draws no more than one benefit at a time, that the subsidy is claimed only for subscribers who actually use the service, and that every dollar of federal support can be traced back to a verified eligibility determination. Carriers, meanwhile, need a predictable operational surface — they must be able to enroll applicants, hand out handsets, port subscribers in from competitors, and file monthly reimbursement claims without waiting on manual program-office intervention.

This document specifies the service that mediates between those two interests. It is mounted under the `/lifeline` path prefix and it owns the full lifecycle of a benefit: the normalization and duplicate-adjudication of service addresses, the National Verifier screening of applicants by income or by qualifying assistance program, enrollment under a signed one-benefit-per-household certification, the non-usage rule with its fifteen-day cure period, annual recertification with de-enrollment on non-response, handset issuance under a single-device-per-subscriber control, benefit ports between carriers behind a porting freeze, monthly subsidy claim filing with risk-ranked audit sampling and recoupment, and the subscriber complaint and de-enrollment appeal channel that can compensate an erroneous de-enrollment and unwind the money that followed from it.

Two structural characteristics of the domain shape almost every requirement below. First, a single enrollment carries four independent compliance postures at once — whether the benefit is being drawn, whether the line is being used, where the subscriber sits in the annual recertification cycle, and whether a handset is in the subscriber's hands — and these four advance on unrelated triggers rather than in lockstep. Second, most write operations are not local: de-enrolling one subscriber can free a dwelling, release other applicants who were held behind a duplicate flag, reclaim a handset, and move a carrier's device and ledger counters, all inside the same request. The service is immediately consistent: when a request returns, every counter, flag and cascaded state it implies has already been written, and a subsequent read observes the settled world.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint under `/lifeline` must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it acts in. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before the request body is parsed, so a request that both omits its headers and carries a malformed body still reports the authentication failure rather than a schema failure.

Exactly five roles exist, and their spellings are `ProgramAdministrator`, `ProviderAgent`, `NationalVerifier`, `ComplianceAuditor`, and `Subscriber`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of these five is treated as a permitted-role failure rather than as a schema problem; the same code is returned when a recognised role attempts an operation it is not authorized for. In both cases the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Section 4 lists the authorized roles for each operation.

The two roles that are backed by persisted rows scope their writes by identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's carrier reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Subscriber`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Subscriber`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Subscriber` compare the resource's subscriber reference to `X-User-ID`. The remaining three roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque program-office operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes an actor reference — for example as `de_enrolled_by_ref` on an `Enrollment` de-enrolled by the compliance sweep, or as `decided_by_ref` on a decided `SubscriberCase` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `NationalVerifier`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque verifier-interface identifier that is not compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque audit-staff identifier; it is recorded as `sample_drawn_by_ref` on the claim whose sample it drew, as `drawn_by_ref` on each drawn sample, and as `adjudicated_by_ref` on each sample it adjudicates, but it is never matched against any entity's `id`.

Peer isolation is enforced separately from role permission. A `ProviderAgent` whose `X-User-ID` is not the carrier that owns the addressed row is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even though the operation is permitted to the role in general. This applies to enrolling a subscriber, stocking a handset into a carrier's inventory, issuing a handset to an enrollment, returning a handset, drafting a reimbursement claim, filing a reimbursement claim, opening a port request, recording usage, and de-enrolling a benefit. On a port request the carrier that must match is the source carrier derived from the enrollment's current carrier reference; on accepting or rejecting a port the acting `X-User-ID` must instead be the gaining carrier named by `target_provider_id`. The `Subscriber` role is confined in the same way to its own subscriber identity when answering a recertification notice and when filing a complaint or appeal: acting against another subscriber's enrollment is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A value outside those bounds, or of the wrong type, is a schema failure and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Every collection is returned in ascending `created_at` order unless the endpoint's own narrative says otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` (the page size actually applied), `offset` (the starting position actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every one of the ten persisted resource types — carriers, service addresses, applicants, eligibility checks, enrollments, handsets, port requests, reimbursement claims, audit samples and subscriber cases — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade originating in some other resource.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every subsequent mutation. Recomputing a derived counter is a mutation: when a de-enrollment forces a carrier's device counters to be recalculated, that carrier's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by one even though no field the client supplied has changed.

No resource type in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`; all four properties are present on all ten types.

All temporal reasoning is done against a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts and never advances on its own. It moves only when a caller supplies a new instant as `to_iso8601` to the test-only clock endpoint, which answers with the newly seated instant as `current_utc`. Setting the clock backwards is permitted. Seating the clock writes no business row and triggers no sweep. Wherever this document says "the current instant" or "clock now", it means the value the logical clock holds when the request is processed, and every timestamp the server writes — a confirmation instant, a de-enrollment instant, a handset issuance instant — is taken from it.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Rows

Three write operations reach across an aggregate boundary in a way that can silently discard work the caller never observed, and each therefore requires the caller to pin the parent row's `version` in the request body. The field is mandatory, not optional, and the check compares it against the parent row's current `version`.

- Opening a port request requires `expected_enrollment_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Enrollment`. If it does not equal the enrollment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a newly created port request legitimately supersedes any still-open request on the same enrollment; without the pin, a caller working from a stale view would cancel a port it never saw.
- Opening a draft reimbursement claim requires `expected_provider_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the named `Provider`. A mismatch with the carrier's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new draft for the same carrier and service month legitimately supersedes the prior draft, so a stale writer would otherwise discard a draft priced from ledger figures it never read.
- Resolving a duplicate-flagged service address requires `expected_address_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `HouseholdAddress`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ADDRESS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A resolved dwelling may legitimately be re-flagged and re-resolved with a different declared household count, so a stale resolver would otherwise decide against occupancy figures it never read.

No other operation takes a version field. Adding one to a request that is not on this list has no effect, because a supplied field the specification does not define as an input is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.6. Segregation of Duties

Two separations of duty are enforced, both keyed on `X-User-ID`, and both report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The auditor who drew a claim's audit sample may not adjudicate any sample of that claim: adjudication is refused when `X-User-ID` equals the owning claim's `sample_drawn_by_ref`. Settling a claim therefore always requires two distinct auditor identities.

On the appeal side, the operator whose write caused a de-enrollment may not decide the case that contests it: deciding a case is refused when `X-User-ID` equals the appealed enrollment's `de_enrolled_by_ref`. The same refusal applies when `X-User-ID` equals the case's own `reviewer_ref` and the case has been remanded at least once, so a reviewer cannot both remand a case and then decide the round it returns in.

Both separations are evaluated ahead of the concurrency check of §2.5, so an identity that is barred from a step is told about the conflict even when the version it supplied is stale.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When a single request would trip several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in this order:

1. Identity headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The role is one of the five declared roles and is permitted on this operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Body schema: a required input field missing, of the wrong JSON type, outside a declared numeric range, or carrying a value outside a declared enumeration — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Computed and system-managed fields supplied in the body are silently ignored, never rejected.
4. Path existence, resolved outer to inner: for every identifier segment in the path, the addressed row must exist, otherwise the entity-specific not-found code for that segment: `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. When an applicant's eligibility check is opened, for instance, the applicant is resolved before the body is examined at all.
5. Body foreign-key existence, resolved in declaration order: a body key naming a row that does not exist yields the referenced entity's not-found code. For enrollment creation the declaration order is `subscriber_id`, then `provider_id`, then `eligibility_check_id`, then `certified_address_id`.
6. Ownership and peer isolation per §2.1 — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
7. Segregation of duties per §2.6 — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. Optimistic concurrency per §2.5 — the relevant stale-version code. Because this sits above every state and business check, a stale writer never learns business facts about a row it is not permitted to write.
9. State preconditions on the addressed rows — the 409-class conflict codes.
10. Business rules: referential, safety, constraint, temporal and fiscal checks, in that broad order.

Three specific precedences are worth stating outright because a request can plausibly trip both members of each pair.

- A request to enroll a subscriber whose eligibility certification has lapsed **and** whose carrier is not licensed in the address's state reports `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CHECK_EXPIRED`, not `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE`. The certification-validity check outranks every business check on enrollment creation.
- A request to enroll that would both breach the household's permitted benefit occupancy **and** push the carrier past its enrollment cap reports `ERR_ONE_PER_HOUSEHOLD_VIOLATION`, not `ERR_PROVIDER_ENROLLMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The one-benefit-per-household rule is the program's central safety constraint and outranks every fiscal check.
- A handset issuance request that falls inside the reissue freeze **and** faces an empty in-stock pool reports `ERR_DEVICE_FREEZE_ACTIVE`, not `ERR_NO_DEVICE_IN_STOCK`. The freeze is a policy fact about the subscriber; reporting an inventory problem instead would invite the carrier to restock and retry pointlessly.

The full ordering of checks on enrollment creation, from first to last, is: the certifying check is confirmed; the certifying check has not expired; the applicant is in the verified-eligible posture; the carrier is operating; household benefit occupancy permits the new interval; the address is not sitting in an unresolved duplicate cohort; the household certification has been attested; the carrier is licensed in the address's state; the carrier is below its enrollment cap.

On handset issuance the ordering is: the enrollment's benefit is active; the enrollment is not already holding a handset; the reissue freeze is clear or waived; the subscriber holds no outstanding handset anywhere; a handset is available in stock.

### 2.8. Amounts, Counters and Rounding

Every monetary figure in this system is an integer number of **cents** and is stored and returned as a JSON integer. There are no decimal money fields and no money strings. Income figures, handset acquisition costs, per-line support amounts, recoupments and penalties are all cents.

Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, it is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent — a value ending in exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 92.5 cents becomes 93, not 92. Rounding is applied only at the points this document names, and the number of rounding steps is part of the specification: rounding earlier or later than specified produces self-consistent but wrong figures. In particular, per-enrollment support lines are each rounded before they are summed and the sum itself is never rounded; the income-threshold computation rounds exactly once, before its clamps are applied; the annualization of a trailing-quarter income figure rounds exactly once; and the recoupment computation rounds exactly twice, once when the error is extrapolated and once when the penalty is taken from it. Where a ratio feeds a later step, it is carried as an exact rational value and is never rounded to a decimal approximation before use.

Every counter field on every entity is an integer and is **derived**, not supplied: it is recomputed from the rows it counts whenever any of those rows changes. Clients may not write counters, and a counter supplied in a request body is ignored.

### 2.9. Interval and Boundary Conventions

The program runs on seven distinct temporal windows and they deliberately do not share one convention, so each is stated explicitly here and repeated where it is used.

- **Eligibility certification validity** is closed at its upper end. A certification remains usable while the current instant is less than or equal to `expires_at`; the instant exactly equal to `expires_at` still enrolls, and one millisecond later does not.
- **The non-usage cure window** is closed at its upper end. A usage event at the instant exactly equal to `cure_deadline_at` still cures; expiry requires the current instant to be strictly greater than `cure_deadline_at`.
- **The recertification response window** is closed at its upper end: a response at the instant exactly equal to `recert_response_deadline_at` is accepted, and non-response requires the current instant to be strictly greater. The window in which a notice may be issued is closed at its lower end: the notice fires once the current instant is greater than or equal to the notice-opening instant.
- **The porting freeze** is half-open: a port request is refused while the current instant is strictly less than `port_freeze_until`, and a request at exactly `port_freeze_until` is accepted.
- **The handset reissue freeze** is closed at *both* ends. A request whose instant falls anywhere in the interval, including exactly on the upper boundary instant, is refused; the first acceptable instant is one millisecond past that boundary. This is deliberately the opposite convention from the porting freeze.
- **A household's benefit occupancy interval** is half-open, closed at its start and open at its end, so adjacency is permitted: a new benefit starting at exactly the instant a prior benefit ended does not overlap it.
- **The de-enrollment appeal window** is closed at its upper end, and its upper end is the last millisecond of a calendar day: an appeal filed at exactly `appeal_deadline_at` is still inside the window.

Two further conventions apply to elapsed time. Where a rule counts *idle days*, the elapsed time is divided by one day and truncated toward zero — a gap of 29 days, 23 hours and 59 seconds counts as 29 idle days, not 30. Where a rule adds a number of days to a *date* rather than to an instant, the time-of-day component of the anchor is discarded first and the result is stamped either at `00:00:00.000Z` or at `23:59:59.999Z` as the individual rule specifies. Where a rule adds days to an *instant*, the time-of-day is preserved.

### 2.10. Cascades, Counters and Conserved Quantities

A cascade is not a background job. When one write implies another, both land inside the same request, and the caller's response already reflects the settled result. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, including rows the caller never named.

Three quantities in the system are **conserved**: a forward action and its compensating action must leave them exactly as they were.

The first is a carrier's handset inventory. The five device counters partition the same set of rows, so after every single write the following must hold: `devices_total_count` equals `devices_in_stock_count` plus `devices_issued_count` plus `devices_reclaim_pending_count` plus `devices_retired_count`. `devices_total_count` counts every handset the carrier owns regardless of status and never decreases, because no handset row is ever deleted. `devices_in_stock_count` counts handsets in stock, `devices_issued_count` those out with a subscriber, `devices_reclaim_pending_count` those awaiting physical return, and `devices_retired_count` those that are retired or lost taken together. Issuing a handset and then taking it back in good condition must restore all five counters to the values they held before the issuance; taking it back damaged or lost instead leaves the total unchanged but does not return the unit to stock.

The second is a carrier's subsidy ledger. `claim_gross_cents` is the sum of `final_amount_cents` over that carrier's claims that are filed, held, under audit, audit-passed or audit-failed; draft and superseded claims contribute nothing. `recouped_cents` is the sum of `recoupment_amount_cents` over that carrier's audit-failed claims. `open_claim_count` counts that carrier's claims that are draft, filed, held or under audit. `audit_hold_flag` is true exactly when at least one of the carrier's claims is audit-failed, and false otherwise. After every write the invariant `net_reimbursed_cents` equals `claim_gross_cents` minus `recouped_cents` must hold. The ledger always sums the **gross** final amount; the per-claim `net_payable_cents` figure is for display only and is never added into any carrier counter, because doing so would double-count the recoupment. When a granted appeal reverses an audit failure, all four ledger figures must return to precisely the values they held before the adjudication.

The third is a dwelling's benefit occupancy, described in §3.2 and §3.4: de-enrolling and then reinstating a benefit must leave the dwelling's occupancy figure exactly where it started.

### 2.11. Household Income Thresholds

Program eligibility by income is measured against a household-size-scaled threshold derived from the Federal Poverty Guidelines, and the same computation is used in three places: when an applicant is created, when an eligibility check is opened, and when a recertification attestation is processed. It reads exactly two facts about the household — its size and whether it sits on qualifying Tribal lands — together with a fixed constant table.

The computation proceeds in four steps. First, a poverty-guideline figure is built as a base annual amount of 1560000 cents plus 552000 cents for each household member beyond the first; a household of one takes the base alone, a household of four takes the base plus three increments. Second, a multiplier is selected: 1.50 when the household is flagged as residing on qualifying Tribal lands, and 1.35 otherwise. Third, the guideline figure is multiplied by that multiplier and the product is rounded half up to a whole cent — this is the only rounding step in the computation. Fourth, the rounded figure is clamped, **ceiling first and floor second**: it is reduced to the program income ceiling of 6000000 cents if it exceeds it, and the result of that reduction is then raised to the program minimum of 2106000 cents if it falls below it.

The clamp order is observable and the two clamps are independently reachable. A large non-Tribal household and a slightly smaller Tribal household can produce different raw products and still store the identical ceiling value. The program minimum is by construction exactly the figure a one-person non-Tribal household produces, so the floor never raises a standard household above its computed value; it exists to protect very small Tribal-adjacent edge cases from arithmetic below the statutory minimum.

The resulting figure is stored on the applicant as `income_threshold_cents` and is recomputed whenever the applicant's `household_size` or `tribal_lands_flag` is written — which happens at creation and again on every accepted recertification attestation. Eligibility checks and recertifications additionally take their own immutable snapshot of the figure, so a later change to the household does not rewrite the basis on which a past determination was made.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Carrier Registration and Handset Inventory

Nothing else in the system can happen until a **Provider** — an eligible telecommunications carrier — exists. Registration is reserved to a `ProgramAdministrator`, who supplies the carrier's legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, its `spin_id` (the Service Provider Identification Number, exactly 9 digits and unique across all carriers), its `study_area_code` (exactly 6 digits, naming the FCC study area it draws support from), and `licensed_state_codes`, a non-empty array of distinct two-letter uppercase state codes it is licensed to enroll subscribers in. A second registration reusing an existing `spin_id` is a schema-level failure and returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two further inputs are optional: `enrollment_cap`, the maximum number of concurrently active benefits the carrier may hold, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 1000; and `status`, which is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`. The operating `status` is set once at registration and is never mutated afterwards — there is no endpoint that suspends or reinstates a carrier.

At registration every derived figure on the carrier starts at zero and `audit_hold_flag` starts false. Those derived figures are the carrier's live operational picture and are recomputed, never supplied. `active_enrollment_count` is the number of enrollments held by this carrier whose benefit is currently active. The five handset counters and the four ledger figures are defined in §2.10.

Handsets enter a carrier's inventory one at a time. A `ProviderAgent` acting for that carrier — or a `ProgramAdministrator` — stocks a **Device** by supplying its `imei` (exactly 15 digits, unique across all handsets in the system; a repeat is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), its `model_tier`, which is one of `BASIC`, `STANDARD` or `SMART`, and `unit_cost_cents`, the carrier's acquisition cost, which must be zero or greater. A newly stocked handset is born `IN_STOCK` with `issued_enrollment_id`, `issued_at`, `returned_at` and `return_condition` all null. The owning carrier's `devices_total_count` and `devices_in_stock_count` are then recomputed and its `version` advances by one. A suspended carrier cannot take delivery of stock: the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Reads are open to all five roles. A carrier can be read individually, complete with its live ledger and device counters; handsets can be read individually or listed with filters on `provider_id`, on `issued_enrollment_id`, on `status` and on `model_tier`.

### 3.2. Service Addresses and Duplicate-Household Adjudication

Because the one-benefit-per-household rule is enforced against a *dwelling* rather than against a mailing label, every applicant is bound to a registered **HouseholdAddress**, and the system does its own normalization rather than trusting the enrolling agent's typing. A `ProviderAgent` or a `ProgramAdministrator` registers a dwelling with `address_line1` (1 to 160 characters), an optional `unit_designator` (up to 16 characters, or null when the dwelling has no unit designation — null is also its default), `city` (1 to 80 characters), `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters) and `postal_code` (either 5 or 9 digits).

From those five inputs the system derives `address_hash`, the duplicate-detection key. It is built as follows. The postal component is first shortened to its first five characters. Then each of the five parts — the street line, the unit designator (treated as an empty string when null), the city, the state code and the shortened postal component — is put through the same two-step normalization: every ASCII letter is uppercased, and then every character that is not an uppercase letter A through Z or a digit 0 through 9 is deleted. The five normalized parts are finally joined into a single string with one vertical-bar character between consecutive parts, in the order street line, unit designator, city, state code, shortened postal component. There is no hashing function and no truncation: the joined canonical form *is* the stored value. A street line of "1200  N. Main St." with unit "Apt 4-B" in "El Paso", state "tx", postal "79901-2214" therefore normalizes to `1200NMAINST|APT4B|ELPASO|TX|79901`; a second dwelling entered as "1200 n main st", unit "apt. 4b", "EL PASO", "TX", "79901" normalizes to exactly the same string and is a duplicate, while unit "Apt 4-C" yields `1200NMAINST|APT4C|ELPASO|TX|79901` and is not.

Two dwellings belong to the same **cohort** exactly when their `address_hash` values are byte-equal. `cohort_address_count` on every dwelling is the number of registered dwellings sharing its hash, counting itself, so a dwelling standing alone reports 1. A dwelling's duplicate-resolution posture lives in its `status`, which is one of `UNVERIFIED`, `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`, `RESOLVED_MULTI_HOUSEHOLD` or `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD`, and begins at `UNVERIFIED`. `occupied_benefit_slots` starts at 0 and `max_permitted_benefit_slots` starts at 1.

Registering a dwelling re-evaluates its whole cohort inside the same request. `cohort_address_count` is recomputed on the new row and on every pre-existing row sharing the hash. If the cohort now holds two or more rows, every row in it whose posture is `UNVERIFIED`, `RESOLVED_MULTI_HOUSEHOLD` or `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD` moves to `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`, has `max_permitted_benefit_slots` reset to 1, has `updated_at` refreshed and has `version` incremented by one; the newly created row is itself created directly in `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` rather than passing through `UNVERIFIED`. The `RESOLVED_MULTI_HOUSEHOLD → DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` and `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD → DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` edges are genuine re-entry edges: a dwelling that has already been adjudicated can be pulled back into a duplicate cohort by a later arrival. On the multi-household re-entry edge the previously recorded `declared_household_count` and `independent_household_worksheet_ref` are deliberately left intact for the audit trail, but `max_permitted_benefit_slots` still recomputes to 1, so the dwelling loses its extra capacity until it is re-adjudicated.

Clearing a flag is a `ProgramAdministrator`'s job and always names one of two `resolution` values. The request pins the dwelling's version in `expected_address_version` per §2.5 and may carry a free-form `resolution_note`. A dwelling whose `status` is not `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` cannot be resolved at all: the attempt returns `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_FLAGGED`.

Choosing `MULTI_HOUSEHOLD` asserts that the dwelling genuinely contains several economically independent households. It requires `declared_household_count`, which must be at least 2, and `independent_household_worksheet_ref`, a reference to the independent-economic-household worksheet on file; a request omitting either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both values are written onto the dwelling, its posture becomes `RESOLVED_MULTI_HOUSEHOLD`, `max_permitted_benefit_slots` recomputes to the declared count, every applicant held at that dwelling behind a duplicate hold is released as described in §3.3, and `version` advances by one. No enrollment is disturbed: raising the permitted occupancy can only make the dwelling more permissive.

Choosing `SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD` asserts the opposite, and it is the most far-reaching single write in the system. `declared_household_count` and `independent_household_worksheet_ref` are cleared to null and `max_permitted_benefit_slots` recomputes to 1. Then the cascade runs in four linked steps. First, every active benefit certified at that dwelling is ordered by `benefit_start_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; the earliest is retained and every subsequent one is de-enrolled with `de_enrollment_reason` set to `DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD`, `benefit_end_date` and `de_enrolled_at` set to the current instant, and `de_enrolled_by_ref` set to the resolving operator's `X-User-ID`. Second, caused by the first step, each de-enrolled benefit that was holding a handset moves its `device_status` from `DEVICE_ISSUED` to `RECLAIM_PENDING` and moves that handset from `ISSUED` to `RECLAIM_PENDING`. Third, caused by the second step, the owning carrier's `devices_issued_count` and `devices_reclaim_pending_count` are recomputed and its `version` incremented by one; its `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed for the same reason. `occupied_benefit_slots` is recomputed on the dwelling, every applicant held there behind a duplicate hold is released, the dwelling's posture becomes `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD` and its `version` advances by one.

There is a fourth way a flag can clear, and no operator triggers it. Whenever a benefit at a `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` dwelling is de-enrolled — by any route at all — the dwelling's `occupied_benefit_slots` is recomputed, and if it reaches 0 the dwelling resolves itself to `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD`: `max_permitted_benefit_slots` recomputes to 1, `declared_household_count` and `independent_household_worksheet_ref` are cleared to null, every applicant held at the dwelling is released, and `version` advances by one. An emptied dwelling can no longer be a duplicate, so the flag clears itself.

### 3.3. Applicant Intake and National Verifier Screening

An applicant enters the system as a **Subscriber** row created by a `ProviderAgent` or a `ProgramAdministrator`. The request carries the applicant's legal `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, `last4_ssn` (exactly four digits), and `address_id` naming the dwelling the applicant resides in. It also carries the household facts the income test needs: `household_size`, an integer of at least 1 and at most 12; `annual_income_cents`, the self-reported gross annual household income, zero or greater; and optionally `last_3mo_income_cents`, the gross household income over the trailing three months, also zero or greater. Two further optional inputs describe the qualification route: `qualifying_benefit`, naming the federal assistance program the applicant claims participation in, which is one of `NONE`, `SNAP`, `MEDICAID`, `SSI`, `FPHA`, `VETERANS_PENSION`, `TRIBAL_TANF`, `BIA_GENERAL_ASSISTANCE` and defaults to `NONE`; and `tribal_lands_flag`, which defaults to false and, when true, both raises the income multiplier of §2.11 and unlocks the Tribal support enhancement of §3.8.

Two derivations happen at creation. The system computes `identity_key`, a duplicate-identity fingerprint formed by taking the applicant's name, deleting every character that is not a letter or digit, uppercasing what remains, and then appending a vertical bar, the date of birth as supplied, another vertical bar, and the four SSN digits. If any existing applicant already carries the same `identity_key`, creation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBSCRIBER_IDENTITY`. The system also computes `income_threshold_cents` per §2.11.

The applicant's lifecycle lives in `status`, whose values are `PROSPECT`, `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING`, `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE`, `DUPLICATE_HOLD`, `ENROLLED`, `DE_ENROLLED` and `BARRED`. A new applicant is normally born `PROSPECT`, but if the dwelling named by `address_id` is sitting in `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` at the instant of creation, the row is born directly in `DUPLICATE_HOLD` instead — no separate request performs that edge. Creating the applicant also refreshes the referenced dwelling's `updated_at`, since its `occupied_benefit_slots` and `cohort_address_count` are recomputed even though neither value changes. `eligibility_basis` starts at `NONE` and `de_enrollment_count` starts at 0.

Screening is a two-identity affair by design: the party that opens a check cannot be the party that determines it. A `ProviderAgent` or `ProgramAdministrator` opens an **EligibilityCheck** against an applicant, declaring `claimed_basis` as either `INCOME` or `BENEFIT` and optionally attaching a `proof_document_ref` (null when none is supplied). The check snapshots the household income threshold and records which arithmetic produced the figure it compared. For an income-claimed check, `income_variant` is `STRICT_ANNUALIZED` and `evaluated_income_cents` is the applicant's `annual_income_cents` taken verbatim with no derivation at all; the snapshot of the threshold is written to the check's own `income_threshold_cents`. For a benefit-claimed check the income arm is not consulted: `income_variant` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` and both `evaluated_income_cents` and the check's `income_threshold_cents` are null.

Each basis has its own gate. An income-claimed check whose evaluated figure is strictly greater than the snapshotted threshold is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_INCOME_ABOVE_THRESHOLD`; a figure exactly equal to the threshold qualifies. A benefit-claimed check is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_BASIS` in two situations: when the applicant's `qualifying_benefit` is `NONE`, and when the claimed program is one of the two Tribal-only programs — `TRIBAL_TANF` or `BIA_GENERAL_ASSISTANCE` — while the applicant's `tribal_lands_flag` is false.

Opening a check also moves the applicant. An applicant in `PROSPECT` advances to `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING`. An applicant in `DUPLICATE_HOLD` may open a check but keeps its posture, because the hold is about the dwelling and not about the person. A previously de-enrolled applicant may re-apply: the `DE_ENROLLED → PROSPECT` edge fires provided `de_enrollment_count` is below 2, and the very same request then applies the `PROSPECT → ELIGIBILITY_PENDING` edge, so a re-applying applicant is observably `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING` when the create returns and is never left sitting at `PROSPECT`. An applicant in `BARRED` cannot be screened at all: the attempt is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_BARRED`.

A fresh check is born `PENDING_VERIFIER` with `resubmission_count` at 0 and `verifier_outcome`, `verifier_reason_code`, `matched_benefit`, `confirmed_at` and `expires_at` all null. The check's own `status` is one of `PENDING_VERIFIER`, `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`, `ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMED` or `INELIGIBLE`.

Only the `NationalVerifier` role may post a determination, and only onto a check that is still `PENDING_VERIFIER` — a second determination on the same check is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CHECK_NOT_PENDING`. The determination carries `verifier_outcome`, one of `MATCH`, `NO_MATCH` or `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`, optionally a free-form `verifier_reason_code` accompanying a no-match or documentation-required outcome, and optionally `matched_benefit`, which names the program the verifier actually matched and must be supplied when the outcome is `MATCH` on a benefit-claimed check. Its permitted values are the same seven programs the applicant may claim: `SNAP`, `MEDICAID`, `SSI`, `FPHA`, `VETERANS_PENSION`, `TRIBAL_TANF` and `BIA_GENERAL_ASSISTANCE`.

The three outcomes fan out into three distinct results, each specified in full. On `MATCH` the check advances `PENDING_VERIFIER → ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMED`, `confirmed_at` is stamped with the current instant, and `expires_at` is computed as `confirmed_at` plus 90 days at the identical time of day in UTC — a certification confirmed at 13:45:30.500 on one date expires at 13:45:30.500 on the date 90 days later. A check confirmed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` therefore has an `expires_at` of `2026-10-30T00:00:00Z`, and one confirmed at `2026-08-01T13:45:30.500Z` has an `expires_at` of `2026-10-30T13:45:30.500Z`. The applicant advances `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING → VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE`, except that an applicant sitting in `DUPLICATE_HOLD` is left exactly where it is. The applicant's `eligibility_basis` is then recomputed from the most recent confirmed check: it becomes `TRIBAL_BENEFIT` when that check was benefit-claimed and the applicant's `tribal_lands_flag` is true, `BENEFIT` when it was benefit-claimed and the flag is false, `INCOME` when it was income-claimed, and `NONE` when the applicant has no confirmed check at all. On `NO_MATCH` the check advances `PENDING_VERIFIER → INELIGIBLE`, the `verifier_reason_code` is stored, and the applicant is returned `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING → PROSPECT` so that a fresh check can be opened — this screening retry loop, `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING → PROSPECT → ELIGIBILITY_PENDING`, has no bound of its own. On `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED` the check advances `PENDING_VERIFIER → DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`, the reason code is stored, and the applicant is deliberately left in `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING`.

A bounced check can be re-armed, but only by a `ProviderAgent` and only three times at most. Resubmitting documentation requires a replacement `proof_document_ref`, which overwrites the stored reference. The check must be sitting in `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`, otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_NOT_REQUESTED`; and `resubmission_count` must be below 2, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A successful resubmission returns the check `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED → PENDING_VERIFIER`, increments `resubmission_count` by one, and clears `verifier_outcome` and `verifier_reason_code` back to null so the verifier faces a clean slate. The counter therefore bounds the `PENDING_VERIFIER → DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED → PENDING_VERIFIER` cycle at two round trips, and the third attempt is where the limit bites.

When a dwelling's duplicate flag clears — by either resolution arm or by the self-resolving edge of §3.2 — every applicant held at that dwelling is released in the same request. The release is decided by a simple existence test: if the applicant holds at least one check that is `ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMED` and whose `expires_at` is greater than or equal to the current instant, the applicant moves `DUPLICATE_HOLD → VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE`; if it holds no such check, it moves `DUPLICATE_HOLD → PROSPECT`. Because the test is an existence test, several confirmed checks require no ranking between them. Either way the applicant's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by one.

Finally, repeated program failure bars an applicant permanently. `de_enrollment_count` counts that applicant's benefits that were de-enrolled for one of the four fault reasons — `NON_USAGE`, `RECERT_NON_RESPONSE`, `INELIGIBLE_ON_RECERT` or `AUDIT_INELIGIBLE`. A voluntary exit or a duplicate-household clean-up does not count. Whenever one of the applicant's benefits stops being drawn, the counter is recomputed and the applicant moves out of `ENROLLED`: to `DE_ENROLLED` when the recomputed count is still below 2, and to `BARRED` when it has reached 2 or more. `BARRED` is terminal — no further screening is possible — and the only route out of `DE_ENROLLED` other than re-application is a granted appeal, which restores the applicant to `ENROLLED` as described in §3.9.

Applicants can be read individually and listed with filters on `address_id` (useful for reviewing a duplicate cohort), on `status`, on `eligibility_basis` and on `identity_key`. An applicant's complete screening history can be listed with filters on the check's `status` and on `claimed_basis`, and any single check can be read on its own to inspect its verifier outcome and validity window.

### 3.4. Enrollment and the One-Benefit-Per-Household Guarantee

An **Enrollment** is the benefit itself, and only a `ProviderAgent` may create one — for its own carrier. The request names four rows: `subscriber_id` for the applicant, `provider_id` for the carrier that will serve the benefit, `eligibility_check_id` for the confirmed check the benefit rests on, and `certified_address_id`, which snapshots the dwelling the applicant resides in and must name the same dwelling as the applicant's own `address_id` (a mismatch is a schema-level failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). The certified dwelling is never rewritten afterwards, not even by a carrier change. The request also declares `service_type`, one of `VOICE`, `BROADBAND` or `BUNDLED`, and carries `one_per_household_attested`, the subscriber's signed one-benefit-per-household certification, which must be true. `benefit_start_date` may be supplied to backdate or forward-date the benefit and otherwise defaults to the current instant.

A benefit carries **four independent state dimensions** on the same row, and this independence is the single most important structural fact about an enrollment. `benefit_status` records whether the federal benefit is being drawn and is either `ACTIVE` or `DE_ENROLLED`. `usage_status` records the non-usage posture and is one of `PENDING_FIRST_USE`, `USING`, `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` or `CURE_EXPIRED`. `recert_status` records the annual recertification posture and is one of `NOT_DUE`, `NOTICED`, `NON_RESPONSIVE` or `RESPONDED_INELIGIBLE`. `device_status` records handset custody and is one of `AWAITING_DEVICE`, `DEVICE_ISSUED` or `RECLAIM_PENDING`. They advance on unrelated triggers; nothing about a benefit's usage posture constrains its recertification posture, and a benefit can be simultaneously `ACTIVE`, `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED`, `NOTICED` and `DEVICE_ISSUED`. A fresh enrollment initialises them to `ACTIVE`, `PENDING_FIRST_USE`, `NOT_DUE` and `AWAITING_DEVICE` respectively.

Two temporal anchors are derived from `benefit_start_date` at creation and, once set, never move. `port_freeze_until` is the date component of `benefit_start_date` plus 60 days, stamped at `00:00:00.000Z`; it governs §3.7. `recert_due_date` is the date component of `benefit_start_date` plus 365 days; it governs §3.5. Three counters start at 0 — `transfer_count`, `device_issuance_count` and `reinstatement_count` — and `audit_risk_score` is computed per §3.8. Everything else on the row starts null: `benefit_end_date`, `last_usage_at`, `non_usage_flagged_at`, `cure_deadline_at`, `recert_notice_sent_at`, `recert_response_deadline_at`, `recert_last_responded_at`, `recert_evaluated_income_cents`, `recert_income_threshold_cents`, `de_enrollment_reason`, `de_enrolled_at`, `de_enrolled_by_ref`, `appeal_deadline_at` and `last_device_issued_at`.

Creating the benefit also settles three neighbours in the same request. The applicant advances `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE → ENROLLED`. The certified dwelling's `occupied_benefit_slots` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by one. The carrier's `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by one.

**Household occupancy** is the program's central safety constraint, and it is measured over intervals rather than over counts. Each benefit occupies the interval that begins at its `benefit_start_date` and ends at its `benefit_end_date`, closed at the start and open at the end; a benefit that is still being drawn has a null `benefit_end_date` and its interval is unbounded above. A candidate enrollment is refused with `ERR_ONE_PER_HOUSEHOLD_VIOLATION` when the number of existing intervals at the certified dwelling that overlap the candidate's interval, plus the candidate itself, exceeds the dwelling's `max_permitted_benefit_slots`. Two intervals overlap when the new interval's start is strictly earlier than the existing interval's end (or the existing end is null) *and* the existing interval's start is strictly earlier than the new interval's end (or the new end is null). Crucially, this overlap test ranges over **every** benefit ever certified at the dwelling, not only the ones currently being drawn: a benefit that has ended still occupies its closed-off interval, so a household cannot backdate a second benefit into a period it has already consumed. Because the interval is open at its end, adjacency is permitted — a new benefit starting at exactly the instant a prior benefit ended is accepted, while one starting a single millisecond earlier is refused. Against a prior benefit occupying `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` to `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` at a dwelling with one permitted slot, a `benefit_start_date` of `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` is accepted and a `benefit_start_date` of `2026-08-31T23:59:59.999Z` is refused; and against a prior benefit that is still being drawn, with a null `benefit_end_date`, every candidate start is refused. Once that dwelling has been resolved as multiple independent households with a `declared_household_count` of 3, two further overlapping benefits are accepted, bringing occupancy to 3, and the fourth is refused.

`occupied_benefit_slots` answers a different question from that overlap test and the two figures diverge as soon as any benefit ends: the slot figure counts only the benefits at the dwelling that are currently `ACTIVE`, and it is recomputed on every write that changes any benefit's `benefit_status`. `max_permitted_benefit_slots` is the dwelling's `declared_household_count` when its posture is `RESOLVED_MULTI_HOUSEHOLD` and that count is non-null, and 1 in every other case — including `UNVERIFIED`, `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` and `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD`.

The remaining gates on enrollment creation are checked in the order given in §2.7. The certifying check must be `ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CHECK_NOT_CONFIRMED` — this covers checks that are still `PENDING_VERIFIER`, that are `DOCUMENTATION_REQUIRED`, or that came back `INELIGIBLE`. The certification must still be inside its validity window: an attempt at exactly `expires_at` succeeds, and one millisecond later is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CHECK_EXPIRED` — with an `expires_at` of `2026-10-30T00:00:00Z`, an enrollment at `2026-10-30T00:00:00Z` is accepted and one at `2026-10-30T00:00:00.001Z` is refused. The applicant must be `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE`, otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, which is what a `PROSPECT`, `ELIGIBILITY_PENDING`, `DUPLICATE_HOLD`, already-`ENROLLED` or `DE_ENROLLED` applicant receives. The carrier must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Then household occupancy is tested. Then the certified dwelling must not be sitting in `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`, otherwise `ERR_ADDRESS_DUPLICATE_UNRESOLVED` — an unresolved cohort must be adjudicated by a `ProgramAdministrator` before any of its dwellings can take a new benefit. Then `one_per_household_attested` must be present and true, otherwise `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422); this is the subscriber's own attestation and is a separate rule from the system-side occupancy test. Then the certified dwelling's `state_code` must appear in the carrier's `licensed_state_codes`, otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_LICENSED_IN_STATE`. Finally the carrier's `active_enrollment_count` plus one must not exceed its `enrollment_cap`, otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_ENROLLMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`; this is an aggregate check in which each individual benefit is perfectly valid and only the collective breaches the ceiling.

A benefit can also be stopped deliberately. A `ProviderAgent` for the owning carrier, or a `ProgramAdministrator`, de-enrolls a benefit by supplying `de_enrollment_reason`, which on this route must be either `SUBSCRIBER_REQUEST` or `DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD`, plus an optional free-form `note`. The full set of reasons the field can ever hold is `NON_USAGE`, `RECERT_NON_RESPONSE`, `INELIGIBLE_ON_RECERT`, `DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD`, `SUBSCRIBER_REQUEST` and `AUDIT_INELIGIBLE`; the other four are written only by the automated and audit routes described later. A benefit that is already `DE_ENROLLED` cannot be de-enrolled again — the second attempt is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ALREADY_DE_ENROLLED` rather than silently rewriting `benefit_end_date`.

This is the **canonical de-enrollment cascade**, and every other route that stops a benefit fires exactly the same one. `benefit_status` moves `ACTIVE → DE_ENROLLED`. `benefit_end_date` and `de_enrolled_at` are both stamped with the current instant, `de_enrolled_by_ref` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and `de_enrollment_reason` is written. `appeal_deadline_at` is recomputed as the date component of `de_enrolled_at` plus 30 days, stamped at `23:59:59.999Z`. The certified dwelling's `occupied_benefit_slots` is recomputed, and if it reaches 0 while that dwelling is `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`, the dwelling self-resolves to `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD` and releases every applicant held there, exactly as described in §3.2. The carrier's `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by one. The applicant's `de_enrollment_count` is recomputed and the applicant moves `ENROLLED → DE_ENROLLED` or `ENROLLED → BARRED` per §3.3. If the benefit was holding a handset — `device_status` is `DEVICE_ISSUED` — then `device_status` moves to `RECLAIM_PENDING`, the handset itself moves `ISSUED → RECLAIM_PENDING`, the carrier's `devices_issued_count` and `devices_reclaim_pending_count` are recomputed and its `version` incremented by one. Finally `audit_risk_score` is recomputed.

Benefits can be read individually, with all four dimensions visible at once, and listed with filters on `subscriber_id`, `provider_id`, `eligibility_check_id`, `certified_address_id` and on any of the four state dimensions independently — `benefit_status`, `usage_status`, `recert_status` and `device_status`.

### 3.5. Ongoing Compliance: Usage, the Cure Period and Annual Recertification

Two obligations run for the whole life of a benefit. The subscriber must actually use the service, and the subscriber must re-attest eligibility once a year. Both are policed by a single per-carrier **compliance sweep**, and both can end the benefit.

**Recording usage.** A `ProviderAgent` for the owning carrier, or a `ProgramAdministrator`, records subscriber activity against an active benefit. The request may carry `usage_at`, the instant the activity occurred, and `usage_kind`, a free-form label for it; both are optional. When `usage_at` is supplied, `last_usage_at` takes that value, otherwise it takes the current instant. The activity anchor only ever moves forward: an event carrying an explicit `usage_at` earlier than the anchor already recorded is still accepted, but the anchor is not rewound. Recording usage against a benefit whose `benefit_status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Usage drives the second state dimension. A first activity moves `usage_status` from `PENDING_FIRST_USE` to `USING`. Further activity on a benefit already `USING` is a self-edge: the posture stays `USING`, the anchor advances, and the row's `version` still increments by one. Activity on a benefit sitting in `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` is a **cure**: provided the current instant is less than or equal to `cure_deadline_at`, the posture returns `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED → USING`, `last_usage_at` is set to the current instant, and both `non_usage_flagged_at` and `cure_deadline_at` are cleared to null. An activity arriving after the cure deadline has passed but before the next sweep has run cannot retroactively cure: it is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_EXPIRED`. Every usage write recomputes `audit_risk_score`.

**Detecting non-usage.** The sweep computes an activity anchor for each active benefit: `last_usage_at` when it is non-null, and otherwise the benefit's `benefit_start_date`, so a benefit that has never been used is measured from the day it started. Idle time is the elapsed time from that anchor to the current instant, divided by one day and truncated to a whole number of days. A benefit whose `usage_status` is `PENDING_FIRST_USE` or `USING` and whose idle days have reached 30 is flagged: `usage_status` moves to `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED`, `non_usage_flagged_at` is stamped with the current instant, and `cure_deadline_at` is set to the date component of `non_usage_flagged_at` plus 15 days, stamped at `23:59:59.999Z`. A sweep run when the truncated idle count is still 29 changes nothing. For a benefit that started at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and has never been used, a sweep at `2026-08-30T23:59:59Z` computes 29 idle days and does not flag it, while a sweep at `2026-08-31T00:00:00Z` computes 30 and flags it with a `cure_deadline_at` of `2026-09-15T23:59:59.999Z`. Curing resets the anchor, so a cured benefit cannot be flagged again until another 30 idle days have accumulated from the cure instant.

**Expiring the cure.** A benefit sitting in `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` whose `cure_deadline_at` is strictly behind the current instant advances `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED → CURE_EXPIRED`, and because `usage_status` became `CURE_EXPIRED` the benefit stops being drawn: `benefit_status` moves `ACTIVE → DE_ENROLLED` with `de_enrollment_reason` set to `NON_USAGE`, `benefit_end_date` and `de_enrolled_at` stamped with the current instant, `de_enrolled_by_ref` set to the sweeping operator's `X-User-ID`, `appeal_deadline_at` recomputed, and the whole canonical de-enrollment cascade of §3.4 — dwelling occupancy and possible self-resolution, carrier counters, applicant status, handset reclaim — fired in the same request. A sweep at the instant exactly equal to `cure_deadline_at` does *not* expire the benefit; expiry needs a strictly later instant. Continuing the example above: a usage event at `2026-09-15T23:59:59.999Z` cures the benefit back to `USING`, a sweep at that same instant does not expire it, and a sweep at `2026-09-16T00:00:00Z` does — and had the cure landed at `2026-09-15T23:59:59.999Z`, the next flag could not occur before `2026-10-15T23:59:59.999Z`, because curing resets the anchor.

**The annual cycle.** `recert_due_date` is set at enrollment to the date component of `benefit_start_date` plus 365 days. The notice window opens 60 days before that date: once the current instant has reached or passed `recert_due_date` minus 60 days, a benefit whose `recert_status` is `NOT_DUE` advances `NOT_DUE → NOTICED`, `recert_notice_sent_at` is stamped with the current instant, and `recert_response_deadline_at` is set to the date component of the current instant plus 60 days, stamped at `23:59:59.999Z`. A sweep one second before the window opens leaves the posture at `NOT_DUE`. If the response deadline passes without a response, the sweep advances `NOTICED → NON_RESPONSIVE`, and because `recert_status` became `NON_RESPONSIVE` the benefit stops being drawn with `de_enrollment_reason` set to `RECERT_NON_RESPONSE`.

That non-response path is the clearest illustration of how deeply a single sweep step reaches, and all four of its links must land in the same request. Link one sets `recert_status` to `NON_RESPONSIVE`. Link two, caused by link one, moves `benefit_status` from `ACTIVE` to `DE_ENROLLED`, writes `de_enrollment_reason` as `RECERT_NON_RESPONSE`, stamps `benefit_end_date` and `de_enrolled_at` with the current instant, and records the sweeping operator in `de_enrolled_by_ref`. Link three, caused by link two, recomputes the certified dwelling's `occupied_benefit_slots`; when that figure reaches 0 while the dwelling is `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`, the dwelling's posture moves to `RESOLVED_SINGLE_HOUSEHOLD` and its `version` increments by one. Link four, caused by link three, releases every applicant at that dwelling who was sitting in `DUPLICATE_HOLD` — to `VERIFIED_ELIGIBLE` where an unexpired confirmed check exists and to `PROSPECT` where none does — each with `version` incremented by one. Alongside those four links the carrier's `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed, the applicant moves to `DE_ENROLLED` or `BARRED`, and any issued handset is reclaimed, exactly as on the manual route.

**Answering the notice.** Only the `Subscriber` role may recertify, and only for its own benefit. The attestation carries a fresh `household_size`, a fresh `annual_income_cents`, a fresh `last_3mo_income_cents`, and `attested_qualifying_benefit` drawn from the same value set as `qualifying_benefit` — `NONE`, `SNAP`, `MEDICAID`, `SSI`, `FPHA`, `VETERANS_PENSION`, `TRIBAL_TANF` or `BIA_GENERAL_ASSISTANCE` — plus an optional `attestation_ref`. The benefit must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A notice must actually be outstanding: a benefit whose `recert_status` is `NOT_DUE`, `NON_RESPONSIVE` or `RESPONDED_INELIGIBLE` is refused with `ERR_RECERT_NOT_NOTICED`, because a subscriber cannot recertify before the sweep has issued the notice. And the response must be inside the window: an instant strictly past `recert_response_deadline_at` is refused with `ERR_RECERT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, while an instant exactly equal to it is accepted.

Recertification uses a **different income arithmetic** from initial screening, and this is deliberate. Initial screening takes `annual_income_cents` verbatim; recertification instead annualizes the trailing quarter, computing `recert_evaluated_income_cents` as `last_3mo_income_cents` multiplied by 4, rounded half up to a whole cent. `recert_income_threshold_cents` is a fresh snapshot of the §2.11 threshold taken with the *attested* `household_size` and the applicant's `tribal_lands_flag`. The attestation qualifies when `recert_evaluated_income_cents` is less than or equal to `recert_income_threshold_cents`, or when `attested_qualifying_benefit` is anything other than `NONE` — a benefit attestation short-circuits the income arm entirely and neither income variant is consulted. Because the two variants read different fields, the same household can pass one and fail the other: a household with a large one-off receipt early in the year annualizes badly under the strict reading but comfortably under the trailing-quarter reading, and a household whose income has just risen does the reverse.

Both arms of the response write the same facts before they diverge. The applicant's `household_size`, `annual_income_cents`, `last_3mo_income_cents` and `qualifying_benefit` are overwritten from the attestation and the applicant's `income_threshold_cents` is recomputed; `recert_evaluated_income_cents` and `recert_income_threshold_cents` are written onto the benefit. On the **eligible arm** the benefit then returns `NOTICED → NOT_DUE`, `recert_last_responded_at` is stamped with the current instant, `recert_notice_sent_at` and `recert_response_deadline_at` are cleared to null, and `recert_due_date` is re-anchored to the date component of the current instant plus 365 days. The cycle re-anchors on the *response* date rather than on the previous due date, so a late-but-in-window response shifts every future cycle forward. On the **ineligible arm** — reached when `recert_evaluated_income_cents` is strictly greater than `recert_income_threshold_cents` *and* `attested_qualifying_benefit` is `NONE` — `recert_status` advances `NOTICED → RESPONDED_INELIGIBLE`, which is a terminal posture for that dimension, and because it became `RESPONDED_INELIGIBLE` the benefit stops being drawn with `de_enrollment_reason` set to `INELIGIBLE_ON_RECERT`, firing the same dwelling, carrier, applicant and handset cascade as the manual route.

**The sweep itself.** Only a `ProgramAdministrator` may run it, it takes no request body, and it operates on exactly one carrier's benefits: those whose carrier reference is the addressed carrier and whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` at the moment each step is evaluated. Benefits are visited in `benefit_start_date` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — never in identifier order. For each benefit the four steps run in this fixed order, and each step re-reads the state the previous step has just written: step one detects non-usage and flags it; step two expires an already-open cure and de-enrolls for non-usage; step three issues the recertification notice; step four de-enrolls for recertification non-response.

Two consequences of that ordering are load-bearing. Because step one writes a `cure_deadline_at` that lies in the future, a benefit flagged during this sweep can never be expired by step two of the same sweep. And because step two may stop the benefit, steps three and four skip a row it de-enrolled: a benefit de-enrolled for non-usage in this sweep never appears among the newly noticed benefits. The sweep answers with `swept_at` (the instant it ran), `evaluated_enrollment_count` (the number of benefits that were `ACTIVE` when the sweep began), and four arrays of benefit identifiers listing, in visit order, the benefits newly flagged for non-usage in `non_usage_flagged_enrollment_ids`, those de-enrolled for an expired cure in `cure_expired_de_enrolled_enrollment_ids`, those newly noticed for recertification in `recert_noticed_enrollment_ids`, and those de-enrolled for recertification non-response in `recert_non_response_de_enrolled_enrollment_ids`. Running the sweep a second time at the same logical instant adds nothing to any array; only advancing the clock moves rows on.

### 3.6. Handset Issuance, Reissue Control and Returns

A benefit is born `AWAITING_DEVICE` on its fourth state dimension, and a handset is what moves it forward. A `ProviderAgent` for the owning carrier — or a `ProgramAdministrator` — issues one. The request body carries at most `device_id`, and even that is optional; the response is the handset that was issued, not the benefit.

**Choosing the handset.** When `device_id` is omitted the system selects one itself, from the pool of handsets whose owning carrier is the benefit's carrier and whose `status` is `IN_STOCK`. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_NO_DEVICE_IN_STOCK`. Otherwise the pool is ranked by `model_tier` ascending along the fixed ladder `BASIC`, then `STANDARD`, then `SMART`; ties broken by `unit_cost_cents` ascending; and remaining ties broken by `created_at` ascending as the final key. The row identifier never participates in the ranking. Rank one is issued. The tier key dominates cost, which is the point of the policy: given a pool of one `SMART` handset at 4500 cents, a `BASIC` one at 3900 cents, two `BASIC` handsets at 2100 cents each, and a `STANDARD` one at 1200 cents, the ranking is the older 2100-cent `BASIC`, then the newer 2100-cent `BASIC`, then the 3900-cent `BASIC`, then the 1200-cent `STANDARD`, then the `SMART` — so the cheapest handset in the pool is the fourth choice, and the two identical `BASIC` handsets are separated only by `created_at`. When `device_id` *is* supplied the named handset must exist, or the request fails with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`; it must belong to the benefit's carrier, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_DEVICE_IN_STOCK`, since a handset in another carrier's warehouse is not stock this carrier can draw on; and it must itself be `IN_STOCK`, or the request fails with `ERR_DEVICE_ALREADY_ISSUED`.

**The reissue freeze.** Lifeline handsets are a recurring fraud target, so a benefit that has already been given a handset is frozen against getting another. When `last_device_issued_at` is null there is no freeze at all. Otherwise the frozen interval runs from `last_device_issued_at` to `last_device_issued_at` plus 395 days — 365 freeze days plus a 30-day reissue buffer — and, unlike every other window in this system, it is **closed at both ends**. A request whose current instant falls anywhere in that interval, *including exactly on the upper boundary*, is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_FREEZE_ACTIVE`; only a strictly later instant is accepted. With `last_device_issued_at` at `2026-06-05T00:00:00Z` the boundary is `2027-07-05T00:00:00Z`: a request at `2027-07-04T23:59:59.999Z` is refused, a request at exactly `2027-07-05T00:00:00Z` is also refused, and a request at `2027-07-05T00:00:00.001Z` succeeds.

The freeze is waived when the handset most recently issued to this benefit is no longer usable by the subscriber, which the system reads off that handset's own custody record: a `status` of `RECLAIM_PENDING` waives the freeze, and so does a `status` of `RETIRED` or `LOST` whose `return_condition` is `DAMAGED` or `LOST`. A handset returned in `GOOD` condition — which re-enters stock — does **not** waive the freeze, because the subscriber gave back a working handset and has no need of a replacement. So an issuance one day after the previous one succeeds when the prior handset sits in `RECLAIM_PENDING`, succeeds when it was returned `DAMAGED` and retired, and is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_FREEZE_ACTIVE` when it was returned `GOOD`.

**One live handset per person.** Independently of the freeze, and regardless of which benefit or carrier is involved, a subscriber may hold only one outstanding handset. If any handset whose `issued_enrollment_id` points at *any* benefit of the same applicant currently has `status` of `ISSUED` or `RECLAIM_PENDING`, the request is refused with `ERR_SINGLE_DEVICE_PER_SUBSCRIBER`. This rule spans benefits and carriers, so it catches an applicant who was de-enrolled at one carrier without returning the handset and has since re-enrolled at another. The two rules interact deliberately: `RECLAIM_PENDING` *waives* the freeze but still trips the one-handset rule, so a re-enrolled applicant must physically return the old handset before a replacement can be issued — only a return clears both.

**The gate order.** A device-issuance request is evaluated in this order: the benefit must exist; the calling agent must own it; the benefit must be `ACTIVE` on its `benefit_status`, or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the benefit's `device_status` must be `AWAITING_DEVICE`, so a benefit already in `DEVICE_ISSUED` or `RECLAIM_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_ALREADY_ISSUED`; then the freeze; then the one-handset rule; and only last the availability of stock. That last ordering is the paired precedence recorded in §2.7: a frozen benefit at a carrier with an empty warehouse reports `ERR_DEVICE_FREEZE_ACTIVE`, never `ERR_NO_DEVICE_IN_STOCK`.

**What issuance writes.** The chosen handset moves `IN_STOCK → ISSUED`, its `issued_enrollment_id` is set to the benefit and its `issued_at` is stamped with the current instant. The benefit moves `AWAITING_DEVICE → DEVICE_ISSUED`, its `last_device_issued_at` is stamped with the current instant, its `device_issuance_count` is recomputed as the number of handsets that have ever been issued to it — which rises by one — and its `audit_risk_score` is recomputed. The carrier's `devices_in_stock_count` and `devices_issued_count` are recomputed and its `version` advances by one. `devices_total_count` does not move: issuance transfers custody, it does not create or destroy hardware.

**Returns.** A `ProviderAgent` for the owning carrier — or a `ProgramAdministrator` — records a return against the handset itself, supplying the mandatory `return_condition`. Only a handset that is out with a subscriber can be returned: a `status` outside `ISSUED` and `RECLAIM_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_ISSUED`, so returning a handset that is already `IN_STOCK`, `RETIRED` or `LOST` fails. `returned_at` is stamped with the current instant and `return_condition` is recorded, and the condition then decides custody: `GOOD` sends the handset back to `IN_STOCK`, where it is available to the selector again; `DAMAGED` sends it to `RETIRED`; and `LOST` sends it to `LOST`. `RETIRED` and `LOST` are both terminal — nothing returns a handset from either — and both are counted together by `devices_retired_count`. The benefit that held the handset moves its `device_status` to `AWAITING_DEVICE` whichever condition was recorded, so it is eligible for another handset once the freeze permits, and the carrier's handset counters are recomputed with its `version` advanced by one.

That closes the custody cycle in two shapes. The ordinary shape is `IN_STOCK → ISSUED → IN_STOCK`, a `GOOD` return by a still-active subscriber; the reclaim shape is `IN_STOCK → ISSUED → RECLAIM_PENDING → IN_STOCK`, where the middle step is the reclaim cascade that fires whenever a benefit stops being drawn or is ported away, and the last step is the eventual physical return. A `RECLAIM_PENDING` handset returned `GOOD` re-enters stock; returned `DAMAGED` or `LOST` it retires. In every one of those transitions `devices_total_count` is unchanged, because no route in the system deletes a handset row; and across all of them the conservation identity of §2.10 holds after each write. A carrier with three handsets in stock reads 3 / 0 / 0 / 0 with a total of 3; after one issuance, 2 / 1 / 0 / 0 with a total of 3; after that benefit is de-enrolled and the handset reclaimed, 2 / 0 / 1 / 0 with a total of 3; after the handset comes back `DAMAGED`, 2 / 0 / 0 / 1 with a total of 3.

Note that `issued_enrollment_id` is retained after a reclaim, a retirement or a loss rather than cleared, which is what keeps `device_issuance_count` monotone: a benefit that has been given three handsets over its life reports three forever, and the audit risk weighting of §3.8 reads that history, not the current custody state.

### 3.7. Porting a Benefit Between Carriers

A subscriber may move a live benefit from its current carrier to a gaining carrier without giving up the benefit itself. The move is a two-sided handshake modelled as a **ServiceTransfer**: the gaining carrier opens the request, and the gaining carrier also decides it. The losing carrier is never asked; it is simply informed by the consequences.

**Opening the request.** A `ProviderAgent` supplies `enrollment_id`, the benefit being ported; `target_provider_id`, the carrier taking it over; `subscriber_consent_ref`, a reference of 1 to 64 characters to the recorded subscriber consent; and `expected_enrollment_version`, the version of the benefit the requester believes it saw. The system snapshots `source_provider_id` from the benefit's current carrier, stamps `requested_at` with the current instant, and records in `port_freeze_until_snapshot` the exact freeze anchor it tested, so an audit can later show which anchor the port cleared. The request is born `REQUESTED`, and creating it advances the benefit's `version` by one.

Two business bars sit in front of the request. The benefit must not be inside its **porting freeze**: while the current instant is strictly earlier than the benefit's `port_freeze_until` the request is refused with `ERR_PORT_FREEZE_ACTIVE`. That window is half-open — a request at exactly `port_freeze_until` is accepted. A benefit that began on `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` has a `port_freeze_until` of `2026-09-30T00:00:00Z`, so a request at `2026-09-29T23:59:59.999Z` is refused and a request at exactly `2026-09-30T00:00:00Z` opens the port. And the port must actually go somewhere: a `target_provider_id` equal to the benefit's current carrier is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_SAME_PROVIDER`. The benefit must also be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Because creating a request advances the benefit's `version`, only one port can be in flight for a benefit at a time. When a new request is created for a benefit that already has one in `REQUESTED`, the older request moves `REQUESTED → SUPERSEDED` with its `completed_at` stamped at the current instant. This is exactly why `expected_enrollment_version` is required rather than a plain state check: the second request that arrives with the version it read *before* the first request landed is a genuine lost update, and it is refused with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION`, while a second request that reads the fresh version supersedes its predecessor legitimately.

**Accepting.** Only the gaining carrier may accept: the `X-User-ID` of the accepting `ProviderAgent` must equal `target_provider_id`. Acceptance takes an optional `acceptance_note`. The request must still be `REQUESTED`, or it is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_REQUESTED` — so a request already accepted, rejected or superseded cannot be decided again. The gaining carrier's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`; note that this bar is evaluated against the *target* carrier here, not the caller's own record in some other sense. And the gaining carrier must have room: its `active_enrollment_count` plus one must not exceed its `enrollment_cap`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_ENROLLMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The benefit must still be `ACTIVE` at acceptance time.

Acceptance moves the request `REQUESTED → ACCEPTED_COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped at the current instant, and then fires a three-link cascade whose reach is the point of the whole workflow.

Link one rewrites the benefit's carrier: its `provider_id` becomes `target_provider_id`, its `transfer_count` is recomputed and rises by one, and its `audit_risk_score` is recomputed — a ported benefit picks up the transfer weighting described in §3.8. Crucially, `benefit_start_date`, `port_freeze_until` and `recert_due_date` are **preserved verbatim**. The gaining carrier inherits the original anchors and cannot restart either clock: it cannot buy itself a fresh 60-day porting freeze, and it cannot push the annual recertification further out.

Link two, caused by the carrier change, reclaims the hardware. The handset whose `issued_enrollment_id` is this benefit and whose `status` is `ISSUED` belongs to the *losing* carrier, so it moves to `RECLAIM_PENDING`; the losing carrier's `devices_issued_count` and `devices_reclaim_pending_count` are recomputed and its `version` advances by one. The handset is not silently reassigned to the gaining carrier — hardware never changes owner.

Link three, caused by the handset entering `RECLAIM_PENDING`, resets the benefit's service posture. Its `device_status` moves to `RECLAIM_PENDING`, and its `usage_status` returns to `PENDING_FIRST_USE` with `last_usage_at`, `non_usage_flagged_at` and `cure_deadline_at` all cleared to null. The gaining carrier therefore starts a fresh 30-day non-usage window with the ported benefit and must get the subscriber onto its own network — a benefit that ports and then sits idle is flagged for non-usage against the *gaining* carrier. Alongside the three links, `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed on both carriers and both carrier `version` values advance by one.

The combination of link three with §3.6 is deliberate: the ported benefit is now `AWAITING_DEVICE`-eligible only once the reclaimed handset comes back, because `RECLAIM_PENDING` waives the reissue freeze but still trips the one-handset-per-subscriber rule.

**Rejecting.** The gaining carrier may instead decline, again as the `ProviderAgent` whose `X-User-ID` equals `target_provider_id`, supplying a mandatory `rejection_reason` of `CONSENT_INVALID`, `TARGET_CAPACITY` or `OTHER`, and an optional `rejection_note`. The request moves `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, the reason is stored, and `completed_at` is stamped with the current instant. The benefit is left **completely untouched**: same carrier, same handset, same usage posture, same `version`. A rejection is a decision about a request, not an event in the life of a benefit.

All three of `ACCEPTED_COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal, and a request in any of them refuses both decision routes with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_REQUESTED`. Port requests can be read individually and listed with filters on `enrollment_id`, on `source_provider_id`, on `target_provider_id` and on `status`, so either side of a port can find its own traffic; reads are open to all five roles.

### 3.8. Monthly Subsidy Claims and Audit Sampling

Carriers are reimbursed one calendar month at a time. A **ReimbursementClaim** is priced twice — provisionally when it is drafted and finally when it is filed — and a filed claim may then be sampled and adjudicated by a compliance auditor, with money clawed back when a sample fails.

**Drafting.** A `ProviderAgent` opens a draft with `provider_id`, the carrier claiming subsidy; `service_month`, the month of service the claim covers in `YYYY-MM` form; and `expected_provider_version`. The draft is born `DRAFT`, and `final_amount_cents`, `variance_cents`, `recoupment_amount_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `audit_sample_count` and `failed_sample_count` all initialise to 0. The carrier's `open_claim_count` is recomputed and its `version` advances by one.

A draft may be opened before the month has closed, which is exactly why it is only provisional. It is blocked in two situations. If any claim of the carrier sits in `AUDIT_FAILED` — equivalently, while the carrier's `audit_hold_flag` is true — the draft is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_AUDIT_HOLD`: no new money may be drafted while an audit failure is outstanding. And if another claim of the same carrier already covers the same `service_month` with a status outside `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` — that is, one already `FILED`, `HELD`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `AUDIT_PASSED` or `AUDIT_FAILED` — the draft is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_MONTH`. A pre-existing *draft* does not block: it moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` instead. A suspended carrier cannot draft at all: `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

That supersede is what makes `expected_provider_version` a genuine race guard rather than a state check. A new draft prices itself from the carrier's ledger figures and destroys the previous draft, so a writer holding a stale view of the carrier would discard a draft priced from numbers it never read; such a writer is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION`.

**Pricing a claim.** Both pricings share a per-benefit monthly rate and a per-benefit day count. The monthly rate starts at 925 cents, adds 2500 cents when the subscriber's `tribal_lands_flag` is true, and adds a further 275 cents when the benefit's `service_type` is `BROADBAND` or `BUNDLED`. The four rates that result are: non-tribal `VOICE` 925, non-tribal `BROADBAND` 1200, tribal `VOICE` 3425, and tribal `BROADBAND` or `BUNDLED` 3700. Each selected benefit then contributes a line equal to the monthly rate multiplied by its support days, divided by the number of calendar days in the service month, rounded half up to a whole cent.

**The rounding order is pinned and observable.** Every line is rounded to an integer *first*, and the integer lines are *then* summed. Summing the exact rational products and rounding the total once is incorrect. For a 30-day service month with two identical non-tribal `VOICE` benefits each earning 16 support days, each line is 925 × 16 ÷ 30 = 493.333…, which rounds to 493, and the claim amount is 493 + 493 = 986. The forbidden sum-then-round path would produce 987. A selected benefit with fewer than 1 support day contributes no line at all and is not counted in `covered_enrollment_count`.

**The provisional pricing**, stored in `provisional_amount_cents`, selects the carrier's benefits whose `benefit_status` is `ACTIVE` at the instant the draft is created and whose `benefit_start_date` is on or before the earlier of today's date and the last day of the service month. Support days run from the later of `benefit_start_date` and the first day of the month, to the earlier of today's date and the last day of the month, counting whole calendar days inclusive at both ends. A snapshot taken mid-month therefore prices only the month so far.

**The final pricing**, stored in `final_amount_cents`, uses a wider and differently shaped selection: every benefit of the carrier that had at least one day of `ACTIVE` benefit inside the service month, *including* benefits de-enrolled mid-month, whose `benefit_end_date` falls inside the month, and *including* benefits that arrived at this carrier through an `ACCEPTED_COMPLETED` port during the month. Support days run from the later of `benefit_start_date` and the first day of the month, to the earlier of the last day of the month and — when `benefit_end_date` falls inside the month — the day before the date of `benefit_end_date`, again inclusive at both ends. A still-active benefit earns the full remaining month.

The two pricings diverge substantially, and `variance_cents`, written at file time as `final_amount_cents` minus `provisional_amount_cents`, is the observable difference. Take a 30-day service month of `2026-06` with two benefits at one carrier: E1, non-tribal `VOICE`, started `2026-01-10`, still active; and E2, tribal `BROADBAND`, started `2026-06-21`, still active. A draft created at `2026-06-16T00:00:00Z` gives E1 16 support days for a line of 493, excludes E2 because it has not yet started, and records `provisional_amount_cents` 493 with `covered_enrollment_count` 1. Filed at `2026-07-02T00:00:00Z`, E1 earns the full 30 days for a line of 925 and E2 earns 10 days for 3700 × 10 ÷ 30 = 1233.333…, rounding to 1233; `final_amount_cents` is 925 + 1233 = 2158, `covered_enrollment_count` is 2, and `variance_cents` is 1665. A mid-month de-enrollment shows the other half of the divergence: a non-tribal `VOICE` benefit that started `2026-06-01` and ended at `2026-06-11T09:00:00Z` earns days `2026-06-01` through `2026-06-10`, that is 10 days, for a line of 308 — and it *is* included by the final pricing even though it is not active at file time.

**Filing.** A `ProviderAgent` for the owning carrier certifies the claim with `certifying_officer_ref`. The claim must be `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DRAFT` — which is what a second filing, or a filing of a superseded draft, receives. The month must be closed: while today's date is on or before the last calendar day of `service_month` the request is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_MONTH_NOT_CLOSED`, which forces the switch from provisional to final pricing to happen only once the month is complete. The carrier must be `ACTIVE` and must not be under audit hold, or `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_PROVIDER_AUDIT_HOLD` respectively. Filing recomputes `final_amount_cents` and `covered_enrollment_count` under the final pricing, writes `variance_cents`, stores `certifying_officer_ref`, stamps `filed_at` with the current instant, and moves the claim `DRAFT → FILED`. The carrier's `claim_gross_cents`, `net_reimbursed_cents` and `open_claim_count` are then recomputed and its `version` advances by one.

**Risk scoring.** Before sampling can be understood, the benefit-level risk score must be. `audit_risk_score` is recomputed on every write that touches a benefit's `usage_status`, `recert_status`, `device_issuance_count`, `transfer_count` or the status of its certified dwelling. It is the sum of five weights: 40 when the certified dwelling's status is `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED`; 30 when `usage_status` is `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` or `CURE_EXPIRED`; 20 when `recert_status` is `NON_RESPONSIVE` or `RESPONDED_INELIGIBLE`; 10 for each handset ever issued, counting `device_issuance_count` but capped at 2 handsets, so at most 20; and 15 when `transfer_count` is 1 or more. The total is then clamped to a ceiling of 100, and the clamp is applied **last**, after all five weights are added. Because the unclamped maximum is 125, the clamp is reachable: a benefit at a `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` dwelling that is `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED`, `NON_RESPONSIVE`, has had two handsets and one port scores 40 + 30 + 20 + 20 + 15 = 125 and reports 100. A fresh benefit at a clean dwelling with no handset scores 0; one handset scores 10; two score 20; three still score 20 because the handset cap binds; `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` with one handset scores 40; and a `DUPLICATE_FLAGGED` dwelling with `NON_USAGE_FLAGGED` and two handsets scores 90.

**Drawing the sample.** A `ComplianceAuditor` draws a sample from a filed claim, supplying `sample_size`. The claim must be `FILED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FILED` — which is also what a `HELD` claim returns, so a carrier under audit hold cannot have a second draw opened, as well as what a `DRAFT`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `AUDIT_PASSED` or `AUDIT_FAILED` claim returns. The candidate pool is exactly the set the final pricing selected: the benefits that contributed a line to `final_amount_cents`. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_SAMPLE_POOL` and no sample row is created. `sample_size` must be between 1 and 50 inclusive, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SAMPLE_SIZE`; a `sample_size` of 0 and a `sample_size` of 51 both fail. A `sample_size` larger than the pool is **not** an error: the effective size is the smaller of `sample_size` and the pool count, so an over-large request is clamped.

Candidates are ranked by `audit_risk_score` descending, then `benefit_start_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break. The row identifier never participates. Given candidates A (score 100, started `2026-03-01`), B (score 100, started `2026-01-15`), C (score 40, started `2025-11-02`) and D (score 100, started `2026-01-15` but created after B), the order is B, D, A, C: the three score-100 rows sort by start date ascending, and B beats D only on `created_at`. A `sample_size` of 2 over that pool draws B at rank 1 and D at rank 2; a `sample_size` of 9 is clamped to all 4 rows.

The draw creates one **AuditSample** per selected benefit, each born `DRAWN` with `claim_id` and `enrollment_id` set, `selection_rank` assigned 1-based in ranking order, `drawn_by_ref` recording the drawing auditor, `risk_score_at_draw` recording the benefit's `audit_risk_score` at that moment, and `claimed_line_cents` recording that benefit's line inside `final_amount_cents` — so a later recomputation of the score cannot rewrite audit history. The claim moves `FILED → UNDER_AUDIT`, its `audit_sample_count` is recomputed, and `sample_drawn_by_ref` records the drawing auditor's `X-User-ID`. Note that the `claimed_line_cents` of *all* candidates sum to `final_amount_cents`, while those of the drawn subset do not — the recoupment arithmetic below extrapolates from a rate rather than from the drawn cents, deliberately.

**Adjudicating.** A `ComplianceAuditor` records a `finding` on one sample with an optional `finding_note`. The sample must be `DRAWN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, and the owning claim must be `UNDER_AUDIT`. The adjudicator must not be the auditor who drew the sample: an `X-User-ID` equal to the claim's `sample_drawn_by_ref` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every adjudication stores `finding` and `adjudicated_by_ref`.

The two arms of adjudication have deliberately different reach. A `finding` of `COMPLIANT` moves the sample `DRAWN → ADJUDICATED_PASS` and sets `disallowed_support_cents` to 0. The claim advances to `AUDIT_PASSED` only when this adjudication leaves no sample of the claim in `DRAWN` **and** `failed_sample_count` is 0 — it is a join, waiting for every sample. On that arm `recoupment_amount_cents` and `penalty_cents` are 0, `net_payable_cents` equals `final_amount_cents`, the carrier's `net_reimbursed_cents` and `open_claim_count` are recomputed with its `version` advanced by one, and `audit_hold_flag` is untouched. There is no hold, no recoupment and no knock-on effect on other claims.

Any `finding` other than `COMPLIANT` — `INELIGIBLE_SUBSCRIBER`, `DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD`, `NON_USAGE_UNCURED` or `MISSING_CERTIFICATION` — moves the sample `DRAWN → ADJUDICATED_FAIL`, sets `disallowed_support_cents` to that sample's `claimed_line_cents`, and **fails fast**: it does not wait for the remaining samples. The claim's `failed_sample_count` is recomputed and, because it has become 1 or more, the claim moves `UNDER_AUDIT → AUDIT_FAILED` immediately with its recoupment figures computed. That is cascade link one. Link two, caused by that status write, recomputes the carrier's `recouped_cents` and `net_reimbursed_cents`, sets `audit_hold_flag` to true, and advances the carrier's `version` by one. Link three, caused by the hold flag becoming true, moves **every other claim of that carrier** whose status is `FILED` to `HELD`; those claims are not repriced and `claim_gross_cents` still counts them. Additionally, a `finding` of `INELIGIBLE_SUBSCRIBER`, `DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD` or `MISSING_CERTIFICATION` de-enrolls the sampled benefit, moving `benefit_status` from `ACTIVE` to `DE_ENROLLED` with `de_enrollment_reason` set to `AUDIT_INELIGIBLE` and firing the full de-enrollment cascade of §3.4. A `finding` of `NON_USAGE_UNCURED` does **not** de-enroll: it disallows the money without ending the benefit, because the usage machinery of §3.5 owns that decision.

**Computing the clawback.** The recoupment arithmetic has a pinned evaluation order, and getting the order wrong produces a wrong answer rather than a rounding discrepancy. Step one computes the sample error rate as `failed_sample_count` divided by `audit_sample_count`, kept as an **exact rational** — never rounded, never stored. Step two computes the extrapolated error as `final_amount_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. Step three selects the penalty rate **from the extrapolated figure**, not from `final_amount_cents`: 0.05 while the extrapolated error is below 500 cents, 0.10 while it is at least 500 and below 1500, and 0.20 once it reaches 1500. Step four computes `penalty_cents` as the extrapolated error multiplied by the selected rate, rounded half up. Step five sets `recoupment_amount_cents` to the extrapolated error plus `penalty_cents`. Step six sets `net_payable_cents` to `final_amount_cents` minus `recoupment_amount_cents`. Rounding happens at step two and again at step four, nowhere else. When `failed_sample_count` is 0, `recoupment_amount_cents` and `penalty_cents` are 0 and `net_payable_cents` equals `final_amount_cents`.

The tier cut-offs sit in the low hundreds of cents on purpose, because a month of Lifeline support is worth 925 to 3700 cents per subscriber, so a realistic claim straddles them and the ordering is observable. With `final_amount_cents` 2158, `audit_sample_count` 2 and `failed_sample_count` 1, the rate is one half, the extrapolated error is 1079, the tier read from 1079 is 0.10, the penalty is 107.9 rounding to 108, the recoupment is 1187 and `net_payable_cents` is 971. Reading the tier off `final_amount_cents` instead — 2158 is at least 1500, so 0.20 — would give a penalty of 216, a recoupment of 1295 and a net payable of 863, and is wrong. Three further worked cases pin the rest: `final_amount_cents` 986 with 1 of 2 samples failed extrapolates to 493, which is below 500 so the rate is 0.05, the penalty is 24.65 rounding to 25, the recoupment is 518 and the net payable is 468. `final_amount_cents` 1850 with 1 of 2 failed extrapolates to 925, the rate is 0.10, and the penalty is exactly 92.5, which rounds half **up** to 93 — banker's rounding would give 92 — for a recoupment of 1018 and a net payable of 832. `final_amount_cents` 2158 with 2 of 3 failed keeps the rate as exactly two thirds, extrapolating to 1438.666… which rounds to 1439; 1439 is below 1500 so the rate is 0.10, the penalty is 143.9 rounding to 144, the recoupment is 1583 and the net payable is 575 — rounding the rate to 0.67 first would extrapolate to 1446 and give a different answer throughout. At the boundaries: an extrapolated 499 takes 0.05, 500 takes 0.10, 1499 takes 0.10 and 1500 takes 0.20.

Carrier ledger writes always use the **gross** `final_amount_cents` for `claim_gross_cents` and `recoupment_amount_cents` for `recouped_cents`. `net_payable_cents` is a display figure on the claim and is never summed into any carrier counter; doing so would double-count the clawback.

Claims can be read individually, showing provisional, final and recoupment figures side by side, and listed with filters on `provider_id`, `service_month` and `status`. Audit samples can be read individually and listed with filters on `claim_id`, `enrollment_id`, `status` and `finding`. All reads are open to all five roles.

### 3.9. Complaints, Appeals and the Compensating Reinstatement

A **SubscriberCase** is the one place where a decision can be undone. It comes in two shapes, distinguished by `case_type`: a `SERVICE_COMPLAINT` about a live benefit, and a `DE_ENROLLMENT_APPEAL` against a benefit that has been stopped. The two shapes accept disjoint decisions and fire entirely different side effects.

**Filing.** A `Subscriber` — confined to its own `subscriber_id` — or a `ProviderAgent` or `ProgramAdministrator` files a case with `subscriber_id`, `enrollment_id`, `case_type` and a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters. The case is born `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` 0, `decision`, `decision_rationale`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_ref` and `reviewer_ref` all null, and `reinstated_enrollment_flag` false.

The two shapes have mirror-image preconditions on the benefit. A `SERVICE_COMPLAINT` requires the benefit's `benefit_status` to be `ACTIVE`; a `DE_ENROLLMENT_APPEAL` requires it to be `DE_ENROLLED`. Getting that backwards — appealing a live benefit, or complaining about a stopped one — is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_APPLICABLE`. An appeal must also be timely: while the current instant is strictly past the benefit's `appeal_deadline_at` the filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. That deadline is the date component of `de_enrolled_at` plus 30 days stamped at `23:59:59.999Z`, and the boundary instant is still **inside** the window, so an appeal filed at exactly `appeal_deadline_at` is accepted. Because `appeal_deadline_at` is recomputed on every de-enrollment route — manual, sweep-driven cure expiry, recertification non-response, ineligible recertification, duplicate-household reclamation and audit finding alike — every de-enrollment carries the same 30-day appeal right.

**Assigning a reviewer.** Only a `ProgramAdministrator` assigns review, supplying `reviewer_ref`. The case must be `SUBMITTED` or `REMANDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`. `reviewer_ref` is stored — rewritten, on a re-assignment after a remand — `review_round` rises by one as the count of successful assignments, and the case moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`. Review is bounded at three rounds: once `review_round` has reached 3 a further assignment is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`, so a fourth assignment on the same case always fails and the `UNDER_REVIEW → REMANDED → UNDER_REVIEW` negotiation cannot spin forever.

**Deciding.** Only a `ProgramAdministrator` decides, supplying a mandatory `decision` and `decision_rationale`. The case must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. Every decision writes `decision`, `decision_rationale`, `decided_at` at the current instant, and `decided_by_ref` with the deciding administrator's `X-User-ID`.

Two identity bars apply. On a `DE_ENROLLMENT_APPEAL` the decider's `X-User-ID` must not equal the appealed benefit's `de_enrolled_by_ref`: the operator whose write stopped the benefit may not decide the appeal against that stoppage, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Separately, once a case has been remanded at least once, the decider's `X-User-ID` must not equal the case's current `reviewer_ref`. Both bars rank above optimistic concurrency, so a conflicted identity is told about the conflict even when it also holds a stale version.

The `decision` is **polymorphic against `case_type`**, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_APPLICABLE`. `RESOLVE_WITH_CREDIT` is legal only on a `SERVICE_COMPLAINT`, moving the case to `RESOLVED` and leaving the benefit untouched. `GRANT_REINSTATE` is legal only on a `DE_ENROLLMENT_APPEAL`, and additionally requires the benefit still to be `DE_ENROLLED`. `DENY` is legal on either shape, moves the case to `DENIED`, and leaves the benefit untouched — though it still respects the `de_enrolled_by_ref` bar on an appeal. `REMAND` is also legal on either shape, moves the case `UNDER_REVIEW → REMANDED`, writes the decision fields and leaves the benefit untouched; the case can then be re-assigned, subject to the three-round cap. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `RESOLVED` are all terminal.

**The reinstatement.** A granted appeal moves the case to `GRANTED`, sets `reinstated_enrollment_flag` true, and restores the benefit. `benefit_status` returns `DE_ENROLLED → ACTIVE`, and `benefit_end_date`, `de_enrollment_reason`, `de_enrolled_at`, `de_enrolled_by_ref` and `appeal_deadline_at` are all cleared to null. `benefit_start_date` is **preserved verbatim**, which is the load-bearing detail: because the start date never moves, neither `port_freeze_until` nor `recert_due_date` moves either. A reinstated benefit inherits its original porting freeze anchor and its original annual due date — a reinstatement is not a fresh enrollment and cannot be used to buy a new cycle.

The service posture, by contrast, does reset. `usage_status` returns to `PENDING_FIRST_USE` with `last_usage_at` and `non_usage_flagged_at` cleared to null, so a fresh 30-day non-usage window opens and the subscriber must actually use the restored service. `recert_status` returns to `NOT_DUE` with `recert_notice_sent_at` and `recert_response_deadline_at` cleared to null — but since `recert_due_date` was preserved, a benefit reinstated close to its anniversary will be noticed again almost immediately. Alongside those writes, `reinstatement_count` is recomputed as the number of granted de-enrollment appeals against this benefit, the certified dwelling's `occupied_benefit_slots` is recomputed, the carrier's `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed, and the applicant's `status` returns `DE_ENROLLED → ENROLLED`.

**The audit reversal.** When the reinstated benefit's `de_enrollment_reason` was `AUDIT_INELIGIBLE`, the grant additionally unwinds the money, because the disallowance rested on a finding that has now been overturned. The newest `ADJUDICATED_FAIL` sample for that benefit — when several exist, the one with the greatest `created_at` — moves `ADJUDICATED_FAIL → ADJUDICATED_PASS` with its `finding` rewritten to `COMPLIANT` and its `disallowed_support_cents` returned to 0, and the owning claim's `failed_sample_count` is recomputed. When that recomputation brings `failed_sample_count` to 0, the claim moves `AUDIT_FAILED → AUDIT_PASSED` with `recoupment_amount_cents` and `penalty_cents` returned to 0 and `net_payable_cents` restored to `final_amount_cents`. The carrier's `recouped_cents` and `net_reimbursed_cents` are recomputed back to their pre-adjudication values and `audit_hold_flag` returns to false; and because the hold flag became false, every claim of that carrier sitting in `HELD` returns to `FILED`, closing the `FILED → HELD → FILED` cycle and re-opening the carrier's ability to draft and file.

The reversal must restore the ledger **exactly**, with no drift. Consider a carrier with two claims filed at `final_amount_cents` 1850 each: after both filings the ledger reads `claim_gross_cents` 3700, `recouped_cents` 0, `net_reimbursed_cents` 3700, `audit_hold_flag` false. A failing adjudication on the second claim, with `audit_sample_count` 2 and `failed_sample_count` 1, drives it to `AUDIT_FAILED` with `recoupment_amount_cents` 1018, and the ledger reads `claim_gross_cents` 3700, `recouped_cents` 1018, `net_reimbursed_cents` 2682, `audit_hold_flag` true — while the first claim, still `FILED`, is now `HELD`. After a granted appeal flips the failed sample to `ADJUDICATED_PASS` and the claim back to `AUDIT_PASSED`, the ledger reads `claim_gross_cents` 3700, `recouped_cents` 0, `net_reimbursed_cents` 3700, `audit_hold_flag` false, and the `HELD` claim is back to `FILED`. The post-compensation figures are identical to the pre-adjudication figures, and the ledger invariant of §2.10 holds at all three points.

Cases can be read individually with their full review history, and listed with filters on `subscriber_id`, `enrollment_id`, `case_type` and `status`. Reads are open to all five roles.
