# Dealer Floorplan Curtailment Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Floor plan lending is inventory finance with a peculiar risk profile: the lender advances cash against vehicles it does not physically hold, the collateral depreciates every day it sits on a dealer's lot, and the dealer — who is also the seller — controls both the vehicle and the paperwork. A floor plan lender therefore does not manage a loan book so much as it manages a rolling reconciliation between three ledgers that constantly drift apart: the principal it has disbursed, the vehicles it believes are still on the lot, and the manufacturer statements of origin and certificates of title it holds in its vault. When those ledgers diverge without the lender noticing, the loss is already realized — the unit has been sold, the buyer has driven away, and the sale proceeds never reached the lender. The industry calls that condition *sold out of trust*, and detecting it late is the single largest source of credit loss in the business.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that discipline end to end for a commercial floor plan lender. The service establishes a dealer's credit facility with distinct advance rates and per-unit caps for new, used and demonstrator inventory; funds individual units against their invoices only under document control; materialises a **curtailment ladder** for every funded unit so that principal is paid down as the unit ages; schedules and reconciles physical inventory audits whose frequency is driven by the dealer's own risk rating; opens out-of-trust incidents when a count and a title check disagree with the ledger; tests capital, cash-flow and out-of-trust covenants against dealer-reported financials; and drives the remedy ladder from line suspension through inventory repossession to termination when the relationship fails. Every operation is mounted under `/floorplan`, with a single additional test-driving route outside that prefix for the logical clock.

Two engineering constraints shape the whole specification and should be read before anything else. First, **every notion of "now" is a logical clock**, never wall-clock time: unit age, curtailment due dates, audit notice periods, cure deadlines and reversal windows all read a server-held instant that starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and only moves when an operator moves it. Second, **the service is a ledger, and its arithmetic is exact**: all money is integer cents, all rates are basis points, every division is rounded exactly once and in a pinned position, and a family of invariants (capacity identity, money conservation, audit count conservation) must hold after every single mutation, including every mutation reached by cascade. An implementation that is "close enough" on rounding order is wrong in a way that compounds across a portfolio.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other consideration. The six roles are `DealerPrincipal`, `CreditOfficer`, `CreditRiskManager`, `FieldAuditor`, `AuditManager` and `SystemOperator`. Each endpoint declares the roles permitted to invoke it (see §4); a syntactically valid role that is not on that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

No role is a superset of another. There is no administrative override and no escalation path: a `CreditOfficer` cannot reconcile an audit, an `AuditManager` cannot fund an advance, and a `CreditRiskManager` cannot record a physical observation. All three attempts produce `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

`DealerPrincipal` is the only **entity-backed** identity in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `DealerPrincipal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. That comparison is transitive: for a dealer resource it is the dealer's own `id`, and for everything downstream it is the `dealer_id` reachable from the resource — a unit's `dealer_id`, an obligation's `dealer_id`, an audit's `dealer_id`, and through those the advances, exception requests, observations, incidents and remittances that hang off them. A `DealerPrincipal` acting on another dealership's resource is refused with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and this applies to read traffic exactly as it applies to writes.

The other five roles carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `CreditOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (for example as `funded_by_user_id` on both the `InventoryUnit` and the `FundingAdvance` when an advance is disbursed) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CreditRiskManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` on a covenant test, as `decided_by_user_id` on an aging exception decision, and as `issued_by_user_id` or `executed_by_user_id` on a remedy action. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier written to `observed_by_user_id` on each observation and appended to the audit's `observer_user_ids` list. When `X-User-Role` is `AuditManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier written to `reconciled_by_user_id` when a count is signed off. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is never stored on any business entity at all. In each of those five cases the recorded identifier is used only for the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.9 — never for ownership filtering.

One asymmetry is worth stating plainly because it is easy to get backwards: a dealer's own `requested_by_user_id` on an aging exception request is an entity-backed value, equal to the `Dealer.id` of the owning dealership, because only a `DealerPrincipal` may file one.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than silently clamped.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, collections are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The documented exceptions are: a dealer's covenant tests are returned newest `period_end_date` first; a line's curtailment ladder rungs are returned by `age_days_from` ascending; and the projected obligation array inside a unit's curtailment-schedule read is ordered by `due_date` ascending.

Filter parameters, where an endpoint offers them, are exact-match and combine conjunctively. Across the collection endpoints these are `status`, `risk_rating`, `state_code`, `dealer_id`, `credit_line_id`, `funding_status`, `title_custody_status`, `eligibility_status`, `inventory_class`, `unit_id`, `audit_id`, `observation_id`, `supersedes_obligation_id`, `target_unit_id`, `target_obligation_id`, `due_before`, `classification`, `is_out_of_trust`, `audit_type`, `action_type`, `intent` and `result`. A filter naming a value outside its field's declared enumeration is a malformed request.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource exposes a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Most resources also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` is fixed at insertion. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations reached only by cascade from another entity's operation. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation, again including cascaded ones — a dealer whose counters are rewritten by an audit reconciliation has its `version` advanced by that reconciliation just as surely as if it had been written directly.

Three resources are deliberately immutable and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, with **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all: `CurtailmentTier` (a configuration rung of a ladder), `UnitAuditObservation` (a physical count reading, which by design cannot be corrected — a mistake is superseded by scheduling another audit, never by editing the row) and `CovenantTest` (a filed financial certification). Anything that reads or writes a `version` on one of these three is wrong.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads the host's wall clock. A server-held logical clock supplies every instant the specification refers to as "now", "today", or "the current date": unit age at funding, curtailment due dates, grace-period expiry, audit notice and gap arithmetic, audit window admission, out-of-trust cure deadlines and the advance reversal window all read it.

Before any operator moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `SystemOperator` repositions it by supplying `to_iso8601`, an arbitrary UTC instant; the response reports `current_utc` (the instant just set) and `previous_utc` (the instant that was in force before the call). Moving the clock mutates no entity — it changes only what subsequent operations consider to be the present moment.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; there are no floating-point money fields anywhere in the API. All rates, advance percentages and curtailment paydown fractions are integers denominated in basis points, where 10,000 basis points is one hundred percent — an `advance_rate_bps_new` of 9,000 is a ninety-percent advance rate, and a `cumulative_paydown_bps` of 1,000 is a ten-percent cumulative paydown.

Wherever a computation divides, the quotient is rounded **half up** and rounding happens exactly once, at the position each formula specifies. Money results are rounded half up to whole cents (zero decimal places). The two debt-service coverage ratios are the sole non-integer values in the system: each is rounded half up to exactly four decimal places, and every subsequent comparison against a covenant threshold uses the already-rounded four-decimal value rather than the raw quotient. Two rounding orders are pinned explicitly in §3 because the natural implementation gets them backwards: an advance folds both of its rate reductions together before its single division, and a portfolio-wide curtailment total rounds each obligation's requirement individually and then sums the resulting integers.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure short-circuits the request:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. **Dealer scope** — a `DealerPrincipal` may only touch its own dealership's resources (`ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`).
4. **Existence** — path segments and body references are resolved strictly left to right along the URL, and then left to right through the body in the order the endpoint declares its inputs. Each missing referent has its own code: `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. A request naming a nonexistent dealer *and* a nonexistent unit reports the dealer.
5. **Request schema** — required fields present, enum members declared, `*_cents` and `*_bps` fields non-negative integers, every `*_bps` value no greater than 10,000, `requested_amount_cents` and `amount_cents` strictly positive, `odometer_miles` zero or greater, date fields valid ISO 8601, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` zero or greater (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400).
6. **Optimistic concurrency** — the version field described in §2.8 (`ERR_STALE_CREDIT_LINE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION`, HTTP 412).
7. **State preconditions** — on the target resource and on its parents (the HTTP 409 family).
8. **Segregation of duties and compliance gates** — including the three separations in §2.9 (HTTP 403 for the separations themselves) and the remedy-justification and reinstatement-criteria gates.
9. **Title, document and collateral-eligibility control** — the manufacturer-statement-of-origin rule, the lender-custody funding gate, the title release gate, the ineligible-collateral gate and the invoice-age gate.
10. **Uniqueness, interval and temporal constraints** — duplicate VINs and observations, overlapping audit windows, overlapping or non-monotonic ladder rungs, audit notice and gap periods, and extension ceilings.
11. **Cross-entity referential consistency** — open out-of-trust blocks on forbearance, audit-scope membership, remittance intent/target agreement, allocatable-obligation existence and ladder existence.
12. **Fiscal capacity** — evaluated dead last, so that a capacity shortfall never masks a state, compliance or title failure. This band holds `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_CLASS_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Two orderings inside this scheme deserve emphasis because they are observable. A suspended credit line that is also over its limit reports the state error `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE`, never the fiscal one. And an operator who both funded a unit and is trying to approve forbearance on it while the unit sits out of trust gets the segregation-of-duties refusal `ERR_SOD_EXCEPTION_APPROVER`, not the referential refusal `ERR_UNIT_HAS_OPEN_SOT`, because duty separation is evaluated in band 8 and referential consistency in band 11.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency on a **parent** entity's `version`, because in both cases repeating the operation is legitimate product behaviour and therefore no state precondition can protect the caller from acting on a stale ledger.

Disbursing a funding advance requires `expected_credit_line_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the governing `CreditLine`. If that value does not equal the line's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_LINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what protects the facility's capacity view: funding a second, different advance on the same line is perfectly normal — the second advance really is still `REQUESTED` and the line really is still `ACTIVE` — but a caller who has not re-read the line since the first advance was funded is holding an `available_credit_cents` figure that no longer exists. The same guard, with the same field and the same error, applies to reversing a funded advance, since a reversal likewise moves the line's capacity. The client resolves a 412 by re-reading the credit line and retrying with the fresh `version`.

Applying a payment remittance requires `expected_dealer_version` as a mandatory input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Dealer`. A mismatch with the dealer's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A dealership may legitimately remit any number of times, so no state check can catch this; but the allocation waterfall consumes obligations in a pinned order, and a caller whose view of the dealer predates an earlier allocation would be directing money at buckets that have already been satisfied. Forcing a re-read makes the post-allocation ledger visible before the next payment is composed.

No other endpoint in the service takes a version field of any kind. In particular, activating a line, reinstating a line, deciding an aging exception, starting, observing on, reconciling or aborting an audit, filing a covenant test, and issuing or executing a remedy all carry no expected-version input, and none of them can fail with a 412.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three separations are enforced, each by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded earlier. All three produce HTTP 403 and all three are evaluated in band 8 of §2.7 — ahead of referential and fiscal checks, behind state checks.

**Funding versus forbearance.** The operator identifier that disbursed a unit's advance, recorded as that unit's `funded_by_user_id`, may never decide an aging exception on any obligation of that unit. Violation yields `ERR_SOD_EXCEPTION_APPROVER`. This applies to both decision branches — a self-denial is refused exactly as a self-approval is.

**Counting versus signing off.** An operator identifier that appears in an audit's `observer_user_ids` may never reconcile that audit. Violation yields `ERR_SOD_AUDIT_RECONCILER`. Because counting is a `FieldAuditor` capability and reconciliation an `AuditManager` capability, this check only bites when the same `X-User-ID` is presented under both roles — which is precisely the abuse it exists to stop.

**Issuing versus executing a remedy.** A remedy action's `issued_by_user_id` may never equal the `X-User-ID` executing it, even though both operators hold `CreditRiskManager`. Violation yields `ERR_SOD_REMEDY_EXECUTOR`.

### 2.10. Dealer Risk Rating and Audit Cadence

Every dealership carries a live risk assessment that the service maintains itself; it is never supplied by a client. The assessment starts from a perfect 100 and deducts points for the three conditions the lender considers predictive: 15 points for each out-of-trust incident of that dealer currently in `OPEN`, 10 points for each of the dealer's units currently sitting in `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`, and a flat 20 points when the dealer's most recent covenant test resulted in `FAIL`. The resulting `risk_score` is clamped so that it is never below 0 and never above 100.

That score maps to a letter `risk_rating` on inclusive lower bounds: a score of 90 or more is `A`, 75 or more is `B`, 55 or more is `C`, and anything below 55 is `D`. A score of exactly 90 is an `A` and a score of exactly 75 is a `B`. The rating in turn fixes `audit_frequency_days`: an `A` dealer is counted every 180 days, a `B` dealer every 90 days, a `C` dealer every 45 days, and a `D` dealer every 21 days. A dealer with a single delinquent unit still scores 90 and remains an `A` on a 180-day cadence; a second delinquent unit takes it to 80, a `B`, and halves the cadence to 90 days; two open out-of-trust incidents take it to 70, a `C`, on a 45-day cadence.

Whenever the rating is recomputed, `next_audit_due_at` is rewritten from the anchor it already used — the `reconciled_at` of the dealer's most recent audit that reached either reconciled outcome, or, if the dealership has never had an audit reconciled, the `activated_at` of its credit line — plus the newly computed `audit_frequency_days`. The assessment reads only the dealer's own denormalized counters (`open_sot_incident_count`, `delinquent_unit_count`, `last_covenant_result`); it never re-walks incidents or units. Those counters are therefore the single source of truth and every cascade described in §3 is responsible for maintaining them. The recomputation fires on every mutation that can move one of the three inputs: an audit reconciliation, a curtailment run, a remittance allocation, a covenant test filing, and a remedy execution.

### 2.11. Facility Capacity and the Borrowing Base

A credit line's derived figures are recomputed from its units after every mutation that can move principal — a funding, a funding reversal, a remittance allocation, a repossession charge-off and a line termination. Four aggregates and one identity define the facility's health.

`outstanding_principal_cents` is the sum of `outstanding_principal_cents` across the line's units whose `funding_status` is `FLOORED`, `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` or `SOT_OPEN`. `eligible_collateral_cents` is the narrower sum across the line's units whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE` **and** whose `funding_status` is `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`. A unit sold out of trust therefore still counts toward outstanding principal — the debt is entirely real — but contributes nothing to eligible collateral, because the collateral itself is gone. Units in `REGISTERED`, `PENDING_FUNDING`, `PAID_OFF` and `REPOSSESSED` contribute to neither figure.

`available_credit_cents` is `credit_limit_cents` reduced by `outstanding_principal_cents` and reduced again by `charged_off_principal_cents`. `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents` is the amount by which outstanding principal exceeds eligible collateral, floored at zero — when eligible collateral is at least as large as outstanding principal the deficiency is exactly 0, never a negative number. The two class aggregates, `class_outstanding_cents_used` and `class_outstanding_cents_demonstrator`, are the same outstanding-principal sum restricted to units whose `inventory_class` is `USED` and `DEMONSTRATOR` respectively.

The **capacity identity** must hold after every single mutation, cascaded ones included: `credit_limit_cents` always equals `available_credit_cents` plus `outstanding_principal_cents` plus `charged_off_principal_cents`. `charged_off_principal_cents` is monotonic — it accumulates principal removed by repossession and never decreases — which is what makes a charge-off permanently consume facility capacity while a payoff returns it.
### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Many operations in this domain are single client calls that mutate a chain of entities. The specification treats those chains as part of the operation, not as eventual background work: everything described as a side effect of an operation is visible the instant that operation's response is returned, and the response body is the created or updated primary resource with all of its computed fields already written. Where a cascade touches several rows of the same kind, the processing order is always pinned in §3, because ordering is observable through `created_at` sequences and through allocation logs.

Three quantities are conserved and can be asserted at any observable moment. **Money conservation** on a remittance: the `amount_cents` received always equals `applied_principal_cents` plus `applied_fee_cents` plus `applied_sot_cure_cents` plus `unapplied_cents`. **Count conservation** on an audit: `units_in_scope_count` always equals `units_verified_count` plus `units_exception_count` plus `units_unobserved_count`. **Capacity conservation** on a credit line, as stated in §2.11.

Two operations are explicitly *compensating*: reversing a funded advance, and aborting an in-progress audit. Each is specified so that the invariant it protects reads identically before the operation it undoes and after the compensation — the facility's capacity figures after a reversal match their pre-funding values exactly, and an aborted audit's three observation counters return to the configuration they had before its first observation was recorded.

Finally, a note on enumerations: the state strings, reason codes and classification values quoted throughout §3 are the complete and exact vocabularies. A payload offering a value outside the declared set is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not a silently ignored field.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Dealership and Establishing the Facility

A relationship begins when a `CreditOfficer` onboards the dealership. The onboarding payload carries `legal_name` (the registered legal name of the borrower, between 1 and 200 characters), the optional trade name `dba_name` (absent means the stored value is null), `franchise_type` — one of `FRANCHISE_NEW_CAR`, `INDEPENDENT_USED` or `POWERSPORTS`, which partitions portfolio reporting but drives no computation — and `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the state where the dealership is licensed. The new dealership lands in `PROSPECT` with a clean slate: `risk_score` 100, `risk_rating` `A`, `audit_frequency_days` 180, `next_audit_due_at` still null, `last_covenant_result` null, and `open_sot_incident_count`, `open_sot_exposure_cents`, `delinquent_unit_count`, `covenant_breach_count` and `unapplied_credit_cents` all zero, with `audit_in_progress` false. Its `version` is 1.

The dealership's lifecycle runs `PROSPECT → ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and, when the relationship fails, `SUSPENDED → DEFAULTED → TERMINATED`, with `TERMINATED` terminal. The cycle back from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` is what makes this a workout-capable relationship rather than a one-way ratchet.

Establishing the facility is a separate `CreditOfficer` act. The payload names the borrower with `dealer_id` and sets the commercial terms: `credit_limit_cents` (the committed facility size, at least 100,000 cents), the three base advance rates `advance_rate_bps_new`, `advance_rate_bps_used` and `advance_rate_bps_demonstrator` (each between 0 and 10,000 basis points), the three per-unit basis ceilings `max_unit_advance_cents_new`, `max_unit_advance_cents_used` and `max_unit_advance_cents_demonstrator` (each strictly positive), and the two aggregate class ceilings `class_sublimit_cents_used` and `class_sublimit_cents_demonstrator` (each strictly positive). `curtailment_grace_days` is optional, must lie between 0 and 30 inclusive, and when the payload omits it the line is created with a grace period of 10 days. A dealership may hold at most one credit line whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` at any time; lines that have reached `TERMINATED` do not count against that restriction.

A freshly established line sits in `PENDING` with `activated_at` null, `suspension_reason_code` null, `available_credit_cents` equal to `credit_limit_cents`, and `outstanding_principal_cents`, `charged_off_principal_cents`, `eligible_collateral_cents`, `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents`, `class_outstanding_cents_used` and `class_outstanding_cents_demonstrator` all zero. Its `version` is 1. The line's own lifecycle is `PENDING → ACTIVE`, then the cycle `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, with `TERMINATED` reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and terminal.

Before the facility can be switched on, its **curtailment ladder** must exist. A ladder rung is a configuration row scoped to one line and one `inventory_class`, added by a `CreditOfficer` with a payload carrying `inventory_class` (`NEW`, `USED` or `DEMONSTRATOR`), `age_days_from` (the inclusive lower bound of the age bucket in days since the unit was floored, zero or greater), the optional `age_days_to` (the **exclusive** upper bound; omitted or null means the rung is unbounded) and `cumulative_paydown_bps` between 0 and 10,000. Rungs may be added while the line is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` line refuses new rungs with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Because the intervals are half-open, touching endpoints do not collide: a rung covering days 90 up to 180 coexists happily with a rung covering days 180 up to 270, while a rung covering days 150 up to 200 intersects both and is refused with `ERR_TIER_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. At most one unbounded rung may exist per line-and-class pair, and a second attempt is refused with the same code. Separately, `age_days_to` must be either null or strictly greater than `age_days_from`, and the ladder must climb: a new rung's `cumulative_paydown_bps` must be strictly greater than the `cumulative_paydown_bps` of every existing rung of the same line and class whose `age_days_from` is smaller. A flat or descending ladder is refused with `ERR_TIER_INTERVAL_INVALID`. Ladder rungs are immutable and, per §2.4, carry no `version`. They also only bind units funded *after* they exist, because a unit's obligations are materialised once, at funding time.

Activation is the join between the two lifecycles. A `CreditOfficer` may activate only a line still in `PENDING` — any other status is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and only when at least one ladder rung exists for that line. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the capacity figures of §2.11, advances the line `PENDING → ACTIVE`, and cascades: the dealership moves `PROSPECT → ACTIVE` and its `next_audit_due_at` is set to `activated_at` plus its current `audit_frequency_days` — 180 days out for a brand-new `A`-rated dealer. Both the line's `version` and the dealer's `version` advance by 1.

### 3.2. Registering Inventory and Controlling the Document

Registration is a dealer act: a `DealerPrincipal` records a vehicle against its own facility before any money moves. The payload carries `dealer_id`, which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, and `credit_line_id`, which must name a line belonging to that dealership; it carries `vin`, exactly 17 uppercase alphanumeric characters; `inventory_class`; `invoice_amount_cents`, strictly positive, being the manufacturer or acquisition invoice amount that forms the advance basis before capping; `invoice_date`, the invoice issue instant that later drives both the aged-invoice rate reduction and the maximum-age gate; the optional `odometer_miles`, which defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative; and `title_document_type`, either `MSO` or `TITLE`.

The document rule is a hard collateral-control requirement, not a preference. `NEW` inventory must present a manufacturer statement of origin, so `title_document_type` must be `MSO`; `USED` inventory must present a certificate of title, so it must be `TITLE`; `DEMONSTRATOR` inventory accepts either. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_MSO_REQUIRED_FOR_NEW_UNIT`. Registration also requires the governing line to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the dealership to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`) — a `PROSPECT` dealership cannot floor inventory, and neither can a suspended, defaulted or terminated one. The `vin` must be unique among the dealership's units whose `funding_status` is anything other than `REPOSSESSED`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN`. A repossessed unit's VIN is thereby freed for re-registration.

A registered unit starts in `funding_status` `REGISTERED`, `title_custody_status` `NOT_RECEIVED` and `eligibility_status` `ELIGIBLE`, with `ineligibility_reason_code` null, `advance_amount_cents`, `outstanding_principal_cents`, `principal_paid_cents`, `aging_exception_count` and `curtailment_extension_days_total` all zero, `floored_at` and `funded_by_user_id` null, and `version` 1.

Each unit carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**, and this independence is deliberate. `funding_status` tracks where the unit sits in the money lifecycle, moving through `REGISTERED`, `PENDING_FUNDING`, `FLOORED`, `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`, `SOT_OPEN` and terminating in either `PAID_OFF` or `REPOSSESSED`. `title_custody_status` tracks the whereabouts of the physical document, moving through `NOT_RECEIVED`, `LENDER_HELD` and `LOST` and terminating in `RELEASED_FOR_SALE`. The document dimension advances on its own trigger set and gates the money dimension — a unit cannot be funded unless its document is in lender custody — but the money dimension never moves the document dimension. A `FLOORED` unit may lose its title, and a merely `REGISTERED` unit may already be `LENDER_HELD`.

Custody moves are a `CreditOfficer` capability, driven by a payload naming `new_custody_status` and an optional `document_reference`. The legal successors are: from `NOT_RECEIVED`, either `LENDER_HELD` (the document arrived and is now vaulted) or `LOST`; from `LENDER_HELD`, either `LOST` or `RELEASED_FOR_SALE`; from `LOST`, back to `LENDER_HELD`, which requires `document_reference` to be present in the payload because a duplicate document has to be identified; and from `RELEASED_FOR_SALE`, nowhere at all, since that state is terminal. Any other requested move — including any attempt to leave `RELEASED_FOR_SALE` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TITLE_TRANSITION`.

Losing a document has collateral consequences. Whether the unit was `NOT_RECEIVED` or `LENDER_HELD`, moving to `LOST` sets `eligibility_status` to `INELIGIBLE_TITLE_DEFECT` and `ineligibility_reason_code` to `TITLE_DEFECT`, then recomputes the line's `eligible_collateral_cents` and `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents`. Recovering the document — moving `LOST` back to `LENDER_HELD` — restores `eligibility_status` to `ELIGIBLE` and clears `ineligibility_reason_code` to null, **but only when the recorded reason was `TITLE_DEFECT`**: a unit that was also flagged for damage or wholesale disposal stays ineligible for that other reason, and the recovery does not launder it back into the borrowing base. Either way, the line's collateral figures are recomputed and the unit's `version` advances by 1.

Releasing the document is the end of the lender's interest and is gated accordingly. Beyond requiring the current custody state to be `LENDER_HELD`, release demands that the unit owe nothing: `outstanding_principal_cents` must be 0, the `funding_status` must be either `REGISTERED` or `PAID_OFF`, no obligation of the unit may still be `DUE` or `DELINQUENT`, and no out-of-trust incident of the unit may still be `OPEN`. A release attempt that fails any of those money-side conditions is refused with `ERR_TITLE_RELEASE_BLOCKED`.

Independently of the document, collateral can be written down. A `CreditOfficer` or an `AuditManager` may classify a unit as ineligible with a payload carrying `ineligibility_reason_code` and optional free-text `notes`. The reason code is one of `DAMAGE`, `WHOLESALE`, `MISSING`, `TITLE_DEFECT` or `SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST`, and it selects the matching `eligibility_status` one-for-one: `DAMAGE` gives `INELIGIBLE_DAMAGE`, `WHOLESALE` gives `INELIGIBLE_WHOLESALE`, `MISSING` gives `INELIGIBLE_MISSING`, `TITLE_DEFECT` gives `INELIGIBLE_TITLE_DEFECT` and `SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST` gives `INELIGIBLE_SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST`. Only a unit currently `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` may be classified this way; anything else is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FLOORED`. The debt survives the classification untouched — `outstanding_principal_cents` does not move — while `eligible_collateral_cents` shrinks and `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents` rises accordingly. Both the unit's `version` and the line's `version` advance by 1.
### 3.3. Funding an Invoice: Rate Reductions, Capacity Gates and the Reversal Window

Funding is a two-party act. The dealership asks; the lender disburses; the two steps are separate resources so that the request can be declined without a trace on the ledger.

A `DealerPrincipal` opens the request against a unit that is still `REGISTERED`, supplying `requested_amount_cents` (strictly positive — the amount the dealership would like to draw, which is informational only, because the amount actually disbursed is always the computed advance) and `dealer_invoice_reference` (the dealership's own invoice or purchase-order reference). The request is refused if the unit is not `REGISTERED` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_FLOORED`), if the unit already has an advance in `REQUESTED` or `FUNDED` (at most one live advance per unit), if the unit's `eligibility_status` is any of the ineligible values (`ERR_UNIT_INELIGIBLE`), if the line is not `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE`) or if the dealership is not `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`). On success a `FundingAdvance` is created in `REQUESTED` with `unit_id` and `credit_line_id` copied from the unit — the line reference is copied at request time precisely so that the concurrency parent of the later disbursement is unambiguous — with `disbursement_reference`, `funded_at`, `funded_by_user_id`, `invoice_age_days_at_funding` and `reason_code` all null, `approved_advance_amount_cents` and `effective_advance_rate_bps` zero, `rate_adjustment_codes` an empty list, and `version` 1. The unit advances `REGISTERED → PENDING_FUNDING` and its `version` increments by 1. No money has moved and no rate is fixed yet.

Disbursement is a `CreditOfficer` act on an advance still in `REQUESTED` — any other advance status is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_PENDING`. Its payload is deliberately small: `expected_credit_line_version` (see §2.8) and `disbursement_reference`, the wire or ACH reference for the outgoing payment. Everything else the disbursement needs, it reads from the unit, the line and the ladder. Before any arithmetic runs, the operation requires the line to be `ACTIVE` and the dealership to be `ACTIVE`, the unit to be `PENDING_FUNDING`, the unit's `title_custody_status` to be exactly `LENDER_HELD` — `NOT_RECEIVED`, `LOST` and `RELEASED_FOR_SALE` all block disbursement with `ERR_TITLE_NOT_LENDER_HELD` — the unit's `eligibility_status` to be `ELIGIBLE` (`ERR_UNIT_INELIGIBLE`), the invoice to be young enough, and at least one ladder rung with a non-zero `cumulative_paydown_bps` to exist for the line and the unit's `inventory_class`. Without a ladder there is no obligation schedule to create, so funding is refused with `ERR_NO_CURTAILMENT_SCHEDULE`.

The invoice-age gate is measured in whole days between the unit's `invoice_date` and the logical clock at disbursement, and that same figure is what the advance records as `invoice_age_days_at_funding`. An invoice may be at most 90 days old: exactly 90 days still funds, 91 days is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_TOO_OLD_TO_FLOOR`.

**The advance amount.** The computation proceeds in four steps and rounds exactly once. First, the **basis** is the smaller of the unit's `invoice_amount_cents` and the per-unit ceiling for its class — `max_unit_advance_cents_new` for `NEW`, `max_unit_advance_cents_used` for `USED`, `max_unit_advance_cents_demonstrator` for `DEMONSTRATOR`. The ceiling caps the basis; it never rejects the request. Second, the **effective rate** starts at the line's base rate for that class (`advance_rate_bps_new`, `advance_rate_bps_used` or `advance_rate_bps_demonstrator`) and absorbs up to two reductions, applied additively to the rate and never to the basis. A `DEMONSTRATOR` unit whose `odometer_miles` is strictly greater than 3,000 loses 500 basis points, and the code `HIGH_MILEAGE_DEMO` is appended to `rate_adjustment_codes`; a unit whose invoice age in whole days is 45 or more loses a further 250 basis points, and the code `AGED_INVOICE` is appended. The boundaries are strict in opposite directions: an odometer reading of exactly 3,000 miles triggers nothing while 3,001 does, and an invoice age of exactly 45 days does trigger the aged-invoice reduction while 44 days does not. Both reductions stack, so a demonstrator with 3,001 miles on a 45-day-old invoice loses 750 basis points in total. When both apply, `rate_adjustment_codes` lists `HIGH_MILEAGE_DEMO` before `AGED_INVOICE`; when neither applies the list stays empty. The reduced rate is floored at zero basis points — it can never go negative — and the result is stored as `effective_advance_rate_bps`. Third, the advance is the basis multiplied by the effective rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. That single rounding step happens after both reductions have been folded into the rate; rounding an intermediate figure and then applying the second reduction is wrong. Fourth, two capacity gates apply, and only here can the amount cause a rejection rather than a reduction: the computed advance must not exceed the line's `available_credit_cents` (an advance exactly equal to available credit is permitted; only a strictly greater one is refused, with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), and for `USED` and `DEMONSTRATOR` units the class's current outstanding total plus the computed advance must not exceed that class's sublimit — `class_outstanding_cents_used` against `class_sublimit_cents_used`, `class_outstanding_cents_demonstrator` against `class_sublimit_cents_demonstrator` — with a breach refused as `ERR_CLASS_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDED`. `NEW` inventory has no class sublimit. Neither gate ever funds a partial amount; the whole request either succeeds at the computed figure or fails.

A successful disbursement writes a wide cascade in one request. On the advance: `approved_advance_amount_cents` set to the computed figure, `effective_advance_rate_bps`, `rate_adjustment_codes` and `invoice_age_days_at_funding` recorded, `funded_at` stamped from the logical clock, `funded_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID`, and the status moving `REQUESTED → FUNDED`. On the unit: `advance_amount_cents` and `outstanding_principal_cents` both set to the computed advance, `floored_at` stamped from the logical clock as the age-zero anchor for the entire curtailment ladder, `funded_by_user_id` copied from `X-User-ID` — this is the value the forbearance separation of §2.9 later compares against — and `funding_status` moving `PENDING_FUNDING → FLOORED`. On the line: `outstanding_principal_cents` and, for used and demonstrator inventory, the matching class total increase by the advance; `eligible_collateral_cents` increases by the advance too, since a freshly floored unit is eligible collateral; `available_credit_cents` and `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents` are recomputed per §2.11. And the unit's obligation ladder is materialised, as described in §3.4. The advance's, the unit's and the line's `version` each advance by 1.

Declining is the sibling branch, and it is deliberately inert on the ledger. A `CreditOfficer` declines an advance still in `REQUESTED` with a payload carrying `reason_code`, one of `CREDIT_DECLINE`, `TITLE_DEFECT`, `DUPLICATE_INVOICE`, `LINE_SUSPENDED`, `DEALER_REQUEST` or `FUNDING_ERROR`. The advance moves `REQUESTED → REJECTED` with that code recorded, and the unit returns `PENDING_FUNDING → REGISTERED` so a fresh request can be filed against it. No money moves, no obligation is created, and `available_credit_cents` is untouched — this inertness is exactly what distinguishes a decline from the void branch below. The advance's and the unit's `version` each advance by 1.

There is a third terminal branch out of `REQUESTED` that no client can reach directly. When a credit line moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` for any reason, every advance on that line still in `REQUESTED` is moved to `VOIDED` with `reason_code` `LINE_SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each voided advance returns its unit `PENDING_FUNDING → REGISTERED`. Like a decline, a void moves no money.

Reversal is the compensating action for a disbursement booked in error, and it is the only way out of `FUNDED`. A `CreditOfficer` supplies `expected_credit_line_version` and `reason_code`, which for a reversal records the booking error `FUNDING_ERROR`. The advance must be in `FUNDED` — anything else is `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_PENDING` — and four conditions must all hold: the logical clock must be no later than `funded_at` plus 5 days, the unit's `principal_paid_cents` must be exactly 0, the unit must carry no observation on any audit, and no obligation of the unit may be in `DUE`, `DELINQUENT` or `PAID`. Any of those failing yields `ERR_ADVANCE_REVERSAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A reversal moves the advance `FUNDED → REVERSED`, returns the unit `FLOORED → REGISTERED` with `advance_amount_cents` and `outstanding_principal_cents` reset to 0, `floored_at` cleared to null and `funded_by_user_id` cleared to null, cancels every obligation of the unit still in `SCHEDULED` (processed in `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order), and recomputes the line's capacity figures so that `available_credit_cents` reads exactly what it read before the disbursement. The advance's, the unit's and the line's `version` each advance by 1.
### 3.4. The Curtailment Ladder: Obligations, the Nightly Run and Delinquency

Curtailment is the mechanism that forces principal down as inventory ages, and the lender's whole aging discipline is expressed as a ladder of dated obligations materialised at the moment of disbursement. For each ladder rung of the unit's line and `inventory_class` whose `cumulative_paydown_bps` is strictly greater than zero, taken in `age_days_from` ascending order, one obligation is created in `SCHEDULED`. Rungs with a zero paydown fraction create nothing.

Each obligation freezes the rung that produced it. `tier_age_days_from` and `tier_cumulative_paydown_bps` are copied from the rung at creation and never change afterwards, even if the ladder configuration is extended later. `due_date` is the unit's `floored_at` plus `tier_age_days_from` days. `unit_id` and `dealer_id` are denormalized onto the obligation so that a portfolio-wide run can scope by dealership without walking the unit table. `supersedes_obligation_id` is null (it is populated only on replacement rows, see §3.5), `extension_days_applied` is 0, `required_principal_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `total_amount_due_cents`, `principal_paid_cents` and `fee_paid_cents` are all 0, `became_due_at` and `became_delinquent_at` are null, and `version` is 1. The obligation's lifecycle is `SCHEDULED → DUE → DELINQUENT`, with `PAID` reachable from `DUE` or `DELINQUENT`, `SUPERSEDED` reachable from `SCHEDULED` or `DUE`, and `CANCELLED` reachable from `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `DELINQUENT`. `PAID`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

**The forward-looking requirement.** At creation, each obligation records `projected_principal_cents`, which answers the question "if the dealership pays every rung exactly on schedule and never prepays voluntarily, how much principal falls due at this rung?". Build it from cumulative figures over the unit's *original* `advance_amount_cents`: for each rung, the cumulative requirement is the original advance multiplied by that rung's `cumulative_paydown_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. The rung's own projected requirement is that cumulative figure minus the cumulative figure of the previous rung in the ladder, where the previous cumulative figure for the first rung is zero. Each cumulative figure is rounded half up independently and the subtraction is performed on the already-rounded integers; the difference itself is never rounded. Because the figure is blind to actual payments, it never changes after funding. As a worked illustration: a unit floored at an advance of 3,000,000 cents against rungs of 1,000, 2,500 and 5,000 basis points at 90, 180 and 270 days yields cumulative figures of 300,000, 750,000 and 1,500,000 cents, and therefore projected requirements of 300,000, 450,000 and 750,000 cents on the three obligations.

A dealership, credit officer, risk manager or system operator can read that ladder back at any time as a computed view of the unit. The read mutates nothing and returns the unit's `unit_id`, its `advance_amount_cents`, its `inventory_class`, a `basis` field carrying the literal string `FORWARD_PROJECTED`, a `projected_obligations` array and a `projected_total_required_cents` total. Each element of `projected_obligations` carries `obligation_id`, `tier_age_days_from`, `tier_cumulative_paydown_bps`, `due_date`, `projected_principal_cents` and `status`, and the array is ordered by `due_date` ascending. `projected_total_required_cents` is the sum of `projected_principal_cents` across the unit's obligations excluding any in `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED`. The figures returned here are exactly the ones frozen at funding, which is what makes this view directly comparable against the assessed figures a run writes.

**The nightly run.** Portfolio aging is advanced by a `SystemOperator` checkpoint rather than by a background timer, so that behaviour is reproducible. The checkpoint accepts an optional `dealer_id`, which restricts the sweep to a single dealership, and an optional `as_of_date`, which supplies the effective run date in place of the logical clock. Dealerships whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` participate; a `PROSPECT`, `DEFAULTED` or `TERMINATED` dealership is skipped entirely. Within a dealership, only units whose `funding_status` is `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` are walked, and obligations already in `PAID`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` are never touched. The processing order is fully pinned: dealerships by `created_at` ascending, then units by `floored_at` ascending and within equal values by `created_at` ascending, then obligations by `due_date` ascending and within equal values by `created_at` ascending.

Two transitions fire during a sweep. An obligation in `SCHEDULED` whose `due_date` is at or before the run date matures to `DUE`: `became_due_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `required_principal_cents` is written from the **assessed** variant of the curtailment requirement — the variant that reads what the dealership has actually paid. Take the cumulative requirement for that rung over the unit's original advance (rounded half up exactly as above), subtract the unit's *actual* `principal_paid_cents`, and then take the smaller of that difference and the unit's current `outstanding_principal_cents`; if the result is negative, the requirement is 0. `total_amount_due_cents` is set equal to `required_principal_cents`, because no late fee exists yet. Continuing the illustration above: if the dealership pays the 90-day obligation of 300,000 cents on time and then voluntarily prepays a further 200,000 cents on day 100, then at day 180 the unit's `principal_paid_cents` is 500,000 and its `outstanding_principal_cents` is 2,500,000, so the second obligation's assessed requirement is 750,000 minus 500,000, capped at 2,500,000, which is 250,000 cents — while the very same row's `projected_principal_cents` still reads 450,000. The two figures are meant to differ, and neither is a correction of the other.

An obligation in `DUE` becomes `DELINQUENT` when the run date is **strictly** past its `due_date` plus the line's `curtailment_grace_days`, and its `principal_paid_cents` is still short of its `required_principal_cents`. A run date landing exactly on the last day of grace leaves the obligation `DUE`; only a run date beyond it tips the obligation over. The delinquency assessment then writes the late fee, and its evaluation order is pinned to break an apparent circularity: `required_principal_cents` is already written from the maturity step, the late fee is a function of that figure and of nothing else, and only then is the total formed. The raw fee is `required_principal_cents` multiplied by 200 basis points (two percent) divided by 10,000; that raw figure is rounded half up to whole cents **before** the ceiling is applied, and the recorded `late_fee_cents` is the smaller of the rounded fee and the ceiling of 50,000 cents (five hundred dollars). `total_amount_due_cents` is then `required_principal_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`. A required principal of 300,000 cents therefore carries a fee of 6,000 and a total of 306,000; a required principal of 3,000,000 cents would carry an uncapped fee of 60,000, so the ceiling binds at 50,000 and the total is 3,050,000. Charging the fee on the fee-inclusive total instead — which for a required principal of 300,000 would produce 306,122 — is explicitly wrong.

Delinquency also cascades. `became_delinquent_at` is stamped; any aging exception request still `PENDING` against that obligation is moved to `EXPIRED` (the dealership does not get to hold a request open while the obligation rots); and the owning unit, if at least one of its obligations turned `DELINQUENT` during this run, moves `FLOORED → DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`. That unit transition increments the dealership's `delinquent_unit_count`, which re-triggers the risk assessment of §2.10 — rewriting `risk_score`, `risk_rating` and `audit_frequency_days`, and re-deriving `next_audit_due_at` from the unchanged anchor with the new cadence. Note what does *not* change: the line's `eligible_collateral_cents` is untouched by delinquency, because a late unit is still on the lot and still eligible collateral. It leaves the borrowing base only when it is classified ineligible or sold out of trust.

The checkpoint's response summarises the sweep with `run_completed_at`, `dealer_ids_processed` (the identifiers of the dealerships actually walked, in processing order), `obligations_marked_due_count`, `obligations_marked_delinquent_count`, `total_required_paydown_cents`, `units_flagged_delinquent_count` and `exceptions_expired_count`. Each count is exactly the number of rows that made the corresponding transition during this sweep. `total_required_paydown_cents` is the sum of `required_principal_cents` across the obligations that matured to `DUE` in this sweep, and **the rounding order here is pinned**: round each obligation's requirement individually first, then add the already-rounded integers. Summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is wrong and observably different — three `NEW` units each carrying an advance of 3,333,333 cents crossing a 1,000-basis-point rung in the same sweep produce 333,333 cents each for a total of 999,999, not the 1,000,000 that a sum-then-round implementation reports. Every obligation, unit and dealership the sweep mutates has its `version` incremented by 1.
### 3.5. Aging Exceptions: Asking for Time and Granting It

Sometimes a unit legitimately ages past a rung — a retail deal is days from closing, a title is stuck at a state agency, a fleet order has not shipped, a damaged vehicle is in the body shop. The service models that as a forbearance request against a single obligation, filed by the `DealerPrincipal` that owns it.

The filing payload carries `requested_extension_days` — the relief the dealership is asking for, which must be at least 1 and no more than 45 days for a single request — and `justification_code`, one of `RETAIL_DEAL_PENDING`, `TITLE_DELAY`, `FLEET_ORDER` or `DAMAGE_REPAIR`. A request outside the 1-to-45 range is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_EXCEEDS_MAX`. Relief must be sought while the obligation is still open, meaning its status is `SCHEDULED` or `DUE`; an obligation already in `DELINQUENT`, `PAID`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_OPEN`. The dealership itself must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DEALER_NOT_ACTIVE`). The new request lands in `PENDING` with `requested_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID` (which, per §2.1, is the dealership's own identifier), `decided_extension_days`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `reason_code` and `replacement_obligation_id` all null, and `version` 1.

Filing does **not** pause the clock. The targeted obligation keeps its `status` and its `due_date` exactly as they were, and if a curtailment run subsequently tips that obligation into `DELINQUENT`, the untouched request is moved `PENDING → EXPIRED` and counted in that run's `exceptions_expired_count`. No replacement obligation is created by an expiry; the dealership must either remit or file again against whatever obligation the ladder then presents.

Deciding a request is a `CreditRiskManager` act. The decision payload carries `decision`, either `APPROVE` or `DENY`; `decided_extension_days`, which is required on an approval; and `reason_code`, one of `RELIEF_GRANTED`, `INSUFFICIENT_JUSTIFICATION`, `EXPOSURE_TOO_HIGH` or `DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`. The checks run in a fixed order, and knowing the order matters because several can fail at once. First the request must still be `PENDING`; a request already `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_PENDING`. Then the funding-versus-forbearance separation of §2.9 is applied — the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the unit's `funded_by_user_id`, on both branches of the decision, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_EXCEPTION_APPROVER`. Then, on an approval only, the extension arithmetic: `decided_extension_days` must be present, at least 1, no greater than the `requested_extension_days` the dealership asked for, and the unit's `curtailment_extension_days_total` plus the granted days must not exceed 60 days of cumulative relief across the unit's entire life; any of those failing yields `ERR_EXTENSION_EXCEEDS_MAX`. Then the unit's `aging_exception_count` — the number of approvals it has already absorbed — must be fewer than 2, so the third approval on a single unit is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_LIMIT_REACHED`. And last, the unit must have no out-of-trust incident in `OPEN`: a unit already sold out of trust does not get more time, and the refusal is `ERR_UNIT_HAS_OPEN_SOT`. Because the duty separation is checked before the out-of-trust condition, an operator attempting to self-approve relief on an out-of-trust unit sees the separation error, not the out-of-trust one.

An approval rewrites the ladder rather than editing it. The request moves `PENDING → APPROVED` with `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the logical clock and `reason_code` recorded. The targeted obligation moves to `SUPERSEDED` and a **replacement** obligation is created in `SCHEDULED` whose `supersedes_obligation_id` points at the superseded row, whose `extension_days_applied` is the granted `decided_extension_days`, whose `due_date` is the superseded row's `due_date` plus the granted days, and whose `projected_principal_cents` is copied across unchanged. The replacement's `required_principal_cents` and `late_fee_cents` start at 0 and will be assessed at its own maturity — which is why an approved exception on an obligation that had already matured to `DUE` can change the *amount* owed and not merely the date: by the time the replacement matures, the unit's actual `principal_paid_cents` may have moved, and the assessed variant reads that live figure. The identifier of the replacement is written back onto the request as `replacement_obligation_id`, though the canonical link between the two obligations is always the replacement's `supersedes_obligation_id`. The unit's `aging_exception_count` increases by 1 and its `curtailment_extension_days_total` increases by the granted days. If retiring the superseded obligation leaves the unit with no obligation still in `DELINQUENT`, the unit moves `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT → FLOORED`, the dealership's `delinquent_unit_count` is recomputed and the risk assessment of §2.10 re-runs. The request's and the obligation's `version` each advance by 1.

A denial is far simpler and by design changes nothing else: the request moves `PENDING → DENIED` with `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `reason_code` written, the targeted obligation keeps both its `status` and its `due_date`, no replacement is created, and no counter on the unit or the dealership moves.

### 3.6. Physical Inventory Audits and the Out-of-Trust Cascade

An audit is the only mechanism by which the lender's ledger is confronted with the physical lot, and the specification treats it as a four-stage workflow with two very different endings.

**Scheduling.** An `AuditManager` schedules a count against a dealership with a payload naming `dealer_id`, `audit_type` (`ANNOUNCED` or `UNANNOUNCED`), `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`. The window is a **closed** interval: both endpoints are inside it. Four temporal rules apply, each with its own refusal. An `ANNOUNCED` count must give the dealership at least 7 days of notice, so `window_start_date` must be at least 7 days after the current logical-clock date; exactly 7 days is acceptable, and less is `ERR_AUDIT_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`. An `UNANNOUNCED` count needs no notice at all — that is its whole point — but it may not be used to harass a dealership, so its `window_start_date` must be at least 14 days after the `window_end_date` of that dealership's most recent count that reached either reconciled outcome; exactly 14 days is acceptable, and less is `ERR_AUDIT_GAP_TOO_SHORT`. An `UNANNOUNCED` window may span at most three calendar days, meaning `window_end_date` may be no more than 2 days after `window_start_date`, and for every audit type `window_end_date` must not precede `window_start_date`; a violation of either is `ERR_UNANNOUNCED_WINDOW_INVALID`. Finally, the new closed window must not intersect the closed window of any existing audit of the same dealership whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `RECONCILED_CLEAN` or `RECONCILED_EXCEPTIONS`; **touching endpoints do collide**, so an existing window ending on 2026-08-10 blocks a new one starting on 2026-08-10, and the refusal is `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Audits that ended in `ABORTED` release their window for reuse. A scheduled audit begins in `SCHEDULED` with `started_at` and `reconciled_at` null, `units_in_scope_count`, `units_verified_count`, `units_exception_count`, `units_unobserved_count` and `sot_exposure_cents` all 0, `observer_user_ids` an empty list, `reconciled_by_user_id` and `abort_reason_code` null, and `version` 1.

**Opening the count.** A `FieldAuditor` starts the audit, which moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`; starting anything not in `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`. The dealership must be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — a suspended dealership is exactly the one you most want to count — and the dealership must not already have another count in flight, which the `audit_in_progress` flag on the dealership records. The logical clock must fall inside the closed window, comparing calendar dates inclusively at both ends; outside it, the refusal is `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Starting **freezes the scope**: `units_in_scope_count` is set to the number of that dealership's units whose `funding_status` is `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` at that instant, `units_unobserved_count` is initialised to the same number, `units_verified_count` and `units_exception_count` are 0, `started_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the dealership's `audit_in_progress` becomes true. No unit funded after this instant joins the count. Both the audit's and the dealership's `version` advance by 1.

**Recording observations.** Each observation is one immutable reading of one unit, recorded by a `FieldAuditor` while the audit is `IN_PROGRESS` (otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`). The payload names `unit_id`, `observed_condition`, `title_verified`, and optionally `odometer_miles` and `sale_contract_reference` — the odometer reading is kept for the audit trail only and never re-prices an advance, and the sale contract reference is the retail buyer's order the dealership produces for a unit it has sold. The named unit must belong to the audit's dealership and must have been inside the frozen scope at start time; otherwise the reading is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_IN_AUDIT_SCOPE`. At most one observation may exist per audit-and-unit pair, and because observations are immutable there is no way to correct one — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION`. The unit's own `funding_status` must be `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_FLOORED`).

`observed_condition` is what the auditor physically found, one of `PRESENT`, `PRESENT_DAMAGED`, `SOLD_NO_PAYOFF`, `WHOLESALED` or `MISSING`, and `title_verified` is true when the lender-held manufacturer statement of origin or certificate of title was physically matched to the VIN during the count. From those two inputs, plus a snapshot of the unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` frozen onto the row as `unit_outstanding_principal_cents_at_observation`, the service computes and freezes two fields. `classification` is `VERIFIED` when the unit was `PRESENT` and the title was verified; `TITLE_EXCEPTION` when the unit was `PRESENT` but the title was not verified; `DAMAGE_EXCEPTION` when the condition was `PRESENT_DAMAGED`; `WHOLESALE_EXCEPTION` when the condition was `WHOLESALED`; and `SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST` when the condition was either `SOLD_NO_PAYOFF` or `MISSING`. `is_out_of_trust` is true for `SOLD_NO_PAYOFF` and for `MISSING`; it is false for `PRESENT` with a verified title, for `PRESENT` without one, and for `PRESENT_DAMAGED`; and for `WHOLESALED` it is true exactly when the frozen principal snapshot is strictly greater than zero. Note the asymmetry deliberately: a wholesaled unit keeps its own `WHOLESALE_EXCEPTION` classification even when it *is* out of trust, so `classification` alone cannot decide whether an incident is owed — reconciliation keys on `is_out_of_trust`. A wholesaled unit whose snapshot principal is exactly 0 is a `WHOLESALE_EXCEPTION` with `is_out_of_trust` false, so it becomes ineligible collateral without generating an incident.

Recording also sets `observed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and appends that identifier to the audit's `observer_user_ids` if it is not already there, preserving first-observation order. The audit's `units_verified_count` increments when the classification is `VERIFIED` and its `units_exception_count` increments otherwise, and `units_unobserved_count` decrements by 1 in either case, so the count-conservation identity of §2.12 holds after every single reading. The observation row itself is immutable and carries no `version`; the unit is not mutated at all at this stage — nothing about a unit's eligibility or funding changes until the count is signed off. The audit's `version` advances by 1.
**Signing off.** Reconciliation is an `AuditManager` act on an audit still `IN_PROGRESS` (otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), with an optional `notes` field on the payload. Two gates precede everything else. The counting-versus-signing-off separation of §2.9 requires that the reconciling `X-User-ID` not appear in the audit's `observer_user_ids`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_AUDIT_RECONCILER`. And an audit that froze at least one unit into scope must have produced at least one reading: if `units_in_scope_count` is greater than zero while `units_verified_count` plus `units_exception_count` is zero, the request is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NO_OBSERVATIONS`, because a count with no readings is a refusal to count rather than a clean result. An audit that froze zero units into scope may be reconciled with no readings at all.

Which of the two reconciled outcomes the audit reaches is decided by `units_exception_count` alone. When it is exactly zero, the audit moves `IN_PROGRESS → RECONCILED_CLEAN`: `sot_exposure_cents` is written as 0 (the out-of-trust exposure summed over an empty set), no out-of-trust incident is created, and no unit's `eligibility_status` or `funding_status` changes at all. When it is 1 or more, the audit moves `IN_PROGRESS → RECONCILED_EXCEPTIONS` and the full cascade below fires. Both outcomes share the same join point: `reconciled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `reconciled_by_user_id` is set from `X-User-ID`, the dealership's `audit_in_progress` returns to false, the risk assessment of §2.10 re-runs, and `next_audit_due_at` is then rewritten as `reconciled_at` plus the dealership's (possibly just-changed) `audit_frequency_days`. That shared join is what makes an unannounced count "count" toward the frequency requirement exactly as an announced one does. The audit's `version` advances by 1 on either branch.

On the exceptions branch, `sot_exposure_cents` is the sum, taken over this audit's observations whose `is_out_of_trust` is true in `created_at` ascending order, of each such observation's unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` **read live at reconcile time** — not the `unit_outstanding_principal_cents_at_observation` snapshot stored on the row. The distinction is deliberate and observable: if the dealership remitted between the count and the sign-off, the incident is opened at the smaller, current figure. Observations belonging to other audits, even on the same units, never participate. From there the cascade runs five hops, in order:

1. For every observation of this audit whose `is_out_of_trust` is true, in `created_at` ascending order, an `OutOfTrustIncident` is created in `OPEN` carrying `dealer_id`, `unit_id`, `audit_id` and `observation_id`, with `exposure_cents` set to that unit's live `outstanding_principal_cents`, `cure_deadline_at` set to `reconciled_at` plus 10 days, `cured_at`, `charged_off_at` and `resolution_reason_code` null, and `version` 1. The ordering matters because it fixes the `created_at` sequence of the resulting incidents, which later drives cure allocation order.
2. Each unit behind such an observation moves from `FLOORED` or from `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` to `SOT_OPEN`, and its eligibility is written from the observation's classification: a `SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST` classification gives `eligibility_status` `INELIGIBLE_SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST` with `ineligibility_reason_code` `SOLD_OUT_OF_TRUST`, while a `WHOLESALE_EXCEPTION` classification that was nonetheless out of trust gives `INELIGIBLE_WHOLESALE` with reason `WHOLESALE`. A unit that was in `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT` when it left for `SOT_OPEN` also decrements the dealership's `delinquent_unit_count`, since it is no longer counted as a delinquent unit.
3. Every *other* exception observation — the ones whose `is_out_of_trust` is false — writes its unit's eligibility without creating any incident and without touching `funding_status`: `TITLE_EXCEPTION` gives `INELIGIBLE_TITLE_DEFECT` with reason `TITLE_DEFECT`, `DAMAGE_EXCEPTION` gives `INELIGIBLE_DAMAGE` with reason `DAMAGE`, and `WHOLESALE_EXCEPTION` gives `INELIGIBLE_WHOLESALE` with reason `WHOLESALE`.
4. Every unit that left `ELIGIBLE` forces the line's `eligible_collateral_cents` and `borrowing_base_deficiency_cents` to be recomputed per §2.11, and the dealership's `open_sot_incident_count` and `open_sot_exposure_cents` to rise by the incidents just opened and their exposures.
5. When the dealership's `open_sot_exposure_cents` reaches or exceeds 10,000,000 cents (one hundred thousand dollars), the credit line moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason_code` `SOT_EXPOSURE_THRESHOLD`. That suspension in turn voids every advance on the line still in `REQUESTED` (with `reason_code` `LINE_SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each voided advance returning its unit `PENDING_FUNDING → REGISTERED`) and moves the dealership `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`.

Every entity the cascade touches — the audit, the dealership, the credit line, each mutated unit and each voided advance — has its `version` incremented by 1. Incidents opened this way are not returned in the reconciliation response; they are discovered through the incident read and list capabilities, which expose `exposure_cents`, `cure_deadline_at`, `status`, `resolution_reason_code` and the four references, and which are the only discovery channel for incidents born inside a cascade.

**Abandoning a count.** An `AuditManager` may abort an audit that is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, supplying `abort_reason_code` — one of `DEALER_UNAVAILABLE`, `AUDITOR_REASSIGNED` or `DATA_ERROR`. Aborting draws no conclusions whatsoever: no incident is created, no unit's `eligibility_status` or `funding_status` changes, and `sot_exposure_cents` is left at 0. It is the compensating action for the observation transitions, so the three observation counters are restored to the configuration they had before the first reading — `units_verified_count` and `units_exception_count` return to 0 and `units_unobserved_count` returns to `units_in_scope_count` — which keeps the count-conservation identity of §2.12 reading identically before and after. Observations already recorded remain immutable and remain readable through both the audit's observation list and the per-unit observation history, which is the only channel that survives an abort. The dealership's `audit_in_progress` returns to false, its `next_audit_due_at` is **not** advanced (an abandoned count does not satisfy the cadence), and the audit's window is freed so a replacement can be scheduled over it. The audit's and the dealership's `version` each advance by 1.

### 3.7. Remittances and the Allocation Waterfall

Money moves in exactly one direction and through exactly one door: the `DealerPrincipal` remits, and the service allocates. There is no operator-side "post a payment to obligation X" capability, no manual adjustment and no reversal of an allocation. A remittance therefore names *what the money is for* rather than *where it goes*, and the waterfall decides the destinations deterministically. Because the same door serves four different purposes, the payload carries an `intent`: one of `CURTAILMENT`, `PRINCIPAL_PREPAYMENT`, `UNIT_PAYOFF` or `SOT_CURE`. Alongside it come `dealer_id`, `amount_cents` (strictly greater than 0), the dealership's own `payment_reference` (its wire or check identifier, stored verbatim and never interpreted), `expected_dealer_version`, and — depending on intent — `target_unit_id` and `target_obligation_id`.

**Who may remit, and against which view.** `dealer_id` must equal the calling `X-User-ID`; a `DealerPrincipal` cannot remit on another dealership's behalf, and the refusal is `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Every target named must belong to that same dealership. The remittance is the second of the two optimistically-concurrent writes described in §2.8: `expected_dealer_version` must equal the dealership's current `version` or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION`. The reason this particular write needs a version guard, when a payment is never blocked by a state precondition, is that the waterfall consumes obligations in a fixed order. A caller whose picture of the dealership predates an earlier allocation would be aiming money at obligations that are already `PAID`, and the money would silently land somewhere else — or in the residue. Forcing a re-read makes the dealership acknowledge the post-allocation ledger before remitting again.

**Intent and target must agree.** The pairing rules are exact. `CURTAILMENT` accepts an optional `target_unit_id` *or* an optional `target_obligation_id` but never both, and accepts neither for a portfolio-wide payment. `PRINCIPAL_PREPAYMENT`, `UNIT_PAYOFF` and `SOT_CURE` each require `target_unit_id` and forbid `target_obligation_id`. Any violation is `ERR_REMITTANCE_INTENT_TARGET_MISMATCH`. The permitted `funding_status` of a named unit also depends on intent: `CURTAILMENT`, `PRINCIPAL_PREPAYMENT` and `UNIT_PAYOFF` require the unit to be `FLOORED` or `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`, while `SOT_CURE` requires `SOT_OPEN` — it is the only intent that may name a unit already sold out of trust. A mismatch here is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FLOORED`.

**The waterfall.** Allocation is pure integer arithmetic with no division anywhere, so nothing is rounded and every cent is accounted for. The engine begins with the whole `amount_cents` as its remaining balance, walks the buckets that the intent selects, and writes an ordered `allocation_breakdown` in which each element is a record of `bucket`, `target_id`, `principal_cents` and `fee_cents` — appended in the exact order money was consumed, so the array is an audit trail of the allocation and not merely a summary. Within any single obligation, fees are always consumed before principal.

- **`CURTAILMENT`.** The candidate set is every obligation of this dealership whose `status` is `DELINQUENT` or `DUE`, narrowed to one obligation when `target_obligation_id` was supplied and to one unit's obligations when `target_unit_id` was supplied. The set is then split into two buckets consumed strictly in sequence: bucket 1 is every `DELINQUENT` obligation, bucket 2 every `DUE` obligation, each ordered by `due_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Delinquency is therefore paid down before currency, and within a bucket the oldest deadline is paid first. For each obligation in turn, the engine takes the smaller of the remaining balance and the fee gap (`late_fee_cents` minus `fee_paid_cents`), adding it to `fee_paid_cents` and to the remittance's `applied_fee_cents`; then the smaller of what is still remaining and the principal gap (`required_principal_cents` minus `principal_paid_cents`), adding it to `principal_paid_cents` and to `applied_principal_cents`, and simultaneously increasing the unit's `principal_paid_cents` and decreasing its `outstanding_principal_cents` by the same figure. An obligation whose `fee_paid_cents` now equals `late_fee_cents` *and* whose `principal_paid_cents` now equals `required_principal_cents` transitions to `PAID` — from `DUE` or from `DELINQUENT` alike, except that a `DUE` obligation carries no fee to clear, so its `PAID` condition is the principal one alone. The walk stops the moment the remaining balance reaches zero, leaving later obligations untouched. If both buckets are empty — that is, the dealership has no `DUE` or `DELINQUENT` obligation left after the target filters — the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_OBLIGATION` rather than accepted as pure residue.
- **`PRINCIPAL_PREPAYMENT`.** A single-bucket allocation against the named unit: the engine takes the smaller of `amount_cents` and the unit's `outstanding_principal_cents`, records it as principal in one breakdown element under the `PRINCIPAL_PREPAYMENT` bucket with the unit as `target_id`, and stops. Prepayment never touches fees and never marks an obligation `PAID`; it reduces the unit's principal ahead of schedule, which is what later makes a curtailment obligation cheap or free to satisfy.
- **`UNIT_PAYOFF`.** The payoff figure is the unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` plus the sum, over that unit's `DUE` and `DELINQUENT` obligations, of `late_fee_cents` minus `fee_paid_cents`. All-or-nothing: if `amount_cents` is below that figure the request is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_BELOW_PAYOFF` and nothing is allocated. Otherwise every unpaid fee is consumed first, in `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, and then the entire outstanding principal. The unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` is forced to 0, its `principal_paid_cents` becomes its full `advance_amount_cents`, and every obligation of the unit still `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `DELINQUENT` is `CANCELLED` in `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order. The unit moves to `PAID_OFF` — from `FLOORED` or from `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT`, and in the latter case the dealership's `delinquent_unit_count` also falls.
- **`SOT_CURE`.** The buckets are the named unit's out-of-trust incidents still `OPEN`, oldest first by `created_at` ascending. For each, the engine takes the smaller of the remaining balance and the incident's frozen `exposure_cents`, accumulating it into `applied_sot_cure_cents` — a separate accumulator from principal and fees, recorded in the breakdown under the `SOT_CURE` bucket with the incident as `target_id`. An incident that receives at least its full `exposure_cents` moves `OPEN → CURED` with `cured_at` stamped from the logical clock and `resolution_reason_code` `DEALER_PAYOFF`; the unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` falls and its `principal_paid_cents` rises by the cure amount; the unit returns `SOT_OPEN → FLOORED` with `eligibility_status` restored to `ELIGIBLE` and `ineligibility_reason_code` cleared to null; and the dealership's `open_sot_incident_count` and `open_sot_exposure_cents` both fall. This is the second cycle on the funding dimension: a unit that left the borrowing base as sold out of trust can re-enter it. An incident that receives less than its exposure stays `OPEN` with nothing resolved. No rule in this specification refuses a cure that arrives after `cure_deadline_at`; the deadline is a stamped, readable fact that the remedy machinery of §3.8 uses as justification, not a gate on the dealership's ability to pay.

**Residue, status and the closing cascade.** Whatever the waterfall could not place becomes `unapplied_cents`, and the money-conservation identity of §2.12 holds unconditionally: `amount_cents` always equals `applied_principal_cents` plus `applied_fee_cents` plus `applied_sot_cure_cents` plus `unapplied_cents`. The residue is added to the dealership's `unapplied_credit_cents`, where it sits as a recorded credit balance; it is not swept into any later obligation by the service. The remittance itself is created in `RECEIVED` and never observed there — `RECEIVED` is an instant that exists only inside the request. Allocation runs synchronously and the row is immediately transitioned to `APPLIED` when `unapplied_cents` is exactly 0, or to `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` when any residue remains, with `applied_at` stamped from the logical clock. Both are terminal, so a partially applied remittance is never revisited or topped up; the dealership simply remits again. The response always carries one of the two terminal statuses, never `RECEIVED`. Finally, a unit that reaches `outstanding_principal_cents` of 0 with every one of its obligations `PAID` or `CANCELLED` transitions to `PAID_OFF` even when the payment arrived as curtailment rather than as a payoff; a unit whose last `DELINQUENT` obligation was just cleared returns `DELINQUENT_CURTAILMENT → FLOORED` and decrements `delinquent_unit_count`; the dealership's `delinquent_unit_count` and `open_sot_incident_count` are recomputed and the risk assessment of §2.10 re-runs, which can rewrite `risk_score`, `risk_rating`, `audit_frequency_days` and `next_audit_due_at`; and the line's availability is recomputed per §2.11 so the capacity identity survives. The dealership's `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every mutated unit, obligation and incident. Remittances are readable individually — with the full `allocation_breakdown` — and as a filtered list, which is the only reverse-reachability channel from a `dealer_id`, `target_unit_id` or `target_obligation_id` back to the payments that touched it.

### 3.8. Covenant Testing, Suspension, Reinstatement and the Remedy Ladder

The final workflow is the credit agreement itself. Three covenants are tested every reporting period; a second failure suspends the facility; a suspension can be lifted; and if it is not, a three-rung remedy ladder walks the dealership out of the portfolio. Two things make this workflow demanding. The dual-variant coverage ratio of §2.6 is used with two different thresholds in two different decisions, and the ladder's rungs are each gated on a *different* dealership status, so the order in which remedies are issued and executed is not a matter of preference.

**Filing a covenant test.** A `CreditRiskManager` files a test against a dealership for a reporting period. The filing is an immutable event: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no capability to amend or withdraw it. The payload reports `period_end_date` (the fiscal close the figures cover, which also anchors the trailing out-of-trust window), `reported_tangible_net_worth_cents` (which may be negative — a dealership can be balance-sheet insolvent), and four figures that must each be zero or greater: `reported_ebitda_cents`, `reported_interest_expense_cents`, `reported_curtailment_paid_cents` and `reported_unrestricted_cash_cents`. The last of those is recorded for the covenant package and deliberately participates in neither ratio; it is stored and returned, nothing more. `trailing_sot_incident_count` is also named on the payload, but its authoritative value is derived by the service, not trusted from the filer: it is the number of out-of-trust incidents of this dealership whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CURED` or `CHARGED_OFF` — a cured or charged-off incident still counts against the borrower's history — whose `created_at` falls at or after `period_end_date` minus 364 days at 00:00:00.000Z and strictly before `period_end_date` plus 1 day at 00:00:00.000Z. That window is a full 365 calendar days ending on the period close inclusive.

Filing computes four things. `required_tangible_net_worth_cents` is `round_half_up` of the credit limit multiplied by 1000 basis points and divided by 10,000 — ten percent of the facility — read from the dealership's line whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; a `TERMINATED` line is ignored. Then both variants of the coverage ratio, over a common denominator of `reported_interest_expense_cents` plus `reported_curtailment_paid_cents`: `dscr_lender_standard` is `reported_ebitda_cents` divided by that denominator, and `dscr_dealer_adjusted` is `reported_ebitda_cents` plus `reported_interest_expense_cents`, divided by the same denominator. When the denominator is exactly zero — a period with neither interest nor curtailment paid — both variants are written as 9999.9999 rather than failing; a dealership with no debt service cannot fail a debt-service test. Both are quantized to exactly 4 decimal places with `round_half_up`, and every comparison below is made against the **rounded** value, so a ratio that would round up to the threshold satisfies it.

`failure_reason_codes` is then assembled in one fixed emission order — `CAPITAL` before `CASH_FLOW` before `OUT_OF_TRUST` — regardless of which combination breached:

- `CAPITAL` when `reported_tangible_net_worth_cents` is strictly less than `required_tangible_net_worth_cents`. Exactly meeting the requirement passes.
- `CASH_FLOW` when `dscr_lender_standard` is strictly below 1.2500. Exactly 1.2500 passes.
- `OUT_OF_TRUST` when the derived `trailing_sot_incident_count` is strictly greater than 1. One incident in the trailing year is tolerated; the second breaches.

`result` is `PASS` only when `failure_reason_codes` is empty, and `FAIL` otherwise. Note precisely which variant decides this: `dscr_lender_standard` is the only one consulted, because floor plan interest is a real cash cost the lender does not add back. `dscr_dealer_adjusted` is always computed and never affects `result`; its sole consumer is the reinstatement test below, at a different and higher threshold. The two must not be conflated, because a single filing can simultaneously breach the covenant and satisfy the reinstatement test — with EBITDA of 1,000,000 cents against interest of 400,000 and curtailment paid of 560,000, the lender-standard ratio is 1.0417 and the filing `FAIL`s with `CASH_FLOW`, while the dealer-adjusted ratio on the same numbers is 1.4583 and clears the 1.4000 reinstatement bar. Since the adjusted variant adds interest expense into the numerator, it is strictly greater than the lender-standard variant whenever interest expense is non-zero.

**What a filing does to the dealership.** `submitted_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`. The dealership's `last_covenant_result` is set to this filing's `result`, and on a `FAIL` its `covenant_breach_count` rises by 1. That counter is a running tally of failures since the last successful reinstatement, not a lifetime total. The risk assessment of §2.10 re-runs — `last_covenant_result` is one of its three inputs, so a `FAIL` immediately adds risk points and can shorten `audit_frequency_days` and pull `next_audit_due_at` forward. And when `covenant_breach_count` reaches 2, the second breach suspends the facility: the line moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason_code` `COVENANT_BREACH_LIMIT`, which voids every advance still `REQUESTED` on the line in `created_at` ascending order with `reason_code` `LINE_SUSPENDED`, returns each of those units `PENDING_FUNDING → REGISTERED`, and moves the dealership `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. The dealership's `version` advances by 1. A single `FAIL` suspends nothing; two do, and they need not be consecutive periods. Covenant tests are readable as a per-dealership list ordered by `period_end_date` descending — newest period first, an explicit exception to the default ordering of §2.3 — filterable by `result`, and that list is the only reverse-reachability channel from a dealership to its filings. It is also the one read in the service that the `AuditManager` and `FieldAuditor` roles cannot perform: covenant financials are not audit-programme information.

**Suspension is a funding stop, not a freeze.** While the line is `SUSPENDED` nothing can be funded, because every funding call re-checks that the line is `ACTIVE`, and no new unit may be registered against it. But the dealership keeps paying: remittances of every intent still apply, obligations still fall due and go delinquent under the nightly run, audits still run and still reconcile. Suspension stops the lender's money going out, not the borrower's money coming in — which is exactly what makes reinstatement reachable.

**Reinstatement.** A `CreditRiskManager` may lift a suspension, supplying `reason_code` — one of `COVENANT_CURE_PLAN`, `SOT_CURED` or `DELINQUENCY_CURED` — which is recorded on the audit trail and has no effect on the checks. The line must be `SUSPENDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LINE_NOT_ACTIVE` (the same code that guards activation's `PENDING` requirement). Three substantive conditions must then all hold, and any of them failing yields `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`: the dealership must have no out-of-trust incident in `OPEN` and therefore `open_sot_exposure_cents` of 0; its `delinquent_unit_count` must be 0; and the dealership's most recent covenant test — the one with the greatest `period_end_date`, breaking a tie on the greatest `created_at` — must have `dscr_dealer_adjusted` at or above 1.4000. This is the one place the dealer-adjusted variant governs, and its threshold is deliberately higher than the covenant minimum: a borrower being let back in must clear a stiffer bar than one merely staying in compliance, but measured on the more forgiving add-back basis. A line whose covenant test `FAIL`ed on the lender-standard reading can therefore still be reinstated. On success the line moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason_code` cleared to null, the dealership moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, and its `covenant_breach_count` is reset to 0 — the count starts fresh, so the next suspension again requires two failures. Both the line's and the dealership's `version` advance by 1. Because the whole cycle can repeat, both status dimensions are genuinely cyclic.

**Issuing a remedy.** A `CreditRiskManager` issues a remedy against a dealership with `action_type` — one of `LINE_SUSPENSION`, `REPOSSESSION` or `LINE_TERMINATION` — and `reason_code`, one of `SOT_INCIDENT`, `COVENANT_BREACH` or `CURTAILMENT_DELINQUENCY`. Issuance only records the intent; it changes nothing about the dealership, the line or any unit. The remedy is created in `ISSUED`, `issued_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`, and `justification_snapshot` freezes a copy of the dealership's `open_sot_incident_count`, `covenant_breach_count` and `delinquent_unit_count` as they read at the moment of issuance — so the record of *why* the remedy was warranted survives even after the dealership cures those very conditions. `units_repossessed_count` and `principal_charged_off_cents` are initialised to 0 and stay there until execution. The dealership's `version` advances by 1.

A remedy must be justified by the dealership's record at issuance, or it is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_JUSTIFIED`: `LINE_SUSPENSION` requires `open_sot_incident_count` greater than 0 or `covenant_breach_count` greater than 0; `REPOSSESSION` requires at least one unit of the dealership in `SOT_OPEN`; `LINE_TERMINATION` requires the dealership to be `DEFAULTED`. Remedies are readable individually and as a per-dealership list ordered by `created_at` ascending, filterable by `status` and `action_type` — a read the `DealerPrincipal` is not granted, since workout planning is lender-internal.

**Executing a remedy.** Execution is a second `CreditRiskManager` act, and the issuing-versus-executing separation of §2.9 is what makes it a second *operator*: the executing `X-User-ID` must differ from `issued_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_REMEDY_EXECUTOR`, even though both operators hold the same role. The remedy must still be `ISSUED`; an already-executed remedy is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_ISSUED`, which is what stops a repossession being run twice. Each `action_type` additionally re-checks its own status gate at execution time, so a dealership that cured its condition between issuance and execution cannot be remedied on a stale justification:

- **`LINE_SUSPENSION`** requires the dealership `ACTIVE` and the line `ACTIVE`. It drives the line `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason_code` `REMEDY_ACTION` and the full suspension cascade — `REQUESTED` advances voided in `created_at` ascending order, their units returned to `REGISTERED`, and the dealership `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. This is the third of the three distinct routes into suspension, and the reason code is what tells the three apart after the fact.
- **`REPOSSESSION`** requires the dealership `SUSPENDED` with `open_sot_incident_count` of at least 1. Every unit of the dealership in `SOT_OPEN` is processed in `created_at` ascending order and moves `SOT_OPEN → REPOSSESSED`. For each: its `OutOfTrustIncident` in `OPEN` moves to `CHARGED_OFF` with `charged_off_at` stamped from the logical clock and `resolution_reason_code` `REPOSSESSION_CHARGE_OFF`; the unit's `outstanding_principal_cents` is moved into the line's `charged_off_principal_cents` and then forced to 0, with the line's `outstanding_principal_cents` reduced by the same amount so the capacity identity of §2.11 still balances — the debt is not forgiven, it is reclassified, which is why `charged_off_principal_cents` is a separate term of that identity and never decreases; every obligation of the unit still `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `DELINQUENT` is `CANCELLED` in `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order; and the dealership's `open_sot_incident_count` and `open_sot_exposure_cents` fall. The remedy records `units_repossessed_count` and the total `principal_charged_off_cents` it moved. Finally the dealership moves `SUSPENDED → DEFAULTED`. `REPOSSESSED` is terminal for a unit: it can never be re-floored, and it is the one funding state that also releases its VIN for re-registration under §3.2.
- **`LINE_TERMINATION`** requires the dealership `DEFAULTED`, which in practice means a repossession has already been executed. The line moves to `TERMINATED` — reachable from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED` — with `available_credit_cents` forced to 0, and the dealership moves `DEFAULTED → TERMINATED`. Both are terminal and there is no path back from either; a terminated relationship is re-established, if at all, as a new dealership record.

Every execution stamps `executed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `executed_at` from the logical clock, moves the remedy `ISSUED → EXECUTED` (its only transition, and terminal), re-runs the availability computation of §2.11 and the risk assessment of §2.10, and advances the `version` of the remedy, the dealership and every entity the cascade touched by 1.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every route below is mounted under the `/floorplan` prefix, with the single exception of the clock-control route, which sits outside the business namespace because it is test infrastructure rather than a floor plan capability. All requests carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers of §2.1.

Status codes follow one rule. An endpoint that brings a new resource into existence responds `201 Created`; every other successful response is `200 OK`. Eleven endpoints therefore return `201`: onboarding a dealership, filing a covenant test, issuing a remedy, establishing a credit line, adding a ladder rung, registering a unit, requesting a funding advance, filing an aging exception request, scheduling an audit, recording an audit observation, and posting a remittance. Everything else returns `200`, including every state transition on an existing resource (funding, rejecting or reversing an advance, activating or reinstating a line, deciding an exception, starting, reconciling or aborting an audit, executing a remedy, moving title custody, classifying a unit ineligible), the nightly curtailment run — which creates no new resource of its own and returns a summary object — and the clock control route.
