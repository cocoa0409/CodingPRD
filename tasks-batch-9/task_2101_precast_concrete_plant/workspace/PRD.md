# Precast Concrete Plant API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A precast concrete plant is a factory whose product is poured, cured, tested and shipped on a schedule that never stops moving. Strand is pulled across a long casting bed on Monday afternoon, concrete is placed on Monday evening, steam runs overnight, cylinders are broken on Tuesday morning, and the bed is detensioned and stripped only when a quality engineer is satisfied that the concrete has reached the release strength the mix design promised. Today that chain of custody lives in a mix of clipboards, spreadsheets and radio traffic. When a mix is pulled from service mid-week, nobody can say with confidence which beds were about to pour it or which pieces are now sitting on hold. When an oversize girder rolls out of the yard, the permit envelope is checked by eye. When a piece is patched, the record of who found the defect, who decided the repair and who performed it lives in three different notebooks.

This document specifies the API for a plant production system that replaces all of that with a single, auditable system of record. It covers the mix book and its strength classes, casting bed occupancy and the casting cycle, strand tensioning and elongation verification, steam curing and its energy accounting, cylinder break testing and the release hold it gates, yard inventory and stacking, piece marks ordered by erection sequence, oversize haul permit linkage, and the nonconformance and repair disposition workflow. Every resource collection is mounted at the URL root as a peer collection; relationships between them are carried by explicit identifier references in request payloads rather than by nesting depth.

Three constraints shape the design. First, the plant runs on a **logical clock** rather than on wall time, so that a full production week can be exercised deterministically. Second, all derived money, strength, force and energy figures are computed by pinned formulas with pinned rounding, because a cent of drift in a cost allocation or a psi of drift in a qualified strength changes a business outcome. Third, safety-critical signatures are subject to **segregation of duties**: the operator who breaks the cylinders may not sign the release that those cylinders justify, and the engineer who dispositions a nonconformance may not perform the repair.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Plant Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401 Unauthorized) before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are present but the supplied role is not one of the roles listed for that endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403 Forbidden).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QCTechnician`, `QualityEngineer`, `YardForeman`, `PermitCoordinator` and `ProjectOwner`.

`PlantAdmin` is the plant IT and operations administrator. It owns the static plant topology — erection packages, boiler zones, casting beds and yard slots — and the logical clock, and holds no production, QC or shipping authority whatsoever. `ProductionSupervisor` runs the casting cycle: it schedules bed occupancy, tensions and aborts strand, casts, schedules and completes curing cycles, strips beds, creates pieces and assigns them to runs, and performs repairs. `QCTechnician` casts and breaks test cylinders and opens nonconformance reports. `QualityEngineer` owns the mix book and every quality decision: it qualifies, suspends, reinstates and retires mix designs, signs detensioning releases, dispositions nonconformances and reinspects repairs. `YardForeman` owns everything downstream of stripping: yard placement and pulls, load building, dispatch and delivery. `PermitCoordinator` applies for and issues oversize haul permits and cannot build or dispatch a load. `ProjectOwner` is the general contractor for one erection package and can confirm erection of that package's pieces and nothing else.

For `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QCTechnician`, `QualityEngineer`, `YardForeman` and `PermitCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever an audit trail or a duty-segregation check requires it — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a cylinder break, as an appended entry in `recorded_by_user_ids` on a cylinder set, and as `opened_by_user_id`, `dispositioned_by_user_id` and `repaired_by_user_id` on a nonconformance report — but it never compares that value to any row's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Project`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Project` compare the resource's project reference to `X-User-ID`: confirming that a piece has been erected is permitted only when the `project_id` of the targeted piece equals the acting `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (403 Forbidden).

Read access is uniform: every GET endpoint in §4 is available to all seven roles. Write authority is exclusive and never shared between roles — no two roles may perform the same write operation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filters

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many items are skipped before the page begins; when it is omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Several lists accept additional filters, and every filter is optional. Bed runs may be narrowed by `bed_id`, by `mix_design_id` and by `status`. Pieces may be narrowed by `project_id`, by `bed_run_id`, by `mix_design_id`, by `replaces_piece_id`, and by either of the two independent piece status dimensions — `production_status` and `qc_status`. Haul permits may be narrowed by `project_id` and by `status`; shipments by `project_id`, by `haul_permit_id` and by `status`. The cylinder sets of a run may be narrowed by `purpose`. The curing cycles of a boiler zone, the placement history of a piece, the placements at a yard slot and the nonconformance reports of a piece may each be narrowed by `status`. The remaining list endpoints — the casting beds of a boiler zone, the curing cycles of a run, the breaks of a cylinder set, the shipment lines that have carried a piece and the lines of a shipment — accept only `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.4. The Plant Logical Clock

The server keeps a single logical clock, and every instant it stamps or compares is read from that clock rather than from the host's wall time. A `PlantAdmin` moves the clock by calling `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a body carrying `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant; the call responds with 200 OK and a `data` object of the shape `{ "current_utc": "..." }`. Setting the clock mutates no entity, and the clock may be advanced or rewound freely.

Before the first clock call of a server's life, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Every server-stamped instant reads this clock: `qualified_at` on a mix design, `cast_at` on a bed run and on a cylinder set, `released_at` on a bed run and on a yard placement, `break_at` on a cylinder break, `placed_at` on a yard placement, `loaded_at` on a shipment line and `dispatched_at` on a shipment. Every window comparison reads it too, including the permit validity test against `valid_from` and `valid_to`.

### 2.5. Common Record Properties

Every stored resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply it.

Almost every resource additionally carries three system-managed fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that is 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every subsequent mutation of that row.

There is exactly one exception. A `ShipmentLine` is written once and never modified, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a shipment line.

A mutation, for the purposes of `updated_at` and `version`, means any change the server writes to a row — including changes the client did not directly request. Recomputing a project's counters, recomputing a slot's occupancy, recomputing a run's `piece_count`, adjusting a bed's committed force and lifting a QC hold as part of a cascade are all mutations of the affected row, and each one refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Identifiers that appear in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the response. Each entity family has its own 404 Not Found code: a `Project` reference that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; a `MixDesign` reference yields `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`; a `SteamZone`, `CastingBed` or `YardSlot` reference yields `ERR_PLANT_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a `BedRun` or `CuringCycle` reference yields `ERR_BED_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; a `CylinderSet` reference yields `ERR_CYLINDER_SET_NOT_FOUND`; a `Piece` or `YardPlacement` reference yields `ERR_PIECE_NOT_FOUND`; a `HaulPermit` reference yields `ERR_HAUL_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`; a `Shipment` reference yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and a `RepairDisposition` reference yields `ERR_REPAIR_DISPOSITION_NOT_FOUND`.

The same mapping governs identifiers supplied inside a request body, but body references are resolved only after the body itself has passed schema validation. The endpoints that resolve body references are casting bed registration, bed run scheduling, curing cycle scheduling, piece creation, run assignment of a piece, yard placement creation, haul permit application, shipment creation, shipment line creation, and nonconformance report creation.

### 2.7. Units, Precision and Rounding

The plant works in US customary units throughout: feet for dimensions, pounds for weight, pounds-force for break loads, psi for strength, kips for strand force, degrees Fahrenheit for temperature, boiler horsepower for steam demand and therms for energy. All money is carried as an integer number of **cents**; no monetary field is ever a decimal.

Wherever a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied at each named step of a calculation, never deferred to the end, and never applied twice to the same intermediate.

The pinned precisions are: `computed_psi` and `qualified_strength_psi` to whole psi; `age_hours`, `raw_mean_psi`, `elongation_deviation_pct`, `modeled_therms`, `energy_therms` and `energy_variance_pct` to two decimal places; `theoretical_elongation_in` to three decimal places; `computed_jacking_force_kips` and `committed_strand_force_kips` to two decimal places. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a whole-cent integer. Two quantities are never rounded at all: `occupied_weight_lb` carries the exact decimal sum of the contributing piece weights, and `gross_weight_lb` is an exact sum. Boiler horsepower is compared exactly as supplied. One step deliberately does not round: the per-piece cost base share is taken with a floor, never a round, and is described where the allocation is specified.

### 2.8. The Order of Refusal

When a request would fail more than one check, the response is decided by the **first** failing check in the following fixed order, and no later check runs. This ordering is a contract in its own right: it is what makes a request that is simultaneously in the wrong state and in breach of a duty rule return the state error rather than the duty error.

1. **Headers.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty, or the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role.** The supplied role is listed for the endpoint in §4, or the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, or the request fails with the matching 404 code from §2.6. This applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Project scope.** When the acting role is `ProjectOwner`, `X-User-ID` equals the `project_id` of the targeted piece, or the request fails with `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`. This applies only to confirming a piece erected.
5. **Body schema.** Every required field is present, every type matches, every enum value is a member of its declared set, every numeric field satisfies its declared range, every ISO 8601 datetime parses, and every interval satisfies its ordering rule — `scheduled_end` after `scheduled_start`, `planned_end` after `planned_start`, `valid_to` after `valid_from`. Any breach fails with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400 Bad Request). This applies to every endpoint that takes a request body.
6. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign identifier supplied in the body resolves, or the request fails with the matching 404 code. This applies to the ten endpoints listed in §2.6.
7. **Expected version.** The `expected_piece_version` or `expected_yard_slot_version` supplied in the body equals the parent row's current `version`, or the request fails with the matching 412 Precondition Failed code described in §2.9. This applies to nonconformance report creation and to yard placement creation.
8. **Primary state.** The row the request targets is in a state the requested transition accepts, or the request fails with that entity family's 409 Conflict state code: `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CURING_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_HAUL_PERMIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_YARD_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. This applies to every state-transition endpoint.
9. **Gating state of other rows.** States of entities other than the primary target are checked here: the mix design of a run being scheduled, tensioned or cast is `QUALIFIED` or the request fails with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_QUALIFIED`; the casting bed being tensioned is `AVAILABLE` and the yard slot being placed into is not `BLOCKED`, or the request fails with `ERR_PLANT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`; the permit linked to an oversize shipment being dispatched is `ISSUED` or the request fails with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`. This applies to bed run scheduling, tensioning, casting, yard placement creation and shipment dispatch.
10. **Quality and safety gates.** These guard four operations — casting a run, tensioning a run, requesting a release and adding a line to a shipment — and within this step the order is itself fixed: `ERR_NO_PIECES_ASSIGNED` first, then `ERR_ELONGATION_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, then `ERR_RELEASE_SET_MISSING`, then `ERR_CURING_NOT_COMPLETE`, then `ERR_NO_VALID_SPECIMENS`, then `ERR_RELEASE_STRENGTH_NOT_MET`, then `ERR_PIECE_QC_HOLD`.
11. **Segregation of duties.** Checked only after the state and safety gates above, and described in §2.10. It guards requesting a release and starting a repair, and raises `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
12. **Permit window.** The logical clock lies inside the linked permit's window when an oversize shipment is dispatched, or the request fails with `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`. This applies only to shipment dispatch.
13. **Occupancy and capacity.** These guard bed run scheduling, curing cycle scheduling, tensioning, yard placement creation and cylinder break recording, and within this step the order is fixed: `ERR_BED_OCCUPIED` first, then `ERR_STEAM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_BED_STRAND_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_YARD_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_YARD_STACK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_SPECIMEN_LIMIT_REACHED`.
14. **Uniqueness and linkage.** These guard mix design creation, boiler zone creation, casting bed creation, yard slot creation, haul permit application, piece creation, run assignment of a piece and shipment line creation, and within this step the order is fixed: `ERR_DUPLICATE_MIX_CODE` first, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_PIECE_MARK`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOAD_POSITION`, then `ERR_MIX_CLASS_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_PIECE_PROJECT_MISMATCH`.
15. **Load composition.** These guard shipment dispatch and erection confirmation, and within this step the order is fixed: `ERR_EMPTY_LOAD` first, then `ERR_ERECTION_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_ERECTION_PREDECESSOR_PENDING`.
16. **Permit envelope.** These guard shipment dispatch only, and within this step the order is fixed: `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED` first, then `ERR_PERMIT_ENVELOPE_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_PERMIT_WEIGHT_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Plant Records

Two write operations act on a parent row that the state machine deliberately does not protect, because repeating the operation is a legitimate business act. Both therefore require the client to prove it has seen the parent's latest state, using the parent's `version` as described in §2.5.

A `POST /repair-dispositions` request must carry `expected_piece_version`, a required field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Piece` named by `piece_id`. Opening a second nonconformance report on a piece is a legitimate operation — it supersedes the earlier open report — so nothing in the piece's state machine would reject it; the version check is the only defence against a writer acting on a stale view of the piece's QC standing. If `expected_piece_version` does not equal the piece's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PIECE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 Precondition Failed. On success the operation increments the piece's `version` by 1, so a client that replays the same expected value a second time is refused.

A `POST /yard-placements` request must carry `expected_yard_slot_version`, a required field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `YardSlot` named by `yard_slot_id`. A slot legitimately accepts several stacked pieces, so a later placement into the same slot is not blocked by any state precondition; the version check is what stops a writer from committing a placement computed against an out-of-date `occupied_weight_lb`. If `expected_yard_slot_version` does not equal the slot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_YARD_SLOT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 Precondition Failed. On success the operation increments the slot's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes an expected-version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two signatures in this plant may not be provided by the operator who produced the evidence for them.

The first governs the detensioning release. The `X-User-ID` of the `QualityEngineer` signing a release must not appear anywhere in the `recorded_by_user_ids` list of the release cylinder set that justifies it. A `QCTechnician` who breaks cylinders under a given `X-User-ID` has, by that act, disqualified that identifier from signing the corresponding release.

The second governs repair work. The `X-User-ID` of the `ProductionSupervisor` starting a repair must differ from the `dispositioned_by_user_id` written when the `QualityEngineer` decided the disposition.

Both checks raise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403 Forbidden), and both are evaluated at step 11 of §2.8 — after the state gates and after the quality and safety gates. A request that is both in the wrong state and in breach of a duty rule therefore returns the state error, not the duty error.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

Many operations in this plant touch more than one row. When they do, the effect is described here as a chain of numbered hops, and every hop of a chain must complete: a hop that is caused by an earlier hop is not optional, and stopping halfway would leave the plant's counters lying. Each row a cascade touches is mutated in the sense of §2.5 — its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented.

Several operations exist purely as **compensating actions**, and their contract is exactness rather than approximation. Aborting a tensioning must leave the casting bed's `committed_strand_force_kips` at precisely the value it held before the tensioning; pulling a placement with a restack reason must return the slot's `occupied_weight_lb` and `stacked_count` to precisely their pre-placement values; reinstating a suspended mix must return every blocked run and every held piece to precisely the standing it had before the suspension.

Derived counters are never incrementally patched by the client and are always recomputed from the rows they summarise. A `Project` carries six of them: `total_piece_count` counts every piece of the project in any status; `held_piece_count` counts the project's pieces whose `qc_status` is `HOLD`; `cleared_piece_count` counts the project's pieces whose `qc_status` is either `CLEARED` or `REPAIRED`; `scrapped_piece_count` counts the project's pieces whose `production_status` is `SCRAPPED`; `erected_piece_count` counts the project's pieces whose `production_status` is `ERECTED`; and `committed_cost_cents` sums `allocated_cost_cents` over the project's pieces for which that value has been written. All six initialise to 0 when the project is registered.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Mix Book: Qualification, Suspension and Retirement

Everything the plant pours starts life in the mix book. A `QualityEngineer` enters a **mix design** by supplying its plant mix `code`, its `strength_class`, its specified 28-day compressive strength `design_28day_psi`, the `required_release_psi` that concrete made from it must reach before a bed carrying it may be detensioned, its water/cement ratio `w_c_ratio`, its cement content `cement_lb_per_cy` in pounds per cubic yard, and `unit_cost_cents_per_cy`, the material cost of one cubic yard in cents. The `code` is between 1 and 32 characters long and must be unique across the whole mix book; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MIX_CODE`. The `strength_class` is one of `C40`, `C50`, `C60` or `C70`, and it later shifts the tensioning arithmetic. Both strength figures must be greater than zero, and `required_release_psi` must additionally be strictly less than `design_28day_psi` — a mix that claims to release at its own 28-day strength is a data-entry error. The `w_c_ratio` must be greater than 0 and no greater than 1, and both `cement_lb_per_cy` and `unit_cost_cents_per_cy` must be greater than zero. However the caller fills in the body, the new row's `status` is forced to `DRAFT`, its `suspension_reason` and its `qualified_at` are both null.

A draft mix is a proposal, not a product. Qualification — the `QualityEngineer`'s act of accepting the mix into production, moving `status` from `DRAFT` to `QUALIFIED` — is permitted only from `DRAFT`, and any other starting status is refused with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. Qualification stamps `qualified_at` with the current logical clock instant and increments the mix's `version` by 1. From that moment the mix may be scheduled onto a bed, tensioned and cast; a mix that is not `QUALIFIED` may do none of those things, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_QUALIFIED`.

Suspension is the emergency brake, and it is the widest cascade in the system. A `QualityEngineer` suspends a mix by supplying a free-text `reason`, which is stored as `suspension_reason`; the mix moves `QUALIFIED → SUSPENDED`, and suspension from any other status is refused with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. Three hops then fire in order. In the first hop, every bed run that names this mix and is still in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → BLOCKED` and has its `blocked_reason` set to `MIX_SUSPENDED`. In the second hop — caused by the first, not by the suspend call itself — every piece assigned to one of the runs that just became `BLOCKED` and whose `qc_status` is `PENDING` moves `PENDING → HOLD` and has its `qc_hold_reason` set to `MIX_SUSPENDED`. In the third hop, caused by the second, every project that owns a piece which just moved to `HOLD` recomputes both `held_piece_count` and `cleared_piece_count`.

Reinstatement reverses that cascade hop for hop. A `QualityEngineer` moves the mix `SUSPENDED → QUALIFIED`, which clears `suspension_reason` back to null while deliberately leaving `qualified_at` untouched — the first qualification instant is a historical fact and is never overwritten. In the first compensation hop, every bed run on this mix whose status is `BLOCKED` returns `BLOCKED → SCHEDULED` and has `blocked_reason` cleared to null. In the second compensation hop, caused by the first, every piece on one of those runs whose `qc_status` is `HOLD` **and** whose `qc_hold_reason` is `MIX_SUSPENDED` returns `HOLD → PENDING` with `qc_hold_reason` cleared to null — pieces held for any other reason are left exactly as they are. In the third compensation hop, every affected project recomputes `held_piece_count`, which returns it to its pre-suspension value. Reinstatement from any status other than `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`. This suspend-and-reinstate pair is the revision loop of the mix book: a mix may travel `QUALIFIED → SUSPENDED → QUALIFIED` as often as the plant needs.

Retirement is terminal and is reachable only from `SUSPENDED`, so retiring a live mix always costs a suspension first. A `QualityEngineer` moves the mix `SUSPENDED → RETIRED` and the mix's `version` increments by 1. Two conditions guard it, and both are reported with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_INVALID_STATE`: the mix must currently be `SUSPENDED`, and no bed run naming this mix may be in any of `SCHEDULED`, `BLOCKED`, `TENSIONED`, `CURING`, `RELEASE_READY` or `RELEASED`. Only runs that have already reached `STRIPPED` are compatible with retirement. No transition leaves `RETIRED`.

### 3.2. Erection Packages and Piece Marshalling

An **erection package** is the contractual unit the plant delivers against, and a `PlantAdmin` registers it by supplying the jobsite or contract `name`, the delivery city `jobsite_city` — which is carried for reporting only and drives no rule — and `erection_start_date`, the ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which the erector expects to start setting pieces. All six of the package's counters described in §2.11 begin at 0.

A `ProductionSupervisor` creates each **piece** against that package. Creation takes the owning `project_id`, the intended `mix_design_id`, the erection mark `piece_mark` that will be stencilled on the concrete, the `erection_sequence_no` that fixes the order in which the erector will set it, the four physical measurements `length_ft`, `width_ft`, `height_ft` and `weight_lb`, and the placed concrete volume `concrete_volume_cy` in cubic yards, which later feeds the run's cost allocation. Every one of those numeric measurements must be greater than zero, and `erection_sequence_no` must be greater than zero as well. A recast replacement additionally names the piece it supersedes through the optional `replaces_piece_id`, which points at another piece and is otherwise null. The `piece_mark` must be unique within its own project — two projects may reuse the same mark freely — and a collision inside one project is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PIECE_MARK`.

At creation the server derives `requires_haul_permit` from the dimensions alone: it is true when `width_ft` is greater than 8.50 feet, or `height_ft` is greater than 13.50 feet, or `length_ft` is greater than 53.00 feet, and false otherwise. Each of those three comparisons is strictly greater, so a piece measuring exactly 8.50 feet wide is legal. A new piece begins with `production_status` `PLANNED` and `qc_status` `PENDING`; its `qc_hold_reason` is null, its `bed_run_id` is null, its `allocated_cost_cents` is null and its `repair_count` is 0. Its owning project recomputes `total_piece_count`.

The two piece status dimensions run independently of one another for the whole life of the piece, and neither one is derived from the other. `production_status` — one of `PLANNED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CAST`, `STRIPPED`, `YARDED`, `LOADED`, `SHIPPED`, `ERECTED` or `SCRAPPED` — records the physical whereabouts of the piece, and both `ERECTED` and `SCRAPPED` are terminal. `qc_status` — one of `PENDING`, `HOLD`, `CLEARED`, `NONCONFORMING`, `REPAIRED` or `REJECTED` — records its quality standing, and `REJECTED` is terminal. `qc_hold_reason` is populated only while the piece is on hold or nonconforming, and takes one of `AWAITING_RELEASE_STRENGTH`, `MIX_SUSPENDED` or `OPEN_NCR`; whenever `qc_status` is neither `HOLD` nor `NONCONFORMING`, `qc_hold_reason` is null.

Marshalling a piece onto a bed is the `ProductionSupervisor`'s act of binding it to a run, moving `production_status` from `PLANNED` to `SCHEDULED`. The request names the target run through `bed_run_id`. Three conditions apply. The piece must currently be `PLANNED`, and a piece in any other production status is refused with `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`. The run must be in `SCHEDULED` or `TENSIONED` — strand may still be added to a bed that is pulled but not yet poured — and a run in any other status is refused with `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. Finally the piece's own `mix_design_id` must equal the run's `mix_design_id`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_MIX_CLASS_MISMATCH`. On success the piece's `bed_run_id` is set and the run's `piece_count` is recomputed as the number of pieces assigned to it whose `production_status` is anything other than `SCRAPPED`.

### 3.3. Casting Beds, Bed-Run Scheduling and Strand Tensioning

A `PlantAdmin` registers each **casting bed** with its identifier `bed_code`, which must be unique across the plant and collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`; the `steam_zone_id` of the boiler zone that serves it; its `bed_type`, one of `LONG_LINE_PRESTRESS`, `SELF_STRESSING_FORM` or `FLAT_BED_RC`; its casting length between abutments `length_ft` in feet; its abutment capacity `max_strand_force_kips` in kips; and `turnaround_buffer_hours`, the whole number of stripping and clean-up hours — zero or more — that is appended to every run's window when bed occupancy is tested. The bed's `status` defaults to `AVAILABLE`, and the only way a bed is ever unavailable at registration is for the operator to seed the optional `status` as `DOWN`. `DOWN` is absorbing: no transition leaves it, and any attempt to tension a run on a bed that is `DOWN` is refused with `ERR_PLANT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`. A new bed's `committed_strand_force_kips` initialises to 0.00 and its `cumulative_cycle_count` to 0.

A `ProductionSupervisor` schedules a **bed run** — one occupancy of one bed by one pour — by naming the `bed_id`, the `mix_design_id` to be poured, and the window `scheduled_start` (inclusive) and `scheduled_end` (exclusive), both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly after the start. The mix must be `QUALIFIED` at scheduling time or the request is refused with `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_QUALIFIED`. Occupancy is then tested: a run's true footprint on the bed runs from its `scheduled_start` up to its `scheduled_end` plus that bed's `turnaround_buffer_hours`, and a new run is rejected with `ERR_BED_OCCUPIED` when that footprint intersects the footprint of any other run on the same bed whose status is not yet `STRIPPED`. A stripped run has vacated the bed and no longer blocks anything. The new run starts in `SCHEDULED` with `piece_count` 0, with `blocked_reason`, `strand_count`, `strand_diameter_in`, `measured_elongation_in`, `theoretical_elongation_in`, `elongation_deviation_pct`, `computed_jacking_force_kips`, `cast_at` and `released_at` all null, and with all eight of its cost fields null.

**Tensioning** is where the strand arithmetic happens. The `ProductionSupervisor` reports three field measurements: `strand_count`, the number of strands pulled; `strand_diameter_in`, which is either `0.500` or `0.600` inches; and `measured_elongation_in`, the elongation the crew actually measured at the abutment. The run must be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`), the bed must be `AVAILABLE` (otherwise `ERR_PLANT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`, which also covers a bed already `OCCUPIED`), and the mix must still be `QUALIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_QUALIFIED`).

The jacking force is derived as follows. Each strand has a nominal area determined by its diameter: a half-inch strand is 0.153 square inches and a six-tenths-inch strand is 0.217 square inches. The base force in one strand is that area multiplied by the ultimate strand stress of 270.0 ksi and then by the jacking fraction of 0.75. A single adjustment percentage is then formed by adding two contributions: the seating loss of the bed's form family, which is 2.5 percent for `LONG_LINE_PRESTRESS`, 4.5 percent for `SELF_STRESSING_FORM` and 0.0 percent for `FLAT_BED_RC`; and the temperature adjustment of the mix's strength class, which is 0.0 percent for `C40` and for `C50` and 1.5 percent for `C60` and for `C70`. The base force is scaled by one plus that combined percentage expressed as a fraction, and the required force per strand is the greater of that scaled figure and the floor of 32.0 kips per strand — a floor high enough that on some combinations it swallows the strength-class contribution entirely. Multiplying the required force per strand by `strand_count` and rounding half up to two decimal places gives `computed_jacking_force_kips`.

Two verification figures follow from the same pull. `theoretical_elongation_in` is obtained by dividing `computed_jacking_force_kips` by `strand_count` to recover the per-strand force, multiplying by the bed's `length_ft` and then by 12 to convert that length to inches, dividing by the product of the strand's nominal area and the strand modulus of 28500.0 ksi, and rounding half up to three decimal places. `elongation_deviation_pct` is the difference between `measured_elongation_in` and `theoretical_elongation_in`, divided by `theoretical_elongation_in`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; it is signed, so a short pull reads negative.

Two safety gates then decide whether the pull stands. If `computed_jacking_force_kips` exceeds the bed's `max_strand_force_kips`, the tensioning is rejected with `ERR_BED_STRAND_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. If the absolute value of `elongation_deviation_pct` exceeds the elongation tolerance of 5.0 percent, the tensioning is rejected with `ERR_ELONGATION_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`; a deviation of exactly 5.0 percent in either direction is within tolerance and passes. When both gates are satisfied, the run moves `SCHEDULED → TENSIONED`, the three computed figures are written on it, the bed moves `AVAILABLE → OCCUPIED`, and the bed's `committed_strand_force_kips` increases by this run's `computed_jacking_force_kips`.

**Aborting a tensioning** is the compensating action for that pull, and it exists because crews do detension a bad pull and start over. Performed by the `ProductionSupervisor` with a free-text `reason`, it moves the run `TENSIONED → SCHEDULED`. The bed's `committed_strand_force_kips` decreases by exactly the `computed_jacking_force_kips` this run had contributed, restoring the bed's committed force to precisely its pre-tension value, and the bed moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE`. Six fields on the run are reset to null: `strand_count`, `strand_diameter_in`, `measured_elongation_in`, `theoretical_elongation_in`, `elongation_deviation_pct` and `computed_jacking_force_kips`. The bed may then be tensioned again, which makes `SCHEDULED → TENSIONED → SCHEDULED` the plant's re-pull loop.

### 3.4. Casting, Boiler Zones and Steam Curing Energy

Steam is a shared and finite plant utility, and it is modelled as a set of **boiler zones**. A `PlantAdmin` registers a zone with its identifier `zone_code`, which must be unique across the plant and collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`; `boiler_capacity_bhp`, the maximum aggregate boiler horsepower the zone can serve at any single instant; and `therm_rate_cents_per_therm`, the cost of steam in cents per therm. Both numbers must be greater than zero. Each casting bed names the zone that serves it, and every curing cycle names the zone that supplies it.

A `ProductionSupervisor` schedules a **curing cycle** against a bed run. The request names the supplying `steam_zone_id` and describes the thermal profile: `planned_start` and `planned_end`, the ISO 8601 UTC bounds of steam demand with the start inclusive and the end exclusive and the end strictly after the start; `ramp_rate_f_per_hr`, the temperature rise rate in degrees Fahrenheit per hour, which must be greater than zero; `soak_temp_f`, the peak soak temperature in degrees Fahrenheit, which must be greater than 60; `soak_hours`, the number of hours held at that soak temperature, which must be greater than zero; and `steam_demand_bhp`, the boiler horsepower the cycle draws across its whole window, which must be greater than zero. The cycle's `bed_run_id` is taken from the run the call is made against, and the new cycle begins in `SCHEDULED` with `metered_therms`, `energy_therms`, `energy_source`, `energy_variance_pct` and `energy_cost_cents` all null and `variance_flagged` false.

Before the cycle is persisted, the zone's boiler is checked for headroom. The candidate set is every curing cycle already drawing on that same zone whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, plus the cycle being created; cycles that have reached `COMPLETED` have released their boiler load and are excluded. Each candidate occupies the half-open interval from its `planned_start` up to but not including its `planned_end`, so a cycle that ends at a given instant and one that starts at that same instant never overlap. Demand is evaluated at each candidate's `planned_start`: the demand at such an instant is the sum of `steam_demand_bhp` over every candidate whose `planned_start` is at or before that instant and whose `planned_end` is strictly after it. The zone's peak concurrent demand is the largest of those sums. If the peak exceeds `boiler_capacity_bhp` the request is rejected with `ERR_STEAM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the comparison is strictly greater, so a schedule that exactly saturates the boiler is admitted.

At creation the cycle's `modeled_therms` is computed immediately from the profile alone and stored. The ramp duration in hours is the difference between `soak_temp_f` and the ambient baseline of 60.0 degrees Fahrenheit, divided by `ramp_rate_f_per_hr`. The cooldown duration in hours is that same temperature difference divided by the plant's fixed cooldown rate of 20.0 degrees Fahrenheit per hour. The weighted duration is then one half of the ramp hours, plus the full `soak_hours`, plus one quarter of the cooldown hours — the ramp and the cooldown draw less steam than the soak, and are discounted accordingly. Multiplying `steam_demand_bhp` by the therm-per-boiler-horsepower-hour factor of 0.335 and then by the weighted duration, and rounding half up to two decimal places, gives `modeled_therms`.

**Casting** the run is the `ProductionSupervisor`'s act of placing the concrete, moving the run `TENSIONED → CURING`. The run must be in `TENSIONED` (otherwise `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`), its mix must still be `QUALIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_MIX_DESIGN_NOT_QUALIFIED`), and its `piece_count` must be at least 1 — pouring an empty bed is refused with `ERR_NO_PIECES_ASSIGNED`. Casting takes no request body. On success the run's `cast_at` is stamped from the logical clock and four effects follow. Every piece assigned to the run whose `production_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CAST`. Those same pieces move `qc_status` from `PENDING` to `HOLD` with `qc_hold_reason` set to `AWAITING_RELEASE_STRENGTH` — green concrete is on hold by default until cylinders say otherwise. Every curing cycle of this run whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → RUNNING`. Finally every project owning one of the affected pieces recomputes `held_piece_count`.

**Completing** a curing cycle moves it `RUNNING → COMPLETED` and is refused with `ERR_CURING_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` from any other status. The `ProductionSupervisor` may supply an optional `metered_therms` reading, and whether that value is present selects one of two accounting variants, which are otherwise identical in shape.

When `metered_therms` is omitted, the cycle is accounted under the `MODELED` variant: `energy_source` is set to `MODELED`, `energy_therms` takes the value of `modeled_therms`, and `energy_variance_pct` stays null. Under this variant `variance_flagged` stays false no matter how far the real boiler drifted, because there is no meter reading to compare against. When `metered_therms` is supplied, the cycle is accounted under the `METERED` variant: `energy_source` is set to `METERED`, `energy_therms` takes the supplied `metered_therms`, and `energy_variance_pct` is computed as the difference between `metered_therms` and `modeled_therms`, divided by `modeled_therms`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places. The comparison is made against the already-rounded `modeled_therms` stored at creation, not against the unrounded product — rounding happens before the comparison. `variance_flagged` is true when `energy_variance_pct` is not null and its absolute value is greater than the energy variance tolerance of 15.0 percent, and false otherwise; a variance of exactly 15.0 percent in either direction does not flag.

In both variants the charge is the same shape: `energy_cost_cents` is `energy_therms` multiplied by the supplying zone's `therm_rate_cents_per_therm`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The difference between the two variants is real money. A cycle drawing 40 boiler horsepower, soaking at 150 degrees Fahrenheit, ramping at 30 degrees per hour and holding for 8 hours models out at 142.38 therms, which at 95 cents per therm bills 13526 cents; if the operator instead reports a meter reading of 170.00 therms, the same cycle bills 16150 cents and flags a 19.40 percent variance. Completing a cycle also re-evaluates the owning run's automatic move out of `CURING`, described next.

### 3.5. Cylinder Testing and the Detensioning Release

A `QCTechnician` casts a **cylinder set** alongside a run by declaring its `purpose` — `RELEASE`, `ACCEPTANCE_28DAY` or `INFORMATION` — the `specimen_count` of cylinders moulded into the set, which must be at least 2 and at most 6, and the boolean `cured_with_member`, which records whether the cylinders cured under the same tarp and steam as the members themselves. The set's `bed_run_id` is taken from the run the call is made against. Only a set whose `purpose` is `RELEASE` gates detensioning; the other two purposes are informational and gate nothing. The set's `cast_at` is stamped from the logical clock at creation and becomes the zero point for specimen age. A new set begins in `CAST` with `recorded_break_count` and `valid_break_count` at 0, `raw_mean_psi`, `qualified_strength_psi` and `strength_variant` all null, and an empty `recorded_by_user_ids` list.

Recording a **break** is the `QCTechnician`'s report of one cylinder crushed on the machine, and it carries just two measurements: `load_lbf`, the machine failure load in pounds-force, which must be greater than zero, and `diameter_in`, the cylinder diameter, which is either `4.00` or `6.00` inches. Three preconditions apply. The set's `recorded_break_count` must be strictly less than its `specimen_count`; a set with no unbroken specimen left is refused with `ERR_SPECIMEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. The owning run must not have reached `STRIPPED`; a break recorded against a set whose run is already stripped is refused with `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. And the set's `cylinder_set_id` must resolve, per §2.6.

Each new break stamps `break_at` from the logical clock and derives two values. `age_hours` is the elapsed time from the set's `cast_at` to this break's `break_at`, expressed in hours by dividing the elapsed seconds by 3600 and rounding half up to two decimal places. `computed_psi` is the failure load divided by the cylinder's cross-sectional area and rounded half up to whole psi, where the area in square inches is pi times the square of half the `diameter_in`, taking pi as 3.14159265358979 — which makes the area of a 4.00-inch cylinder 12.566370614359172 square inches and that of a 6.00-inch cylinder 28.274333882308138 square inches. The acting `X-User-ID` is written on the break as `recorded_by_user_id` and appended to the set's `recorded_by_user_ids` list when it is not already present, so that the list holds the distinct identifiers that have touched the set, in first-record order.

Every new break then triggers a **complete recomputation of the whole set from scratch**, including the `inclusion_status` of breaks recorded earlier. The set's breaks are taken in order of `break_at` ascending and, where two share an instant, `created_at` ascending. `raw_mean_psi` is the arithmetic mean of `computed_psi` over *all* recorded breaks, regardless of inclusion, rounded half up to two decimal places. Which of two variants runs next depends only on `specimen_count`, and the chosen variant is written to `strength_variant`.

A set with a `specimen_count` of exactly 2 is evaluated under the `SET_AVERAGE` variant. No outlier trim runs at all: every break keeps `inclusion_status` `VALID`, and all of them are included. A single weak cylinder therefore drags the whole set down. A set with a `specimen_count` of 3 or more is evaluated under the `TRIMMED_AVERAGE` variant. An acceptance band is built around `raw_mean_psi` using the outlier tolerance of 10.0 percent: the lower bound is `raw_mean_psi` multiplied by 1 minus one tenth, and the upper bound is `raw_mean_psi` multiplied by 1 plus one tenth. A break keeps `inclusion_status` `VALID` when its `computed_psi` is greater than or equal to the lower bound and less than or equal to the upper bound; otherwise its `inclusion_status` becomes `DISCARDED_OUTLIER`. The band is computed once, from `raw_mean_psi` taken over every break, and is never recomputed from the surviving subset — this is a single pass, not an iteration to convergence. The included breaks are those left marked `VALID`.

`valid_break_count` is then the number of included breaks and `recorded_break_count` the number of breaks of any inclusion. If fewer than 2 breaks survive, the evidence set is too thin and `qualified_strength_psi` is null; otherwise `qualified_strength_psi` is the arithmetic mean of `computed_psi` over the included breaks, rounded half up to whole psi. The two variants can disagree materially: three cylinders reading 3600, 3550 and 2900 psi yield 3575 under `TRIMMED_AVERAGE`, because the 2900 falls outside the band, but would yield 3350 under `SET_AVERAGE` — the same three cylinders either clear or fail a 3500 psi release threshold depending on which variant applies.

After each recomputation the set's own `status` is decided by a single rule with three outcomes. The set is `TESTING` when `valid_break_count` is below 2. It is `RELEASE_MET` when `qualified_strength_psi` is greater than or equal to the `required_release_psi` of the run's mix design. It is `RELEASE_NOT_MET` otherwise. The first break always takes a set out of `CAST` into `TESTING`, and from there the set may move `TESTING → RELEASE_MET` or `TESTING → RELEASE_NOT_MET` as soon as two specimens survive. A later, stronger cylinder can lift a set `RELEASE_NOT_MET → RELEASE_MET`, and a later, weaker one can push it `RELEASE_MET → RELEASE_NOT_MET`; that pair is the retest loop. A set can even fall back `RELEASE_MET → TESTING` when a new break widens the outlier band enough to discard specimens that were previously valid and leave fewer than two survivors, in which case `qualified_strength_psi` is reset to null.

Whenever a set reaches `RELEASE_MET`, a second hop is attempted. If that set's `purpose` is `RELEASE`, the owning run's status is `CURING`, and every curing cycle of that run has reached `COMPLETED`, the run moves `CURING → RELEASE_READY` automatically — no client request names the run. The same automatic evaluation is performed after every curing cycle completion, which is what allows the last cycle to complete after the cylinders have already qualified and still release the run. The run must have at least one curing cycle for this to fire. Two further hops follow the run's move: every piece on the run whose `qc_status` is `HOLD` and whose `qc_hold_reason` is `AWAITING_RELEASE_STRENGTH` moves `HOLD → CLEARED` with `qc_hold_reason` cleared to null, and then every project owning such a piece recomputes both `held_piece_count` and `cleared_piece_count`. A set that later falls back out of `RELEASE_MET` does **not** drag the run back: a run that has already reached `RELEASE_READY` or `RELEASED` stays where it is.

**Requesting the release** is the `QualityEngineer`'s detensioning signature, moving the run `RELEASE_READY → RELEASED` and stamping `released_at` from the logical clock. Its state gate deliberately admits a run in either `CURING` or `RELEASE_READY`, so that a premature attempt is answered by a specific quality error rather than a generic state error; a run in any other status is refused with `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. Past that gate, the quality conditions are checked in the fixed order of step 10 in §2.8. The run must have a cylinder set whose `purpose` is `RELEASE`, or the request fails with `ERR_RELEASE_SET_MISSING`. Every curing cycle on the run must have reached `COMPLETED`, and the run must have at least one — a run with no cycle at all, or with any cycle still `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, fails with `ERR_CURING_NOT_COMPLETE`. That release set's `qualified_strength_psi` must not be null, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_VALID_SPECIMENS`. And `qualified_strength_psi` must be greater than or equal to the mix design's `required_release_psi`, or the request fails with `ERR_RELEASE_STRENGTH_NOT_MET`; a qualified strength exactly equal to the requirement passes. Only after all of those does the duty check of §2.10 run: the signing `X-User-ID` must not appear in the release set's `recorded_by_user_ids`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because these gates are ordered, only a run that is genuinely `RELEASE_READY` can satisfy all of them and actually transition.

### 3.6. Stripping the Bed and Allocating the Run's Cost

Stripping is the `ProductionSupervisor`'s last act on a run and the moment its books are closed. The run moves `RELEASED → STRIPPED`, which is terminal; a strip issued from any other status is refused with `ERR_BED_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, as is any subsequent action on a run that has already reached `STRIPPED`.

The cost allocation runs in a pinned order. First the run's poured volume is taken as the sum of `concrete_volume_cy` over the pieces on the run whose `production_status` is `CAST` at the moment of the strip call, rounded half up to two decimal places; scrapped pieces are excluded from the run's volume and pay nothing. `concrete_cost_cents` is that volume multiplied by the mix design's `unit_cost_cents_per_cy`, rounded half up to whole cents. `strand_cost_cents` is the run's `computed_jacking_force_kips` multiplied by the strand price of 145 cents per kip, rounded half up to whole cents. The run's `energy_cost_cents` is the exact sum of the `energy_cost_cents` of the run's curing cycles whose `status` is `COMPLETED`; cycles left `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` contribute nothing. `form_setup_cents` is keyed on the bed's `bed_type` alone: 180000 cents for `LONG_LINE_PRESTRESS`, 240000 cents for `SELF_STRESSING_FORM` and 90000 cents for `FLAT_BED_RC`.

`direct_subtotal_cents` is the sum of those four figures — concrete, strand, energy and form setup. The overhead tier is then keyed on `direct_subtotal_cents`, the pre-overhead figure, and deliberately never on the total, which would be circular. `overhead_rate_pct` is 22.0 when `direct_subtotal_cents` is below 500000 cents, 18.0 when it is at least 500000 cents but below 1000000 cents, and 14.0 when it is at least 1000000 cents. `overhead_cents` is `direct_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `overhead_rate_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. `total_cost_cents` is `direct_subtotal_cents` plus `overhead_cents`.

That total is then split across the pieces, exactly to the cent and with no residue left over. The pieces that share the cost are those on the run whose `production_status` is `CAST`, taken in order of `erection_sequence_no` ascending and, where two share a sequence number, `created_at` ascending. The base share is the total divided by the number of those pieces, taken as a floor rather than a round. The remainder is the total minus the base share multiplied by the piece count, and the whole remainder is given to the first piece in that ordering: that piece receives the base share plus the remainder, and every other piece receives exactly the base share. Each piece's figure is written to its `allocated_cost_cents`.

Four further effects complete the strip. Those same `CAST` pieces move `production_status` `CAST → STRIPPED`. Every project owning one of them recomputes `committed_cost_cents`. The bed's `committed_strand_force_kips` decreases by this run's `computed_jacking_force_kips`, releasing the abutment capacity for the next pour. And the bed moves `OCCUPIED → AVAILABLE` while its `cumulative_cycle_count` increases by 1 — the count of complete casting cycles that bed has turned.

### 3.7. The Storage Yard: Placement, Restack and Pull

A `PlantAdmin` registers each **yard slot** with its identifier `slot_code`, which must be unique across the yard and collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`; the `bay_code` that groups it; `capacity_weight_lb`, the maximum total stored weight it can carry, which must be greater than zero; and `max_stack_count`, the maximum number of active placements it can hold, which must be at least 1. A slot's `status` defaults to `EMPTY`, and the only way a slot is ever closed is for the operator to seed the optional `status` as `BLOCKED` at registration. `BLOCKED` is absorbing: it never changes afterwards, and a placement targeting a blocked slot is refused with `ERR_PLANT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`. A new slot's `occupied_weight_lb` and `stacked_count` both initialise to 0.

A `YardForeman` stores a stripped piece by creating a **yard placement**, naming the `piece_id`, the `yard_slot_id`, the 1-based `stack_level` within the stack, which must be at least 1, and the `expected_yard_slot_version` required by §2.9. The piece must be in `production_status` `STRIPPED`, and a piece in any other production status is refused with `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`. The slot's `status` must be `EMPTY` or `PARTIAL`.

Admission is then tested against the slot's live occupancy, before the placement is inserted. If the slot's current `occupied_weight_lb` plus the piece's `weight_lb` would exceed `capacity_weight_lb`, the placement is rejected with `ERR_YARD_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. If the slot's current `stacked_count` plus one would exceed `max_stack_count`, the placement is rejected with `ERR_YARD_STACK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Both comparisons are strictly greater, so a placement that exactly fills a slot by weight or by count is admitted and simply leaves the slot full.

On success the placement is written with `status` `ACTIVE`, `placed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `released_at` and `release_reason` both null. The piece moves `production_status` `STRIPPED → YARDED`. The slot is then recomputed from its active placements alone: `occupied_weight_lb` becomes the exact sum of `weight_lb` over the pieces of the slot's `ACTIVE` placements, with no rounding whatsoever; `stacked_count` becomes the number of those active placements; and the slot's `status` becomes `EMPTY` when `stacked_count` is 0, `FULL` when `stacked_count` equals `max_stack_count` or `occupied_weight_lb` equals `capacity_weight_lb`, and `PARTIAL` in every other case — except that a slot seeded `BLOCKED` stays `BLOCKED` forever and is never recomputed into another value. Released placements never contribute to either figure, which is precisely what makes placement and pull conserve the plant's stored weight.

**Pulling** a placement moves it `ACTIVE → RELEASED` and is the compensating action for storing a piece. The `YardForeman` supplies a `release_reason` of `LOAD`, `SCRAP` or `RESTACK`; the placement's `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the `release_reason` recorded. A pull issued against a placement that is already `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_YARD_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Whatever the reason, the slot decrements: `occupied_weight_lb` falls by the piece's `weight_lb`, `stacked_count` falls by 1, and the slot's `status` is recomputed by the same rule. With a `RESTACK` reason the piece moves `production_status` `YARDED → STRIPPED`, returning the slot's occupancy to exactly its pre-placement value and making `STRIPPED → YARDED → STRIPPED` the yard's restack loop; the piece may then be placed again, in the same slot or another. A `LOAD` release accompanies loading the piece onto a trailer and takes the piece `YARDED → LOADED`, and a `SCRAP` release accompanies the scrapping of a piece and takes it to `SCRAPPED`; both of those are driven by the workflows described in §3.8 and §3.9 rather than by an explicit pull request.

### 3.8. Load Building, Oversize Permits, Dispatch and Erection

Oversize haul is a permitted activity, and a `PermitCoordinator` applies for a **haul permit** ahead of the load. The application names the `project_id` the permit was pulled for and the state `permit_number`, which must be unique across all permits and collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_CODE`; the two-letter `issuing_state`; the envelope the permit authorises as `max_width_ft`, `max_height_ft`, `max_length_ft` and `max_gross_weight_lb`, all of which must be greater than zero; the validity window `valid_from` and `valid_to`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly after the start and both bounds inclusive; and `escort_count`, the number of pilot-car escorts the permit mandates, which must be zero or more and which later feeds the permit fee. A new permit begins in `APPLIED`. Issuing it moves it `APPLIED → ISSUED`, and an issue attempt from any other status is refused with `ERR_HAUL_PERMIT_INVALID_STATE`.

A `YardForeman` opens a **shipment** — a trailer load — by naming the `project_id` it delivers for and the `scheduled_departure`, an ISO 8601 UTC planned departure instant, and may optionally link a permit through `haul_permit_id`. Linking is optional at every point because a legal load travels without a permit. The new shipment begins in `BUILDING` with `line_count` 0, `is_oversize` false, `permit_fee_cents` 0, `max_width_ft`, `max_height_ft` and `max_length_ft` all 0, `dispatched_at` null, and `gross_weight_lb` at the tractor tare of 32000 pounds.

Adding a **shipment line** loads one yarded piece onto the trailer. The `YardForeman` names the `piece_id` and the 1-based `load_position`, which must be at least 1 and must be unique within the shipment; a repeat position is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOAD_POSITION`. Position 1 is loaded first and buried deepest, and therefore comes off the trailer last. The line's `shipment_id` is taken from the shipment the call is made against, and `loaded_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The shipment must be in `BUILDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The piece must be in `production_status` `YARDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`. Its `qc_status` must be either `CLEARED` or `REPAIRED`; a piece in any other quality standing is refused with `ERR_PIECE_QC_HOLD`. And the piece's `project_id` must equal the shipment's `project_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PIECE_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. On success the piece moves `production_status` `YARDED → LOADED`; then, as a consequence of that move, its `ACTIVE` yard placement moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `release_reason` `LOAD`; then, as a consequence of that release, its yard slot decrements `occupied_weight_lb` and `stacked_count` and recomputes its `status`.

Two derived figures are then recomputed on the shipment, and they are recomputed again at dispatch. `gross_weight_lb` is the tractor tare of 32000 pounds plus the exact sum of `weight_lb` over the pieces of every line, with no rounding. `max_width_ft`, `max_height_ft` and `max_length_ft` are the largest `width_ft`, `height_ft` and `length_ft` among those pieces, and each is 0 when the shipment has no lines. `is_oversize` is true when `max_width_ft` is greater than 8.50 feet, or `max_height_ft` is greater than 13.50 feet, or `max_length_ft` is greater than 53.00 feet, or `gross_weight_lb` is greater than 80000 pounds. Every one of those four comparisons is strictly greater: a piece measuring exactly 8.50 feet wide is legal and needs no permit, while 8.51 feet does. Lines are never removed, so these metrics only ever grow with `line_count`.

The permit fee is recomputed at the same two moments. While `is_oversize` is false, `permit_fee_cents` is 0 and every line's `line_surcharge_cents` is 0, even when a permit happens to be linked — a legal load never accrues a fee. Once the load is oversize, each line is priced individually, taken in order of `load_position` ascending. For a line, the width overhang is the amount by which its piece's `width_ft` exceeds 8.50 feet, or zero when it does not exceed it; the height overhang is the amount by which `height_ft` exceeds 13.50 feet, or zero; and the length overhang is the amount by which `length_ft` exceeds 53.00 feet, or zero. Those three overhangs are priced at 12.75 dollars per foot of width, 21.40 dollars per foot of height and 3.66 dollars per foot of length; the three products are added, converted from dollars to cents by multiplying by 100, and rounded half up to whole cents to give that line's `line_surcharge_cents`. The rounding order is pinned: each line is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded lines are then summed. Summing the raw dollar amounts and rounding once at the end is the wrong reading and differs by whole cents on real loads. `permit_fee_cents` is finally the permit base of 8500 cents, plus the sum of the rounded `line_surcharge_cents` of every line, plus 27500 cents for each escort the linked permit mandates in its `escort_count` — and the escort count is taken as zero when `haul_permit_id` is null.

**Dispatching** the load moves the shipment `BUILDING → DISPATCHED` and stamps `dispatched_at`; dispatch from any other status is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Dispatch runs the widest set of gates in the system, and their order matters. First the permit's temporal window: when the load is oversize and a permit is linked, the logical clock must lie inside that permit's inclusive window from `valid_from` to `valid_to`. A permit whose `valid_to` has already passed is moved `ISSUED → EXPIRED` and the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`. Next the load composition gates: a shipment with a `line_count` of 0 is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_LOAD`; and the lines taken in order of `load_position` ascending must carry strictly descending `erection_sequence_no`, so that the piece at position 1 — loaded first, buried deepest — carries the highest erection sequence number on the trailer, because it comes off last. Any pair that breaks that ordering is rejected with `ERR_ERECTION_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`. Last the permit envelope gates: an oversize load with `haul_permit_id` null is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`; a load whose `max_width_ft`, `max_height_ft` or `max_length_ft` exceeds the corresponding permit maximum is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_ENVELOPE_EXCEEDED`; and a load whose `gross_weight_lb` exceeds the permit's `max_gross_weight_lb` is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_WEIGHT_EXCEEDED`. Separately, and earlier in the ladder of §2.8, an oversize load whose linked permit is not in `ISSUED` is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`. On success the linked permit moves `ISSUED → ACTIVE`.

**Delivering** the load moves the shipment `DISPATCHED → DELIVERED`, and delivery from any other status is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Every piece carried on a line of the shipment moves `production_status` `LOADED → SHIPPED`, and the linked permit moves `ACTIVE → CONSUMED`. Both `CONSUMED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal for a permit.

Erection closes the loop, and it is the only operation a `ProjectOwner` may perform. Confirming a piece erected moves it `production_status` `SHIPPED → ERECTED`, and a piece in any other production status is refused with `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the piece's `project_id`, as §2.1 requires. And the erector may not set out of order: every piece of the same project whose `erection_sequence_no` is lower than this piece's must already be in `ERECTED` or `SCRAPPED`, counting all of the project's pieces regardless of which shipment carried them. A single lower-sequence sibling still in any other production status rejects the confirmation with `ERR_ERECTION_PREDECESSOR_PENDING`. On success the owning project recomputes `erected_piece_count`. `ERECTED` is terminal.

### 3.9. Nonconformance, Repair and Recast

Defects are found by a `QCTechnician`, who opens a **nonconformance report** against a piece by supplying the `piece_id`, the `defect_type` — one of `SPALL`, `HONEYCOMB`, `CRACK`, `DIMENSIONAL` or `FINISH` — the `severity` as judged in the field, one of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `STRUCTURAL`, a free-text `description` of the defect, and the `expected_piece_version` required by §2.9. The report is written with `status` `OPEN`, its `disposition` null, its `reinspection_fail_count` 0, its `opened_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and its `dispositioned_by_user_id` and `repaired_by_user_id` both null.

Opening a report may be done against a piece in any `qc_status` except `REJECTED`, which is terminal — that is, from `PENDING`, `HOLD`, `CLEARED`, `NONCONFORMING` or `REPAIRED`; a report against a rejected piece is refused with `ERR_PIECE_INVALID_STATE`. The piece moves to `qc_status` `NONCONFORMING` with `qc_hold_reason` set to `OPEN_NCR`, and its `version` increments by 1. Opening a report on a piece that is already `NONCONFORMING` is a legitimate self-loop that leaves the piece `NONCONFORMING` and supersedes the earlier paperwork: every earlier report on the same piece whose `status` is `OPEN`, `DISPOSITIONED`, `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS` or `REINSPECTION` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Because that self-loop is legal, no state precondition would stop a writer working from a stale view of the piece — which is exactly why the version check of §2.9 guards this operation. The owning project also recomputes `cleared_piece_count`, since a piece that was `CLEARED` or `REPAIRED` no longer counts as cleared once it is nonconforming. The rework path `NONCONFORMING → REPAIRED → NONCONFORMING` on the quality dimension is a loop the plant may travel repeatedly, and it runs independently of the production dimension, which keeps advancing meanwhile.

The `QualityEngineer` **dispositions** the report by supplying a single `disposition` value, and that value is a three-way branch out of `OPEN`; a disposition issued from any other status is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE`. In every branch the acting `X-User-ID` is written to `dispositioned_by_user_id`.

- A `disposition` of `PATCH_MINOR` or of `STRUCTURAL_REPAIR` moves the report `OPEN → DISPOSITIONED` and writes the chosen value; the piece is untouched and physical repair work follows.
- A `disposition` of `USE_AS_IS` closes the report immediately, moving it `OPEN → CLOSED_ACCEPTED`. The piece moves `qc_status` `NONCONFORMING → REPAIRED` with `qc_hold_reason` cleared to null, its `repair_count` is recomputed, and the owning project recomputes `cleared_piece_count`.
- A `disposition` of `REJECT_RECAST` condemns the piece and moves the report `OPEN → REJECTED_RECAST` through a four-hop cascade. In the first hop the `disposition` and `dispositioned_by_user_id` are written. In the second hop, caused by the first, the piece moves `qc_status` to `REJECTED` and `production_status` to `SCRAPPED` — reachable from `PLANNED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CAST`, `STRIPPED` or `YARDED`. In the third hop, caused by the second, any `ACTIVE` yard placement of that piece moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `release_reason` `SCRAP`, and its yard slot decrements `occupied_weight_lb` by the piece's `weight_lb`, decrements `stacked_count` by 1 and recomputes its `status`. In the fourth hop, caused by the third, the owning project recomputes both `scrapped_piece_count` and `cleared_piece_count`.

A piece condemned this way is normally replaced by a fresh piece carrying `replaces_piece_id` pointing back at the scrapped one, which is how a recast chain is recorded.

Physical repair is deliberately performed by someone other than the engineer who ordered it. A `ProductionSupervisor` **starts the repair**, moving the report `DISPOSITIONED → REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS` and writing the acting `X-User-ID` to `repaired_by_user_id`; a start issued from any other status is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE`, and a start whose `X-User-ID` equals the report's `dispositioned_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, as §2.10 requires. When the work is done the same role **submits it for reinspection**, moving the report `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS → REINSPECTION`.

The `QualityEngineer` then **reinspects**, supplying a `result` of either `PASS` or `FAIL`; a reinspection issued from any status other than `REINSPECTION` is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE`. A `PASS` moves the report `REINSPECTION → CLOSED_ACCEPTED`, moves the piece `qc_status` `NONCONFORMING → REPAIRED` with `qc_hold_reason` cleared to null, recomputes the piece's `repair_count` and recomputes the owning project's `cleared_piece_count`. A `FAIL` increments `reinspection_fail_count` by 1. The plant tolerates a limited number of failed reinspections: while the incremented `reinspection_fail_count` remains below the limit of 2, the report returns `REINSPECTION → REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS` for another attempt, which makes `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS → REINSPECTION → REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS` the repair rework loop. When the increment would bring `reinspection_fail_count` to exactly 2, patience is exhausted: the report moves `REINSPECTION → REJECTED_RECAST` with `reinspection_fail_count` at 2, and exactly the same four-hop scrap cascade described for a `REJECT_RECAST` disposition fires — the piece to `REJECTED` and `SCRAPPED`, its `ACTIVE` placement released with `release_reason` `SCRAP`, its slot decremented and recomputed, and its project's `scrapped_piece_count` and `cleared_piece_count` recomputed.

A piece's `repair_count` is the number of reports raised against it whose `status` is either `CLOSED_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED_RECAST`. `CLOSED_ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED_RECAST` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal for a report, and any action attempted against a report in one of those three states is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INVALID_STATE`.
