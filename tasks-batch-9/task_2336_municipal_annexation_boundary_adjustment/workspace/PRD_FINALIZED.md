# Municipal Annexation And Boundary Adjustment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Annexation is one of the few municipal acts in which a city rewrites its own jurisdictional footprint, and every state supervises the exercise closely. A city that wants to bring unincorporated territory inside its corporate limits must pick a method its legislature actually authorizes, prove the land is contiguous and urban in character, publish a defensible plan for extending police, fire, water and sewer service, demonstrate that the revenue the territory generates covers the cost of serving it, notice and hold a public hearing, tabulate written objections from the owners of record, sometimes submit the result to a boundary commission or to a ratification referendum, and only then set an effective date on which the tax roll and the utility billing schedules change hands. Get any step out of order and the ordinance is void.

This API is the system of record for that entire procedure. It exists because the procedure differs materially from state to state — one legislature measures petition support by assessed value while its neighbour counts owners, one requires a boundary commission and another does not, one permits de-annexation and another forbids it — and because municipal staff, county assessors, finance analysts and commission members all touch the same case from different offices with different authority. Rather than encoding one state's rules, the service treats the statute itself as configuration: a **statute profile** carries the thresholds, windows, methodologies and feature flags that switch entire code paths on the cases beneath it.

The design goals follow from that. The procedural record must be reconstructible from the API alone, so every threshold evaluation stamps its inputs and its result onto a durable field rather than being recomputed on read. Steps that state law separates must be separated in the software, including the segregation-of-duties bar that prevents the analyst who signed off on a case's fiscal numbers from also sitting in judgment of it at the boundary commission. And because a single owner withdrawing a signature can retroactively invalidate a scheduled hearing, the service is expected to unwind dependent work automatically rather than leaving a clerk to notice the inconsistency months later.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, timekeeping, identity, precision, concurrency control, cascade integrity, and the order in which validations fire. Domain workflows are in §3.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Jurisdictional Scope

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Seven roles are recognized, spelled exactly as follows: `StateLawAdmin`, `MunicipalPlanner`, `CityClerk`, `PropertyOwner`, `FiscalAnalyst`, `BoundaryCommissioner`, and `CountyAssessor`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is any string other than those seven, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles listed for the requested endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles divide the work along the lines the statute itself draws. A `StateLawAdmin` publishes statute profiles, registers municipalities and owners of record, and drives the logical clock described in §2.4. A `MunicipalPlanner` is the city's planning officer: they open cases, assemble and certify territory, register service providers and utility accounts, draft the plan of services and its commitments, submit approved annexations to the boundary commission, and effectuate the boundary change. A `CityClerk` keeps the statutory record — petition certification, plan adoption, hearing scheduling, notice publication, holding the hearing, closing and tabulating the protest period, ordinance adoption, referendum recording, and adoption of the municipal service district alternative. A `PropertyOwner` signs and withdraws petition consents and files and withdraws written protests. A `FiscalAnalyst` authors and finalizes the fiscal impact analysis. A `BoundaryCommissioner` approves or denies annexations where state law requires commission review. A `CountyAssessor` transfers annexed parcels onto the municipal tax roll. Read endpoints are open to all seven roles.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against persisted foreign keys.

- When `X-User-Role` is `MunicipalPlanner` or `CityClerk`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Municipality`. Ownership checks on any resource that reaches a municipality compare that municipality's identifier to `X-User-ID`: directly for an `AnnexationCase` (through its `municipality_id`), a `ServiceProvider` (through its `municipality_id`) or a `PublicHearing` (through its `municipality_id`), and transitively for a `Parcel`, `Petition`, `PlanOfServices`, `ServiceCommitment` or `UtilityAccount` by walking to the annexation case that owns it and then to that case's municipality. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_MUNICIPALITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check guards every municipal write: case creation and every case-level action, parcel addition and exclusion, petition creation and certification, plan creation and adoption, commitment creation, activation and voiding, hearing scheduling, notice publication, holding and protest closure, utility account registration, and service provider registration.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PropertyOwner`. On consent creation and on protest filing creation, `X-User-ID` must equal the `property_owner_id` supplied in the payload; on withdrawal of a consent or a filing, it must equal the `property_owner_id` recorded on the target `ParcelOwnerConsent` or `ProtestFiling`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check applies to exactly four operations: consent creation, consent withdrawal, protest filing creation, and protest filing withdrawal. A `CityClerk` may perform these same four operations on any owner's behalf — clerks transcribe paper filings — and is therefore not subject to the owner-scope comparison, only to the municipality-scope one.
- When `X-User-Role` is `StateLawAdmin`, `FiscalAnalyst`, `BoundaryCommissioner` or `CountyAssessor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a `FiscalAnalyst`'s identifier is written to `finalized_by_user_id` on the analysis and mirrored to `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id` on the case when an analysis is finalized — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

One further access rule is a genuine business rule rather than a mere permission. The `BoundaryCommissioner` deciding a case must not be the operator who finalized that case's analysis of record: if the commissioner's `X-User-ID` equals the case's `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). This bar applies only to the commission decision.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` reflects the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied.

Beyond pagination, each collection accepts optional equality filters. Municipalities may be filtered by `statute_profile_id` or `state_code`. Service providers may be filtered by `municipality_id`. Annexation cases may be filtered by `municipality_id`, `predecessor_case_id`, `status` or `case_type`. Parcels accept `annexation_case_id`, `primary_owner_id` and `parcel_status`. Petitions accept `annexation_case_id` and `sufficiency_status`. Consents accept `petition_id`, `parcel_id`, `property_owner_id` and `consent_status`. Fiscal impact analyses accept `annexation_case_id` and `status`. Plans of services accept `annexation_case_id` and `adoption_status`. Service commitments accept `plan_of_services_id`, `service_provider_id`, `commitment_status` and `service_type`. Public hearings accept `annexation_case_id`, `municipality_id` and `hearing_status`. The cross-hearing notice listing accepts `public_hearing_id` and `notice_status`, while the per-hearing notice listing accepts `notice_status` on its own. Protest filings accept `public_hearing_id`, `parcel_id`, `property_owner_id` and `filing_status`. Utility accounts accept `parcel_id`, `service_provider_id` and `account_status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Statutory Timekeeping

Nothing in this system is evaluated against wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and a `StateLawAdmin` may move it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response echoes the new reading as `current_utc`. Before that endpoint is ever called the logical clock reads its startup default and never falls back to the host's real time.

Every temporal rule in the domain reads that clock: the petition circulation deadline, the minimum interval between notice publication and holding a hearing, the opening and closing of the written-objection period, the statutory cooling-off period between hearing and ordinance, the admissibility of a submitted effective date, and every system-stamped timestamp such as `circulation_started_at`, `signed_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `published_at`, `held_at`, `filed_at`, `adopted_at`, `hearing_held_at` and `ordinance_adopted_at`. Where a rule is expressed in whole days — thirty days of notice, ten days of cooling off, a protest window measured in days — the days are added to the stored instant and the resulting instant is compared against the clock.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Revision Stamps and Immutable Notices

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that resource — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only change a computed field.

`NoticePublication` is the single exception. A published notice is an immutable statutory artifact: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and there is no update endpoint for it. The one way a notice changes is the supersession cascade described in §2.9, which flips its `notice_status`; that flip does not produce timestamps or version numbers the notice does not have.

### 2.6. The Statute Profile as Configuration Hub

A `StatuteProfile` is not a lookup table — it is the switchboard that decides which code paths a case runs at all. Its `state_code` is two uppercase letters and must be unique across profiles. Its `authorized_methods` is a non-empty array whose members are drawn from `PETITION_100`, `PETITION_MAJORITY`, `REFERENDUM` and `INVOLUNTARY_ORDINANCE`; a case whose `method` is not one of them can never be certified or adopted.

Four flags in particular switch whole branches of the lifecycle. `boundary_commission_required` is a boolean defaulting to false; when true, a case must pass through boundary commission review before it can be effectuated, and when false the commission step is skipped entirely. `referendum_required_methods` is an array — empty by default — of the methods whose ordinance adoption must be ratified by referendum before effectuation; a case whose `method` appears there branches to a referendum instead of straight to adoption. `de_annexation_allowed` is a boolean defaulting to true that gates whether de-annexation cases may exist under the statute at all. `msd_alternative_allowed` is a boolean defaulting to true that gates whether a municipal service district may replace an annexation defeated by protest.

The remaining fields are thresholds and windows, all optional on creation with the declared default applied when omitted. `signature_basis` (`ASSESSED_VALUE` or `OWNER_COUNT`, default `ASSESSED_VALUE`) selects which measure of petition support is compared to `signature_threshold_pct` (default 50.00, greater than 0 and at most 100, two decimal places). `protest_basis` (`ASSESSED_VALUE` or `OWNER_COUNT`, default `ASSESSED_VALUE`) selects the measure of objection compared to `protest_threshold_pct` (default 20.00, greater than 0 and at most 100, two decimal places). `contiguity_min_ratio` (default 0.125, greater than 0 and at most 1, four decimal places), `urban_density_min_units_per_acre` (default 2.00, greater than 0), `subdivision_test_min_developed_ratio` (default 0.60, greater than 0 and at most 1) and `min_qualification_index` (default 60.00, from 0 through 100) govern territory certification. `notice_min_days` (default 30, at least 0) and `hearing_to_ordinance_min_days` (default 10, at least 0) govern the notice and cooling-off windows. `required_service_types` (default the four values `POLICE`, `FIRE`, `WATER`, `SEWER`, non-empty with distinct members) lists the services a plan must cover, while `police_fire_timeline_max_days` (default 60, at least 1) and `water_sewer_timeline_max_days` (default 730, at least 1) cap commitment window lengths. `fiscal_method` (`AVERAGE_COST` or `MARGINAL_COST`, default `AVERAGE_COST`) mandates the service-cost methodology and `per_capita_shared_revenue_cents` (default 12000, at least 0) is the annual state-shared revenue per projected resident. `de_annexation_refund_basis` (`DAILY_PRORATION` or `WHOLE_QUARTER`, default `DAILY_PRORATION`) picks the refund formula, `msd_cost_recovery_pct` (default 0.75, greater than 0 and at most 1) the district's cost-recovery fraction, `effective_date_rule` (`IMMEDIATE`, `NEXT_QUARTER_START` or `NEXT_FISCAL_YEAR`, default `IMMEDIATE`) the admissible effective date, and `fiscal_year_end_month_day` (a `MM-DD` string, default `12-31`) the proration horizon used by both tax-roll posting and de-annexation refunds.

### 2.7. Cents, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

Money is integral. Every monetary field in this system is a whole number of cents, and every monetary result is rounded half up to a whole cent — there are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or a response body. Percentages and index scores are carried to two decimal places, rounded half up. Three ratios are carried to four decimal places, rounded half up: `contiguity_ratio`, the developed-lot ratio of the subdivision test, and the dwelling-unit density of the density test. Territory acreage is rounded half up to two decimal places. Day counts and account counts are integers.

Two rounding orders are pinned and are not interchangeable with the arithmetically tempting alternative. First, when petition or protest weight is aggregated across owner shares, each individual share is rounded to a whole cent **before** the shares are summed — the per-consent `qualified_value_cents` and the per-filing `protest_value_cents` are each rounded, and the petition and hearing totals are exact integer sums of those already-rounded values. Second, when the tax roll is prorated, each parcel's prorated amount is rounded to a whole cent before the case-level total is accumulated; summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and can differ by cents.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Case and Plan Revisions

Two creation endpoints supersede records their client may not have seen, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent's `version`.

`POST /fiscal-impact-analyses` requires a non-optional `expected_case_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `AnnexationCase` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new analysis legitimately supersedes every prior analysis of the case and clears the case's `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id`; a writer holding a stale view of the case would silently discard an analysis it never read.

`POST /service-commitments` requires a non-optional `expected_plan_of_services_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `PlanOfServices` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new commitment voids the plan's existing draft commitment for the same service type; a writer holding a stale view of the plan would destroy a commitment authored after its read.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection perform no concurrency check and must not require one.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity Across Cases, Parcels, Plans and Utility Accounts

The service is expected to keep dependent records aligned without a client asking it to. Several operations mutate records the request never names, and each such secondary mutation is a full mutation: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` on the record it touches.

The `AnnexationCase` is the hub, and its `version` advances on every one of the following: adding a parcel, excluding a parcel, certifying territory, opening a petition, certifying petition sufficiency, withdrawing a consent, publishing a notice, holding a hearing, closing a protest period, creating a fiscal impact analysis, finalizing one, adopting the ordinance, recording the referendum, submitting to the commission, recording the commission decision, effectuating, posting the tax roll, and adopting the municipal service district alternative. The `PlanOfServices` `version` advances on commitment creation, commitment activation, commitment voiding, and plan adoption. When the de-annexation cascade runs, both the successor case and the predecessor case have their `version` incremented.

Three cascade families recur throughout §3 and are worth stating once here. The **supersession cascade** fires when a withdrawn consent drops a petition below its threshold: the petition falls from `SUFFICIENT` to `INSUFFICIENT`, the case reverts to `PETITION_CIRCULATING`, every hearing of that case still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `VACATED`, every notice of those hearings still in `PUBLISHED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and each affected hearing's `notice_published_at` is set back to null so that the council chamber slot is released and the notice clock must be restarted. The **rescind cascade** fires when a case is denied, by referendum rejection or by commission denial: every parcel of the case in `PENDING_ANNEX` moves to `EXCLUDED`, the case's `ADOPTED` plan moves to `RESCINDED`, every commitment of that plan in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED` moves to `VOIDED`, each voided commitment's `timeline_days` is released from its provider's `encumbered_capacity_days`, and the plan's `committed_cost_cents` and `covered_service_types` are recomputed to zero and empty with `service_readiness` falling back to `PENDING`. The **effectuation cascade** and its de-annexation mirror are described link by link in §3.8 and §3.9.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and the Order of Validation

Requests whose URL carries more than one identifier are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error. Each identifier has its own not-found code: `ERR_STATUTE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MUNICIPALITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_UTILITY_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404. A request for the notices of a non-existent hearing therefore yields `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the notice lookup. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after the path, in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, and an unresolvable one yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Because many endpoints in this domain stack half a dozen checks, the precedence between them is fixed rather than incidental. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first one that fails is the one reported:

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role permitted for the endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Municipality scope for `MunicipalPlanner` and `CityClerk` — `ERR_MUNICIPALITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. Owner scope for `PropertyOwner` — `ERR_OWNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
5. Segregation of duties on the commission decision — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. Path identifier resolution, left to right — the per-entity 404 codes above.
7. Request-body foreign key resolution — `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.
8. Payload shape: required inputs present, enum values inside their declared member sets, numbers inside their declared ranges, dates and datetimes well formed ISO 8601, a commitment's `window_end_date` not earlier than its `window_start_date`, a hearing's `scheduled_end_at` strictly later than its `scheduled_start_at`, and any `ownership_share_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
9. Pagination bounds on list endpoints — `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID`.
10. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_case_version` on analysis creation (`ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`), then `expected_plan_of_services_version` on commitment creation (`ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`).
11. Case terminality: the case reached from the target resource must not be in `DENIED`, `MSD_ALTERNATIVE_ADOPTED` or `DE_ANNEXED` — `ERR_CASE_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). This applies to every write endpoint that touches a case or any of its children.
12. State admissibility, in this internal order: the case's `status` is a declared origin state for the requested transition (`ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`), the petition is not `EXPIRED` (`ERR_PETITION_EXPIRED`), no petition of the case is already `CIRCULATING` or `SUFFICIENT` (`ERR_PETITION_ALREADY_CIRCULATING`), the consent is still `SIGNED` (`ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN`), the filing is still `FILED` (`ERR_FILING_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN`), the hearing is still `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED`), no sibling hearing of the case is already `HELD` (`ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_HELD`), the protest period is in the state the operation needs (`ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_CLOSED`), no sibling plan is already `ADOPTED` (`ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ADOPTED`), the commitment is in an admissible state (`ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`), the analysis is still `DRAFT` (`ERR_ANALYSIS_STATE_INVALID`), the tax roll has not already posted (`ERR_TAX_ROLL_ALREADY_POSTED`), the commission review is `PENDING` (`ERR_COMMISSION_REVIEW_NOT_PENDING`), the parcel is in an admissible state (`ERR_PARCEL_STATE_INVALID`), and the parcel number is not already claimed by a live sibling case (`ERR_PARCEL_ALREADY_IN_PENDING_CASE`).
13. Statutory and business checks, in this internal order: `ERR_METHOD_NOT_AUTHORIZED_BY_STATUTE`, `ERR_TERRITORY_EMPTY`, `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`, `ERR_URBAN_DEVELOPMENT_TEST_FAILED`, `ERR_SERVICE_CAPABILITY_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ADOPTED`, `ERR_SERVICE_TYPE_NOT_COVERED`, `ERR_PLAN_SERVICE_TIMELINE_EXCEEDS_STATUTE`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_OPEN`.
14. Temporal checks, in this internal order: `ERR_PETITION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_NOT_SATISFIED`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_STARTED`, `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`, `ERR_PROTEST_FILING_OUT_OF_WINDOW`, `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`, `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_CONFLICT`.
15. Referential and quantitative business checks, in this internal order: `ERR_PROVIDER_SERVICE_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PARCEL_CASE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CONSENT_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_FILING_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_DE_ANNEXATION_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_EFFECTIVE`, `ERR_MSD_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_COMMISSION_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CONSENT_OWNERSHIP_SHARE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROTEST_OWNERSHIP_SHARE_INVALID`.
16. Fiscal checks, in this internal order: `ERR_FISCAL_ANALYSIS_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_FISCAL_METHOD_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_FISCAL_DEFICIT_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`.
17. Petition sufficiency last — `ERR_PETITION_INSUFFICIENT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Jurisdiction: Statutes, Cities, Departments and Owners

Before any case can exist, a `StateLawAdmin` publishes the governing statute profile for a state, supplying its `state_code` and `authorized_methods` and overriding as many of the thresholds catalogued in §2.6 as the state's law requires; every omitted flag takes its declared default, so a minimal profile still yields a fully specified statute.

A municipality is then registered against that statute. The payload names the governing `statute_profile_id` together with the city's `name` (1 to 200 characters), its `state_code` — two uppercase letters, which must equal the referenced profile's `state_code` — and its `population` (at least 0). Three fiscal inputs describe the city's cost structure: `citywide_annual_service_cost_cents` is the total annual general-fund service cost in cents (at least 0), `citywide_population` is the denominator used to turn that into a per-capita rate (at least 1), and `general_fund_millage_rate` is the general-fund levy expressed in mills per one thousand dollars of assessed value, carried to four decimal places and at least 0. One geometric input, `corporate_boundary_perimeter_feet`, records the length of the existing corporate boundary in feet and must be greater than 0; it is the denominator of every contiguity test the city ever runs. Three optional policy inputs round out the record: `fiscal_deficit_tolerance_pct` (default 5.00, from 0 through 100) is the largest fiscal deficit the council will tolerate when adopting an ordinance, `hearing_changeover_buffer_minutes` (default 30, at least 0) is the trailing buffer appended to every council-chamber booking, and `utility_outside_surcharge_pct` (default 0.50, from 0 through 2) is the surcharge fraction applied to accounts served from outside the corporate limits. A newly registered city starts with `taxable_assessed_value_cents` at 0; that running ledger of assessed value on the municipal tax roll rises only when a case posts its tax roll and falls only when a posting is reversed by de-annexation.

Departments and utility authorities that will actually deliver service are registered as providers under a city by a `MunicipalPlanner`, who supplies a `provider_name` (1 to 200 characters) and a non-empty list of distinct `service_types` drawn from `POLICE`, `FIRE`, `WATER` and `SEWER`. The provider's `municipality_id` is the city it was registered under. `concurrent_project_capacity` (default 1, at least 1) is the number of commitments whose date windows may cover any single calendar day, and `encumbered_capacity_days` — a running total starting at 0 — is the sum of the closed-window day counts of every commitment of that provider currently in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`.

Owners of record are registered by a `CityClerk` or a `StateLawAdmin` with an `owner_name` (1 to 200 characters) and a `contact_email`, which must contain an at-sign because mailed notice is addressed to it.

### 3.2. Assembling and Certifying Territory

A `MunicipalPlanner` opens a case in `DRAFT` by naming the annexing `municipality_id` and choosing a `method` from `PETITION_100`, `PETITION_MAJORITY`, `REFERENDUM` and `INVOLUNTARY_ORDINANCE`. The optional `case_type` is `ANNEXATION` by default; the alternative, `DE_ANNEXATION`, requires a `predecessor_case_id` naming the effective annexation the new case unwinds, while an `ANNEXATION` case must leave `predecessor_case_id` null. Supplying a predecessor on an annexation case, omitting one on a de-annexation case, or opening a de-annexation case under a statute whose `de_annexation_allowed` is false all draw `ERR_DE_ANNEXATION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Four measurements of the proposed territory are supplied at the same time: `shared_boundary_feet`, the length in feet of territory boundary shared with the existing corporate boundary (at least 0); `total_lot_count`, the number of subdivision lots (at least 0); `developed_lot_count`, how many of those carry a permanent structure (at least 0 and not more than `total_lot_count`); and `dwelling_units`, the number of dwelling units in the territory (at least 0). An optional `max_service_distance_miles` (default 0, at least 0) records the distance from the nearest existing service facility to the farthest point of the territory. A fresh case starts with `status` `DRAFT`, `tax_roll_status` `NOT_TRANSFERRED`, `protest_outcome` `NOT_EVALUATED`, `referendum_result` `NOT_HELD` and `commission_review_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`.

Parcels are added one at a time while the case is still `DRAFT`, and only to an `ANNEXATION` case — a de-annexation case owns no parcels of its own, since its territory *is* the predecessor's incorporated land. Each parcel names its `primary_owner_id`, its `parcel_apn` (the assessor parcel number, 1 to 40 characters), its county-assessed `assessed_value_cents` (greater than 0) and its `acreage` in acres to two decimal places (greater than 0), plus optional `is_developed` (default false) and `dwelling_units` (default 0, at least 0) flags. A new parcel enters with `parcel_status` `PROPOSED`. Adding a parcel to a case that has left `DRAFT`, or excluding a parcel whose `parcel_status` is neither `PROPOSED` nor `PENDING_ANNEX`, is refused with `ERR_PARCEL_STATE_INVALID`. A parcel number may not be claimed twice within a city while an earlier claim is still live: if the same `parcel_apn` already appears on a parcel in another case of the same municipality whose status is neither terminal nor `EFFECTIVE`, the addition is refused with `ERR_PARCEL_ALREADY_IN_PENDING_CASE` — an already-annexed parcel, by contrast, may be re-described by a later case.

The planner may also carve a parcel back out. Excluding it moves `parcel_status` to `EXCLUDED`, disconnects each of that parcel's utility accounts still in `OUTSIDE_CITY` by moving them to `DISCONNECTED`, and recomputes the case's territory aggregates. Both adding and excluding a parcel bump the case's `version`, and while the case is still `DRAFT` both also refresh the territory measurements described next.

Three aggregates define the territory. For an `ANNEXATION` case they are computed over that case's own parcels whose `parcel_status` is `PROPOSED`, `PENDING_ANNEX` or `INCORPORATED`; for a `DE_ANNEXATION` case they are computed over the predecessor's parcels whose `parcel_status` is `INCORPORATED`. Parcels in `EXCLUDED` or `DE_ANNEXED` never enter any territory aggregate. `parcel_count` is the number of such parcels, `territory_acreage` is the sum of their `acreage` values rounded half up to two decimal places, and `territory_assessed_value_cents` is the exact integer sum of their `assessed_value_cents`.

Certification is the planner's act of proving the territory annexable, and it moves the case from `DRAFT` to `TERRITORY_CERTIFIED`. It requires the case to be in `DRAFT`, and it requires the case's `method` to appear in the statute's `authorized_methods` — otherwise `ERR_METHOD_NOT_AUTHORIZED_BY_STATUTE`. For a de-annexation case it additionally requires the statute to permit de-annexation (`ERR_DE_ANNEXATION_NOT_ALLOWED`) and the predecessor case to be in `EFFECTIVE` status and belong to the same municipality — a predecessor in any other status, or one belonging to another city, draws `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. An annexation case must hold at least one parcel in `PROPOSED` or `PENDING_ANNEX`, and a de-annexation case's predecessor must hold at least one parcel in `INCORPORATED`; an empty territory draws `ERR_TERRITORY_EMPTY`.

Then the three statutory tests run, in the order the statute states them.

**Contiguity.** `contiguity_ratio` is the case's `shared_boundary_feet` divided by the municipality's `corporate_boundary_perimeter_feet`, rounded half up to four decimal places. If it is below the statute's `contiguity_min_ratio` the certification is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`; a ratio exactly equal to the minimum passes.

**Urban development.** Two independent limbs are evaluated, and passing either one is enough. The subdivision limb computes the developed-lot ratio as `developed_lot_count` divided by the larger of `total_lot_count` and 1, rounded half up to four decimal places, and passes when that ratio is at or above the statute's `subdivision_test_min_developed_ratio`. The density limb computes dwelling-unit density as the case's `dwelling_units` divided by the larger of `territory_acreage` and one hundredth of an acre, rounded half up to four decimal places, and passes when that density is at or above the statute's `urban_density_min_units_per_acre`. `urban_development_qualified` is set true when either limb passes and false when neither does; false refuses the certification with `ERR_URBAN_DEVELOPMENT_TEST_FAILED`.

**Service capability.** The `qualification_index` is a score from 0 through 100 assembled from four terms and then clamped. The base term is one hundred multiplied by the smaller of 1 and the quotient of `contiguity_ratio` divided by twice the statute's `contiguity_min_ratio` — that is, contiguity earns credit on a ramp that saturates at twice the statutory minimum and earns no further credit beyond it. A flat urban bonus of twenty-five points is added when `urban_development_qualified` is true and nothing is added when it is false. A flat density penalty of fifteen points is subtracted when the dwelling-unit density is strictly below `urban_density_min_units_per_acre` **and** the developed-lot ratio is strictly below `subdivision_test_min_developed_ratio`, and nothing is subtracted otherwise. A distance penalty subtracts ten points for every mile of service distance beyond the first three: take the case's `max_service_distance_miles`, subtract three, round the remainder up to the next whole number, treat any result below zero as zero, and multiply by ten. The four terms are combined as base plus urban bonus minus density penalty minus distance penalty; the result is capped at 100, floored at 0, and rounded half up to two decimal places to give `qualification_index`. An index below the statute's `min_qualification_index` refuses the certification with `ERR_SERVICE_CAPABILITY_INSUFFICIENT`; an index exactly equal to the minimum passes.

A successful certification stamps `parcel_count`, `territory_acreage`, `territory_assessed_value_cents`, `contiguity_ratio`, `urban_development_qualified` and `qualification_index` onto the case, promotes every parcel of the case still in `PROPOSED` to `PENDING_ANNEX`, and increments the case's `version`.

### 3.3. The Petition Limb: Circulation, Signatures and the Cure Cycle

Only the two petition methods walk this path. With the case in `TERRITORY_CERTIFIED` and its `method` equal to `PETITION_100` or `PETITION_MAJORITY`, a `MunicipalPlanner` opens a petition by naming the `annexation_case_id`, choosing a `petition_type` of `OWNER_100` or `OWNER_MAJORITY`, and optionally setting `circulation_days` (default 180, from 1 through 365). The petition is created with `sufficiency_status` `CIRCULATING`, `circulation_started_at` set to the current logical clock reading, and `circulation_deadline_at` set to `circulation_started_at` advanced by `circulation_days` days; the case moves to `PETITION_CIRCULATING` and its `version` increments. Because state law lets a city try again after a petition lapses, the same act is admissible a second time from `PETITION_CIRCULATING` itself — a `PETITION_CIRCULATING → PETITION_CIRCULATING` self-loop that mints a fresh petition with a new circulation window measured from the clock, but only when every prior petition of the case has already reached `EXPIRED` or `INSUFFICIENT`. If any petition of the case is still `CIRCULATING` or already `SUFFICIENT`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_PETITION_ALREADY_CIRCULATING`. Opening a petition from any other case status draws `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`.

Signatures arrive as consents. A `PropertyOwner` — or a `CityClerk` transcribing a paper signature — records one by naming the `petition_id`, the `parcel_id` whose ownership interest is being pledged, the signing `property_owner_id`, and the `ownership_share_pct` that signature represents (greater than 0 and at most 100, two decimal places). The consent is stored with `consent_status` `SIGNED` and `signed_at` set to the clock, and its `qualified_value_cents` is computed immediately as the parcel's `assessed_value_cents` multiplied by `ownership_share_pct`, divided by one hundred, and rounded half up to a whole cent; the owning petition's signature aggregates are then recomputed by the rule stated below. Four checks guard the act beyond the owner-scope rule of §2.1. A consent may not be recorded against an `EXPIRED` petition (`ERR_PETITION_EXPIRED`). The referenced parcel must belong to the same case as the referenced petition, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARCEL_CASE_MISMATCH`. The logical clock must lie inside the closed interval running from `circulation_started_at` through `circulation_deadline_at` inclusive; outside it, `ERR_PETITION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. No `SIGNED` consent may already exist for the same combination of petition, parcel and owner — a repeat draws `ERR_CONSENT_DUPLICATE`. And the sum of `ownership_share_pct` across all `SIGNED` consents for that same petition and parcel may not exceed one hundred, or `ERR_CONSENT_OWNERSHIP_SHARE_INVALID` is returned.

Sufficiency is measured on the statute's chosen basis. The qualifying set is every consent of the petition whose `consent_status` is `SIGNED` and whose `signed_at` falls inside the closed interval from `circulation_started_at` through `circulation_deadline_at`; withdrawn consents and consents dated after the deadline are excluded outright. `qualified_signature_assessed_value_cents` is the exact integer sum of the qualifying consents' already-rounded `qualified_value_cents`, and `qualified_signature_owner_count` is the number of distinct `property_owner_id` values among them. `signature_value_pct` is that value sum divided by the case's `territory_assessed_value_cents`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places — or exactly 0.00 when the territory's assessed value is zero. `signature_owner_pct` is the distinct-owner count divided by the number of distinct `primary_owner_id` values across the territory's parcels, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places — or exactly 0.00 when the territory has no distinct owners. The measured percentage is `signature_value_pct` when the statute's `signature_basis` is `ASSESSED_VALUE` and `signature_owner_pct` when it is `OWNER_COUNT`. A petition of type `OWNER_100` meets its threshold only when the measured percentage is at least 100.00; a petition of type `OWNER_MAJORITY` meets it when the measured percentage is at least the statute's `signature_threshold_pct`.

Certification of sufficiency is the clerk's act, and it forks on the clock. If the logical clock is past `circulation_deadline_at`, the petition is instead marked `EXPIRED` and the case's `status` is left untouched; this applies to a petition in `CIRCULATING` and equally to one in `INSUFFICIENT`, and `EXPIRED` is terminal — an expired petition can never be certified again, and a later attempt draws `ERR_PETITION_EXPIRED`. If the clock is at or before the deadline, the aggregates above are stamped onto the petition. When the threshold is met the petition moves to `SUFFICIENT` and the case advances to `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW`; this holds both for the first certification out of `CIRCULATING` and for the re-certification limb out of `INSUFFICIENT` after new signatures have arrived, and both limbs stamp `qualified_signature_assessed_value_cents`, `qualified_signature_owner_count`, `signature_value_pct` and `signature_owner_pct` identically. When the threshold is not met, the certification is refused with `ERR_PETITION_INSUFFICIENT`. Either way the case's `version` increments.

Withdrawal is where the cure cycle bites. A `PropertyOwner` or `CityClerk` may withdraw a consent that is still `SIGNED`; a second attempt on the same consent draws `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawal sets `consent_status` to `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `withdrawn_at` from the clock, and resets that consent's `qualified_value_cents` to 0 so it stops contributing. The petition's aggregates are then recomputed. If the petition had been `SUFFICIENT` and no longer meets its threshold, the supersession cascade of §2.9 runs in full: the petition drops to `INSUFFICIENT`, the case reverts to `PETITION_CIRCULATING` — from `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW` if the hearing had not yet been noticed, and equally from `HEARING_NOTICED` if it had — every hearing of that case in `SCHEDULED` is `VACATED`, every `PUBLISHED` notice of those hearings becomes `SUPERSEDED`, each such hearing's `notice_published_at` reverts to null so the chamber slot is freed and the notice clock must restart, and the case's `version` increments. The petition may then be cured with fresh consents and re-certified, closing the `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW → PETITION_CIRCULATING → SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW` loop that state law contemplates.

### 3.4. Weighing Service Extension Against Projected Revenue

A `FiscalAnalyst` authors the fiscal impact analysis. The payload names the `annexation_case_id`, declares an `analysis_method` of `AVERAGE_COST` or `MARGINAL_COST`, supplies the `projected_population` expected to reside in the territory at build-out (at least 0), carries the `expected_case_version` guard described in §2.8, and may set `horizon_years` (default 5, from 1 through 20, recorded for the record only) together with four marginal cost inputs — `marginal_police_cost_cents`, `marginal_fire_cost_cents`, `marginal_water_cost_cents` and `marginal_sewer_cost_cents`, each defaulting to 0 and each at least 0. The declared `analysis_method` must equal the statute's mandated `fiscal_method`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_FISCAL_METHOD_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Creating an analysis moves every prior analysis of the same case to `SUPERSEDED`, whether it was `DRAFT` or `FINALIZED`, and when the superseded one was the `FINALIZED` analysis of record the case's `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id` reverts to null. The case's `version` increments.

Every computed figure is stamped at creation time and is never re-derived afterwards; the only way to revise an analysis is to author a new one. On the revenue side, `property_tax_revenue_cents` is the case's `territory_assessed_value_cents` multiplied by the municipality's `general_fund_millage_rate`, divided by one thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent. `projected_utility_revenue_cents` is the projected annual utility revenue at post-annexation inside-city rates: take every utility account whose parcel belongs to the case and whose parcel is in `PROPOSED`, `PENDING_ANNEX` or `INCORPORATED`, exclude any account in `DISCONNECTED`, multiply each remaining account's `monthly_base_cents` by the twelve months of the year, and sum — the outside-city surcharge is deliberately absent, because the projection describes the world after annexation. `shared_revenue_cents` is `projected_population` multiplied by the statute's `per_capita_shared_revenue_cents`. `gross_annual_revenue_cents` is the sum of those three figures.

The cost side runs in a pinned order, and the methodology variant changes only its first step. Under the `AVERAGE_COST` variant, `base_service_cost_cents` is the municipality's `citywide_annual_service_cost_cents` multiplied by `projected_population`, divided by `citywide_population`, and rounded half up to a whole cent; the four marginal inputs are ignored entirely. Under the `MARGINAL_COST` variant, `base_service_cost_cents` is instead the plain sum of `marginal_police_cost_cents`, `marginal_fire_cost_cents`, `marginal_water_cost_cents` and `marginal_sewer_cost_cents`; the citywide averages are ignored entirely. Identical territory and population data therefore produce a different base cost under each variant, and — because the overhead tier is keyed to the base cost — potentially a different overhead rate, a different net impact and a different deficit ratio.

Step two selects `overhead_tier_pct` from `base_service_cost_cents`, that is, from the cost **before** overhead is added. When the base cost is below five million cents the rate is 0.12; otherwise, when it is below twenty-five million cents the rate is 0.09; otherwise the rate is 0.06. The tier is never re-selected from the post-overhead total even though the total later includes the overhead, and there is no iteration and no fixed point to converge on. Step three computes `administrative_overhead_cents` as `base_service_cost_cents` multiplied by `overhead_tier_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Step four sets `projected_annual_service_cost_cents` to `base_service_cost_cents` plus `administrative_overhead_cents`. Step five sets `net_annual_impact_cents` to `gross_annual_revenue_cents` minus `projected_annual_service_cost_cents`; a negative value is a deficit. Step six derives `deficit_ratio_pct`: it is exactly 0.00 when the net impact is at or above zero; it is exactly 100.00 when the net impact is negative and gross annual revenue is zero; otherwise it is the magnitude of the negative net impact divided by `gross_annual_revenue_cents`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places.

An analysis begins in `DRAFT` with `finalized_by_user_id` null. Finalizing it is the analyst's separate act: it requires the analysis to still be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_ANALYSIS_STATE_INVALID`), requires the owning case to be outside `EFFECTIVE`, `DENIED`, `MSD_ALTERNATIVE_ADOPTED` and `DE_ANNEXED`, and requires that no other analysis of the case is already `FINALIZED`. Finalization moves `status` to `FINALIZED`, writes the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` into `finalized_by_user_id` on the analysis and mirrors it into `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id` on the case, and increments the case's `version`. That mirrored identifier is precisely what the segregation-of-duties bar of §2.1 later compares against, which is why superseding a finalized analysis clears it.

### 3.5. The Plan of Services: Commitments, Provider Capacity and Readiness

A plan of services is the city's promise about how police, fire, water and sewer will actually reach the new territory, and it carries two entirely independent dimensions. `adoption_status` runs `DRAFT → ADOPTED → RESCINDED` and is driven by the clerk; `service_readiness` runs among `PENDING`, `PARTIAL` and `READY` and is derived from the plan's commitments. Neither axis gates the other: a plan can be `ADOPTED` while still `PENDING`, and `READY` while still `DRAFT`. A `MunicipalPlanner` opens one by naming its `annexation_case_id`; it starts with `adoption_status` `DRAFT`, `service_readiness` `PENDING`, `committed_cost_cents` 0 and `covered_service_types` empty, and `adopted_at` null.

Commitments hang off the plan. Creating one names the `plan_of_services_id`, the `service_provider_id` pledging capacity, the `service_type` being committed (`POLICE`, `FIRE`, `WATER` or `SEWER`), the inclusive `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` of the commitment window as `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates, the annual `cost_cents` of the extension (at least 0), the `expected_plan_of_services_version` guard of §2.8, and optionally `committed_account_count` (default 0, at least 0) — the number of utility accounts of that service type the provider undertakes to cut over, which is 0 for police and fire since neither is metered. The committed `service_type` must be a member of the provider's `service_types`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_SERVICE_TYPE_MISMATCH`, and the provider must belong to the case's municipality, or `ERR_PARCEL_CASE_MISMATCH` is returned. A new commitment is created in `DRAFT` with `activated_account_count` 0 and `timeline_days` computed as the number of calendar days from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` counting both endpoints — a one-day window has a `timeline_days` of 1. Creating it also supersedes any commitment of the same plan and the same `service_type` still in `DRAFT`, moving that older draft to `VOIDED`; because a `DRAFT` commitment never encumbered anything, no capacity is released by that supersession. The plan's `version` increments.

Activation is the moment a drafted promise becomes binding, and it is the admission test against the provider's calendar. The commitment must be in `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`), its `service_type` must still be one the provider offers (`ERR_PROVIDER_SERVICE_TYPE_MISMATCH`), and its `timeline_days` must not exceed the statutory ceiling for its service type — `police_fire_timeline_max_days` for `POLICE` and `FIRE`, `water_sewer_timeline_max_days` for `WATER` and `SEWER` — with a longer window refused as `ERR_PLAN_SERVICE_TIMELINE_EXCEEDS_STATUTE`. Then capacity is tested day by day. A commitment occupies every calendar day from its `window_start_date` through its `window_end_date` inclusive, so two commitments that merely touch — one ending on the day the other begins — do overlap on that shared day. The peers considered are the provider's other commitments in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`; commitments in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` occupy nothing. For each calendar day the candidate occupies, the number of peers occupying that same day plus the candidate itself must not exceed the provider's `concurrent_project_capacity`; if any single day would exceed it, activation is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Admitted, the commitment moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, its `timeline_days` is added to the provider's `encumbered_capacity_days`, and the plan's derived figures are recomputed. When a commitment's `committed_account_count` is 0 there are no accounts to cut over, so it advances `ACTIVE → FULFILLED` immediately in the same request.

The plan's derived figures follow one rule, applied after every activation, every voiding, the effectuation cascade and the rescind cascade. Consider only the plan's commitments in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`; those in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` contribute neither cost nor coverage. `committed_cost_cents` is the sum of those commitments' `cost_cents`. `covered_service_types` is the distinct set of their `service_type` values, always ordered by the fixed sequence `POLICE`, `FIRE`, `WATER`, `SEWER` regardless of the order the commitments were created in. `service_readiness` is `PENDING` when that set of commitments is empty; it is `READY` when every member of the statute's `required_service_types` appears in `covered_service_types` **and** every one of those commitments is in `FULFILLED`; and it is `PARTIAL` in every other case. A first activation therefore typically drives `PENDING → PARTIAL`, a last fulfilment drives `PARTIAL → READY`, and voiding work walks the same ladder back down — `READY → PARTIAL` when a fulfilled commitment is removed, and `PARTIAL → PENDING` when the void removes the last commitment in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`.

Voiding is available to the planner for a commitment in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`, but only while the owning case has not yet reached `EFFECTIVE` or `DE_ANNEXED`; voiding an already-`VOIDED` commitment draws `ERR_COMMITMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The void moves `commitment_status` to `VOIDED`, and when the commitment had been `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED` its `timeline_days` is subtracted from the provider's `encumbered_capacity_days` — a `DRAFT` commitment releases nothing because it encumbered nothing. `committed_cost_cents`, `covered_service_types` and `service_readiness` are then recomputed by the rule above, and the plan's `version` increments.

Adoption is the clerk's act and moves `adoption_status` from `DRAFT` to `ADOPTED`, stamping `adopted_at` from the logical clock and incrementing the plan's `version`. It requires the plan to be in `DRAFT`, requires the owning case to be in one of `TERRITORY_CERTIFIED`, `PETITION_CIRCULATING`, `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW`, `HEARING_NOTICED` or `HEARING_HELD`, requires that no other plan of the case is already `ADOPTED` (otherwise `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ADOPTED`), requires `covered_service_types` to contain every member of the statute's `required_service_types` (otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_TYPE_NOT_COVERED`), and re-checks that every commitment of the plan in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED` respects the statutory ceiling for its service type (otherwise `ERR_PLAN_SERVICE_TIMELINE_EXCEEDS_STATUTE`). An adopted plan is a precondition for publishing notice, for adopting the ordinance and for adopting the municipal service district alternative; where it is missing, those three operations are refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ADOPTED`.

### 3.6. Notice, Hearing and the Property-Owner Protest

The council chamber holds one hearing at a time. A `CityClerk` schedules one by naming the `annexation_case_id` being heard and the `municipality_id` whose chamber is occupied, giving the inclusive `scheduled_start_at` and the exclusive `scheduled_end_at` of the occupation — the end must be strictly later than the start — and optionally setting `protest_window_days` (default 30, from 1 through 120), the number of days the written-objection period stays open after the hearing is held. Occupation is tested against the city's other hearings in `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`; hearings in `VACATED` release the chamber and are ignored. Each occupation is extended by a trailing changeover buffer equal to the municipality's `hearing_changeover_buffer_minutes`. Two hearings conflict when the candidate's start is strictly earlier than the peer's end plus the buffer **and** the peer's start is strictly earlier than the candidate's end plus the buffer; any conflict is refused with `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Because both comparisons are strict, a candidate starting exactly at a peer's end plus the buffer is admitted. A new hearing is created with `hearing_status` `SCHEDULED` and `protest_outcome` `OPEN`, with `held_at`, `notice_published_at`, `protest_opens_at` and `protest_closes_at` all null and `protest_pct` 0.

Statutory notice starts the clock that makes the hearing holdable. A clerk publishes one against a hearing by declaring its `publication_type` — `NEWSPAPER`, `MAILED` or `POSTED`. The hearing must still be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED` is returned, and the case must have an `ADOPTED` plan of services, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ADOPTED`. The notice is created with `notice_status` `PUBLISHED` and `published_at` set to the logical clock, and the hearing's `notice_published_at` is set to that instant. `notice_published_at` always reflects the `published_at` of the latest notice of that hearing still in `PUBLISHED`, and is null when the hearing has none. The case advances to `HEARING_NOTICED` and its `version` increments — from `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW` on the petition path, and directly from `TERRITORY_CERTIFIED` when the case's `method` is `REFERENDUM` or `INVOLUNTARY_ORDINANCE`, since those two methods skip the petition limb entirely and never pass through `SUFFICIENCY_REVIEW` at all. Publishing notice from any other case status draws `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. A notice's `notice_status` is never set by a client: the only path to `SUPERSEDED` is the supersession cascade of §2.9, which is also the reason the per-hearing notice listing exists — it is the primary way an operator discovers that a notice was superseded behind their back.

Holding the hearing moves it `SCHEDULED → HELD` and the case `HEARING_NOTICED → HEARING_HELD`. Four conditions apply, and their order is the order of §2.10: the hearing must still be `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED`), no sibling hearing of the same case may already be `HELD` (`ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_HELD`), the hearing must have a `notice_published_at` that is not null (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`), the logical clock must have reached `notice_published_at` advanced by the statute's `notice_min_days` days (`ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_NOT_SATISFIED`), and the clock must have reached the hearing's own `scheduled_start_at` (`ERR_HEARING_NOT_STARTED`). Both temporal comparisons are satisfied at exact equality. Holding stamps `held_at` from the clock, sets `protest_opens_at` equal to `held_at` and `protest_closes_at` to `held_at` advanced by `protest_window_days` days, sets `protest_outcome` to `OPEN`, mirrors `held_at` onto the case as `hearing_held_at`, and increments the case's `version`.

Objections are filed during that window. A `PropertyOwner`, or a `CityClerk` on their behalf, files one by naming the `public_hearing_id`, the `parcel_id` whose interest is protesting, the protesting `property_owner_id` and the `ownership_share_pct` the filing represents (greater than 0 and at most 100, two decimal places). The referenced parcel must belong to the same case as the referenced hearing, or `ERR_PARCEL_CASE_MISMATCH`. The protest period must be open, or `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_CLOSED`, and the logical clock — which becomes the filing's `filed_at` — must lie inside the closed interval from `protest_opens_at` through `protest_closes_at` inclusive, or `ERR_PROTEST_FILING_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. No `FILED` filing may already exist for the same hearing, parcel and owner (`ERR_FILING_DUPLICATE`), and the sum of `ownership_share_pct` across all `FILED` filings for the same hearing and parcel may not exceed one hundred (`ERR_PROTEST_OWNERSHIP_SHARE_INVALID`). The filing is stored with `filing_status` `FILED` and `filed_at` from the clock, and its `protest_value_cents` is computed as the parcel's `assessed_value_cents` multiplied by `ownership_share_pct`, divided by one hundred, and rounded half up to a whole cent. An owner may withdraw a filing that is still `FILED` while the protest period remains open, which sets `filing_status` to `WITHDRAWN` and resets `protest_value_cents` to 0; withdrawing an already-withdrawn filing draws `ERR_FILING_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN`, and withdrawing after the period closed draws `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Closing the protest period is the tabulation, and it is admissible only when the hearing is `HELD` with `protest_outcome` still `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_CLOSED` — and only once the logical clock has moved strictly past `protest_closes_at`, since an equal reading still leaves the last day open and draws `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. The qualifying set is every filing of the hearing whose `filing_status` is `FILED` and whose `filed_at` lies inside the closed interval from `protest_opens_at` through `protest_closes_at`; withdrawn and late filings are excluded. Two bases exist and the statute picks one. Under `ASSESSED_VALUE`, `protest_pct` is the exact integer sum of the qualifying filings' already-rounded `protest_value_cents`, divided by the case's `territory_assessed_value_cents`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places — or exactly 0.00 when the territory's assessed value is zero. Under `OWNER_COUNT`, `protest_pct` is instead the number of distinct `property_owner_id` values among the qualifying filings divided by the number of distinct `primary_owner_id` values across the territory's parcels, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places — or exactly 0.00 when the territory has no distinct owners. The two bases can disagree sharply on the identical set of filings: under assessed value one high-value parcel can carry a protest by itself, while under owner count value is irrelevant and only heads matter, so the same filings can flip the outcome from one basis to the other.

The tabulation then forks. When `protest_pct` is at or above the statute's `protest_threshold_pct`, the hearing's `protest_outcome` moves `OPEN → SUSTAINED`, the hearing's `protest_pct` is stamped, the case mirrors both — `protest_pct` copied and `protest_outcome` set to `SUSTAINED` — and the case's `status` advances `HEARING_HELD → PROTEST_SUSTAINED`. When `protest_pct` is strictly below the threshold, the hearing's `protest_outcome` moves `OPEN → FAILED`, the hearing's `protest_pct` is stamped, the case mirrors both with `protest_outcome` set to `FAILED`, and the case's `status` stays exactly where it was at `HEARING_HELD` — a self-loop that leaves the case adoptable. Either branch increments the case's `version`.

### 3.7. Ordinance Adoption, Referendum and Boundary Commission Review

Adoption of the annexation ordinance is the clerk's act and the most heavily guarded operation in the system. The case must be in `HEARING_HELD` with `protest_outcome` equal to `FAILED`; a case whose `HELD` hearing still has `protest_outcome` `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_PERIOD_OPEN`, and a case in any other status with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The statutory cooling-off period must have run: the logical clock must have reached the case's `hearing_held_at` advanced by the statute's `hearing_to_ordinance_min_days` days, with an earlier reading refused as `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED` and exact equality accepted. The case must have an `ADOPTED` plan of services (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_ADOPTED`) and a `FINALIZED` fiscal impact analysis (`ERR_FISCAL_ANALYSIS_NOT_FINALIZED`), that analysis's `deficit_ratio_pct` must be no greater than the municipality's `fiscal_deficit_tolerance_pct` — equality is tolerated, anything above it is refused with `ERR_FISCAL_DEFICIT_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE` — and the case's `method` must still appear in the statute's `authorized_methods` (`ERR_METHOD_NOT_AUTHORIZED_BY_STATUTE`).

Adoption then branches on the statute's `referendum_required_methods`. In both branches `ordinance_adopted_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the case's `version` increments. When the case's `method` is **not** among the methods requiring ratification, the case advances `HEARING_HELD → ORDINANCE_ADOPTED`, and its `commission_review_status` becomes `PENDING` if the statute's `boundary_commission_required` is true and stays `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise. When the case's `method` **is** among them, the case advances `HEARING_HELD → REFERENDUM_PENDING` instead and its `referendum_result` stays `NOT_HELD` until the vote is recorded.

Recording the referendum requires the case to be in `REFERENDUM_PENDING` and carries a single `result` input. A `result` of `APPROVED` sets `referendum_result` to `APPROVED`, advances the case `REFERENDUM_PENDING → REFERENDUM_APPROVED`, and sets `commission_review_status` to `PENDING` when the statute requires commission review. A `result` of `REJECTED` sets `referendum_result` to `REJECTED` and advances the case `REFERENDUM_PENDING → DENIED`, which is terminal and triggers the rescind cascade in full: every parcel in `PENDING_ANNEX` moves to `EXCLUDED`, the `ADOPTED` plan moves to `RESCINDED`, every commitment of that plan in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED` moves to `VOIDED`, each voided commitment's `timeline_days` is released from its provider's `encumbered_capacity_days`, and the plan's `committed_cost_cents` and `covered_service_types` are recomputed with `service_readiness` returning to `PENDING`. Either result increments the case's `version`.

Where state law demands boundary commission or county approval, the planner submits the adopted annexation for review. The case must be in `ORDINANCE_ADOPTED` or `REFERENDUM_APPROVED`, the statute's `boundary_commission_required` must be true, and `commission_review_status` must be `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_COMMISSION_REVIEW_NOT_PENDING`. Submission advances the case to `COMMISSION_REVIEW` from either origin state and increments its `version`; both origins are equally admissible and neither is privileged.

The commissioner's decision closes the review and carries a single `decision` input. The case must be in `COMMISSION_REVIEW`, or `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` is returned, with `commission_review_status` still `PENDING`, or `ERR_COMMISSION_REVIEW_NOT_PENDING` is returned, and the commissioner's `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `fiscal_finalized_by_user_id` or the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `decision` of `APPROVED` sets `commission_review_status` to `APPROVED` and advances the case `COMMISSION_REVIEW → COMMISSION_APPROVED`. A `decision` of `DENIED` sets `commission_review_status` to `DENIED`, advances the case `COMMISSION_REVIEW → DENIED`, and runs exactly the same rescind cascade as a referendum rejection — `PENDING_ANNEX` parcels to `EXCLUDED`, the `ADOPTED` plan to `RESCINDED`, its `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` and `FULFILLED` commitments to `VOIDED`, provider `encumbered_capacity_days` released, and the plan's `committed_cost_cents`, `covered_service_types` and `service_readiness` recomputed with readiness falling to `PENDING`. Both decisions increment the case's `version`.

### 3.8. Effectuation: The Boundary Change, the Utility Cut-Over and the Tax Roll

Effectuation is the planner's act of making the boundary change real on a stated date, and it is admissible from three origin states — `ORDINANCE_ADOPTED`, `REFERENDUM_APPROVED` and `COMMISSION_APPROVED` — each advancing to `EFFECTIVE` with the identical preconditions and the identical cascade. Where the statute's `boundary_commission_required` is true, `commission_review_status` must be `APPROVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMMISSION_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`; where the statute does not require commission review, no such check applies. Where the case's `method` appears in `referendum_required_methods`, `referendum_result` must be `APPROVED`. Where the case's `case_type` is `DE_ANNEXATION`, the case named by `predecessor_case_id` must still be in `EFFECTIVE` and must still belong to the same municipality; a predecessor already unwound, or one in any other status, is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.

The single input is `effective_date`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, and its admissibility is decided by the statute's `effective_date_rule` against the date part of the logical clock. Under `IMMEDIATE`, the submitted date must equal the clock's own date. Under `NEXT_QUARTER_START`, it must equal the first day of the calendar quarter following the quarter that contains the clock's date — the first of April for a clock in January through March, the first of July for April through June, the first of October for July through September, and the first of January of the following year for October through December. Under `NEXT_FISCAL_YEAR`, first locate the fiscal year end: form the date in the clock's own year using the statute's `fiscal_year_end_month_day`, keep it if the clock's date is on or before it, and otherwise use the same month and day in the following year; the submitted date must equal the day after that fiscal year end. Any other date is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`.

Admitted, the case's `status` becomes `EFFECTIVE` and `effective_date` is stamped, and a six-link cascade runs. Because the case became `EFFECTIVE`, every parcel of the case in `PENDING_ANNEX` moves to `INCORPORATED`; a de-annexation case owns no parcels of its own, so this link is a no-op for it and the predecessor cascade of §3.9 runs instead. Because those parcels became `INCORPORATED`, each of their utility accounts in `OUTSIDE_CITY` moves to `INSIDE_CITY_PENDING_TRANSITION`, its `rate_schedule` moves from `OUTSIDE_CITY_SURCHARGE` to `INSIDE_CITY`, and its `effective_monthly_cents` is recomputed with the surcharge dropped. Because those accounts entered `INSIDE_CITY_PENDING_TRANSITION`, for each of them the commitment on the case's `ADOPTED` plan that is in `ACTIVE` and matches both the account's `service_type` and its `service_provider_id` has its `activated_account_count` incremented by one; once `activated_account_count` reaches or exceeds that commitment's `committed_account_count`, the commitment advances `ACTIVE → FULFILLED`. Because commitments became `FULFILLED`, the plan's `covered_service_types` and `service_readiness` are recomputed by the readiness rule of §3.5. And because `service_readiness` reached `READY`, every utility account of the case still in `INSIDE_CITY_PENDING_TRANSITION` whose service type's commitment is `FULFILLED` advances to `INSIDE_CITY_ACTIVE`. The case's `version` increments once for the whole operation.

Utility billing is the visible consequence of that cut-over. A `MunicipalPlanner` registers each account against a `parcel_id` and a `service_provider_id`, declaring the `service_type` — only `WATER` or `SEWER`, since police and fire are not metered — and the inside-city `monthly_base_cents` (greater than 0); the referenced parcel and provider must belong to the same case and municipality or `ERR_PARCEL_CASE_MISMATCH` is returned. An account starts with `account_status` `OUTSIDE_CITY` and `rate_schedule` `OUTSIDE_CITY_SURCHARGE`. `effective_monthly_cents` is recomputed on registration and on every change of `rate_schedule`: while the schedule is `OUTSIDE_CITY_SURCHARGE` it is `monthly_base_cents` multiplied by one plus the municipality's `utility_outside_surcharge_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent; while the schedule is `INSIDE_CITY` it is simply `monthly_base_cents` with no surcharge at all. An account whose parcel is excluded — by planner carve-out or by the district cascade of §3.9 — moves from `OUTSIDE_CITY` to the terminal `DISCONNECTED`.

The fiscal dimension of a case moves on its own axis. `tax_roll_status` starts at `NOT_TRANSFERRED` and is advanced only by the county assessor and by de-annexation reversal; it is entirely independent of the procedural `status`. The assessor posts the roll once the case is `EFFECTIVE` and `tax_roll_status` is still `NOT_TRANSFERRED`; a second attempt draws `ERR_TAX_ROLL_ALREADY_POSTED`, and posting before the case is effective draws `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. Posting prorates the municipal levy over the remainder of the fiscal year. First locate the fiscal year end: form the date in the `effective_date`'s own year using the statute's `fiscal_year_end_month_day`, keep it if `effective_date` is on or before it, and otherwise take the same month and day in the following year. The remaining days are the number of calendar days from `effective_date` through that fiscal year end counting both endpoints. Then walk the case's parcels in `INCORPORATED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier, and for each one stamp `annual_tax_cents` as the parcel's `assessed_value_cents` multiplied by the municipality's `general_fund_millage_rate`, divided by one thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent; then stamp `prorated_tax_cents` as that `annual_tax_cents` multiplied by the remaining days, divided by the three hundred and sixty-five days of the fiscal year, rounded half up to a whole cent. Only parcels in `INCORPORATED` at the moment of posting participate; excluded parcels contribute nothing. The case's `tax_roll_transferred_tax_cents` is the exact integer sum of those already-rounded per-parcel amounts — rounding is per parcel first and summation second, never the other way around — and `tax_roll_transferred_assessed_value_cents` is the exact integer sum of their `assessed_value_cents`. The municipality's `taxable_assessed_value_cents` is increased by that transferred assessed value, `tax_roll_status` advances `NOT_TRANSFERRED → POSTED`, and the case's `version` increments.

### 3.9. Unwinding: De-Annexation and the Municipal Service District Alternative

A de-annexation case is an ordinary case with `case_type` `DE_ANNEXATION` and a `predecessor_case_id` pointing at the `EFFECTIVE` annexation it unwinds. It walks the same procedural ladder — certification, plan, analysis, notice, hearing, protest, ordinance and, where required, referendum and commission review — but its territory aggregates read through the predecessor rather than through parcels of its own, and its effectuation carries an extra cascade.

When a de-annexation case becomes `EFFECTIVE`, the predecessor advances `EFFECTIVE → DE_ANNEXED`, which is terminal. Because the predecessor became `DE_ANNEXED`, every parcel of the predecessor in `INCORPORATED` moves to `DE_ANNEXED`. Because those parcels were de-annexed, each of their utility accounts in `INSIDE_CITY_PENDING_TRANSITION` or `INSIDE_CITY_ACTIVE` returns to `OUTSIDE_CITY`, its `rate_schedule` returns to `OUTSIDE_CITY_SURCHARGE`, and its `effective_monthly_cents` is recomputed with the surcharge restored — closing the account cycle `OUTSIDE_CITY → INSIDE_CITY_PENDING_TRANSITION → INSIDE_CITY_ACTIVE → OUTSIDE_CITY`. And because the predecessor was de-annexed while its `tax_roll_status` was `POSTED`, that status advances `POSTED → REVERSED`, the municipality's `taxable_assessed_value_cents` is decreased by the predecessor's `tax_roll_transferred_assessed_value_cents`, and the successor's `de_annexation_refund_cents` is computed. Both cases have their `version` incremented. A predecessor whose tax roll never posted refunds nothing, and its `tax_roll_status` stays `NOT_TRANSFERRED`.

The refund itself follows the statute's `de_annexation_refund_basis` and is measured against the predecessor's already-posted `tax_roll_transferred_tax_cents`. Under `DAILY_PRORATION`, locate the fiscal year end relative to the de-annexation case's own `effective_date` — the statute's `fiscal_year_end_month_day` in the effective date's year if the effective date is on or before it, otherwise the same month and day the following year — count the days from the effective date through that fiscal year end inclusive of both endpoints, and set the refund to the predecessor's posted tax multiplied by those remaining days, divided by the three hundred and sixty-five days of the fiscal year, rounded half up to a whole cent. Under `WHOLE_QUARTER`, count the calendar quarters remaining including the one that contains the effective date — four for an effective date in January through March, three for April through June, two for July through September, one for October through December — and set the refund to the predecessor's posted tax multiplied by those remaining quarters, divided by the four quarters of the year, rounded half up to a whole cent. Daily proration tracks the exact remaining days, so a mid-quarter effective date refunds only a fraction of that quarter; whole-quarter refunds the containing quarter in full, and therefore returns a strictly larger amount than daily proration for any effective date after the first day of a quarter.

The other unwinding path is the alternative state law offers a city whose annexation was defeated by objection. With the case in `PROTEST_SUSTAINED`, a `CityClerk` may adopt a municipal service district instead of the boundary change, advancing the case `PROTEST_SUSTAINED → MSD_ALTERNATIVE_ADOPTED`, which is terminal. The statute's `msd_alternative_allowed` must be true, or the request is refused with `ERR_MSD_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_ALLOWED`, and the case must have an `ADOPTED` plan of services, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ADOPTED`. Adoption computes `msd_annual_assessment_cents` as that plan's `committed_cost_cents` — which already excludes commitments in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` — multiplied by the statute's `msd_cost_recovery_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Because no boundary changes, every parcel of the case in `PENDING_ANNEX` moves to `EXCLUDED`, and because those parcels were excluded, each of their utility accounts still in `OUTSIDE_CITY` moves to `DISCONNECTED`. The case's `version` increments. The district recovers a share of the promised service cost through an annual assessment without the territory ever entering the corporate limits — which is exactly the compromise the protest threshold was designed to force.
