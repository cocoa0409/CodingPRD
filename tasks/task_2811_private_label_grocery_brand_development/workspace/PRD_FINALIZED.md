# Private-Label Brand Development API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A private brand agency sits between a grocery retailer and the manufacturers who will make the retailer's own-label goods, and it earns its fee by compressing a very long chain of judgements into a defensible record. The chain begins with a category gap analysis — how much of the shelf a national brand currently owns, how fast the category turns, what the benchmark item retails for — and ends, sometimes three years later, with a decision to rationalize an item off the planogram in favour of a successor. In between sit supplier qualification and plant capability audits, cost modelling against quoted and audited yields, bench-top cuttings and blind consumer panels against the national-brand control, packaging artwork with a generated nutrition panel, regulatory claim review, first-production approval and specification lock, ongoing quality auditing, complaint trending, supplier scorecards, and the cost-change negotiations that inevitably reopen a specification the agency thought was closed.

Today that chain lives in spreadsheets, and it fails in predictable ways. A specification gets locked against a cost model that a plant audit re-costed the previous afternoon. A launched item keeps a "reduced sodium" claim on its label after an accepted ingredient substitution changed the nutrition panel out from under it. Two shelf slots are promised where the retailer authorised one. A slotting fee is committed twice against a development budget that has no accounting of what was released and what was not. The system specified here exists to make each of those failures a rejected request with a named error code rather than a discovery made at first shipment.

This document defines the API that agency staff, the retailer's category team and the suppliers themselves all transact against. It is mounted under `/private-label`. Fourteen resource collections form a flat surface — every path is a collection, optionally an id, optionally an action — and the relationships between them are carried by identifier fields in request payloads rather than by nested URLs. Read it as a specification of the business, not of a database: every state, every threshold, every rounding decision and every cascade described below is load-bearing, and a compliant implementation reproduces all of them exactly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including reads and including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries any string other than the six role names enumerated below, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ProgramDirector`, `SourcingManager`, `QualityAuditor`, `RegulatoryReviewer`, `PackagingDesigner` and `SupplierUser`.

A `ProgramDirector` is the agency's program owner: budgets, tier decisions, supplier qualification decisions, specification lock, production-run approval, item launch and item rationalization are all theirs alone. A `SourcingManager` is the category sourcing lead: gap analysis, supplier and plant onboarding, quote acceptance, specification drafting, run scheduling and cost-change countering. A `QualityAuditor` owns the quality function — plant audits, comparison studies, run completion, complaint intake. A `RegulatoryReviewer` owns label compliance: claim review and artwork review, and by design may never review a claim it submitted itself. A `PackagingDesigner` staffs the artwork studio, creating rounds, generating nutrition panels, drafting claims and submitting rounds for review, and never reviews anything. A `SupplierUser` is the manufacturer acting on its own records: quote submission and cost-change negotiation.

Identity semantics differ across the roles and must be implemented precisely. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `SourcingManager`, `QualityAuditor`, `RegulatoryReviewer` or `PackagingDesigner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `designer_user_id` or `reviewer_user_id` on an artwork round, `submitted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a label claim, `locked_by_user_id` on a specification, `approved_by_user_id` on a production run, or `decided_by_user_id` on a cost change request) and may compare two such recorded identifiers to each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a supplier compare that resource's supplier foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SupplierUser` may submit a cost model only for its own `supplier_id`, may open, revise or withdraw a cost change request only where the target cost model's `supplier_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read a supplier scorecard only for its own supplier. Any of those addressed at another supplier's records fails with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

Two further authorisation rules exist that no endpoint role list can express, because they depend on stored data rather than on the caller's role alone. A label claim may not be reviewed by the user who submitted it, and a production run may not be approved by the user who locked the specification the run is produced against; the first yields `ERR_CLAIM_SELF_REVIEW` and the second `ERR_RUN_SELF_APPROVAL`, both HTTP 403. Additionally, acceptance authority over a cost change request depends on the escalation tier the settlement arithmetic computed for it: a request whose `escalation_tier` is `T1` may be decided by either a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`, while a request whose `escalation_tier` is `T2` or `T3` may be decided only by a `ProgramDirector`; a `SourcingManager` attempting the latter is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_ROLE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a list endpoint's description says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"`, and their `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned window.

Each collection's list endpoint also accepts the foreign keys of that collection as optional equality filters, so that every relationship is reverse-reachable: category gaps filter by `program_id`, suppliers by `program_id`, plant lines by `supplier_id`, plant audits by `supplier_id`, items by `gap_id`, `supplier_id` and `replacement_item_id`, cost models by `item_id` and `supplier_id`, comparison studies by `item_id`, artwork rounds by `item_id`, label claims by `artwork_round_id`, specifications by `item_id`, `pinned_cost_model_id` and `pinned_artwork_round_id`, production runs by `item_id`, `specification_id` and `plant_line_id`, complaints by `item_id`, and cost change requests by `item_id` and `cost_model_id`. Enumerated attributes are filterable in the same way: `status` on programs, gaps, audits, cost models, studies, artwork rounds, claims, specifications, runs and change requests; `fiscal_year` on programs; `recommended_tier` on gaps; `qualification_status` on suppliers; `audit_type` and `result` on plant audits; `development_status`, `quality_posture` and `tier` on items; `study_type` and `verdict` on studies; `nutrition_status` on artwork rounds; `claim_type` on claims; `run_type` on runs; `severity` and `defect_category` on complaints; and `escalation_tier` and `substitution_scope` on change requests. Programs are the one deliberate exception to the default sort: the program list is returned newest `created_at` first.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix and no embedded semantics, and clients must never derive meaning from them; in particular, no business rule anywhere in this specification orders, selects or breaks a tie by `id`. Where an ordering or a "most recent" selection is needed, the rule names the timestamp field it uses, and where two candidates tie on that field the tie-break is `created_at`.

Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded at insert and never changed thereafter. Thirteen of the fourteen collections additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused by a cascade from some other resource rather than by a request addressed at the row directly.

`Complaint` is the exception. Complaints are append-only: a logged complaint is never updated and never deleted, so a complaint resource exposes `id` and `created_at` and nothing else of that family — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. A complaint does carry `received_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the quality desk logged it, and `received_at` always equals the complaint's `created_at`; `received_at` is the reference time for every trailing-window computation that reads complaints.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Windows, certificate expiries and trailing-period aggregations all depend on "now", and the system therefore reads time from a logical clock rather than from wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. Any role may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; from that moment every subsequent request sees the supplied instant as "now" until the clock is set again. Moving the clock is inert with respect to data: no entity is modified, no score is recomputed and no state transition fires merely because the clock moved.

Two interval conventions coexist and must not be conflated. Plant audit windows are **half-open** intervals — the span from `window_start` up to but not including `window_end` — so an audit that starts exactly when another ends does not overlap it. Production run windows are **closed** intervals, spanning `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive, so two runs whose windows merely touch at an endpoint do overlap. Run windows are further expanded by a sanitation buffer before overlap is tested; audit windows are never buffered.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere: not in a landed cost, not in a slotting fee, not in a negotiated increase, not in a retail price. Percentages, scores, indices and per-serving nutrient grams are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places unless a rule states otherwise.

Rounding is round half up everywhere unless a step explicitly names a different mode, and a computation rounds **once**, at the point the rule says it rounds — intermediate quotients are carried at full precision. Three places deliberately break the round-half-up default and must be implemented as stated: the escalation tier cap in a cost change settlement is **floored** to a whole cent; the label rounding of a nutrition panel snaps to bands (nearest 5 or nearest 10 calories, nearest half gram or nearest whole gram, nearest 5 or nearest 10 milligrams of sodium) rather than to a decimal place; and a retail price is walked **upward** from a rounded figure to the next integer ending in the digit 9. The commitment ledger performs no rounding at all — its quantities are exact integers.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and on those two the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

`POST /private-label/specifications` requires `expected_item_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `ProductItem` that the client last read. Specification creation legitimately supersedes an item's earlier specification revision — that is exactly how a relaunch after an ingredient substitution proceeds — so no state precondition blocks a second create; the stale-version check is the only thing standing between a writer and pinning cost and artwork versions it never observed. If `expected_item_version` does not equal the item's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

`POST /private-label/cost-change-requests` requires `expected_cost_model_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `CostModel` that the supplier last read. The requested cost deltas are settled against that cost model's `effective_base_cents`, which an audit-driven yield recompute or a previously accepted change request may already have moved; a request carrying a stale version would therefore be settled against a base the supplier never saw. If `expected_cost_model_version` does not equal the cost model's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COST_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind. Distinct from optimistic concurrency control, and not to be confused with it, is **pin staleness**: a specification stores the cost-model and artwork-round versions it observed at create time in `pinned_cost_model_version` and `pinned_artwork_version`, and if either pinned entity has advanced by the time the specification is locked, the lock is refused with `ERR_PIN_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 409). Optimistic concurrency control guards a create against an unobserved parent; pin staleness guards a lock against pins that went out of date after the specification was drafted.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one row. Wherever that is so, this document enumerates every affected row, and every one of those rows has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 as part of the same operation — a cascaded change is a mutation like any other. Cascades are also **conditional**: a cascade hop applies only to rows that are currently in a state the hop names, and rows already in a terminal state are left alone. Closing a program rejects only its `DRAFT` and `ANALYZED` gaps; suspending a supplier holds only its `SCHEDULED` and `APPROVED` runs; rationalizing an item cancels only its `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED` and `HOLD` runs.

The longest chain in the system runs five hops deep from a single accepted cost change request, and each hop is conditional on the hop before it having actually fired. Because a cascade can move a resource the caller never named, clients that hold a version of a related row must re-read it after any operation described below as cascading.

### 2.9. Request Validation Order

Checks are evaluated in a strict order, and the **first** violated check determines the response; later violations are neither reported nor merged into the response, even when they are also true. The order is: presence of the two auth headers; recognisability of the role; the role's authorisation for the endpoint; well-formedness of the request body and query string; existence of every path identifier; existence of every foreign key in the body; the supplier-scope restriction on `SupplierUser`; the claim self-review prohibition; the run self-approval prohibition; the two optimistic-concurrency version checks; the program-closed check; the program-relaunch-frozen check; category-gap state; supplier qualification state and plant-audit state; the addressed resource's own transition state; item quality suspension; awarded-supplier qualification; GFSI certificate validity; audit-basis validity; claim substantiation; nutrition-panel validity; claim approval completeness; tier agreement; specification pin agreement; pin version currency; run-to-specification and line-to-supplier agreement; cost-model acceptance; replacement-item validity; claim-to-round state; window ordering; line window conflict; audit concurrency capacity; run window elapsed; shelf-slot availability; override justification; duplicate line code; duplicate draft specification; open change request; substitution description; negotiation round limit; minimum order quantity; line capacity; program budget; and finally the escalation-tier role check.

Well-formedness, evaluated fourth, covers a single rejection code, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), for all of: an unknown field in the body or query string, a missing required field, a value of the wrong type, an enumerated value outside its declared set, a numeric value violating one of the constraints stated in the workflow narratives below, a datetime that is not ISO 8601 UTC, and a pagination `limit` or `offset` outside its permitted range.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the 404 code. Foreign keys in a request body are resolved after path identifiers, in the order the receiving resource declares its fields. Each identifier has its own not-found code: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` for `program_id`, `ERR_GAP_NOT_FOUND` for `gap_id`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND` for `supplier_id`, `ERR_PLANT_LINE_NOT_FOUND` for `plant_line_id`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` for `plant_audit_id` or `basis_audit_id`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for `item_id` or `replacement_item_id`, `ERR_COST_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` for `cost_model_id` or `pinned_cost_model_id`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND` for `study_id`, `ERR_ARTWORK_ROUND_NOT_FOUND` for `artwork_round_id` or `pinned_artwork_round_id`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for `label_claim_id`, `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` for `specification_id`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND` for `production_run_id`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` for `complaint_id`, and `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for `cost_change_request_id`. All fourteen are HTTP 404.

Two program-level gates sit above every per-entity state check. Any mutating request whose resource resolves to a program that is `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). While a program is in `RELAUNCH_FREEZE`, exactly two operations are refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RELAUNCH_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): creating a new item, and locking a specification. Everything else, rationalization included, stays open during a freeze.

### 2.10. Two-Dimensional State

Two resources carry two independent state dimensions each, and the dimensions must be tracked separately rather than collapsed into one status.

A `ProductItem` has a `development_status` — one of `CONCEPT`, `SOURCED`, `BENCHMARKED`, `PACKAGING_APPROVED`, `SPEC_LOCKED`, `LAUNCHED`, `RELAUNCH_PENDING`, `RATIONALIZED` or `DISCONTINUED`, starting at `CONCEPT` — and a `quality_posture` — one of `GREEN`, `WATCH`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, starting at `GREEN`. Development progress and quality standing move on their own tracks: a `LAUNCHED` item can be `PROBATION`, and a `CONCEPT` item can be `SUSPENDED` because its awarded supplier was suspended. `RATIONALIZED` and `DISCONTINUED` are the terminal development states; the posture dimension has no terminal state and continues to move for as long as the item is not rationalized or discontinued.

An `ArtworkRound` has a review `status` — one of `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUPERSEDED`, starting at `DRAFT`, with `SUPERSEDED` terminal — and a `nutrition_status` — one of `NOT_GENERATED`, `VALID` or `INVALIDATED`, starting at `NOT_GENERATED`. The nutrition dimension gates the review dimension in one direction only: a round cannot be submitted for review, and cannot be approved, unless its nutrition panel is `VALID`.

### 2.11. The Commitment Ledger: Two Conserved Quantities

A brand program's development budget and a category's shelf slots are both scarce, both drawn down by operations that happen weeks apart, and both released by compensating actions — so both are maintained as reconciling ledgers rather than as simple counters, and the reconciliation is exposed as a computed read.

For money, `committed_budget_cents` on a program must at all times equal `lifetime_committed_cents` minus `lifetime_released_cents`, where both lifetime figures are monotonically increasing and never decrease. Equivalently, `committed_budget_cents` is the sum of `slotting_fee_cents` over the program's specifications whose status is `LOCKED`, `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` or `SUPERSEDED`, plus the sum of `committed_tooling_cents` over the program's production runs whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED`, `HOLD` or `COMPLETED`. The program's available budget is `dev_budget_cents` minus `committed_budget_cents`. The budget-ledger read returns `program_id`, `dev_budget_cents`, `committed_budget_cents`, `available_budget_cents`, `lifetime_committed_cents`, `lifetime_released_cents` and `active_item_count`.

For shelf space, `slots_consumed` on a category gap must at all times equal `slots_consumed_lifetime` minus `slots_released_lifetime`, and `slots_open` is `shelf_slots_quota` minus `slots_consumed`. Each item counts as exactly one slot: `slots_consumed` is the count of the gap's items whose `development_status` is neither `RATIONALIZED` nor `DISCONTINUED`. The program-level `active_item_count` is the same count taken across every gap of the program.

The two ledgers have different release triggers and that asymmetry is intentional. Cancelling a production run releases its committed tooling but no shelf slot. Rationalizing or discontinuing an item releases its shelf slot and cancels its non-terminal runs — thereby releasing their tooling too — but the slotting fee of that item's locked specification stays committed forever. A shelf slot is recoverable; a slotting fee is not. Completing a run does not release its tooling either: only cancellation does. The ledger itself is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding anywhere.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Program and Sizing the Category Gap

A `ProgramDirector` begins by standing up a brand program, naming the private brand in `brand_name`, the sponsoring retailer in `retailer_name`, the `fiscal_year` whose budget the work belongs to — a whole year no earlier than 2020 and no later than 2100, inclusive at both ends — and the total development money available for slotting fees and tooling in `dev_budget_cents`, which may be zero but never negative. A new program opens in `ACTIVE` status with `committed_budget_cents`, `lifetime_committed_cents`, `lifetime_released_cents` and `active_item_count` all initialised to zero.

Under that program, category gap analyses are opened by a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`, each one carrying the `program_id` it belongs to, the retailer's `category_code`, the `national_brand_name` used as the benchmark, and four commercial measurements: `nb_retail_price_cents`, the national brand's shelf price, which must be strictly greater than zero; `category_annual_sales_cents`, the retailer's annual sales in the category, which may be zero but not negative; `pl_share_pct`, the private label's current unit share of the category expressed as a percentage between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive; and `unit_velocity_per_week`, the category's per-store weekly unit velocity, which may be zero but not negative. The gap also carries `shelf_slots_quota`, the number of private-label facings the retailer authorises for the category, which must be at least 1. A new gap opens in `DRAFT` with `gap_score`, `recommended_tier` and `approved_tier` all null, `slots_consumed` at zero, `slots_open` equal to `shelf_slots_quota`, and `slots_consumed_lifetime` and `slots_released_lifetime` both at zero.

Scoring the gap is the act that advances it `DRAFT → ANALYZED`, and it may be repeated from `ANALYZED` as many times as the category team likes — a re-analysis is a self-loop that leaves the status at `ANALYZED` while recomputing the score. Either way the acting role is a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`, the governing program must be `ACTIVE`, and any of the four analysis measurements — `category_annual_sales_cents`, `pl_share_pct`, `unit_velocity_per_week`, `nb_retail_price_cents` — may be restated in the payload. Restated values are written to the gap **first**, and the score is then computed from the stored values, so a re-analysis that supplies a corrected velocity scores against the corrected velocity. Analysis reads nothing but the single gap being analysed; no other row participates. A gap that has already reached `TIER_APPROVED` or `REJECTED` can no longer be analysed, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_GAP_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The **gap score** is the sum of four components, each capped or floored in its own way, and the sum itself capped. The sales component is the annual category sales in cents divided by 25,000,000 — that is, one point per two hundred and fifty thousand dollars of category turnover — rounded half up to two decimals, then capped at 40.00. The share component is 35.00 minus the current private-label share percentage, floored at 0.00, so a category already above thirty-five percent private-label penetration contributes nothing. The velocity component is the weekly unit velocity divided by 40, rounded half up to two decimals, then capped at 15.00. The price component is a flat 10.00 whenever the national brand's shelf price is at least 400 cents, and otherwise the shelf price in cents divided by 40, rounded half up to two decimals. The four components are added together, the total is capped at 100.00, and the result is rounded half up to two decimals and stored in `gap_score`.

From that score the system derives `recommended_tier`. The recommendation is `PREMIUM` when the gap score is at least 70.00 **and** the national brand's shelf price is at least 500 cents; otherwise it is `NBE` when the gap score is at least 45.00; otherwise it is `OPENING_PRICE`. The tier band is deliberately not a pure function of the score — a gap scoring 79.50 against a national brand retailing at 420 cents recommends `NBE`, not `PREMIUM`, because the price floor is unmet.

The tier decision belongs to a `ProgramDirector` alone and requires the gap to be `ANALYZED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_GAP_NOT_ANALYZED` (HTTP 409). With `decision` set to `APPROVE`, the director supplies `approved_tier` — one of `OPENING_PRICE`, `NBE` or `PREMIUM` — and the gap moves `ANALYZED → TIER_APPROVED`, which is terminal. A director is free to overrule the recommendation, but when `approved_tier` differs from `recommended_tier` a non-empty `override_justification` must accompany the decision; omitting it, or supplying it empty, is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). With `decision` set to `REJECT` the gap moves `ANALYZED → REJECTED`, also terminal, and no tier or justification is involved.

Late in a brand's life a director drives the program itself through a relaunch or a closure, supplying an `action`. With `action` set to `START_RELAUNCH` the program moves `ACTIVE → RELAUNCH_FREEZE`, but only if at least one item under the program is `LAUNCHED` or `RELAUNCH_PENDING` — there is nothing to relaunch otherwise, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_LAUNCHED_ITEMS` (HTTP 409). With `action` set to `COMPLETE_RELAUNCH` the program returns `RELAUNCH_FREEZE → ACTIVE`, but only once no item under it is still `RELAUNCH_PENDING`; while one remains, the attempt is refused with `ERR_RELAUNCH_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). `ACTIVE → RELAUNCH_FREEZE → ACTIVE` is a genuine cycle and a program may be relaunched as often as the retailer's reset schedule demands. With `action` set to `CLOSE` the program moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and this is permitted only when no production run anywhere in the program is still `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED` or `HOLD`; otherwise the closure is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_RUNS_PRESENT` (HTTP 409). Closing cascades downward: every category gap of the program whose status is `DRAFT` or `ANALYZED` transitions to `REJECTED`, each such gap having its `version` incremented and `updated_at` refreshed, while gaps already `TIER_APPROVED` or `REJECTED` are left untouched.

### 3.2. Qualifying the Supply Base

Suppliers are enrolled into a program by a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector` with a `program_id`, the manufacturer's `legal_name`, the two-letter ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 `plant_country` of the manufacturing site, and `gfsi_certificate_expiry` — the instant the plant's GFSI food-safety certificate lapses, as an ISO 8601 UTC datetime. Enrolment opens the supplier at `qualification_status` `PROSPECT` with `qualified_at` null, `composite_score_cached` null, `probation_item_count` at zero and `weighted_complaint_points` at zero. That last counter is a rolling weight of consumer complaints against the supplier's items over the trailing 365 days, counting five points for every `CRITICAL` complaint, two for every `MAJOR` and one for every `MINOR`.

Each plant registers its production lines separately. A line carries the `supplier_id` that owns it, a `line_code` that must be unique within that supplier — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_CODE` (HTTP 409) — a `daily_capacity_units` figure strictly greater than zero describing what the line can produce in a twenty-four-hour day, and `changeover_buffer_minutes`, the sanitation and changeover allowance applied to **both** ends of every production window booked on that line. The buffer may be zero or any positive number of minutes and defaults to two hours when the field is omitted at registration.

Audits are the `QualityAuditor`'s instrument, and they come in two flavours distinguished by `audit_type`: a `CAPABILITY` audit qualifies a prospective plant, and an `ONGOING_QUALITY` audit polices a plant already in production. Scheduling one names the `supplier_id` and a window bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`, where `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` — an inverted or zero-length window is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Audit windows are half-open, and no more than two audits of the same supplier may cover any single instant; a third overlapping audit is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Because the interval excludes its end instant, an audit beginning exactly when another ends does not count as covering a shared instant, and no changeover buffer is applied to audit windows. A newly scheduled audit sits at `SCHEDULED` with `result` and all four pillar scores null.

Finalizing an audit moves it `SCHEDULED → FINALIZED`, which is terminal, and requires the auditor to supply all five measurements at once: `food_safety_score`, `sanitation_score`, `capacity_score` and `traceability_score`, each a whole number from 0 through 100 inclusive, and `audited_yield_pct`, the line yield actually measured, strictly greater than 0.0 and no more than 100.0. Finalizing an audit that is already `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409). From the four pillars the system computes `result`: `PASS` when the food-safety score is at least 90 and the lowest of the four pillar scores is at least 80; failing that, `CONDITIONAL` when the food-safety score is at least 80 and the lowest of the four is at least 70; otherwise `FAIL`.

Finalizing an audit reaches beyond the audit row. Every cost model belonging to that supplier whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` has its audited landed cost recomputed against the freshly measured yield, and each of those cost models has its `version` incremented and `updated_at` refreshed — which is precisely how a cost model a client read yesterday becomes stale for pinning purposes today. The supplier's `composite_score_cached` is also refreshed, under the rolling-twelve-month window described in §3.8.

Qualification decisions belong to a `ProgramDirector` alone and are expressed as a `decision` of `QUALIFY`, `CONDITIONAL`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `DISQUALIFY`. The first three of those five — `QUALIFY`, `CONDITIONAL` and `REINSTATE` — must additionally cite a `basis_audit_id`. The cited audit must be a `FINALIZED` `CAPABILITY` audit of this same supplier whose `result` satisfies the decision being taken: `PASS` for `QUALIFY`, and `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL` for `CONDITIONAL` and for `REINSTATE`. Where freshness matters — annual recertification of an already-`QUALIFIED` supplier, promotion of a `CONDITIONAL` supplier to `QUALIFIED`, and reinstatement of a `SUSPENDED` one — the cited audit's `window_end` must also be strictly after the supplier's current `qualified_at`, so that yesterday's basis cannot be reused to renew today. A basis that fails any of those conditions is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_BASIS_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Separately, a `QUALIFY` or `CONDITIONAL` decision requires `gfsi_certificate_expiry` to be strictly after the logical clock at decision time; a certificate expiring exactly at the current instant has already lapsed, and the decision is refused with `ERR_GFSI_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). A decision that is simply not defined from the supplier's current standing — reinstating a `PROSPECT`, disqualifying a `QUALIFIED` supplier — is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The qualification lifecycle is a cycle rather than a ladder. `PROSPECT → QUALIFIED` requires a passing capability audit and a live certificate, and on success sets `qualified_at` to the logical clock's current instant and refreshes `composite_score_cached`. `PROSPECT → CONDITIONAL` is the softer landing, accepting a basis audit whose result is `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL`, and it likewise stamps `qualified_at` — but it does not refresh the cached score. `QUALIFIED → QUALIFIED` is the annual recertification self-loop: it re-stamps `qualified_at` to now, which deliberately resets the period-to-date scorecard window described in §3.8, and refreshes `composite_score_cached`. `CONDITIONAL → QUALIFIED` promotes the supplier, re-stamps `qualified_at`, and releases the supply chain: every production run of that supplier's items sitting at `HOLD` transitions back to `SCHEDULED`, each with its `version` incremented, while runs in any other status are untouched.

Suspension runs the cascade in the other direction. `QUALIFIED → SUSPENDED` and `CONDITIONAL → SUSPENDED` both require nothing beyond the supplier currently standing at `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and both do the same three things beyond flipping the supplier's own status: every item awarded to that supplier whose `development_status` is neither `RATIONALIZED` nor `DISCONTINUED` has its `quality_posture` set to `SUSPENDED`; every production run of those items whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `APPROVED` transitions to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` recorded as `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`; and every one of those touched rows has its `version` incremented. Reinstatement, `SUSPENDED → CONDITIONAL`, re-stamps `qualified_at` and recomputes the `quality_posture` of every item still sitting at `SUSPENDED` from that item's complaint trend, landing each on `GREEN`, `WATCH` or `PROBATION`. That is the only path out of a `SUSPENDED` posture. Finally `SUSPENDED → DISQUALIFIED`, the terminal state, is permitted only when no production run of that supplier's items remains `SCHEDULED` or `APPROVED`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_RUNS_PRESENT` (HTTP 409). `SUSPENDED → CONDITIONAL → QUALIFIED` closes the qualification cycle, and a plant may travel it repeatedly.

One further supplier transition is driven by market data rather than by a director: `QUALIFIED → CONDITIONAL` fires automatically when a second of the supplier's items falls into `PROBATION`. That path is described with the quality trending it comes from, in §3.8.

### 3.3. Claiming a Facing and Awarding the Quote

An item may only be created against a gap whose tier decision has already been made. Creation is open to a `ProgramDirector` or a `SourcingManager` and carries the `gap_id`, an `item_name` describing the private-label article, the price `tier` it will sell at, and `pack_size_desc` exactly as the figure will be printed on the label. `trailing_90d_units` may optionally be seeded at creation from the retailer's shipment feed; when omitted it starts at zero, and it may never be negative. If the gap is anything other than `TIER_APPROVED`, creation is refused with `ERR_GAP_NOT_TIER_APPROVED` (HTTP 409); if the item's `tier` is a valid member of `OPENING_PRICE`, `NBE` or `PREMIUM` but is not the same tier the director approved on the gap, it is refused with `ERR_TIER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Because the retailer authorises a finite number of facings, creation also requires the gap to have at least one open slot; when `slots_open` has fallen to zero the attempt is refused with `ERR_SHELF_SLOT_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). And because a frozen program is not accepting new articles, creation during a `RELAUNCH_FREEZE` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RELAUNCH_FROZEN` (HTTP 409).

A successful creation opens the item at `development_status` `CONCEPT` and `quality_posture` `GREEN`, with `supplier_id` and `retail_price_cents` null, `replacement_item_id` null, `complaint_ppm_90d` at 0.00 and `spec_revision_count` at zero. It also takes the slot: the gap's `slots_consumed` and `slots_consumed_lifetime` each increase by 1, `slots_open` decreases by 1, and the program's `active_item_count` increases by 1.

Quotes arrive as cost models, submitted either by the manufacturer itself acting as a `SupplierUser` — restricted to its own `supplier_id` — or by a `SourcingManager` on the supplier's behalf. A quote names the `item_id` it prices and the `supplier_id` offering it, then breaks the unit cost into three components that may each be zero but never negative: `ingredient_cost_cents_per_unit`, `packaging_cost_cents_per_unit` and `conversion_cost_cents_per_unit`, the last covering labour and overhead. It also carries `quoted_yield_pct`, the yield the supplier is willing to be held to, strictly above 0.0 and no more than 100.0; `freight_cents_per_pallet` and `units_per_pallet` for inbound freight amortisation, the divisor being strictly greater than zero; `moq_units`, the minimum order quantity, strictly greater than zero; and `tooling_cents`, the one-time tooling charge, which may be zero. At create, the three component costs are summed into `effective_base_cents`, and **the three components are immutable thereafter** — every later movement of cost happens to `effective_base_cents` alone. A new quote opens at `SUBMITTED` with `open_change_request_count` at zero.

The **landed unit cost** turns a quote into a comparable number, and it is computed twice, in two variants that share one formula. In both, the base is the cost model's current `effective_base_cents`; that base is divided by the selected yield expressed as a fraction of one (the yield percentage divided by one hundred); the per-unit freight is the freight per pallet divided by the units per pallet; a scrap allowance in whole cents is then added; and the whole chain — carried at full precision throughout and rounded exactly once, at the end, half up to whole cents — becomes the landed figure. The **as-quoted** variant selects `quoted_yield_pct` as the yield and a scrap allowance of zero cents regardless of any audit finding; it is always computed, it is the number the supplier is held to commercially, and it is the fallback whenever the supplier has no finalized audit at all. Its result is stored in `landed_unit_cost_cents_as_quoted`. The **audited-actual** variant selects the `audited_yield_pct` measured by an audit and a scrap allowance of zero cents when that audit's `result` is `PASS` or 3 cents when the result is `CONDITIONAL`; its result is stored in `landed_unit_cost_cents_audited`, and it is this variant that ultimately drives the item's pricing. The audit chosen is the supplier's finalized plant audit with the greatest `window_end`, tie-broken by the greater `created_at`; audits still `SCHEDULED` never participate. An audit whose result came back `FAIL` produces no audited cost at all — it leaves `landed_unit_cost_cents_audited` exactly as it was. When no finalized audit exists for the supplier, `landed_unit_cost_cents_audited` is null. Identical stored inputs therefore yield two different answers: a base of 221 cents at a quoted yield of 96.50% with freight of 48,000 cents over 1,200 units per pallet lands at 269 cents, while the same model measured by a `PASS` audit at 91.00% yield lands at 283 — or 286, had that audit come back `CONDITIONAL`.

Both landed figures are refreshed whenever a plant audit of the supplier is finalized (the audited variant), and both are refreshed whenever a cost change is accepted against the model (§3.9). Awarding the business is a `decision` on the quote, taken by a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`. With `decision` set to `REJECT` the quote moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and nothing else changes. With `decision` set to `ACCEPT` the quote moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and three things happen together: the item's `supplier_id` is set to the quoting supplier, the item advances `CONCEPT → SOURCED`, and every **other** cost model for the same item still standing at `SUBMITTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` — each of those siblings having its own `version` incremented, alongside the accepted model's and the item's. Acceptance requires the item to be at `CONCEPT` and the quoting supplier to stand at `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`; a quote from a supplier that is `PROSPECT`, `SUSPENDED` or `DISQUALIFIED` cannot be awarded and is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Conditional standing is enough to win the business, though — as §3.6 shows, it is not enough to lock a specification. A decision addressed at a quote that is already `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_COST_MODEL_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Proving the Article Against the National Brand

Before packaging work begins, the agency's quality function has to demonstrate that the private-label article is close enough to the national brand to earn its tier. Two study kinds do that, distinguished by `study_type`, and a `QualityAuditor` opens both. Every study names the `item_id` under test and `nb_control_lot_code`, the lot code of the national-brand control sample that was cut against it. A `BENCH_TOP` study carries four sensory deltas measured by the agency's own panel — `appearance_delta`, `aroma_delta`, `texture_delta` and `flavor_delta` — each a decimal from -3.0 through +3.0 inclusive, conventionally recorded in half-point increments, where a positive delta means the private label read better than the national brand. A `CONSUMER_PANEL` study instead carries `n_panelists`, the panel size, at least 1; and three counts that may each be zero but never negative: `prefer_pl_count`, `prefer_nb_count` and `no_preference_count`. The panel size must equal the three counts added together. A new study opens at `DRAFT` with `parity_index`, `preference_share_pct` and `verdict` all null.

Finalizing a study moves it `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, which is terminal; finalizing one that is already `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_STUDY_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). Finalizing also requires the item to be at `SOURCED` or `BENCHMARKED` — there is nothing to benchmark before a supplier has been awarded and no point after packaging has been approved — and from any other development status the attempt is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

For a bench-top study the system computes a **parity index**. A penalty is accumulated from the four sensory deltas, each taken as an absolute magnitude and weighted: flavor at 8, texture at 6, aroma at 4 and appearance at 2. The parity index is 100 minus that penalty, floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimals. The `verdict` is then `SUPERIOR` when the parity index is at least 95.00 **and** `flavor_delta` is strictly positive; failing that, `PARITY` when the parity index is at least 85.00; otherwise `INFERIOR`. Because the penalty uses absolute magnitudes, being better than the national brand still costs index points — and because `SUPERIOR` additionally demands a strictly positive flavor delta, a high index earned by being different in the wrong direction can never read `SUPERIOR`. Deltas of flavor +0.5, texture 0.0, aroma -0.5 and appearance +0.5 accumulate a penalty of 7 and a parity index of 93.00, which reads `PARITY`. A bench-top study's `preference_share_pct` stays null, and the panelist count fields are ignored entirely.

For a consumer panel the system computes a **preference share**. The denominator is `prefer_pl_count` plus `prefer_nb_count` — `no_preference_count` sits inside `n_panelists` but is deliberately **excluded** from the denominator. When that denominator is zero the share is defined as 0.00; otherwise the share is the private-label preference count multiplied by 100 and divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimals. The `verdict` is `SUPERIOR` when the share is at least 55.00; failing that, `PARITY` when the share is at least 45.00; otherwise `INFERIOR`. A panel of 140 recording 45 preferring the private label, 55 preferring the national brand and 40 expressing no preference reads exactly 45.00 and therefore `PARITY` at the inclusive boundary — whereas folding the no-preference panelists into the denominator would read 32.14 and `INFERIOR`. A consumer panel's `parity_index` stays null, and the sensory delta fields are ignored entirely.

Finalizing a study also re-evaluates the item's **tier benchmark gate**, and passing it advances the item `SOURCED → BENCHMARKED`. What the gate demands depends on the item's `tier`. An `OPENING_PRICE` item needs one finalized bench-top study whose `parity_index` is at least 70.00. An `NBE` item needs a finalized bench-top study whose `parity_index` is at least 85.00 **and** a finalized consumer panel whose `verdict` is `PARITY` or `SUPERIOR`. A `PREMIUM` item needs a finalized bench-top study whose `parity_index` is at least 92.00 with a `flavor_delta` no lower than 0.0, **and** a finalized consumer panel whose `verdict` is `SUPERIOR`. The gate is one-way: it is evaluated only while the item still stands at `SOURCED`, so a later, weaker study can never pull a benchmarked item back. When the gate is satisfied the item's `version` increments alongside the study's.

### 3.5. Packaging, the Printed Panel and Claim Compliance

Artwork proceeds in numbered rounds opened by a `PackagingDesigner`. A round names the `item_id` whose packaging it designs and carries the analytical basis for the printed nutrition panel: `serving_size_g`, the label serving size in grams, strictly above 0.0; `servings_per_container`, strictly above 0.0; and eight per-100-gram figures, each of which may be zero but never negative — `fat_g_per_100g`, `satfat_g_per_100g`, `carb_g_per_100g`, `fiber_g_per_100g`, `sugars_g_per_100g`, `added_sugars_g_per_100g`, `protein_g_per_100g` and `sodium_mg_per_100g`. Its `round_number` is assigned by the server as one more than the number of rounds that already exist for the item, so the first round of an item is round 1. The creating designer's identifier is captured in `designer_user_id`; `reviewer_user_id` stays null until a reviewer decides the round. A new round opens at `status` `DRAFT` and `nutrition_status` `NOT_GENERATED` with all nine declared panel figures null.

Generating the panel is also the designer's job, and it is valid only while the round's review status is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; attempting it on a round that is `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_ARTWORK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Any of the ten nutrient and serving inputs may be restated in the payload, and restated values are written to the round **before** the panel is computed. Generation carries `nutrition_status` from `NOT_GENERATED → VALID`, and re-generation carries it from `INVALIDATED → VALID`; both paths recompute all nine declared figures and increment the round's `version`.

The **nutrition panel declaration** runs in a strict order, and the order is load-bearing. First, each per-100-gram figure is converted to an unrounded per-serving amount by multiplying it by the serving size in grams and dividing by 100; these per-serving amounts are carried at full precision and are **not** rounded at this stage. Second, calories are computed from those unrounded amounts: dietary fiber is counted at 2 calories per gram and is therefore subtracted from total carbohydrate before the remaining carbohydrate is counted at 4 calories per gram; protein is counted at 4 calories per gram and fat at 9 calories per gram; the four terms are added together. Third, and only for the printed figures, banded label rounding is applied. Calories are declared as 0 when the exact figure is below 5, as the nearest multiple of 5 when it is at most 50, and as the nearest multiple of 10 above that, and the result is stored in `declared_calories`. Each macronutrient gram figure is declared as 0.0 when the unrounded per-serving amount is below 0.5, as the nearest half gram when it is below 5, and as the nearest whole gram at 5 or above; this rule produces `declared_fat_g`, `declared_satfat_g`, `declared_carb_g`, `declared_fiber_g`, `declared_sugars_g`, `declared_added_sugars_g` and `declared_protein_g`, each from its own unrounded per-serving amount. Sodium is declared as 0 when the unrounded per-serving amount is below 5 milligrams, as the nearest multiple of 5 when it is at most 140, and as the nearest multiple of 10 above that, and stored in `declared_sodium_mg`. Every declared figure is rounded exactly once, from its unrounded per-serving amount, and every tie rounds half up. Consider a 50-gram serving of a product carrying, per 100 grams, 13.0 g fat, 5.0 g saturated fat, 65.2 g carbohydrate, 2.8 g fiber, 30.0 g sugars, 24.0 g added sugars, 6.8 g protein and 640 mg sodium: the exact calorie figure is 199.7 and `declared_calories` is 200, whereas summing the already-rounded macros would give 206.0 and declare 210. Calories must come from the unrounded amounts.

Claims are drafted onto a round by a `PackagingDesigner` or a `RegulatoryReviewer`. A claim names the `artwork_round_id` it will print on, the `claim_text` exactly as it appears on the package, and a `claim_type` from `NUTRIENT_CONTENT`, `HEALTH`, `ORGANIC`, `FREE_FROM` and `STRUCTURE_FUNCTION`. A `HEALTH` or `ORGANIC` claim must be accompanied by a non-empty `substantiation_ref` naming the lab report or certificate that supports it; omitting it is refused with `ERR_SUBSTANTIATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The other three claim types may carry a substantiation reference but need not. From the claim type the server derives `depends_on_nutrition`, which is true exactly for `NUTRIENT_CONTENT` and `HEALTH` claims and false for the other three — this flag is what decides, later, whether an invalidated nutrition panel takes the claim down with it. The drafting user's identifier is captured in `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id` stays null, and the claim opens at `PENDING`. Claims may only be added to a round whose review status is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; adding one to a round that is `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_ARTWORK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Claim review belongs to a `RegulatoryReviewer` and must never be performed by the user who drafted the claim — segregation of duties is absolute here, and a self-review is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_SELF_REVIEW` (HTTP 403). A review carries a `decision` and optionally a `rationale`, which is stored as the reviewer's recorded reasoning. With `decision` set to `APPROVE` the claim moves `PENDING → APPROVED`, which additionally requires the round's review status to be `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. With `decision` set to `REJECT` the claim moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, terminal, with no round-status requirement. Either way `reviewed_by_user_id` is set to the acting reviewer and the claim's `version` increments. Reviewing a claim that has already reached `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409).

Submitting a round for regulatory review is the designer's act and drives `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` or, after a revision request, `CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW`. It requires the round's `nutrition_status` to be `VALID`; a round whose panel is `NOT_GENERATED` or `INVALIDATED` is refused with `ERR_NUTRITION_PANEL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). It also requires at least one label claim to exist on the round; a round with no claims at all is refused with `ERR_UNAPPROVED_CLAIM_PRESENT` (HTTP 422). Submitting from any status other than `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_ARTWORK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Round review belongs to a `RegulatoryReviewer` and requires the round to be `IN_REVIEW`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_ARTWORK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). With `decision` set to `REQUEST_CHANGES` the round returns `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `reviewer_user_id` is stamped with the acting reviewer, and the designer may regenerate the panel, add claims and resubmit; `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` is the revision loop and it may repeat without limit. With `decision` set to `APPROVE` the round moves `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, and three further conditions apply: the round's `nutrition_status` must be `VALID`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_NUTRITION_PANEL_INVALID` (HTTP 422); no claim on the round may still be `PENDING` or sit at `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED`, and at least one claim must exist, or the approval is refused with `ERR_UNAPPROVED_CLAIM_PRESENT` (HTTP 422); and the item must stand at `BENCHMARKED`. On approval, `reviewer_user_id` is stamped, every **other** artwork round of the same item still standing at `APPROVED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` — each with its own `version` incremented — and the item advances `BENCHMARKED → PACKAGING_APPROVED`, incrementing the item's `version` too.

### 3.6. The Specification Revision and Its Lock

A specification is the document that freezes an item's commercial and regulatory basis together, and it does so by **pinning** two other rows. Drafting one is open to a `ProgramDirector` or a `SourcingManager` and carries the `item_id` it governs, the `pinned_cost_model_id` whose economics it adopts, the `pinned_artwork_round_id` whose packaging and claims it adopts, and `expected_item_version` — the optimistic-concurrency guard described in §2.7, which must carry the item `version` the client last observed and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) when it does not match.

The pinned rows must genuinely belong to the item and must be in the right standing: the pinned cost model must be `ACCEPTED` and its `item_id` must equal the specification's `item_id`, and the pinned artwork round must be `APPROVED` and likewise belong to the same item. A mismatch on either count is refused with `ERR_PIN_ITEM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The pinned round's `nutrition_status` must also be `VALID` at draft time, or the create is refused with `ERR_NUTRITION_PANEL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Only one draft may be open at a time: a second specification created for an item that already has one at `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_SPECIFICATION_EXISTS` (HTTP 409).

Creation records the exact versions it observed. `pinned_cost_model_version` receives the cost model's current `version` and `pinned_artwork_version` receives the artwork round's current `version`, both as read at that instant. The item's `spec_revision_count` increases by 1 and the new specification's `revision_no` is set to the resulting count, so revision numbers are per-item ordinals; the item's `version` increments as a consequence, which is why two clients cannot both draft a revision from the same observed item state. The specification itself opens at `DRAFT` with `slotting_fee_cents`, `locked_at` and `locked_by_user_id` all null.

Locking the specification belongs to a `ProgramDirector` alone and is the most heavily gated operation in the system. The specification must be `DRAFT` — locking one that is `LOCKED`, `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). The governing program must be `ACTIVE`; during a `RELAUNCH_FREEZE` the lock is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RELAUNCH_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The item's `development_status` must be `PACKAGING_APPROVED` or `RELAUNCH_PENDING`. The item's awarded supplier must stand at `QUALIFIED` — and here, unlike quote acceptance, `CONDITIONAL` is not sufficient: a lock against a supplier that is `PROSPECT`, `CONDITIONAL`, `SUSPENDED` or `DISQUALIFIED` is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 422). Both pins must still be current: if `pinned_cost_model_version` no longer equals the cost model's `version`, or `pinned_artwork_version` no longer equals the artwork round's `version`, the lock is refused with `ERR_PIN_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 409) — which is exactly what happens when a plant audit was finalized between drafting and locking. The pinned round must still be `APPROVED` with `nutrition_status` `VALID`, the latter failure surfacing as `ERR_NUTRITION_PANEL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). And the money must be there: the program's `committed_budget_cents` plus the slotting fee this lock would compute must not exceed `dev_budget_cents`, or the lock is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

**Lock pricing** computes two figures from one input. That input, the effective landed cost, is the pinned cost model's `landed_unit_cost_cents_audited` whenever that field is not null, and its `landed_unit_cost_cents_as_quoted` otherwise — the audited number always wins where it exists, which is why the same specification locks at a different retail price depending on whether an audit has refreshed the model. From it, `slotting_fee_cents` is a tier base plus half a percent of the first order's value: the tier base is 150,000 cents for an `OPENING_PRICE` item, 250,000 cents for `NBE` and 400,000 cents for `PREMIUM`, and the variable part is the effective landed cost multiplied by the cost model's `moq_units`, multiplied by 0.50 and divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Separately, the retail price is derived from the tier's gross margin: the margin is 22.00 percent for `OPENING_PRICE`, 30.00 percent for `NBE` and 38.00 percent for `PREMIUM`; the effective landed cost is divided by one minus that margin expressed as a fraction of one, and that quotient is rounded half up to whole cents; the result is then walked **upward** to the smallest whole number of cents at or above it whose last digit is 9, and that is `retail_price_cents` on the item. An `NBE` item with an effective landed cost of 283 cents and a minimum order of 12,000 units therefore locks at a slotting fee of 250,000 plus 16,980, or 266,980 cents, and a retail price of 409 cents — the rounded quotient being 404 and the next price ending in 9 being 409.

Locking writes broadly. The computed fee lands in `slotting_fee_cents` and the computed price lands in the item's `retail_price_cents`. The specification moves `DRAFT → LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant, and `locked_by_user_id` records the acting director — a field that later forbids that same user from approving a production run against this specification. The program's `committed_budget_cents` and `lifetime_committed_cents` both increase by the slotting fee. Any other specification of the same item standing at `LOCKED` or `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, with its `version` incremented — and its own slotting fee stays committed, permanently. Finally the item's development dimension moves only conditionally: an item at `PACKAGING_APPROVED` advances `PACKAGING_APPROVED → SPEC_LOCKED`, while an item at `RELAUNCH_PENDING` keeps its development status unchanged, because a relaunch is resolved by the launch step rather than by the lock.

### 3.7. Booking the Line, Approving the Run and Reaching the Shelf

Production runs are scheduled by a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector` and are the point at which the plant's physical capacity becomes a constraint. A run names the `item_id` it produces, the `specification_id` it is produced against, the `plant_line_id` it occupies, a `run_type` of `FIRST_PRODUCTION` or `ONGOING`, a window bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`, and `planned_units`, strictly greater than zero.

Scheduling is gated on a chain of agreements. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The named specification must belong to the same item as the run, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPECIFICATION_ITEM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409), and it must stand at `LOCKED` — a `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` or `SUPERSEDED` specification is refused with `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). The named line must belong to the item's awarded supplier; a line at some other plant is refused with `ERR_PLANT_LINE_SUPPLIER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The item's `quality_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ITEM_QUALITY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422).

Two physical constraints follow. **Line exclusivity**: the requested window, expanded by the line's `changeover_buffer_minutes` at both ends, must not intersect the equally expanded window of any other run on the same line whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED`, `HOLD` or `COMPLETED`. Run windows are closed intervals, so touching endpoints count as an intersection, and a held run still occupies its line. A conflict is refused with `ERR_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409); a cancelled run's window, by contrast, is free and never conflicts. **Line capacity**: `planned_units` must not exceed the line's pro-rated capacity for the requested window, which is the line's `daily_capacity_units` multiplied by the window's duration in minutes, divided by 1,440 — the number of minutes in a twenty-four-hour day — and floored to a whole number of units. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_LINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A `FIRST_PRODUCTION` run must additionally plan at least as many units as the pinned cost model's `moq_units`; planning fewer is refused with `ERR_BELOW_MOQ` (HTTP 422). This minimum applies only to first-production runs, not to `ONGOING` replenishment.

Scheduling also spends money. `committed_tooling_cents` is snapshotted from the pinned cost model's `tooling_cents` as it stands at that instant, and the program's `committed_budget_cents` and `lifetime_committed_cents` both increase by that amount — which means the same budget headroom test applies here as at lock time: the program's `committed_budget_cents` plus the pinned model's tooling must not exceed `dev_budget_cents`, or scheduling is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A newly scheduled run opens at `SCHEDULED` with `produced_units`, `completed_on_time`, `completed_at`, `approved_by_user_id` and `hold_reason` all null.

Approval belongs to a `ProgramDirector`, moves the run `SCHEDULED → APPROVED` and stamps `approved_by_user_id`. It requires the run to be `SCHEDULED` — from any other status it is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — the governing specification to still be `LOCKED`, so that a specification which slipped to `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` blocks approval with `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), and the item's `quality_posture` not to be `SUSPENDED`, refused with `ERR_ITEM_QUALITY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422) otherwise. The specification-to-item and line-to-supplier agreements are re-checked at approval as well as at scheduling. Segregation of duties applies: the approving director may not be the user recorded in the specification's `locked_by_user_id`, and an attempt to be both is refused with `ERR_RUN_SELF_APPROVAL` (HTTP 403).

Completion is the `QualityAuditor`'s act, moves the run `APPROVED → COMPLETED` and supplies two facts: `produced_units`, the units actually made, which may be zero but never negative, and `completed_on_time`, a boolean recording whether the run finished inside its window — the input that feeds the delivery component of the supplier scorecard. Completion is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) from any status other than `APPROVED`, and refused with `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 409) while the logical clock is still strictly before the run's `window_end`; a clock sitting exactly at `window_end` is late enough. On completion `completed_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant and the supplier's `composite_score_cached` is refreshed under the rolling-twelve-month window. Committed tooling stays committed on completion — completion is not a release.

Two supplier-driven transitions push runs sideways. When the item's awarded supplier moves to `CONDITIONAL` or to `SUSPENDED`, every run of that supplier's items sitting at `SCHEDULED` or at `APPROVED` transitions to `HOLD`, with `hold_reason` set to `SUPPLIER_CONDITIONAL` or `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` respectively; a run held from `APPROVED` retains its `approved_by_user_id`, and in neither case is the committed tooling released — a held run continues to occupy both its line window and its budget commitment. Releasing a hold is open to a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`, moves the run `HOLD → SCHEDULED`, and retains `hold_reason` for the audit trail rather than clearing it. Release requires the run to be at `HOLD`, refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) otherwise; requires the supplier to stand at `QUALIFIED`, where `CONDITIONAL` is not enough and the refusal is `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 422); and requires the item's `quality_posture` not to be `SUSPENDED`, refused with `ERR_ITEM_QUALITY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). `SCHEDULED → HOLD → SCHEDULED` is a cycle driven entirely by supplier posture.

Cancellation is available to a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector` from any of `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED` or `HOLD`, and it is the system's one compensating action on the money ledger: the run moves to `CANCELLED`, the program's `committed_budget_cents` decreases by the run's `committed_tooling_cents` while `lifetime_released_cents` increases by the same amount, and the run's line window is freed for future overlap checks. Cancelling a run that is already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_RUN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Launching the item is the `ProgramDirector`'s final development act and has two distinct forms. From `SPEC_LOCKED`, launching moves the item `SPEC_LOCKED → LAUNCHED` and requires a completed first-production run: at least one run of the item with `run_type` `FIRST_PRODUCTION` and status `COMPLETED` must exist, or the launch is refused with `ERR_FIRST_PRODUCTION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). It also requires the item's `quality_posture` not to be `SUSPENDED`, refused with `ERR_ITEM_QUALITY_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422) otherwise. From `RELAUNCH_PENDING`, launching moves the item `RELAUNCH_PENDING → LAUNCHED` and instead requires that the item's regulatory basis has been rebuilt: a specification of the item must stand at `LOCKED` with its `revision_no` equal to the item's current `spec_revision_count` — that is, the newest revision must be the locked one — and that specification's pinned artwork round must have `nutrition_status` `VALID` with no claim on it standing at `PENDING` or `RESCINDED`. A relaunch attempted before that basis exists is refused with `ERR_RELAUNCH_PRECONDITION_UNMET` (HTTP 409). Launching from any development status other than `SPEC_LOCKED` or `RELAUNCH_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `LAUNCHED → RELAUNCH_PENDING → LAUNCHED` is a cycle, and it may be travelled once per accepted ingredient substitution.

### 3.8. Life on the Shelf: Complaints, Trending and the Supplier Scorecard

Once an item ships, the quality desk logs consumer complaints against it. Intake is open to a `QualityAuditor` or a `RegulatoryReviewer` and records the `item_id` complained about, a `defect_category` from `FOREIGN_MATERIAL`, `SPOILAGE`, `PACKAGING`, `LABEL` and `SENSORY`, a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`, the `lot_code` the consumer cited, and the `narrative` as logged. The server stamps `received_at` with the logical clock's current instant, which is also the row's `created_at`. Complaints are append-only, as §2.4 sets out: there is no update, no deletion, no `version` and no status to filter on, so every trending computation sees every complaint ever logged inside its window.

The denominator of the trend comes from the retailer rather than from the consumer. Posting the shipment feed is open to a `SourcingManager` or a `QualityAuditor` and supplies a single figure, `trailing_90d_units`, the units shipped over the trailing ninety days, which may be zero but never negative. It overwrites the stored value and then triggers the same recomputation a new complaint triggers.

The **complaint trend** works as follows. Weighted points are accumulated over the item's complaints whose `received_at` falls inside the trailing ninety days of the logical clock — the half-open span from ninety days before now up to and including now — counting five points for each `CRITICAL` complaint, two for each `MAJOR` and one for each `MINOR`. If the item's `trailing_90d_units` is zero, `complaint_ppm_90d` is defined to be 0.00 rather than an error or an unbounded figure, so complaints logged against an item with no shipment history never move its posture. Otherwise `complaint_ppm_90d` is the weighted point total multiplied by 1,000,000 and divided by `trailing_90d_units`, rounded half up to two decimals. From that figure the recomputed posture is `PROBATION` when the parts-per-million figure is at least 150.00, otherwise `WATCH` when it is at least 60.00, otherwise `GREEN`. Fifty thousand trailing units carrying one `CRITICAL`, one `MAJOR` and one `MINOR` complaint accumulate eight points, read 160.00 and land on `PROBATION`; drop the critical complaint and the same units read exactly 60.00 and land on `WATCH` at the inclusive boundary.

The posture dimension moves in both directions, and only from this recomputation. `GREEN → WATCH` and `WATCH → PROBATION` fire when the recomputed posture is worse than the stored one; `PROBATION → WATCH` and `PROBATION → GREEN` fire when a growing shipment denominator dilutes the complaint rate. Each such move increments the item's `version`. There is one hard exception: the recomputation never sets and never clears a `SUSPENDED` posture. An item whose posture is `SUSPENDED` has its numeric `complaint_ppm_90d` refreshed but keeps its posture, and a `SUSPENDED` posture is cleared only by the supplier reinstatement described in §3.2, which recomputes the posture from the trend at that moment.

Logging a complaint also refreshes the supplier's `weighted_complaint_points`, `probation_item_count` and `composite_score_cached`. The `probation_item_count` is the number of that supplier's items currently standing at `PROBATION`, and it carries a cascade: when an item's move into `PROBATION` brings that count to 2 or more while the supplier stands at `QUALIFIED`, the supplier transitions `QUALIFIED → CONDITIONAL`. That supplier transition — not the complaint itself — is then what pushes every one of that supplier's `SCHEDULED` and `APPROVED` production runs to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` `SUPPLIER_CONDITIONAL`, as §3.7 describes. The chain is deliberately three links long: a complaint moves an item's posture, the posture moves the supplier's standing, and the supplier's standing holds the runs.

The **supplier composite score** aggregates three components inside a time window, and everything about it except the window is variant-independent. The audit component is 80.00 whenever no finalized plant audit of that supplier has a `window_end` inside the window; otherwise it is the weighted sum of the pillar scores of the single most relevant audit — 0.40 times `food_safety_score`, plus 0.25 times `sanitation_score`, plus 0.20 times `traceability_score`, plus 0.15 times `capacity_score` — where the audit chosen is the finalized audit with the greatest `window_end` inside the window, tie-broken by the greater `created_at`. The complaint component starts at 100 and subtracts 2.5 points for every weighted complaint point accumulated over complaints against that supplier's items whose `received_at` falls inside the window, using the same five-two-one severity weights, and is floored at 0.00. The delivery component is 100.00 whenever no run of that supplier's items reached `COMPLETED` with a `completed_at` inside the window; otherwise it is the count of those completed runs whose `completed_on_time` is true, multiplied by 100, divided by the total count of those completed runs, and rounded half up to two decimals. The composite is 0.50 times the audit component plus 0.30 times the complaint component plus 0.20 times the delivery component, rounded half up to two decimals; every component is itself expressed to two decimals. Audits still `SCHEDULED` never participate, and runs sitting at `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED`, `HOLD` or `CANCELLED` never participate.

Two windows are in play, and they give different answers from the same stored rows. The **rolling-twelve-month** window spans the 365 days ending at the logical clock's current instant, and it is the variant written to `composite_score_cached` — refreshed on plant-audit finalize, on a `QUALIFY` decision including the recertification self-loop, and on run completion. It therefore keeps counting history that predates the current qualification period. The **period-to-date** window instead spans from the supplier's `qualified_at` to now, and because every `QUALIFY` decision re-stamps `qualified_at`, this variant is reset by recertification while the rolling window is not. A supplier whose only in-window audit scored 95, 90, 92 and 88 across food safety, sanitation, traceability and capacity, carrying 12 weighted complaint points and no completed runs, reads a rolling-twelve-month composite of 87.05, built from an audit component of 92.10, a complaint component of 70.00 and a delivery component of 100.00. Re-qualify that same supplier after those events and the period-to-date window contains no audit and no complaints, so the neutral constants apply and the period-to-date composite reads 90.00 — the same rows, two windows, two answers.

The scorecard read returns both variants side by side with each component broken out: `supplier_id`, then `rolling_12m_composite_score`, `rolling_12m_audit_component`, `rolling_12m_complaint_component`, `rolling_12m_delivery_component` and `rolling_12m_window_start`, then `period_to_date_composite_score`, `period_to_date_audit_component`, `period_to_date_complaint_component`, `period_to_date_delivery_component` and `period_to_date_window_start`. Every role may read a scorecard, except that a `SupplierUser` may read only its own, as §2.1 requires.

### 3.9. Renegotiating Cost, Substituting Material and Rationalizing the Range

Costs move after launch, and a supplier that wants more money opens a cost change request against the `ACCEPTED` cost model that currently governs the item. Only a `SupplierUser` may open one, and only against its own supplier's model. The request names the `item_id` and the `cost_model_id` it re-prices, restates all three cost components as `requested_ingredient_cost_cents_per_unit`, `requested_packaging_cost_cents_per_unit` and `requested_conversion_cost_cents_per_unit` — each of which may be zero but never negative — declares a `substitution_scope` of `NONE`, `PACKAGING_MATERIAL` or `INGREDIENT`, and carries `expected_cost_model_version`, the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.7 which is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COST_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412) when it does not match the model's current `version`. When the scope is anything other than `NONE`, a non-empty `substitution_material_desc` must describe the substituted material; omitting it is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_DESC_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The named cost model must be `ACCEPTED` and its `item_id` must match the request's `item_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COST_MODEL_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409). Only one negotiation may be live per cost model: when the model's `open_change_request_count` is not zero, a further request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_REQUEST_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). A successful create opens the request at `SUBMITTED` with `round_number` at 1, `counter_base_cents` and `decided_by_user_id` null, increases the cost model's `open_change_request_count` by 1 and increments that cost model's `version`.

The **settlement** of a cost change runs in exactly ten steps, in exactly this order; reordering any pair changes the answer. First, the requested total per unit is the three requested component costs added together. Second, the old base is the cost model's **current** `effective_base_cents` — not the three component fields captured when the model was created. Third, `raw_increase_cents` is the requested total minus that old base. Fourth, `increase_pct` is the raw increase multiplied by 100, divided by the old base, and rounded half up to two decimals. Fifth, `escalation_tier` is `T1` when that percentage is at most 3.00, `T2` when it is at most 8.00, and `T3` above that. Sixth, a tier cap in cents is derived from the old base and the tier's cap percentage — 3.00 percent for `T1`, 6.50 percent for `T2`, 9.00 percent for `T3` — as the old base multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, and **floored** to a whole cent. Seventh, `capped_increase_cents` is the smaller of the raw increase and that cap. Eighth, `offset_fee_cents` is the capped increase multiplied by 12.50, divided by 100, and rounded half up to a whole cent — an agency offset charged on the capped increase, not on the raw one. Ninth, `net_increase_cents` is the capped increase minus that offset fee. Tenth, and only when the request is accepted, the new base is the old base plus the net increase. Three order traps live in those steps: the tier is chosen from the **raw** percentage while the cap is applied to the raw cents afterwards, so tiering off a capped amount would wrongly demote `T3` requests to `T2`; the cap is floored while the fee is rounded half up, so using one mode for both shifts the result by a cent; and the fee is charged after the cap, not before. Against an old base of 221 cents with requested components of 168, 33 and 50, the raw increase is 30, the percentage is 13.57, the tier is `T3`, the cap floors to 19, the capped increase is 19, the fee rounds to 2, the net increase is 17 and the accepted base becomes 238 — where charging the fee before the cap would have given a net of 19 and a base of 240. All ten steps are recomputed from scratch on every revision, and `counter_base_cents` never enters the arithmetic: it is a negotiating position only. Sibling requests in `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` do not participate either.

Negotiation is a genuine loop. A `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector` may counter a `SUBMITTED` request, supplying `counter_base_cents` — the total base cost per unit the agency is willing to pay, which may be zero but never negative — and moving the request `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED`. The supplier may then revise, which moves the request `COUNTERED → SUBMITTED`, replaces the three requested component costs with fresh payload values, optionally restates `substitution_scope` and `substitution_material_desc`, increases `round_number` by 1 and recomputes every settlement output from the revised amounts. Only the owning `SupplierUser` may revise. `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → SUBMITTED` is the negotiation cycle, and it is bounded: a revision is accepted only while `round_number` is still below 3, so the fourth submission is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). The owning supplier may also withdraw its request from either `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED`, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, which decreases the cost model's `open_change_request_count` by 1 and increments that model's `version`. Countering from anything but `SUBMITTED`, revising from anything but `COUNTERED`, deciding from anything but `SUBMITTED`, or withdrawing from a terminal status, are all refused with `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Deciding a request is open to a `SourcingManager` or a `ProgramDirector`, subject to the escalation authority of §2.1: a `T1` request may be decided by either, while `T2` and `T3` requests are the `ProgramDirector`'s alone and a `SourcingManager` attempting them is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_ROLE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403). With `decision` set to `REJECT` the request moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, the cost model's `open_change_request_count` decreases by 1 and the cost model's `version` increments — nothing else moves.

With `decision` set to `ACCEPT` the request moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, `open_change_request_count` decreases by 1, and the system's longest cascade fires — five hops, each conditional on the one before it. **Hop one**: the cost model's `effective_base_cents` increases by `net_increase_cents`, both `landed_unit_cost_cents_as_quoted` and `landed_unit_cost_cents_audited` are recomputed from the new base, and the cost model's `version` increments. **Hop two**: because the pinned cost model changed, the item's specification standing at `LOCKED` whose `pinned_cost_model_id` is this cost model transitions `LOCKED → SUPERSEDE_PENDING`, with its `version` incremented — and note that this is the *only* thing that supersedes a locked specification; an audit-driven cost recompute bumps the cost model's `version` without touching any specification's status. **Hop three**: because that specification entered `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` **and** the accepted request's `substitution_scope` is `INGREDIENT`, the artwork round named by that specification's `pinned_artwork_round_id` moves `nutrition_status` `VALID → INVALIDATED`, with its `version` incremented. A `PACKAGING_MATERIAL` or `NONE` substitution stops the cascade here, leaving the panel `VALID`. **Hop four**: because the round's panel became `INVALIDATED`, every label claim on that round standing at `APPROVED` whose `depends_on_nutrition` is true transitions `APPROVED → RESCINDED`, each with its `version` incremented; claims that do not depend on nutrition, and claims not in `APPROVED`, are left alone. **Hop five**: because at least one claim was rescinded, if the item whose specification pinned that round is `LAUNCHED`, the item transitions `LAUNCHED → RELAUNCH_PENDING`, with its `version` incremented. From there the item is off the market in specification terms until a new artwork round has been drafted, its panel regenerated, its claims re-reviewed and re-approved, a new specification revision drafted, pinned and locked, and the item relaunched as §3.7 describes. When a still newer specification for the item is later locked, the specification sitting at `SUPERSEDE_PENDING` — like one sitting at `LOCKED` — transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal, with its `version` incremented.

Rationalization closes the loop on an item's life, and only a `ProgramDirector` may perform it. It carries a `disposition`. With `disposition` set to `REPLACE` a `replacement_item_id` must accompany it, naming a different item that shares this item's `gap_id` and whose own `development_status` is neither `RATIONALIZED` nor `DISCONTINUED`; the field is stored on the item and the item moves to the terminal `RATIONALIZED`. With `disposition` set to `DISCONTINUE` no replacement may be supplied and the item moves to the terminal `DISCONTINUED`. A replacement that is missing, self-referential, drawn from another gap or itself already retired — or a replacement supplied alongside a `DISCONTINUE` disposition — is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_ITEM_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Rationalization is available from `LAUNCHED` and from `RELAUNCH_PENDING`; from any other development status it is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Notably, rationalization remains available while the program is in `RELAUNCH_FREEZE` — freezing a program stops new items and new locks, not the pruning that a relaunch exists to accomplish.

Both dispositions do the same accounting. The gap's `slots_consumed` decreases by 1 and its `slots_released_lifetime` increases by 1, so `slots_open` rises and the facing becomes available to a successor item; the program's `active_item_count` decreases by 1; and every production run of the item standing at `SCHEDULED`, `APPROVED` or `HOLD` transitions to `CANCELLED`, each one releasing its `committed_tooling_cents` back to the program — decreasing `committed_budget_cents` and increasing `lifetime_released_cents` — and each one having its `version` incremented. Runs already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` are untouched. The slotting fee of the item's locked specification, however, stays committed permanently: the shelf slot comes back, the slotting fee does not.
