# Oil & Gas Land Administration and Joint Interest Billing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An exploration and production land department carries two obligations that pull in opposite directions. The first is defensive: every oil and gas lease in the inventory is a wasting asset that must be actively *held* — by production, by shut-in royalty payments, or by an unbroken chain of continuous-development operations — and a single missed deadline silently forfeits acreage that a pooled unit and everything billed against it depend on. The second is fiscal: the operator spends the joint account's money on behalf of partners who each own a fraction of the well, and every cent of that spend must be authorized, allocated, billed, audited, disputed and ultimately reconciled against the revenue distributed back to those same partners. Today both jobs are done in spreadsheets that cannot answer the only question that matters at an audit: *why does this partner owe exactly this amount?*

This document specifies the API for a land administration and joint interest billing (JIB) service that makes that question answerable. The service maintains lease tenure and obligation compliance as two independent, simultaneously-tracked dimensions; forms pooled units out of committed lease acreage with allocation factors that provably sum to unity; processes authorization-for-expenditure (AFE) elections including deemed non-consent and the penalty recovery that follows; prepares joint interest billing statements under two selectable cost bases and two overhead pricing modes; runs a partner audit and dispute pipeline with contractual response and rebuttal clocks; distributes revenue through a four-tier waterfall with statutory payment deadlines and late interest; and records assignments of working interest subject to preferential purchase rights and plugging bond coverage.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. First, **the service is a ledger, not a report generator** — derived balances such as a well's billed-to-date total or an owner's suspense balance are maintained as running quantities that every reversal must unwind exactly, and money is never created or destroyed by a rounding step. Second, **every deadline is evaluated against a server-held logical clock**, never against wall-clock time, so that a lease expiry, an election window, an audit window and a statutory payment deadline can all be reproduced deterministically by a test harness or by an auditor reconstructing a historical position.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being addressed (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are:

- **`LandAdministrator`** — the land department itself. It maintains the lease inventory, creates wells and drives their operational status, commits lease acreage into pooled units and declares those units effective, creates and terminates working-interest positions, issues AFEs and closes election windows, registers interest owners, and serves and closes assignment notices.
- **`JointInterestAccountant`** — the joint-account bookkeeper. It books and settles cost entries, opens, issues, revises, voids and settles JIB statements, answers partner disputes, books monthly production revenue and runs the release waterfall, and pays owner disbursements.
- **`AuditReviewer`** — the independent adjudicator. It is the only role that may resolve a dispute, and it may also file an exception on a partner's behalf and record a partner's rebuttal or escalation.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — regulatory and bonding compliance. It maintains each owner's tax and bond posture, lifts suspense holds on disbursements, and runs the periodic obligation evaluation pass.
- **`PartnerRepresentative`** — a working-interest partner acting for itself: electing on AFEs, disputing bills, and exercising or waiving preferential purchase rights.

Four of these five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `LandAdministrator`, `JointInterestAccountant`, `AuditReviewer` or `ComplianceOfficer`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an operator-side user identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — it is stored as `prepared_by_user_id` on the JIB statement a `JointInterestAccountant` opens, and as `resolved_by_user_id` on a dispute an `AuditReviewer` resolves — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

`PartnerRepresentative` is different: its identity is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `InterestOwner`, and ownership checks on any resource that references an interest owner compare that resource's owner foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `PartnerRepresentative` may only submit an AFE election whose `owner_id` is itself, may only file a dispute whose `owner_id` is itself, may only record an exchange on a dispute whose `owner_id` is itself, and when listing owner disbursements sees only rows whose `owner_id` is itself. A violation of any of these is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). A `PartnerRepresentative` exercising or waiving a preferential purchase right must additionally be an eligible holder on the addressed transfer; if it is not, the request is rejected with `ERR_PPR_HOLDER_INVALID` (HTTP 403).

Because the opaque roles are not entity-backed, segregation of duties between them is enforced by comparing recorded user identifiers rather than entity ownership. The `AuditReviewer` who resolves a dispute must not be the same user who prepared the statement under dispute: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the disputed statement's `prepared_by_user_id`, the resolution is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. If `limit` is not an integer within the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or `offset` is not an integer of zero or greater, the request is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — with one deliberate exception: the lease inventory listing is presented newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending, because land staff work the most recently recorded instruments. When a list endpoint responds, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any record remains beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource the service persists carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, recording when the row was written. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every subsequent mutation of that row. Every other *instant* this service records or reports is rendered in that same format — a statement's `issued_at`, an election's `submitted_at`, an AFE's `elections_closed_at`, a dispute's `filed_at` and `operator_responded_at`, a revenue record's `released_at`, a disbursement's `paid_at` and the evaluation pass's reported `evaluated_at` are all ISO 8601 UTC instants with exactly three fractional digits. Fields that are calendar *dates* rather than instants are rendered as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD`.

There is one exception, and it is deliberate. JIB line items are immutable by construction — they are synthesized in a single pass when a statement is issued and are never edited afterwards, only retained or excluded from balances. A JIB line item therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Every other entity in this specification — leases, wells, lease allocations, interest owners, well interests, AFEs, AFE elections, cost entries, JIB statements, billing disputes, production revenue records, owner disbursements and assignment transfers — carries the full set of four common properties.

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a server-held **logical clock**. The clock's value at process start is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to the host's wall-clock time. An administrative clock-setting capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it. The clock is monotonic: if the supplied `current_utc` is earlier than the clock's present value, the request is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MOVED_BACKWARD` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock is purely a time operation — it mutates no entity, and it does not itself evaluate any deadline, expiry or obligation. Where a rule below compares "the logical clock date" against a calendar date, the comparison is made on whole calendar dates; where it compares timestamps, the full instant is used.

### 2.5. Request Evaluation Order

Because a single request can fail several ways at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Every request is evaluated in the following sequence, and the first failing check determines the response:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Required fields.** On every creating or mutating request, each field the endpoint declares as required is present and non-null, else `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
4. **Field shapes.** Enum-typed fields carry a declared value; integer cent fields are zero or greater; percentage fields lie within the inclusive range 0.000000 through 1.000000 and carry at most six decimal places; date fields are valid ISO 8601 calendar dates; `production_month` matches a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month in the range 01 through 12; `non_consent_penalty_multiple` lies within the inclusive range 1.0 through 5.0 and carries at most one decimal place. Any breach is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
5. **Pagination shape.** On list endpoints, `limit` and `offset` are within range, else `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (400).
6. **Path resolution, left to right.** Each identifier embedded in the URL path is resolved in the order it appears, outermost segment first, before any body content is inspected. `lease_id` must resolve to a lease, else `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). `well_id` must resolve to a well, else `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND` (404). `owner_id` must resolve to an interest owner, else `ERR_INTEREST_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` (404). `interest_id` must resolve to a working-interest position, else `ERR_WELL_INTEREST_NOT_FOUND` (404). `afe_id` must resolve to an AFE, else `ERR_AFE_NOT_FOUND` (404). `statement_id` must resolve to a JIB statement, else `ERR_JIB_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). `dispute_id` must resolve to a billing dispute, else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` (404). `revenue_id` must resolve to a production revenue record, else `ERR_PRODUCTION_REVENUE_NOT_FOUND` (404). `disbursement_id` must resolve to an owner disbursement, else `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). `transfer_id` must resolve to an assignment transfer, else `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Three detail reads are addressed by an identifier that must name an existing row of its own kind: `allocation_id` names a lease-to-well acreage allocation, `cost_entry_id` names a joint-account cost entry, and `election_id` names an AFE election. Two nested list reads are addressed by a plain `id` path variable — the owning interest owner in one case and the owning statement in the other.
7. **Body reference resolution, left to right in the order the endpoint lists them.** A `lease_id` supplied in an acreage-commitment body must resolve to a lease, else `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). An `owner_id`, `assignor_owner_id` or `assignee_owner_id` supplied in a body must each resolve to an interest owner, checked in exactly that order, else `ERR_INTEREST_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies when recording a working-interest position, when submitting an AFE election, when filing a dispute and when serving an assignment notice. An `afe_id` supplied on a cost entry must resolve to an AFE, else `ERR_AFE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
8. **Partner identity.** The `PartnerRepresentative` ownership match described in §2.1, else `ERR_OWNER_MISMATCH` (403).
9. **Segregation of duties.** The dispute-resolution reviewer differs from the statement preparer, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** The version expectation described in §2.7, else the relevant stale-version code (412).
11. **Terminal-state guard.** The addressed entity is not already in a terminal state, else `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` (409). A lease whose `tenure_status` is `EXPIRED`, a well whose `well_status` is `PLUGGED`, a statement whose `statement_status` is `ADJUSTED`, `SETTLED` or `VOID`, a dispute whose `dispute_status` is `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `RESOLVED_ADJUSTED` or `EXPIRED`, a transfer whose `transfer_status` is `CLOSED`, a disbursement whose `disbursement_status` is `PAID`, and a working-interest position whose `interest_status` is `TERMINATED` are all terminal and accept no further mutation.
12. **Own-entity state preconditions** (409), described alongside each workflow in §3.
13. **Cross-entity state preconditions** (409), likewise described in §3.
14. **Temporal windows and deadlines** (422).
15. **Referential eligibility of the parties named in the body** (422, except the preferential-right holder check which is 403).
16. **Uniqueness, interval overlap and aggregate constraints** (409 or 422).
17. **Fiscal capacity — bond coverage, bonus payment and suspense balance — checked last** (402, except the suspense-balance guard which is 409).

The practical consequence of this ladder is that a request which is both unauthorized and malformed reports the authorization failure, a request against a non-existent well reports the missing well rather than a body validation problem, and a bond shortfall — the most expensive check to evaluate — is only ever reported once every structural, state and temporal condition has already been satisfied.

### 2.6. Money, Acreage and Interest Precision

All monetary amounts in this service are integers denominated in **cents**; there are no fractional currency values anywhere in the API. Wherever a monetary amount is derived from a multiplication or a division, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value of exactly one half cent always rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Interest percentages — working interest, net revenue interest, royalty rate, allocation factor, minimum consent threshold and every normalized variant of these — are decimal fractions of one, carried to **exactly six decimal places** and likewise rounded half up. A full interest is therefore written 1.000000 and a half interest 0.500000. Acreage figures are carried to two decimal places. The non-consent penalty multiple is carried to one decimal place.

Three rounding disciplines recur and are stated here once because they are the most common source of off-by-one-cent disagreement:

- **Round the parts, then reconcile with a signed residual.** Whenever a total must be split among several rows — allocation factors across a pooled unit, lease pools across a revenue month, owner shares across a bill — each part is rounded half up independently, the signed difference between the intended total and the sum of the rounded parts is computed, and that entire residual is added to one designated row chosen by a stated tie-break. The parts then sum to the total exactly. The residual can be negative.
- **Round before you clamp.** Where a computed amount is subject to a floor or a cap, the raw amount is rounded to whole cents first and the floor and the cap are applied to the rounded value. Clamping first and rounding afterwards is a different answer and is not acceptable.
- **Round each component, then add.** Where a row's total is the sum of several independently computed components, each component is rounded to whole cents on its own and the total is the sum of those rounded components. Summing raw components and rounding once at the end is a different answer and is not acceptable.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Wells and Statements

Two write operations in this service can silently destroy work that another writer has already committed, so both require the client to declare which revision of the parent record it was looking at.

Recording a working-interest position on a well requires `expected_well_version` as a mandatory, non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that well. If the supplied value does not equal the well's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WELL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The scenario this protects against is concrete: an assignment closing legitimately supersedes the ownership rows of a well and rewrites the division of interest, so a division order posted from a version observed before that closing would push the well's active working-interest total away from 1.000000.

Revising a JIB statement requires `expected_jib_statement_version` as a mandatory, non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the statement being revised. If the supplied value does not equal that statement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STATEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A revision supersedes the statement it targets and reverses that statement's contribution to the well's billed-to-date ledger; two revisions built from the same observed version would each supersede the same statement and reverse the same amount twice, so the stale-version check makes the second one fail deterministically.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, issuance, voiding, settlement, dispute filing, dispute exchanges, dispute resolution, revenue release and disbursement payment do not accept a version expectation, and a well status change accepts `expected_well_version` only as an optional field that, when supplied, is checked the same way.

### 2.8. The Joint Account Ledger and Its Invariants

A well maintains `billed_to_date_cents` as a running ledger of what the joint account has charged its partners. Membership in that ledger is defined by statement status, not by history: a statement contributes its `total_billed_cents` to the well's running total while its `statement_status` is `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED` or `SETTLED`, and contributes nothing while it is `DRAFT`, `ADJUSTED` or `VOID`. A statement therefore *enters* the billed set at issuance and *leaves* it when it is adjusted or voided; a dispute filed against it does not remove it, and settlement does not remove it either. Every operation that moves a statement across that boundary must adjust the well's running total by exactly the statement's own `total_billed_cents` and increment the well's `version`.

An interest owner maintains `suspense_balance_cents` as the sum of `net_amount_cents` over that owner's disbursement rows whose `disbursement_status` is `SUSPENDED`. It is recomputed — never incrementally patched — every time a disbursement enters or leaves suspense.

Three conserved quantities are invariants of the system and hold after every successful operation. First, the working-interest percentages of a well's active positions sum to exactly 1.000000 once the well is fully subscribed, and an assignment redistributes interest between parties without creating or destroying any. Second, the allocation factors of a pooled unit's committed acreage allocations sum to exactly 1.000000. Third, cash is conserved through the revenue waterfall: for any released production month, the gross revenue equals the severance tax withheld plus the total lessor royalty plus the total disbursed to working-interest owners.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this service mutate more than one entity, and in the hardest cases a mutation triggers a second mutation that triggers a third. Where §3 describes a cascade in numbered hops, each hop is caused by the write performed in the preceding hop rather than by the originating request, and every hop is applied within the same request so that no intermediate state is ever observable. Consistency is immediate: there is no deferred reconciliation pass, and a read issued after a successful write always sees every cascaded consequence of that write.

Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including entities the caller never named. Derived fields are recomputed from their inputs rather than adjusted by a delta wherever the specification names a recomputation — an owner's required bond, a well's allocation factor total, a well's consenting working interest and an owner's suspense balance are all recomputed in full.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Lease Inventory, Tenure and the Obligation Calendar

A `LandAdministrator` records each recorded instrument in the land inventory by supplying its `lease_number`, the `lessor_name` of the mineral owner who granted it, the `gross_acres` described in the instrument, the `royalty_rate` the lessor retains, and the `primary_term_end_date` — the last calendar date on which the primary term is still running. The lease number is printed on the instrument and must be unique across the whole inventory, between 1 and 32 characters long; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEASE_NUMBER`. Gross acreage must be strictly greater than zero and is carried to two decimal places. The royalty rate is strictly greater than zero and strictly less than one, carried to exactly six decimal places, because a lease that reserved the whole proceeds or none of them is not a lease the land department can administer.

Four further terms are optional at recording, each with a default that describes the least generous instrument. `extension_option_months` is the number of months the primary term advances if the extension option is exercised; it defaults to 0, must be zero or greater, and may not exceed 60. `extension_bonus_per_acre_cents` is the bonus consideration per gross acre owed to the lessor on exercise; it defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. `shut_in_royalty_per_acre_cents` is the per-acre shut-in royalty rate owed for each shut-in maintenance period; it defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. `continuous_dev_window_days` is the number of days allowed between the end of one continuous-development operation and the start of the next; it defaults to 180, and must be at least 30 and at most 365. A newly recorded lease begins with `tenure_status` set to `PRIMARY_TERM`, `obligation_status` set to `COMPLIANT`, `extension_exercised` set to false, `extension_bonus_paid_cents` set to 0, `shut_in_payments_made` set to 0, `committed_acres_total` and `held_acres` both 0, and a `version` of 1. Its `shut_in_start_date`, `shut_in_next_due_date`, `last_operation_end_date` and `continuous_dev_deadline` are all null, since nothing has yet been committed to a well and no maintenance event has been recorded. Land staff browse the inventory filtered by `tenure_status`, by `obligation_status` or by `lease_number`.

The lease carries **two independent status dimensions that advance separately**. `tenure_status` answers "how is this lease currently held?" and takes the values `PRIMARY_TERM`, `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION`, `SHUT_IN_HELD`, `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD` and `EXPIRED`, the last of which is terminal. `obligation_status` answers "is the open obligation being met?" and takes the values `COMPLIANT`, `OBLIGATION_DUE`, `CURED` and `IN_BREACH`. A lease can be `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION` and simultaneously `OBLIGATION_DUE`; it can be `SHUT_IN_HELD` and `CURED`. Neither dimension is derivable from the other, and a transition on one dimension leaves the other alone unless this specification says otherwise.

**Exercising the extension option.** The option is single-use. A `LandAdministrator` exercising it supplies `bonus_paid_cents`, the consideration actually tendered to the lessor. The lease must still be in `PRIMARY_TERM`, must have `extension_exercised` false, must have an `extension_option_months` strictly greater than zero, and the logical clock date must be on or before `primary_term_end_date` — an option cannot be exercised out of a term that has already run. A lease failing any of those conditions, including a second exercise attempt on an option already used, is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The tendered amount is then checked against what the instrument actually calls for: the required bonus is the lease's `gross_acres` multiplied by `extension_bonus_per_acre_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and if `bonus_paid_cents` is not exactly equal to that figure the exercise is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_BONUS_MISMATCH`. On success the lease stays in `PRIMARY_TERM` — this is a self-loop on the tenure dimension, not a move — while `primary_term_end_date` advances by `extension_option_months` calendar months, clamping the day of month to the last day of the target month when the target month is shorter than the source month. `extension_exercised` becomes true, `extension_bonus_paid_cents` records the paid amount, and the lease's `version` increments by 1. If the lease was sitting at `OBLIGATION_DUE` on the obligation dimension, exercising the extension moves it to `CURED`; if it was in any other obligation posture, that dimension is untouched.

**Recording maintenance events.** The land department keeps a lease alive by recording obligation events. Each event carries an `event_type` of `SHUT_IN_PAYMENT`, `CONTINUOUS_OPERATION` or `PRODUCTION_ESTABLISHED`, together with an `event_date`; a continuous-development event may also carry `operation_end_date`, and the first shut-in payment of a period may carry `shut_in_start_date`.

A `SHUT_IN_PAYMENT` may only be recorded while the lease's tenure is `PRIMARY_TERM`, `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION` or `SHUT_IN_HELD`, and only while at least one of the lease's committed acreage allocations feeds a well whose operational status is `SHUT_IN`. The amount owed for the period is the lease's `gross_acres` multiplied by `shut_in_royalty_per_acre_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. On the first payment of a shut-in chain the supplied `shut_in_start_date` is recorded; later payments in the same chain leave that date untouched. `shut_in_payments_made` increments by one and `shut_in_next_due_date` is recomputed, and the tenure dimension moves to `SHUT_IN_HELD` — a self-loop when the lease was already `SHUT_IN_HELD`, which is what the second payment of a chain produces. **A shut-in chain admits at most two payments.** A third attempt on a lease whose `shut_in_payments_made` has already reached two is rejected with `ERR_SHUT_IN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, which is why the eventual expiry of a long shut-in is unavoidable rather than merely likely.

A `CONTINUOUS_OPERATION` event may be recorded while the lease's tenure is `PRIMARY_TERM`, `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION` or `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD`, and the `event_date` must be on or before the logical clock date — the land department records operations that have happened, not operations it intends. The operation's end date, supplied as `operation_end_date` (or, when the request carries only `event_date`, that date) becomes the lease's `last_operation_end_date`, and `continuous_dev_deadline` is recomputed from it. The tenure dimension moves to `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD`. When the lease is already `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD`, the event is a self-loop that rolls the window forward, and it is admitted only while the logical clock date is on or before the existing `continuous_dev_deadline`: once that date has passed, the window has lapsed and the operation cannot revive it. A `PRODUCTION_ESTABLISHED` event moves the tenure dimension to `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION`.

Recording any obligation event on a lease sitting at `OBLIGATION_DUE` moves the obligation dimension to `CURED`, provided the tenure dimension is not already `EXPIRED`. For a shut-in payment that is the only circumstance in which the obligation dimension changes. For a continuous-operation or production-established event, an `IN_BREACH` posture is also cured. Whichever event was recorded, the lease's `version` increments by 1. When the cure comes from a shut-in payment, `shut_in_payments_made` increments and `shut_in_next_due_date` advances by 365 days; when it comes from a continuous-development operation, `last_operation_end_date` is rewritten and `continuous_dev_deadline` is recomputed. The event itself is never persisted as a resource of its own — it exists only in its effects on the lease — so recording one creates nothing and, by the rule of §4, the call returns status code 200 with the updated lease in `data`, not 201.

**How the two obligation deadlines are computed.** `shut_in_next_due_date` is null whenever `shut_in_start_date` is null. Otherwise the first payment of a chain is due 90 days after the shut-in start date, and each payment already made pushes the next due date out by a further 365 days — so the due date is the shut-in start date plus 90 days plus 365 days for every payment recorded so far. `continuous_dev_deadline` is null whenever `last_operation_end_date` is null, and is otherwise that date plus `continuous_dev_window_days` days. The lease's single **open obligation date** is the earlier of those two dates, ignoring whichever of them is null; when the two dates fall on the same day, the shut-in date is the one that governs. Every survival comparison against the logical clock date is inclusive: the primary term survives while the clock date is on or before `primary_term_end_date`; a shut-in holding survives while at least one payment has been made and the clock date is on or before `shut_in_next_due_date`; a continuous-development holding survives while the clock date is on or before `continuous_dev_deadline`. A lease whose deadline falls exactly on the clock date is still held, and fails only on the following day.

**How obligations become due and how they breach.** A periodic obligation evaluation pass, run by a `ComplianceOfficer`, walks every time-gated rule in the system against the logical clock in a single idempotent sweep — running it twice at the same clock value changes nothing the second time. On the obligation dimension it does four things. A `COMPLIANT` lease with an open obligation date moves to `OBLIGATION_DUE` once the clock date has reached the 30-day warning window — that is, once the clock date is on or after the open obligation date less 30 days and still on or before that date itself. An `OBLIGATION_DUE` lease whose open obligation date has passed moves to `IN_BREACH`, and the tenure dimension expires with it through the matching tenure transition and its full cascade. A `CURED` lease returns to `COMPLIANT` once no open obligation date lies within 30 days of the clock date, and a `CURED` lease moves back to `OBLIGATION_DUE` once the next obligation date falls within that 30-day window — which is exactly how a lease held by shut-in royalties cycles from `COMPLIANT` to `OBLIGATION_DUE` to `CURED` and back to `OBLIGATION_DUE` once per anniversary.

**How tenure is maintained or lost.** The same evaluation pass drives the tenure dimension. Once the clock date has passed `primary_term_end_date`, a lease with at least one committed acreage allocation feeding a well that is both `PRODUCING` and in a `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` unit moves from `PRIMARY_TERM` to `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION`; its `held_acres` is recomputed as the lesser of `gross_acres` and `committed_acres_total`, and its obligation dimension is set to `COMPLIANT`. If instead its committed allocation feeds a well that is `SHUT_IN` inside a `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` unit, and at least one shut-in payment has been made and the clock date is on or before `shut_in_next_due_date`, the lease moves from `PRIMARY_TERM` to `SHUT_IN_HELD` with `held_acres` likewise recomputed. A `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION` lease drops to `SHUT_IN_HELD` when every one of its committed allocations feeds a well that is `SHUT_IN` and the shut-in chain is still current on the same two conditions. A `SHUT_IN_HELD` lease returns to `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION` as soon as any committed allocation feeds a `PRODUCING` well in a `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` unit; that transition resets the shut-in chain outright — `shut_in_start_date` and `shut_in_next_due_date` become null, `shut_in_payments_made` returns to 0 — and sets the obligation dimension to `COMPLIANT`, which is what makes a second, later shut-in chain start again from its own 90-day first payment. A `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD` lease likewise returns to `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION`, with the obligation dimension set to `COMPLIANT`, when any committed allocation feeds a `PRODUCING` well in a `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` unit.

Expiry arrives on three paths, all of them driven by the evaluation pass, and all three write the same terminal outcome: `tenure_status` becomes `EXPIRED`, `obligation_status` becomes `IN_BREACH`, and `held_acres` is set to 0. A lease expires out of `PRIMARY_TERM` when the clock date has passed `primary_term_end_date`, no committed allocation feeds a well that is both `PRODUCING` and `POOLED_EFFECTIVE`, the lease is not being carried by a current shut-in chain on a `SHUT_IN` well (meaning it is not the case that at least one payment has been made and the clock date is on or before `shut_in_next_due_date`), and either `continuous_dev_deadline` is null or the clock date has passed it. A lease expires out of `SHUT_IN_HELD` when the clock date has passed `shut_in_next_due_date` and no committed allocation feeds a `PRODUCING` well. A lease expires out of `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD` when the clock date has passed both `continuous_dev_deadline` and `primary_term_end_date` and no committed allocation feeds a `PRODUCING` well.

**The four-hop expiry cascade.** Losing a lease is the most destructive event in this system, and its consequences must be applied in full, in order, within the expiring request:

1. Every acreage allocation of the expiring lease whose `allocation_status` is `COMMITTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, with `withdrawn_reason` set to `LEASE_EXPIRED`.
2. Caused by hop 1 — not by the lease's own status change — each well that lost an allocation recomputes `allocation_factor` across its remaining committed allocations and refreshes `allocation_factor_total`; and if the count of allocations still committed to that well has fallen below two, the well's `pooling_status` becomes `POOLED_TERMINATED`.
3. Caused by hop 2's termination write, every production revenue record of that well whose `distribution_status` is `ACCRUED` or `RELEASED` becomes `SUSPENDED`. A released month keeps its computed waterfall totals for audit; it is the money that stops moving, not the arithmetic that is erased.
4. Caused by hop 3, every owner disbursement belonging to those suspended revenue months whose `disbursement_status` is `ACCRUED` becomes `SUSPENDED` with `suspense_reason` set to `POOLING_TERMINATED`, and each affected owner's `suspense_balance_cents` is recomputed. Rows already `PAID` are untouched. On the branch where the suspended revenue month was still `ACCRUED`, no disbursement rows exist yet, so hop 4 does nothing on that branch.

The evaluation pass reports what it did: an `evaluated_at` instant, and counters for `leases_expired`, `leases_flagged_obligation_due`, `leases_in_breach`, `disputes_escalated`, `disputes_expired`, `transfers_deemed_waived`, `allocations_withdrawn`, `revenues_suspended` and `disbursements_suspended`. Every entity the pass mutates has its `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.2. Wells, Pooled Units and the Allocation of Committed Acreage

A well is created by a `LandAdministrator` with a `well_name` — the operator's name for it, unique across all wells, between 1 and 32 characters, and also used as the prefix land staff expect on statement numbers — an `api_number` carrying the regulatory API well number, a `product_type` of either `OIL` or `GAS`, and a `total_depth_ft` giving total measured depth in feet, which must be strictly greater than zero and at most 30000. A `spud_date` may be supplied at creation but is not required. The API number must be unique across all wells; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_API_NUMBER`. The product type is more consequential than it looks: it selects both the severance tax rate applied to the well's proceeds and the statutory window inside which those proceeds must reach the owners.

A new well begins with `well_status` set to `PROPOSED` and `pooling_status` set to `UNPOOLED`, with `allocation_factor_total`, `consent_working_interest_pct` and `billed_to_date_cents` all 0, `first_production_date` and `shut_in_since` null, and `version` 1. Its `plugging_liability_cents` is estimated immediately from depth alone — no cost history participates. The estimate is a base charge of $35,000 plus $12 for every foot of measured depth, and it is capped at $250,000; expressed in the cents the service actually stores, it is 3,500,000 plus 1,200 multiplied by `total_depth_ft`, and if that sum exceeds 25,000,000 the estimate is 25,000,000 instead. Because every input is an integer, no rounding occurs. Operators browse the well inventory filtered by `well_status`, by `pooling_status` or by `product_type`.

**Committing acreage.** A well produces from a pooled unit assembled out of tracts contributed by several leases. A `LandAdministrator` commits a tract by naming the `lease_id` and the `committed_acres` being dedicated to this well's unit. The lease must not have expired — an allocation against a lease whose `tenure_status` is `EXPIRED` is rejected with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The same lease may not be committed to the same well twice: if a `COMMITTED` allocation already exists for that pair of well and lease, the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALLOCATION`. And a lease cannot contribute acreage it does not have left: `committed_acres` must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than the lease's `gross_acres` less its `committed_acres_total`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LEASE`. Those three checks are evaluated in that order — expiry first, then duplication, then the acreage arithmetic — so a duplicate commitment against an expired lease reports the expiry. All three sit behind the well's own pooling-state guard, which is the addressed entity's own-entity state precondition and therefore runs ahead of them on the ladder of §2.5: a commitment against a well whose `pooling_status` is neither `UNPOOLED` nor `POOLED_FORMING` — that is, one already `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` or `POOLED_TERMINATED` — is rejected with `ERR_POOLING_NOT_FORMING` before any of those three lease checks is reached (the body's `lease_id` is still resolved first, at rung 7), so a duplicate commitment against an already-effective unit reports the pooling state rather than the duplication.

Each allocation is written with `allocation_status` set to `COMMITTED` and `withdrawn_reason` null, and the first commitment on a well moves its `pooling_status` from `UNPOOLED` to `POOLED_FORMING`. Later commitments are self-loops on `POOLED_FORMING` and are only admitted while the well is in that pooling state. Every commitment recomputes the whole unit, not just the new row, and increments both the well's `version` and the contributing lease's `version` by 1.

**How allocation factors are computed.** An allocation's `allocation_factor` is its share of the well's pooled unit. Only allocations whose `allocation_status` is `COMMITTED` participate: withdrawn rows are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator, and they keep whatever `allocation_factor` they last held, frozen for audit. When a well has no committed allocations at all, every factor is 0.000000 and the well's `allocation_factor_total` is 0.000000, and no further work is done. Otherwise the total committed acreage of the unit is the sum of `committed_acres` across the committed allocations, and each allocation's provisional factor is its own `committed_acres` divided by that total, rounded half up to six decimal places. Those provisional factors will generally not sum to exactly one, so the signed difference between 1.000000 and their sum — always some whole multiple of 0.000001, and possibly negative — is added in its entirety to a single allocation: the one with the largest `committed_acres`, and among ties the one with the earliest `created_at`. After that adjustment the well's `allocation_factor_total` is exactly 1.000000. In the same pass, each contributing lease has its `committed_acres_total` recomputed as the sum of `committed_acres` over that lease's committed allocations, and its `held_acres` recomputed as the lesser of its `gross_acres` and that committed total — except on a lease whose `tenure_status` is `EXPIRED`, where `held_acres` is 0. Allocations are browsable filtered by `well_id`, by `lease_id` or by `allocation_status`, and a single allocation is readable by its own identifier.

**Declaring the unit effective.** Pooling is declared effective once, out of the forming state, by a `LandAdministrator` supplying the `effective_date` on which the unit takes force. Three conditions gate it, evaluated in this order. The well's `pooling_status` must be `POOLED_FORMING` — a unit that is already effective, already terminated, or still unpooled is rejected with `ERR_POOLING_NOT_FORMING`. Then every lease behind a committed allocation must still be held, that is, must have a `tenure_status` of `PRIMARY_TERM`, `HELD_BY_PRODUCTION`, `SHUT_IN_HELD` or `CONTINUOUS_DEV_HELD` rather than `EXPIRED`; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_HELD`. Finally the unit must actually be a unit: at least two allocations must be `COMMITTED`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_POOLING_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`, and `allocation_factor_total` is re-verified as exactly 1.000000. On success `pooling_status` becomes `POOLED_EFFECTIVE` and the well's `version` increments by 1. The reverse move to `POOLED_TERMINATED` is never requested by a caller: it happens only as hop 2 of a lease expiry cascade, when the count of allocations still committed to the well falls below two, and `POOLED_TERMINATED` is terminal.

**Driving the well's operational lifecycle.** The well's `well_status` dimension runs `PROPOSED` to `DRILLING` to `PRODUCING`, cycles between `PRODUCING` and `SHUT_IN` without bound, and reaches `TEMPORARILY_ABANDONED` and finally the terminal `PLUGGED`. A status change names the desired `to_status` and the `effective_date` of the change, and may optionally carry `expected_well_version`, which when supplied is checked against the well's current `version` exactly as described in §2.7. Any requested `to_status` that is not a declared transition out of the well's present `well_status` is rejected with `ERR_WELL_INVALID_STATUS`.

Moving `PROPOSED → DRILLING` requires the unit to be in force: the well's `pooling_status` must be `POOLED_EFFECTIVE`, because a well is not spudded before its pooled unit exists, and a well that fails this is rejected with `ERR_WELL_NOT_POOLED`. The transition overwrites `spud_date` with the logical clock date — which is why a well that has reached `DRILLING` always carries a spud date, whether or not one was supplied at creation — and increments the well's `version` by 1.

Moving `DRILLING → PRODUCING` requires an operator of record: at least one working-interest position on the well must be `ACTIVE` with `is_operator` true, else the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_OPERATOR_INTEREST`. On success `first_production_date` is set to the logical clock date if it is currently null, and left alone otherwise, so a well that returns to production years later keeps its original first-production date. The `version` increments by 1.

Moving `PRODUCING → SHUT_IN` records `shut_in_since` as the logical clock date and reaches across into the land records: **every lease with a committed allocation to this well** has its `shut_in_start_date` set to the logical clock date, its `shut_in_payments_made` reset to 0, and its `shut_in_next_due_date` set to the clock date plus 90 days. That is what opens the shut-in royalty obligation the land department must then service. Returning `SHUT_IN → PRODUCING` clears `shut_in_since` to null and reverses that cross-entity write on the same leases: `shut_in_start_date` becomes null, `shut_in_payments_made` returns to 0, and `shut_in_next_due_date` becomes null. Both directions increment the well's `version` by 1.

A well may be taken to `TEMPORARILY_ABANDONED` from either `PRODUCING` or `SHUT_IN`, incrementing `version` by 1 and changing nothing else. Only from `TEMPORARILY_ABANDONED` may a well be moved to `PLUGGED`, and that move is the most heavily gated transition in the system. First, no bill may be outstanding: if any statement on the well is still `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED`, the move is rejected with `ERR_UNSETTLED_STATEMENTS`, because a well cannot be abandoned while partners still owe or contest a charge. Then `plugging_liability_cents` is recomputed from depth, and every owner holding an `ACTIVE` working-interest position on the well must have `bond_posted_cents` of at least its freshly recomputed `required_bond_cents` — the boundary is inclusive, so exact coverage passes — else the move is rejected with `ERR_PLUGGING_BOND_SHORTFALL`. On success the recomputed `plugging_liability_cents` is frozen at that value, the `version` increments by 1, and the well closes to further activity: no cost entry, no JIB statement and no production revenue record may be created against it afterwards.

### 3.3. The Division of Interest: Owners, Positions and Bonding

Before any interest can be recorded, the party holding it must be registered. A `LandAdministrator` registers an interest owner with an `owner_name`, an `owner_type` of `WORKING_INTEREST_PARTNER`, `ROYALTY_OWNER` or `OVERRIDING_ROYALTY_OWNER`, and a `contact_email` that serves as the notice address for AFE, audit and division-order correspondence. Three optional attributes describe the owner's compliance posture: `tax_id_on_file`, a flag that defaults to false and whose absence forces that owner's revenue into suspense; `bond_posted_cents`, the face amount of the plugging bond or letter of credit the owner has posted, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `audit_window_days`, the contractual audit-rights window measured from the moment a statement is issued, defaulting to 730 days and constrained to at least 90 and at most 1460. A newly registered owner has `required_bond_cents` and `suspense_balance_cents` both 0 and a `version` of 1. Owners are browsable filtered by `owner_type`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` — and only a `ComplianceOfficer` — updates that posture afterwards, supplying any subset of `tax_id_on_file`, `bond_posted_cents` and `audit_window_days`. Every such update recomputes the owner's `required_bond_cents` and increments the owner's `version` by 1. One of these updates has a further consequence: setting `tax_id_on_file` to true releases the owner's revenue that was being held for want of a taxpayer identification number. Every disbursement of that owner whose `disbursement_status` is `SUSPENDED` with `suspense_reason` of `MISSING_TAX_ID` returns to `ACCRUED` with its `suspense_reason` cleared to null, and the owner's `suspense_balance_cents` is recomputed. Suspense held for any other reason is not affected by a tax-id update.

**Recording a position.** A working-interest position ties an owner to a well over a time interval. A `LandAdministrator` records one by naming the `owner_id`, the `working_interest_pct` — the share of costs that owner bears — the `net_revenue_interest_pct` — the share of post-royalty net proceeds payable to that owner — the `effective_from` date on which the position begins, and `expected_well_version`, which is mandatory here and enforced as described in §2.7. Optionally the caller supplies an `effective_to` date, an `is_operator` flag and a `preferential_right` flag. Both percentages must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1, carried to exactly six decimal places. `is_operator` defaults to false and marks the single operating partner of the well — the partner deemed to consent to every AFE it proposes. `preferential_right` defaults to false and marks an owner holding a preferential purchase right over assignments on this well.

Four business checks guard the write, in this order. The named owner must have `owner_type` of `WORKING_INTEREST_PARTNER`: royalty and overriding-royalty owners hold no working interest and cannot be given one, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_TYPE_INVALID`. Then the interval is checked for collision. Intervals are **half-open**: `effective_from` is inclusive, `effective_to` is exclusive, and an `effective_to` of null means the position runs open-ended. Two intervals that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending 2026-06-01 and the next beginning 2026-06-01 — are adjacent and perfectly legal. A genuine intersection with any other non-`TERMINATED` position for the same pairing of well and owner is rejected with `ERR_INTEREST_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Next, the well's cost side may not be oversubscribed: the sum of `working_interest_pct` over the well's `ACTIVE` positions, counting the row about to be written, may not exceed 1.000000, else the request is rejected with `ERR_WI_SUM_EXCEEDED`. Finally, on the row itself, `net_revenue_interest_pct` may not exceed `working_interest_pct`, since an owner cannot take more of the revenue than it bears of the cost; a breach is rejected with `ERR_NRI_EXCEEDS_WI`.

A recorded position begins with `interest_status` of `ACTIVE`, `non_consent_flag` false and `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` of 0, and a `version` of 1. Writing it recomputes the owner's `required_bond_cents` and the well's `consent_working_interest_pct`, and increments the well's `version` by 1. Positions are browsable filtered by `well_id`, by `owner_id`, by `interest_status` or by `non_consent_flag`, and a single position is readable by its `interest_id` together with its outstanding `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents`.

`interest_status` takes three values. `ACTIVE` means the interval currently governs. `SUPERSEDED` means an assignment split the position and a replacement row carries the remainder. `TERMINATED` means the owner's residual interest on that well reached zero. Both non-active values are terminal for the row.

**Terminating a position.** A `LandAdministrator` terminates a position by supplying the `effective_to` date that closes it. The position must still be `ACTIVE`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_INTEREST_NOT_ACTIVE`. And an owner may not walk away while the operator is still holding its money: if terminating this position would leave the owner with no `ACTIVE` position anywhere and the owner's `suspense_balance_cents` is not zero, the request is rejected with `ERR_SUSPENSE_BALANCE_NONZERO`. On success `interest_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, the owner's `required_bond_cents` and the well's `consent_working_interest_pct` are recomputed, the well's `version` increments by 1, and the position's own `version` increments by 1.

**The consenting share of a well.** A well's `consent_working_interest_pct` is a derived figure: the sum of `working_interest_pct` across the well's positions that are both `ACTIVE` and carry `non_consent_flag` false. It is recomputed whenever a position is written or terminated, whenever a non-consent flag is set, and whenever an assignment closes. It is the denominator against which capital costs are reallocated at billing time.

**Bond coverage.** The plugging bond an owner must post is derived from its exposure, not from its own election. Consider the owner's positions that are `ACTIVE` on wells that are not yet `PLUGGED`; positions that are `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED`, and positions on wells already plugged, are excluded. For each such position, the owner's share of that well's plugging cost is its `working_interest_pct` multiplied by that well's `plugging_liability_cents`, and **that per-well product is rounded half up to whole cents before anything is added together**. Those rounded per-well amounts are then summed, the sum is multiplied by a coverage factor of 1.25 — the joint operating agreement requires 125% of estimated exposure, not merely 100% — and that product is rounded half up once more to produce `required_bond_cents`. Multiplying the unrounded sum by the coverage factor is a different figure and is not acceptable. The requirement is recomputed whenever a position is recorded, whenever a compliance update is applied, and whenever an assignment closes, and it is read as a precondition both when a well is moved to `PLUGGED` and when an assignment is closed.

An assignment notice carries its own copy of that arithmetic in `bond_check_required_cents`. It is the same formula, evaluated for the *prospective grantee* as though the conveyance had already been recorded — that is, over the grantee's existing `ACTIVE` positions on wells that are not `PLUGGED`, plus a further position on this transfer's well carrying `transfer_wi_pct`.

### 3.4. Authorizations for Expenditure, Elections and Non-Consent

Before the operator spends the joint account's money on a discretionary operation, it circulates an authorization for expenditure to the well's working-interest partners. A `LandAdministrator` issues one by supplying the `afe_number` printed on the document — which must be unique across every AFE in the system — the `operation_type` being authorized, one of `DRILL`, `COMPLETION`, `WORKOVER` or `RECOMPLETION`; an `estimated_cost_cents` strictly greater than zero; a `cost_class` of either `CAPITAL` or `OPERATING`; and an `election_deadline_date`, the last calendar date on which a partner may respond. Two commercial terms are optional. `non_consent_penalty_multiple` is the multiple of a non-consenting owner's capital share that the operator may later recover out of that owner's revenue; it defaults to 3.0 and must lie within the inclusive range 1.0 through 5.0 with at most one decimal place. `minimum_consent_pct` is the minimum consenting working interest required for the operation to proceed at all; it defaults to 0.500000, must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1, and is carried to exactly six decimal places. A new AFE opens with `afe_status` of `ELECTIONS_OPEN`, `consent_working_interest_pct` and `non_consent_working_interest_pct` both 0, `elections_closed_at` null, and `version` 1. AFEs are browsable filtered by `well_id`, by `afe_status` or by `cost_class`.

Only `CAPITAL` spend carries a penalty multiple. An `OPERATING` AFE can still be non-consented, but no penalty accrues against the non-consenting owner.

**Submitting an election.** A `PartnerRepresentative` elects for itself; a `LandAdministrator` may also submit an election on a partner's behalf. Either way the request names the `owner_id` electing and an `election_type` of `CONSENT` or `NON_CONSENT`. Seven conditions guard it, and they are evaluated in the order given, so the earliest applicable failure is the one reported. The AFE must still be in `ELECTIONS_OPEN`; an election against an AFE whose window has closed in any of the three closing outcomes is rejected with `ERR_AFE_ELECTIONS_CLOSED`. The owner must not already have an election on this AFE whose `election_status` is `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_ELECTION_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. The owner's position on the well must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_INTEREST_NOT_ACTIVE`. The logical clock date must be on or before `election_deadline_date` — the deadline day itself is still open — else `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The owner's `owner_type` must be `WORKING_INTEREST_PARTNER`, else `ERR_OWNER_TYPE_INVALID`. The owner must actually hold an `ACTIVE` position on the AFE's well, else `ERR_NOT_AN_INTEREST_HOLDER`. And the operator may not non-consent its own operation: an election of `NON_CONSENT` submitted for the owner whose position on the well carries `is_operator` true is rejected with `ERR_OPERATOR_CANNOT_NON_CONSENT`.

A submitted election is written with `election_status` of `SUBMITTED`, a `submitted_at` timestamp taken from the logical clock, and a `wi_pct_snapshot` copied from the owner's `ACTIVE` `working_interest_pct` at the moment the row is written — the snapshot is what the tally will use later, so a subsequent assignment does not retroactively change an election already cast. Its `estimated_penalty_cents` is 0 on every `CONSENT` row, and 0 as well on a `NON_CONSENT` row whose AFE has a `cost_class` of `OPERATING`. On a `NON_CONSENT` row against a `CAPITAL` AFE it is the AFE's `non_consent_penalty_multiple` multiplied by its `estimated_cost_cents` multiplied by the row's `wi_pct_snapshot`, rounded half up to whole cents. Writing an election increments the AFE's `version` by 1, and the election's own `version` is 1. Elections are browsable filtered by `afe_id`, by `owner_id`, by `election_status` or by `election_type`, and a single election is readable by its `election_id`.

**Closing the window and tallying consent.** A `LandAdministrator` closes an AFE's election window; the request carries no body. The AFE must still be in `ELECTIONS_OPEN`, else `ERR_AFE_ELECTIONS_CLOSED`. Closure requires either that the logical clock date has passed `election_deadline_date`, or that every non-operator owner holding an `ACTIVE` position on the well has already filed a `SUBMITTED` election; closing while the window is still open and at least one non-operator holder is still silent is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_OPEN`.

The tally then runs. Only elections whose `election_status` is `SUBMITTED` participate — rows that are `DEEMED_NON_CONSENT` or `VOIDED` are excluded. The operator is deemed to consent without filing anything, so the tally starts from the `working_interest_pct` of the well's `ACTIVE` position carrying `is_operator` true, or from 0.000000 when the well has no such position. To that is added the `wi_pct_snapshot` of every submitted election whose `election_type` is `CONSENT`, excluding any election filed for the operator itself so that its interest is never counted twice. The result, rounded half up to six decimal places, is written to the AFE's `consent_working_interest_pct`, and `non_consent_working_interest_pct` is written as 1.000000 less that figure. If the consenting tally comes out at exactly 0.000000 the closure is rejected with `ERR_NO_CONSENTING_INTEREST`: there is no candidate consenting set and the operation has no one to fund it.

Otherwise the closure forks into one of three terminal outcomes, and the branch is chosen from the tally alone. A tally of exactly 1.000000 closes the AFE as `FULLY_CONSENTED`. A tally that is at least `minimum_consent_pct` but strictly below 1.000000 closes it as `PARTIALLY_CONSENTED` — the threshold comparison is inclusive, so a tally exactly equal to `minimum_consent_pct` is a partial consent and not a failure. A tally strictly below `minimum_consent_pct` closes it as `FAILED`. Every branch records `elections_closed_at` from the logical clock, writes both tallies, and increments the AFE's `version` by 1.

The three branches diverge sharply in what else they do, and each is specified in full because the differences are the point.

On the **`FULLY_CONSENTED`** branch nothing further is written beyond the tallies and the timestamp; the well's `consent_working_interest_pct` is recomputed, and since no owner is non-consenting it comes out unchanged.

On the **`PARTIALLY_CONSENTED`** branch a four-hop cascade runs:

1. For every non-operator owner holding an `ACTIVE` position on the well that filed no election at all, a synthesized election row is written directly with `election_type` of `NON_CONSENT`, `election_status` of `DEEMED_NON_CONSENT`, a `wi_pct_snapshot` taken from that owner's current `ACTIVE` `working_interest_pct`, a `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and `estimated_penalty_cents` computed by the same rule that governs a filed non-consent row. Silence is non-consent. The operator never receives a synthesized row.
2. Caused by hop 1's election rows — and this covers both the rows synthesized in hop 1 and the rows a partner actually filed as `SUBMITTED` `NON_CONSENT` — each such owner's `ACTIVE` position on the well has `non_consent_flag` set to true.
3. Caused by hop 2's flag writes, the well's `consent_working_interest_pct` is recomputed as the sum of `working_interest_pct` over its `ACTIVE` positions whose `non_consent_flag` is false, and the well's `version` increments by 1.
4. Caused by the same flag writes, every owner disbursement belonging to a newly non-consenting owner on this well whose `disbursement_status` is `ACCRUED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspense_reason` set to `NON_CONSENT_RECOVERY`, and each affected owner's `suspense_balance_cents` is recomputed. Note what does *not* happen here: no recoverable penalty balance accrues at closure. The `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` on the owner's position moves only later, when a statement that includes the AFE's capital costs is issued.

On the **`FAILED`** branch the divergence is total: every election of that AFE — whether `SUBMITTED` or `DEEMED_NON_CONSENT` — moves to `election_status` of `VOIDED` with its `estimated_penalty_cents` reset to 0, no `non_consent_flag` is set on any position, no penalty balance accrues, and no disbursement is suspended. `VOIDED` is terminal for an election row. The operation simply does not proceed.

### 3.5. Booking Joint-Account Costs and Preparing a Statement

Every charge the operator intends to bill the joint account is first booked as a cost entry by a `JointInterestAccountant`. A cost entry carries a `cost_category` — one of `DRILLING`, `COMPLETION`, `LEASE_OPERATING`, `WORKOVER` or `PLUGGING` — a `cost_class` of either `CAPITAL` or `OPERATING`, an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and an `incurred_date` recording when the charge arose. It may name the `afe_id` that authorized it, and routine charges that no AFE covers leave that reference null. It may also carry a `paid_date`, the date the charge was settled with the vendor. A cost entry booked with a paid date is written with `cost_status` of `PAID`; one booked without a paid date is written `ACCRUED` and its `paid_date` stays null. When an AFE is named, it must belong to the same well the cost is being booked against; a cost entry referencing an AFE on a different well is rejected with `ERR_COST_AFE_MISMATCH`. Cost entries are browsable filtered by `well_id`, by `afe_id`, by `cost_status` or by `cost_class`, and a single entry is readable by its `cost_entry_id`.

Settling an accrued charge is a separate step: the accountant supplies the `paid_date` on which the vendor was paid, and the entry's `cost_status` becomes `PAID` with its `version` incremented by 1. That step is not merely bookkeeping — under one of the two cost bases described below, the paid date is what pulls a charge into a billing period.

The distinction between `CAPITAL` and `OPERATING` on a cost entry drives the whole reallocation machinery: capital charges are subject to non-consent reallocation and penalty recovery, and operating charges never are.

**Opening a draft statement.** A `JointInterestAccountant` opens a joint interest billing statement for one closed billing period on one well, supplying a `statement_number` label, a `billing_period_start`, a `billing_period_end`, a `cost_basis`, an `activity_class` and an `overhead_rate_class`. The statement number is between 1 and 32 characters and must be unique among the statements of that same well. The billing period is a **closed interval**: both endpoints are inclusive days of the period. `billing_period_start` must be on or before `billing_period_end`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID`. Then the period is checked against every other statement on the same well whose `statement_status` is anything other than `VOID`: if the proposed closed interval intersects one of theirs, the request is rejected with `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because the intervals are closed, **two periods that merely touch at an endpoint collide** — a period ending 2026-06-30 and a period beginning 2026-06-30 share that day and cannot coexist. Voided statements are excluded from this check, which is precisely how voiding a statement frees its period for a fresh one.

`cost_basis` selects between `ACCRUAL` and `CASH` and determines which charges the period captures. `activity_class` is the COPAS activity class, either `DRILLING` or `PRODUCING`, and drives the fixed overhead rate. `overhead_rate_class` selects the overhead pricing mode, either `FIXED_RATE` or `PERCENTAGE_BASIS`. A new statement is written with `statement_status` of `DRAFT`, `superseded_statement_id` null, `issued_at` and `payment_due_date` null, a `version` of 1, and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the value that will later bar this same user from adjudicating a dispute against the bill. Provisional values for `capital_direct_cents`, `operating_direct_cents`, `direct_cost_cents`, `billable_months`, `overhead_cents` and `total_billed_cents` are computed straight away so the accountant can see what the bill will look like, and they are all recomputed authoritatively at issuance. Statements are browsable filtered by `well_id`, by `superseded_statement_id`, by `statement_status` or by `cost_basis` — the `superseded_statement_id` filter being the channel through which a revision chain is discovered.

**Selecting the direct cost pool.** The pool is drawn from the cost entries of the statement's well, and the `cost_basis` chooses both which entries qualify and which date decides whether they fall inside the period. Both ends of the period are inclusive under either basis.

Under the `ACCRUAL` basis, an entry qualifies when its `cost_status` is `ACCRUED` or `PAID` and its `incurred_date` falls on or after `billing_period_start` and on or before `billing_period_end`. A charge incurred inside the period but settled with the vendor after the period closes **is** billed.

Under the `CASH` basis, an entry qualifies only when its `cost_status` is `PAID`, its `paid_date` is not null, and that `paid_date` falls on or after `billing_period_start` and on or before `billing_period_end`. The same charge incurred inside the period but settled afterwards is **not** billed, while a charge incurred before the period but settled inside it **is**. The two bases therefore produce different values of `capital_direct_cents`, `operating_direct_cents`, `direct_cost_cents`, `total_billed_cents`, and — under percentage-basis overhead — different `overhead_cents`, from identical underlying cost data.

Whichever basis applies, `capital_direct_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the qualifying entries whose `cost_class` is `CAPITAL`, `operating_direct_cents` is the sum over those whose `cost_class` is `OPERATING`, and `direct_cost_cents` is the sum of those two figures. All three are whole-cent integer additions with no rounding.

**Computing overhead.** The overhead charge begins with `billable_months`, the count of distinct calendar-year-and-month pairs having at least one day inside the closed billing period. A period running from the middle of one month to the middle of the next is therefore two billable months, and a period entirely inside one month is one.

Under the `FIXED_RATE` mode the rate is per month and depends on the activity class: a `DRILLING` statement is charged $9,500 per billable month and a `PRODUCING` statement $950 per billable month — that is, 950000 cents per month for drilling activity and 95000 cents per month for producing activity. That per-month rate is multiplied by `billable_months` and by a wage index adjustment of 1.042, reflecting the annually published labour escalation the joint operating agreement incorporates.

Under the `PERCENTAGE_BASIS` mode no month count enters the arithmetic at all: overhead is 5% of `capital_direct_cents` plus 3% of `operating_direct_cents`.

Whichever mode produced it, the raw figure is **rounded half up to whole cents first**, and only then clamped: an overhead charge is never less than $250 (25000 cents) and never more than $15,000 (1500000 cents). Both the floor and the cap apply under both modes. Applying the floor or the cap to the unrounded figure and rounding afterwards is a different answer and is not acceptable. The clamped result is written to `overhead_cents`, and `total_billed_cents` on the statement is `direct_cost_cents` plus `overhead_cents`.

### 3.6. Issuing the Statement: Reallocation, Per-Owner Shares and the Bill

Issuance is the single most consequential operation in the service. A `JointInterestAccountant` issues a draft; the request carries no body. Six preconditions gate it. The statement's `statement_status` must be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. The well's `pooling_status` must be `POOLED_EFFECTIVE`, because a bill can only be raised against a unit that is in force, else `ERR_WELL_NOT_POOLED`. The well's `well_status` must be neither `PROPOSED` nor `PLUGGED`, and the statement's `activity_class` must match the well's operational posture: a `DRILLING` statement requires a well in `DRILLING`, and a `PRODUCING` statement requires a well in `PRODUCING`, `SHUT_IN` or `TEMPORARILY_ABANDONED`. The `billing_period_end` must be strictly before the logical clock's calendar date — an open period cannot be billed — else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. The proposed closed period must not intersect the closed period of any other statement of the same well whose `statement_status` is `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED` or `SETTLED`. And the cost pool selected by the statement's basis and period must not be empty, else `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_COSTS`. Those checks fire in the stated order, so a draft on an unpooled well reports the pooling failure rather than the empty pool.

The direct pools, `billable_months` and `overhead_cents` are then recomputed authoritatively by the rules of §3.5, replacing the provisional values recorded when the draft was opened.

**Which owners are billed.** One line item is created per owner holding an `ACTIVE` position on the well **at the moment of issuance**, not at the end of the billing period. An owner whose position began after `billing_period_end` is therefore still billed, because the snapshot is taken at issuance. Each line item records `statement_id` and `owner_id`, and a `wi_pct_snapshot` copied from that owner's `ACTIVE` `working_interest_pct` at issuance. For ordering purposes throughout the arithmetic below, owners are considered in descending order of `working_interest_pct`, and among owners with equal working interest, in ascending order of the position's `created_at`.

**Reallocating the capital that non-consenting owners refused.** The dependency here looks circular — owner shares depend on overhead, overhead depends on the direct pools, and the direct pools are exactly what gets shifted between owners — so the cut is stated explicitly: **overhead is always computed from the pre-reallocation gross pools, and only its allocation among owners uses post-reallocation weights.** With that fixed, the reallocation proceeds in a pinned order.

First, the capital pool is split in two. The **AFE capital** portion is the sum of `amount_cents` over the qualifying cost entries whose `cost_class` is `CAPITAL`, whose `afe_id` is not null, and whose referenced AFE has an `afe_status` of `PARTIALLY_CONSENTED`. Everything else in the capital pool — capital costs with no AFE, and capital costs under an AFE that closed `FULLY_CONSENTED` or `FAILED` — is **non-AFE capital**, obtained as `capital_direct_cents` less the AFE capital portion. Only the AFE capital portion is ever reallocated; capital under a fully consented or failed AFE is treated as ordinary capital and is shared by working interest like any other charge.

Second, the consenting weights are built. The denominator is the well's `consent_working_interest_pct`. For each consenting owner — one whose `non_consent_flag` is false — `consent_normalized_wi_pct` on its line item is that owner's `working_interest_pct` divided by that denominator, rounded half up to six decimal places; for every non-consenting owner it is 0.000000. The signed difference between 1.000000 and the sum of those normalized weights is added in full to the consenting owner with the largest `working_interest_pct`, breaking ties by the earliest position `created_at`.

Third, each owner's raw share of each pool is computed and held **exactly, unrounded**, at this stage. The owner's raw operating share is `operating_direct_cents` multiplied by its own `working_interest_pct`. Its raw non-AFE capital share is the non-AFE capital portion multiplied by its own `working_interest_pct`. Its raw AFE capital share is the AFE capital portion multiplied by its `consent_normalized_wi_pct` — which is zero for a non-consenting owner, meaning that owner is billed nothing for the operation it refused, and more than its bare working interest for the consenting owners who carried it. The owner's total raw capital share is the sum of its raw non-AFE capital share and its raw AFE capital share.

Fourth, overhead is distributed. The statement's `overhead_cents` was already fixed from the gross pools and is never recomputed here. Each owner's raw overhead share is `overhead_cents` multiplied by the sum of that owner's raw capital and raw operating shares, divided by the statement's `direct_cost_cents`. That denominator is guaranteed positive, because an empty pool would have failed issuance already.

Fifth, four reporting figures are written on each line item, each rounded half up to whole cents **independently of the others**:

- `non_consent_withheld_cents` — for a non-consenting owner, the AFE capital portion multiplied by that owner's own `working_interest_pct`, rounded half up; this is the capital the owner was *not* billed. It is 0 for a consenting owner.
- `non_consent_reallocated_cents` — for a consenting owner, its raw AFE capital share less the AFE capital portion multiplied by its own `working_interest_pct`, rounded half up; this is the extra capital it absorbed on the refusers' behalf. It is 0 for a non-consenting owner.
- `penalty_accrued_cents` — for a non-consenting owner whose AFE has a `cost_class` of `CAPITAL`, that AFE's `non_consent_penalty_multiple` multiplied by this owner's `non_consent_withheld_cents`, rounded half up. It is 0 for a consenting owner, and 0 when the AFE's cost class is `OPERATING`.
- `rounding_residual_cents` — the signed cent residual assigned to this row by the bucket reconciliation described next, and 0 on every row that received none.

When more than one `PARTIALLY_CONSENTED` AFE contributes cost to the same statement, the AFE capital split, the raw share computation and these reporting figures are evaluated **per AFE, in ascending order of the AFE's `created_at`**, and the per-AFE rounded amounts are summed onto the line item.

**Turning raw shares into whole cents.** The last step allocates three buckets in a fixed order: first direct capital, then direct operating, then overhead. Each bucket's total is the statement's own figure — `capital_direct_cents`, `operating_direct_cents` and `overhead_cents` respectively. For each bucket in turn, every owner's raw share for that bucket is rounded half up to whole cents on its own; the signed residual is the bucket total less the sum of those rounded amounts; and the whole residual is added to one target owner's amount for that bucket and also accumulated into that owner's `rounding_residual_cents`. The target is the owner with the largest `working_interest_pct`, ties broken by the earliest position `created_at` — with one exception that matters: **in the direct capital bucket, if that owner's `consent_normalized_wi_pct` is 0 (that is, the largest holder is a non-consenting owner), the residual instead goes to the consenting owner with the largest `working_interest_pct`, using the same tie-break.** A non-consenting owner must not be handed capital cents it was excused from bearing.

Rounding each owner's share per bucket and then summing is mandatory. Summing an owner's three raw shares and rounding once, or rounding the three buckets together, produce different cents and are not acceptable.

The line item's `direct_capital_cents`, `direct_operating_cents` and `overhead_cents` are the rounded amounts from the three buckets, and its `total_billed_cents` is their sum. The identity that closes the arithmetic is exact: the statement's `total_billed_cents` equals `direct_cost_cents` plus `overhead_cents`, and equals the sum of `total_billed_cents` across every line item it produced. Line items are the only immutable rows in the system, and they are discoverable in two ways: as the line items of one statement, optionally filtered by `owner_id`; and as everything ever billed to one owner across statements, optionally filtered by `statement_status`.

**What issuance writes.** On success `issued_at` is taken from the logical clock and `payment_due_date` is set to 30 days after it; `statement_status` becomes `ISSUED`; the statement's `version` increments by 1. The well's `billed_to_date_cents` increases by the statement's `total_billed_cents` and the well's `version` increments by 1. And for each non-consenting owner, that owner's position on the well has its `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` increased by that owner's `non_consent_withheld_cents` plus its `penalty_accrued_cents` — this is the moment the penalty becomes a real receivable the operator will recover out of the owner's future revenue.

### 3.7. Partner Audit Rights, the Dispute Exchange and the Fate of a Bill

A joint operating agreement gives every non-operator two rights against a bill it did not prepare: a **billing dispute**, taken off the face of the statement while the money is still owed, and an **audit exception**, taken out of a later audit long after the bill was paid. Both are recorded as the same kind of record, distinguished by `dispute_kind` — `BILLING_DISPUTE` or `AUDIT_EXCEPTION` — and the distinction decides which clock the filing is measured against.

**Filing.** A `PartnerRepresentative` or an `AuditReviewer` files against a statement. The body carries `owner_id`, `dispute_kind`, `disputed_amount_cents` (which must be greater than zero) and `basis_note`, a written basis of between 1 and 500 characters that the agreement requires. Because a `PartnerRepresentative` is entity-backed, its `X-User-ID` must equal the `owner_id` it files under, or `ERR_OWNER_MISMATCH` is returned. The statement's `statement_status` must be `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED` — a second partner may file against a statement already under dispute — else `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_DISPUTABLE` with status 409. Then the window: for `AUDIT_EXCEPTION`, the logical clock's calendar date must be on or before the date part of the statement's `issued_at` advanced by the filing owner's `audit_window_days`, with the last day of that window inclusive, else `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` with status 422; for `BILLING_DISPUTE`, the clock's date must be on or before the statement's `payment_due_date`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Because `audit_window_days` defaults to 730 days while `payment_due_date` is only 30 days after issuance, the audit right normally outlives the billing right by years — which is exactly why the two kinds exist. Next, no other dispute may already exist for the same statement and the same `owner_id` with a `dispute_status` of `OPEN`, `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_DISPUTE` with status 409; a partner gets one live exception per bill, though it may file again once the previous one closed. The named owner must hold an `ACTIVE` position on the disputed statement's well, else `ERR_NOT_AN_INTEREST_HOLDER`. And `disputed_amount_cents` must not exceed the filing owner's own `total_billed_cents` on that statement's line items, else `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_BILL` — a partner may contest its own share, not the whole well's.

On success the dispute is created with `dispute_status` of `OPEN` and `round_count` of 0; `filed_at` is taken from the logical clock; `response_due_date` is 60 days after the date part of `filed_at`, and `rebuttal_due_date` is 30 days after `response_due_date`; `adjustment_amount_cents` is 0, `deemed_in_favor_of_partner` is false, `operator_responded_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` are null. The statement moves to `DISPUTED` and its `version` increments by 1. Critically, `DISPUTED` is still a **billed** status: the well's `billed_to_date_cents` does not change, because contesting a bill does not un-bill it.

**The exchange cycle.** The dispute then alternates between the two sides. Each move is one exchange carrying an `exchange_type` and a written `exchange_note`, and may optionally restate `disputed_amount_cents`.

- `OPERATOR_RESPONSE`, by a `JointInterestAccountant`, requires `dispute_status` of `OPEN` (else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_OPEN`, 409) and the clock's date on or before `response_due_date` (else `ERR_DISPUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, 422). It sets `dispute_status` to `OPERATOR_RESPONDED`, records `operator_responded_at` from the clock, and **recomputes** `rebuttal_due_date` as 30 days after the date part of `operator_responded_at`. A prompt response therefore shortens the partner's remaining rebuttal time compared with the original schedule; the rebuttal window is measured from the answer, not from the filing.
- `PARTNER_REBUTTAL`, by a `PartnerRepresentative`, requires `dispute_status` of `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` (else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`, 409), the clock's date on or before `rebuttal_due_date` (else `ERR_REBUTTAL_DEADLINE_PASSED`, 422), and `round_count` strictly less than 2 (else `ERR_MAX_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`, 422). It returns `dispute_status` to `OPEN`, increments `round_count` by 1, clears `operator_responded_at`, and sets `response_due_date` to 60 days after the logical clock's date. The cycle from `OPEN` to `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` and back may therefore run twice; the third rebuttal is refused, and the partner's only remaining move is escalation.
- `PARTNER_ESCALATION`, by a `PartnerRepresentative`, requires `dispute_status` of `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` and the clock's date on or before `rebuttal_due_date`. It sets `dispute_status` to `ESCALATED`, handing the matter to the audit reviewer. Every exchange increments the dispute's `version` by 1. An exchange, like a lease obligation event, is never persisted as a resource of its own — it exists only in its effects on the dispute — so recording one returns status code 200 with the updated dispute in `data`, not 201.

**Deemed outcomes when a side goes silent.** Deadlines are enforced by the obligation run, not by a background timer. A dispute that is `OPEN` when the clock passes `response_due_date` is escalated by the run — `dispute_status` becomes `ESCALATED` and `deemed_in_favor_of_partner` is set to true, recording that the operator, not the partner, let the clock run out. A dispute that is `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` when the clock passes `rebuttal_due_date` is closed by the run with `dispute_status` of `EXPIRED` and `adjustment_amount_cents` of 0: the partner abandoned it. In that case the parent statement returns from `DISPUTED` to `ISSUED`, but only when no other dispute against it is still `OPEN`, `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED`. The run's counters report these two paths separately as `disputes_escalated` and `disputes_expired`.

**Resolution.** Only an `AuditReviewer` resolves, and only a dispute whose `dispute_status` is `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED` (else `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`). Segregation of duties is absolute: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `prepared_by_user_id` recorded on the disputed statement, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with status 403. The accountant who prepared a bill may never adjudicate the exception taken against it, even though both roles are otherwise trusted. The body carries `outcome` and `adjustment_amount_cents`. When `outcome` is `UPHELD`, `adjustment_amount_cents` must be 0; when it is `ADJUSTED`, `adjustment_amount_cents` must be greater than 0 and no greater than the dispute's `disputed_amount_cents`. A violation of either rule returns `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_DISPUTE` with status 422. One further restriction applies to run-escalated disputes: a dispute whose `deemed_in_favor_of_partner` is true **cannot be upheld** and may only be resolved `ADJUSTED`, because the operator forfeited the point by missing its own deadline.

Upholding sets `dispute_status` to `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `adjustment_amount_cents` to 0 and `resolved_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and returns the statement from `DISPUTED` to `ISSUED` once no other dispute against it remains live. There is no ledger change: the bill stood.

Adjusting sets `dispute_status` to `RESOLVED_ADJUSTED`, records `adjustment_amount_cents` and `resolved_by_user_id`, and moves the statement from `DISPUTED` to `ADJUSTED` — a terminal statement state — with a three-hop cascade. Hop 1: because `ADJUSTED` leaves the billed set, the well's `billed_to_date_cents` decreases by the statement's `total_billed_cents` and the well's `version` increments by 1. Hop 2, caused by that de-billing leaving the period unbilled: a superseding statement is created in `DRAFT` with `superseded_statement_id` set to the adjusted statement's `id`, the same `well_id`, `billing_period_start`, `billing_period_end`, `cost_basis`, `activity_class` and `overhead_rate_class`, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the resolving reviewer's `X-User-ID`, and a `statement_number` derived as the adjusted statement's `statement_number` followed by a hyphen, the letter R, and the count of existing statements on this well whose `superseded_statement_id` is not null, plus 1. Hop 3, caused by that new draft existing: every **other** dispute against the original statement whose `dispute_status` is `OPEN`, `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED` is closed with `dispute_status` of `EXPIRED` and `deemed_in_favor_of_partner` of true — the bill those partners contested no longer exists, so they are treated as having prevailed rather than as having abandoned their claims. The adjusted statement's `version` also increments by 1. Note that the credit is *not* applied to the old statement's line items, which are immutable; the correction is expressed by superseding the whole bill.

**Revising and voiding.** The operator can also correct or discard a bill without a dispute. A revision, by a `JointInterestAccountant`, requires the addressed statement's `statement_status` to be `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, and carries `statement_number` for the replacement, the mandatory `expected_jib_statement_version` (a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_STATEMENT_VERSION`, 412), and optional overrides for `cost_basis`, `activity_class` and `overhead_rate_class`; an omitted override inherits the addressed statement's value. The addressed statement moves to `ADJUSTED` and a fresh statement is created in `DRAFT` with `superseded_statement_id` pointing at it and the same billing period, so the replacement must be issued in its own right. The ledger consequences depend on where the addressed statement was: revising an `ISSUED` statement decreases the well's `billed_to_date_cents` by its `total_billed_cents`, increments the well's `version` by 1, and applies a compensating reversal to every owner it had charged — for each line item with a `penalty_accrued_cents` above zero, that owner's `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` decreases by that line item's `non_consent_withheld_cents` plus its `penalty_accrued_cents`, exactly undoing what issuance added. Revising a `DRAFT` touches no balances at all, because a draft was never counted. The addressed statement's `version` increments by 1 and the new statement's `version` is 1.

A void, also by a `JointInterestAccountant`, carries `void_reason` and requires `statement_status` of `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, else `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_VOIDABLE` with status 409. It sets `statement_status` to `VOID` and applies the same ledger reversal as a revision when the statement was `ISSUED`, and none when it was `DRAFT` — but it creates **no** superseding statement. The voided statement's line items are retained for audit and excluded from every balance, and because void statements are excluded from the period-overlap test, the billing period is freed for a fresh draft. This is the difference between the two operations: a revision replaces a bill, a void withdraws it.

**Settlement.** A `JointInterestAccountant` settles a statement by supplying `settled_on`, which must be on or before the logical clock's date. `settled_on` is a request field only: it is required and validated, but it is not stored on the statement and no `settled_on` field appears on the statement resource the service returns. The statement's `statement_status` must be `ISSUED` and no dispute against it may be `OPEN`, `OPERATOR_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_SETTLEABLE` with status 409 — cash may be applied to a contested bill only after the contest closes. There is deliberately no due-date precondition on this path, because partners often pay early. The obligation run provides the second, deemed path: an `ISSUED` statement with no live dispute whose `payment_due_date` the clock has passed is moved to `SETTLED` by the run. Either way `statement_status` becomes `SETTLED`, which remains inside `billed_to_date_cents`, and `SETTLED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Revenue Distribution and Owner Disbursements

Cost flows out to partners; revenue flows back. The revenue side of the joint account is organised by well-month, and one month of proceeds is booked as a single record before it is distributed.

**Booking a month.** A `JointInterestAccountant` books proceeds against a well. The body carries `production_month` in `YYYY-MM` form (matching a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month from `01` through `12`), `gross_revenue_cents` (greater than zero), `volume_units` (greater than zero — barrels when the well's `product_type` is `OIL`, MMBtu when it is `GAS`) and `sale_date`, the date the operator received the purchaser's proceeds. The well's `well_status` must be `PRODUCING` or `SHUT_IN`, else `ERR_WELL_NOT_PRODUCING` with status 409: revenue cannot be booked against a well that never produced or that has been plugged. A shut-in well may still book proceeds, because sales of previously produced volumes can settle after the wellhead is closed. The `production_month` must be unique for the well across every revenue record, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCTION_MONTH` with status 409.

On success `distribution_status` is `ACCRUED`, `released_at` is null, and `severance_tax_cents`, `net_proceeds_cents`, `total_royalty_cents` and `total_disbursed_cents` are all 0 until release. The `statutory_due_date` is computed immediately: take the last calendar day of `production_month` and advance it by 60 days when the well's `product_type` is `OIL`, or by 90 days when it is `GAS`. This is the statutory deadline for paying the owners their share, and it is the reference point for late interest later.

**Releasing the month.** Release is the operation that runs the waterfall and creates the payables. It takes no body. Four preconditions apply: `distribution_status` must be `ACCRUED`, else `ERR_REVENUE_NOT_ACCRUED` with status 409; the well's `pooling_status` must be `POOLED_EFFECTIVE`; the well's `allocation_factor_total` must be exactly 1.000000, so the acreage basis for splitting the money is complete; and the well must carry at least one `ACTIVE` position, which is what makes the normalized net revenue interests sum to exactly 1.000000 by construction. The input set is snapshotted at release: only `COMMITTED` allocations and only `ACTIVE` positions participate. `WITHDRAWN` allocations and `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED` positions are excluded entirely.

The waterfall then runs in four tiers, each of which must exhaust its own total exactly.

*Tier 1 — severance tax and the split among leases.* The severance tax rate is 4.6 percent of gross when the well's `product_type` is `OIL` and 7.5 percent when it is `GAS`. `severance_tax_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up; `net_proceeds_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` less `severance_tax_cents`. The committed allocations of the well, taken in ascending `created_at` order, each receive a raw pool of `net_proceeds_cents` multiplied by that allocation's `allocation_factor`; each raw pool is rounded half up; and the signed difference between `net_proceeds_cents` and the sum of the rounded pools is added to the pool of the allocation with the largest `allocation_factor`, breaking ties by the earliest `created_at`. The lease pools therefore sum to `net_proceeds_cents` exactly.

*Tier 2 — the lessor's royalty.* From each lease pool, the royalty due the mineral owner is that pool multiplied by the lease's `royalty_rate`, rounded half up. `total_royalty_cents` on the revenue record is the sum of those amounts across the committed leases. What remains of each pool after its royalty is the working-interest pool for that lease.

*Tier 3 — the working-interest owners, on renormalized net revenue interest.* A position's raw `net_revenue_interest_pct` is already net of the lessor royalty that tier 2 has just taken, so applying it directly would leave part of the working-interest pool unallocated. It is therefore renormalized first. Taking the well's `ACTIVE` positions in ascending `created_at` order, the denominator is the sum of their `net_revenue_interest_pct` values; each owner's normalized interest is its own `net_revenue_interest_pct` divided by that denominator, rounded half up to six decimal places; and the signed difference between 1.000000 and the sum of the normalized values is added to the owner with the largest `net_revenue_interest_pct`, breaking ties by the earliest position `created_at`. Then, for each lease pool independently, each owner's raw share is that lease's working-interest pool multiplied by the owner's normalized interest, and `gross_share_cents` is that raw share rounded half up. The signed difference between the lease's working-interest pool and the sum of the rounded shares for that lease is added to the `gross_share_cents` of the owner with the largest `net_revenue_interest_pct`, ties broken by the earliest position `created_at`. Each lease pool is thus exhausted on its own; residuals are not pooled across leases.

*Tier 4 — non-consent recovery.* This is where the operator recoups what it advanced for owners who refused an AFE. Rows are processed in a strict order: by allocation `created_at` ascending, and within an allocation by position `created_at` ascending. For each row, `withheld_recovery_cents` is the **lesser** of that row's `gross_share_cents` and the owner's remaining `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents`; the remaining balance is then reduced by that amount before the next row is considered, so one owner's balance is consumed progressively across its rows and cannot be recovered twice. `net_amount_cents` is `gross_share_cents` less `withheld_recovery_cents`. After all rows are processed, each owner's `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` is written back as the balance that survived. The revenue record's `total_disbursed_cents` is the sum of `gross_share_cents` — the gross, not the net — across every disbursement created, because withholding moves money between joint-account accounts rather than out of the distribution.

The conservation invariant closes the month exactly: `gross_revenue_cents` equals `severance_tax_cents` plus `total_royalty_cents` plus `total_disbursed_cents`. Every cent of proceeds is accounted for as tax, royalty or owner distribution.

**What release creates.** One disbursement row is created per pair of committed allocation and active position, recording `revenue_id`, `owner_id`, `lease_id`, `gross_share_cents`, `withheld_recovery_cents` and `net_amount_cents`. An owner holding interest in a well pooled from three committed leases therefore receives three rows for one month, one per lease, which is what allows lease-level royalty and lease-level accounting to be traced. `distribution_status` becomes `RELEASED`, `released_at` is taken from the logical clock, each affected owner's `suspense_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the revenue record's `version` increments by 1.

**Suspense at release.** A new row starts `ACCRUED` and payable unless one of two conditions holds. If the owner's `tax_id_on_file` is false, the row is created `SUSPENDED` with `suspense_reason` of `MISSING_TAX_ID` — the operator may not pay proceeds it cannot report to the taxing authority. If instead the entire gross share was consumed by non-consent recovery, so that `net_amount_cents` is 0, the row is created `SUSPENDED` with `suspense_reason` of `NON_CONSENT_RECOVERY`. When both conditions hold at once, `MISSING_TAX_ID` wins, and this precedence matters beyond bookkeeping because it also selects the late-interest variant later. A third reason, `POOLING_TERMINATED`, is never set at release; it arises only from the lease-expiry cascade described in §3.1, which suspends `ACCRUED` rows of a released month retroactively while leaving `PAID` rows untouched.

A hold is lifted in one of two ways. A `ComplianceOfficer` may release a suspense hold directly, which returns `disbursement_status` to `ACCRUED`, clears `suspense_reason` and recomputes the owner's `suspense_balance_cents`. Alternatively, recording an owner compliance update with `tax_id_on_file` set to true automatically returns every row of that owner whose `suspense_reason` is `MISSING_TAX_ID` to `ACCRUED`, as described in §3.3; that automatic path is specific to the missing-tax-id reason and does not disturb rows held for the other two reasons.

**Payment and statutory late interest.** A `JointInterestAccountant` pays a row. The `disbursement_status` must be `ACCRUED`, else `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_PAYABLE` with status 409; `net_amount_cents` must be greater than zero, so a fully withheld row is never paid; and the parent revenue record must have `released_at` set with its date on or before the logical clock's date, else `ERR_PAYMENT_BEFORE_RELEASE` with status 422. The body may optionally carry `interest_basis` to force a variant.

Interest is a decay function of lateness, and which accrual start applies is the substantive question. When `interest_basis` is supplied in the request it is used as given. Otherwise the variant is chosen from the row's own suspense history: if the row has **ever** carried a `suspense_reason` of `MISSING_TAX_ID`, the basis is `FROM_PROCEEDS_RECEIPT`; in every other case it is `FROM_DUE_DATE`.

- Under `FROM_DUE_DATE`, days late are the whole days from `statutory_due_date` to the date part of `paid_at`, and the annual rate is 12 percent. Interest accrues only after the statutory grace period has run.
- Under `FROM_PROCEEDS_RECEIPT`, days late are the whole days from the day **after** the last calendar day of `production_month` to the date part of `paid_at`, and the annual rate is 6 percent. The rationale is that the operator was holding identified proceeds it simply could not pay out, so the clock starts when the money arrived rather than when the grace period lapsed.

In both variants a negative day count is treated as zero, so paying early yields no negative interest. `late_interest_cents` is `net_amount_cents` multiplied by the annual rate, multiplied by the day count, divided by a 365-day basis, and rounded half up to whole cents once, at the end — the day count and the rate are never rounded on the way through. The two variants trade a longer window against a halved rate, so for the same row and the same payment date neither is reliably the larger; both must be implemented exactly as specified rather than collapsed into a single rule. On success `interest_basis` is recorded, `paid_at` is taken from the logical clock, `disbursement_status` becomes `PAID` — a terminal state — and the row's `version` increments by 1. Note that `late_interest_cents` is a reported figure computed at payment time; it does not alter `net_amount_cents`.

### 3.9. Assignments, Preferential Purchase Rights and the Conveyance of Interest

Working interest changes hands, and a joint operating agreement almost always gives the other partners a preferential purchase right: the chance to take the interest themselves, on the same terms, before an outsider does. The service models this as a notice, an election window, and a closing whose payee depends on how the window ended.

**Serving notice.** A `LandAdministrator` records a proposed assignment against a well. The body carries `assignor_owner_id`, `assignee_owner_id`, `transfer_wi_pct`, `transfer_nri_pct`, `sale_price_cents`, `notice_date` and `effective_date`. Both percentages must be greater than 0, no greater than 1, and expressed to exactly six decimal places; `sale_price_cents` must be greater than zero. Body foreign keys are resolved left to right — `assignor_owner_id` before `assignee_owner_id` — and a failure returns `ERR_INTEREST_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`. Then `notice_date` must be on or before `effective_date`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` with status 422. Both named parties must have an `owner_type` of `WORKING_INTEREST_PARTNER`, else `ERR_OWNER_TYPE_INVALID` with status 422 — a royalty or overriding-royalty owner holds no working interest and can neither convey nor receive one. The assignor must hold an `ACTIVE` position on the well, else `ERR_NOT_AN_INTEREST_HOLDER` with status 422. Finally the conveyance must fit inside what the assignor actually owns: `transfer_wi_pct` must not exceed the assignor's `ACTIVE` `working_interest_pct`, `transfer_nri_pct` must not exceed `transfer_wi_pct`, and `transfer_nri_pct` must not exceed the assignor's `ACTIVE` `net_revenue_interest_pct`. Any of those three failures returns `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_HOLDING` with status 422.

On success `ppr_deadline_date` is set to 30 days after `notice_date` — measured from the notice, not from the logical clock, so a notice recorded late in the system may open a window that has already closed. `ppr_eligible_count` is snapshotted as the number of `ACTIVE` positions on the well whose `preferential_right` is true and whose owner is neither `assignor_owner_id` nor `assignee_owner_id`: the seller obviously does not get to pre-empt itself, and a named assignee that already holds a preferential right is buying anyway. `ppr_waiver_count` is 0 and `ppr_holder_owner_id` is null. `bond_check_required_cents` is computed for the prospective grantee under the bond rule of §3.3, evaluated as if the conveyance were already recorded, so the notice itself discloses the bonding the buyer will have to have posted. `transfer_status` is `NOTICED` — except in the empty-candidate-pool case: when `ppr_eligible_count` is 0 there is nobody to elect, and the transfer is created directly in `PPR_WAIVED`, ready to close.

**The election window.** Each eligible holder responds through a `PartnerRepresentative` with a single `election` value of `EXERCISE` or `WAIVE`. The transfer's `transfer_status` must be `NOTICED`, else `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_PENDING` with status 409; the electing owner must not already have an election recorded on this transfer, else `ERR_PPR_ELECTION_ALREADY_SUBMITTED` with status 409; the logical clock's date must be on or before `ppr_deadline_date`, else `ERR_PPR_WINDOW_EXPIRED` with status 422; and the electing `X-User-ID` must hold an `ACTIVE` position on the transfer's well with `preferential_right` true while being neither `assignor_owner_id` nor `assignee_owner_id` — that is, it must be one of the holders the notice was served on — else `ERR_PPR_HOLDER_INVALID` with status 403.

An `EXERCISE` ends the window at once: `transfer_status` becomes `PPR_EXERCISED` and `ppr_holder_owner_id` is set to the electing owner. The first holder to exercise takes the interest; later holders have nothing left to elect on, because the transfer is no longer `NOTICED`.

A `WAIVE` increments `ppr_waiver_count` by 1 and then depends on arithmetic. If this waiver is the last one outstanding — that is, if `ppr_waiver_count` after the increment equals `ppr_eligible_count` — `transfer_status` becomes `PPR_WAIVED` and the transfer is closeable. If waivers remain outstanding, `transfer_status` stays `NOTICED` and the transfer waits: this is a self-loop that changes the tally without changing the state. Either way `bond_check_required_cents` is recomputed for the prospective grantee and the transfer's `version` increments by 1.

Silence is also an answer. A transfer still `NOTICED` when the logical clock passes `ppr_deadline_date` is moved to `PPR_WAIVED` by the obligation run as a deemed waiver, leaving `ppr_holder_owner_id` null, and the run reports the count as `transfers_deemed_waived`. A partner who says nothing is treated exactly as a partner who waived.

**Closing.** A `LandAdministrator` closes the transfer, supplying `closing_date`. The `transfer_status` must be `PPR_WAIVED` or `PPR_EXERCISED`, else `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_CLOSEABLE` with status 409 — `CLOSED` is terminal and a still-`NOTICED` transfer is refused here rather than at the terminal-state check. The window must also genuinely be shut: either `ppr_waiver_count` equals `ppr_eligible_count`, meaning every holder waived, or the logical clock's date is strictly past `ppr_deadline_date`; otherwise `ERR_PPR_WINDOW_OPEN` with status 422. The grantee must have `tax_id_on_file` true, else `ERR_OWNER_NOT_COMPLIANT` with status 409, because an interest may not be conveyed to an owner the operator cannot report to the taxing authority. And last of all, the grantee's `bond_posted_cents` must be at least its `bond_check_required_cents`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOND` with status 402; the coverage test is inclusive, so a grantee posted at exactly the required figure closes.

Who the grantee **is** depends on the branch, and this is the substance of the fork-join. On the **waiver branch**, entered from `PPR_WAIVED`, the grantee is `assignee_owner_id`: the buyer named in the notice takes the interest, because nobody pre-empted. On the **preferential-right branch**, entered from `PPR_EXERCISED`, the grantee is `ppr_holder_owner_id` and **not** `assignee_owner_id` — the named assignee receives nothing at all, even though it was the party the notice was about. Every precondition above is applied to whichever party is the grantee on the branch actually taken.

The two branches converge on identical mechanics for the assignor. Its `ACTIVE` position is closed out with `effective_to` set to the closing date, which is an exclusive end, and a replacement `ACTIVE` row is created with `effective_from` set to that same date, giving half-open adjacency with no overlap. The replacement carries `working_interest_pct` reduced by `transfer_wi_pct` and `net_revenue_interest_pct` reduced by `transfer_nri_pct`, with `is_operator` and `preferential_right` copied across, so an assignor who was the operator remains the operator over its retained interest. The one exception is a **full** conveyance: when the assignor's `working_interest_pct` less `transfer_wi_pct` would be exactly 0, its position becomes `TERMINATED` rather than `SUPERSEDED` and **no** replacement row is created — and its `non_consent_recoverable_balance_cents` is carried onto the grantee's new `ACTIVE` row, conserved and never dropped, because an outstanding non-consent receivable runs with the interest rather than with the person.

The grantee side is where the branches differ in shape rather than in rule. If the grantee already holds an `ACTIVE` position on the well, that position is `SUPERSEDED` and re-created with the transferred percentages **added** to its own, again with `effective_to` and `effective_from` at the closing date. If it holds none, a brand-new `ACTIVE` row is created with exactly `transfer_wi_pct` and `transfer_nri_pct`, `is_operator` false and `preferential_right` false — a newcomer neither operates nor acquires a pre-emption right by the mere fact of buying in. On the preferential-right branch the second case can never arise, because a preferential-right holder by definition already owns an interest on the well, so that branch always takes the supersede-and-add path.

Two conserved quantities are the point of all this bookkeeping, and both hold identically on both branches: the sum of `working_interest_pct` over the well's `ACTIVE` positions remains exactly 1.000000, and the sum of `net_revenue_interest_pct` over those positions is unchanged by the conveyance. Interest is redistributed between assignor and grantee, never created or destroyed. Alongside that, `transfer_status` becomes `CLOSED`, the transfer's `effective_date` is **rewritten** to the supplied `closing_date` so the record reflects when the conveyance actually happened rather than when it was projected, `required_bond_cents` is recomputed for both parties under the rule of §3.3, the well's `consent_working_interest_pct` is recomputed because the ownership rows underneath it changed, the well's `version` increments by 1, and the transfer's `version` increments by 1. That well version bump is precisely why recording a new position carries mandatory optimistic concurrency: a client holding a division order read before a closing is holding a stale one, and §2.7 makes it fail rather than push the working-interest sum off 1.000000.
