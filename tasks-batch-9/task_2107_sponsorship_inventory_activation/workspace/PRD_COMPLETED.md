# Sponsorship Inventory & Activation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Sports properties sell the same scarce commodity twice: a finite number of signage faces, LED ribbon minutes, media spots, hospitality seats, licensing rights and activation footprints, priced once on a rate card and promised again, clause by clause, inside individual sponsorship contracts. The commercial team that runs this business today reconciles three disconnected artefacts — a spreadsheet rate card, a contract folder, and a shared calendar of who is standing in front of which LED board on which night — and discovers the contradictions only after the season has run. This API replaces that reconciliation with a single system of record for sponsorship inventory, contract entitlements, activation delivery and post-season reporting.

The system is built on a deliberate tension. Inventory is a conserved quantity: units held by one sponsor's booking are units no other sponsor can occupy, and every reservation, cancellation, delivery and preemption must return the ledger to a consistent total. Money, by contrast, is recognized twice at two different fidelities — once conservatively at signature, when the property books committed value against its season target, and once again after the post-season audit, when the in-kind and trade legs are re-valued and any shortfall is compensated with makegood credit. Because the same declared trade value can legitimately produce two different recognized amounts depending on which of the two orderings applies, the specification below pins the evaluation order of every calculation rather than leaving it to implementation taste.

Finally, the system is an execution tool as much as a commercial one. Activation staff are assigned to shifts, check in on site, and are released or marked absent when the occupation window closes; photographers and analysts capture proof of performance; auditors adjudicate that evidence, and a rejection reaches all the way back through the delivery rollup to the property's sellout percentage. Segregation of duties is enforced throughout: the operator who submits a deal may not countersign it, and the operator who captures evidence may not adjudicate it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, this section describes how every request is shaped, authenticated, ordered and rounded.

### 2.1. Access Control and Operator Identity

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Once the caller is authenticated, the role is matched against the roles authorized for the requested endpoint in §4; a caller whose role is not listed there is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The six roles are `PropertyAdmin`, `SponsorshipSeller`, `ContractApprover`, `ActivationManager`, `PerformanceAuditor` and `SponsorContact`. A `PropertyAdmin` owns the sellable estate — properties, rate cards, priced lines, physical and digital assets — and is the only role permitted to move the season clock. A `SponsorshipSeller` builds the deal: registering sponsor brands, drafting contracts, adding clause entitlements and submitting the result for approval. A `ContractApprover` disposes of submitted deals by returning, countersigning, activating, terminating or closing them. An `ActivationManager` runs delivery: scheduling and cancelling occupations of an asset, registering and rostering staff, checking staffers in, marking bookings delivered and capturing proof of performance. A `PerformanceAuditor` adjudicates captured evidence and reads the reporting surface. A `SponsorContact` is the buying brand itself.

For `PropertyAdmin`, `SponsorshipSeller`, `ContractApprover`, `ActivationManager` and `PerformanceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `countersigned_by_user_id` on a contract, and as `captured_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a proof of performance — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. For `SponsorContact` the rule is different: `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Sponsor`, and every contract-scoped operation compares the contract's `sponsor_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `SponsorContact` reading a single contract, requesting its ROI/ROO report, listing its surveys or filing a new survey against it must be the sponsor named on that contract; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). The same restriction applies to the contract list: the result set returned to a `SponsorContact` contains only contracts whose `sponsor_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a request that filters on any other sponsor is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0, and a supplied value must be zero or greater. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied in a form that does not parse as a whole number, is a body/parameter validation failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (422).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by the order in which the server wrote the rows. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` reflects the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned falls short of `total_count`. Every filter that names an identifier is applied as plain string equality and the identifier is never resolved, so a filter value matching no record yields an empty page rather than a 404.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Versioning

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix; wherever a URL contains an `id` path variable, that variable is the UUID of the resource at that position in the path. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written; `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` are read from the host's real time and not from the logical clock of §2.5. Instants the caller supplies — `season_start`, `season_end`, `term_start`, `term_end`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `shift_start` and `shift_end` — are stored and echoed back in exactly the string form the caller wrote, never re-formatted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that is set to 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative when a cascade reconciles a derived field. Two resource kinds are exceptions and are written once: a rate-card line and a sponsor survey carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a rate-card line or on a sponsor survey, and neither is ever returned for them.

### 2.5. The Season Clock

Every temporal precondition in this system — whether a season has opened, whether a contract term has started or ended, whether an occupation window has closed, whether a shift has begun — is evaluated against the server's logical clock, never against real wall-clock time. Until the clock is explicitly moved it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's wall clock. A `PropertyAdmin` repositions it by supplying `to_iso8601` to the clock endpoint mounted at `/_clock/set_current_utc` — forwards or backwards, with no ordering restriction — and the response is a `200 OK` whose `data` holds the single field `current_utc`, echoing the supplied string verbatim. Any system-stamped instant — a rate card's `published_at`, a booking's `delivered_at` — is the clock reading at the moment the write occurred, carried in that same verbatim string form rather than re-formatted to three fractional digits.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities in this system are integer counts of cents and are named with a `_cents` suffix. Wherever a monetary intermediate is not already an integer, it is rounded half up to a whole cent before it is stored or carried into the next step, and the specification below states exactly which intermediate is rounded and in which order — the ordering is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, because rounding a per-unit value before multiplying yields a different total than multiplying before rounding.

Percentages and index-style numbers are stored with 2 decimal places using the same round-half-up rule: `sellout_pct`, `delivery_pct`, `volume_discount_pct`, `renewal_likelihood_score`, `composite_score`, `roo_attainment_pct` and `cost_per_thousand_impressions_cents` all follow that rule. The single exception is `roi_ratio`, which is stored with 4 decimal places, again rounded half up. Every one of these travels in JSON as a bare number rather than a string, so a stored 0.00 comes back as `0.0` and a stored 1.50 as `1.5`. `seasonality_index` and `exclusivity_multiplier` are supplied by the operator with at most 2 decimal places and are used at the precision supplied. One calculation — the allocation of makegood credit — deliberately truncates rather than rounds, and §3.9 states where.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Shared Inventory and Pricing

Two write operations act on state that other operators also reshape, and both are protected by an explicit stale-version guard rather than by locking.

Scheduling an occupation of an inventory asset — the create operation mounted at `POST /asset-bookings` — requires `expected_inventory_asset_version` in the request body. It is not optional and there is no default: the caller must supply the `version` of the `InventoryAsset` as they last observed it. If that number differs from the asset's current `version`, the request is refused with `ERR_ASSET_VERSION_STALE` (412) and nothing is written. This holds even when the residual capacity would still admit the requested units, because a stale version means the caller's picture of `reserved_units_total`, of the surrounding buffer neighbourhood and of the asset's occupied windows predates an earlier accepted booking, cancellation, delivery or preemption on that same asset.

Countersigning a contract — the action mounted at `POST /contracts/{id}/countersign` — requires `expected_property_version` in the request body, again mandatory and again carrying the `version` of the owning `Property` as the approver last observed it. If it differs from the property's current `version`, the countersign is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_VERSION_STALE` (412). A property's version advances whenever a newer rate card is published — which supersedes the very card this countersign would pin — and whenever another countersign, termination, close or delivery moves `committed_gross_value_cents` or `delivered_media_value_cents`, so a stale property version means the approver is pricing the deal against a superseded card or against an inventory position that has already been sold into.

Both guards read a plain integer, and a missing or non-integer value is a body-shape failure at rung 5 (`ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`) rather than a stale-version failure. No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and any such field supplied elsewhere is an unknown field and is ignored.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Identifiers embedded in a URL are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. A request that names a rate card under a property that does not exist fails with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND` before the rate card is ever looked up. The 404 vocabulary is `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROOF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each naming the kind of resource that could not be found.

When several checks would fail on the same request, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fixed by the following priority ladder. Lower rungs are evaluated first and short-circuit everything below them.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Resolution of every identifier in the path, left to right — the matching 404.
4. Sponsor scoping for a `SponsorContact` — `ERR_SPONSOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
5. Request-body and query-parameter shape: required fields present, declared types, enum membership, numeric ranges, `measured_value` of zero for `PHOTO` evidence, `reject_reason` present when the decision is `REJECTED`, a season whose `season_end` is strictly after its `season_start`, and `limit`/`offset` inside their permitted ranges — `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. Unknown fields in a request body are ignored rather than rejected, and a request carrying no body at all, or a body the server cannot parse as JSON, is treated as an empty object — which fails only where the endpoint has a required field — while a JSON body that parses to anything other than an object is itself `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`.
6. Resolution of every foreign key supplied in the request body — the matching 404.
7. State legality: the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. A survey may only be filed against a contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`; a priced line may only be added while its card is `DRAFT`; an entitlement may only be added while its contract is `DRAFT`. Failures here are `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
8. Cross-entity state guards — `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_SELLING`, `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RATE_CARD`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ASSET_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_STAFF_INACTIVE` and `ERR_OPEN_CONTRACTS`.
9. Logical-clock gates — `ERR_SEASON_NOT_STARTED`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_STARTED`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_OPEN` and `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_STARTED`.
10. Version guards — `ERR_ASSET_VERSION_STALE` and `ERR_PROPERTY_VERSION_STALE`.
11. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
12. Category exclusivity — `ERR_CATEGORY_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`.
13. Referential coherence — `ERR_ASSET_MISMATCH` and `ERR_RATE_LINE_MISSING`.
14. Interval bounds — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422) for an occupation window or shift whose end is not strictly after its start, and `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM` (409) for an occupation window that escapes the contract term or a shift that escapes the booking window.
15. Neighbourhood separation — `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_CONFLICT` for the asset changeover buffer and `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_CONFLICT` for staff overlap and rest.
16. Capacity and completeness — `ERR_ASSET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_STAFFING_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAUSE_REF`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATE_LINE`, `ERR_NO_ENTITLEMENTS`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and `ERR_OPEN_BOOKINGS`.
17. The fiscal oversell ceiling — `ERR_INVENTORY_VALUE_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity Across the Sponsorship Graph

Derived values in this system are never left to drift. Whenever an event changes a quantity that some other row summarises, the summary is recomputed immediately as part of the same request, and each such recomputation is a mutation of the row it touches: `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` increases by 1, unconditionally, even when the recomputed figure lands back on the value the row already carried. Two writes are the exception and move `updated_at` and `version` only when a value actually changes — the sponsor row rewritten by renewal rescoring and by churn, which are two separate writes so a close that moves both the band and the status advances the sponsor's `version` twice, and an entitlement's `makegood_credit_cents` at close. A single delivery therefore ripples upward through four rows — the booking itself, the entitlement whose delivered units it feeds, the contract whose fulfilment and delivered media value it feeds, and the property whose delivered media value and sellout percentage it feeds.

Three conserved quantities deserve explicit mention because they are the invariants the whole design protects. First, `reserved_units_total` on an inventory asset is at all times the sum of `units_booked` over that asset's bookings whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`: creating a booking raises it, and cancellation, delivery and preemption each lower it by exactly the units the booking held, in every case incrementing the asset's `version` by 1. Second, `scheduled_minutes` on a staff member is at all times the total minutes across that member's assignments whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `CHECKED_IN`, so any move out of those two states subtracts that shift's whole-minute length. Third, `committed_gross_value_cents` on a property is at all times the sum of `total_recognized_value_cents` over that property's contracts whose `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`, so a contract entering or leaving that pair of states moves the property's position and, with it, `sellout_pct` and the property's `version`.

Because a property's `version` moves on every one of those reconciliations, an `expected_property_version` captured before any of them is stale by the time it is presented. That is the intended serialization point between publishing a rate card, countersigning one deal and countersigning the next.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this system may not be made by the operator who set them up. A contract may not be countersigned by the operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` when the contract was last submitted, and a proof of performance may not be adjudicated by the operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `captured_by_user_id` when the evidence was captured. In both cases the offending request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Note that returning a contract to draft clears `submitted_by_user_id`, so the constraint always names the operator behind the most recent submission and not an earlier one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Property and Its Season Envelope

A **Property** is a sellable sports franchise-season: the container that owns rate cards, physical and digital assets, and every contract sold against them. A `PropertyAdmin` registers one by supplying a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, a `sport_code` drawn from `FOOTBALL`, `BASKETBALL`, `SOCCER`, `HOCKEY` and `BASEBALL`, a `season_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive, the two instants `season_start` and `season_end` that bracket the season, and an `inventory_target_value_cents` of zero or more that states how much sellable value the season is expected to carry. `season_end` must be strictly after `season_start`. A `seasonality_index` may also be supplied — a multiplier between 0.50 and 2.00 with at most 2 decimal places that scales every rate-card unit price when this property's entitlements are valued — and it defaults to 1.00 when omitted. A freshly registered property opens in `PLANNING` with `committed_gross_value_cents` at 0, `delivered_media_value_cents` at 0 and `sellout_pct` at 0.00.

The property's `status` walks a strict one-way path: `PLANNING → SELLING → IN_SEASON → CLOSED`. Each move is requested by a `PropertyAdmin` naming the desired `to_status`. Moving to `SELLING` requires that at least one of the property's rate cards has already reached `PUBLISHED`, since there is nothing to sell against otherwise; without one the request fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RATE_CARD`. Moving to `IN_SEASON` requires that the season has actually opened — the logical clock must have reached `season_start` or passed it — and otherwise fails with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_STARTED`. Moving to `CLOSED` requires that the property carries no contract whose `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`; if one remains, the request fails with `ERR_OPEN_CONTRACTS`. Any other requested move — a backwards step, a skip such as `PLANNING` straight to `IN_SEASON`, or a repeat of the state the property already holds — is rejected unconditionally with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and in that case no field is written and the property's `version` does not change. Each accepted move writes the new `status` and increments the property's `version` by 1.

Two of the property's money fields and the percentage derived from them are never written by an operator; they are recomputed by the system whenever a contract of the property is countersigned, terminated or closed, and whenever a delivery or a proof rejection changes a contract's delivered media value. `committed_gross_value_cents` is the sum of `total_recognized_value_cents` across those contracts of the property whose `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`. `delivered_media_value_cents` is the sum of the contracts' own `delivered_media_value_cents` across those contracts whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`. Contracts in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `TERMINATED` never contribute to either figure. `sellout_pct` is 0.00 when `inventory_target_value_cents` is 0; otherwise it is 100 multiplied by `committed_gross_value_cents`, divided by `inventory_target_value_cents`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Every write of these three fields increments the property's `version` by 1.

### 3.2. Pricing the Inventory: Rate Cards and Publication

A **RateCard** is a versioned price list for one property's inventory classes. A `PropertyAdmin` opens one against a property by giving it a `label` of 1 to 80 characters and an `effective_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive; the card is born in `DRAFT` with `line_count` at 0, `published_at` null and `superseded_by_rate_card_id` null, and its `property_id` is taken from the position of the property in the request path. Cards can later be enumerated and narrowed by `property_id`, by `status`, or by `superseded_by_rate_card_id` to find every card that a given card displaced.

Prices live on the card's lines. A **RateCardLine** prices exactly one inventory class and is added only while the owning card is still `DRAFT` — adding a line to a `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` card is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The line names its `asset_class` from `SIGNAGE`, `DIGITAL_BOARD`, `MEDIA_SPOT`, `HOSPITALITY`, `IP_RIGHTS` and `ACTIVATION_SPACE`, and carries a `base_unit_price_cents` of at least 1 as the list price per unit before any adjustment. A card may hold at most one line per class; a second line for a class the card already prices is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATE_LINE`. Volume pricing is expressed through `tier_breaks`, an optional array that defaults to empty and holds between 0 and 5 entries, each an object with a `min_units` integer of at least 1 and a `unit_price_cents` integer of at least 1; the `min_units` values within one line must all be distinct. An `exclusivity_multiplier` between 1.00 and 2.00 with at most 2 decimal places, defaulting to 1.00, records the premium this class commands when the buying contract locks its category. Adding a line raises the owning card's `line_count` by 1 and increments that card's `version` by 1; the line itself is written once and carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and it records its owning `rate_card_id` from the request path.

Publication is what makes a card sellable, and it is also the moment the property's pricing view moves. A card must carry at least one line to be published; a card with `line_count` of 0 is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` before any state check runs. The owning property must be in `PLANNING`, `SELLING` or `IN_SEASON` — publishing against a `CLOSED` property is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and so is publishing a card that has already left `DRAFT`, whether it now sits in `PUBLISHED` or in `SUPERSEDED`. A successful publication drives the card `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and then cascades: the property's previously published card, of which there is at most one, is driven `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_by_rate_card_id` set to the id of the card that just displaced it. Contracts that were already pinned to the displaced card keep their `rate_card_id` unchanged — a signed deal's pricing survives supersession. Finally, because the property's published-card pointer has moved, the property's `version` increments by 1, which invalidates any `expected_property_version` an approver captured before this publication.

### 3.3. The Inventory Estate

An **InventoryAsset** is a concrete thing a sponsor can occupy: a ribbon board, a hospitality suite, a media spot rotation, a plaza footprint. A `PropertyAdmin` creates one under a property, supplying a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `asset_class` from the same six-value vocabulary the rate card uses, and a `capacity_units` of at least 1 stating how many units may be occupied by intersecting bookings at any instant. The asset's `property_id` comes from the path. Three optional operational settings shape how the asset behaves under load. `changeover_buffer_minutes`, an integer from 0 to 240 defaulting to 0, is the idle gap the property insists on between two non-intersecting occupations that belong to different contracts — the time it takes to strike one sponsor's dressing and hang the next. `requires_staffing`, a boolean defaulting to false, marks assets that cannot be reported as delivered unless activation staff were physically present. `min_staff_count`, an integer from 0 to 20 defaulting to 0, is how many of them had to be checked in. The asset's own `status` may be supplied at creation and is one of `AVAILABLE`, `MAINTENANCE` or `RETIRED`, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`; only an `AVAILABLE` asset accepts new occupations. A new asset opens with `reserved_units_total` at 0. Assets can be enumerated and narrowed by `property_id`, by `asset_class` or by `status`.

### 3.4. Sponsors, Draft Contracts and Clause-Level Entitlements

A **Sponsor** is the buying brand. A `SponsorshipSeller` registers one with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `category` drawn from `BEVERAGE`, `AUTOMOTIVE`, `FINANCIAL`, `TELECOM`, `APPAREL` and `TECH`, and a `contact_email` that must contain an `@` character. The brand opens as a `PROSPECT` with `renewal_likelihood_score` at 0.00 and `renewal_band` at `LOW`. Its `status` becomes `ACTIVE` the first time any of its contracts is countersigned — only a brand still sitting at `PROSPECT` is promoted, so one that has already reached `ACTIVE` or `CHURNED` is left exactly where it is — and it may later become `CHURNED` under the close-out conditions described in §3.9. The category is not decoration: it is the axis along which exclusivity is enforced.

A **Contract** binds one sponsor to one property for a term. A `SponsorshipSeller` drafts it by naming the `sponsor_id` and `property_id` it links — both must resolve, failing with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND` respectively — together with `term_start` and `term_end`. The term must sit inside the property's season: `term_start` must be at or after the property's `season_start`, `term_end` must be strictly after `term_start`, and `term_end` must be at or before the property's `season_end`; a term that violates any of these three is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`, but only once both foreign keys have resolved — `sponsor_id` first, then `property_id` — so an identifier that does not resolve is reported ahead of any term problem. Three further inputs are optional. `exclusivity_scope` is either `NONE` or `CATEGORY` and defaults to `NONE`; `CATEGORY` locks the sponsor's category on that property for the term and, as §3.5 explains, also unlocks the exclusivity premium on every priced line. `in_kind_declared_value_cents`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 0, is the face value of the trade component as the sponsor declares it. `in_kind_category` selects which haircut that trade attracts and is one of `MEDIA_TRADE`, `PRODUCT`, `SERVICES` or `TICKETS`, defaulting to `PRODUCT`. A new contract opens in `DRAFT` with `fulfillment_status` at `NOT_STARTED`, `revision_round` at 0, `rate_card_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `countersigned_by_user_id` all null, and every money field — `gross_entitlement_value_cents`, `volume_discount_pct`, `cash_value_cents`, `in_kind_recognized_value_cents`, `total_recognized_value_cents`, `delivered_media_value_cents` and `makegood_credit_cents` — at zero. Contracts can be enumerated and narrowed by `sponsor_id`, by `property_id`, by the pinned `rate_card_id`, or by `status`.

What the property actually owes the sponsor is written clause by clause. An **Entitlement** is one contractual clause pointing at one asset, and it may only be added while its contract is still `DRAFT`; adding one to a contract that has left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The seller supplies the `inventory_asset_id` of the asset that satisfies the clause — which must resolve, failing with `ERR_INVENTORY_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, though it is never checked against the contract's own `property_id` — a `clause_ref` of 1 to 24 characters such as `4.2(a)`, and a `promised_units` of at least 1. The clause reference must be unique within its contract; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAUSE_REF`. On creation the entitlement copies its `asset_class` from the linked asset — that copy, and not the rate card, is what later selects the priced line — takes its `contract_id` from the request path, and opens in `PENDING` with `unit_value_cents`, `gross_value_cents`, `delivered_units` and `makegood_credit_cents` all at 0 and `delivery_pct` at 0.00. Nothing is priced yet; pricing happens at countersignature. Entitlements can be enumerated and narrowed by `contract_id`, by `inventory_asset_id` or by `status`.

### 3.5. Submission, Revision Rounds and Countersignature

Once the clauses are written, a `SponsorshipSeller` submits the deal — any of them, since the server never compares the caller against whoever drafted the contract — driving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Ahead of everything else the contract must actually be `DRAFT`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Three further conditions then gate the submission and are evaluated in this order. The owning property must be in `SELLING` or `IN_SEASON`; a property still in `PLANNING`, or already `CLOSED`, yields `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_SELLING`. The contract must carry at least one entitlement, or the submission fails with `ERR_NO_ENTITLEMENTS`. And the contract's `revision_round` must be at most 3; once it has reached 4 the revision loop is exhausted and the submission fails with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. A successful submission stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`.

A `ContractApprover` who is not satisfied returns the deal, driving it `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` with an optional free-text `reason` that is validated as a string but persisted nowhere — the same holds for the `reason` accepted by termination and by booking cancellation. There is no precondition beyond the contract actually being `SUBMITTED`. The return raises `revision_round` by 1 and clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, so the next submission establishes a fresh submitter. Three completed revision rounds are permitted; the fourth return leaves `revision_round` at 4, and any attempt to resubmit after that is refused.

Countersignature is the commercial commitment, and it is the single densest operation in the system. The approver supplies `expected_property_version` and nothing else. The checks run in a fixed order. First the contract must actually be `SUBMITTED`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Then the owning property must have exactly one rate card in `PUBLISHED`; with none, the request is `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RATE_CARD`. Then `expected_property_version` must equal the property's current `version`, or the request is `ERR_PROPERTY_VERSION_STALE`. Then the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then category exclusivity is tested: the countersign is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` when some other contract of the same property, whose `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`, belongs to a sponsor of the same `category` as this contract's sponsor and either that other contract holds `exclusivity_scope` of `CATEGORY` or the contract now being countersigned holds it. Then the published card must carry a priced line for every distinct `asset_class` appearing among the contract's entitlements; a missing line is `ERR_RATE_LINE_MISSING`. Only after all of those does the money get computed, and the resulting figure is finally tested against the property's oversell ceiling.

Pricing runs clause by clause first. For each entitlement of the contract — and at this point none can be `WAIVED`, so every clause is priced — the system locates the line on the pinned card whose `asset_class` equals the entitlement's `asset_class`. The starting unit price is that line's `base_unit_price_cents`. The line's `tier_breaks` are then considered in ascending order of `min_units`, and each break whose `min_units` is less than or equal to the entitlement's `promised_units` replaces the running unit price with its own `unit_price_cents`; the boundary is inclusive, so an entitlement promising exactly the break's `min_units` earns the break. In effect, the highest break the promised volume reaches wins, and if the promised volume reaches none of them the base price stands. The exclusivity factor is the line's `exclusivity_multiplier` when the contract's `exclusivity_scope` is `CATEGORY`, and 1.00 otherwise. The entitlement's `unit_value_cents` is then the tiered unit price multiplied by the property's `seasonality_index` and by that exclusivity factor, rounded half up to a whole cent. The rounding order is pinned: the per-unit figure is rounded first, and only then is `gross_value_cents` obtained by multiplying `unit_value_cents` by `promised_units`, a multiplication that is exact and is not rounded again.

The contract-level rollup then runs in five pinned steps. Step one sets `gross_entitlement_value_cents` to the sum of `gross_value_cents` over the contract's entitlements whose `status` is not `WAIVED`. Step two selects `volume_discount_pct` from that gross alone — never from the headline total, even though the total is the number the deal is announced at: a gross of 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars) or more earns 0.12, a gross of at least 2,500,000 cents earns 0.08, a gross of at least 1,000,000 cents earns 0.04, and anything below one million cents earns 0.00; each threshold is inclusive. Step three sets `cash_value_cents` to `gross_entitlement_value_cents` less the discount amount, where the discount amount is `gross_entitlement_value_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent. Step four values the trade leg into `in_kind_recognized_value_cents`. Step five sets `total_recognized_value_cents` to `cash_value_cents` plus `in_kind_recognized_value_cents`. The dependency chain only looks circular; it is not, because the discount tier is keyed on the pre-discount, cash-only gross and never on the total.

The trade leg is valued from two inputs and one derived ceiling. The haircut rate is fixed by `in_kind_category`: `MEDIA_TRADE` is discounted by 0.50, `SERVICES` by 0.40, `PRODUCT` by 0.35 and `TICKETS` by 0.20. The ceiling is `cash_value_cents` multiplied by 0.25 — a quarter of the cash leg — rounded half up to a whole cent. At countersignature the **conservative** ordering applies: `in_kind_declared_value_cents` is multiplied by one minus the haircut rate and rounded half up to a whole cent, and `in_kind_recognized_value_cents` is the smaller of that haircut figure and the ceiling. In other words, the haircut is taken first and the ceiling then bites on an already-discounted number. §3.9 describes the second, audited ordering that replaces this figure at close.

Before anything is persisted, the fiscal ceiling is tested. The property's current `committed_gross_value_cents` plus this contract's freshly computed `total_recognized_value_cents` must be no greater than `inventory_target_value_cents` multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to a whole cent — a ten percent oversell allowance over the season target. Exceeding it aborts the countersign with `ERR_INVENTORY_VALUE_EXCEEDED` and leaves every field untouched.

An accepted countersignature pins `rate_card_id` to the id of the property's published card — pinned once, never re-pinned, and unaffected by any later supersession — stamps `countersigned_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, writes the entitlement and contract money fields computed above, and drives the contract `SUBMITTED → COUNTERSIGNED`. A sponsor still sitting at `PROSPECT` is promoted to `ACTIVE`. Finally the property's committed position is recomputed, refreshing `committed_gross_value_cents` and `sellout_pct` and incrementing the property's `version` by 1 — which is exactly what makes any other approver's previously captured `expected_property_version` stale.

Activation follows once the season and the term have both begun. A `ContractApprover` drives the contract `COUNTERSIGNED → ACTIVE` with no request body. The owning property must be `IN_SEASON`, or the request fails with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_SELLING`, and the logical clock must have reached `term_start` or passed it, or it fails with `ERR_TERM_NOT_STARTED`. Any lifecycle action requested from a state that does not declare it — submitting an already-`SUBMITTED` contract, countersigning a `DRAFT` one, activating an `ACTIVE` one, closing a `COUNTERSIGNED` one, terminating a `CLOSED` one, or any action at all against a `CLOSED` or `TERMINATED` contract — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and fires no side effect.

### 3.6. Scheduling Occupations: Asset Bookings and Conflict Geometry

An **AssetBooking** is the operational instrument that turns a promise into an occupation: it says that a named clause takes a stated number of units of a named asset for a stated window. An `ActivationManager` creates one by supplying the `entitlement_id` of the clause it delivers against, the `inventory_asset_id` of the asset it occupies, the `window_start` and `window_end` instants that bound the occupation, a `units_booked` count of at least 1, and the `expected_inventory_asset_version` guard described in §2.7. The occupation window is treated as half-open: `window_start` is inside the window and `window_end` is not, so a booking that ends exactly when another begins does not intersect it.

The checks run in a fixed order. Both foreign keys must resolve, failing with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVENTORY_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`. The contract that owns the entitlement must be `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the asset must be `AVAILABLE`, or it fails with `ERR_ASSET_UNAVAILABLE` — an asset in `MAINTENANCE` or `RETIRED` takes no new occupations. Next the version guard is applied, yielding `ERR_ASSET_VERSION_STALE` on a mismatch. Next the supplied `inventory_asset_id` must equal the entitlement's own `inventory_asset_id`, since a clause can only be delivered on the asset it names; a divergence is `ERR_ASSET_MISMATCH`. Then the window itself is checked: `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422), and the whole window must lie inside the owning contract's term, between `term_start` and `term_end` inclusive, or it fails with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM` (409).

Two geometric constraints then guard the asset. The first is the changeover buffer. Consider every other booking already on this asset whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED`, that does **not** intersect the new half-open window, and whose entitlement belongs to a **different** contract than the new booking's. For each such neighbour the idle gap must be at least the asset's `changeover_buffer_minutes`: when the new window follows the neighbour, the gap is measured from the neighbour's `window_end` to the new `window_start`; when the new window precedes it, the gap is measured from the new `window_end` to the neighbour's `window_start`. A gap shorter than the buffer is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Neighbours belonging to the same contract are exempt — a sponsor may run its own dressings back to back — and intersecting neighbours are not tested here at all, because they are the capacity check's business.

The second constraint is capacity. Sum `units_booked` over every booking on the asset whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` and whose half-open window intersects the new one, add the new `units_booked`, and the result must be no greater than the asset's `capacity_units`; exceeding it is `ERR_ASSET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note that both geometric rules count `DELIVERED` bookings as well as `SCHEDULED` ones — a face that was physically occupied last night still constrains the changeover and the concurrency — while `CANCELLED` and `PREEMPTED` bookings never constrain anything.

An accepted booking opens in `SCHEDULED` with `proof_state` at `NONE`, `verified_impressions` at 0 and `delivered_at` null. It raises the asset's `reserved_units_total` by `units_booked` and increments the asset's `version` by 1 — which is precisely what makes the next writer's stale `expected_inventory_asset_version` fail. Bookings can be enumerated and narrowed by `entitlement_id`, by `inventory_asset_id` or by `status`.

Cancellation is the operator-initiated exit and takes an optional `reason`. It is permitted only while the owning entitlement's contract is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. It drives the booking `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, drops the asset's `reserved_units_total` by exactly the `units_booked` the booking held — restoring the value the asset carried before the booking was created — and increments the asset's `version` by 1. Every one of the booking's staff assignments still sitting at `ASSIGNED` or `CHECKED_IN` is moved to `RELEASED`, lowering each staffer's `scheduled_minutes` accordingly. No entitlement or contract money changes, because a `SCHEDULED` booking never contributed to `delivered_units` in the first place. Requesting delivery or cancellation of a booking that has already reached `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED` or `PREEMPTED` is rejected unconditionally with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.7. Staffing the Activation and Working the Shift

A **StaffMember** is an activation staffer on the property's roster. An `ActivationManager` registers one with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `hourly_rate_cents` of zero or more, and an optional `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` that defaults to `ACTIVE`. A new staffer opens with `scheduled_minutes` at 0, and that number is maintained thereafter as the total whole-minute length of every one of that member's assignments whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `CHECKED_IN`.

A **StaffAssignment** rosters one staffer onto one shift inside one booking. The `ActivationManager` names the `staff_member_id` — which must resolve, failing with `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` — together with `shift_start`, `shift_end` and a `role_label` of 1 to 60 characters such as `Brand Ambassador`; the assignment takes its `asset_booking_id` from the booking named in the request path. Only an `ACTIVE` staffer may be rostered; an `INACTIVE` one yields `ERR_STAFF_INACTIVE`. The booking's own `status` is not tested at rostering time — only the staffer's availability and the shift's geometry gate the write. The shift must be well formed — `shift_end` strictly after `shift_start`, or `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422) — and must lie inside the booking's occupation window, between `window_start` and `window_end` inclusive, or `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM` (409).

Shift geometry deliberately differs from booking geometry. Where occupation windows are half-open, shifts are treated as **closed** intervals, so two shifts that merely touch at an endpoint do collide: a new shift conflicts with an existing one when the new `shift_start` is at or before the existing `shift_end` and the existing `shift_start` is at or before the new `shift_end`. Beyond outright collision, two non-touching shifts of the same staffer must be separated by at least 45 minutes of rest. Both tests are evaluated only against that same staff member's assignments whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `CHECKED_IN`; `NO_SHOW` and `RELEASED` shifts constrain nothing. A violation of either test is reported as `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_CONFLICT`. An accepted assignment opens at `ASSIGNED` and raises that staffer's `scheduled_minutes` by the shift's length in whole minutes — the elapsed seconds divided by 60 and truncated downward. Assignments can be enumerated and narrowed by `asset_booking_id`, by `staff_member_id` or by `status`.

On the day, the `ActivationManager` checks the staffer in, driving the assignment `ASSIGNED → CHECKED_IN`. Three conditions gate it: the assignment must itself still be `ASSIGNED`, so checking in one already at `CHECKED_IN`, `NO_SHOW` or `RELEASED` fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the owning booking must still be `SCHEDULED`, or the request likewise fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and the logical clock must have reached the assignment's `shift_start` or passed it, or it fails with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_STARTED`. No contract or property state is consulted. Checking in changes nothing but the assignment's own `status`; the shift is still counted in `scheduled_minutes`, because both `ASSIGNED` and `CHECKED_IN` are working states.

### 3.8. Delivery, Proof of Performance and Adjudication

Delivery is the moment an occupation becomes revenue. An `ActivationManager` drives a booking `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED` once three conditions hold, evaluated in this order: the owning entitlement's contract must be `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the logical clock must have reached the booking's `window_end` or passed it, or it fails with `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_OPEN`; and when the occupied asset has `requires_staffing` set to true, the number of this booking's assignments whose `status` is `CHECKED_IN` must be at least the asset's `min_staff_count`, or it fails with `ERR_STAFFING_INCOMPLETE`. Assets with `requires_staffing` false skip the staffing test entirely regardless of `min_staff_count`.

An accepted delivery stamps `delivered_at` with the current logical-clock instant and then unwinds the roster: every one of the booking's assignments still sitting at `ASSIGNED` becomes `NO_SHOW`, because the shift ended without anyone reporting, while every assignment at `CHECKED_IN` becomes `RELEASED`. Both outcomes lower that staffer's `scheduled_minutes` by the shift's whole-minute length; they differ only in the label they leave behind. The occupation itself is over, so the asset's `reserved_units_total` drops by `units_booked` and the asset's `version` increments by 1. From there the value cascade runs upward: the owning entitlement recomputes its delivery figures, the owning contract recomputes its fulfilment dimension and its delivered media value, and the property recomputes its own `delivered_media_value_cents`, incrementing the property's `version` by 1.

The delivery rollup itself is a pure recomputation, not an increment, and it is defined identically no matter which event triggers it. For one entitlement, `delivered_units` is the sum of `units_booked` over that entitlement's bookings whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `proof_state` is anything other than `DEFICIENT`; bookings that are `SCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED` or `PREEMPTED` never count, and neither do deficient ones. `delivery_pct` is 100 multiplied by `delivered_units`, divided by `promised_units`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The entitlement's `status` follows directly from those numbers: a `WAIVED` entitlement stays `WAIVED`; otherwise zero delivered units means `PENDING`, delivered units below `promised_units` means `PARTIAL`, and delivered units at or above `promised_units` means `FULFILLED`. Because the rule is a pure function of the current facts, an entitlement moves `PENDING → PARTIAL` on a first partial delivery, `PARTIAL → FULFILLED` when the promise is met, and back `PARTIAL → PENDING` when a rejected proof strips the only delivered booking's units away again.

The contract's second state dimension follows from the same rollup, and it is recomputed only while the contract's `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`. A contract with no entitlements at all sits at `FULFILLED`. Otherwise, if every entitlement is `FULFILLED` or `WAIVED` the `fulfillment_status` is `FULFILLED`; if every entitlement is `PENDING` or `WAIVED` it is `NOT_STARTED`; in every other mix it is `IN_PROGRESS`. This dimension moves `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS` as the first clause starts landing, `IN_PROGRESS → FULFILLED` when the last one lands, and `IN_PROGRESS → NOT_STARTED` again if every clause falls back to `PENDING` — for instance when the only delivered booking is made deficient by a rejected proof. It never touches the lifecycle `status`, and the lifecycle `status` never touches it, except at close. In the same rollup the contract's `delivered_media_value_cents` is set to the sum, over that contract's bookings whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `proof_state` is not `DEFICIENT`, of `units_booked` multiplied by the owning entitlement's `unit_value_cents`.

Evidence is captured against an occupation as a **ProofOfPerformance**, and the booking's own `status` is never tested: a `CANCELLED` or `PREEMPTED` booking accepts evidence exactly as a `DELIVERED` or `SCHEDULED` one does. The `ActivationManager` supplies an `evidence_type` of `PHOTO`, `IMPRESSIONS` or `SOCIAL`, a `measured_value` of zero or more counting impressions or engagements, and an optional `media_uri` pointing at the stored artefact, which defaults to null. Photographic evidence measures nothing, so a `PHOTO` proof carrying a non-zero `measured_value` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. The proof records its `asset_booking_id` from the request path, opens at `SUBMITTED`, stamps `captured_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, and leaves `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `reject_reason` null. Capturing evidence moves the booking's `proof_state` to `PENDING_REVIEW` unless it already stands at `VERIFIED` or `DEFICIENT`; either way the booking row is rewritten, so its `updated_at` refreshes and its `version` increments by 1 while `delivered_units` and every money field stay put. In general, a booking's `proof_state` is derived from its proofs by strict precedence: `DEFICIENT` whenever any of its proofs is `REJECTED`; otherwise `VERIFIED` whenever any is `VERIFIED`; otherwise `PENDING_REVIEW` whenever any is `SUBMITTED`; otherwise `NONE`.

Adjudication belongs to the `PerformanceAuditor`, who supplies a `decision` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` and, for a rejection, a `reject_reason`. The checks run in this order: a rejection without a `reject_reason` fails with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (422); a proof that has already reached `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, or whose owning booking is not `DELIVERED`, fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); and an auditor whose `X-User-ID` matches the proof's `captured_by_user_id` fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

The two decisions carry deliberately disjoint consequences. A verification drives the proof `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, stamps `adjudicated_by_user_id` with the auditor's `X-User-ID`, and — when the evidence type is `IMPRESSIONS` or `SOCIAL` — raises the booking's `verified_impressions` by that proof's `measured_value`, so that `verified_impressions` is always the sum of `measured_value` across the booking's verified impressions-or-social proofs. The booking's `proof_state` becomes `VERIFIED` unless an earlier rejection has already made it `DEFICIENT`. A `reject_reason` supplied alongside a `VERIFIED` decision is ignored and the proof's `reject_reason` stays null. Nothing else moves: `delivered_units` is unchanged, because a delivered, non-deficient booking was already being counted before the evidence was verified, and no entitlement, contract or property rollup runs — only the proof row and the booking row are rewritten, each advancing its own `version` by 1. A rejection drives the proof `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps `adjudicated_by_user_id`, stores the `reject_reason`, and then cascades in four hops. The booking's `proof_state` becomes `DEFICIENT`, and rejection dominates: a `VERIFIED` sibling proof does not restore the booking, and the deficiency is not reversible. That booking's units therefore leave the owning entitlement's `delivered_units`, so `delivery_pct` and the entitlement `status` are recomputed downward by the delivery rollup. The owning contract then recomputes its `fulfillment_status` and `delivered_media_value_cents`. Finally the property's `delivered_media_value_cents` is recomputed and the property's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Unwinding, Closing Out and the Reporting Surface

Not every deal survives the season. A `ContractApprover` may terminate a contract that is `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE`, optionally recording a `reason`; there is no other precondition, and the contract's `makegood_credit_cents` stays at 0 because a terminated deal earns no compensation. Termination unwinds the whole subtree in four hops. Every entitlement of the contract whose `status` is `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` moves to `WAIVED`; entitlements already `FULFILLED`, and any already `WAIVED`, are left alone. Each newly waived entitlement then drives its own `SCHEDULED` bookings `SCHEDULED → PREEMPTED` — a state reachable only through this cascade and never by operator request. Each preempted booking releases its hold, so the asset's `reserved_units_total` drops by that booking's `units_booked` and the asset's `version` increments by 1. Each preempted booking also moves its assignments sitting at `ASSIGNED` or `CHECKED_IN` to `RELEASED`, lowering each staffer's `scheduled_minutes` by the shift's whole-minute length. In parallel the property's committed position is recomputed: because a `TERMINATED` contract belongs to neither of the contributing status sets, it drops out of `committed_gross_value_cents`, `sellout_pct` is refreshed, and the property's `version` increments by 1. The contract's money fields are not recomputed after termination.

Closing is the orderly ending, and it applies only to an `ACTIVE` contract. Two conditions gate it, in this order: the logical clock must have reached `term_end` or passed it, or the request fails with `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED`; and no booking belonging to any of the contract's entitlements may still be `SCHEDULED`, or it fails with `ERR_OPEN_BOOKINGS` — every occupation must have been delivered, cancelled or preempted before the books can be shut.

Close-out then executes a fixed sequence. First the trade leg is re-valued under the **audited** ordering, which reverses the two operations used at signature: the ceiling — `cash_value_cents` multiplied by 0.25, rounded half up to a whole cent — is applied to the raw `in_kind_declared_value_cents` first, taking whichever of the two is smaller, and only then is that capped figure multiplied by one minus the category haircut rate and rounded half up to a whole cent. The haircut rates are unchanged (0.50 for `MEDIA_TRADE`, 0.40 for `SERVICES`, 0.35 for `PRODUCT`, 0.20 for `TICKETS`). Whenever the ceiling binds, the audited figure is strictly lower than the conservative one booked at signature. The new `in_kind_recognized_value_cents` replaces the old, and `total_recognized_value_cents` is set again to `cash_value_cents` plus the new in-kind figure; `gross_entitlement_value_cents`, `volume_discount_pct`, `cash_value_cents` and `delivered_media_value_cents` are not recomputed at close.

Second, makegood credit is allocated, while entitlement statuses still reflect what was actually delivered. For every entitlement of the contract whose `status` is not `WAIVED`, its shortfall value is the greater of zero and `promised_units` minus `delivered_units`, multiplied by that entitlement's `unit_value_cents`. The total shortfall is the sum of those shortfall values. The credit pool is the smaller of that total shortfall and 15 percent of `cash_value_cents` — that is, `cash_value_cents` multiplied by 0.15 and then truncated downward to a whole cent. If the total shortfall is zero, every entitlement's `makegood_credit_cents` and the contract's `makegood_credit_cents` are all 0 and nothing further is allocated. Otherwise the entitlements carrying a positive shortfall value are ordered by shortfall value descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier, and each in turn receives the credit pool multiplied by its own shortfall value, divided by the total shortfall, truncated downward to a whole cent. Truncating each share individually leaves a remainder — the credit pool less the sum of the truncated shares — and that entire remainder is added to the first entitlement in the ordering, the one with the largest shortfall. Summing first and rounding once is not equivalent and is not permitted. `WAIVED` entitlements take no part in this at all: they are absent from the ordering and keep whatever `makegood_credit_cents` they already carry. The contract's own `makegood_credit_cents` is set to the credit pool.

Third, the entitlement statuses are frozen: every entitlement still `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` moves to `SHORTFALL`, while entitlements already `FULFILLED` or `WAIVED` are untouched. Fourth, the contract's `fulfillment_status` is finalized to `SHORTFALL` if any entitlement ended in `SHORTFALL`, and to `FULFILLED` otherwise. Fifth, the contract's `status` becomes `CLOSED`. Sixth, the property is reconciled: a `CLOSED` contract no longer contributes to `committed_gross_value_cents` but does still contribute to `delivered_media_value_cents`, `sellout_pct` is refreshed, and the property's `version` increments by 1. Seventh and last, the sponsor's renewal outlook is rescored, and a sponsor whose recomputed band is `LOW` and who is left with no contract in `COUNTERSIGNED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `CHURNED`. However many of these steps write to the contract row, they land as a single rewrite of it, so a close advances the contract's `version` by exactly 1 — as does a countersignature, and as does a termination.

Sponsor sentiment reaches the system through the **SponsorSurvey**, filed by the `SponsorContact` against one of its own contracts. A survey may only be filed against a contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`; filing against a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERSIGNED` or `TERMINATED` contract is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The sponsor rates the deal on three integer scales from 1 to 10 — `satisfaction_score` for the relationship overall, `activation_quality_score` for what happened on site, and `reporting_quality_score` for the numbers that came back — and declares a `renewal_intent` of `NO`, `MAYBE` or `YES`. The survey records its `contract_id` from the request path and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, which by definition equals the contract's `sponsor_id`. A survey is written once and never revised: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Its `composite_score` is computed at filing: the `satisfaction_score` weighted at 0.40, the `activation_quality_score` at 0.35 and the `reporting_quality_score` at 0.25 are summed, that sum is multiplied by 10 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and then an intent adjustment of plus 10 for `YES`, zero for `MAYBE` and minus 15 for `NO` is added; the result is finally clamped into the range 0 to 100.

Renewal likelihood is recomputed for the sponsor both when a contract closes and whenever a survey is filed. The scoring always looks at a single **target** contract: the sponsor's contract whose `status` is `CLOSED` with the greatest `term_end`, ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier. If the sponsor has no closed contract at all, `renewal_likelihood_score` stays 0.00 and `renewal_band` stays `LOW`. Otherwise three factors are assembled from the target. The delivery factor is 100.00 when the target's non-`WAIVED` entitlements promise nothing at all; otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the sum of `delivered_units` over those entitlements, divided by the sum of `promised_units` over the same entitlements, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The reliability factor starts from a penalty: zero when the target's `cash_value_cents` is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the target's `makegood_credit_cents`, divided by its `cash_value_cents`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; reliability is then 100 less that penalty, with the penalty capped at 100 so reliability never goes below zero. The survey factor, used only when the target carries at least one survey, is the arithmetic mean of those surveys' `composite_score` values, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Two weightings then apply, and which one is used depends entirely on whether the target contract carries any survey. When at least one survey exists, the survey factor is weighted at 0.50, the delivery factor at 0.35 and the reliability factor at 0.15, and the three weighted terms are summed. When no survey exists, the survey term is absent rather than treated as zero, and the raw score is the delivery factor weighted at 0.75 plus the reliability factor weighted at 0.25 — so identical delivery data scores differently under the two variants. The raw score is clamped into the range 0 to 100 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places to produce `renewal_likelihood_score`. `renewal_band` follows from it directly: `HIGH` at 70 or above, `MEDIUM` at 40 or above but below 70, and `LOW` below 40.

The last surface is the ROI/ROO report, a read-only projection computed on demand for one contract that persists nothing. The caller may supply a `basis` query parameter of either `VERIFIED` or `GROSS`; when it is omitted the basis is `VERIFIED`, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. Under the verified basis only adjudicated evidence counts: the bookings that contribute are the contract's bookings whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `proof_state` is exactly `VERIFIED`, and the proofs that contribute are those of the contract's bookings whose `evidence_type` is `IMPRESSIONS` or `SOCIAL` and whose own `status` is `VERIFIED`. Under the gross basis unadjudicated evidence counts too: contributing bookings are those with `status` `DELIVERED` and `proof_state` of `NONE`, `PENDING_REVIEW` or `VERIFIED`, and contributing proofs are the impressions-or-social proofs whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`. Under neither basis does a `CANCELLED` or `PREEMPTED` booking ever count, and only the contract's own bookings and their proofs are considered. The proof sweep is not confined to the contributing bookings: a qualifying proof hanging off a still-`SCHEDULED` booking of the contract counts toward `impressions_total` under either basis even though that booking contributes nothing to `media_value_cents`.

From that selection the report returns eight fields. `contract_id` and `basis` echo what was asked. `media_value_cents` is the sum, over the contributing bookings, of `units_booked` multiplied by the owning entitlement's `unit_value_cents`. `impressions_total` is the sum of `measured_value` over the contributing proofs. `roi_ratio` is 0.0 when the contract's `total_recognized_value_cents` is 0, and otherwise `media_value_cents` divided by `total_recognized_value_cents`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. `roo_attainment_pct` measures promise attainment rather than money: it is 100.00 when the contract's non-`WAIVED` entitlements promise nothing, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the sum over those entitlements of the lesser of `delivered_units` and `promised_units`, divided by the sum of their `promised_units`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — capping each clause at its own promise so that over-delivery on one clause cannot mask a shortfall on another. `cost_per_thousand_impressions_cents` is null when `impressions_total` is 0, and otherwise the contract's `total_recognized_value_cents` divided by the number of thousands of impressions — that is, by `impressions_total` divided by 1000 — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Finally `makegood_credit_cents` reports the contract's own allocated credit, which is 0 until the contract closes.
