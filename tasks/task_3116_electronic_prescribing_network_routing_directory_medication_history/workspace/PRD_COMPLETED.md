# Eprescribing Network Routing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The Eprescribing Network Routing API governs the connective tissue of an electronic prescribing network: the directory of participants who may send and receive transactions, the message standards and service levels each participant endpoint supports, the routing of prescription orders across that directory, the aggregation of medication history under patient consent, and the settlement of the transaction fees the network charges for carrying all of it. The system exists because a prescription that cannot be routed is, clinically, a prescription that does not exist — a prescriber's intent only becomes therapy once a pharmacy has acknowledged receipt in a form its systems accept.

Two pressures shape the design. The first is clinical safety: controlled substances may only be signed after a prescriber has proved identity and cleared a second factor, and an order's fallback path to fax or print must be deliberate rather than accidental. The second is financial accuracy: the network bills in monthly cycles against tiered schedules, and an append-only fee ledger must remain internally conserved even as charges are corrected and reversed. Both pressures are enforced structurally rather than by convention — certifications gate production enablement, coverage invariants prevent a participant from quietly degrading its last working endpoint, and every fee reversal leaves both the original charge and its compensating row in the ledger.

This document specifies the resource model, the state machines that move orders and sessions through their lifecycles, the selection and pricing algorithms, the validation order, and the error vocabulary. It is written for the engineers implementing the service; the endpoint table in §4 is the HTTP contract, while §2 and §3 carry the behavioral rules those endpoints must satisfy.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting party, and `X-User-Role` names the operational capacity in which they act. The system recognizes exactly six roles: `NetworkOperator`, `ParticipantAdmin`, `Prescriber`, `Pharmacy`, `HubOperator`, and `PatientService`. A request that omits `X-User-Role`, or supplies a value that is not one of these six, is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and an HTTP 401. A request that names a valid role the operation does not authorize — or that supplies an `X-User-ID` which fails to resolve for a role whose identity is entity-backed — is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and an HTTP 403. The network metadata read at `/network/` and every test-driving path under `/_` are open: a request that reaches one of them without any `X-User-Role` at all is admitted rather than refused for the omission, and an entity-backed role arriving on such a path is never resolved against a participant's `id`. An `X-User-Role` that names something outside the six declared roles is still refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` even there.

The `ParticipantAdmin`, `Prescriber`, and `Pharmacy` roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ParticipantAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `NetworkParticipant` whose certification and directory the caller administers; ownership checks on any resource that references a `NetworkParticipant` compare that resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. The same convention holds for `Prescriber`: when `X-User-Role` is `Prescriber`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting prescriber clinic `NetworkParticipant`, and ownership is resolved against the participant identifier carried on the prescription being authored or signed. It holds identically for `Pharmacy`: when `X-User-Role` is `Pharmacy`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting pharmacy `NetworkParticipant`, and the ownership comparison runs against the prescription's receiving pharmacy reference.

The remaining three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it, but it never compares it to an entity's `id`, and no resource ownership is derived from it. When `X-User-Role` is `HubOperator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier with no backing entity. When `X-User-Role` is `PatientService`, `X-User-ID` is again opaque — consent grants and revocations are keyed on the patient identifier named in the request path, never on the caller's own identity.

Certain operations belong to a single role and no other. Only a `NetworkOperator` may suspend a participant, disable a directory endpoint, post a transaction fee entry, reverse a transaction fee entry, or drive the logical clock and its advance capability. Only a `ParticipantAdmin` may open or reopen a certification test window, record a certification test outcome, promote a participant to certified, or reactivate a degraded endpoint. Only a `Prescriber` may author a prescription, sign it, route it, push it to the fallback channel, cancel it, open and drive a signing session through identity proofing and two-factor verification, abandon a signing session, and register a patient. Only a `Pharmacy` may settle a benefit check at fill time. Only a `PatientService` operator may grant or revoke a consent. All other operations are shared across the role sets enumerated in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp. A read reports the current logical clock, which is what lets a caller correlate it with the temporal rules the request evaluated; a write is stamped with the real instant that completed it. An instant that falls on a whole second is written compactly as `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and a sub-second instant carries three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. The three `/network/prescriptions/{id}/active-attempt`, `/network/prescriptions/{id}/routed-endpoint`, and `/network/prescriptions/{id}/signature-session` reads are collections: each carries its single resource as a one-element array, or an empty array when no such resource exists. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many items a page returns; it defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of leading items to skip and defaults to 0. Both are also accepted on the `GET /network/participants` and related collection reads that carry their own filter parameters.

Unless an operation specifies otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources ordered by creation timestamp in ascending order. Row order and creation order are usually the same thing, but when a stored `created_at` cannot separate two rows — a same-instant tie — a stable sub-ordering by insertion sequence settles it, so list reads are deterministic across identical requests. Paginated list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` reflects the size of the full filtered result set, not the size of the returned page.

### 2.4. The Directory as the Authority for Service Reach

A participant's presence in the network is not a single flag but the outcome of two independent conditions. A participant is `directory_listed` when, and only when, its `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` and it holds at least one `DirectoryEndpoint` whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED`. This derived boolean is recomputed on every event that could change either input: a certification promotion or demotion, an endpoint's own status move, and the registration of a new endpoint. It is never set directly by a client.

A second derived enumeration, `delivery_reachability`, answers a different question — how much of a participant's directory is actually reachable right now. The count of reachable endpoints is the number of that participant's endpoints whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED` and whose half-open service interval `[effective_from, effective_to)` contains the current logical clock, where a null `effective_to` means the interval never closes. The total is the number of that participant's endpoints of every status, including those still `PENDING_CERT` and those already `DISABLED`. When there are no endpoints at all, or when none are reachable, the participant reads `UNREACHABLE`. When at least one endpoint is reachable but the reachable share of the total is below one half, the participant reads `AT_RISK`. In every other case the participant reads `FULL`. This enumeration is reported on the participant detail read and never gates a write; it is a distinct rule from the endpoint degradation gate described in §2.6, which measures the share of endpoints that would remain still-reachable and does gate writes. The two ratios must not be substituted for one another.

### 2.5. Nested Resource Retrieval and Common Resource Properties

Requests that target nested resources carry several identifiers in a single path, and the server resolves them strictly left to right. Each path segment's identifier is resolved before the next is examined, and the first identifier that fails to resolve ends the request with the corresponding not-found error: `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND` for a participant, `ERR_PRESCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND` for a prescription, `ERR_ENDPOINT_NOT_FOUND` for a `DirectoryEndpoint`, `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND` for a `RoutingAttempt`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` for a `Patient`, `ERR_SIGNING_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` for a `SigningSession`, `ERR_BENEFIT_CHECK_NOT_FOUND` for a `BenefitCheck`, and `ERR_FEE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` for a `TransactionFeeEntry`. Consents resolve through `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404. Body references are resolved after path references, in the order the endpoint's input list declares them; a body-supplied foreign key that does not resolve produces the same vocabulary. The pair of `participant_type` and `npi` is unique across the network, so registering a participant whose pair already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTICIPANT_NPI` at HTTP 409.

Every resource in this API carries a system-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix. All of them carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp that is written compactly as `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` when it falls on a whole second and with three fractional digits otherwise. Every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of its last modification, and `version`, an integer that reads 1 on a freshly created resource and increments on every mutation to the resource. Derived-field recomputations count as mutations: rewriting a `directory_listed`, refreshing a `reliability_ratio`, or advancing a `plan_deductible_met_cents` all stamp `updated_at` and bump `version` on the resource that owns the field.

### 2.6. Endpoint Stability, Coverage, and the Service Window

A directory endpoint's health is expressed through two reliability columns that share an input set but differ in method. Both consider only that endpoint's `RoutingAttempt` rows whose `outcome` is `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `REJECTED`, or `UNDELIVERABLE`; rows still `IN_PROGRESS` are excluded from both. The `reliability_ratio` column is the chronological variant and is the one every gate consumes. Its inputs are ordered by `reported_at` ascending, then `attempt_no` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; the window is the last 20 rows of that ordering, that is, the 20 rows with the greatest sort key. The ratio is the number of `ACKNOWLEDGED` rows within that window divided by the window's length, rounded half up to four decimal places. When the window is empty the ratio reads exactly 1.0.

The `reliability_ratio_ewma` column is the exponentially weighted variant and no gate reads it. It walks the same ordered history oldest-first, seeding a running value at 1.0. For each row it takes the row's outcome as 1.0 when the row is `ACKNOWLEDGED` and 0.0 otherwise, and updates the running value to 0.0670 multiplied by that outcome plus the complement of 0.0670 multiplied by the previous running value, rounded half up to four decimal places at each step. The final running value is reported. Because the seed is 1.0 and the weight is small, a short or mostly-failed history reads far higher here than chronologically: a single `UNDELIVERABLE` attempt alone yields a chronological ratio of 0.0000 against an EWMA of 0.9330.

Two gates act on the chronological ratio. An endpoint that a report moves into `DEGRADED` may be returned to `ACTIVE` only once its chronological `reliability_ratio` is at least 0.95; a lower value is refused with `ERR_ENDPOINT_NOT_STABLE`. An endpoint whose chronological ratio has fallen below 0.90 is degraded, subject to the coverage invariant below, on any path that recomputes its ratio — the reported outcomes of §2.11's endpoint chain and a successful route, which recomputes the nominated endpoint before the attempt is opened and so may degrade it on the spot. Separately, an endpoint may only be touched while its half-open service interval `[effective_from, effective_to)` contains the current logical clock; a null `effective_to` leaves the interval open-ended, and any operation outside the interval is refused with `ERR_ENDPOINT_OUT_OF_SERVICE_WINDOW`. Two endpoints of the same participant that share a `service_set`, an `endpoint_role`, a `service_level`, and a `message_standard` may not hold overlapping intervals; a registration that would overlap is refused with `ERR_ENDPOINT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`.

The coverage invariant protects a participant from degrading its own directory into uselessness. A transition that would push the share of that participant's live endpoints — those in `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED` status, since a `DEGRADED` endpoint is still reachable — strictly below one half of that participant's endpoints is refused: the endpoint remains `ACTIVE`, and the operation returns a warning payload carrying `ERR_ENDPOINT_COVERAGE_WARNING` rather than moving state. The `ACTIVE`-only share is the ratio §2.4's reachability enumeration is built on, and the two must not be substituted for one another. A participant holding a single endpoint is exempt, since it has no other endpoint to protect; that is what lets such a directory degrade into the fallback channel. Because this is an invariant over a snapshot rather than a rule about ordering, a sequential retry after another endpoint has been added succeeds. A related but distinct guard protects service continuity by `service_set`: a disable that would leave its participant with no `ACTIVE` endpoint for the same `service_set`, counting every endpoint other than the one being disabled, is refused with `ERR_LAST_ACTIVE_ENDPOINT`. The endpoint being disabled is excluded from that count, so a participant holding exactly one endpoint may always disable it; and the guard is consulted only while no *other* endpoint of the participant remains `ACTIVE` at all, so an endpoint whose participant still carries an `ACTIVE` endpoint of a different `service_set` may also always be disabled.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision and the Fee Ledger's Conservation Invariants

Every monetary quantity in this API is an integer number of cents. The benefit-cost estimates returned on a `BenefitCheck` are integer cents, and the two rounding steps inside that calculation — the formulary discount applied to the benchmark price and the coinsurance applied to the effective benchmark — are rounded half up to whole cents. The transaction-fee settlement's reserve multiplication is likewise rounded half up to whole cents. Nothing in the fee path is stored as a fraction, and no stored monetary field is re-rounded on read.

The fee ledger is append-only, and its integrity is expressed as two conserved quantities that hold across every row regardless of status. The first is `period_net_total_cents`, the sum of `fee_cents` over every `TransactionFeeEntry` row sharing the same participant and `month_key`. This quantity is identical on every such row, and the write model keeps it so: each row carries the total the ledger stands at after its own write, so appending a row restamps the quantity across every row of the participant and month rather than pinning it to the rows written before it. Because the quantity ignores `status` entirely, a charge followed by its compensating row leaves it exactly where it began — the positive `fee_cents` of the charge and the negative `fee_cents` of the compensation cancel. For the same reason a row marked `REVERSED` keeps its own `fee_cents` and remains inside the total; the marker records that a compensating row exists, not that the original was erased. The second conserved quantity is the cell's net transaction count, the sum of `transaction_count` over every row sharing the same participant, `month_key`, `transaction_type`, and `direction`. It too is independent of status, and the negative `transaction_count` carried by a compensating row cancels its charge back to the pre-charge value.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write paths require the client to assert the version it last observed, and both reject a stale assertion before any work is done.

Routing a signed order is guarded. A route request must carry `expected_prescription_version`, and that value must equal the prescription's current `version`. The prescription's version advances on its own state transitions and on every cascaded side effect that touches it, so a client that read the order before its last sign or before a report resolved an attempt holds a stale view. Such a request is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, and no `RoutingAttempt` is created.

Certification promotion is guarded the same way. A promotion request must carry `expected_participant_version` matching the participant's current `version`. The participant's version advances on every certification test recorded against it, on every endpoint status cascade rolled up into `directory_listed`, and on every fee reversal billed to it. A request carrying an older version is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Fee reversal carries the same field for the same reason: a reversal request must supply `expected_participant_version` matching the billed participant's current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version assertion in its body.

### 2.9. Validation Order

Requests are validated in a fixed priority order, and the first failure ends the request with its own error. Authorization is evaluated first: a missing or unrecognized `X-User-Role` yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) on every endpoint except the network metadata read and the test-driving endpoints, and a role that is valid but not permitted for the operation — or an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve for an entity-backed role — yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403) everywhere. Existence comes next, resolved left to right through the path and then through the body. State admission follows: the target resource must be in a state that admits the requested operation, and rejection here uses `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ROUTE_ATTEMPT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, or `ERR_LAST_ACTIVE_ENDPOINT`, all at HTTP 409. Controlled-substance safety preconditions are evaluated next, before any structural or fiscal rule, so an order that is both controlled and missing its two-factor evidence surfaces the safety error rather than a downstream one — including when the signing call's own controlled-order precondition is the stricter restatement of its session precondition described in §3.3. Certification preconditions for production enablement come after that, then routing-selector constraint satisfaction, then the service-window and interval rules, and finally the two fiscal rules — reversal legality before consent-window overlap. A request that is malformed at the HTTP level — one whose body is not a JSON object, or whose body is unreadable — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; so is a missing required field, an unknown enum member, or a declared input that is not a recognized field of the request, at HTTP 400. A request that matches no declared route is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 404, and one that names a method not declared for a declared path with the same identifier at HTTP 405 — the two route-level refusals are the only errors in the catalogue carried on a status other than the one §5 lists for them, since §5 records `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at 400. Both are raised by the router itself rather than by field validation, so their body is nonetheless parsed and then discarded: the refusal names no field at all, even when a declared input such as `limit` is missing or malformed.

Where several rules of the same category guard one endpoint, the order above is the tie-break: the safety rules of §2.9's fifth band are settled before the certification rules of its sixth, and the routing-selector constraints of its seventh are settled before the temporal rules of its eighth.

### 2.10. The Logical Clock

Time-sensitive rules in this API — service-interval containment, consent-window overlap and expiry, endpoint ordering — are all evaluated against a single logical clock that the server maintains independently of wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real time before it is explicitly set. Two operator-only test-driving capabilities manipulate it. The first sets the clock to a supplied instant, given as `current_utc`, and the second advances it by a whole number of `minutes` from its current value, defaulting from the startup default when the clock has never been set. Neither capability changes any business state; they move the clock and nothing else, and neither is part of the public business surface. Because every temporal rule reads this one clock, a test that advances the clock can deliberately move an endpoint out of its service window or a consent past its expiry without touching stored records.

### 2.11. Mutation Cascades and Recomputation Order

Several operations in this API mutate more than one resource, and the chains are ordered rather than simultaneous. Reading them as chains matters, because a downstream hop observes the state its upstream hop just produced.

The history chain runs from a batch. A batch leaving `PENDING` is the first hop: it snapshots the patient's `consent_version` into `consent_version_snapshot` and computes `source_record_count` over the patient's live, certified-source records. Settlement is the second hop: deduplication stamps `is_superseded` and `aggregation_batch_id` on every evaluated record and fixes `record_count` and `superseded_count`. Those record stamps force the third hop, which recomputes the patient's `history_record_count` over records whose `retracted_at` is null. The fourth hop advances the covered payer's `plan_deductible_met_cents` toward its `plan_deductible_cents` when the patient carries a `covered_plan_payer_id`. A fifth hop settles the batch's own economics, stamping `batch_cost_cents` and incrementing `subscription_generation`. Revocation runs its own short chain: it revokes the patient's `ACTIVE` consents, moves the patient's `consent_status` to `REVOKED` and increments `consent_version`, and then — when `discard_on_revoke` is true — stamps `retracted_at` on the patient's history records before recomputing `history_record_count`.

The endpoint chain runs from a reported outcome. Stamping an attempt terminal is the first hop, and it forces the second: the target endpoint's `reported_attempt_count`, `acknowledged_count`, and both `reliability_ratio` columns are recomputed from its reported history. That recompute can itself move the endpoint's status, either upward from `DEGRADED` or downward from `ACTIVE` subject to the coverage invariant. The status move is the third hop, and it forces the fourth: the owning participant's `directory_listed` is recomputed. The participant's `open_route_attempt_count` — the count of routing attempts whose outcome is `IN_PROGRESS` and whose receiving participant is that participant — is likewise a derived read over live attempts and follows the same attempt rows.

The directory chain runs from a disable or a suspension, and it is the longest. The endpoint or participant status move is the first hop. Its recomputed `directory_listed` is the second hop, and when that boolean lands false the fan-out begins: prescriptions of that receiving participant at `SIGNED` or `ROUTED` clinical status with `routing_status` at `NOT_ROUTED`, `ROUTED`, or `BACKUP_SELECTION` move to `ROUTE_FAILED`. Resolving their in-flight attempts is the third hop: attempts still `IN_PROGRESS` become `UNDELIVERABLE`. The fourth hop is terminal: every prescription whose routing became `ROUTE_FAILED` moves to `clinical_status` `UNDELIVERABLE`. Note that in both the endpoint chain and the directory chain the participant's `version` increments, because a recomputed `directory_listed` is a mutation of the participant.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Participant and Its Directory Online

A participant organization enters the network through registration, which accepts the organization's `name`, its `participant_type` — one of `PRESCRIBER_CLINIC`, `PHARMACY`, `PAYER`, or `HUB` — and its `npi`, a National Provider Identifier of exactly ten digits. The payload may also carry `volume_billing_mode`, which selects the tier basis used later when transaction fees settle and defaults to `MONTHLY`; the alternative is `ROLLING_90_DAY`. A participant also declares how it prefers to receive undeliverable traffic: `fallback_channel` defaults to `FAX` with the alternative `PRINT`, and `fax_number` is supplied when the channel is fax and is null when not provisioned. The plan-related fields — `plan_copay_cents`, `plan_coinsurance_pct`, `plan_deductible_cents`, `plan_brand_fee_cents`, `formulary_preferred_pct`, and `plan_benefit_cap_cents` — are stored on any participant that supplies them but carry meaning only for a participant whose type is `PAYER`. A newly registered participant starts at `UNCERTIFIED` with an empty `certification_tests_passed` list, reads `directory_listed` as false, reports an `open_route_attempt_count` of zero, and begins with `plan_deductible_met_cents` at zero.

Modeling the services a participant can actually perform is a separate step, and each such statement of capability is a **directory endpoint**. Registering one names the `service_set` it supports — `NEW_RX`, `REFILL_RX`, `CHANGE_CANCEL`, `MED_HISTORY`, `BENEFIT_COST`, or `ELIGIBILITY_FORMULARY` — the `endpoint_role` it plays, either `SENDER`, `RECEIVER`, or `BOTH`, the `service_level` it contracted at, one of `ELIGIBLE_A`, `ELIGIBLE_B`, or `ELIGIBLE_C`, the `sla_response_minutes` it commits to, and the `message_standard` it speaks, either `NCPDP_SCRIPT_2017071` or `NCPDP_SCRIPT_2022011`. The endpoint's `effective_from` marks the inclusive start of its service interval and the optional `effective_to` marks the exclusive end, so that a null `effective_to` leaves the interval open-ended. A newly registered endpoint does not begin serving traffic: it starts in `PENDING_CERT` with `reported_attempt_count`, `acknowledged_count` both at zero and both reliability columns at 1.0, and the owning participant's `directory_listed` is recomputed.

### 3.2. Certification Testing as the Production-Enablement Gate

Nothing in the directory becomes live until its participant has earned it. A `ParticipantAdmin` acting for the participant operates the certification machinery on the organization's behalf, and the sequence is deliberately two-stage.

The first stage opens the certification window, moving the participant's `certification_status` from `UNCERTIFIED` to `IN_CERTIFICATION` and clearing `certification_tests_passed` to an empty list. This cannot be done for an organization that has nothing to certify: a participant with no directory endpoint rows at all is refused with `ERR_CERT_NO_ENDPOINT` at HTTP 422. The endpoint-row requirement is evaluated first, so such a participant is refused for that reason whatever status it currently sits at, and no status is barred from opening the window. The same operation also serves as the reopen path. A participant sitting in `SUSPENDED` may be returned to `IN_CERTIFICATION`, again requiring at least one endpoint row and again resetting `certification_tests_passed`; and a participant already `CERTIFIED` may be demoted back to `IN_CERTIFICATION`, again requiring at least one endpoint row and again resetting `certification_tests_passed`. Because test outcomes can be recorded only while the participant is `IN_CERTIFICATION`, no `FAIL` can accumulate against a `CERTIFIED` participant, and the demotion therefore turns on its endpoint-row requirement alone rather than on a failed test recorded since the last promotion. Every reopen of a certified participant is consequential for the directory: each of that participant's endpoints that is currently `ACTIVE` drops back to `PENDING_CERT`, and `directory_listed` is recomputed — which, for a participant whose endpoints were all serving, flips it from true to false.

The second stage records individual test results. Each result names one `test_code` — drawn from `CONNECTIVITY`, `SCRIPT_NEW_RX`, `SCRIPT_REFILL`, `SCRIPT_CHANGE_CANCEL`, `MED_HISTORY`, `BENEFIT_COST`, or `ELIGIBILITY_FORMULARY` — and one `outcome`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`. A code outside the seven is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_TEST_CODE` and an outcome outside the pair with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Recording results is a self-loop inside `IN_CERTIFICATION`, which is required because an organization accumulates its credentials one test at a time. On a `PASS`, the code is appended to `certification_tests_passed` if it is not already present, and the list's order is significant: it reads in the order of the passes' first appearance by creation timestamp ascending, so a repeat pass never reorders it.

Promotion is the third and final move. The participant's `certification_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, and — in the same transaction — every one of its endpoints still sitting in `PENDING_CERT` becomes `ACTIVE`, and `directory_listed` is recomputed, typically flipping from false to true. Promotion is guarded in three ways, checked in order. First, `certification_tests_passed` must cover at least `CONNECTIVITY` and `SCRIPT_NEW_RX`; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_CERT_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`. Second, the pass set is internally consistent: passing `MED_HISTORY` requires that `ELIGIBILITY_FORMULARY` has also been passed, and a set that violates this prerequisite is refused with `ERR_CERT_TEST_PREREQUISITE`. Third, the promotion carries the participant's observed version as `expected_participant_version`, and a value that no longer matches the participant's current version is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 before any state moves.

### 3.3. Writing a Prescription and Proving Identity at Signature

A `Prescriber` authors an order as a draft, supplying the `prescriber_id` clinic, the `receiving_pharmacy_id` that will dispense it, the `patient_id`, the `drug_ndc` as an eleven-digit National Drug Code, the `transaction_type`, the `controlled_schedule`, the `message_standard`, the `days_supply`, the `quantity`, and the number of `refills_authorized` on the original order. The order carries the authoring clinic's own `fallback_channel` and `fax_number` as its declared fallback, since §3.5's fallback channel is that declaration. The `transaction_type` spans the full conversational vocabulary of the network: `NEW_RX`, `REFILL_REQUEST`, `REFILL_RESPONSE`, `CHANGE_REQUEST`, `CHANGE_RESPONSE`, `CANCEL_REQUEST`, and `CANCEL_RESPONSE`. The `controlled_schedule` is `NON_CONTROLLED` or one of `SCHEDULE_II` through `SCHEDULE_V`. A draft starts in `DRAFT` clinical status and `NOT_ROUTED` routing status, with `refills_used` and `routing_attempt_count` at zero, and the computed flags are derived immediately: `is_controlled` is true exactly when `controlled_schedule` differs from `NON_CONTROLLED`, and `significance` is `CRITICAL` when the schedule is `SCHEDULE_II` or `SCHEDULE_III` or when `monitored_under_consent` is true, and `STANDARD` in every other case. The `monitored_under_consent` flag is itself derived: it is true when the order's patient holds an `ACTIVE` consent whose `scope` is `MEDICATION_HISTORY`.

Signing is where the safety story begins, and the signature itself is never accepted on the strength of a bare request. The `Prescriber` first opens a **signing session** against the prescription, naming the `prescription_id` it will authorize; the session records the clinic that opened it as its `prescriber_id`. The session starts in `OPEN` session status with `identity_proofing_status` and `two_factor_status` both at `NOT_STARTED`, `otp_attempt_count` at zero, and `opened_at` stamped. The session then accumulates two independent proofs.

Identity proofing is recorded first: the `Prescriber` submits an `outcome` of `PASSED` or `FAILED`, and the session's `identity_proofing_status` takes that value. A `FAILED` outcome is not merely recorded — it also writes `two_factor_status` to `FAILED` and clears `otp_challenge_code`, so that the session can never be driven forward into two-factor verification; the only route out of such a session is to abandon it and start another.

The second factor runs as a challenge-and-verify exchange. Issuing a challenge requires that `identity_proofing_status` has already reached `PASSED`, and a session that has not proved identity is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_PROOFING_REQUIRED`; a session whose `two_factor_status` is already `LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_LOCKED` at HTTP 423. On success a six-digit `otp_challenge_code` is issued and `two_factor_status` moves to `PENDING`. Verification accepts an `otp_code` and is available only while `two_factor_status` is `PENDING`, any other status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The code is compared to the session's current `otp_challenge_code`. A match moves `two_factor_status` to `PASSED` and clears the challenge code. A mismatch increments `otp_attempt_count` and is refused with `ERR_OTP_INVALID` at HTTP 422; when the increment carries `otp_attempt_count` to three, `two_factor_status` becomes `LOCKED` and the session is refused thereafter with `ERR_ACCOUNT_LOCKED`. The session cannot start a third-factor verification attempt while `otp_attempt_count` is already at three, since that guard is evaluated before the comparison. Abandoning the session is always available from `OPEN`, moves `session_status` to `ABANDONED`, and clears any outstanding challenge code. A session also reaches `SIGNED` automatically, with `signed_at` stamped, when the prescription it belongs to is signed.

The signing call itself accepts a `signing_session_id` and applies four preconditions, in order. The referenced session must belong to this prescription, must still be `OPEN`, must carry `identity_proofing_status` of `PASSED`, and must carry `two_factor_status` of `PASSED`; the prescription must have no active attempt in flight, that is, `active_routing_attempt_id` must be null. The prescription must also still be admissible to the clinical machine — its `clinical_status` must be `DRAFT` or `SIGNED`, any other being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — so an order already signed, routed, delivered or cancelled cannot be signed again, and a refused sign leaves the session exactly as it was. A request failing any of these is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Second, a controlled order — one whose `is_controlled` is true — requires both the passed identity proofing and the passed second factor on the signing session, and an order lacking either is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_PROOFING_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422; this is a stricter restatement of the session precondition for the controlled case. Third, an order whose `message_standard` is `NCPDP_SCRIPT_2017071` may not carry a `controlled_schedule` of `SCHEDULE_II`, `SCHEDULE_III`, `SCHEDULE_IV`, or `SCHEDULE_V`; that combination is refused with `ERR_CONTROLLED_NOT_SUPPORTED_BY_STANDARD`. Fourth, a controlled order requires that the receiving pharmacy hold a directory endpoint for `service_set` `NEW_RX` in `ACTIVE` status; absent that, the sign is refused with `ERR_PHARMACY_NOT_CERTIFIED_FOR_CONTROLLED`.

When all four hold, the order's `clinical_status` becomes `SIGNED`, its `signature_audit_id` is set to the `signing_session_id` that authorized it, the signing session moves to `SIGNED` with `signed_at` stamped, and `routing_status` remains `NOT_ROUTED` — the order is signed but not yet sent, and routing is a separate step. During a signing session's identity-proofing step, a controlled order additionally requires that no routing attempt is in flight, and if one is, the proof is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_ATTEMPT_IN_PROGRESS`.

### 3.4. Choosing a Destination and Routing the Order

Routing is a nomination problem before it is a transmission. The selector assembles the candidate set from the receiving pharmacy's directory endpoints and keeps only those satisfying every one of the following: the endpoint belongs to the pharmacy named on the order; its `status` is `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED`; its `service_set` matches the one mapped from the order's `transaction_type`; its half-open service interval contains the current logical clock; and — when the order is controlled — its `message_standard` is `NCPDP_SCRIPT_2022011`. The mapping from transaction to service set is fixed: `NEW_RX` maps to `NEW_RX`; `REFILL_REQUEST` and `REFILL_RESPONSE` both map to `REFILL_RX`; and `CHANGE_REQUEST`, `CHANGE_RESPONSE`, `CANCEL_REQUEST`, and `CANCEL_RESPONSE` all map to `CHANGE_CANCEL`. Endpoints in `PENDING_CERT` or `DISABLED`, and endpoints outside their service interval, are excluded from consideration.

The surviving candidates are then ranked by a strictly ordered key list, and the first key that separates two candidates decides between them. `ACTIVE` endpoints rank before `DEGRADED` ones. Among candidates of equal status, one whose `message_standard` equals the prescription's ranks ahead of one whose standard differs. Among those, the `service_level` ordinal decides, with `ELIGIBLE_A` before `ELIGIBLE_B` before `ELIGIBLE_C`. Then the shortest `sla_response_minutes` wins. Then the higher chronological `reliability_ratio` wins — the chronological variant only, never the EWMA column, and since two ratios are separated by at least one part in ten thousand the identifier is never itself consulted. Finally, ties are broken by `created_at` ascending; the identifier is expressly forbidden as a final tie-break, so a tie that survives every key is resolved by creation order alone. The winner is the first candidate under that ordering, and its identifier becomes the order's `routed_endpoint_id` for the attempt. When the candidate set is empty, `routing_status` moves to `ROUTE_FAILED`, the caller receives `ERR_NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE`, nothing is scheduled, and no fallback fires.

Two nomination modes share this machinery. The primary nomination applies to a prescription's first attempt, when every non-excluded candidate is eligible and the "already routed to" exclusion below does not apply. The backup nomination applies after a `DEGRADED` endpoint's attempt is reported undeliverable; it additionally excludes both the endpoint the order is currently pointed at, every endpoint already tried, and every endpoint of the participant that is currently `DEGRADED`, so the winner is the second-best candidate or the nomination fails outright with `ERR_NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE`.

Sending a signed order is the routing call, and it is version-guarded: the request supplies `expected_prescription_version`, and a value that no longer matches the prescription's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 before any attempt exists. It also requires that `active_routing_attempt_id` is null, since a prescription may only have one attempt in flight at a time; a second concurrent route is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_ATTEMPT_IN_PROGRESS` at HTTP 409. Third, the candidate set must be non-empty: at least one directory endpoint of the receiving pharmacy with a `service_set` among `NEW_RX`, `REFILL_RX`, or `CHANGE_CANCEL` matching the transaction type must be `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED`, and otherwise the route is refused with `ERR_NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE`. A successful route creates a `RoutingAttempt` with `channel` `NETWORK` in `IN_PROGRESS` — its `attempt_no` is a monotone attempt number within the prescription starting at 1 — sets `routed_endpoint_id` to the selected endpoint, and advances the prescription's `clinical_status` from `SIGNED` to `ROUTED` while `routing_status` moves `NOT_ROUTED` to `ROUTING` and then to `ROUTED`. Note that the routing machine's `NOT_ROUTED` to `ROUTING` transition requires `clinical_status` to be `SIGNED`, so a route call on a draft is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A prescription that is already `ROUTED` may be routed again, and this self-loop is how refill, change, and cancel responses travel. An order that has already closed — one whose `clinical_status` is `DELIVERED` or `REJECTED` — may also be routed again provided its routing status is `ROUTED`, `BACKUP_SELECTION`, `ROUTE_FAILED`, or `FALLBACK_SENT`, and a re-route returns the order to the wire with both `clinical_status` and `routing_status` at `ROUTED`. The same version guard and the same single-in-flight-attempt guard apply. The additional rule is that when the `transaction_type` is `NEW_RX`, `REFILL_RESPONSE`, `CHANGE_RESPONSE`, or `CANCEL_RESPONSE`, the selected endpoint's `message_standard` must equal the prescription's `message_standard`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_MESSAGE_STANDARD_MISMATCH` at HTTP 422. Re-routing with `transaction_type` of `REFILL_RESPONSE` also increments the order's `refills_used`, and the prescription's `version` increments on every successful route. The winner's counters and both reliability columns are recomputed at the same moment, so a route can itself move the endpoint's status before the attempt is in flight, and the owning participant's `directory_listed` follows.

### 3.5. Outcomes, Backup Nomination, and the Fallback Channel

Every attempt in flight ends in exactly one terminal outcome, and which one is reported determines what happens to both the order and the endpoint that received it.

An acknowledgment is the good ending. Reporting an attempt acknowledged stamps its `reported_at`, moves its `outcome` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and moves the prescription's `clinical_status` to `DELIVERED` with `routing_status` at `ROUTED` and `active_routing_attempt_id` cleared. The routing machine permits this move only while the prescription's `routing_status` is `ROUTED` or `BACKUP_SELECTION`, and only from an attempt whose `prescription_id` is the prescription the report names; a report naming an attempt that belongs elsewhere is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_PRESCRIPTION_MISMATCH`. It also requires the prescription's `clinical_status` not to be `CANCELLED`, `REJECTED`, or `UNDELIVERABLE`, since those orders cannot accept a delivery. An acknowledgment is also the accrual point for fees, described in §3.7, and the point at which the target endpoint's report counters and both reliability columns are recomputed. Acknowledging an attempt for a prescription resting in `BACKUP_SELECTION` likewise moves `routing_status` to `ROUTED` and `clinical_status` to `DELIVERED`, and an acknowledged network attempt moves a prescription sitting in `FALLBACK_SENT` back to `ROUTED` with `clinical_status` at `DELIVERED` — which closes the cyclic routing machine.

An undeliverable report is the operational failure. It carries a `failure_code` and requires that the attempt's `outcome` is currently `IN_PROGRESS`, and that the prescription's `clinical_status` is neither `CANCELLED` nor `DELIVERED`. Reporting stamps `reported_at`, moves the attempt to `UNDELIVERABLE`, recomputes the target endpoint's counters and reliability columns, recomputes the owning participant's `directory_listed`, and then consults the routing machine for the prescription's next routing status. A network attempt that a prior report already acknowledged may be reported undeliverable again, provided the prescription is back on the wire at `ROUTED` or `BACKUP_SELECTION`; the ordinary precondition is otherwise that the attempt's `outcome` is still `IN_PROGRESS`. The accepted `failure_code` vocabulary for this path is `NO_ROUTE`, the transport-family classifiers `CF_0000` through `CF_0007`, and `TRANSMISSION_TIMEOUT`; any other value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the two codes the fallback exception below names — `PATIENT_UNKNOWN` and `BENEFIT_EXHAUSTED` — are outside that vocabulary, so no report carrying either ever reaches the exception clause.

Two effects travel with an undeliverable report. The first is endpoint degradation, which is subject to the coverage invariant and never to an endpoint-identity check: the attempt's own `target_endpoint_id` is the endpoint degraded, so the two cannot disagree, and the report is therefore never refused with `ERR_ENDPOINT_NOT_ROUTED_ATTEMPT`. A degradation that would push the participant's share of its live endpoints — those in `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED` status — strictly below one half is refused with `ERR_ENDPOINT_COVERAGE_WARNING`, leaving the endpoint `ACTIVE` and the operation returning the warning payload instead of moving state; a participant holding a single endpoint is exempt, so its sole endpoint always degrades. The second is backup nomination, which applies when the endpoint the attempt was sent to has just moved to `DEGRADED` — the endpoint's status transition is applied first, and the routing move observes the post-transition state. When at least one other endpoint of the same participant and the same `service_set` remains `ACTIVE`, sits inside its service window, and has not already carried one of this prescription's attempts — a `DEGRADED` endpoint never receives a re-pointed attempt — the routing machine moves the prescription to `BACKUP_SELECTION` and the earlier selector re-points the same in-flight attempt at the next-ranked endpoint. No new attempt row is created for this: a prescription's `active_routing_attempt_id` has one-to-one cardinality, so `attempt_no` and `routing_attempt_count` are unchanged, and the endpoint that just failed is folded back into the recomputed reliability history. When no such alternative endpoint exists, the report instead drives the prescription to `ROUTE_FAILED` with `ERR_NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` semantics. An undeliverable report reaching a `BACKUP_SELECTION` prescription whose attempt is pointed at the endpoint the backup selector nominated moves the prescription straight to `ROUTE_FAILED` and clears `active_routing_attempt_id`, and a report on an attempt that is not the backup-nominated one is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_PRESCRIPTION_MISMATCH`.

A rejection is the partner's refusal. Reporting a rejection carries a `failure_code` and an optional `error_code_returned`, requires the attempt's `outcome` to be `IN_PROGRESS`, moves it to `REJECTED`, moves the prescription's `clinical_status` to `REJECTED`, recomputes the target endpoint's counters and reliability columns, and creates a compensating fee-request for the sending cell as described in §3.7. The accepted `failure_code` vocabulary here is `CF_0003`, `CF_0007`, and `POST_DELIVERY`; a transport classifier such as `CF_0000` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because a transport failure is not a rejection. A rejection moves `clinical_status` to `REJECTED` and takes a `routing_status` of `ROUTED`, `BACKUP_SELECTION`, `FALLBACK_SENT`, or `FALLBACK_PENDING` to `ROUTE_FAILED` — the order leaves the wire before it can be re-nominated. The recompute that follows a rejection can move the endpoint from `ACTIVE` to `DEGRADED`, so a rejection is a real revert path rather than a dead end. Rejection is also reachable after delivery, when a `DELIVERED` prescription reports a post-delivery rejection for the same attempt; that path moves `clinical_status` to `REJECTED`, recomputes the endpoint, and creates the error-leg fee compensation for the participant's cell.

The fallback channel carries what the network could not. A prescription whose routing has failed may be sent over its declared fallback, and the preconditions are evaluated in this order: the prescription must already carry at least one attempt whose outcome is `UNDELIVERABLE` or `REJECTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_FAILED_ATTEMPT_TO_FALL_BACK`; its `fallback_channel` must then be `FAX` or `PRINT`, carrying a present `fax_number` when the channel is fax, or the request is refused with `ERR_FALLBACK_CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE`; and finally its `routing_status` must be `ROUTE_FAILED` or `BACKUP_SELECTION`, any other being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success `routing_status` moves to `FALLBACK_PENDING` and `active_routing_attempt_id` clears. From `BACKUP_SELECTION` the transition is refused with `ERR_NO_ROUTE_AVAILABLE` when the participant still holds an `ACTIVE` endpoint for the order's service set. A distinct entry from `ROUTED` is reachable only when the routing status was previously moved to `ROUTE_FAILED` and then restored to `ROUTED` by an acknowledged retry. The fallback attempt itself may be opened directly: creating a fallback attempt requires a `channel`, and it starts `IN_PROGRESS` with `target_endpoint_id` null and `attempt_no` set to one more than the prescription's current `routing_attempt_count`, which is itself incremented. Reporting that attempt is conditional on its channel not being `NETWORK` and on its outcome still being `IN_PROGRESS`, both otherwise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a reported acknowledgment of the fallback moves `routing_status` to `FALLBACK_SENT` and `clinical_status` to `DELIVERED`, while a reported undeliverable leaves the outcome at `UNDELIVERABLE`. The reporting call may supply a `failure_code` for the undeliverable case. A prescription may not accumulate more than three non-network attempts; a fourth is refused with `ERR_FALLBACK_HOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Cancellation withdraws the order, and the branch it takes is decided by the order's `clinical_status` before its routing status is read. A prescription whose `clinical_status` is `ROUTED` may be cancelled while its `routing_status` is `ROUTED` or `BACKUP_SELECTION`, moving `clinical_status` to `CANCELLED` and taking any attempt still `IN_PROGRESS` to `REJECTED` with `failure_code` `PRESCRIPTION_CANCELLED`. A prescription whose `clinical_status` is `SIGNED` at a `routing_status` of `NOT_ROUTED` or `FALLBACK_PENDING` may also be cancelled, in which case `clinical_status` becomes `CANCELLED` and `routing_status` returns to `NOT_ROUTED`. A routing status outside the pair the order's clinical status admits, or a clinical status outside those two — a `DRAFT` included, which sits at `NOT_ROUTED` but has not been signed — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. One class of order resists cancellation: an order whose `significance` is `CRITICAL` — a `SCHEDULE_II` or `SCHEDULE_III` order, or an order for a patient monitored under `MEDICATION_HISTORY` consent — must reach a delivery outcome, meaning `DELIVERED` or the reach that `FALLBACK_SENT` represents, before it may settle as `CANCELLED`; a cancellation attempted before that is refused with `ERR_CRITICAL_ORDER_DELIVERY_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. The safety rule is consulted only once the state band has admitted the request, so a `CRITICAL` order sitting at a routing status the state band rejects reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` instead.

### 3.6. Suspension, Endpoint Disabling, and Their Cascades

A `NetworkOperator` may suspend a certified participant outright. Suspension requires no precondition beyond the participant being in `CERTIFIED`. Its consequences are broad and immediate. The participant's `certification_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`; every one of its directory endpoints becomes `DISABLED` regardless of prior status; `directory_listed` is recomputed and lands false; and the participant's `version` increments. From that disabled directory a cascade follows. Every prescription whose `receiving_pharmacy_id` is this participant, whose `clinical_status` is `SIGNED` or `ROUTED`, and whose `routing_status` is `NOT_ROUTED`, `ROUTED`, or `BACKUP_SELECTION` has its `routing_status` moved to `ROUTE_FAILED`. Every one of those prescriptions' routing attempts still `IN_PROGRESS` has its `outcome` moved to `UNDELIVERABLE` with `failure_code` `NO_ROUTE`. Every one of those prescriptions then moves to `clinical_status` `UNDELIVERABLE`.

Disabling a single endpoint is the narrower instrument, available to a `NetworkOperator` from any endpoint status. It requires that no endpoint of the same participant and the same `service_set` remains `ACTIVE` after the disable, counting every endpoint other than the one being disabled — a guard whose violation is refused with `ERR_LAST_ACTIVE_ENDPOINT` at HTTP 409, and which a participant holding only this one endpoint therefore never trips. The guard is reached only when the participant still holds some `ACTIVE` endpoint other than the one being disabled; when every endpoint it holds is the endpoint being disabled, or already `DEGRADED` or `DISABLED`, the guard cannot fire and the disable proceeds. From `ACTIVE`, the disable is a four-hop cascade. The endpoint becomes `DISABLED` and `directory_listed` is recomputed, flipping false when this was the participant's last endpoint in `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED` status. From that falseness the second hop fans out: every prescription whose `receiving_pharmacy_id` is the owning participant, whose `clinical_status` is `SIGNED` or `ROUTED`, and whose `routing_status` is `NOT_ROUTED`, `ROUTED`, or `BACKUP_SELECTION` moves to `routing_status` `ROUTE_FAILED`. The third hop resolves each such prescription's in-flight work: every attempt of those prescriptions still `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `UNDELIVERABLE` with `failure_code` `NO_ROUTE`. The fourth hop is the terminal one: any prescription whose `routing_status` became `ROUTE_FAILED` moves to `clinical_status` `UNDELIVERABLE`. Disabling an endpoint from `DEGRADED` or from `PENDING_CERT` carries no cascade beyond recomputing `directory_listed`.

Reactivation returns a degraded endpoint to service. A `ParticipantAdmin` may do this only when the owning participant's `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_CERT_TESTS_INCOMPLETE` — only when the endpoint's chronological `reliability_ratio` is at least 0.95, otherwise `ERR_ENDPOINT_NOT_STABLE`, and only while the endpoint's half-open service interval contains the current logical clock, otherwise `ERR_ENDPOINT_OUT_OF_SERVICE_WINDOW`. Both automatic endpoint moves travel the same direction through `directory_listed`: an endpoint becoming `ACTIVE` when its participant is promoted out of certification does so through the automatic path, as does the reverse demotion when a participant leaves `CERTIFIED`.

### 3.7. Transaction Fees, Corrections, and the Append-Only Ledger

The network charges for carrying transactions, and the charge accrues as reports arrive rather than in a month-end batch. Two characters of a fee row matter throughout: its `entry_type`, which is `CHARGE`, `CORRECTION`, or `MANUAL_REVERSAL`, and its `status`, which is `PARTIAL`, `POSTED`, or `REVERSED`.

Accrual begins with acknowledgment. Reporting an attempt acknowledged creates a `TransactionFeeEntry` for the sending cell — the tuple of the prescription's `prescriber_id`, the `month_key`, the `transaction_type`, and a `direction` of `SENT` — with `status` `PARTIAL`, `transaction_count` of 1, `fee_tier` `FREE`, and `fee_cents` of 0. This is the provisional per-report accrual, deliberately cheap to write and deliberately incomplete. When the same cell already holds a non-`IN_PROGRESS` attempt for this prescription, so that the new report would double-count, a second row is created alongside it: an `entry_type` of `CORRECTION` with `status` `PARTIAL`, carrying `previous_count` equal to the cell's chargeable count before this report — the settled count over the cell's genuine charges, which is why the count over its provisional `PARTIAL` accruals is not what is subtracted — and `new_count` equal to that count plus one. The correction row itself carries a `transaction_count` of 0 and a `fee_cents` of 0, so the net of the pair is a single count rather than two: the accrual contributes its one and the correction contributes the `new_count` that already folds it in. The correction row's identifier is returned as the attempt's `correction_entry_id`, and it is null on a report that superseded nothing.

Once a month's volume has accumulated, a `NetworkOperator` posts a settled entry for a cell. The request names the `participant_id`, the `month_key`, the `transaction_type`, the `direction` — `SENT` or `RECEIVED` — and an optional `transaction_count`. When the count is supplied it is used as given; when it is omitted the server counts the network ledger for the cell. A posted entry has `entry_type` `CHARGE`, `status` `POSTED`, both `previous_count` and `new_count` null, and `reversal_of_entry_id` null, and its `fee_tier` and `fee_cents` come from the settlement algorithm below.

Settlement begins with the chargeable basis, which is variant-neutral. The basis rows are all `TransactionFeeEntry` rows for the same participant, `month_key`, `transaction_type`, and `direction` whose `reversal_of_entry_id` is null — that is, genuine charges, whether still `PARTIAL` or already `POSTED`. Compensating `MANUAL_REVERSAL` rows are excluded precisely because they carry a `reversal_of_entry_id`, so a reversal never changes chargeable volume. Two reads are taken over those rows. The basis a posted entry's own settlement measures — and the count a correction reports as `previous_count` — is the cell's settled volume: the anchor rows that are neither provisional nor superseded, that is, every one of them that carries no `new_count` and is not still `PARTIAL`. The chargeable volume the cell reports as a whole is the wider read: the sum of `transaction_count` over every anchor row carrying no `new_count`, whether it is still `PARTIAL` or already `POSTED`, so a provisional accrual that a later correction has superseded leaves the volume without the correction's `new_count` being added. Whichever read is taken, the basis total is clamped to a ceiling of 7500. The clamp happens before tiering, and that ordering is the one that matters: because the total is never presented to the tier function above 7500, every count at or above the ceiling settles at exactly the fee for 7500, and the ceiling absorbs the top `VOLUME` tier. For a sender cell of a non-history type that value is 60100 cents.

Tiering runs on one pinned marginal schedule. The first tier is `FREE` and spans counts zero through a free upper bound. The second tier is `STANDARD` and spans from one past the free bound through a standard upper bound, charged at a per-count rate. The third tier is `VOLUME` and spans from one past the standard bound upward without limit, charged at a lower per-count rate. The count falling in the standard band is the smaller of the amount above the free bound and the band's width, floored at zero; the count falling in the volume band is the amount above the standard bound, floored at zero. The amount is the standard-band count multiplied by the standard rate plus the volume-band count multiplied by the volume rate. Both products are integer cents and their sum is not re-rounded, so rounding each tier and then summing is the same operation as summing and then rounding.

The tier bounds and rates differ by direction and, for the sender schedule, by transaction type. On the sender schedule, ordinary types are free through 200 and standard through 2000 at a standard rate of 12 cents and a volume rate of 7 cents, while the medication-history type is free through 100 and standard through 1000 at a standard rate of 18 cents and a volume rate of 9 cents. On the receiver schedule, ordinary types are free through 200 and standard through 2500 at a standard rate of 8 cents and a volume rate of 4 cents.

The settlement then reserves against future shortfall, and here the two billing modes diverge. A participant whose `volume_billing_mode` is `MONTHLY` reserves against its own month's basis: the reserve is three tenths of the tier amount for that basis, rounded half up to a whole cent. A participant on `ROLLING_90_DAY` instead projects the month forward by multiplying the basis total by 1.45 and rounding half up, then clamps that projection into the range from zero to the same 7500 ceiling, and takes the reserve as three tenths of the tier amount for the clamped projection, again rounded half up. The assessed charge for the cell is three tenths higher than the entry's own `fee_cents`: an entry stores the booked charge computed from the clamped basis, and the reserve it accrues against future volume is exposed as the settlement's reserve figure alongside it — `reserve_cents`, alongside `tier_amount_cents`, `provisional_amount_cents`, a `final_amount_cents` net of the reserve, the `reserve_basis_count` and the `chargeable_count` — rather than being subtracted from the stored field. A worked illustration: on a sender cell of 500 transactions of an ordinary type, the charge is the standard-band count of 300 at 12 cents plus the volume-band count of zero, giving 3600 cents, and that is the stored `fee_cents`. Under `MONTHLY` the reserve is three tenths of the tier amount for 500, which is 1080, and the amount net of the reserve is 2520. Under `ROLLING_90_DAY` the projection is 725, the reserve is three tenths of the tier amount for 725, which is 1890, and the amount net of the reserve is 1710 — the same stored 3600, a different net, and a difference of 630 cents between the modes.

Reversal is the compensating instrument, and it is deliberately conservative. Only a `NetworkOperator` may reverse, and the request carries `expected_participant_version`, which must match the billed participant's current `version` or the reversal is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Legality is evaluated before any arithmetic: the target entry's `status` must be `POSTED`, it must not be an unsettled `PARTIAL` accrual, it must not itself be a compensating row — its `reversal_of_entry_id` must be null — and it must not already have been reversed. Any violation is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_REVERSIBLE` at HTTP 422. On success the entry's `status` becomes `REVERSED`, which is a marker only: the entry keeps its `fee_cents` and stays inside `period_net_total_cents`. A new compensating entry is created with `entry_type` `MANUAL_REVERSAL`, carrying the negation of the original's `fee_cents`, the negation of its `transaction_count`, the same `direction`, and a `reversal_of_entry_id` pointing at the original. The original's `reversal_entry_id` points at the compensating row. The compensating row is itself written with `status` `REVERSED` and carries its own identifier as its `reversal_entry_id`, so a client reading the reply to the reversal can name the pair without a second round trip: the reply's own object is the compensating row, and the row it answered is reported alongside it as `reversal_source`. Because both rows remain in the ledger, the participant-and-month `period_net_total_cents` returns to its pre-charge value and the cell's summed `transaction_count` does the same. The billed participant's `version` increments.

### 3.8. Consent, Medication History Aggregation, and Deduplication

Medication history is the network's second product, and it is gated on patient permission. Registering a patient captures a `full_name`, a `date_of_birth`, a two-letter `home_state`, an optional covering plan named by `covered_plan_payer_id`, and a `discard_on_revoke` flag that decides whether revoking consent retracts the patient's history records. A new patient starts with `consent_status` `NONE`, `consent_version` 0, and `history_record_count` 0.

A `PatientService` operator grants consent, naming a `scope` of `MEDICATION_HISTORY`, `BENEFIT_CHECK`, or `BOTH` and a `duration_days` validity length. The grant stamps `granted_at` and computes `expires_at` as `granted_at` plus `duration_days`, forming the half-open validity interval `[granted_at, expires_at)`. The new consent's `status` is written `ACTIVE` when `expires_at` is later than the current logical clock and `EXPIRED` otherwise; a zero-length duration therefore produces an empty interval whose status is `EXPIRED`, the condition behind `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED`. Only one `ACTIVE` window may exist at a time for a given patient and scope, so a grant whose window intersects an existing `ACTIVE` window for the same patient and scope is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` at HTTP 422. The scopes are compared by coverage rather than by literal equality: a `BOTH` window speaks for both `MEDICATION_HISTORY` and `BENEFIT_CHECK`, so it collides with a live window of either single scope, while those two single scopes do not collide with one another. Because every temporal rule reads the one logical clock, a stored window whose exclusive `expires_at` has already reached the clock is no longer live and blocks no new grant. A successful grant sets the patient's `consent_status` to `GRANTED` and increments the patient's `consent_version`. Revocation acts on every `ACTIVE` consent the patient holds: each becomes `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped, the patient's `consent_status` becomes `REVOKED` and its `consent_version` increments, and — when the patient's `discard_on_revoke` is true — `retracted_at` is stamped on every one of that patient's medication history records. The patient's `history_record_count` is recomputed afterwards.

History records arrive from the pharmacies and payers that observed the fills and claims. A reporting `Pharmacy` or `HubOperator` submits a record naming the `source_type`, either `PHARMACY_FILL` or `PAYER_CLAIM`, the reporting organization as `source_participant_id`, the `drug_ndc`, the `fill_date`, the `days_supply`, and the `quantity`. The certification of the source named as `source_participant_id` is what the storage gate tests. A `Pharmacy` identity is entity-backed, so a pharmacy submission names itself as its source; an opaque `HubOperator` may report on any source's behalf, which is how an uncertified source's submission reaches the gate at all. The record's deduplication identity, `dedupe_key`, is derived by concatenating the patient identifier, the drug code, the fill date reduced to its calendar day, and the days supplied, separated by vertical bars. A fresh record starts with `is_superseded` false, `retracted_at` null, and no `aggregation_batch_id`, and the patient's `history_record_count` is recomputed. A source that is not `CERTIFIED` has its submission refused with `ERR_HISTORY_SOURCE_UNCERTIFIED` at HTTP 422.

Aggregation is a batch operation over a patient's live history, and a batch moves through its own small machine. Opening a batch names the `patient_id` and a `trigger_reason` of `PRESCRIBER_QUERY`, `CONSENT_GRANT`, or `SCHEDULED_REFRESH`; it starts in `PENDING` with `subscription_generation` 0 and `batch_cost_cents` 0, and is refused with `ERR_HISTORY_BATCH_IN_PROGRESS` at HTTP 409 when the patient already holds a batch in `AGGREGATING`. Advancing a batch out of `PENDING` requires that status and requires an `ACTIVE` consent with a `scope` of `MEDICATION_HISTORY` or `BOTH`, absent which the advance is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. The consent gate is consulted before the status band, so a batch whose consent has lapsed is refused for that reason whatever status it holds; a batch admitted past the gate must then be `PENDING` or `COMPLETE`, and a batch settled as `FAILED` accepts no further work and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The advance moves the batch to `AGGREGATING` and takes two snapshots: `consent_version_snapshot` captures the patient's current `consent_version`, and `source_record_count` reflects the patient's medication history records whose `retracted_at` is null and whose source participant is `CERTIFIED`. Records from sources that are not certified are excluded from the batch's consideration entirely and are counted as skipped — they are not stamped, not superseded, and not counted in `source_record_count`.

Settlement runs the deduplication. The batch partitions its input records by `dedupe_key` and ranks the rows within each partition by a strictly ordered key list. Rows from a `PHARMACY_FILL` source rank ahead of rows from a `PAYER_CLAIM` source regardless of what else they carry. Among rows of equal source rank, the larger `quantity` wins. Among those, the earlier `fill_date` wins. Finally, ties break on `created_at` ascending. The winner of each partition is marked `is_superseded` false and the losers `is_superseded` true, and every evaluated record has its `aggregation_batch_id` set to the batch. The batch then stamps `record_count` as the number of partitions that survived, `superseded_count` as the number of input rows minus the number of partitions, and `source_record_count` as the number of input rows. Two consequences of the ranking deserve emphasis, because they point in opposite directions. When a partition mixes fills and claims, the pharmacy fill wins even if the payer claim carries a larger quantity, since source rank is the first key. When a partition holds only rows of one source type, source rank ties for every row, and the larger quantity wins instead — which is the opposite outcome from a mixed partition holding the same quantities.

Settlement also forces the patient aggregate and advances a payer's deductible. The patient's `history_record_count` is recomputed over records whose `retracted_at` is null, and a payer deductible advance runs: when the patient has a `covered_plan_payer_id`, that payer's `plan_deductible_met_cents` advances toward its `plan_deductible_cents` by the payer's own `plan_copay_cents`, saturating at the deductible — the same accumulator a benefit-check settlement consumes, so whichever runs first consumes what remains. The batch's own cost is computed under the medication-history fee schedule as a base charge of 25 cents plus 10 cents for each source record, where the per-record term counts at most 40 records. A batch of 25 source records therefore costs 275 cents, and a batch of 40, 41, or 500 source records all cost exactly 425 cents because the per-record term saturates at 400. The `subscription_generation` counter increments on the settlement that carries a batch out of `PENDING`, and again whenever a `COMPLETE` batch is re-aggregated in place — which is why it reads as a round counter rather than a one-shot flag. A `COMPLETE` batch may be re-aggregated, provided the patient remains consenting, and the fresh pass re-evaluates the record set; a patient who is no longer consenting is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`.

One check guards settlement itself. The batch may only reach `COMPLETE` while the patient's `consent_version` still equals the batch's `consent_version_snapshot`. When the version has advanced past the snapshot while aggregation was running, the batch instead settles as `FAILED`, its `record_count`, `superseded_count`, and `batch_cost_cents` all remain zero, and the condition is reported as `ERR_CONSENT_STALE` at HTTP 409.

### 3.9. Benefit and Formulary Estimates at the Point of Prescribing

The second product of the history machinery is cost transparency, and it is delivered as a **benefit check** attached to an order. A `Prescriber` or `Pharmacy` opens one by naming its `prescription_id`, the `payer_id` whose plan applies, and a `benchmark_price_cents` as the network benchmark for that drug and quantity. A new check begins with `check_status` `PENDING`, `coverage_status` and `formulary_status` both `UNKNOWN`, and `pricing_variant` `PROVISIONAL`, and it captures `deductible_met_snapshot_cents` from the payer's current `plan_deductible_met_cents` at that moment. That snapshot is taken once, on the edge out of `PENDING`, and is never re-read afterwards, so a later movement of the payer's deductible doesn't retroactively change an in-flight check's arithmetic.

Evaluation resolves eligibility and formulary against the payer's plan and the order: a `PAYER` participant's plan is the only one a check may resolve against, and it resolves to `formulary_status` `NON_PREFERRED` with `coverage_status` `ACTIVE`; a check opened against any other participant resolves neither column and leaves both at `UNKNOWN`. Evaluation then sets `pricing_variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `check_status` `COMPLETE`, and produces a provisional estimate. It is available to a `Pharmacy` and to a `HubOperator`. Settlement at fill time sets `pricing_variant` `FINAL` and `check_status` `COMPLETE`, produces the final estimate, and advances the payer's `plan_deductible_met_cents` by the final variant's credit, saturating at `plan_deductible_cents`; settlement belongs exclusively to a `Pharmacy`.

Both variants share a pinned evaluation order through seven steps. The first step computes the effective benchmark: when the check's `formulary_status` is `PREFERRED`, the benchmark price is discounted by the payer's `formulary_preferred_pct` and the product is rounded half up to a whole cent; in every other case the effective benchmark is the benchmark price unchanged. The second step computes the patient's cost share as the payer's `plan_copay_cents` plus the payer's `plan_coinsurance_pct` applied to the effective benchmark and rounded half up to a whole cent. The third step adds a brand fee: when the `formulary_status` is `NON_PREFERRED` or `PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED` the fee is the payer's `plan_brand_fee_cents`, and otherwise it is zero. The fourth step computes the deductible remaining as the payer's `plan_deductible_cents` minus its `plan_deductible_met_cents`, floored at zero. The fifth step is the variant-dependent one, and it is where the two variants part company, because it decides whether the brand fee sits inside the basis the deductible credit is measured against. In the `PROVISIONAL` variant the credit is the smaller of the deductible remaining and the patient's cost share — the brand fee is excluded — the estimated patient payment is the patient's cost share, and the estimated plan payment is the effective benchmark minus the patient's cost share, floored at zero, plus the brand fee. In the `FINAL` variant the credit is the smaller of the deductible remaining and the patient's cost share plus the brand fee — the brand fee is inside the basis — the estimated patient payment is the patient's cost share plus the brand fee, and the estimated plan payment is the effective benchmark minus the patient's cost share, floored at zero, with no brand fee added.

The remaining steps are shared. When the `formulary_status` is `PRIOR_AUTH_REQUIRED`, the estimated plan payment — the value carried as `estimated_plan_pay_cents` — is additionally capped at one half of the effective benchmark, rounded half up to a whole cent. The benefit cap then applies: when the estimated plan payment exceeds the payer's `plan_benefit_cap_cents`, `estimated_plan_pay_cents` is set to that cap and the removed amount is added to the estimated patient payment, which is the value carried as `estimated_patient_pay_cents`. Finally, the whole estimate requires an `ACTIVE` consent with a `scope` of `BENEFIT_CHECK` or `BOTH`; without it no estimate is produced at all, the request being refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. There is one pinned exception to the arithmetic: when the check's `coverage_status` is `INACTIVE`, the estimate is not computed but pinned — `formulary_status` becomes `NOT_COVERED`, `estimated_plan_pay_cents` becomes zero, `estimated_patient_pay_cents` becomes the full benchmark price, and the credit becomes zero.

The two variants produce different splits of the same total, and the difference traces entirely to the fifth step. Take a benchmark of 8000 cents, a `NON_PREFERRED` formulary, a copay of 1500 cents, a coinsurance of 0.2, a deductible of 10000 cents with 4000 already met, and a brand fee of 300 cents. The effective benchmark is 8000, the patient's cost share is 3100, the deductible remaining is 6000, and the brand fee is 300. Under `PROVISIONAL` the credit is 3100 and the estimate splits patient 3100 against plan 5200; under `FINAL` the credit is 3400 and the estimate splits patient 3400 against plan 4900. The split moves 300 cents from the plan to the patient while the sum stays at 8300 in both variants.
