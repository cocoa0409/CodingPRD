# DTC Mattress Trial Returns API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Selling a mattress online means selling a promise: sleep on it for a hundred nights, and if it is wrong for you, we take it back. Honouring that promise is a logistics and accounting problem rather than a marketing one. Every accepted return sets in motion a truck route with finite capacity, a warehouse inspection that grades the returned unit, a charity or recycler that must physically accept it, and a refund whose amount depends on how many nights the customer slept, what promotional gifts they keep, and what condition the mattress came back in. When any one of those steps is skipped or double-counted, the business either refunds money it should have retained or retains money it owes.

This document specifies the service that governs that entire reverse-logistics and settlement pipeline. It covers the in-home trial clock with its mandatory break-in window, return pickup routing with per-truck unit capacity and drive buffers, the donation-versus-recycle disposition waterfall over charity and recycler partners, refund proration and promotional bundle unwind, comfort exchanges to a different firmness with escalating handling fees and price differences, delivery-damage and compression-recovery complaints backed by photographic evidence, warranty claims on measured body indentation, retail-partner sales attribution and price-match settlement, and the fraud controls that detect households cycling through repeat trials.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is a ledger, not a field**: every cent of an order's paid principal is at all times accounted for as refunded, still refundable, or retained by the business, and every reversal restores that identity exactly. Second, **time is a server-controlled logical clock**, never wall-clock time; break-in windows, trial expiry, warranty decay, fraud-signal decay, and every proration basis read that single clock, so the whole system is deterministically test-drivable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The authorized roles for each endpoint are listed in the endpoint table in section 4.

The six roles are `Customer`, `RetailPartner`, `TrialSupportAgent`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `FinanceController`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

Two of these roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Customer record; ownership checks on any resource that references a Customer compare the resource's customer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Customer` may act on a trial order only when that order's `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and on a return case, comfort exchange, quality claim, or refund authorization only when the trial order it hangs from is owned that way. When `X-User-Role` is `RetailPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting RetailPartner record; a partner may act on an attribution claim only when the claim's `partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and when submitting a new claim the `partner_id` in the request body must equal `X-User-ID`. A violation of either ownership rule is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly these operations: reading a customer record, opening a return case, cancelling a return case, keeping a trial order, filing a quality claim, submitting a further evidence round on a quality claim, requesting a comfort exchange, submitting an attribution claim, resubmitting an attribution claim, and the single-resource reads of a trial order, a return case, a quality claim, a comfort exchange, a refund authorization, and an attribution claim.

The remaining four roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `TrialSupportAgent`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `FinanceController`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where the workflow demands an audit trail — as `approved_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` on a quality claim, and as `authorized_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on a refund authorization — and compares recorded operator identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

Role responsibilities divide as follows. A `Customer` is the trialling household: they open returns, cancel them, keep the mattress, file quality complaints, supply evidence rounds, and request comfort exchanges. A `RetailPartner` is a reseller claiming commission or a price match. A `TrialSupportAgent` is front-line support: they create customer records, open trial orders, adjudicate quality claims, and approve or decline comfort exchanges. A `LogisticsCoordinator` owns the physical world: dispatch, delivery, route creation and publication, appointment booking and closeout, inspection, disposition, and partner intake confirmation. A `FinanceController` owns money: refund authorization, release and void, quality-claim settlement, and attribution-claim review and resolution. A `PlatformAdmin` owns configuration and fraud operations: the mattress catalog, promotional bundles, retail and disposition partners, manually recorded abuse signals, and the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative says otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). All datetime fields anywhere in the API — whether supplied by a client or stamped by the server — use that same format.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, using the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. Whenever a mutation is described in this document as bumping a resource's version, it also refreshes that resource's `updated_at`. The one exception in this system is the abuse signal record: because signals are append-only and are never edited after they are written, a `TrialAbuseSignal` carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. Every other resource type carries all four common properties.

A lazy re-derivation is not a mutation. When a read of a trial order advances its trial clock, or a read of a customer decays their abuse score, no `version` counter moves and no `updated_at` is refreshed; only the derived values change.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a single logical clock which, before any request has moved it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and which never falls back to real time. Every temporal rule in this document — the break-in window, the trial window, the compression-recovery wait, the damage-report window, the retail attribution window, warranty ownership years, fraud-signal decay, and every proration basis — is evaluated against that clock.

An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, taking a single `to_iso8601` value and answering with the new `current_utc`. Advancing the clock has no immediate write side effects; instead it changes the answers that lazy re-derivations produce on subsequent loads, which is how a trial order silently moves from `BREAK_IN` to `TRIAL_ACTIVE` to `TRIAL_EXPIRED` and how a blocked customer eventually returns to a lesser restriction.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents, and every field holding money is named with a `_cents` suffix. Rates and discounts are expressed in basis points — hundredths of a percent — so a value of 2500 basis points means twenty-five percent and a value of 10000 basis points means the whole amount.

Wherever a calculation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties rounded away from zero (round half up). Each rounding step named in this document happens exactly where it is named and nowhere else: where a formula rounds three components separately and then adds the whole-cent results, summing the exact fractions and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect number, and where a formula rounds once at the end, intermediate terms must be carried as exact rationals.

Unit counts, night counts, scores, and basis-point values are integers throughout. Wherever a duration in nights or days is derived from two instants, the derivation takes the whole number of complete 86,400-second days between them, discarding any partial day.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Ledgers

Three write operations mutate a shared parent whose derived state the client must have observed before it can safely write. Each of them therefore requires a non-optional expected-version field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

Booking a pickup appointment — `POST /pickup-appointments` — requires `expected_pickup_route_version` and it must equal the target `PickupRoute`'s current `version`. Booking a second appointment for a different return case in a non-overlapping slot is perfectly legitimate product behaviour, so the write cannot be refused on state grounds; a stale version is the only signal that the client's view of `booked_units` and `available_units` predates another booking that has already consumed capacity. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Creating a refund authorization — `POST /refund-authorizations` — requires `expected_return_case_version` and it must equal the target `ReturnCase`'s current `version`. Because a voided authorization may legitimately be superseded by a fresh one, a second create is not blocked by state either; a stale version is the only signal that the inspection grade, bundle unwind, or damage offset the client priced against has since been re-stamped. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RETURN_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Approving a comfort exchange — `POST /comfort-exchanges/{id}/approve` — requires `expected_trial_order_version` and it must equal the original `TrialOrder`'s current `version`. A customer may hold several outstanding exchange requests, so approving any one of them is legitimate on state grounds; a stale version is the only signal that the order's ledger or trial fields moved after the agent priced the exchange. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

Because most operations in this domain are guarded by many checks at once, the order in which those checks run is part of the contract: validations are evaluated strictly in ascending priority, and the first failing check alone decides the response. The full ordering, from highest precedence downward, is:

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request-body shape: every required field present, no unknown fields, every enum value a declared member, every amount, count and unit value an integer inside its declared range, every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and every conditionally required field present — `measurement_method` and `measured_indentation_mm` when a quality claim's `claim_type` is `WARRANTY_INDENTATION`, `competitor_price_cents` when an attribution claim's `claim_type` is `PRICE_MATCH`, and `retail_partner_id` when a trial order's `channel` is `RETAIL_PARTNER`. Any failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Path-identifier resolution, checked left to right along the URL, answering the not-found code of whichever segment failed first.
5. Foreign-key resolution for identifiers supplied in the request body, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, answering the corresponding not-found code.
6. Ownership for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_OWNER`.
7. Trial-order state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.
8. Return-case state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`.
9. Route-published and appointment-booked state admissibility — `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED` or `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`.
10. State admissibility for quality claims, attribution claims, comfort exchanges, refund authorizations and disposition assignments — `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ATTRIBUTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXCHANGE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REFUND_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.
11. The cross-entity disposition guard on refund creation — `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_CONFIRMED`.
12. Route expected-version match — `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION`.
13. Return-case expected-version match — `ERR_STALE_RETURN_CASE_VERSION`.
14. Trial-order expected-version match — `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`.
15. The fraud posture gate — `ERR_TRIAL_ABUSE_BLOCK`.
16. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
17. The break-in window — `ERR_BREAK_IN_NOT_ELAPSED`.
18. The trial window — `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
19. The compression-recovery wait — `ERR_RECOVERY_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`.
20. The delivery-damage report window — `ERR_DAMAGE_REPORT_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
21. The retail attribution window — `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
22. The household trial limit — `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_TRIAL_LIMIT`.
23. The one-active-return-per-order rule — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_RETURN`.
24. The exchange chain limit — `ERR_EXCHANGE_LIMIT_REACHED`.
25. The exchange size-match rule — `ERR_MODEL_SIZE_MISMATCH`.
26. Quality-claim photographic evidence minimums — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
27. Attribution-claim evidence-link minimum — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
28. The warranty indentation threshold and kept-order requirement — `ERR_INDENTATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.
29. Evidence-round and revision-round ceilings — `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`.
30. Sales-attribution channel match — `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`.
31. Price-match policy availability — `ERR_PRICE_MATCH_NOT_OFFERED`.
32. Slot containment inside the route window — `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_ROUTE_WINDOW`.
33. Slot separation from other slots on the route — `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`.
34. Donation eligibility of the graded unit — `ERR_DONATION_INELIGIBLE`.
35. Existence of an eligible disposition partner — `ERR_NO_DISPOSITION_PARTNER`.
36. The open-quality-claim block on the refund ledger — `ERR_OPEN_QUALITY_CLAIM`.
37. Validity of the superseded refund reference — `ERR_REFUND_SUPERSEDE_INVALID`.
38. Partner daily intake capacity — `ERR_PARTNER_INTAKE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
39. Route unit capacity — `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
40. A strictly positive final refund — `ERR_NO_REFUNDABLE_BALANCE`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve calling out, because they are easy to get backwards. Trial-order state admissibility at step 7 asks only whether the order's two status dimensions are compatible with the operation in the abstract: for opening a return case or requesting a comfort exchange it requires only that `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED` or `DAMAGED_REPORTED` and that `trial_status` is one of `BREAK_IN`, `TRIAL_ACTIVE` or `TRIAL_EXPIRED`. The break-in and trial-window clocks are never consulted at step 7; they are consulted at steps 17 and 18. An order whose trial window has lazily expired therefore reaches step 18 and answers `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, not `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Likewise, because the intake-capacity check sits at step 38 and the route-capacity check at step 39, a booking that is both slot-overlapping and over capacity answers with the overlap.

### 2.9. The Refund Ledger Invariant

Each trial order maintains four money fields that together account for its refundable principal. `paid_amount_cents` is the principal actually charged; `refunded_total_cents` is the sum of the net refunds on that order's released refund authorizations; `outstanding_refundable_cents` is principal still eligible to be refunded; and `retained_amount_cents` is principal the business keeps after a released refund. At every observable instant, `paid_amount_cents` equals `refunded_total_cents` plus `outstanding_refundable_cents` plus `retained_amount_cents`. Any operation that moves one of these fields moves the others in the same request so the identity never breaks, and any reversal restores the pre-movement values exactly.

Goodwill and promotional credits sit outside that identity. `goodwill_credit_cents` accumulates settled quality-claim credit and `price_match_credit_cents` accumulates approved price-match credit; both are additive counters that record what the business paid out as goodwill, and neither is ever subtracted from the refundable principal. Posting either credit therefore leaves the four-way identity untouched.

### 2.10. Conserved Capacity

Two capacity pools in this system are conserved quantities, and both are maintained by explicit addition and subtraction rather than recomputed guesswork.

A pickup route holds `capacity_units` logistics units. `booked_units` is the total units consumed by appointments on that route whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `COMPLETED`; `available_units` is `capacity_units` less `booked_units`. At every observable instant `capacity_units` equals `booked_units` plus `available_units`. A slot that is cancelled or closed as missed returns its units to the pool, because those two statuses are excluded from the sum; a slot that is completed keeps consuming its units forever, because the truck did in fact carry the mattress.

A disposition partner holds `daily_intake_capacity_units` units of intake per calendar `intake_date`. The units booked against a partner for a given date are the total units on that partner's disposition assignments for that date whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `DELIVERED`. An assignment that is rejected at intake frees its units from that date's bucket; an assignment confirmed as delivered holds them permanently. Buckets are keyed strictly by calendar date, so assignments on adjacent intake dates never contend with each other no matter what time of day the pickups happened.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two finance actions may not be performed by the operator who performed the preceding step. Releasing a refund authorization requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `authorized_by_user_id` recorded when the authorization was created. Settling an approved quality claim requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `approved_by_user_id` recorded at adjudication. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both checks compare opaque operator identifiers only; the role in both cases is a finance or support role and the check is about the individual, not the role.

### 2.12. Independent Status Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Two entities in this system carry two status dimensions that advance on entirely different triggers and must never be collapsed into one. A trial order carries `fulfillment_status`, the physical-logistics axis, and `trial_status`, the trial-clock axis. A return case carries `logistics_status`, the physical axis of scheduling, collection, inspection and disposition, and `financial_status`, the money axis, which moves only as a consequence of refund-authorization activity. Dispatching an order, for instance, advances `fulfillment_status` while leaving `trial_status` exactly where it was.

Several operations in this domain mutate more than one entity in a single request. Where that happens, this document states every hop of the cascade, and the implementation must perform all of them atomically: the deepest hop of a cascade is as much part of the contract as the first. Cascades also run in reverse — a rejected partner intake reopens the return case for re-selection, a cancelled appointment gives its units back and reverts the return case, and voiding a released refund reverses every ledger movement the release made. Each entity a cascade touches has its own `version` bumped exactly once per request.

### 2.13. Resource Addressing and Filtered Reads

Every collection in this API is mounted at the top level; there are no nested ownership URLs, and relationships between resources are expressed by identifier fields in request bodies rather than by URL nesting. The one path that does read as a hierarchy — the abuse-signal listing beneath a customer — is still validated left to right: the customer identifier in the path is resolved first, and an unknown customer answers `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` before the listing is attempted.

List endpoints expose filters over the identifier and enumeration fields of the resource they return, and filters combine conjunctively: a request that supplies several filters matches only records satisfying all of them. An unknown filter name, or a filter value that is not a member of the relevant enumeration, is a malformed request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Setting Up the Catalog, the Promotions and the Partner Network

Before a single mattress can be sold, a `PlatformAdmin` populates four reference collections. Registering a mattress model captures its `sku`, which must be unique and non-empty across the catalog, a marketing `name`, the comfort level it ships at as `firmness` (one of `SOFT`, `MEDIUM` or `FIRM`), its `size` (one of `TWIN`, `QUEEN` or `KING`), and its `list_price_cents`, which must be greater than zero and serves as the reference price whenever a comfort exchange has to price a difference between two models. The trial policy for the model travels with it: `break_in_nights` is the minimum number of nights that must elapse after delivery before a return or a comfort exchange may be requested and defaults to 21 nights with zero permitted as a lower bound, while `trial_nights` is the total in-home trial length counted from delivery and defaults to 100 nights, and must always be strictly greater than `break_in_nights`. Two warranty parameters complete the record: `indentation_warranty_threshold_mm` is the minimum measured body impression in millimetres for an indentation warranty claim to be actionable, defaulting to 25 millimetres and required to be greater than zero, and `warranty_years` is the warranty term in whole years, defaulting to ten and required to be greater than zero. Finally, `donation_eligible` marks whether the model's construction permits charitable resale at all; it defaults to true, and a model registered with it false can only ever be recycled.

A promotional bundle is registered with a unique, non-empty `code` and a mandatory `unwind_policy` describing how the promotion is clawed back when the mattress comes back. Three components make up a bundle, each defaulting to zero when omitted: `accessory_retail_cents` is the retail value of the free pillow or protector shipped with the mattress and may not be negative, `percent_off_bps` is the discount applied to the mattress at purchase expressed in basis points of the quoted price and must lie between 0 and 5000 inclusive (so a promotion can never exceed fifty percent off), and `gift_card_cents` is the face value of a gift card issued at purchase and may not be negative. The `unwind_policy` is one of `DEDUCT_RETAIL`, which claws back full retail value, `DEDUCT_PRORATED`, under which the customer earns the gift over the course of the trial, or `FORFEIT_NONE`, under which nothing is clawed back at all. A bundle may also set `requires_accessory_return`, defaulting to false; when it is true and the accessory physically comes back with the mattress, the accessory-linked portion of the claw-back is waived.

Onboarding a retail partner records its storefront `name`, the two-letter `region_code` it sells in, which must be non-empty, its `commission_bps` — the attribution commission in basis points of an order's paid principal, constrained to between 0 and 3000 inclusive — and its `price_match_policy`, one of `MATCH_TO_LOWEST`, `MATCH_TO_ADVERTISED` or `NO_MATCH`. Two optional fields round the record out: `attribution_window_days` is the number of days after delivery during which the partner may submit a sales-attribution or price-match claim, defaulting to 30 and required to be greater than zero, and `status` is the partner's standing, one of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaulting to `ACTIVE`. A `SUSPENDED` partner cannot submit claims.

Charities and recyclers are onboarded into a single collection of disposition partners, distinguished by `partner_type`: a `CHARITY` receives donated units and a `RECYCLER` receives recycled ones. Each partner records a `name`, the `region_code` it serves, which must be non-empty and which must equal the region of the pickup route that collected a unit for that partner to be eligible for it, and `daily_intake_capacity_units`, greater than zero, being the maximum units it will accept on any one calendar intake day. Acceptance rules are captured in `accepts_condition_grades`, a non-empty subset of the four inspection grades `A`, `B`, `C` and `D`, and in `soil_policy`, either `REJECT_SOILED` or `ACCEPT_SOILED`. `priority_rank` is the primary ranking key of the partner selector and must be at least 1, with lower numbers winning. `recycle_fee_cents_per_unit` is the per-unit fee a recycler charges, defaulting to zero — charities charge nothing — and may not be negative. `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; only `ACTIVE` partners ever enter the candidate set. A `LogisticsCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin` can browse this network with a paginated listing filterable by `region_code`, by `partner_type` and by `status`.

### 3.2. Households, Abuse Signals and the Fraud Posture

A customer record carries a unique, non-empty `email`, a display `full_name`, and a `household_hash` — an opaque hash of shipping address and surname that acts as the household fraud key. The hash is deliberately not unique: two customer records sharing a `household_hash` are treated as members of the same household, which is exactly how the system catches a family cycling one mattress after another through free trials under different names. On creation a customer starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, both abuse scores at zero, and every counter at zero.

Four counters are maintained on the customer as the trial business happens around them. `lifetime_trial_count` is the number of that customer's trial orders whose fulfillment has ever reached `DELIVERED`. `completed_return_count` is the number of return cases on that customer's orders whose logistics axis reached `PICKED_UP`. `signal_count` is the number of abuse signals recorded against the customer. And `trial_block_until` is the logical-clock instant until which new trial orders are refused, or null when no block is in force.

Abuse signals are append-only records and are never retracted or edited. Each names the `customer_id` it is recorded against, a `signal_type`, and a `weight`, which the server derives from the signal type rather than accepting from the caller. There are four signal types with fixed weights: `HOUSEHOLD_MATCH` is worth 30 points and fires when another member of the same household has already taken a trial; `REPEAT_RETURN` is worth 25 points and fires when a return completes pickup for a customer who has already completed one; `SHORT_TRIAL` is worth 15 points and fires when a mattress comes back very early in the trial; and `MULTI_CHANNEL` is worth 10 points and fires when the same household has bought through both the direct channel and a retail partner. A signal also carries `recorded_at`, the logical-clock instant the signal is dated, which fraud operations may deliberately backdate to model an older event and which defaults to the current logical clock when omitted, plus an optional free-text `source_note`. Decay is always measured from `recorded_at` and never from the record's `created_at`. Fraud operations can append a signal directly; doing so recomputes both abuse scores, re-derives the customer's posture and block instant, and bumps the customer's `version` by one. A `TrialSupportAgent` or `PlatformAdmin` can list a customer's signals with a filter on `signal_type`.

The abuse score is computed in two variants from exactly the same signal set, with no status filter of any kind. For each signal, the system first takes the whole number of complete days between the signal's `recorded_at` and the current logical clock. Under the rolling variant — which is the variant that gates behaviour — a signal younger than 180 days contributes its full type weight; a signal at least 180 days old but younger than 365 days contributes half its type weight, rounded to the nearest whole point with ties away from zero; and a signal 365 days old or older contributes nothing at all. Under the lifetime variant, every signal contributes its full type weight regardless of age. Each variant sums its per-signal contributions across every signal for the customer and then, once, caps the total at 100 points. The rolling total is stored in `abuse_score_rolling` and the lifetime total in `abuse_score_lifetime`; both therefore always lie between 0 and 100. The lifetime figure is reported to operators and never used as a gate; it is monotonically non-decreasing, and it diverges from the rolling figure as soon as any signal crosses 180 days of age.

Posture is then re-derived from `abuse_score_rolling` alone, in a strictly ordered cascade. If the rolling score is at least 80, the customer's `status` becomes `BLOCKED` and `trial_block_until` is set to the current logical clock plus 365 days. Otherwise, if `trial_block_until` is already set and the clock has not yet reached it, `status` stays `BLOCKED` and the block instant is left alone. Otherwise, if the rolling score is at least 60, `status` becomes `TRIAL_RESTRICTED` and `trial_block_until` is cleared to null. Otherwise `status` becomes `ACTIVE` and `trial_block_until` is cleared to null. The three postures are exactly `ACTIVE`, `TRIAL_RESTRICTED` and `BLOCKED`.

Those thresholds make the posture axis a genuine cycle rather than a ratchet. A customer walks `ACTIVE → TRIAL_RESTRICTED` when their rolling score enters the band from 60 up to but not including 80, and `TRIAL_RESTRICTED → BLOCKED` (or `ACTIVE → BLOCKED` directly, when a single append pushes them past both bands at once) when it reaches 80. Because a restricted customer may still request comfort exchanges, the restricted posture is advisory for exchange purposes and only the blocked posture actually refuses work. Decay then walks the customer back down: once the clock reaches `trial_block_until` and post-decay scoring leaves the rolling score in the 60-to-80 band, the customer moves `BLOCKED → TRIAL_RESTRICTED` and `trial_block_until` is cleared to null; once post-decay scoring drops the rolling score below 60, the customer moves `TRIAL_RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` with no field other than `status` changing. Both downward steps happen lazily, on any request that loads the customer after the clock has advanced, which is why simply reading a customer record can change the posture it reports.

### 3.3. Opening a Trial Order and Getting It Into the Bedroom

A `TrialSupportAgent` or `PlatformAdmin` opens a trial order by naming the `customer_id` of the trialling household member, the `model_id` of the mattress being sold, the sales `channel` of record — either `DTC` or `RETAIL_PARTNER` — and the `gross_price_cents` actually quoted to the customer, which must be greater than zero. Two identifiers are optional: `bundle_id` names a promotional bundle and is left null when the order carries no promotion, and `retail_partner_id` names the reselling partner. When `channel` is `RETAIL_PARTNER`, `retail_partner_id` is mandatory. An unknown customer answers `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `model_id` or `bundle_id` answers `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `retail_partner_id` answers `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`. An unknown trial-order identifier — whether it appears in a URL or as an `order_id` in a request body — answers `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Pricing happens at creation and never again. When no bundle is attached, `bundle_discount_cents` is zero. When a bundle is attached, `bundle_discount_cents` is the quoted `gross_price_cents` multiplied by the bundle's `percent_off_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero. `paid_amount_cents` — the refundable principal for everything downstream — is then `gross_price_cents` less `bundle_discount_cents`. The order's ledger opens with `outstanding_refundable_cents` equal to `paid_amount_cents`, `refunded_total_cents` at zero, and `retained_amount_cents` at zero, which satisfies the four-way money identity trivially. A new order also starts with `goodwill_credit_cents` and `price_match_credit_cents` at zero, `exchange_chain_depth` at zero unless the order was minted by an exchange or a replacement, `exchanged_from_order_id` null unless likewise, `nights_slept` null, `delivered_at` null, and both `break_in_ends_at` and `trial_ends_at` null, since none of the three windows can be computed before the mattress arrives.

Fraud controls fire at creation, in the precedence described in section 2.8. A customer whose posture is `BLOCKED`, or whose `trial_block_until` is set and not yet reached by the logical clock, is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_ABUSE_BLOCK`; the posture is always re-derived from current signal decay immediately before this check, so a block that has expired does not spuriously refuse. Separately, the household limit is enforced on two clauses that must both hold. No *other* customer sharing this customer's `household_hash` may currently hold a trial order whose `fulfillment_status` is `CREATED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED` or `DAMAGED_REPORTED` — that is, a live mattress somewhere in the household. And the household as a whole, counting every customer that shares the hash, may have at most one trial order that has ever reached `DELIVERED`; a second such order is the limit, so the count of already-delivered household orders must be strictly fewer than two. A failure of either clause answers `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_TRIAL_LIMIT`.

An order's physical journey runs along the fulfillment axis, starting at `CREATED`. A `LogisticsCoordinator` dispatching the order advances `fulfillment_status` `CREATED → IN_TRANSIT` and bumps the order's `version`; the trial axis stays at `NOT_STARTED`, because the two axes are wholly independent and no clock starts until the mattress is in the house. Dispatching an order that is not `CREATED`, or delivering one that is not `IN_TRANSIT`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; so is keeping an order that is already returned or filing a claim on one that was never delivered.

Recording delivery is the single most consequential call in the order's life, and it advances `fulfillment_status` `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` while doing five other things. It stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock. It computes `break_in_ends_at` as `delivered_at` plus the model's `break_in_nights` in days, and `trial_ends_at` as `delivered_at` plus the model's `trial_nights` in days. It advances the trial axis `NOT_STARTED → BREAK_IN`, opening both windows. It increments the customer's `lifetime_trial_count` by one. And it runs the two delivery-time fraud detectors: when another customer sharing this customer's `household_hash` already holds a trial order whose fulfillment has reached `DELIVERED`, a `HOUSEHOLD_MATCH` signal is appended, and when the same household already holds a delivered order whose `channel` differs from this order's `channel`, a `MULTI_CHANNEL` signal is appended. Either append — or both, since they are independent tests and can fire on the same delivery — is followed by recomputing `abuse_score_rolling` and `abuse_score_lifetime` and re-deriving the customer's `status` and `trial_block_until`. The order's `version` increments by one.

From `BREAK_IN` onward the trial axis advances on the clock alone, with no request needed to push it. When the logical clock reaches `break_in_ends_at` the order is `TRIAL_ACTIVE`; the boundary is inclusive, so at exactly `break_in_ends_at` the order has already moved. When the clock reaches `trial_ends_at`, again inclusively, the order is `TRIAL_EXPIRED`. These re-derivations happen on any request that loads the order — a single read, a list, or any operation whose validation consults the order — and they change nothing else: no `version` is incremented and no `updated_at` is refreshed by a lazy clock advance. Reading one order returns both status dimensions along with the full refund ledger; the list view can be narrowed by `customer_id`, `model_id`, `bundle_id`, `retail_partner_id`, `exchanged_from_order_id`, `trial_status` or `fulfillment_status`.

When the customer decides to keep the mattress, the trial axis moves to `KEPT` and the fulfillment axis simultaneously moves to `CLOSED`. Keeping is permitted from any of `BREAK_IN`, `TRIAL_ACTIVE` or `TRIAL_EXPIRED`, with identical side effects in all three cases, so a customer who quietly lets the trial window lapse and then confirms weeks later is handled the same as one who commits on night thirty. The one precondition is that no return case on the order is currently in `REQUESTED`, `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP`, `INSPECTED` or `DISPOSITIONED` — a return in flight must be cancelled first. Keeping freezes `nights_slept` at the whole number of complete days between `delivered_at` and the current logical clock, and bumps the order's `version`. `nights_slept` is in general the whole number of complete days from `delivered_at` up to the earlier of the current logical clock and the instant the order froze, whether that freeze came from keeping the mattress or from a completed pickup, and it stays null until delivery.

### 3.4. When the Mattress Disappoints: Quality Complaints and Evidence Rounds

Three quite different complaints share one claim record, distinguished by `claim_type`. A `SHIPPING_DAMAGE` claim reports that the mattress arrived damaged. A `COMPRESSION_RECOVERY` claim reports that a compressed-and-rolled mattress has not decompressed to its proper shape. A `WARRANTY_INDENTATION` claim reports a body impression in a mattress the customer has already decided to keep. A `Customer` or a `TrialSupportAgent` files a claim by naming the `order_id`, the `claim_type`, and the `photo_evidence_count` — the number of photographs attached in this evidence round, which may not be negative. Indentation claims additionally require `measurement_method`, either `STRAIGHTEDGE_PHOTO` or `LASER_SCAN`, and `measured_indentation_mm`, the reported impression depth in millimetres, which may not be negative; both fields stay null on the other two claim types. The server stamps `reported_at` from the logical clock, opens the claim at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `resolution` `PENDING`, `evidence_round_count` at zero, and `credit_cents` at zero. An unknown claim identifier in any later call answers `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

Both delivery-related complaints are time-boxed, in opposite directions. A compression-recovery complaint may not be filed until the mattress has been given its decompression window: the whole number of complete days between `delivered_at` and the logical clock must be at least three nights, and an earlier filing is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. A shipping-damage complaint runs the other way — it must be filed while the delivery is still fresh, so the whole number of complete days since delivery may not exceed seven nights, and a later filing is refused with `ERR_DAMAGE_REPORT_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Photographic evidence has a floor that depends on what is being claimed and how it was measured. A delivery-damage or compression-recovery complaint requires at least two photographs. An indentation claim measured by straightedge requires at least three, because the straightedge has to be visible in relation to the impression; an indentation claim measured by laser scan requires only one, since the scan itself is the evidence. Falling short of the applicable minimum is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, and that same floor is re-checked at every later point where the evidence is relied on — at filing, at each further evidence submission, and again at adjudication.

Indentation claims carry two extra preconditions of their own. The trial order must have `trial_status` `KEPT` — a warranty is about a mattress the customer owns, not one still on trial — and `measured_indentation_mm` must reach the model's `indentation_warranty_threshold_mm`, with the boundary inclusive so a measurement exactly at the threshold qualifies. Failing either is refused with `ERR_INDENTATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, both at filing and again at adjudication.

Filing a delivery-damage or compression-recovery complaint has a visible effect on the order: the fulfillment axis advances `DELIVERED → DAMAGED_REPORTED` and the order's `version` increments by one. The trial axis is untouched, so the trial clock keeps running underneath the complaint. An indentation claim never touches the fulfillment axis at all — the order reached `CLOSED` when the customer kept the mattress — and instead stamps its two derived warranty fields at filing time: `ownership_years` is the whole number of complete 365-day years between `delivered_at` and `reported_at`, and `coverage_bps` is the remaining warranty coverage in basis points after decay. Coverage starts at 10000 basis points and loses 1000 basis points for each completed ownership year, but never falls below a floor of 2000 basis points; in other words, coverage is whichever is larger of 2000 and 10000 less one thousand times `ownership_years`. On the two non-warranty claim types both `ownership_years` and `coverage_bps` remain null.

An agent who is not satisfied with what was submitted can ask for more, moving the claim `SUBMITTED → EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` and bumping its `version`. The customer answers with a fresh round, supplying a new `photo_evidence_count` and, for an indentation claim, a new `measured_indentation_mm`; this moves the claim `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, **replaces** rather than accumulates the previous values, and increments `evidence_round_count` by one. Only the latest round's photograph count ever counts toward the evidence minimum. The loop is bounded: at most two further evidence rounds are permitted, so a submission attempted once `evidence_round_count` has already reached two is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. Requesting evidence on a claim that is not `SUBMITTED`, submitting evidence when none was requested, adjudicating a claim that is already settled, or settling one that was not approved for credit are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Adjudication is a `TrialSupportAgent` decision that takes a single `resolution` value and moves the claim off `SUBMITTED`. Choosing `REPLACEMENT` or `CREDIT` advances the claim to `APPROVED`; choosing `DENIED` advances it to `DENIED`. In every case `approved_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting operator's identifier and `resolution` is stamped with the chosen branch. A `REPLACEMENT` resolution is only available on the two delivery-related claim types — an indentation warranty is settled in money, never with a new mattress.

The two approval branches diverge sharply and both must be implemented in full. A `CREDIT` resolution prices `credit_cents` and then reverts the fulfillment axis `DAMAGED_REPORTED → DELIVERED`, releasing the order from its complaint state; when the order has meanwhile reached `CLOSED` or `REPLACED`, the fulfillment axis is left exactly as it is. That revert additionally requires that no *other* quality claim on the same order is still `SUBMITTED` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`; while a second complaint is open the order legitimately stays in `DAMAGED_REPORTED`. The credit itself depends on the claim type: a shipping-damage credit is the order's `paid_amount_cents` multiplied by 1500 basis points and divided by 10000 — fifteen percent of the principal — rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero; a compression-recovery credit is a flat 7500 cents regardless of the mattress price; and an indentation credit is `paid_amount_cents` multiplied by the claim's `coverage_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded the same way, so a mattress in its first ownership year settles at full principal and one past eight completed years settles at the twenty-percent floor. A `DENIED` resolution stamps `credit_cents` at zero and applies exactly the same conditional fulfillment revert: `DAMAGED_REPORTED → DELIVERED` when the order is still in `DAMAGED_REPORTED`, and no change to a `CLOSED` or `REPLACED` order.

A `REPLACEMENT` resolution instead mints a fresh trial order and retires the old one. The replacement is created with the *same* `model_id` as the original and its `gross_price_cents` copied from the original, with `bundle_id` null — a replacement never re-issues the promotion — with `exchanged_from_order_id` pointing at the original, with `exchange_chain_depth` one greater than the original's, and with `fulfillment_status` `CREATED` and `trial_status` `NOT_STARTED`, so the replacement has to be dispatched and delivered like any other order and starts its own break-in and trial windows from scratch. The original order simultaneously moves `fulfillment_status` `DAMAGED_REPORTED → REPLACED` and `trial_status` to `EXCHANGED`; because a damage complaint can be adjudicated while the original is still inside its break-in window, the trial axis reaches `EXCHANGED` from either `BREAK_IN` or `TRIAL_ACTIVE`, with identical effect. The trial-axis move requires that no return case on the original order is currently in `REQUESTED`, `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP`, `INSPECTED` or `DISPOSITIONED`. The claim's `version` increments by one.

Money only actually moves when finance settles an approved credit. Settlement is a `FinanceController` action, permitted only when the claim is `APPROVED` with `resolution` `CREDIT`, and only when the acting operator's identifier differs from `approved_by_user_id`; the same person may not both approve and pay. Settling advances the claim `APPROVED → SETTLED`, records `settled_by_user_id`, adds the claim's `credit_cents` to the order's `goodwill_credit_cents`, and bumps both the claim's `version` and the order's `version`. Because goodwill sits outside the refundable principal, settling a credit never disturbs the order's four-way money identity. Claims can be read individually or listed with filters on `order_id`, `claim_type` and `status`.

One more path can end a claim, and it comes from outside the claim's own workflow: when a comfort exchange is approved on the order, every compression-recovery claim on that order still sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with `resolution` `DENIED` and `credit_cents` zero. An open compression complaint does not block a comfort exchange, because approving the exchange *is* the remedy; the complaint is withdrawn rather than adjudicated. The three terminal claim statuses are therefore `DENIED`, `SETTLED` and `WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.5. Trading Firmness: The Comfort Exchange

Many customers do not want their money back; they want a different feel. A comfort exchange moves the household from one `firmness` to another within the same bed `size`, and it is requested by a `Customer` or a `TrialSupportAgent` naming just two things: the `original_order_id` of the order being swapped out and the `target_model_id` of the requested replacement model. Everything else is priced by the server and frozen onto the request row, which opens at `status` `REQUESTED`. An unknown exchange identifier in a later call answers `ERR_EXCHANGE_NOT_FOUND`.

Three gates guard the request. The exchange chain is bounded at two exchanges: `sequence_number`, defined as the original order's `exchange_chain_depth` plus one, may not exceed two, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_EXCHANGE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Declining an exchange does not consume a link in that chain — only an approved exchange raises `exchange_chain_depth` on the order it mints, so a customer whose first two requests were declined can still request again. The target model must be a genuinely different model of the same bed size: a target whose `size` differs from the original order's model, or a target that is the original's own model, is refused with `ERR_MODEL_SIZE_MISMATCH`. And the trial clock applies exactly as it does to returns — the break-in window must have elapsed and the trial window must not yet have closed, answering `ERR_BREAK_IN_NOT_ELAPSED` or `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` respectively, with a blocked customer refused earlier still by `ERR_TRIAL_ABUSE_BLOCK`.

Pricing an exchange runs in a pinned order of seven steps, and the order matters because the loyalty tier is keyed on the *raw* difference rather than the final one. First, `raw_price_difference_cents` is the target model's `list_price_cents` less the original order's model's `list_price_cents`; it is negative when the customer is trading down. Second, `sequence_number` is set to the original order's `exchange_chain_depth` plus one. Third, `exchange_fee_cents` is an escalating handling fee: 9900 cents for the first exchange in a chain, rising by 5000 cents for each subsequent position — that is, 9900 cents plus 5000 cents multiplied by one less than `sequence_number` — and capped so it never exceeds 24900 cents, the fee being whichever is smaller of the cap and the escalating figure. Fourth, the loyalty tier is chosen from the absolute value of `raw_price_difference_cents`: below 20000 cents the tier credit rate is zero basis points; from 20000 cents up to but not including 50000 cents it is 250 basis points; at 50000 cents and above it is 500 basis points. Fifth, `tier_credit_cents` is the original order's `paid_amount_cents` multiplied by that tier rate and divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero — the only rounding step in the whole exchange calculation. Sixth, `price_difference_cents` is `raw_price_difference_cents` plus `exchange_fee_cents` less `tier_credit_cents`. Seventh, `settlement_direction` is `CHARGE_CUSTOMER` when `price_difference_cents` is greater than zero, `CREDIT_CUSTOMER` when it is less than zero, and `EVEN` when it is exactly zero. Keying the tier on the raw difference is deliberate: keying it on the post-fee, post-credit figure would make the definition circular.

Approval is a `TrialSupportAgent` action and it is the one exchange operation under optimistic concurrency, carrying `expected_trial_order_version` which must equal the original order's current `version`; a mismatch answers `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`. Approval further requires that the exchange still be `REQUESTED` — approving or declining an already-decided exchange answers `ERR_INVALID_EXCHANGE_STATE` — that the original order's `fulfillment_status` be either `DELIVERED` or `DAMAGED_REPORTED`, that its `trial_status` be exactly `TRIAL_ACTIVE`, and that the owning customer's posture not be `BLOCKED`.

An approval then executes a three-hop cascade in one request, and all three hops are part of the contract. Hop one mints the replacement trial order: its `model_id` is the exchange's `target_model_id`, its `gross_price_cents` is that target model's `list_price_cents`, its `bundle_id` is null, its `channel` and `retail_partner_id` are copied from the original order, its `exchanged_from_order_id` points at the `original_order_id`, its `exchange_chain_depth` is one greater than the original's, and it opens at `fulfillment_status` `CREATED` and `trial_status` `NOT_STARTED`. The new order's `id` is stored on the exchange as `replacement_order_id`, which is null until this moment. Hop two retires the original: because a replacement now exists, the original order moves `trial_status` `TRIAL_ACTIVE → EXCHANGED` and `fulfillment_status` to `REPLACED` — from `DELIVERED` in the ordinary case, or from `DAMAGED_REPORTED` when a compression complaint was still open. Hop three unwinds those complaints: because the original order has entered `EXCHANGED`, every quality claim on it whose `claim_type` is `COMPRESSION_RECOVERY` and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with `resolution` `DENIED`. The original order's `version` increments by one, and the exchange itself becomes `APPROVED`.

Declining is the quieter branch and its symmetry with approval matters: an agent declining supplies a `decline_note`, the exchange moves `REQUESTED → DECLINED` with the note stored and its `version` bumped, and the original trial order is left entirely untouched — no status change on either axis, no version bump, no claim withdrawal — so the customer remains free to request another exchange. `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are both terminal. Exchanges can be read individually with their settled price difference or listed with filters on `original_order_id`, `replacement_order_id`, `target_model_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. Asking for a Return

Opening a return case takes two inputs from a `Customer` or a `TrialSupportAgent`: the `order_id` of the order being returned and a `reason_code`, one of `COMFORT`, `DAMAGE`, `PRICE` or `OTHER`. The server stamps `requested_at` from the logical clock and opens the case with `logistics_status` `REQUESTED`, `financial_status` `PENDING`, `disposition` `PENDING`, and `nights_used` null — nothing is priced until the mattress has actually been collected and graded. On the order side the trial axis moves `TRIAL_ACTIVE → RETURN_REQUESTED` — that is, the order's `trial_status` becomes `RETURN_REQUESTED` — and the order's `version` increments by one. An unknown return-case identifier in a later call answers `ERR_RETURN_CASE_NOT_FOUND`.

The break-in window is the heart of the trial promise, and it is enforced here: the logical clock must have reached `break_in_ends_at`, with the boundary inclusive so a return opened at exactly that instant is accepted. An earlier request is refused with `ERR_BREAK_IN_NOT_ELAPSED`. Exactly one path bypasses that gate. When `reason_code` is `DAMAGE` *and* the order already carries a quality claim whose `claim_type` is `SHIPPING_DAMAGE` and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, the return may be opened while the order's trial axis is still in `BREAK_IN`, moving it `BREAK_IN → RETURN_REQUESTED`; nobody should be forced to sleep three weeks on a mattress that arrived torn. Every other `reason_code` inside the break-in window is refused. At the far end, the trial window is a hard stop for both returns and exchanges: the logical clock must be strictly before `trial_ends_at`, and a request at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

One order may only have one live return at a time. If any return case on the order is currently in `REQUESTED`, `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP`, `INSPECTED` or `DISPOSITIONED`, a second request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_RETURN`. A cancelled case does not count, so a customer who changes their mind twice is free to reopen. Inspecting a case before pickup, dispositioning one before inspection, cancelling one after collection, or authorizing a refund on a cancelled case are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`.

Cancelling a return is permitted while the case is `REQUESTED` or `PICKUP_SCHEDULED` and while its `financial_status` is still `PENDING`. Cancellation sets `logistics_status` to `CANCELLED` and `financial_status` to `VOID`, discards the refund pricing, and hands the order back to its trial: the trial axis reverts `RETURN_REQUESTED → TRIAL_ACTIVE` when the logical clock is still before `trial_ends_at`, and lands in `TRIAL_EXPIRED` instead when the clock has already passed it. That makes the trial axis a genuine cycle, `TRIAL_ACTIVE → RETURN_REQUESTED → TRIAL_ACTIVE`. If a pickup slot had already been booked, cancelling the return also sets that `BOOKED` appointment to `CANCELLED` and returns its units to the route's `available_units`, bumping the route's `version`. Both the order's and the return case's `version` increment. `CANCELLED` is the return's only terminal logistics state. Return cases can be read individually — showing both status dimensions and the full refund breakdown — or listed with filters on `order_id`, `logistics_status`, `financial_status` and `disposition`.

### 3.7. Routing the Truck: Routes, Slots and Collection

A `LogisticsCoordinator` plans collections a region and a day at a time. Creating a pickup route records the `region_code` it serves, which must be non-empty, the `route_date` as a calendar day in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the inclusive `window_start_at` and exclusive `window_end_at` bounding the service window, with the end required to be strictly after the start, and `capacity_units`, the total logistics units the truck can collect that day, which must be greater than zero. An optional `buffer_minutes` sets the mandatory drive buffer that must separate two slots on the route; it defaults to 30 minutes and may not be negative. A new route opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `booked_units` at zero and `available_units` equal to `capacity_units`. An unknown route identifier answers `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`.

Publishing the route moves it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, requires that it currently be `DRAFT` and that `window_end_at` still be strictly after `window_start_at`, and bumps its `version`. `PUBLISHED` is terminal — a route is never unpublished. Appointments may only be booked on a `PUBLISHED` route; booking against a route still in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Routes can be read individually, showing their capacity ledger.

Booking a slot names the `route_id`, the `return_case_id` being collected, the inclusive `slot_start_at` and exclusive `slot_end_at` of the visit — the end must be strictly after the start — and the `expected_pickup_route_version` that section 2.7 requires. The slot's `units` are not supplied by the caller; they are derived from the bed size of the returned order's model, with a `TWIN` counting as one unit, a `QUEEN` as two and a `KING` as three. The route's `region_code` is the region the appointment is being booked into, and it is the same region that later constrains which charities and recyclers can receive the unit.

Three geometric and one arithmetic constraint apply to the booking, in the precedence given in section 2.8. First, the slot must lie wholly inside the service window: `slot_start_at` must be at or after `window_start_at`, `slot_end_at` must be at or before `window_end_at`, and both instants must fall on the route's `route_date` when read in UTC. A slot that spills outside is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_ROUTE_WINDOW`. Second, the slot must be separated from every other slot on the same route whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `COMPLETED` by at least the route's `buffer_minutes`: treating each slot as the half-open interval from its start up to but not including its end, the new slot's `slot_start_at` must be at or after the existing slot's `slot_end_at` advanced by the buffer, or the new slot's `slot_end_at` advanced by the buffer must be at or before the existing slot's `slot_start_at`. One consequence is worth spelling out: two exactly adjacent slots with no gap between them collide whenever `buffer_minutes` is greater than zero. A collision is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`. Third, the route's capacity must accommodate the booking: the route's current `booked_units` plus the new slot's `units` may not exceed `capacity_units`. Exact equality is allowed — a truck may be filled precisely to the brim — and only strictly exceeding it is refused, with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Cancelled and missed slots are excluded from `booked_units`, which is exactly what makes cancellation a conserving compensation rather than a leak.

A successful booking creates the appointment at `status` `BOOKED` with `closed_at` null, adds its `units` to the route's `booked_units` while re-deriving `available_units`, moves the return case `REQUESTED → PICKUP_SCHEDULED`, and bumps both the route's and the return case's `version`. Appointments can be read individually or listed with filters on `route_id`, `return_case_id` and `status`; an unknown appointment identifier answers `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and completing or cancelling an appointment that is not `BOOKED` answers `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`.

Closing out a visit takes a single `outcome` value and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric. An `outcome` of `MISSED` — nobody home, or the crew ran out of daylight — advances the appointment `BOOKED → MISSED`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, subtracts the slot's `units` from the route's `booked_units` so `available_units` is restored, reverts the return case `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → REQUESTED`, and bumps the route's `version`. Cancelling a booked slot outright does the same thing to the capacity ledger and the return case, advancing the appointment `BOOKED → CANCELLED` and again bumping the route's `version`; the difference is only in the audit trail, since a cancelled slot records no `closed_at`. Either way the return case is back in `REQUESTED` and may be rebooked on any published route, which makes the logistics axis a cycle: `REQUESTED → PICKUP_SCHEDULED → REQUESTED`. `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal appointment statuses.

An `outcome` of `COMPLETED` — requiring the appointment to be `BOOKED` and its route to be `PUBLISHED` — is the four-hop cascade at the centre of the whole reverse-logistics flow, and every hop must land in the same request. The appointment advances `BOOKED → COMPLETED` and stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock; the route's `booked_units` is deliberately left unchanged, because a completed collection genuinely consumed that capacity. Hop one moves the return case `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → PICKED_UP`, stamps `picked_up_at` from the logical clock, and computes `nights_used` as the whole number of complete days between the order's `delivered_at` and `picked_up_at` — this is the proration basis both refund variants will read. Hop two, because the return case has entered `PICKED_UP`, advances the order's trial axis `RETURN_REQUESTED → RETURNED` and its fulfillment axis to `CLOSED`, freezing `nights_slept` at the case's `nights_used`. The fulfillment axis reaches `CLOSED` from `DELIVERED` in the ordinary case and from `DAMAGED_REPORTED` when a quality complaint is still unresolved: the unit is physically collected regardless, and the open complaint instead blocks the refund ledger later rather than blocking the truck. Hop three, because the order has entered `RETURNED`, increments the customer's `completed_return_count` by one and appends abuse signals — a `REPEAT_RETURN` signal when `completed_return_count` was already at least one *before* this increment, and additionally a `SHORT_TRIAL` signal when the case's `nights_used` is fewer than 30 nights. The two are independent, so a repeat returner who sends a mattress back on night twelve earns both. Hop four, because at least one signal was appended, recomputes `abuse_score_rolling` and `abuse_score_lifetime`, re-derives the customer's `status`, and sets `trial_block_until` to the logical clock plus 365 days whenever the rolling score has reached 80. The appointment's, the route's, the return case's and the order's `version` counters each increment by one.

### 3.8. Grading the Unit and Choosing Between Donation and Recycling

Once a mattress is back at the warehouse, a `LogisticsCoordinator` grades it, moving the return case `PICKED_UP → INSPECTED`. Inspection captures three findings: `condition_grade`, one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D` and null before inspection; `soil_flag`, the soiling finding, defaulting to false; and `accessory_returned`, recording whether the bundle accessory physically came back with the mattress, also defaulting to false. Inspection is also the first moment the refund can be priced, and it stamps four money fields onto the case, bumping the case's `version` by one.

The condition offset is the simplest of the three deductions. Each grade carries a retention rate in basis points — grade `A` zero, grade `B` 500, grade `C` 1500 and grade `D` 3000 — and a soiled unit adds a further 1000 basis points on top of the grade's rate. The combined rate is then capped at 4000 basis points, so the worst possible unit retains forty percent and no more. `damage_offset_cents` is the order's `paid_amount_cents` multiplied by whichever is smaller of the combined rate and the 4000-basis-point cap, divided by 10000, and rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero.

The bundle unwind is the most intricate, because a promotion is three separate gifts and each is clawed back on its own terms. When the order carries no `bundle_id` the unwind is zero. Otherwise three components are evaluated in a fixed order and — this matters — **each is rounded to whole cents before they are added**; summing the three exact fractions and rounding once at the end produces a different and incorrect figure. The accessory is waived entirely when the bundle's `requires_accessory_return` is true and the inspection recorded `accessory_returned` as true. The first component covers the accessory's value: it is zero when the `unwind_policy` is `FORFEIT_NONE` or the accessory was waived; it is the full `accessory_retail_cents` when the policy is `DEDUCT_RETAIL`; and under `DEDUCT_PRORATED` it is `accessory_retail_cents` multiplied by the unearned share of the trial — that is, by the model's `trial_nights` less whichever is smaller of `nights_used` and `trial_nights`, divided by `trial_nights` — rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero, so the customer earns the gift progressively over the trial. The second component reallocates the mattress discount to the accessory the customer is keeping: it is zero when the policy is `FORFEIT_NONE`, when the accessory was waived, or when `accessory_retail_cents` is zero, and is otherwise the order's `bundle_discount_cents` multiplied by `accessory_retail_cents` and divided by the sum of `accessory_retail_cents` and the order's `gross_price_cents`, rounded the same way. The third component covers the gift card: zero under `FORFEIT_NONE`, the full `gift_card_cents` under `DEDUCT_RETAIL`, and under `DEDUCT_PRORATED` the `gift_card_cents` multiplied by the same unearned trial share — `trial_nights` less whichever is smaller of `nights_used` and `trial_nights`, over `trial_nights` — rounded the same way. `bundle_unwind_cents` is the integer sum of those three whole-cent components; components that do not apply contribute zero rather than being skipped.

Proration is where the two refund variants part company, and it is the *only* term that differs between them. Both start from `gross_refundable_cents`, which is the order's `paid_amount_cents`, and both subtract the same `bundle_unwind_cents` and the same `damage_offset_cents`. The **provisional** variant, stamped at inspection, prorates by actual usage: it takes `gross_refundable_cents`, multiplies by whichever is smaller of `nights_used` and the model's `trial_nights`, multiplies by a flat usage-retention rate of 2500 basis points, divides by the model's `trial_nights` multiplied by 10000, and rounds the single resulting quantity to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero. The deduction therefore grows linearly with nights slept, reaching a quarter of the principal only if the customer used the entire trial. `provisional_refund_cents` is `gross_refundable_cents` less that provisional proration, less `bundle_unwind_cents`, less `damage_offset_cents`, and at inspection `proration_deduction_cents` holds the provisional figure.

The **final** variant, computed only when finance authorizes the money, prorates by a flat tier band instead: `nights_used` of 30 or fewer retains nothing at all, from 31 through 60 nights retains 500 basis points, from 61 through 100 nights retains 1200 basis points, and beyond 100 nights retains 2000 basis points. `gross_refundable_cents` is multiplied by the band's rate, divided by 10000, and rounded once to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero. `final_refund_cents` is then `gross_refundable_cents` less that tiered proration, less the same `bundle_unwind_cents`, less the same `damage_offset_cents`. Because one curve is linear and the other is a staircase, the two variants disagree on almost every input — a customer returning on night twenty-eight sees a provisional deduction but a final one of zero, while a customer returning on night ninety-nine sees the staircase bite harder than the line. A computed read on the return case exposes exactly this, reporting `variant_in_effect`, `gross_refundable_cents`, `proration_deduction_cents`, `bundle_unwind_cents`, `damage_offset_cents`, `provisional_refund_cents`, `final_refund_cents` and `variant_delta_cents`, the difference between the two variants. Cancelled returns are never priced at all.

Once the unit is graded, the coordinator chooses its fate, supplying a requested `disposition` of either `DONATION` or `RECYCLE` together with the `intake_date` — a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar day — on which the unit will be delivered to the receiving partner. The case must be `INSPECTED` and its `disposition` must still be `PENDING`. A donation request is pre-filtered before any partner is even considered: the mattress model's `donation_eligible` must be true, the `condition_grade` must be `A` or `B`, and `soil_flag` must be false. A request failing any of those three is refused outright with `ERR_DONATION_INELIGIBLE` — a soiled or badly worn mattress is not something a charity should be offered.

Partner selection then runs a waterfall over partner types. A donation request tries `CHARITY` partners first and, if none can take the unit, falls through to `RECYCLER` partners; a recycle request considers only `RECYCLER` partners. Within a type, a partner is eligible when all of the following hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE`; its `partner_type` matches the type currently being tried; its `region_code` equals the `region_code` of the pickup route whose appointment for this return case completed; the case's `condition_grade` is a member of the partner's `accepts_condition_grades`; either the case's `soil_flag` is false or the partner's `soil_policy` is `ACCEPT_SOILED`; and the units already booked against that partner for the requested `intake_date` plus this unit's `units` does not exceed the partner's `daily_intake_capacity_units`. The units already booked for a partner on a date are the sum of `units` over that partner's assignments for that `intake_date` whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `DELIVERED`; a rejected assignment has given its units back and no longer counts.

When a type yields at least one eligible partner, the winner is chosen deterministically: lowest `priority_rank` first; among equal ranks, the partner with the greatest remaining intake headroom for that date — its `daily_intake_capacity_units` less its already-booked units — wins; and if that is still tied, the partner with the earliest `created_at` wins. No random or identifier-based tie-break ever participates. The assignment is then created at `status` `ASSIGNED`, recording the chosen `partner_id`, the `intake_date`, the `units` — the same unit count the pickup appointment consumed, derived from the same bed size — a `disposition_type` of `DONATION` when the winner is a `CHARITY` and `RECYCLE` when it is a `RECYCLER`, a `fee_cents` equal to `units` multiplied by the winner's `recycle_fee_cents_per_unit` (which is zero for charities), and `fallback_applied` set to true precisely when a donation was requested but the waterfall fell through to a recycler. The return case's `disposition` is set from the winner's `partner_type`, its `logistics_status` moves `INSPECTED → DISPOSITIONED`, and its `version` increments by one. The two arms genuinely differ in outcome: the donation arm draws from the charity pool and normally books a zero fee, while the recycle arm draws from the recycler pool and books a real per-unit charge.

When the waterfall exhausts every type without finding an eligible partner, the two failure codes are distinguished by *why* nobody could take it, tested in a fixed order. If some partner of the last type tried satisfies every eligibility clause except the capacity clause — the right region, the right grade, the right soil policy, but its day is already full — the refusal is `ERR_PARTNER_INTAKE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Otherwise the refusal is `ERR_NO_DISPOSITION_PARTNER`.

Confirming intake closes the assignment and takes a single `outcome`. An `outcome` of `DELIVERED` advances the assignment `ASSIGNED → DELIVERED`, and the partner's `intake_date` bucket keeps those units permanently; this is also the event that unlocks refund authorization for the return case. An `outcome` of `REJECTED` advances the assignment `ASSIGNED → REJECTED`, frees its units from the partner's `intake_date` bucket as a conserving compensation, and reopens the return case — `logistics_status` reverts `DISPOSITIONED → INSPECTED` and `disposition` reverts to `PENDING` so a different partner can be selected — incrementing the case's `version` by one. That makes the disposition step a cycle, `INSPECTED → DISPOSITIONED → INSPECTED`, and `DELIVERED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for the assignment. Confirming intake on an assignment that is not `ASSIGNED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, and an unknown assignment identifier answers `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Assignments can be read individually or listed with filters on `return_case_id`, `partner_id`, `intake_date` and `status`.

### 3.9. Authorizing, Releasing and Clawing Back the Refund

The refund ledger is the last stop, and it is deliberately the most heavily guarded operation in the system. A `FinanceController` authorizes a refund by naming the `return_case_id`, the `expected_return_case_version` required by section 2.7, and optionally a `supersedes_refund_id`. Four cross-entity conditions must hold. The return case's `logistics_status` must be `DISPOSITIONED`. The assignment created for that case must have reached `status` `DELIVERED`; authorizing before the charity or recycler has physically accepted the unit is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_CONFIRMED`. No quality claim on the case's trial order may still be `SUBMITTED` or `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` — an unresolved complaint blocks the ledger, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_OPEN_QUALITY_CLAIM`. This is precisely why a pickup is allowed to complete on an order still in `DAMAGED_REPORTED`: the truck is not held up, the money is.

The supersede reference has its own rule with two branches. When `supersedes_refund_id` is supplied, it must reference a refund authorization on this same return case whose `status` is `VOIDED`. When it is omitted, no non-voided authorization may already exist on this return case. Either violation is refused with `ERR_REFUND_SUPERSEDE_INVALID`, while an identifier that resolves to nothing at all answers `ERR_REFUND_NOT_FOUND`.

Authorization computes the final variant of the settlement described in section 3.8 and copies the whole breakdown onto the ledger row: `gross_refundable_cents` is the order's `paid_amount_cents` at authorization time, `proration_deduction_cents` is the tier-based final proration, `bundle_unwind_cents` and `damage_offset_cents` are recomputed against the current inspection findings, and `net_refund_cents` is `gross_refundable_cents` less `proration_deduction_cents` less `bundle_unwind_cents` less `damage_offset_cents`. That net figure must be strictly greater than zero: a refund that computes to exactly zero, or to a negative number because deductions and offsets have consumed the whole balance, is refused with `ERR_NO_REFUNDABLE_BALANCE`. The row opens at `status` `AUTHORIZED`, copies the case's `order_id` onto itself, records `authorized_by_user_id` from the acting operator's identifier, and leaves `released_by_user_id`, `released_at`, `voided_at` and `void_reason` null. On the return case, `final_refund_cents` is stamped with the net figure, `proration_deduction_cents` is **overwritten** with the final-variant value — replacing the provisional figure written at inspection — the money axis moves `PENDING → AUTHORIZED`, and the case's `version` increments by one.

Releasing the refund is the moment cash leaves the business, and it is the segregation-of-duties boundary: the row must be `AUTHORIZED` and the acting operator's identifier must differ from `authorized_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Release advances the row `AUTHORIZED → RELEASED`, records `released_by_user_id` and stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, and then rewrites the order's ledger so the four-way identity of section 2.9 still holds: `net_refund_cents` is added to `refunded_total_cents`, `outstanding_refundable_cents` is set to zero, and `retained_amount_cents` is set to `paid_amount_cents` less `refunded_total_cents`. Because the order's `refunded_total_cents` has become greater than zero, the return case's money axis follows with `AUTHORIZED → RELEASED`. Both the order's and the return case's `version` counters increment.

Voiding takes a `void_reason` and reaches `VOIDED` from either of the two live statuses, but what it has to undo differs. Voiding an `AUTHORIZED` row stamps `voided_at`, moves the case's money axis to `VOID`, and touches no ledger field at all, because no money ever moved. Voiding a `RELEASED` row stamps `voided_at`, moves the case's money axis `RELEASED → VOID`, and performs the exact compensating reversal: `net_refund_cents` is subtracted from the order's `refunded_total_cents`, `outstanding_refundable_cents` is restored to `paid_amount_cents` less `refunded_total_cents`, and `retained_amount_cents` returns to zero once `refunded_total_cents` has come back to zero. The identity that `paid_amount_cents` equals `refunded_total_cents` plus `outstanding_refundable_cents` plus `retained_amount_cents` holds before, during and after the claw-back. Releasing a voided row, releasing twice, or voiding an already-voided row are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFUND_STATE`. `VOIDED` is terminal for the ledger row itself.

The money axis of the return case, by contrast, has no terminal state, and that is the point of the supersede path. A voided settlement can be re-authorized with a fresh row that points at the voided one, recomputed against whatever the current state now says — perhaps the unit was re-graded, perhaps a different partner accepted it — carrying the case's money axis `VOID → AUTHORIZED` and completing the cycle `PENDING → AUTHORIZED → VOID → AUTHORIZED`. This is exactly why authorization needs optimistic concurrency rather than a state guard: a second create is legitimate, so only a stale `expected_return_case_version` can tell the client that the numbers it priced against have since been re-stamped. Ledger rows can be read individually with their full deduction breakdown, or listed with filters on `order_id`, `return_case_id`, `supersedes_refund_id` and `status`.

### 3.10. Retail Partner Attribution and Price Matching

Retail resellers interact with the platform through a single claim record used for two quite different asks, distinguished by `claim_type`. A `SALES_ATTRIBUTION` claim asks for the commission owed on an order the partner sourced. A `PRICE_MATCH` claim asks for a customer credit against a competitor's advertised price. A claim is filed by a `RetailPartner` — or by a `PlatformAdmin` acting on their behalf — naming the `partner_id`, the `order_id`, the `claim_type`, and the `evidence_url_count`, being the number of supporting evidence links attached in the current round, which may not be negative. A price-match claim additionally requires `competitor_price_cents`, the advertised competitor price, which must be greater than zero and stays null on a sales-attribution claim. The server stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock and opens the claim at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round_count` at zero, `commission_cents` at zero, `price_match_credit_cents` at zero and `resolved_at` null. A partner whose standing is `SUSPENDED` may not submit claims at all. An unknown attribution-claim identifier answers `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

Three eligibility rules are checked both when the claim is filed and again when it is resolved, because state can move underneath a claim while it sits in review. The attribution window is the whole number of complete days between the order's `delivered_at` and the claim's `submitted_at`, and it may not exceed the partner's `attribution_window_days`; the boundary is inclusive, so a claim submitted exactly on the window's last day is still open, and anything later is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A sales-attribution claim additionally requires that the order's `channel` be `RETAIL_PARTNER` and that its `retail_partner_id` equal the claiming `partner_id`; a claim on a direct-channel order, or on an order attributed to somebody else, is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. A price-match claim additionally requires that the partner's `price_match_policy` not be `NO_MATCH`, and a claim against a partner who simply does not price-match is refused with `ERR_PRICE_MATCH_NOT_OFFERED`. Independently, at least two evidence links are required on any claim, whether at first submission or on a resubmission, and falling short answers `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.

Review is a `FinanceController` action taking a single `action` value. An `action` of `ACCEPT_FOR_REVIEW` advances the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. An `action` of `REQUEST_REVISION` sends a claim already under review back to the partner, advancing it `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`; it is permitted only while `revision_round_count` is still below two, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. Either action bumps the claim's `version`. The partner answers a revision request by resubmitting with a new `evidence_url_count` and, on a price-match claim, a new `competitor_price_cents`; resubmission **replaces** the previous values rather than accumulating them, increments `revision_round_count` by one, re-stamps `submitted_at` from the current logical clock, and advances the claim `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. It is likewise bounded at two rounds. Because `submitted_at` is re-stamped, a partner who lets a revision sit too long can push their own claim outside the attribution window. Resolving a claim that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, or resubmitting one that is not `REVISION_REQUESTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ATTRIBUTION_STATE`; the loop `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` is the only cycle in this workflow.

Resolution takes a single `decision`. A `decision` of `REJECT` advances the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, and leaves both `commission_cents` and `price_match_credit_cents` at zero; rejected and revision-pending claims contribute nothing to any order's credit totals. A `decision` of `APPROVE` advances the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stamps `resolved_at`, and prices the claim according to its type. For a sales-attribution claim, `commission_cents` is the order's `paid_amount_cents` multiplied by the partner's `commission_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero, and `price_match_credit_cents` stays zero. For a price-match claim, the raw gap is the order's `gross_price_cents` less the claim's `competitor_price_cents`, floored at zero so a competitor who is more expensive yields nothing. Under a `MATCH_TO_LOWEST` policy the credit is that whole raw gap. Under a `MATCH_TO_ADVERTISED` policy the credit is whichever is smaller of the raw gap and a cap of 1000 basis points of the order's `gross_price_cents` — ten percent — that cap being rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties away from zero. `commission_cents` stays zero on a price-match claim. Approving a price-match claim also adds the resulting `price_match_credit_cents` to the order's own `price_match_credit_cents` counter and bumps the order's `version`; the refundable principal is never touched, so `paid_amount_cents` continues to equal `refunded_total_cents` plus `outstanding_refundable_cents` plus `retained_amount_cents`. The claim's `version` increments on resolution, and `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal. Claims can be read individually with their priced commission or credit, or listed with filters on `partner_id`, `order_id`, `claim_type` and `status`.
