# Rent-To-Own Store API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A rent-to-own retailer sells nothing at the counter. It hands a sofa or a refrigerator to a household that cannot or will not finance it, collects a modest amount every week or every month, and transfers ownership only after the last renewal payment lands. Nobody's credit file is pulled; instead the store forms its own judgement from an employer, a residence history, a stated income and a handful of personal references it telephones itself. In exchange for that risk the store discloses — and the customer signs off on — a total of payments that is materially larger than the cash price of the goods, offers an early purchase option that shrinks as rent accumulates, and undertakes to service the product for as long as the agreement runs.

The operational difficulty is that almost none of this is at the retailer's discretion. Each state writes its own rent-to-own statute: how long a customer has to reinstate an agreement after missing a payment, whether a late fee may be charged at all and how large it may be, which of two early-purchase formulas is mandatory, how many times a collector may telephone in a week and during which hours, and the point at which a customer has paid so much toward ownership that the store loses the right to repossess. This API is the system of record for that variance. Every agreement pins the statutory rule that governed it at signing, so a later amendment to a state's rules never retroactively re-prices a signed deal, and every money movement is recorded in integer cents on a ledger that must reconcile exactly after a posting, after a reversal and after a repossession.

Two engineering conventions shape the whole specification. First, the system is driven by a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time: due dates, reinstatement expiries, quiet hours and quote validity are all evaluated against a clock the test harness sets explicitly, and no row ever changes because time simply passed. Second, the arrears machinery is an explicit **ops-run checkpoint**: late fees are assessed, reinstatement windows are opened and expired, and agreements are defaulted only when a store's day-close is run. Collections are consequently reproducible, auditable, and — importantly for a regulated business — attributable to a named operator.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every resource in this system is a peer top-level collection mounted directly at the server root; there is no umbrella resource that owns the URL space. Relationships between collections are carried by identifier fields, and every such relationship is reverse-reachable through a listing or nested read.

### 2.1. Personnel, Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the actor is exercising. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is well formed but is not among the roles authorized for the operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `StoreManager`, `SalesAssociate`, `CollectionsAgent`, `ServiceTechnician` and `Customer`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is the configuration and break-glass role. It seeds statutory rules and stores and may invoke every operation in the system, but it is bound by every state, temporal, fiscal and safety rule exactly as any other role is — including segregation of duties on repossession.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` approves or rejects customer verification and reads every record for audit purposes. The officer may never create an agreement, post a payment, log a collection contact or authorize a repossession.
- A `StoreManager` owns store operations: inventory units, delivery crews, delivery orders and the day-close checkpoint. A manager may create agreements, post and reverse payments, accept payoff quotes and open returns, but may not verify customers and may not log collection contacts.
- A `SalesAssociate` is the front counter: it creates customers and references, records reference-check results, creates agreements, posts renewal payments, issues early-purchase quotes, accepts them and opens returns. An associate may not verify customers, reverse payments, grade returns, schedule crews, work collections or repossess.
- A `CollectionsAgent` works delinquent accounts only: posting renewal payments, logging collection contacts and authorizing repossession. The agent has no access to inventory, delivery, service or grading, and cannot create agreements or issue quotes.
- A `ServiceTechnician` is the field and bay role: completing or failing delivery attempts, opening service tickets, issuing swaps, resolving tickets and grading returned units. A technician touches no money.
- A `Customer` is the renter.

Identity semantics differ between the staff roles and the renter, and the distinction governs every ownership check. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `StoreManager`, `SalesAssociate`, `CollectionsAgent` or `ServiceTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow attributes an action to a person — as `verified_by_user_id` on a customer, `checked_by_user_id` on a reference, `created_by_user_id` on an agreement, `issued_by_user_id` on a service ticket, `graded_by_user_id` on a return inspection and `contacted_by_user_id` on a collection contact — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting customer record; ownership checks on any resource that references a customer compare the resource's customer identifier to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership enforcement produces `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) and covers exactly seven operations: reading a single customer record, reading a single agreement, reading an agreement's total-of-payments disclosure, requesting an early-purchase quote on an agreement, accepting an early-purchase quote, initiating a voluntary return, and opening a service ticket on an agreement. In each case the addressed customer's `id`, or the addressed agreement's (or quote's, or ticket's) customer identifier, must equal `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Collection Filters

Every collection read accepts `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain after the current page.

Collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending by default. Five collections carry a domain-natural primary sort and fall back to `created_at` ascending only to break ties: renewal payments are ordered by `posted_at` ascending, reinstatement windows by `opened_at` ascending, delivery orders and service tickets by `window_start_at` ascending, early-purchase quotes by `issued_at` ascending, collection contacts by `contacted_at` ascending, and loyalty ledger entries by `occurred_at` ascending. The statutory-rule collection is the one exception with no timestamp ordering at all: it is ordered by `state_code` ascending.

Filters are collection-specific and combine conjunctively with paging. The statutory-rule and store collections filter on `state_code`. A store's inventory listing filters on `status`; the global inventory listing filters on `store_id`, `replaced_unit_id`, `status` and `category`. The customer collection filters on `verification_status`, `state_code` and `account_flag`, and a customer's reference listing filters on `check_status`. The agreement collection filters on `customer_id`, `unit_id`, `store_id`, `payment_status` and `possession_status`. Payment, reinstatement-window, return-inspection and quote listings filter on `status`; delivery orders additionally filter on `agreement_id` and `crew_id`; service tickets filter on `agreement_id`, `store_id`, `unit_id`, `replacement_unit_id` and `status`; return inspections filter on `agreement_id` and `unit_id`; collection contacts filter on `channel`; and the loyalty ledger filters on `customer_id`, `agreement_id`, `source_payment_id` and `entry_type`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system consults wall-clock time. A logical clock, readable as `current_utc`, begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a fresh server and never advances on its own. An operator of any role may move it by submitting a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, which returns the new `current_utc`. Every datetime the server stamps — a payment's `posted_at`, a contact's `contacted_at`, a delivery's `completed_at`, a window's `opened_at`, a quote's `issued_at`, a reference's `checked_at`, a reversal's `reversed_at`, an entry's `occurred_at`, an agreement's `cycle_anchor_at` — is the clock's value at the moment of the call, and every temporal comparison (due dates, expiries, quiet hours, rolling contact windows) is evaluated against the same clock. Because time alone changes no row, an agreement can sit twenty days past due indefinitely and remain `CURRENT` until a day-close is run against its store.

### 2.5. State Rent-To-Own Rules and Snapshot Pinning

Statutory variance is modelled as one rule row per jurisdiction, keyed by a two-letter uppercase USPS `state_code` that must be unique across the collection; a second rule row for the same state is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_RULE` (HTTP 409). Stores and customers reference a jurisdiction by that same natural key rather than by identifier, and three operations therefore require the matching rule row to already exist: creating a store (matched on the submitted `state_code`), creating an agreement (matched on the `state_code` of the store that holds the unit, never on the customer's), and verifying a customer (matched on the customer's own `state_code`). When no rule row matches, the operation fails with `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_CONFIGURED` (HTTP 422) — a store may not be opened in an unregulated state.

A rule row carries the full statutory profile of its jurisdiction. `late_fee_grace_days`, which may not be negative, is the number of days after a renewal due date that must fully elapse before a late fee may be assessed. `late_fee_pct`, a fraction between 0 and 0.25 inclusive, is applied to the agreement's per-period rate to compute a late fee, and `late_fee_max_cents`, which may not be negative, caps that fee for a single period. `reinstate_threshold_months` defaults to 2 and must be at least 1; it is the number of months' worth of rent that switches a reinstatement window from the short length, `reinstate_days_short` (at least 1 day), to the long length, `reinstate_days_long` (which may not be shorter than the short length). `reinstatement_fee_cents`, which may not be negative, is the statutory fee assessed when a reinstatement window opens. `early_purchase_formula` is either `PCT_OF_REMAINING` or `CASH_PRICE_MINUS_CREDIT` and selects which of two mandatory payoff calculations the state imposes; `early_purchase_pct`, greater than 0 and at most 1, is the multiplier the first variant applies to what is still owed, and `rent_credit_pct`, likewise greater than 0 and at most 1, is the fraction of rent already applied that the second variant credits. `processing_fee_max_cents`, which may not be negative, caps the one-time processing fee that may be folded into the disclosure, and a value of 0 forbids the fee entirely. `disclosure_requires_total_of_payments` defaults to true and, when true, forces an explicit customer acknowledgement at signing. `max_collection_contacts_per_week`, which may not be negative, bounds collection contacts on a single agreement in a rolling seven-day window. `quiet_hours_start_utc_hour`, defaulting to 20, is the inclusive UTC hour at which collection contact becomes prohibited, and `quiet_hours_end_utc_hour`, defaulting to 8, is the exclusive UTC hour at which it becomes permitted again; both must fall between 0 and 23 inclusive. `repossession_paid_pct_limit`, greater than 0 and at most 1, is the equity-protection ceiling. `credit_inquiry_prohibited` defaults to true and, when true, makes any verification request that carries a credit score unlawful. `max_term_renewals`, at least 1, caps the number of renewal periods an agreement in that state may contract for.

Agreements do not read the live rule row. At signing an agreement copies the governing jurisdiction into `state_code_snapshot` and the rule row's `version` into `state_rule_version_snapshot`, and thereafter every fee, window length, payoff formula and repossession limit that applies to that agreement is read from the rule identified by `state_code_snapshot`. A later amendment to a state's rules changes the economics of agreements signed after the amendment and of nothing else.

### 2.6. Record Identity, Timestamps and Mutation Bookkeeping

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only touch derived counters such as a store's `units_on_rent_count` or an agreement's `paid_pct`.

Two resources are strictly append-only records of things that happened and are never edited: a collection contact and a loyalty ledger entry. Both carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field. A collection contact's `contacted_at` and a ledger entry's `occurred_at` are immutable once written.

Because a cascade frequently touches several rows at once, a single request can bump the `version` of a store, an agreement, an inventory unit and a customer together. Every row the cascade writes gets a fresh `updated_at` and an incremented `version`; rows the cascade merely reads do not.

### 2.7. Money, Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents and are transported as JSON integers. Any division that is not explicitly described as rounding up or rounding down is resolved by rounding half up: a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero. Two places round up instead: the derivation of an agreement's per-period renewal rate from its target total, and the derivation of a projected re-rent rate from a graded residual. Three places round down: the conversion of elapsed seconds into whole days late, the conversion of a rent pool into whole completed renewal periods, and the conversion of applied rent into whole loyalty points. One place rounds neither up nor down but to fixed precision: `paid_pct` is a ratio stored to exactly four decimal places, round half up. Loyalty points are integers and are never rounded, only floored at the point where cents become points. Configuration fractions supplied on a statutory rule row — `late_fee_pct`, `early_purchase_pct`, `rent_credit_pct`, `repossession_paid_pct_limit` — are transported as numbers and preserve the precision they were submitted with.

No calculation in this system reads an `id` as an ordering key. Wherever a rule iterates over a set of rows — the agreements a day-close sweeps, the reinstatement windows a payment may consume — the ordering is `created_at` ascending.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Agreement Writes

Two write operations require the caller to prove it has observed the agreement's current state, because both of them silently invalidate a sibling record. Issuing an early-purchase quote against an agreement and creating a delivery order against an agreement each require a non-optional `expected_agreement_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that agreement. If `expected_agreement_version` does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created.

The protection is not incidental. A newly issued quote supersedes the agreement's prior open quote and re-pins the payoff economics — the rent snapshot and the loyalty tier it discounts against — so a writer holding a stale view would supersede a quote it never saw and hand the customer a payoff figure computed from outdated ledger state. Likewise a newly created delivery order supersedes the agreement's prior scheduled or failed order and releases that crew's window, so a writer with a stale view would cancel a dispatch it never observed. Because posting a payment, reversing a payment, issuing a quote and creating a delivery order all bump the agreement's `version`, a quote computed against a pre-payment view can never be issued.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Completing a delivery, resolving a ticket, grading a return, accepting a quote, posting a payment, logging a contact and authorizing a repossession all proceed without an expected-version check.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the response: a request addressing a reference under a nonexistent customer fails on the customer. Each collection has its own 404: `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

Validation as a whole is strictly ordered. Checks are evaluated in the sequence below and the **first** failing check determines the response; later checks never mask an earlier one, and a request that fails an early check must not exhibit any side effect of a later one. Where a check applies to a limited set of operations, that set is exhaustive.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The role is authorized for the operation — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. Every path identifier resolves, left to right, in the collection order store, customer, customer reference, inventory unit, agreement, delivery order, service ticket, return inspection, renewal payment, early-purchase quote, statutory rule, loyalty ledger entry — otherwise the collection's 404 above. Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
5. A `Customer` caller owns the addressed resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to the seven operations enumerated in §2.1.
6. The body parses, required fields are present, enum members are legal, integers are non-negative where non-negative is required, and `limit` and `offset` are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
7. Every identifier supplied in the body resolves — `customer_id` and `unit_id` on agreement creation, `crew_id` on delivery-order creation, `replacement_unit_id` on swap issuance — otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to agreement creation, delivery-order creation and swap issuance.
8. A statutory rule row exists for the governing `state_code` — otherwise `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_CONFIGURED` (422). Applies to store creation, agreement creation and customer verification.
9. No credit score accompanies a verification request in a jurisdiction that prohibits credit inquiries — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_REPORT_PROHIBITED` (422). Applies to customer verification.
10. The `X-User-ID` authorizing a repossession differs from the customer's `verified_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to repossession.
11. `expected_agreement_version` equals the agreement's `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to quote issuance and delivery-order creation.
12. The agreement's `payment_status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_CLOSED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_DEFAULTED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_CURRENT` or `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DEFAULTED` (409). Nothing at all may be written to a `CLOSED` agreement; payments require `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT`; quote issuance requires `CURRENT`; repossession requires `DEFAULTED`. Applies to eight operations: payment posting, quote issuance, quote acceptance, voluntary return, repossession, service-ticket creation, delivery-order creation and collection-contact logging.
13. The agreement's `possession_status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_POSSESSION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Delivery completion requires `PENDING_DELIVERY`; payment posting, swap issuance, quote issuance and quote acceptance require `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING`, which is exactly the condition under which `cycle_anchor_at` is set; returns and repossession require `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING`. Applies to seven operations: payment posting, delivery completion, swap issuance, quote issuance, quote acceptance, voluntary return and repossession.
14. The inventory unit's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). `AVAILABLE` is required to reserve a unit at signing, `RESERVED` to deliver it, `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE` to grade it. Applies to three operations: agreement creation, delivery completion and return grading. Replacement-unit eligibility on swap issuance is deliberately **not** decided here — it belongs entirely to check 42, so a swap onto a unit that is already `ON_RENT` yields `ERR_REPLACEMENT_UNIT_INVALID` and never `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`.
15. The delivery order is `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to delivery completion and failed-attempt recording.
16. The service ticket is `SCHEDULED` for swap issuance, and either `SCHEDULED` or `SWAP_ISSUED` for resolution — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to swap issuance and ticket resolution.
17. The return inspection is `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_GRADED` (409). Applies to return grading.
18. The quote is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to quote acceptance.
19. The logical clock is at or before the quote's `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to quote acceptance.
20. The reference's `check_status` is `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_ALREADY_CHECKED` (409). Applies to reference checking.
21. The payment is `POSTED`, and is the agreement's most recent `POSTED` payment — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (409). Applies to payment reversal.
22. No reinstatement window with status `OPEN` exists on the agreement — otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_OPEN` (409). Reinstatement rights outrank repossession. Applies to repossession.
23. The agreement's `paid_pct` is strictly below the jurisdiction's `repossession_paid_pct_limit` — otherwise `ERR_REPOSSESSION_EQUITY_PROTECTED` (422). Applies to repossession.
24. At least one collection contact is on record for the agreement — otherwise `ERR_COLLECTION_NOTICE_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to repossession.
25. The agreement's `payment_status` is `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED` — otherwise `ERR_COLLECTION_NOT_DELINQUENT` (409). A current account may not be worked by collections. Applies to collection-contact logging.
26. The clock's UTC hour lies outside the jurisdiction's quiet hours — otherwise `ERR_QUIET_HOURS_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to collection-contact logging.
27. The rolling seven-day contact count is strictly below `max_collection_contacts_per_week` — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_FREQUENCY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to collection-contact logging.
28. The requested channel has a usable coordinate on the customer — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE` (422). Applies to collection-contact logging.
29. `disclosure_acknowledged` is true wherever the jurisdiction requires the total-of-payments disclosure — otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (422). Applies to agreement creation.
30. The customer's `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_VERIFIED` (422). Applies to agreement creation.
31. On an approval decision, `government_id_verified` is true and `verified_reference_count` is at least 2 — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REFERENCES` (422). Applies to customer verification.
32. On an approval decision, the verification score is at least 70 — otherwise `ERR_VERIFICATION_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (422). Applies to customer verification.
33. `renewal_count` is at most the jurisdiction's `max_term_renewals` — otherwise `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_STATE_CAP` (422). Applies to agreement creation.
34. `serial_number` is unique within its store and `state_code` is unique across statutory rules — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_RULE` (409). Applies to inventory-unit creation and statutory-rule creation.
35. The crew belongs to the agreement's store and its `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CREW_STORE_MISMATCH` or `ERR_CREW_INACTIVE` (422). Applies to delivery-order creation.
36. The crew's `setup_certified` is true when the agreement's unit `category` is `APPLIANCE` and the order's `service_type` is `DELIVERY_SETUP` — otherwise `ERR_CREW_NOT_SETUP_CERTIFIED` (422). Applies to delivery-order creation.
37. `setup_completed` is true when the delivered unit's `category` is `APPLIANCE` — otherwise `ERR_SETUP_NOT_COMPLETED` (422). Applies to delivery completion.
38. `window_end_at` is strictly after `window_start_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (400). Applies to delivery-order creation and service-ticket creation.
39. The crew's remaining scheduled orders do not collide with the requested window under the asymmetric travel-buffer rule — otherwise `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to delivery-order creation.
40. The store's `service_intake_open` is true — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_INTAKE_CLOSED` (409). Applies to service-ticket creation.
41. The number of open tickets at the store whose closed intervals intersect the requested window is strictly below the store's `service_bay_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to service-ticket creation.
42. The replacement unit is `AVAILABLE`, sits at the ticket's store and shares the serviced unit's `category` — otherwise `ERR_REPLACEMENT_UNIT_INVALID` (422). Applies to swap issuance.
43. The submitted `resolution` matches the ticket's current status — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_RESOLUTION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to ticket resolution.
44. `amount_cents` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT` (422). Applies to payment posting.
45. The tendered amount does not exceed the remaining disclosed total plus outstanding arrears — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422). Applies to payment posting.
46. `payment_amount_cents` equals the quote's `payoff_amount_cents` exactly — otherwise `ERR_PAYOFF_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to quote acceptance.

Note the deliberate consequence of this ordering for repossession: segregation of duties (check 10) is evaluated before any state check, so an officer who approved a customer is turned away with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even on an agreement that is not `DEFAULTED`; and among the three repossession bars, the open reinstatement window (check 22) is decided before equity protection (check 23), which is decided before the documented-notice requirement (check 24).

### 2.10. Ledger and Possession Invariants

Three identities must hold after every request that could disturb them, and they are the acceptance criterion for the money, points and possession machinery respectively.

**Cash conservation.** For any agreement, the sum of `amount_cents` over its payments whose `status` is `POSTED` equals the sum of that agreement's `rent_applied_cents`, `late_fee_paid_cents`, `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents`, `partial_credit_cents` and `customer_credit_contributed_cents`, as an exact integer equality. Reversed payments drop out of both sides of the identity simultaneously. This must hold after every payment posting and after every payment reversal: no cent may be created or destroyed.

**Point conservation.** For any customer, `loyalty_points_balance` equals the sum of `delta_points` over every loyalty ledger entry referencing that customer, and `loyalty_points_lifetime` equals the sum of only the positive `delta_points` values. The append-only ledger is the single source of truth for both, so a reversal must emit exactly the negative of the accrual it compensates. This must hold after every loyalty-affecting call: payment posting, payment reversal and early-payoff acceptance.

**Possession conservation.** For any store, the number of its inventory units in status `ON_RENT` equals the number of its agreements whose `possession_status` is `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING`; the store's `units_on_rent_count` equals that same number; and the store's `units_in_service_count` equals the number of its units in status `IN_SERVICE`. Units in `RESERVED`, `IN_SERVICE`, `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE`, `RETIRED` or `SOLD` are excluded from the first count. This must hold after every possession-affecting call — delivery completion, swap issuance, swap resolution, voluntary return, repossession and early-purchase acceptance — which is what makes a swap a transfer of possession from one unit to another rather than a change in how many units sit in customers' homes.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Adverse-Account Posture

The officer who cleared a customer to rent may not be the operator who takes the goods back. Whenever a repossession is authorized, the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the customer's `verified_by_user_id` — the opaque operator identifier recorded when the approval decision set `verification_status` to `VERIFIED` — and an equal value is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The bar applies to every role including `PlatformAdmin`.

Adverse posture is derived, never accumulated by hand. An agreement's `collections_stage` is `NONE` or `ADVERSE` and is moved only by the delinquency and default sweep and by the cure cascade — logging a collection contact never changes it. A customer's `adverse_agreement_count` is at all times the number of that customer's agreements whose `collections_stage` is `ADVERSE`, which is why a repossession following a default sweep leaves the count untouched rather than double-counting the same agreement, and `account_flag` is `ADVERSE` exactly while that count is 1 or more and `NORMAL` otherwise. `lifetime_delinquency_count` behaves differently on purpose: it counts every transition from `CURRENT` to `DELINQUENT` ever recorded across the customer's agreements, is incremented again when a reversal drives an agreement back into `DELINQUENT`, and is never decremented.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Renter Onto the Books

A renter enters the system as a customer record created by front-counter staff with a `full_name`, an `email`, a `phone`, a two-letter `state_code`, a stated `employer_name`, a `monthly_income_cents` figure and a `residence_months` count of months at the current address. Neither income nor residence may be negative and the legal name is limited to 120 characters. Three booleans record what staff physically checked and each defaults to false when omitted: `employer_verified` when the store actually reached the employer, `government_id_verified` when a government photo ID was inspected, and `in_person_contact_consent` when written consent for a doorstep collection visit is on file. A newly created customer is `PENDING` with a `verification_score` of 0, a `verified_reference_count` of 0, an empty-string `verified_by_user_id`, a `loyalty_points_balance` and `loyalty_points_lifetime` of 0, a `loyalty_tier` of `STANDARD`, a `credit_balance_cents` of 0, an `account_flag` of `NORMAL`, and an `adverse_agreement_count` and `lifetime_delinquency_count` of 0. There is no credit-score field on this operation at all: the business model is built on not pulling a bureau file.

In place of a credit report the store telephones people. Staff attach personal references to the customer, each with a `reference_name` of up to 120 characters, a `phone`, and a `relationship` of `FAMILY`, `EMPLOYER`, `LANDLORD` or `PERSONAL`. A reference is born `PENDING` with a null `checked_at` and an empty-string `checked_by_user_id`. Recording the outcome of the telephone call writes a `result` of `VERIFIED`, `UNREACHABLE` or `REFUSED` into `check_status`, stamps `checked_at` with the logical clock and records the caller in `checked_by_user_id`; `PENDING` may never be written back, and a reference whose `check_status` has already left `PENDING` cannot be checked twice — the second attempt is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_ALREADY_CHECKED`. Each newly `VERIFIED` reference refreshes the customer's `verified_reference_count`, which counts only that customer's references in `VERIFIED` status; `UNREACHABLE` and `REFUSED` references contribute nothing to it and nothing to the score below.

**Verification** is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s decision and the only route from `PENDING` to `VERIFIED`. The officer submits a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. A `credit_score` field exists in the request shape for exactly one reason: so that supplying it can be refused with `ERR_CREDIT_REPORT_PROHIBITED` in any jurisdiction whose `credit_inquiry_prohibited` flag is true. It never influences the score under any circumstances.

Every call, including one that ends in rejection, recomputes and stores the customer's `verification_score` before the decision is applied. The score is a sum of four independent terms. Forty points are awarded when `employer_verified` is true and none when it is false. Five points are awarded for each fully completed six months of `residence_months` — so eleven months earns five points and twelve months earns ten — with this term capped at 30 points, which is reached at three years. Ten points are awarded for each `VERIFIED` reference, counting at most three of them, so this term contributes at most 30 points. Finally, an income band contributes 20 points when `monthly_income_cents` is at least 250,000 cents (two thousand five hundred dollars a month), 10 points when it is at least 150,000 cents but below that, and nothing below one thousand five hundred dollars a month.

An `APPROVE` decision succeeds only when all four preconditions hold: no credit score was supplied where the jurisdiction prohibits inquiries, `government_id_verified` is true, `verified_reference_count` is at least 2, and the computed score is at least 70. When it succeeds, `verification_status` moves `PENDING` → `VERIFIED` and `verified_by_user_id` is set to the officer's `X-User-ID`. When any of the reference or identity preconditions fails the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REFERENCES`, and when only the score falls short it is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_SCORE_TOO_LOW`; in both cases the recomputed score is retained and `verification_status` is left exactly where it was — a failed approval never moves the row. Only an explicit `REJECT` decision moves `PENDING` → `REJECTED`, and it too stores the recomputed score while leaving `verified_by_user_id` unchanged. A rejection is not final: once more references have been checked or employment has been confirmed, the same officer or another may re-decide, and a successful approval then takes the row `REJECTED` → `VERIFIED`, again writing `verified_by_user_id`. No second customer record is needed.

### 3.2. The Sales Floor: Stores, Crews and Units That Wear Out

A store is opened with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `state_code` whose statutory rule already exists, and a `service_bay_capacity` of at least 1 that defaults to 1 and expresses how many service jobs may overlap in the back of the shop. A new store starts with `units_on_rent_count` and `units_in_service_count` at 0 and `service_intake_open` at true. Those three fields are derived, not administered: the counts track units in `ON_RENT` and `IN_SERVICE` status at that store, and `service_intake_open` is false exactly when `units_in_service_count` has reached or passed `service_bay_capacity`, in which case no new service ticket may be opened at that store.

Inventory arrives as units belonging to a store, each with a `model_code`, a `serial_number` that must be unique among that store's units, a `category` of `FURNITURE` or `APPLIANCE`, and a `cash_price_cents` of at least 1,000 cents that is the disclosed cash price of a brand-new example of that model and the base for every depreciation computation the unit will ever undergo. A duplicate serial at the same store is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL`. A new unit is `AVAILABLE` with a `condition_grade` of `NEW`, a `rent_cycle_count` of 0, a `residual_valuation_basis` of `NONE`, a null `replaced_unit_id` and a `retired_reason` of `NONE`.

A unit's life is a cycle rather than a line. It moves `AVAILABLE` → `RESERVED` when an agreement is signed against it, `RESERVED` → `ON_RENT` when the delivery is completed, and back to `AVAILABLE` at the end of a rental once it has been graded. Two things happen every time it comes back: `rent_cycle_count` increases by one, and `condition_grade` steps down exactly one notch on the ladder `NEW` → `GRADE_A` → `GRADE_B` → `GRADE_C`, where `GRADE_C` is the bottom and a step down from `GRADE_C` remains `GRADE_C`. Both movements permanently lower every future agreement's per-period rate on that unit, which is the economic point of re-rent grading. A unit may also leave the cycle for good: `RETIRED` when a grading finds it unrentable, or `SOLD` when a customer exercises ownership. Two further statuses exist for the service loop and the grading gap, `IN_SERVICE` and `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE`, and the derived fields `accumulated_rent_received_cents`, `residual_value_cents` and `residual_valuation_basis` follow the unit through all of it.

Delivery crews belong to a store as well. A crew is created with a `crew_name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `setup_certified` flag defaulting to false that authorizes appliance hook-up and installation, and a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` defaulting to `ACTIVE`. A crew hired but not yet cleared for dispatch is created `INACTIVE` and may not take delivery work.

### 3.3. Writing the Agreement and Disclosing the Total of Payments

An agreement is the contract. A `SalesAssociate` or `StoreManager` creates one against a `customer_id` whose `verification_status` must be `VERIFIED` (`ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_VERIFIED` otherwise) and a `unit_id` whose unit must be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` otherwise), choosing a `term_type` of `WEEKLY` or `MONTHLY`, a `renewal_count` of at least 1 that is the number of renewal payments to ownership, and a `disclosure_acknowledged` boolean defaulting to false. A `WEEKLY` term has a period of 7 days, a `MONTHLY` term a period of 30 days. The agreement copies `store_id` from the unit, pins `state_code_snapshot` and `state_rule_version_snapshot`, records the acting operator in `created_by_user_id`, and reserves the unit by taking it `AVAILABLE` → `RESERVED`. The jurisdiction that governs the agreement is always the **store's**, never the customer's: an out-of-state renter is priced under the rules of the shop that stocks the goods. Where that jurisdiction requires the total-of-payments disclosure, `disclosure_acknowledged` must be true or the signing is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, and a `renewal_count` above the jurisdiction's cap is refused with `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_STATE_CAP`.

Pricing runs immediately, in a pinned order of steps; neither the rounding points nor the fee base may be rearranged, because each rearrangement produces a different disclosed number.

First, a grade factor is read from the unit's `condition_grade`: 1.00 for `NEW`, 0.85 for `GRADE_A`, 0.70 for `GRADE_B` and 0.55 for `GRADE_C`. Second, a cycle factor is one minus five hundredths for each completed cycle in `rent_cycle_count`, with a floor of 0.60 — a unit that has been out eight or more times contributes exactly 0.60. Third, `adjusted_cash_price_cents` is the unit's `cash_price_cents` multiplied by the grade factor and then by the cycle factor, with a single round-half-up applied to the complete product; rounding after each individual factor is forbidden. Fourth, a term markup is selected from `term_type`: 2.20 for `WEEKLY` and 1.95 for `MONTHLY`. Fifth, a target total is the adjusted cash price multiplied by that markup, rounded half up. Sixth, `renewal_rate_cents` is that target total divided by `renewal_count`, rounded **up** to the next whole cent. Seventh, `rent_subtotal_cents` is `renewal_rate_cents` multiplied by `renewal_count` — the per-period rate is rounded up first and only then multiplied, so the disclosed subtotal ordinarily exceeds the target total; summing first and rounding afterwards is not permitted. Eighth, `processing_fee_cents` is the smaller of the jurisdiction's `processing_fee_max_cents` and five percent of `rent_subtotal_cents` rounded half up; the base is the rent subtotal as it stands **before** any fee is folded in, no fixed-point iteration is performed, and a jurisdiction whose cap is zero simply never charges the fee. The processing fee is never a client input. Ninth, `total_of_payments_cents` is `rent_subtotal_cents` plus `processing_fee_cents`. Tenth, `monthly_equivalent_rate_cents` is, for a `WEEKLY` agreement, `renewal_rate_cents` multiplied by 52, divided by 12 and rounded half up; for a `MONTHLY` agreement it is simply `renewal_rate_cents`. That monthly equivalent is not decoration — it is the unit in which reinstatement thresholds are measured.

The signed agreement starts with `payment_status` `CURRENT`, `possession_status` `PENDING_DELIVERY`, `collections_stage` `NONE`, `periods_paid` 0, every money counter at 0, `paid_pct` at 0.0000, `swap_count` 0, `service_credit_days` 0, `late_fee_period_index_last` 0 and `collection_contact_count_7d` 0. Crucially, `cycle_anchor_at` and `current_period_due_at` are left null: billing does not begin at signing, so no day-close can assess a fee against goods still sitting in the warehouse.

The mandated disclosure is available as a read-only rendering that never recomputes anything. It reports the agreement's `agreement_id`, `term_type`, `renewal_count`, `adjusted_cash_price_cents`, `renewal_rate_cents`, `rent_subtotal_cents`, `processing_fee_cents`, `total_of_payments_cents`, `monthly_equivalent_rate_cents`, the jurisdiction's `early_purchase_formula` and its `state_code`, plus one derived figure computed nowhere else: `cost_of_rental_cents`, the amount by which `total_of_payments_cents` exceeds `adjusted_cash_price_cents`. That is the number a regulator cares about — what renting rather than buying costs this household.

### 3.4. Delivery: Crew Windows, Failed Attempts and the Start of Billing

Scheduling a delivery is a `StoreManager` action that pins the agreement's `expected_agreement_version` (§2.8) and names a `crew_id`, a `service_type` — `DELIVERY_ONLY` books a drop at the door with no assembly, while `DELIVERY_SETUP` books hook-up and installation as well — and an occupied interval running from an inclusive `window_start_at` to an exclusive `window_end_at` that must be strictly later — a window that ends at or before it starts is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The crew must belong to the same store as the agreement (`ERR_CREW_STORE_MISMATCH`) and must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CREW_INACTIVE`). An `APPLIANCE` unit may only be handed over under `DELIVERY_SETUP`, and that combination demands a crew whose `setup_certified` is true (`ERR_CREW_NOT_SETUP_CERTIFIED`). An order's `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `COMPLETED` and `FAILED_ATTEMPT` are reached by an operator acting on the order itself while `SUPERSEDED` and `CANCELLED` are written only by cascades.

The create is also a reschedule. Before the new window is examined at all, the agreement's own prior order in `SCHEDULED` or `FAILED_ATTEMPT` status is taken to `SUPERSEDED` and its crew window is released, so at most one non-terminal delivery order ever exists per agreement and a failed attempt is superseded rather than resurrected. The new order is created `SCHEDULED` with `attempt_count` 0 and `setup_completed` false, and the agreement's `version` is bumped.

Crew windows collide under a deliberately asymmetric buffer. A candidate window conflicts with an existing order when that order belongs to the same crew, belongs to a **different** agreement — the agreement's own prior order has already been superseded by this very create and therefore cannot block its own replacement — is still `SCHEDULED`, and both of the following hold: the candidate starts strictly before the existing order's `window_end_at` extended by 45 minutes of travel time, and the candidate ends strictly after the existing order's `window_start_at`. The buffer extends only the **existing** order's tail and never the candidate's head, with two consequences worth internalizing: a candidate starting exactly 45 minutes after an existing window's end is accepted, and a candidate ending exactly at an existing window's start is also accepted, so packing a job immediately before another crew commitment is legal while packing one immediately after it is not. Orders in `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `COMPLETED` status never block a window. A collision is refused with `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Completion is the moment the business actually begins. A `StoreManager` or `ServiceTechnician` completes a `SCHEDULED` order by submitting `setup_completed`; for an `APPLIANCE` unit a false value is refused with `ERR_SETUP_NOT_COMPLETED`. The order records the submitted value, stamps `completed_at` with the logical clock and moves `SCHEDULED` → `COMPLETED`. The agreement then advances `PENDING_DELIVERY` → `DELIVERED` and, in the same breath, `cycle_anchor_at` is set to the logical clock and `current_period_due_at` to the anchor plus one period — 7 days for a `WEEKLY` agreement, 30 for a `MONTHLY` one. Renewal period *k* falls due at the anchor plus *k* whole periods. The unit moves `RESERVED` → `ON_RENT` and the store's `units_on_rent_count` rises by one.

Deliveries fail. Recording a failed attempt takes a `reason` of `CUSTOMER_ABSENT`, `ACCESS_BLOCKED` or `VEHICLE_ISSUE` and increments `attempt_count`. The first two failures leave the order `SCHEDULED` so the same crew can try again; the third takes `attempt_count` to 3 and the order `SCHEDULED` → `FAILED_ATTEMPT`, which releases both the crew's window and the goods: the unit returns `RESERVED` → `AVAILABLE` so another agreement may reserve it, while this agreement stays in `PENDING_DELIVERY` with its pricing snapshot fully intact, waiting for a fresh order that will supersede the failed one. No money and no loyalty points move on a failed attempt. Attempting to complete or fail an order that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.5. Renewal Payments: The Allocation Waterfall, Loyalty and Reversal

Money may only be posted against an agreement whose `payment_status` is `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT` — never against a closed row (`ERR_AGREEMENT_CLOSED`) or a defaulted one (`ERR_AGREEMENT_DEFAULTED`) — and only once the goods are in the customer's hands, that is while `possession_status` is `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING` (`ERR_POSSESSION_STATE_INVALID` otherwise), because the due-date arithmetic depends on `cycle_anchor_at`. A payment carries a gross `amount_cents` of at least 1 (`ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT`), a `payment_method` of `CASH`, `CARD`, `ACH` or `MONEY_ORDER`, and an optional `apply_customer_credit` flag defaulting to false; when it is true, the customer's `credit_balance_cents` is drawn on before the tendered amount and the drawn amount is recorded in `allocation_customer_credit_cents`. Overpayment is refused: `amount_cents` may not exceed the part of `total_of_payments_cents` not yet covered by `rent_applied_cents`, plus whatever remains of `late_fee_balance_cents` and `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents` (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`). The payment row is written with `status` `POSTED` and `posted_at` at the logical clock.

The tendered money runs through five ordered buckets, and the order is pinned. **Bucket one** takes the smaller of the remaining money and `late_fee_balance_cents` into `allocation_late_fee_cents`. **Bucket two** looks for the agreement's reinstatement window in `OPEN` status — at most one can exist, and if several ever coexisted the earliest by `created_at` is taken — and, only when such a window exists *and* the logical clock is at or before its `expires_at`, takes the smaller of the remaining money and `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents` into `allocation_reinstatement_fee_cents`; otherwise that bucket is 0. **Bucket three** is rent. Before it runs, the agreement's carried `partial_credit_cents` is copied onto the payment as `partial_credit_before_cents`, then the rent pool is formed as the money still remaining plus that carried credit. `periods_advanced` is the number of whole periods the pool covers at `renewal_rate_cents` each — the pool divided by the rate, rounded **down** — but never more periods than remain between `periods_paid` and `renewal_count`. `allocation_rent_cents` is `periods_advanced` multiplied by `renewal_rate_cents`, and the leftover is what the pool still holds after that. A partial period is never rounded up into a paid period, and partial money never increments `periods_paid`. **Buckets four and five** split the leftover according to whether the term is now complete. If `periods_paid` plus `periods_advanced` is still below `renewal_count`, the whole leftover becomes `allocation_partial_credit_cents` and is carried on the agreement as its new `partial_credit_cents`, while `allocation_customer_credit_cents` is 0. If instead the term has completed with this payment, `allocation_partial_credit_cents` is 0, the whole leftover becomes `allocation_customer_credit_cents`, the agreement's `partial_credit_cents` is cleared to 0, and the leftover is added both to the customer's `credit_balance_cents` and to the agreement's `customer_credit_contributed_cents` — money the household gets back as store credit rather than a windfall for the shop.

Two figures are then derived. `days_late` is the number of whole days by which the logical clock exceeds `current_period_due_at`, floored at 0, computed from the due date as it stood **before** this payment advanced anything. And, when the agreement was `DELINQUENT`, a cure test decides whether the arrears are truly cleared: `late_fee_balance_cents` must be 0, `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents` must be 0, and the recomputed due date — `cycle_anchor_at` plus one more period than the new `periods_paid` — must be strictly later than the logical clock. Posting also updates `periods_paid`, `current_period_due_at`, `rent_applied_cents` (which is always `periods_paid` multiplied by `renewal_rate_cents`), `late_fee_paid_cents`, `late_fee_balance_cents` (always assessed minus paid), `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents`, `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents` and `paid_pct`, and bumps the agreement's `version`.

Loyalty accrues on the same call. The multiplier is read from the customer's `loyalty_tier` **as it stood before this payment** — 1 for `STANDARD`, 2 for `SILVER`, 3 for `GOLD`. A payment that advanced no period earns nothing at all; otherwise one point is minted for each whole 100 cents of `allocation_rent_cents`, with any remainder below a full dollar earning nothing, and that count is multiplied by the tier multiplier. When `days_late` is exactly 0, a flat on-time bonus of 25 points is then added. If the resulting `loyalty_points_awarded` is not zero, a ledger entry is appended with `entry_type` `PAYMENT_ACCRUAL`, a positive `delta_points`, the originating `customer_id`, `agreement_id` and `source_payment_id`, an `occurred_at` at the logical clock, and a `balance_after` equal to the customer's balance immediately after the append; the customer's `loyalty_points_balance` and `loyalty_points_lifetime` both rise by that amount and `loyalty_tier` is then recomputed from the new lifetime total — `GOLD` at 500 lifetime points or more, `SILVER` at 200 or more, `STANDARD` below that. Reading the multiplier before the accrual and awarding one bonus per posting has a deliberate consequence: paying six weeks in one lump sum earns strictly less than six separate on-time weekly payments, because there is one bonus instead of six and no tier ratchet part-way through.

Two thresholds sit at the top of the waterfall's consequences. When the payment clears the whole reinstatement obligation of an `OPEN` window and the cure test passes, the agreement is cured (§3.7). When the payment brings `periods_paid` up to `renewal_count` with no fee balance left and the goods are in `DELIVERED` possession, the term is complete: `payment_status` moves `CURRENT` → `CLOSED`, `possession_status` moves `DELIVERED` → `OWNED`, the unit becomes `SOLD`, and any unapplied remainder has already moved to the customer's `credit_balance_cents`.

**Reversal** is the compensating action, restricted to a `StoreManager` and only ever applied to the agreement's most recent `POSTED` payment. It takes a `reason` of `CHARGEBACK`, `NSF` or `POSTING_ERROR`, sets `status` to `REVERSED`, stamps `reversed_at` with the logical clock and records `reversal_reason`, which sits at `NONE` with a null `reversed_at` on every payment that has not been reversed. Every bucket is then unwound: `allocation_late_fee_cents`, `allocation_reinstatement_fee_cents` and `allocation_rent_cents` are subtracted from the agreement's paid counters, restoring `late_fee_balance_cents` and `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents`; `periods_paid` and `current_period_due_at` roll back by `periods_advanced` periods; and `paid_pct` is recomputed. The carried credit is handled with particular care: `partial_credit_cents` is restored to the payment's `partial_credit_before_cents` and **not** reduced by `allocation_partial_credit_cents`, because bucket three consumed the carried credit on the way in and subtracting the bucket instead would destroy that money out of the ledger. Where `allocation_customer_credit_cents` was positive, that amount is subtracted from both the customer's `credit_balance_cents` and the agreement's `customer_credit_contributed_cents`. A compensating ledger entry with `entry_type` `PAYMENT_REVERSAL` is appended whose `delta_points` is exactly the negation of the payment's `loyalty_points_awarded`, so `loyalty_points_balance` falls while `loyalty_points_lifetime` does not. Reversing a payment that is already `REVERSED`, or an older `POSTED` payment, is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. Reversal bumps the agreement's `version`.

If the reversed payment had `cured_delinquency` set to true — the flag written on the payment that took the agreement `DELINQUENT` → `CURRENT` — the cure itself is undone. The agreement moves `CURRENT` → `DELINQUENT` again, `lifetime_delinquency_count` is incremented once more, and the reinstatement window the payment consumed is reopened `USED` → `OPEN` when the logical clock is still at or before its `expires_at`. When the clock has passed the expiry, the window instead moves `USED` → `EXPIRED` and the agreement is driven on to `DEFAULTED`. A reopened window restores reinstatement rights for whatever remains of its term, and re-entering `DELINQUENT` restores the adverse posture: `collections_stage` moves `NONE` → `ADVERSE`, the customer's `adverse_agreement_count` rises with it and `account_flag` becomes `ADVERSE`.

### 3.6. What the Day-Close Sweep Does

Late fees, reinstatement windows and defaults exist only because someone ran a day-close against a store. It is an ops-run checkpoint invoked by a `StoreManager` or `PlatformAdmin`, and it reports what it did: the `store_id` it swept, the `closed_at` clock instant, `agreements_scanned`, `late_fees_assessed`, `late_fee_total_cents`, `windows_opened`, `windows_expired`, `agreements_defaulted` and `agreements_marked_adverse`.

The sweep considers the store's agreements in `created_at` ascending order, restricted to those whose `payment_status` is `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT`, whose `possession_status` is `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING`, and whose `cycle_anchor_at` is set. Within one agreement the order of operations is fixed: first assess an unpaid overdue period's late fee, second open a reinstatement window and flip `payment_status` from `CURRENT` to `DELINQUENT`, third expire an `OPEN` window whose `expires_at` is strictly before the clock, and fourth, on that expiry, flip `payment_status` from `DELINQUENT` to `DEFAULTED` and set `collections_stage` to `ADVERSE`. The sweep also expires every `OPEN` early-purchase quote whose `expires_at` the clock has passed, taking it `OPEN` → `EXPIRED`; no further acceptance is then possible and a fresh quote must be issued.

The fee itself is a threshold rule. `days_late` is the number of whole days by which the clock exceeds `current_period_due_at`, taking 86,400 seconds to the day and discarding any part-day. When `days_late` is at or below the jurisdiction's `late_fee_grace_days` there is no fee **and no transition at all** — the comparison is strict, so an agreement one day past a three-day grace period is still current and only a fourth day makes it delinquent. When `late_fee_period_index_last` already equals or exceeds one more than `periods_paid`, the fee for this period has been assessed on an earlier sweep and is not charged again, which is what makes repeated day-closes safe. Otherwise the fee is the smaller of `late_fee_max_cents` and `renewal_rate_cents` multiplied by `late_fee_pct`, rounded half up. The fee is added to `late_fee_assessed_cents`, `late_fee_balance_cents` is recomputed as assessed minus paid, and `late_fee_period_index_last` is set to one more than `periods_paid`.

Reaching `DELINQUENT` opens a **reinstatement window**, the statutory right to catch up and carry on. The window records the `agreement_id`, a `missed_period_index` equal to one more than `periods_paid`, an `opened_at` at the clock instant of the sweep, a `status` of `OPEN`, and a `reinstatement_fee_cents` snapshotted from the pinned jurisdiction; that fee is added to the agreement's `reinstatement_fee_assessed_cents` and thus to its `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents`. Its length is a threshold on equity built so far: a threshold in cents is formed by multiplying the jurisdiction's `reinstate_threshold_months` by the agreement's `monthly_equivalent_rate_cents`, and `window_days` is `reinstate_days_long` when `rent_applied_cents` is at or above that threshold and `reinstate_days_short` when it is below. A customer sitting exactly at two months' equivalent therefore gets the long window. `expires_at` is `opened_at` plus `window_days` days. Only completed renewal periods count toward the threshold: `partial_credit_cents`, late fees and reinstatement fees are all excluded from `rent_applied_cents`.

The delinquency also propagates outward in a fixed cascade. `collections_stage` moves `NONE` → `ADVERSE`; the customer's `adverse_agreement_count` rises to reflect the newly adverse agreement and `lifetime_delinquency_count` is incremented; and when that adverse count crosses from 0 to 1 the customer's `account_flag` moves `NORMAL` → `ADVERSE`.

Curing runs the same cascade backwards. When a payment clears every arrear and the recomputed due date is in the future, the payment's `cured_delinquency` is set to true and `payment_status` moves `DELINQUENT` → `CURRENT`; the `OPEN` window on the agreement moves `OPEN` → `USED`, which closes the collections campaign by moving `collections_stage` `ADVERSE` → `NONE`; the customer's `adverse_agreement_count` falls by one; and when it reaches 0 the customer's `account_flag` moves `ADVERSE` → `NORMAL`. `lifetime_delinquency_count` is deliberately untouched by a cure.

If nobody pays, the window runs out. A sweep that finds an `OPEN` window whose `expires_at` is strictly before the clock takes it `OPEN` → `EXPIRED` and, as a direct consequence, takes the agreement `DELINQUENT` → `DEFAULTED`; reinstatement rights are extinguished and only repossession remains.

The sweep is idempotent per store and clock instant. A second day-close at the same clock value re-scans the same agreements and finds every effect already applied, so `agreements_scanned` still reports how many agreements were inspected while `late_fees_assessed`, `late_fee_total_cents`, `windows_opened`, `windows_expired`, `agreements_defaulted` and `agreements_marked_adverse` all come back 0 and no row changes.

### 3.7. Service During the Agreement

The store owes service for as long as the agreement runs. A ticket is opened against an agreement whose `payment_status` is `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT`, by store staff or by the renter, carrying an `issue_code` describing the fault — `MECHANICAL`, `COSMETIC`, `MISSING_PART`, `ELECTRICAL` or `INFESTATION` — and a bay window from `window_start_at` to a strictly later `window_end_at`, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` when the end does not follow the start. The ticket copies `store_id` and `unit_id` from the agreement, starts `SCHEDULED` with a null `replacement_unit_id` and `rent_credit_days` at 0, and records the caller's `X-User-ID` in `issued_by_user_id`.

Two capacity gates guard intake, in this order. The store's derived `service_intake_open` must be true, which fails with `ERR_SERVICE_INTAKE_CLOSED` once `units_in_service_count` has reached `service_bay_capacity`. Then the requested window is counted against the store's other live tickets — those in `SCHEDULED` or `SWAP_ISSUED` status — using **closed** intervals: two windows collide when the new window starts at or before an existing window's end **and** ends at or after that window's start, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide. This is deliberately the opposite convention to crew windows in §3.4, where the intervals are half-open and touching is legal. The number of colliding tickets must be strictly below `service_bay_capacity`, or the request fails with `ERR_SERVICE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Tickets in `RESOLVED` or `VOIDED` status release their bay immediately.

A technician who cannot fix the product on site issues a **swap**, naming a `replacement_unit_id`. The replacement must be `AVAILABLE`, must sit at the ticket's store and must share the serviced unit's `category`, or the request is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_UNIT_INVALID`; that single check owns replacement eligibility entirely, so a swap onto a unit that is already out on rent yields this error rather than a unit-status error. Swap issuance runs a four-step cascade. The ticket moves `SCHEDULED` → `SWAP_ISSUED`, `replacement_unit_id` is recorded and `issued_by_user_id` captures the issuing `ServiceTechnician`. The replacement unit moves `AVAILABLE` → `ON_RENT` with its `replaced_unit_id` set to the serviced unit's identifier — the self-referential link that makes a loaner traceable back to what it stood in for — and the agreement's `unit_id` is re-pointed at the replacement, carrying `accumulated_rent_received_cents` forward onto it. The agreement moves `DELIVERED` → `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING` and its `swap_count` rises by one. The original unit moves `ON_RENT` → `IN_SERVICE`, which raises the store's `units_in_service_count` by one while `units_on_rent_count` stays exactly where it was, because the replacement took the vacated slot; when `units_in_service_count` reaches or passes `service_bay_capacity`, `service_intake_open` flips from true to false and the store stops accepting new tickets.

Resolution closes the ticket and concedes downtime. The technician submits a `resolution` and a `rent_credit_days` count between 0 and 30 inclusive, and that many days are added to the agreement's `service_credit_days` in either branch. A `resolution` of `REPAIRED` is legal only on a ticket still `SCHEDULED`, where no swap was ever issued: the ticket moves `SCHEDULED` → `RESOLVED`, nothing moves in the possession dimension, the agreement stays `DELIVERED`, and the unit stays `ON_RENT` at its existing `condition_grade`. A `resolution` of `SWAPPED` is legal only on a ticket in `SWAP_ISSUED`: the ticket moves `SWAP_ISSUED` → `RESOLVED`, the agreement returns `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING` → `DELIVERED` with its `unit_id` left pointing at the replacement it already moved to, and the swapped-out unit moves `IN_SERVICE` → `AVAILABLE` with its `rent_cycle_count` incremented and its `condition_grade` stepped down one notch, re-entering the rentable pool cheaper than it left it. The store's `units_in_service_count` falls by one and `service_intake_open` returns to true once that count is again below `service_bay_capacity`. A mismatched pairing — `REPAIRED` on a swapped ticket or `SWAPPED` on a merely scheduled one — is refused with `ERR_TICKET_RESOLUTION_MISMATCH`. Issuing a swap on a ticket that is not `SCHEDULED`, or resolving one that is neither `SCHEDULED` nor `SWAP_ISSUED`, is refused earlier still with `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`; a swap on an agreement whose goods were never delivered fails with `ERR_POSSESSION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Three Ways an Agreement Ends

**Early purchase.** A renter who wants to own the goods now asks for a quote, which may be requested by the customer or by store staff and only while `payment_status` is `CURRENT` — a delinquent or defaulted account is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_CURRENT` — and `possession_status` is `DELIVERED`. A quote pins everything it needs so that the number it hands the customer cannot drift: `rent_applied_snapshot_cents` freezes `rent_applied_cents`, `loyalty_tier_snapshot` freezes the customer's `loyalty_tier`, and `formula_applied` records which of the two statutory variants was used, read from the jurisdiction pinned in `state_code_snapshot` rather than from the store's current jurisdiction. Issuing a quote supersedes every other `OPEN` quote on the agreement (`OPEN` → `SUPERSEDED`), so only the newest quote may ever be accepted, and it bumps the agreement's `version`.

The two variants produce materially different figures from identical ledger state, which is exactly why the jurisdiction and not the store chooses. Under `PCT_OF_REMAINING`, `pre_discount_cents` is the amount still owed under the disclosed total — `total_of_payments_cents` less `rent_applied_snapshot_cents` — multiplied by the jurisdiction's `early_purchase_pct` and rounded half up; the processing fee and the entire rental markup therefore remain in the base. Under `CASH_PRICE_MINUS_CREDIT`, `pre_discount_cents` is the depreciated `adjusted_cash_price_cents` less a rent credit, where the credit is `rent_applied_snapshot_cents` multiplied by the jurisdiction's `rent_credit_pct` and rounded half up; the result is floored at 0, and neither the markup nor the processing fee is in the base at all.

Loyalty then discounts the quote, but only the rental part of it. `loyalty_discount_cents` is `pre_discount_cents` multiplied by the tier's discount rate — nothing at all for `STANDARD`, three percent for `SILVER`, five percent for `GOLD` — rounded half up. `late_fee_component_cents` pins the sum of `late_fee_balance_cents` and `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents` at issuance, and `payoff_amount_cents` is `pre_discount_cents` less `loyalty_discount_cents` **plus** `late_fee_component_cents`. The ordering is pinned: arrears are added after the discount is taken and are never themselves discounted, and the payoff never falls below zero. The quote is stamped `OPEN` with `issued_at` at the logical clock and an `expires_at` seven days later.

Accepting a quote is a staff action requiring an exact `payment_amount_cents` equal to `payoff_amount_cents` — a discrepancy of a single cent is refused with `ERR_PAYOFF_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Acceptance is legal only on an `OPEN` quote (`ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`) whose `expires_at` the clock has not passed (`ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`), and only while the agreement is `CURRENT` and `DELIVERED`. The quote moves `OPEN` → `ACCEPTED`; the agreement records `early_purchase_paid_cents` equal to the payoff, clears `late_fee_balance_cents` and `reinstatement_fee_balance_cents`, sets `paid_pct` to 1.0000, and moves `payment_status` `CURRENT` → `CLOSED` and `possession_status` `DELIVERED` → `OWNED`. Note that `early_purchase_paid_cents` is never folded into `rent_applied_cents`. The unit moves `ON_RENT` → `SOLD`, the store's `units_on_rent_count` falls by one, and the unit's `accumulated_rent_received_cents` grows by the agreement's `rent_applied_cents`. Two cascades clean up the operational tail: any delivery order on the agreement still `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED`, releasing its crew window, and any service ticket in `SCHEDULED` or `SWAP_ISSUED` moves to `VOIDED`, releasing its bay. Finally a ledger entry with `entry_type` `EARLY_PAYOFF_BONUS` and a `delta_points` of 500 is appended — it carries the `customer_id` and `agreement_id` but no `source_payment_id`, since no renewal payment produced it — and the customer's balance, lifetime total and tier are recomputed.

**Voluntary return.** A renter may hand the goods back at any time while `payment_status` is `CURRENT` or `DELINQUENT`, either directly or through the front counter, submitting a `return_reason` that must be `VOLUNTARY_RETURN` on this route. Returning does **not** close the agreement — the agreement is only economically closed once the returned unit has been graded — but it does run a four-step cascade. A return inspection is created `PENDING` with the `agreement_id`, the `unit_id` copied from the agreement, a null `assessed_grade` and a `depreciation_tier` sitting at its `TIER_1` default until grading bands it. The agreement moves `DELIVERED` → `RETURN_PENDING_INSPECTION` while the payment dimension is untouched. The unit moves `ON_RENT` → `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE`, the store's `units_on_rent_count` falls by one, and a provisional book residual is written. Every service ticket on that unit still in `SCHEDULED` or `SWAP_ISSUED` status is moved to `VOIDED`, releasing the store's bay capacity, and for each voided ticket that had already issued a replacement, that replacement unit moves `ON_RENT` → `AVAILABLE` with its `rent_cycle_count` incremented. Two further tidyings follow from the goods coming back: a delivery order on the agreement still in `SCHEDULED` status moves `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED`, releasing its crew window, and a reinstatement window still `OPEN` moves `OPEN` → `EXPIRED`, extinguishing reinstatement rights — an expiry on this route does **not** default the agreement, because only an expiry discovered by a day-close carries the `DELINQUENT` → `DEFAULTED` consequence. A return taken while a swap is outstanding — that is, from `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING` → `RETURN_PENDING_INSPECTION` — runs the identical four-step cascade and additionally decrements the store's `units_in_service_count` for the unit already sitting in the bay.

The provisional residual is the store's honest guess before anyone has looked at the goods. A next-cycle factor is formed as one minus five hundredths for each cycle the unit will have completed once this rental is counted — that is, one more than its current `rent_cycle_count` — floored at 0.60. The residual is the unit's `cash_price_cents` multiplied by a grade factor and by that next-cycle factor, rounded half up, with deductions subtracted afterwards and the result floored at 0. In the provisional variant the grade factor is read from one notch **below** the unit's current `condition_grade` — the assumed wear of a completed rental, using the same ladder where a step below `GRADE_C` is still `GRADE_C` — and deductions are 0, because damage and missing parts are still unknown. The figure is written to the unit's `residual_value_cents` and `residual_valuation_basis` moves `NONE` → `PROVISIONAL`.

**Grading** is a `ServiceTechnician` action on a `PENDING` inspection whose unit must be in `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE` status (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` otherwise), and it is what closes the books. The technician submits an `assessed_grade` of `GRADE_A`, `GRADE_B`, `GRADE_C` or `UNRENTABLE`, a `damage_deduction_cents` of 0 or more for damage beyond normal wear, and a `missing_parts_count` between 0 and 20; `graded_by_user_id` records the technician and the inspection moves `PENDING` → `GRADED`. The final residual uses the same product and the same next-cycle factor as the provisional one, but reads the grade factor from the submitted `assessed_grade` — which may be better or worse than the assumed step-down — and subtracts real deductions: `damage_deduction_cents` plus 2,500 cents for each missing part. An `assessed_grade` of `UNRENTABLE` short-circuits the whole formula to a residual of 0. The result is stored as the inspection's `final_residual_value_cents` and overwrites the unit's `residual_value_cents`, with `residual_valuation_basis` moving `PROVISIONAL` → `FINAL`; the gap between the two numbers is the store's grading variance.

Two further figures fall out of the final residual. `re_rent_rate_cents` projects what the unit would rent for next time, always assuming a weekly, fifty-two-renewal re-rent regardless of the closed agreement's `term_type`: the final residual is multiplied by the weekly markup of 2.20 and rounded half up, and that product is divided by 52 and rounded **up** to the next whole cent. An unrentable unit has a residual of 0 and therefore a projected rate of 0; the projection is never negative. `depreciation_tier` bands the unit against its original `cash_price_cents` — never the adjusted price — using the exact, unrounded ratio of `final_residual_value_cents` to `cash_price_cents`: a final residual of exactly 0 is `WRITE_OFF`, a ratio of 0.80 or above is `TIER_1`, a ratio of 0.60 or above is `TIER_2`, and anything else is `TIER_3`.

Grading then cascades. On grades `GRADE_A`, `GRADE_B` or `GRADE_C` the unit takes the assessed grade as its `condition_grade`, increments `rent_cycle_count` and moves `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE` → `AVAILABLE`, closing the re-rent loop. On `UNRENTABLE` — equivalently, on a `WRITE_OFF` tier — the unit instead moves `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE` → `RETIRED` with `retired_reason` set to `UNRENTABLE_ON_GRADING` and a residual of 0. The agreement moves `possession_status` `RETURN_PENDING_INSPECTION` → `RETURNED` and `payment_status` to `CLOSED` — from `CURRENT` if the account was current when the goods came back, or from `DELINQUENT` if it was not. All money counters freeze at their current values and no further renewal may be posted. Where the agreement was delinquent, any `OPEN` reinstatement window is moved to `EXPIRED` and `collections_stage` returns to `NONE`, while the arrears balances freeze in place and stay visible on the closed row. Grading an inspection that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_GRADED`.

### 3.9. Working a Delinquent Account, and the Limits on Taking the Goods Back

Collections is a `CollectionsAgent`'s trade and touches nothing else. A contact may only be logged against an agreement whose `payment_status` is `DELINQUENT` or `DEFAULTED`; a current account is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_NOT_DELINQUENT`. The agent records a `channel` of `PHONE`, `SMS`, `EMAIL` or `IN_PERSON` and an `outcome` of `NO_ANSWER`, `LEFT_MESSAGE`, `SPOKE_TO_CUSTOMER` or `PROMISE_TO_PAY`. The row stamps `contacted_at` with the logical clock, records the agent in `contacted_by_user_id`, and is immutable thereafter.

Three statutory limits gate the call, in a fixed order. **Quiet hours** come first: contact is permitted only when the clock's UTC hour is at or after the jurisdiction's `quiet_hours_end_utc_hour` and strictly before its `quiet_hours_start_utc_hour`, and a call inside the prohibited span is refused with `ERR_QUIET_HOURS_VIOLATION`. **Frequency** comes second: the rolling count is the number of contacts already on **this** agreement whose `contacted_at` falls strictly after the instant seven days before the clock and at or before the clock itself — a half-open window, so a contact made exactly seven days ago has already fallen out — and contacts on the customer's other agreements never count. That count must be strictly below the jurisdiction's `max_collection_contacts_per_week`, so in a jurisdiction permitting three contacts a week the fourth is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_FREQUENCY_EXCEEDED`. **Channel usability** comes third: `PHONE` and `SMS` require a non-empty `phone` on the customer, `EMAIL` requires a non-empty `email`, and `IN_PERSON` requires `in_person_contact_consent` to be true, failing otherwise with `ERR_CONTACT_CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE`. The stored row records `rolling_7d_count_at_contact` as the count evaluated **before** the row was appended, and the agreement's `collection_contact_count_7d` is then rewritten to the count including it. Logging a contact never touches `collections_stage`: working an account does not change its posture.

**Repossession** is the last resort and is hedged on four sides. It requires `confirm` to be true, an agreement whose `payment_status` is `DEFAULTED` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DEFAULTED` otherwise) and whose `possession_status` is `DELIVERED` or `SERVICE_SWAP_PENDING`, and it is refused outright when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the customer's `verified_by_user_id`. Beyond those, and in this order: no reinstatement window may still be `OPEN`, because the statutory right to catch up outranks the store's right to recover (`ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_OPEN`); the customer's equity must be below the statutory ceiling; and at least one collection contact must be on record as documented pre-repossession notice (`ERR_COLLECTION_NOTICE_REQUIRED`).

The equity test is the substantive protection. `paid_pct` is `rent_applied_cents` divided by `total_of_payments_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and repossession is prohibited once `paid_pct` has reached the jurisdiction's `repossession_paid_pct_limit` — a customer sitting exactly at the limit is protected, and the refusal is `ERR_REPOSSESSION_EQUITY_PROTECTED`. Only completed renewal periods build equity: `partial_credit_cents`, late fees and reinstatement fees are all excluded from the numerator, so a household that has handed over a great deal of money in dribs and drabs may still be repossessed while one that completed the same number of whole periods may not.

A permitted repossession moves `possession_status` to `REPOSSESSED` and `payment_status` `DEFAULTED` → `CLOSED`, and sets `collections_stage` to `ADVERSE` — it is already `ADVERSE` whenever the default sweep preceded the recovery, which is why the customer's `adverse_agreement_count` is recomputed as the number of that customer's agreements in `ADVERSE` rather than blindly incremented, leaving the count unchanged in that ordinary case, with `account_flag` set to `ADVERSE` while the count is 1 or more. The recovered goods still pass through condition grading: an inspection is created `PENDING` with a `return_reason` of `REPOSSESSION_RECOVERY` — the only route by which that enum member is ever written — and the `unit_id` taken from the agreement. The unit moves `ON_RENT` → `RETURNED_PENDING_GRADE`, the store's `units_on_rent_count` falls by one, and the provisional residual described in §3.8 is written with `residual_valuation_basis` moving to `PROVISIONAL`, to be overwritten by the final figure when a technician grades it. As with a voluntary return, any service ticket on that unit still in `SCHEDULED` or `SWAP_ISSUED` status moves to `VOIDED`, releasing the store's bay capacity.

| `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` | 403 | a Customer caller addresses a customer, agreement, quote or ticket that is not its own. |
