# Hospital Accreditation Survey Readiness API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Joint Commission accredited hospital lives inside an unannounced survey window that opens somewhere between eighteen and thirty-six months after its last full survey. Between those two dates, accreditation readiness is not a document — it is an operational posture that has to be measured, defended and repaired continuously. Today that work is spread across disconnected artefacts: a spreadsheet that maps each accreditation standard to a named owner, a shared drive of policies and inspection reports that nobody re-validates when they expire, a mock-tracer binder whose scores are computed by hand, a life-safety rounding clipboard whose deficiencies are tracked in email, and a periodic performance review self-assessment that quietly overstates compliance because the people rating the standards are the people accountable for them.

This API replaces all of it with a single accreditation system of record. It holds the standard-to-owner map and the per-chapter evidence repositories; it runs the mock tracer program and derives a defensible unit-level readiness score from it; it turns life safety and environment-of-care rounding into severity-weighted deficiency tickets whose due dates accelerate when a unit is already struggling; it predicts the survey window, gates command-center activation on a lead-time rule, and locks the hospital down while surveyors are on site; it classifies each surveyor finding by observed non-compliance rate, standard risk tier and patient harm, then drives the evidence-of-standards-compliance response cycle including clarification rounds, condition-level escalation and the follow-up survey; and it scores the periodic performance review twice — once from what the units claim and once from what the evidence actually shows — so that overstatement is measured rather than assumed.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. The first is **segregation of duties**: the person who owns a standard may not validate its own evidence, the person who manages a unit may not sign off on that unit's remediation, and only the accrediting body's own surveyor may write findings or rule on a response. The second is **conserved derived state**: every counter, ledger and score in the system is a pure function of the rows beneath it, and every operation that changes those rows must propagate the consequence immediately, in the same request, all the way up the graph.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to perform the requested operation — including the case where the role is permitted on the endpoint generally but not for the specific `action` or `decision` value supplied in the body — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are:

- **`AccreditationDirector`** — owns the standards-to-owner map, the accreditation calendar, the survey command center and the periodic performance review cycle. This is the only role that may register chapters, standards, owner assignments, revocations, hospital units and survey windows, drive survey-window transitions, file, revise and attest periodic performance review self-assessments, and advance the server's logical clock.
- **`StandardOwner`** — the accountable owner of one or more standards. Files documents into a standard's evidence repository, submits them for review, and authors evidence-of-standards-compliance submissions against survey findings.
- **`EvidenceValidator`** — the independent reviewer who validates or rejects filed evidence, and who may never review a standard they themselves own.
- **`TracerSurveyor`** — runs the mock tracer program: schedules tracers, records standard-level observations, and starts, completes or aborts them.
- **`SafetyOfficer`** — performs life safety and environment-of-care rounding, opens and closes rounding sessions, tickets deficiencies, escalates them, and verifies or fails remediation.
- **`UnitManager`** — a unit-scoped remediation role that may only progress remediation work on tickets belonging to its own unit.
- **`ExternalSurveyor`** — the accrediting body's surveyor, who records findings during an active survey, escalates condition-level findings and rules on evidence-of-standards-compliance submissions.

Every read operation in the system is available to all seven roles. Write operations are restricted as described above and enumerated per endpoint in §4.

Identity semantics differ between the roles, and the distinction matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `AccreditationDirector`, `StandardOwner`, `EvidenceValidator`, `TracerSurveyor`, `SafetyOfficer` or `ExternalSurveyor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `owner_user_id` on an owner assignment, as `submitted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on an evidence item, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a tracer observation, as `verified_by_user_id` on a deficiency ticket, as `submitted_by_user_id` on an evidence-of-standards-compliance submission, or as `attested_by_user_id` on a periodic performance review self-assessment — and it may compare it against those previously recorded opaque strings, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `UnitManager`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HospitalUnit`; ownership checks on a deficiency ticket compare the `unit_id` of the ticket's rounding session to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sorting and Collection Filters

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every collection is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The `meta` object of every collection response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page.

Collection reads also accept filters that narrow the result set by a stored attribute before pagination. The standards of a chapter may be filtered by `compliance_state`; the owner assignments of a standard by `status`; a standard's evidence repository by `validation_state`; the mock tracer collection by `unit_id`, `chapter_id` and `state`; both observation collections — the one scoped to a tracer and the one scoped to a standard — by `result`; the rounding-session collection by `unit_id`, `round_type` and `state`; the deficiency-ticket collection by `rounding_session_id`, `standard_id`, `state` and `severity`; the survey-finding collection by `survey_window_id`, `standard_id`, `unit_id` and `state`; and a chapter's periodic performance review collection by `state`. The computed unit readiness read accepts a single `mode` selector, described in §2.7.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Its Sweeps

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** that begins at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time; every deadline, window boundary, expiry and "current instant" in this specification means the value of that logical clock at the moment the request is processed. Only an `AccreditationDirector` may advance it, by supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`. The response to that call reports the resulting `current_utc`, together with `expired_evidence_count` and `eligible_window_count` describing what the advance swept.

Advancing the clock is not a passive assignment; it runs two scheduled sweeps in the same request. First, the **evidence expiry sweep**: every evidence item in `VALIDATED` whose `expires_at` is not null and is at or before the new clock value moves to `EXPIRED`. The boundary is inclusive — an `expires_at` exactly equal to the new clock expires. Each such expiry decrements the owning standard's `validated_evidence_count`, which may in turn pull the standard from `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` back to `MAPPED` and decrement its chapter's `evidence_complete_standard_count`. Second, the **survey eligibility re-evaluation**: every survey window whose `window_state` is `PROJECTED` and whose `predicted_open_date` is at or before the new clock while its `predicted_close_date` is at or after the new clock moves to `window_state` `ELIGIBLE`. `expired_evidence_count` is the number of evidence items expired by the sweep and `eligible_window_count` is the number of windows that became eligible.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Version Semantics

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by one on every mutation of that resource.

Two resources are append-only records of a moment and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: `TracerObservation` and `EscSubmission`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources entirely. Note that the `decision` and `decision_note` of an evidence-of-standards-compliance submission are still written by the reviewing call; that write does not introduce an `updated_at` or a `version` on the submission.

Crucially, "mutation" includes changes the server makes on its own initiative. When a validated evidence item pushes a standard from `MAPPED` to `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, both the evidence item and the standard are mutated, and so is the chapter whose `evidence_complete_standard_count` moved. When a deficiency ticket is raised and the unit's penalty ledger absorbs its points, the unit is mutated. Cascades therefore bump `updated_at` and `version` on every entity they touch, not only on the entity the client addressed. Client-visible consequences of this are spelled out in §2.7: because scheduling a tracer and raising a deficiency both increment `HospitalUnit.version`, a client holding a version read before either of those events is holding a stale view.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Date Arithmetic

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, and every rounding step described in §3 is pinned — intermediate values are rounded where the specification says they are rounded, and the results of those roundings feed the next step.

Scores and rates carry fixed precision. A mock tracer's `tracer_score`, a unit's `readiness_score`, and the periodic performance review's `self_score`, `verified_score` and `gap` are all carried to exactly two decimal places. A finding's `noncompliance_rate` and the per-chapter compliance rate inside the unit readiness computation are carried to exactly four decimal places, and every comparison against a rate threshold uses the **rounded** value, not the raw quotient. A tracer's `scored_weight` and `earned_weight` are kept exact and are not rounded.

Instants — `due_at`, `predicted_open_date`, `predicted_close_date`, `command_center_activation_allowed_from`, `follow_up_due_at`, `initial_response_due_at`, `response_due_at`, `revoked_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `closed_at`, `survey_opened_at`, `survey_closed_at`, `accepted_at` and `last_noncompliant_observed_at` — are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Day arithmetic is exact multiples of twenty-four hours with no calendar, weekend or business-day adjustment. Month arithmetic, which appears only in the survey-window prediction, lands on the same day-of-month in the target month and clamps to the last day of that month when the day does not exist there, preserving the time of day. Interval comparisons for tracer occupancy are minute-exact; interval comparisons for rounding-session capacity are second-exact.

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Unit and Finding Writes

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a second write is *legitimate* product behaviour and therefore cannot be blocked by a state precondition — what must be blocked is a write issued from an out-of-date view of the parent.

Scheduling a mock tracer — `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers` — requires a non-optional `expected_unit_version` field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `HospitalUnit`. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version` at write time, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Scheduling a second tracer on the same unit for a different window is perfectly legal, so the rejection is not about duplication; it exists because the surveyor's picture of the unit — its `posture`, its `open_deficiency_penalty_points`, its already-scheduled tracers — has moved on since they read it.

Submitting evidence of standards compliance — `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions` — requires a non-optional `expected_finding_version` field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SurveyFinding`. If that value does not equal the finding's current `version` at write time, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Resubmission legitimately supersedes the previous round under the clarification cycle, so a second submission would otherwise succeed; the check exists so that an owner who has not yet seen the newest clarification decision — and the `response_due_at` it recomputed — cannot overwrite the round counter.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_*_version` input.

### 2.8. Path Traversal and Referential Resolution

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the rejection. A request addressed at a standard nested under a chapter fails with `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_FOUND` when the chapter does not exist, before the standard is looked up at all. The eleven not-found codes map one-to-one onto the eleven addressable resources: `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OWNER_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRACER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUNDING_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PPR_NOT_FOUND`, all at HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved. A missing `unit_id` yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `chapter_id` yields `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing `standard_id` yields `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to exactly five write operations: `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers`, `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions`, `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/observations`, `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions/{id}/deficiencies` and `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/findings`.

Where a child resource's owning parent is addressable in the path, the stored foreign key is taken from the path and is never read from the body. A standard's `chapter_id`, an owner assignment's and an evidence item's `standard_id`, an observation's `mock_tracer_id`, a deficiency ticket's `rounding_session_id`, a finding's `survey_window_id`, a submission's `survey_finding_id` and a self-assessment's `chapter_id` are all derived this way.

### 2.9. Conserved Ledgers and Cascading Recomputation

Two counters in this system are ledgers in the strict sense: they are conserved quantities that must at every instant equal an aggregate over their contributing rows, and every operation that adds to them must have a compensating operation that removes from them.

The **chapter mapped-standard ledger** pins `Chapter.mapped_standard_count` to the number of standards in that chapter whose `active_owner_count` is at least 1. It is incremented only on the transition of a standard's `active_owner_count` from zero to one, and decremented only on the transition from one to zero. Adding a second or third owner to an already-mapped standard does not move it.

The **unit penalty ledger** pins `HospitalUnit.open_deficiency_penalty_points` to the sum of `penalty_points` over every deficiency ticket of that unit that has not reached `VERIFIED` — that is, every ticket in `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `REMEDIATED` or `ESCALATED`. Raising a ticket adds its points; verifying a ticket removes them; escalating a ticket that gains severity replaces its old contribution with the new one. Failing verification moves a ticket from `REMEDIATED` back to `OPEN` and therefore leaves the ledger untouched, because the ticket never left the non-verified population.

Derived state is recomputed synchronously, in the same request, and cascades follow the graph without regard to which aggregate the client addressed. A single deficiency-ticket escalation can, in one request, raise the ticket's `severity` and `penalty_points`, change the unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points`, flip the unit's `posture`, abort every scheduled tracer on that unit, zero the unit's `scheduled_tracer_count`, and recompute the unit's `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`. A single tracer completion can move several standards to `DEFICIENT`, move their chapters' counters, flip a chapter's `readiness_flag`, and return that chapter's submitted periodic performance review self-assessments for reassessment. Each entity touched by such a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four independence rules are enforced across the accreditation workflow, and they are the reason several operations that look procedurally identical carry different authorization outcomes.

An `EvidenceValidator` performing either the `VALIDATE` or the `REJECT` action on an evidence item must not be the `owner_user_id` of any owner assignment with `status` `ACTIVE` on that item's standard; a validator reviewing evidence for a standard they actively own is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). An evidence-of-standards-compliance submission must come from a principal holding an owner assignment on the finding's standard with `status` `ACTIVE` and `designation` `PRIMARY`; a `SECONDARY` owner, or a principal with no active assignment at all, is rejected with `ERR_SUBMITTER_NOT_STANDARD_OWNER` (HTTP 422). A `UnitManager` may drive a deficiency ticket only through the `START_WORK` and `MARK_REMEDIATED` actions, and only for tickets whose rounding session belongs to the unit named by `X-User-ID`; the actions `VERIFY`, `FAIL_VERIFICATION` and `ESCALATE` are reserved to a `SafetyOfficer`, so that no unit signs off on its own remediation. And findings, escalations and response decisions belong exclusively to the `ExternalSurveyor` — the hospital cannot write its own findings or accept its own corrective-action plans.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

Many operations in this system are guarded by several rules at once, and the rule that fires is determined by a single hospital-wide precedence ladder rather than by the order in which an implementation happens to evaluate its checks. The lower the number below, the earlier the check runs; the first check that fails produces the response, and no later check is evaluated. Where a rule guards more than one endpoint, every endpoint it guards is named.

1. Authentication: `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` names a declared role — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401, on every endpoint.
2. Authorization: the acting role may perform this operation, and on a multi-action endpoint the role is permitted for the requested action specifically (`SUBMIT` is `StandardOwner`-only, `VALIDATE` and `REJECT` are `EvidenceValidator`-only, `VERIFY`, `FAIL_VERIFICATION` and `ESCALATE` are `SafetyOfficer`-only) — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403, on every endpoint.
3. Request shape: every required body field is present, every enum value is a member of its declared set, every number is within range, and `self_ratings` contains no duplicated `standard_id` — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400, on every write endpoint.
4. Pagination bounds: `limit` within 1 through 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 400, on every collection read.
5. Path existence, left to right — the eleven 404 codes of §2.8, on every endpoint with a path identifier.
6. Body foreign-key existence for `unit_id`, `chapter_id` and `standard_id` — `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, 404, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers`, `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions`, `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/observations`, `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions/{id}/deficiencies` and `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/findings`.
7. Unit scoping: when `X-User-Role` is `UnitManager`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `HospitalUnit` reached through the ticket's rounding session — `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403, on `POST /accreditation/deficiency-tickets/{id}/transition`.
8. Stale-view rejection: `expected_unit_version` equals `HospitalUnit.version`, and `expected_finding_version` equals `SurveyFinding.version` — `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FINDING_VERSION`, 412, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers` and `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`.
9. Interval sanity: `window_end` strictly after `window_start` — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers` and `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions`.
10. Sample sanity: `sampled_count` at least 1, and `observed_noncompliance_count` at least 1 and no greater than `sampled_count` — `ERR_SAMPLE_COUNT_INVALID`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/findings`.
11. Uniqueness of `Chapter.code`, `HospitalUnit.name`, `SurveyWindow.cycle_label`, the pair of chapter and `standard_code`, and the pair of chapter and `period_label` — `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/chapters`, `POST /accreditation/units`, `POST /accreditation/survey-windows`, `POST /accreditation/chapters/{id}/standards` and `POST /accreditation/chapters/{id}/ppr-self-assessments`.
12. `VALIDATE` requires `validation_state` to be `SUBMITTED` — `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/evidence-items/{id}/transition`.
13. The requested action is a declared transition out of the resource's current state (evidence `SUBMIT` from `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` and `REJECT` from `SUBMITTED` or `VALIDATED`; tracer `START` from `SCHEDULED` and `ABORT` from `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; deficiency-ticket, survey-window, finding and self-assessment actions as declared in §3; self-assessment revision from `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT`) — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409, on every action-driven write: `POST /accreditation/evidence-items/{id}/transition`, `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/transition`, `POST /accreditation/deficiency-tickets/{id}/transition`, `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`, `POST /accreditation/owner-assignments/{id}/revoke`, `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions/{id}/close` and `POST /accreditation/ppr-self-assessments/{id}/revise`.
14. `COMPLETE` requires the tracer's `state` to be `IN_PROGRESS`, and recording an observation likewise requires the tracer to be `IN_PROGRESS` — `ERR_TRACER_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/transition` and `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/observations`.
15. Raising a deficiency requires the rounding session's `state` to be `OPEN` — `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_CLOSED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions/{id}/deficiencies`.
16. `VERIFY` and `FAIL_VERIFICATION` require the ticket's rounding session `state` to be `CLOSED` — `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CLOSED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/deficiency-tickets/{id}/transition`.
17. `OPEN_SURVEY` requires `window_state` to be `ELIGIBLE` or `CLOSED`; `PROJECTED` and `ACTIVE_SURVEY` are rejected here with `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`. Whether a `CLOSED` window actually requires a follow-up, and whether the follow-up deadline has passed, is checked later at rung 32 — so a `CLOSED` window always reaches this rung successfully regardless of its `follow_up_required` flag.
18. `OPEN_SURVEY` requires `command_center_state` to be `ACTIVATED` — `ERR_COMMAND_CENTER_NOT_ACTIVATED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`.
19. Recording a finding requires the window's `window_state` to be `ACTIVE_SURVEY` — `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/findings`.
20. The finding's `state` permits the operation: a submission from `RESPONSE_PENDING`, `CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED` or `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`; a review from `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`; an escalation from `RESPONSE_PENDING` — `ERR_FINDING_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`, `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-review` and `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/escalate`.
21. Attestation requires `state` to be `SUBMITTED`, rejecting an already-attested assessment with `ERR_PPR_ALREADY_ATTESTED`, and requires `requires_reassessment` to be false, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_PPR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` — both 409, on `POST /accreditation/ppr-self-assessments/{id}/attest`.
22. Revocation requires the owner assignment's `status` to be `ACTIVE` — `ERR_OWNER_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_REVOKED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/owner-assignments/{id}/revoke`.
23. Both scheduling a tracer and the `START` action require the unit's `posture` to be `OPEN` — `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers` and `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/transition`.
24. No deficiency ticket anywhere in the hospital is in `ESCALATED` with `severity` `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` — `ERR_IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ACTIVE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers` and on the `OPEN_SURVEY` action of `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`.
25. Closing a rounding session requires that none of its tickets is in `ESCALATED` — `ERR_ESCALATED_TICKET_BLOCKS_CLOSE`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions/{id}/close`.
26. A finding whose `condition_level` is true must have reached `CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED` before any submission is accepted — `ERR_CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`.
27. Escalation requires `condition_level` to be true — `ERR_NOT_CONDITION_LEVEL`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/escalate`.
28. Tracer occupancy: no conflicting tracer in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` on the same unit under the half-open interval rule plus the sixty-minute trailing buffer — `ERR_TRACER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers`.
29. Survey-window occupancy: the predicted interval does not intersect that of any window whose `window_state` is not `CLOSED`, with touching endpoints counting as an intersection — `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows`.
30. `START` requires the logical clock to be at or after the tracer's `window_start` — `ERR_TRACER_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/transition`.
31. `ACTIVATE_COMMAND_CENTER` requires the logical clock to be at or after `command_center_activation_allowed_from` — `ERR_COMMAND_CENTER_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`.
32. A follow-up `OPEN_SURVEY` requires `follow_up_required` to be true, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_REQUIRED`, and requires the logical clock to be at or before `follow_up_due_at`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — both 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-windows/{id}/transition`.
33. The logical clock is at or before the finding's `response_due_at`, equality still being inside the window — `ERR_ESC_WINDOW_CLOSED`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`.
34. `VALIDATE` requires `expires_at` to be null or strictly after the logical clock — `ERR_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/evidence-items/{id}/transition`.
35. Rounding capacity: fewer than two intersecting `OPEN` sessions of the same `round_type` on the unit — `ERR_ROUNDING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/rounding-sessions`.
36. `clarification_round_count` is strictly below the two-round ceiling before a clarification decision is issued — `ERR_CLARIFICATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-review`.
37. `leadership_signoff` is true — `ERR_LEADERSHIP_SIGNOFF_REQUIRED`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`.
38. `COMPLETE` requires at least one observation on the tracer — `ERR_NO_OBSERVATIONS_RECORDED`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/transition`.
39. No owner assignment with the same `owner_user_id` and `status` `ACTIVE` already exists on this standard — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OWNER_ASSIGNMENT`, 409, on `POST /accreditation/standards/{id}/owner-assignments`.
40. Segregation of duties: the reviewing `X-User-ID` is not the `owner_user_id` of any owner assignment with `status` `ACTIVE` on the standard — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, on the `VALIDATE` and `REJECT` actions of `POST /accreditation/evidence-items/{id}/transition`.
41. The submitting `X-User-ID` holds an owner assignment on the finding's standard with `status` `ACTIVE` and `designation` `PRIMARY` — `ERR_SUBMITTER_NOT_STANDARD_OWNER`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/survey-findings/{id}/esc-submissions`.
42. `SUBMIT` requires the standard's `compliance_state` not to be `UNMAPPED` and the submitting `X-User-ID` to hold an `ACTIVE` owner assignment on that standard — `ERR_STANDARD_UNMAPPED`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/evidence-items/{id}/transition`.
43. Chapter membership: every `standard_id` inside `self_ratings` belongs to the path chapter, and every observation's `standard_id` belongs to the tracer's `chapter_id` — `ERR_STANDARD_CHAPTER_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/chapters/{id}/ppr-self-assessments`, `POST /accreditation/ppr-self-assessments/{id}/revise` and `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers/{id}/observations`.
44. The tracer's chapter has a `mapped_standard_count` of at least 1 — `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_MAPPED`, 422, on `POST /accreditation/mock-tracers`.

Note the two deliberate inversions in this ladder. Rung 17 runs before rung 32, so an attempt to reopen a `CLOSED` window that needs no follow-up surfaces as `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_REQUIRED` rather than as a state-eligibility error. And rung 12 runs before rung 34, so a `VALIDATE` on an evidence item that is both un-submitted and expired surfaces as `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chapters, Standards and the Accountability Map

Everything in this system hangs off the accreditation manual, and the manual is modelled as **chapters** containing **standards**. An `AccreditationDirector` registers a chapter with a `code` — two to four uppercase letters, unique across all chapters, drawn from the accreditation body's chapter set such as EC, LS, IC, MM, NPSG, EM and PC — a human-readable `name`, and a `weight_pct` expressing how much of a unit's readiness score that chapter is responsible for. The weight must be greater than zero and no greater than 100, carried to two decimal places. The chapter also declares `evidence_min_count`, the number of validated documents a standard in that chapter must accumulate before it counts as evidence-complete; when the field is omitted it defaults to 2, and any supplied value must be at least 1. A colliding `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

A freshly registered chapter is an empty shell with every derived counter at zero — `standard_count`, `mapped_standard_count`, `evidence_complete_standard_count`, `deficient_standard_count` and `ppr_escalation_count` — and a `readiness_flag` of `ON_TRACK`. The four population counters mean exactly what their names say: `standard_count` is the number of standards in the chapter regardless of their compliance state, `mapped_standard_count` is the number whose `active_owner_count` is at least 1, `evidence_complete_standard_count` is the number whose `compliance_state` is `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, and `deficient_standard_count` is the number whose `compliance_state` is `DEFICIENT`. `ppr_escalation_count` is the number of periodic performance review self-assessments filed for the chapter that were flagged for overstatement.

The chapter's `readiness_flag` is the hospital's early-warning signal, and it moves between exactly two values, `ON_TRACK` and `AT_RISK`. It flips from `ON_TRACK` to `AT_RISK` as soon as either the chapter accumulates two or more deficient standards or its overstatement escalations reach two. It reverts to `ON_TRACK` only when both conditions have receded — that is, when `deficient_standard_count` is strictly below two **and** `ppr_escalation_count` is strictly below two. The flip to `AT_RISK` is not cosmetic: it reaches sideways into the periodic performance review cycle, as §3.9 describes.

Adding a standard to a chapter is likewise reserved to the `AccreditationDirector`, who supplies a `standard_code` such as EC.02.06.01, a `title` summarising the standard text, and a `risk_tier` of `DIRECT_IMPACT`, `INDIRECT_IMPACT` or `SITUATIONAL_DECISION_RULE`. The owning `chapter_id` is taken from the address of the request, never from the payload. A `standard_code` must be unique within its chapter — the same code under a different chapter is permitted, a repeat within the same chapter is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Creating the standard increments the chapter's `standard_count` and mutates the chapter. The new standard begins at `compliance_state` `UNMAPPED`, with `active_owner_count` and `validated_evidence_count` at zero and `last_noncompliant_observed_at` null.

`compliance_state` is never written directly by a client. It is a derived posture over four values — `UNMAPPED`, `MAPPED`, `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` and `DEFICIENT` — and the machine that drives it is cyclic: a standard can leave `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, return to it, and leave again without limit. What moves it is accountability (§3.1), evidence (§3.2), tracer and surveyor findings (§3.4 and §3.7) and remediation verification (§3.5).

Accountability is recorded as an **owner assignment**. The `AccreditationDirector` names an `owner_user_id` — the opaque identifier of the `StandardOwner` who will answer for the standard — and a `designation` of either `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY`. Only a `PRIMARY` owner may sign an evidence-of-standards-compliance submission; a `SECONDARY` owner may only maintain the evidence repository. The standard is taken from the path. A new assignment begins with `status` `ACTIVE` and `revoked_at` null. If the same `owner_user_id` already holds an `ACTIVE` assignment on that standard, the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OWNER_ASSIGNMENT`; two distinct owners on one standard, or the same owner on two standards, are both fine.

Mapping a standard has three consequences in one request. The standard's `active_owner_count` increases by one. If that count moved from zero to one, the standard transitions `UNMAPPED → MAPPED` and its chapter's `mapped_standard_count` increases by one — the count only moves on that zero-to-one edge, so a second owner leaves it alone. And the standard itself is mutated, so its `version` advances.

Revocation is the compensating half. The `AccreditationDirector` revokes an assignment, optionally recording a `reason`, and the assignment must still be `ACTIVE` or the request is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_REVOKED`. Revocation sets `status` to `REVOKED` and stamps `revoked_at` with the current logical clock, then decreases the standard's `active_owner_count` by one. Only when that count falls from one to zero does the standard actually change posture, and here there are two declared branches with distinct chapter consequences. From `MAPPED`, the standard returns to `UNMAPPED` and the chapter's `mapped_standard_count` decreases by one. From `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, the standard also returns to `UNMAPPED`, the chapter's `mapped_standard_count` decreases by one **and** its `evidence_complete_standard_count` decreases by one — losing the last owner drops the standard out of the evidence-complete population without touching the standard's own `validated_evidence_count`, so the documents are still there and still validated should an owner be reinstated. A standard already in `DEFICIENT` has no declared revocation transition and keeps that `compliance_state`; only the owner counters move.

### 3.2. The Evidence Repository: Filing, Independent Review and Expiry

Each standard carries its own evidence repository, and only a `StandardOwner` may file into it. A filing supplies a `title`, a `doc_type` of `POLICY`, `LOG`, `TRAINING_RECORD`, `INSPECTION_REPORT` or `CONTRACT`, an `effective_date` marking when the artefact became effective, and optionally an `expires_at` marking when it stops counting. When `expires_at` is omitted it is stored as null, which means the artefact never expires. The standard is taken from the path. A newly filed item begins at `validation_state` `DRAFT`, with `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` all null and `rejection_count` at zero.

The evidence lifecycle runs across five values — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `VALIDATED`, `REJECTED` and the terminal `EXPIRED` — and it is driven by a single action-bearing operation that accepts an `action` and an optional `review_note`. Three actions are declared, and the ladder at §2.11 rung 2 restricts each to one role: `SUBMIT` belongs to the `StandardOwner`, while `VALIDATE` and `REJECT` belong to the `EvidenceValidator`.

Submitting for review moves the item `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` on a resubmission — the rejection loop is unbounded, and an owner may keep revising and resubmitting a document indefinitely. Two conditions gate the action, and both are reported through the same code: the standard's `compliance_state` must not be `UNMAPPED`, and the submitting principal must hold an owner assignment on that standard with `status` `ACTIVE`. A failure of either is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_UNMAPPED`. On success the item records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting principal's identifier and its `version` advances.

Validation is where independence bites. `VALIDATE` requires the item to be in `SUBMITTED`; from any other `validation_state` it is rejected with `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The reviewing principal must not be the `owner_user_id` of any `ACTIVE` owner assignment on the standard, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the artefact must still be current: `expires_at` must be null or strictly after the current logical clock, or the request is rejected with `ERR_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED`. When those pass, the item moves `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED`, `reviewed_by_user_id` is set to the reviewing principal and `review_note` is captured from the payload, and the standard's `validated_evidence_count` increases by one. If that increase brings the count to at least the owning chapter's `evidence_min_count`, the standard transitions `MAPPED → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` and the chapter's `evidence_complete_standard_count` increases by one.

Rejection has two declared entry points and they differ in exactly one respect. `REJECT` from `SUBMITTED` moves the item `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, increments `rejection_count` by one and records `reviewed_by_user_id`; the standard's counters are untouched because the document never counted. `REJECT` from an already-validated item moves it `VALIDATED → REJECTED`, likewise increments `rejection_count` and records `reviewed_by_user_id`, and additionally decreases the standard's `validated_evidence_count` by one; if that decrease leaves the count strictly below the chapter's `evidence_min_count`, the standard falls back `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → MAPPED` and the chapter's `evidence_complete_standard_count` decreases by one. Both rejection branches require the same independence check, so an `EvidenceValidator` who actively owns the standard is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` whichever state the item is in.

Expiry is the only transition in the evidence machine that no human triggers. When the logical clock advances (§2.4), every item in `VALIDATED` whose `expires_at` is not null and is at or before the new clock value moves `VALIDATED → EXPIRED`. The boundary is inclusive: an `expires_at` exactly equal to the new clock expires. Each expiry decreases the standard's `validated_evidence_count` by one, and that decrease can carry the standard `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → MAPPED` and decrement the chapter's `evidence_complete_standard_count` in the same sweep. `EXPIRED` is terminal — an expired document cannot be resubmitted, only replaced by a new filing. Because the reverse edge exists too, the cycle `MAPPED → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → MAPPED → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` is entirely legal and unbounded over a standard's life.

### 3.3. Hospital Units, Posture and Readiness Scoring

Units are the physical geography of readiness. An `AccreditationDirector` registers one with a `name` that must be unique across the hospital — `ICU-3`, for example — a `unit_type` of `MED_SURG`, `ICU`, `ED`, `OR`, `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH` or `AMBULATORY`, and a `bed_count` that must be at least zero. A colliding `name` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The new unit starts with `posture` `OPEN`, `open_deficiency_penalty_points` at zero, `scheduled_tracer_count` at zero, `readiness_score` at 0.00 and `readiness_band` `NOT_ASSESSED`.

A unit's `posture` is a gate over everything that happens on it, and it takes three values: `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY`. No client writes it. `OPEN → RESTRICTED` fires automatically whenever the unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points` changes — on ticket creation, on escalation, on a failed verification — and the resulting total is at least fifteen points. That transition drags a cascade behind it: every mock tracer of the unit still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `UNIT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, which zeroes `scheduled_tracer_count` and forces a recomputation of `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`. `RESTRICTED → OPEN` fires on the verification of a deficiency ticket once the released points leave the total strictly below fifteen, and likewise recomputes the score and band. Because both edges exist, the cycle `OPEN → RESTRICTED → OPEN` is unbounded.

Survey activity overrides both. When a survey opens, every unit in `OPEN` or in `RESTRICTED` moves to `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY`, and each such move aborts that unit's `SCHEDULED` tracers with `abort_reason` set to `SURVEY_ACTIVE`, zeroes `scheduled_tracer_count` and recomputes the score and band — identical cascade shape from both source postures. When the survey closes, each unit leaves `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY` for whichever posture its penalty ledger now justifies: `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY → OPEN` when `open_deficiency_penalty_points` is strictly below fifteen, and `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY → RESTRICTED` when it is at fifteen or above. Both release edges recompute `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`.

The readiness score itself is the system's headline number, and it is computed in five pinned steps. **Step one** determines which chapters participate: a chapter participates for this unit when at least one mock tracer exists whose unit is this unit, whose chapter is that chapter, whose `state` is `COMPLETED`, and whose `contributes_to_readiness` is true. If no chapter participates, the computation stops immediately with `readiness_score` 0.00 and `readiness_band` `NOT_ASSESSED`, and the penalty is **not** applied — an unassessed unit is not punished for its open deficiencies. Note the exclusion this creates: a tracer that reached `COMPLETED` but whose every observation was `NOT_APPLICABLE` has `contributes_to_readiness` false and therefore does not make its chapter participate.

**Step two** computes a compliance rate for each participating chapter. Take every observation belonging to that chapter's completed, contributing tracers on this unit whose `result` is not `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Each observation carries a credit determined by its result — a `COMPLIANT` observation earns full credit of 1.0, a `PARTIAL` observation earns half credit of 0.5, and a `NON_COMPLIANT` observation earns no credit at all. The chapter's rate is the sum over those observations of `weight` multiplied by credit, divided by the sum of `weight` over the same observations, rounded half up to four decimal places.

**Step three** turns those rates into a weighted base, and the rounding order here is pinned as round-then-sum; summing first and rounding once is explicitly not the rule. Let the participating weight be the sum of `weight_pct` across the participating chapters — this is the value reported as `participating_weight_pct` on the computed readiness read. Each participating chapter's contribution is its `weight_pct` divided by that participating weight, multiplied by the chapter's four-decimal rate, multiplied by one hundred, and then rounded half up to two decimal places. The base is the sum of those already-rounded per-chapter contributions, rounded half up to two decimal places again, and is reported as `base_score`.

**Step four** applies the deficiency penalty. Each penalised ticket contributes its `penalty_points`, and the sum is capped at forty points no matter how much open deficiency the unit is carrying; the applied figure is reported as `penalty_points_applied`. Severity determines a ticket's points: a `LOW` ticket is worth one point, a `MODERATE` ticket three, a `HIGH` ticket seven, and an `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` ticket fifteen. Which tickets are penalised depends on the variant, and the two variants are the whole point of the `mode` selector on the computed readiness read.

The **attested** variant is the default — it applies when `mode` is omitted or supplied as `ATTESTED`, and it is the only variant ever persisted onto the unit's own `readiness_score` and `readiness_band` fields by a cascade. Under it, the penalised set is every deficiency ticket of the unit whose `state` is `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `REMEDIATED` or `ESCALATED`: remediation that has not yet been verified still costs the unit points, because on paper nothing has been proven fixed.

The **projected** variant applies only when `mode` is supplied as `PROJECTED`, is never persisted, and exists so leadership can forecast where the unit will stand once in-flight work lands. It starts from the same set and then excludes tickets that are simultaneously in `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATED` **and** whose `due_at` is at or after the current logical clock — work that is under way and not yet late is assumed to close by its due date. Tickets in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` are counted regardless of their due date, and an in-flight ticket whose `due_at` has already passed is counted too, because a commitment already missed cannot be assumed. For identical stored data the two variants therefore return different scores, which is intentional.

**Step five** produces the score and the band. The score is the base minus the capped penalty, floored at zero so it can never go negative, rounded half up to two decimal places. The band is then read off four thresholds: `READY` at 90.00 or above, `CONDITIONAL` at 75.00 or above, `AT_RISK` at 60.00 or above, and `NOT_READY` below that. `NOT_ASSESSED` is reserved for the no-participating-chapter case of step one.

The computed readiness read returns the whole derivation rather than just the answer: the `unit_id` it was computed for, the `mode` that was applied, the `base_score` from step three, the `penalty_points_applied` from step four, the `participating_weight_pct`, the resulting `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`, and a `chapter_breakdown` array carrying the per-chapter detail behind the base. Every transition in this specification that says it recomputes readiness runs exactly this computation under the attested variant and writes the result onto the unit.

### 3.4. The Mock Tracer Program

A **mock tracer** is a rehearsal survey: a `TracerSurveyor` walks one unit against one chapter's standards and records what they see. Scheduling one is open to both the `TracerSurveyor` and the `AccreditationDirector`, and the payload names the `unit_id` to be traced, the `chapter_id` whose standards will be sampled, a `tracer_type` of `PATIENT`, `SYSTEM` or `INDIVIDUAL`, a half-open occupation interval given as `window_start` and `window_end`, and the stale-view guard `expected_unit_version` described in §2.7.

Scheduling is the most heavily gated write in the system, and the order in which its guards fire is fixed by the ladder in §2.11. The unit and chapter must exist. The `expected_unit_version` must match the unit's current `version`, or the request fails with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION`. The `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The unit's `posture` must be `OPEN`; a `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY` unit refuses new tracers with `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. No deficiency ticket anywhere in the hospital may be sitting in `ESCALATED` with `severity` `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`, or the request fails with `ERR_IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ACTIVE` — an unresolved immediate threat freezes the rehearsal program hospital-wide, not just on the affected unit. The requested window must not conflict with existing occupation, or `ERR_TRACER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the target chapter must actually have accountability in place: its `mapped_standard_count` must be at least one, or the request fails with `ERR_CHAPTER_NOT_MAPPED`, since tracing standards nobody owns produces findings nobody can answer.

Occupation of a unit by a tracer is exclusive — capacity one — and it is measured on the **half-open** interval running from `window_start` up to but not including `window_end`, with a mandatory sixty-minute trailing turnaround after each existing tracer. Concretely, a proposed tracer conflicts with an existing one when the proposed `window_start` falls strictly before the existing `window_end` extended by sixty minutes, **and** the proposed `window_end` falls strictly after the existing `window_start`. A new tracer beginning exactly sixty minutes after an existing tracer's `window_end` is therefore accepted. Only tracers of the same unit whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` occupy anything; a tracer that has reached `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` releases the unit immediately. Comparisons are minute-exact.

A successfully scheduled tracer begins at `state` `SCHEDULED` with `started_at`, `completed_at` and `abort_reason` all null, `scored_weight`, `earned_weight` and `tracer_score` at zero and `contributes_to_readiness` false. It also increments the unit's `scheduled_tracer_count` by one and advances the unit's `version` — which is precisely why a client scheduling two tracers back to back must re-read the unit between them.

Observations are recorded only while the tracer is running, and only by a `TracerSurveyor`. Each observation names the `standard_id` observed and a `result` of `COMPLIANT`, `PARTIAL`, `NON_COMPLIANT` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, plus an optional sampling `weight` that must be greater than zero and defaults to 1 when omitted, and an optional free-text `note` stored as null when omitted. The tracer is taken from the path and stored as `mock_tracer_id`. The tracer's `state` must be `IN_PROGRESS` or the write is refused with `ERR_TRACER_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, and the observed standard must belong to the tracer's own `chapter_id` or the write is refused with `ERR_STANDARD_CHAPTER_MISMATCH` — a tracer sampling one chapter cannot record evidence about another. The server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting surveyor's identifier. No scoring whatsoever happens at observation time; observations accumulate silently until the tracer is closed out. An observation carries only an `id` and a `created_at`, per §2.5.

The tracer lifecycle spans `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, and the two terminal states `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, and is driven by an operation taking an `action` and an optional `abort_reason`. `START` moves the tracer `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and requires two things: the unit's `posture` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, and the logical clock must be at or after the tracer's `window_start`, else `ERR_TRACER_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`. Starting stamps `started_at` with the current logical clock, decrements the unit's `scheduled_tracer_count` by one, recomputes the unit's readiness, and advances the tracer's `version`.

`ABORT` has two declared entry points that behave almost identically. `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` and `IN_PROGRESS → ABORTED` both set `abort_reason` from the payload and both recompute the unit's `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`. They differ only in bookkeeping: aborting from `SCHEDULED` decrements the unit's `scheduled_tracer_count` by one because the tracer was still counted there, while aborting from `IN_PROGRESS` does not, that decrement having already happened at `START`. In neither case does `contributes_to_readiness` become true — an aborted tracer never contributes. Neither abort branch carries any precondition.

`COMPLETE` is the heavyweight. It moves the tracer `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, requires the current `state` to be `IN_PROGRESS` (else `ERR_TRACER_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`) and requires at least one observation to exist on the tracer (else `ERR_NO_OBSERVATIONS_RECORDED`). It then freezes the tracer's score. Every observation of this tracer whose `result` is not `NOT_APPLICABLE` participates — there is no time window, so every row created before the completion call is included, and because both terms are sums the order in which observations were recorded is irrelevant. `scored_weight` becomes the sum of `weight` across those observations and is kept exact. `earned_weight` becomes the sum, across the same observations, of `weight` multiplied by that observation's credit — full credit of 1.0 for `COMPLIANT`, half credit of 0.5 for `PARTIAL`, and zero for `NON_COMPLIANT` — and is likewise kept exact. If `scored_weight` came out as zero, meaning every single observation was `NOT_APPLICABLE`, then `tracer_score` is set to 0.00 and `contributes_to_readiness` stays false. Otherwise `tracer_score` is `earned_weight` divided by `scored_weight`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `contributes_to_readiness` becomes true. `completed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock.

Completion then propagates outward, and this is the longest cascade in the system. Every standard that carries at least one `NON_COMPLIANT` observation on this tracer moves to `DEFICIENT` and has its `last_noncompliant_observed_at` stamped with the current logical clock. From `MAPPED` the move is `MAPPED → DEFICIENT` and the chapter's `deficient_standard_count` increases by one; from `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` the move is `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → DEFICIENT`, the chapter's `evidence_complete_standard_count` decreases by one **and** its `deficient_standard_count` increases by one. Once a chapter's `deficient_standard_count` reaches two, that chapter's `readiness_flag` flips `ON_TRACK → AT_RISK`, and that flip in turn returns every periodic performance review self-assessment of that chapter still in `SUBMITTED` to `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT` with `requires_reassessment` set to true. Finally, the traced unit's `readiness_score` and `readiness_band` are recomputed under the attested variant — which they must be, since the chapter has just become a participating chapter with a freshly frozen rate.

### 3.5. Rounding, Deficiency Ticketing and Remediation

Life safety and environment-of-care rounding is the hospital's own continuous inspection, and it is the `SafetyOfficer`'s domain from end to end. A rounding session is opened against a `unit_id` with a `round_type` of either `LIFE_SAFETY` or `ENVIRONMENT_OF_CARE` and a **closed** occupation interval given as `window_start` and `window_end`, where `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

Unlike tracers, rounding sessions share a unit — but only up to a point. Capacity is aggregate two per unit per `round_type`, measured on the closed interval with touching endpoints counting as an intersection and no turnaround buffer at all. The server counts the sessions on the same unit with the same `round_type` whose `state` is `OPEN` and whose interval intersects the proposed one, an intersection holding when the existing `window_start` is at or before the proposed `window_end` **and** the existing `window_end` is at or after the proposed `window_start`. If that count is already two or more, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROUNDING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A session in `CLOSED` releases its capacity, and a `LIFE_SAFETY` session never competes with an `ENVIRONMENT_OF_CARE` session. Comparisons here are second-exact. A new session begins at `state` `OPEN` with `deficiency_count` at zero and `closed_at` null; its lifecycle is a single linear step to the terminal `CLOSED`.

Ticketing a deficiency requires the session to be `OPEN`, or the write is refused with `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The `SafetyOfficer` supplies the `standard_id` the deficiency maps to, a free-text `description` of what was observed, and a `severity` of `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`. The session is taken from the path and stored as `rounding_session_id`; the ticket's unit is not supplied at all — it is the `unit_id` of that session.

The due date is where this system's most delicate ordering lives, because the ticket both reads and changes the unit's readiness band. The evaluation order is pinned, and it resolves what looks like a circular dependency. **First**, the server snapshots the unit's `readiness_band` exactly as persisted at the start of the request — this ticket's points are not yet part of `open_deficiency_penalty_points`, so the snapshot does not see itself. **Second**, a base allowance is taken from the severity: a `LOW` deficiency gets forty-five days, a `MODERATE` one thirty days, a `HIGH` one fourteen days, and an `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` one a single day. **Third**, if the snapshot band was `AT_RISK` or `NOT_READY`, the allowance is halved — specifically, it becomes the larger of one day and the base allowance divided by two with any fraction discarded — and the ticket's `accelerated` flag is set to true; if the snapshot band was `READY`, `CONDITIONAL` or `NOT_ASSESSED`, the allowance stands unchanged and `accelerated` is false. **Fourth**, `due_at` is set to the current logical clock plus that many days, counted as exact twenty-four-hour multiples. **Fifth**, `penalty_points` is set from the severity — one point for `LOW`, three for `MODERATE`, seven for `HIGH`, fifteen for `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`. **Sixth**, the ticket is inserted and its points are added to the unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points`. **Seventh** and last, the unit's `readiness_score`, `readiness_band` and `posture` are recomputed. Steps one through four never observe anything written in steps six and seven, even though step seven can push the band into the accelerating set — a second ticket raised immediately afterwards will see that new band, but this one does not.

Creating a ticket also increments the session's `deficiency_count` by one and advances the unit's `version`. The new ticket begins at `state` `OPEN` with `verified_by_user_id` null and `failed_verification_count` at zero.

Closing a session is a single guarded step. The `SafetyOfficer` closes it, the server requires that no ticket of that session sits in `ESCALATED` — otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ESCALATED_TICKET_BLOCKS_CLOSE` — and on success the session moves `OPEN → CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock and its `version` advanced. Closing matters operationally because verification is only available on a closed session: the rounding has to be finished before its findings can be signed off.

The ticket lifecycle spans `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `REMEDIATED`, `ESCALATED` and the terminal `VERIFIED`, and is driven by an operation taking an `action` and an optional `note`. Five actions are declared. `START_WORK` moves a ticket `OPEN → IN_PROGRESS` with no precondition and advances its `version`; it is available to both a `SafetyOfficer` and a `UnitManager`. `MARK_REMEDIATED` likewise carries no precondition and is available to both roles, and it has two declared entry points treated identically: `IN_PROGRESS → REMEDIATED` and `ESCALATED → REMEDIATED`. An escalated ticket can therefore be worked and remediated without first being de-escalated.

`VERIFY` moves a ticket `REMEDIATED → VERIFIED` and is reserved to the `SafetyOfficer` — a `UnitManager` attempting it is refused, since no unit may verify its own remediation. The ticket's rounding session must be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CLOSED`. Verification stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the acting principal and releases the ticket's `penalty_points` from the unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points`. That release can carry the unit `RESTRICTED → OPEN` once the remaining total is strictly below fifteen, which itself recomputes `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`. Verification also has a standard-level consequence: when no other ticket referencing the same standard remains in `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `REMEDIATED` or `ESCALATED`, the standard finally leaves `DEFICIENT`. Where it lands depends on its evidence. If the standard's `validated_evidence_count` is at least the chapter's `evidence_min_count`, the move is `DEFICIENT → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`: the chapter's `deficient_standard_count` decreases by one and its `evidence_complete_standard_count` increases by one. If `validated_evidence_count` is strictly below `evidence_min_count`, the move is `DEFICIENT → MAPPED`: only the chapter's `deficient_standard_count` decreases by one. Either way, the chapter's `readiness_flag` reverts to `ON_TRACK` if and only if `deficient_standard_count` is now strictly below two **and** `ppr_escalation_count` is strictly below two.

`FAIL_VERIFICATION` is the revision path. It moves a ticket `REMEDIATED → OPEN`, is reserved to the `SafetyOfficer`, and likewise requires the rounding session to be `CLOSED` or fails with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CLOSED`. It increments `failed_verification_count` by one and deliberately leaves the unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points` untouched, because the ticket never left the non-verified population that the ledger counts. The revision cycle `REMEDIATED → OPEN → IN_PROGRESS → REMEDIATED` may repeat without limit.

`ESCALATE` is reserved to the `SafetyOfficer` and has two declared entry points with no preconditions, `OPEN → ESCALATED` and `IN_PROGRESS → ESCALATED`, whose consequences are identical. When the ticket's rounding session has `round_type` `LIFE_SAFETY`, escalation raises the ticket's `severity` to `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` and recomputes its `penalty_points` to the fifteen points that severity carries; an `ENVIRONMENT_OF_CARE` escalation leaves severity and points as they were. The unit's `open_deficiency_penalty_points` is then recomputed against the ticket's current points. That recomputation can carry the unit `OPEN → RESTRICTED` once the total reaches fifteen, which aborts every `SCHEDULED` tracer of the unit with `abort_reason` `UNIT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, zeroes `scheduled_tracer_count`, and recomputes `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`. An escalated `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` ticket also has two hospital-wide effects described elsewhere: it blocks all new tracer scheduling and blocks opening a survey, both with `ERR_IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ACTIVE`. And any escalated ticket, regardless of severity, blocks its own session from closing.

Throughout all five actions, a `UnitManager` is confined to its own unit: if `X-User-ID` does not equal the `id` of the `HospitalUnit` reached through the ticket's rounding session, the request is refused with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 3.6. Survey Window Prediction and Command Center Activation

A survey window is the hospital's forecast of when the accrediting body will arrive. An `AccreditationDirector` creates one per accreditation cycle with a `cycle_label` such as CYCLE-2026, which must be unique across all windows or the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, and a `prior_survey_end_date` giving the last day of the previous full survey. Everything else is predicted.

The prediction uses month arithmetic that lands on the same day-of-month in the target month, clamping to the last day of that month when the day does not exist there and preserving the time of day — so an anchor of 31 August 2025 shifted eighteen months forward lands on 28 February 2027. `predicted_open_date` is the anchor shifted eighteen months forward, the earliest an unannounced survey may arrive. `predicted_close_date` is the anchor shifted thirty-six months forward, the latest it may arrive. `command_center_activation_allowed_from` is thirty days before `predicted_open_date`, counted as exact twenty-four-hour multiples — the lead time within which the hospital is permitted to stand up its survey command center.

The hospital can carry only one live window at a time. Occupation is measured on the **closed** interval running from `predicted_open_date` through `predicted_close_date` inclusive, with capacity one hospital-wide, and touching endpoints count as an intersection: a proposed window is rejected when its `predicted_open_date` is at or before some existing window's `predicted_close_date` **and** its `predicted_close_date` is at or after that window's `predicted_open_date`. Only windows whose `window_state` is not `CLOSED` — that is, those in `PROJECTED`, `ELIGIBLE` or `ACTIVE_SURVEY` — participate in the check; a closed cycle releases the calendar. A collision is rejected with `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

A survey window is unusual in this system because it carries **two independent state dimensions** that evolve without reference to each other. The survey dimension, `window_state`, takes `PROJECTED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `ACTIVE_SURVEY` and `CLOSED`, and starts at `PROJECTED`. The command-center dimension, `command_center_state`, takes `STANDBY`, `ACTIVATED` and `DEMOBILIZED`, and starts at `STANDBY`. A new window also has `survey_opened_at` and `survey_closed_at` null, `condition_level_finding_count` at zero, `follow_up_required` false and `follow_up_due_at` null.

The survey dimension's first advance needs no human at all: as described in §2.4, a window in `PROJECTED` moves `PROJECTED → ELIGIBLE` on a clock advance whose new value is at or after `predicted_open_date` and at or before `predicted_close_date`. That transition leaves `command_center_state` completely untouched.

The command-center dimension is driven by the `AccreditationDirector` through the window's action-bearing operation, which accepts an `action` and an optional `note`. `ACTIVATE_COMMAND_CENTER` has two declared entry points, `STANDBY → ACTIVATED` and `DEMOBILIZED → ACTIVATED`, both requiring the logical clock to be at or after `command_center_activation_allowed_from` and both refused with `ERR_COMMAND_CENTER_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` when it is earlier. Activation deliberately does **not** touch `window_state`, which may perfectly well still be `PROJECTED` — the whole point of the thirty-day lead is that the command center stands up before the window opens. The first activation advances the window's `version`. `DEMOBILIZE_COMMAND_CENTER` moves `ACTIVATED → DEMOBILIZED` and requires only that `window_state` is not `ACTIVE_SURVEY`; a command center may not be torn down while surveyors are in the building. Because the reverse edge exists, this dimension is cyclic, which is exactly what a follow-up survey needs.

`OPEN_SURVEY` is the moment the hospital locks down. It has two declared entry points: `ELIGIBLE → ACTIVE_SURVEY` for the initial survey and `CLOSED → ACTIVE_SURVEY` for a follow-up survey, making the survey dimension cyclic too. From `PROJECTED` or from `ACTIVE_SURVEY` the action is refused with `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Both entry points require `command_center_state` to be `ACTIVATED`, else `ERR_COMMAND_CENTER_NOT_ACTIVATED`, and both require that no deficiency ticket anywhere in the hospital sits in `ESCALATED` with `severity` `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`, else `ERR_IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ACTIVE`. The follow-up entry point adds two temporal conditions checked after the eligibility rung: `follow_up_required` must be true, else `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_REQUIRED`, and the logical clock must be at or before `follow_up_due_at`, else `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. On success — identically for both entry points — `survey_opened_at` is stamped with the current logical clock and every hospital unit is driven to `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY`, each such move aborting that unit's `SCHEDULED` tracers with `abort_reason` `SURVEY_ACTIVE`, zeroing its `scheduled_tracer_count` and recomputing its `readiness_score` and `readiness_band`.

`CLOSE_SURVEY` moves the window `ACTIVE_SURVEY → CLOSED` with no precondition, and it is the single busiest operation in the system. It stamps `survey_closed_at` with the current logical clock. Every finding of this window still in `DRAFT` moves to `RESPONSE_PENDING`, and each gets both `initial_response_due_at` and `response_due_at` set to `survey_closed_at` plus that finding's own `response_deadline_days` days, counted as exact twenty-four-hour multiples. `follow_up_required` is then set according to whether the window's `condition_level_finding_count` is at least one. When it is, `follow_up_due_at` is set to `survey_closed_at` plus sixty days and `command_center_state` deliberately stays `ACTIVATED`, because the command center must survive to run the follow-up; when it is not, `command_center_state` moves `ACTIVATED → DEMOBILIZED` in the same request. Every hospital unit then leaves `LOCKED_FOR_SURVEY` for `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` according to its own penalty ledger, recomputing readiness as it goes. And every finding of this window sitting in `FOLLOW_UP_PENDING` — that is, a condition-level finding whose corrective-action plan was accepted on paper during the previous survey — finally moves to `ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` set to `survey_closed_at`. That last clause is what makes the follow-up survey the only thing that can actually close a condition-level finding.

### 3.7. Survey Findings, Risk Classification and Condition-Level Escalation

During an active survey, only the `ExternalSurveyor` writes findings, and the window's `window_state` must be `ACTIVE_SURVEY` or the write is refused with `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_ACTIVE`. A finding names the `standard_id` found non-compliant and the `unit_id` where the non-compliance was observed, and quantifies the observation with `observed_noncompliance_count` non-compliant elements out of `sampled_count` elements sampled, plus an optional `patient_harm_observed` flag that defaults to false. The window is taken from the path and stored as `survey_window_id`. The counts must be coherent — `sampled_count` at least one, and `observed_noncompliance_count` at least one and no greater than `sampled_count` — or the write is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_COUNT_INVALID`.

Classification then runs, and it is deliberately non-linear across four interacting inputs. First, `noncompliance_rate` is the observed count divided by the sampled count, rounded half up to four decimal places; every subsequent comparison uses that rounded value, not the raw quotient. Second, the rate produces a `classification`: it is `LIMITED` when the rate is strictly below 0.3400, `PATTERN` when the rate is at or above 0.3400 but strictly below 0.8000, and `WIDESPREAD` when the rate is at or above 0.8000. Third, two indices are formed and added. The classification index is zero for `LIMITED`, one for `PATTERN` and two for `WIDESPREAD`. The tier index comes from the referenced standard's own `risk_tier`: zero for `SITUATIONAL_DECISION_RULE`, one for `INDIRECT_IMPACT` and two for `DIRECT_IMPACT`. To their sum, two further points are added when `patient_harm_observed` is true and none when it is false, and the total is then capped at six.

That capped index yields the `risk_level`: `LOW` when the index is one or less, `MODERATE` when it is exactly two, `HIGH` when it is three or four, and `IMMEDIATE_THREAT` when it is five or more. Two further outputs follow. `condition_level` is true when either the `risk_level` came out as `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`, or the `classification` came out as `WIDESPREAD` while the referenced standard's `risk_tier` is `DIRECT_IMPACT` — the second branch is what catches a hospital-wide failure of a high-impact standard even when no harm was observed. And `response_deadline_days` is forty-five when the `risk_level` is `HIGH` or `IMMEDIATE_THREAT`, and sixty otherwise. No other finding on the window participates in any of this; the computation reads only the creating payload and the referenced standard's `risk_tier`.

A new finding begins at `state` `DRAFT` with `initial_response_due_at` and `response_due_at` both null, `clarification_round_count` and `submission_count` at zero and `accepted_at` null. When `condition_level` came out true, the window's `condition_level_finding_count` increases by one — which is exactly the counter that decides, at survey close, whether a follow-up survey will be required.

Escalation is the `ExternalSurveyor`'s declaration that a finding is too serious to be closed on paperwork. It is permitted only from `RESPONSE_PENDING` — from any other finding state it is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE` — and it requires the finding's `condition_level` to be true, refusing otherwise with `ERR_NOT_CONDITION_LEVEL`. The surveyor supplies a `rationale`. On success the finding moves `RESPONSE_PENDING → CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED`, the referenced standard is driven to `DEFICIENT` with `last_noncompliant_observed_at` stamped from the current logical clock — carrying the same chapter-counter and `AT_RISK` consequences described in §3.4, including the return of that chapter's submitted self-assessments for reassessment — and the owning window has `follow_up_required` set to true with `follow_up_due_at` set to `survey_closed_at` plus sixty days.

### 3.8. Evidence of Standards Compliance: Submission, Clarification and Acceptance

Once a survey closes, each finding that left `DRAFT` is waiting for the hospital's corrective-action plan, its **evidence of standards compliance**. Only a `StandardOwner` may author one, and the payload carries a `corrective_action_summary` narrating the corrective action, a `measure_of_success` stating how success will be demonstrated, a `leadership_signoff` boolean, and the stale-view guard `expected_finding_version` from §2.7. The finding is taken from the path and stored as `survey_finding_id`.

Submission is guarded from five directions, and the order matters. The finding's `state` must be one of `RESPONSE_PENDING`, `CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED` or `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, else `ERR_FINDING_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE`. A finding whose `condition_level` is true must already have reached `CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED`; a submission against such a finding while it still sits in `RESPONSE_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`, which is what forces the surveyor's escalation to happen first. The logical clock must be at or before the finding's `response_due_at`, equality still counting as inside the window, else `ERR_ESC_WINDOW_CLOSED`. `leadership_signoff` must be true, else `ERR_LEADERSHIP_SIGNOFF_REQUIRED` — an unsigned plan is not a plan. And the submitting principal must hold an owner assignment on the finding's standard with `status` `ACTIVE` and `designation` `PRIMARY`, else `ERR_SUBMITTER_NOT_STANDARD_OWNER`.

On success the finding's `submission_count` increases by one and a submission record is created with `submission_round` equal to that new count — one for the first submission, two for the resubmission after a first clarification, and so on — with `submitted_by_user_id` stamped from the acting owner, `decision` at `PENDING` and `decision_note` null. The finding moves to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, and its `version` advances. That version bump is the mechanism behind the stale-view guard: whichever declared entry point the submission came from — `RESPONSE_PENDING → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, `CONDITION_LEVEL_ESCALATED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` — the effects are identical, and all three require the deadline check and the primary-ownership check.

The accrediting body then rules on the newest round, and only the `ExternalSurveyor` may do so, only while the finding is in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_FINDING_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE`. The ruling supplies a `decision` of `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `decision_note`, and both are written onto the newest submission record. Each decision behaves differently.

A `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED` decision requires that the finding's `clarification_round_count` is still strictly below two, the ceiling on clarification rounds; a third clarification is refused with `ERR_CLARIFICATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. On success the count increases by one, the newest submission's `decision` becomes `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, the finding moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, and — critically — the effective deadline is recomputed. The new `response_due_at` is the earlier of two candidates: the current logical clock plus twenty-one days, and `initial_response_due_at` plus ten days. The cap is measured from `initial_response_due_at`, never from the previous `response_due_at`, so repeated clarifications cannot walk the deadline forward indefinitely; `initial_response_due_at` itself is frozen at survey close and is never rewritten. For a given finding the two deadline derivations therefore produce different instants, and only the initial one is anchored on `survey_closed_at`.

An `ACCEPTED` decision splits on the finding's `condition_level`. When `condition_level` is false, the finding moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, `accepted_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and the newest submission's `decision` becomes `ACCEPTED`; `ACCEPTED` is terminal and the finding is closed. When `condition_level` is true, the newest submission's `decision` likewise becomes `ACCEPTED`, but the finding moves `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → FOLLOW_UP_PENDING` instead, the window's `follow_up_required` stays true, and `accepted_at` remains null. A condition-level finding cannot be closed on paper — it waits in `FOLLOW_UP_PENDING` until the follow-up survey closes, at which point §3.6's close cascade moves it `FOLLOW_UP_PENDING → ACCEPTED` and sets its `accepted_at` to that window's `survey_closed_at`.

A `REJECTED` decision moves the finding `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_PENDING`, records `REJECTED` as the newest submission's `decision`, and leaves `response_due_at` exactly as it was. A rejection therefore does not buy the hospital extra time the way a clarification does — the owner must resubmit inside the original deadline. Because the clarification edge runs in both directions, the cycle `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` is legal but bounded at two clarification rounds, while the rejection cycle through `RESPONSE_PENDING` is bounded only by the deadline.

### 3.9. The Periodic Performance Review Self-Assessment

Between surveys, the hospital grades itself. An `AccreditationDirector` files a periodic performance review self-assessment against a chapter, naming a `period_label` such as 2026-H2 — unique within that chapter, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — and supplying `self_ratings`, an array in which each entry pairs a `standard_id` with a `rating` of `COMPLIANT`, `PARTIAL` or `NON_COMPLIANT`. The chapter is taken from the path and stored as `chapter_id`. A repeated `standard_id` inside `self_ratings` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a `standard_id` that does not belong to the path chapter is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_CHAPTER_MISMATCH`.

The whole value of this exercise is that the same chapter is scored twice from two different sources, and the two scores share one denominator: the chapter's `standard_count`, meaning **every** standard in the chapter, not merely the ones that were rated. A chapter standard with no rating supplied contributes nothing to the numerator but still counts in the denominator, so silence is scored as zero.

The **self-reported** variant reads only what the units claimed. Each supplied rating earns points — one hundred for `COMPLIANT`, fifty for `PARTIAL`, zero for `NON_COMPLIANT` — and `self_score` is the sum of those points divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. Evidence and tracer history are ignored entirely.

The **evidence-verified** variant ignores the ratings and reads the system's own record instead. Each standard of the chapter earns points from its `compliance_state` — one hundred for `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, fifty for `MAPPED`, and zero for both `UNMAPPED` and `DEFICIENT` — and `verified_score` is the sum of those points across every standard of the chapter, divided by the same denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because both terms are sums, the order in which standards are visited is irrelevant. For identical data the two variants disagree precisely whenever a rated standard lacks validated evidence or has gone `DEFICIENT`.

The `gap` is `self_score` minus `verified_score`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `flagged_overstatement` is true when that gap is at least 10.00. A flagged assessment increments the owning chapter's `ppr_escalation_count` by one, and once that counter reaches two the chapter's `readiness_flag` becomes `AT_RISK` — the second of the two independent routes into `AT_RISK` described in §3.1. A new assessment begins at `state` `SUBMITTED` with `requires_reassessment` false and `attested_by_user_id` null.

The assessment lifecycle spans `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT` and the terminal `ATTESTED`, and it is cyclic on the first two. Nothing a client does moves an assessment into `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT`: it happens when the owning chapter's `readiness_flag` flips `ON_TRACK → AT_RISK`, whether that flip came from deficient standards accumulating (§3.4, §3.7) or from overstatement escalations accumulating here. Every assessment of that chapter still in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT` with `requires_reassessment` set to true and its `version` advanced.

Revision is how an assessment comes back. Only an `AccreditationDirector` may revise, only from `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT`, and the payload replaces `self_ratings` wholesale — subject to the same duplicate-`standard_id` and chapter-membership rules as the original filing. Revision recomputes `self_score`, `verified_score` and `gap` under both variants, sets `requires_reassessment` back to false, and moves the assessment `RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT → SUBMITTED`. It also increments the chapter's `ppr_escalation_count` by one, but only when the recomputed `gap` is at least 10.00 **and** `flagged_overstatement` had previously been false — a chapter is not charged twice for the same overstatement. The cycle `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REASSESSMENT → SUBMITTED` may repeat without limit.

Attestation is the end of the line. An `AccreditationDirector` attests, optionally recording an `attestation_note`, and two conditions apply: the assessment must be in `SUBMITTED` — an assessment already in `ATTESTED` is refused with `ERR_PPR_ALREADY_ATTESTED` — and its `requires_reassessment` must be false, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PPR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`. On success the assessment moves `SUBMITTED → ATTESTED` and `attested_by_user_id` is stamped with the attesting principal's identifier.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new, distinct resource into existence — a chapter, a standard, an owner assignment, an evidence item, a unit, a mock tracer, an observation, a rounding session, a deficiency ticket, a survey window, a finding, an evidence-of-standards-compliance submission or a periodic performance review self-assessment — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every action-driven transition, every revocation, the closing of a rounding session, the review of a submission, the revision and attestation of a self-assessment, and the advancing of the logical clock, return `200 OK`. Every read returns `200 OK`. The request and response payloads are not repeated here; they are specified in the workflow narratives of §3, and the failure modes of each operation are specified in §2.11 and §3.
