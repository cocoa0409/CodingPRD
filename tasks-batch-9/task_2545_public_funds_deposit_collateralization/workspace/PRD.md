# Public Funds Collateral API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a school district, county, municipality or state agency deposits tax receipts with a commercial bank, federal deposit insurance covers only a small slice of the balance. Everything above that slice must be secured by securities pledged to the depositor and held by an independent tri-party custodian. The bank's public funds group therefore lives inside a permanent three-way reconciliation: the state banking department decides which banks may hold public deposits and how much they may pledge, the depositing public unit demands evidence that its uninsured balance is covered every single day, and the custodian must be able to produce a safekeeping receipt whose numbers match the bank's own pledge ledger to the cent. Today that reconciliation is done in spreadsheets, and the spreadsheets disagree.

This document specifies the Public Funds Collateral service, a single system of record for eligible-depository qualification, insured-amount determination, required collateral percentages, eligible collateral types and their haircuts, tri-party pledge agreement documentation, periodic mark-to-market with margin calls and substitutions, pooled versus dedicated pledging, public unit reporting, safekeeping receipts and audit confirmations, and deposit runoff planning around tax collection and disbursement cycles. Every number the service reports — an account's uninsured amount, the percentage of that amount that must be collateralised, the haircut value of a pledged bond, a pool participant's waterfall share, a depository's risk grade — is derived by the service from primitive inputs rather than typed in by an operator. The service never guesses: where two conventions exist, this document names the one the service uses.

Two design commitments shape everything below. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the identity that files a qualification package may not attest it, and the identity that requests a collateral substitution may not approve it. Second, **time is explicit**: the service runs on a logical clock that operators drive directly, because cure deadlines, price staleness and pledge effective intervals must be reproducible rather than dependent on wall-clock drift. The service exposes flat, top-level collections; there is no API prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Acting Identities and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including read-only ones, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint in the table in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `StateRegulator`, `BankCollateralOfficer`, `BankComplianceOfficer`, `Custodian`, `PublicUnitTreasurer` and `SystemOperator`.

- A `StateRegulator` is the state treasurer's office or banking department. It qualifies and approves depositories, administers pooled collateral programs, registers public units and custodians, confirms safekeeping receipts, declares collateral ineligible, and suspends depositories or terminates executed agreements.
- A `BankCollateralOfficer` is the bank's first line: it registers the depository, files qualification packages, drafts and circulates pledge agreements, registers collateral, posts prices, pledges and releases par, requests substitutions, executes mark-to-market runs and cures margin calls.
- A `BankComplianceOfficer` is the bank's second line: it attests qualification packages, signs on the depository's behalf, and independently approves or rejects substitution requests. It may never originate the work it reviews.
- A `Custodian` is the tri-party custodian bank holding the pledged collateral. It signs as the custodian party, acknowledges and settles substitutions, and issues safekeeping receipts.
- A `PublicUnitTreasurer` is the treasurer of the depositing government unit. It opens deposit accounts, credits and debits public funds, signs as the public-unit party, requests amendments, reads coverage reports and files runoff forecasts.
- A `SystemOperator` is the operational automation identity. It drives the logical clock and the daily margin-call sweep and feeds market prices from the pricing vendor. It holds no supervisory and no banking authority.

Two of these roles are **entity-backed**, meaning their `X-User-ID` is the identifier of a persisted record rather than a free-form operator string. When `X-User-Role` is `Custodian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Custodian` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Custodian` compare the resource's custodian foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a custodian may acknowledge, settle or issue receipts only against pledge agreements whose `custodian_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PublicUnitTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PublicUnit` record; ownership checks compare the `public_unit_id` on the target deposit account (or, for a runoff forecast, on the account the forecast projects, or, for a signature or amendment request, on the pledge agreement's beneficiary deposit account) to `X-User-ID`. A violation of either ownership rule is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `StateRegulator`, `BankCollateralOfficer`, `BankComplianceOfficer` or `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `attested_by_user_id` on a qualification review, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `compliance_approved_by_user_id` on a substitution request, and as `confirmed_by_user_id` on a safekeeping receipt — and it compares two such recorded identifiers to each other when enforcing segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the zero-based starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and place the page of resources in `data`. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned records is strictly less than `total_count`). Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every collection is sorted by `created_at` ascending, and filters supplied as query parameters are combined conjunctively — a record must match all supplied filters to appear.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the service's logical clock at the moment of creation. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation using the same format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. `MarkToMarketRun` records are the sole exception: a mark-to-market run is an immutable historical record, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and nothing in the service ever updates a run after it is written.

The service's notion of "now" is a **logical clock**. On a freshly started service the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `SystemOperator` may set it to any ISO 8601 UTC instant, which mutates no domain record but changes what every subsequent request observes as the current time. Every `created_at`, every `updated_at`, and every domain timestamp whose name ends in `_at` — `approved_at`, `decided_at`, `depository_signed_at`, `public_unit_signed_at`, `custodian_signed_at`, `executed_at`, `terminated_at`, `released_at`, `priced_at`, `compliance_approved_at`, `custodian_acked_at`, `settled_at`, `escalated_at`, `cured_at`, `confirmed_at`, `superseded_at` — as well as every `as_of` stamp, is read from the logical clock rather than from wall-clock time. Cure deadlines, price staleness, pledge effective intervals and mark-to-market windows are therefore all deterministically reproducible.

### 2.5. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integers denominated in whole cents; field names ending in `_cents` carry no fractional part at any point. All rates, ratios, percentages and haircuts are integers denominated in basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly 100 percent; field names ending in `_bps` follow this convention. A capital ratio of `1200` basis points is 12.00 percent, a required collateral percentage of `10500` basis points is 105.00 percent, and a haircut of `200` basis points is 2.00 percent.

Two rounding conventions are used, and each is pinned to a specific computation.

- **Round half up to whole cents** applies wherever a cent amount is derived by scaling another amount by a basis-point figure. When the exact product ends in a half cent, the result rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Every collateral requirement, every market value, every haircut value and every projected window requirement is rounded this way.
- **Floor to whole basis points** applies to the two derived ratios that measure adequacy: an account's `coverage_ratio_bps` and a run's `deficiency_ratio_bps`. Both truncate toward zero rather than rounding.

Where a total is assembled from parts, the order of rounding and summation is fixed and is stated with the individual calculation; the service never re-derives a total from unrounded intermediates when this document has pinned a per-part rounding order.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Cross-Aggregate Writes

Three write operations create a child record whose correctness depends on a parent record the client read earlier. Because creating a second such child is perfectly legitimate product behaviour, staleness cannot be detected from the parent's state; it is detected by explicit version pinning. Each of these three requests must carry a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the parent as the client last observed it, and the service compares that value to the parent's current stored `version` before doing anything else that could mutate state.

- `POST /pledge-allocations` requires `expected_pledge_agreement_version`. If it does not equal the referenced `PledgeAgreement`'s current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An agreement legitimately carries many allocations, so this check exists purely to stop a writer from pledging against a stale view of the agreement's allocation set, custodian or beneficiary.
- `POST /depositories/{id}/mtm-runs` requires `expected_depository_version`. If it does not equal that `Depository`'s current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPOSITORY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Periodic runs over non-overlapping windows are legitimately repeatable, so without this check a stale request would value collateral against a superseded view of the depository's capital tier, deficiency counter or approval status.
- `POST /runoff-forecasts` requires `expected_deposit_account_version`. If it does not equal the referenced `DepositAccount`'s current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPOSIT_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because filing a forecast supersedes the account's prior forecast for the same cycle, a forecast built on a stale view of the account's balance and requirement must be refused on the version check rather than silently replacing a newer one.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside this list must not be required to carry one, and supplying one to them has no effect.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is determined by the **first** rule that fails in the ordering below, not by the most severe violation. The ladder is global: every endpoint runs the rungs that apply to it, in this sequence.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Entity-backed ownership** — an entity-backed caller owns the target, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This rung applies to exactly ten operations: opening a deposit account, crediting a deposit, debiting a withdrawal, filing a runoff forecast, recording a signature, requesting an agreement amendment, acknowledging a substitution as custodian, settling a substitution as custodian, issuing a safekeeping receipt, and reading an account's coverage report.
4. **Existence** — every identifier in the URL path is resolved left to right, then every foreign key in the request body is resolved; the first unresolved identifier yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Body foreign keys are resolved in this declaration order: `depository_id`, `public_unit_id`, `custodian_id`, `pooled_program_id`, `deposit_account_id`, `pledge_agreement_id`, `collateral_asset_id`, `outgoing_allocation_id`, `incoming_asset_id`. Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Request shape** — all required input fields present, every enum value inside its declared set, every integer amount strictly positive except `reported_par_cents` and `reported_asset_count` which may be zero, every timestamp a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant, and `projected_windows` non-empty; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Version pinning** — the three checks of §2.6, each returning its own error identifier.
7. **State admissibility** — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is defined, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Applies to every action endpoint. A resource in a terminal state rejects every trigger: a `DENIED` depository, a `SETTLED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` substitution request, a `TERMINATED` agreement, a `CURED` or `BREACHED` margin call, and a `SUPERSEDED` forecast are all closed to further action.
8. **Depository approval** — the depository's `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). This rung guards six operations: opening a deposit account, crediting a deposit, debiting a withdrawal, circulating a pledge agreement, registering a collateral asset, and creating a pledge allocation.
9. **Depository posture** — the depository's `collateral_posture` is not `FROZEN`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). This rung guards exactly two operations: opening a new deposit account and creating a new substitution request.
10. **Agreement execution** — the pledge agreement's `status` is `EXECUTED`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). This rung guards exactly three operations: creating a pledge allocation, creating a substitution request, and issuing a safekeeping receipt.
11. **Segregation of duties** — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the identity that performed the incompatible earlier step, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). This rung guards exactly three operations: attesting a qualification review, compliance-approving a substitution request, and rejecting a substitution request.
12. **Custodian eligibility** — the agreement's custodian has `approval_status` `APPROVED` and `tri_party_capable` true, else `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). This rung guards exactly three operations: circulating a pledge agreement, settling a substitution, and issuing a safekeeping receipt.
13. **Collateral type eligibility** — the asset's `asset_type` is a type the state accepts for public funds and its `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, else `ERR_COLLATERAL_TYPE_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). This rung guards exactly four operations: creating a pledge allocation, creating a substitution request, settling a substitution, and curing a margin call.
14. **Risk-grade gate** — the depository's `risk_grade` is not `D`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_RISK_GRADE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). This rung guards exactly one operation: opening a new deposit account.
15. **Custodian alignment** — the asset's `custodian_id` equals the agreement's `custodian_id`, else `ERR_CUSTODIAN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This rung guards exactly three operations: creating a pledge allocation, creating a substitution request, and acknowledging a substitution as custodian.
16. **Depository alignment** — the asset's `depository_id` equals the agreement's `depository_id`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This rung guards creating a pledge allocation and creating a substitution request.
17. **Beneficiary coherence** — `ERR_BENEFICIARY_TARGET_INVALID` (HTTP 422) on drafting a pledge agreement whose beneficiary discriminator and beneficiary reference disagree.
18. **Pledging-mode coherence** — `ERR_PLEDGING_MODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) on opening a deposit account whose mode contradicts its public unit's declared preference.
19. **Outgoing-allocation coherence** — `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_UNDER_AGREEMENT` (HTTP 422) on creating a substitution request whose outgoing allocation does not belong to the named agreement or is not `ACTIVE`.
20. **Price freshness** — `ERR_STALE_COLLATERAL_PRICE` (HTTP 422) on creating a pledge allocation or a substitution request against an asset whose last price observation is too old.
21. **Allocation interval sanity** — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422) on creating a pledge allocation whose interval is empty or begins before the agreement was executed.
22. **Mark-to-market window sanity** — `ERR_MTM_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) on a run whose window collides with, or crowds, a prior run of the same depository.
23. **Forecast window continuity** — `ERR_FORECAST_WINDOW_DISCONTINUITY` (HTTP 422) on a runoff forecast whose windows are not a contiguous chain covering the declared horizon.
24. **Cure window** — `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) on curing a margin call whose cure window has closed.
25. **Par capacity** — `ERR_ALLOCATION_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (HTTP 409) when a pledge would commit more of an asset's par than the asset has, at some instant of the requested interval. This rung guards creating a pledge allocation and settling a substitution.
26. **Custodian concentration** — `ERR_CUSTODIAN_CONCENTRATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) when a new allocation would push one custodian's share of a depository's pledged collateral value above that custodian's limit. This rung guards creating a pledge allocation.
27. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (HTTP 409) on registering a collateral asset, drafting a pledge agreement or issuing a safekeeping receipt whose business identifier is already taken in its scope.
28. **Round ceilings** — `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409) on re-attesting a returned qualification package or requesting an amendment beyond the permitted number of rounds.
29. **Signature discipline** — `ERR_PARTY_ALREADY_SIGNED` (HTTP 409) when the requested party's stamp is already set on the agreement.
30. **Signature entitlement** — `ERR_SIGNATURE_PARTY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) when the acting role is not entitled to stamp the requested party.
31. **Cure-credit eligibility** — `ERR_CURE_ALLOCATION_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422) when any allocation offered against a margin call fails the cure-credit test.
32. **Substitution value adequacy** — `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_VALUE_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 422) when the incoming collateral is worth less than the outgoing collateral it replaces, on compliance approval of a substitution.
33. **Fiscal ceilings and floors** — the statutory deposit limit (`ERR_STATUTORY_DEPOSIT_LIMIT`, HTTP 422) on a deposit; the ledger balance floor (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LEDGER_BALANCE`, HTTP 422) on a withdrawal; the approved pledge capacity ceiling (`ERR_PLEDGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422) on a pledge allocation; the statutory capacity ceiling (`ERR_CAPACITY_ABOVE_STATUTORY_CEILING`, HTTP 422) on filing a qualification package and on deciding one; and the cure overage ceiling (`ERR_CURE_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, HTTP 422) on curing a margin call.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. A frozen depository's rejection surfaces as `ERR_DEPOSITORY_POSTURE_FROZEN` rather than being masked by a later referential check, and a stale parent version surfaces as its own 412 before any state, posture or business check runs, so a client holding an outdated read learns that fact first.

### 2.8. Nested Paths and Reverse Reachability

Where an endpoint path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that does not resolve determines the 404. A qualification review requested under a depository that does not exist fails on the depository, not on the review. Furthermore, a child addressed under a parent must actually belong to that parent: a review that exists but whose `depository_id` is a different depository is treated as not found under the requested parent.

Because the entity graph is entered from several directions, every one-to-many relationship is navigable in reverse through a list endpoint with the corresponding filter. Member depositories of a pooled program are listable from the program; a depository's qualification reviews and mark-to-market runs are listable from the depository; an agreement's safekeeping receipts are listable from the agreement; an account's runoff forecasts are listable from the account; and the flat collections for depositories, deposit accounts, pledge agreements, collateral assets, pledge allocations, substitution requests and margin calls each accept the foreign keys of their parents as filters.

### 2.9. Recomputation Chains and Cascade Discipline

Every derived field in this service is a **function of primitives**, recomputed synchronously inside the request that disturbs one of its inputs. A response never reports a stale derived value: by the time the service replies, every total, ratio, status and posture reachable from the mutation has been brought up to date, and every entity whose stored fields actually changed has had its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Entities merely read during a recomputation are not versioned.

Cascades in this domain run several hops deep, and each hop is caused by the preceding hop's mutation rather than by the originating call. Two cascades reach four levels: suspending a depository restricts its accounts, which escalates the owning public units, which staleifies those units' open forecasts; and settling a substitution rewrites the agreement's totals, which redistributes pooled collateral, which flips participant accounts' coverage status, which moves those accounts' public units to watch. Both are specified in full in §3, alongside the workflows that trigger them. The propagation order within a request is always requirement first, then assigned value, then coverage, then the owning public unit's exposure — a coverage status is never computed from a requirement that the same request is about to change.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the Public-Funds Perimeter and Qualifying a Depository

Nothing in this system can happen until the state has populated its register, and the register is the `StateRegulator`'s exclusive province. Registering a **custodian** records the institution's `name` and its `custodian_type`, which is one of `FEDERAL_RESERVE`, `FHLB` or `TRUST_BANK`. Two further inputs are optional at registration. `tri_party_capable` states whether the institution can hold collateral under a tri-party pledge arrangement and defaults to `false` when omitted, which means a newly registered custodian is presumed *not* tri-party capable until someone says otherwise. `concentration_limit_bps` caps the share of any one depository's total pledged collateral value that this custodian may hold; it defaults to 6000 basis points — sixty percent — and a supplied value must be at least 1000 and at most 10000 basis points. A freshly registered custodian's `approval_status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL` and its `approved_at` is null. State approval moves `approval_status` to `APPROVED` and stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock. The `approval_status` enumeration also admits `REVOKED`, which denotes a custodian the state has withdrawn from public-funds safekeeping; a custodian in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `REVOKED` is not eligible to serve on a tri-party agreement.

A **pooled collateral program** is a state-administered pool in which many depositories pledge into a common pot backing many public units. Creating one records its `name` and the two-letter `administrator_state` code of the state running the pool, plus an optional `minimum_pool_pct_bps` — the statutory floor on pool collateral relative to the pooled requirement — which defaults to 11000 basis points, or 110 percent, and which must be supplied within the inclusive range 10000 to 20000 basis points. At creation the four derived pool figures `aggregate_required_collateral_cents`, `aggregate_collateral_value_cents`, `unallocated_collateral_cents` and `participant_account_count` are all zero and `pool_posture` is `ADEQUATE`.

**Public units** are the depositors. Registering one records its `name`, its `unit_type` — one of `SCHOOL_DISTRICT`, `COUNTY`, `MUNICIPALITY` or `STATE_AGENCY` — the two-letter `jurisdiction_state` code of the public-funds statute that governs it, and its `statutory_max_deposit_cents`, a strictly positive ceiling on the unit's aggregate ledger balance across every account it holds anywhere. Two optional inputs shape later behaviour: `collateral_program_preference`, which is `DEDICATED` or `POOLED` and defaults to `DEDICATED`, and `reporting_frequency`, which is `DAILY` or `MONTHLY` and defaults to `MONTHLY`, expressing the cadence at which the unit expects coverage reports. A new unit begins with `total_deposit_balance_cents` and `total_uninsured_cents` at zero and `exposure_status` `NORMAL`. Thereafter the unit's exposure is a strictly ordered read of its accounts: it is `ESCALATED` whenever any of its accounts has `funding_status` `RESTRICTED`; otherwise it is `WATCH` whenever any of its accounts has `collateral_status` `DEFICIENT`; otherwise it is `NORMAL`. Restriction dominates deficiency, and both figures — the unit's total balance and its total uninsured amount — are sums taken over the unit's accounts whose `funding_status` is `PENDING_FUNDING`, `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED`.

A bank enters the system when a `BankCollateralOfficer` registers it as a candidate **depository**. Registration supplies the bank's legal `name`, its `charter_type` (`STATE_CHARTERED` or `NATIONAL`) and the two-letter `home_state` code of its charter, and may optionally supply `capital_ratio_bps` (its total risk-based capital ratio in basis points, defaulting to 1200 and constrained to the inclusive range 0 to 5000), `insurance_determination_basis` (`AGGREGATED_BY_UNIT` or `PER_ACCOUNT`, defaulting to `AGGREGATED_BY_UNIT`) and `pooled_program_id`, the pooled program the bank belongs to. A null `pooled_program_id` means the bank pledges only on a dedicated basis. From the capital ratio the service derives `capital_tier` immediately, with inclusive lower bounds: a ratio of at least 1000 basis points is `WELL_CAPITALIZED`, a ratio below that but at least 800 basis points is `ADEQUATELY_CAPITALIZED`, and anything below 800 basis points is `UNDERCAPITALIZED`. A new depository starts at `approval_status` `DRAFT`, `collateral_posture` `COMPLIANT`, `risk_grade` `A` with `risk_score_bps` zero, `consecutive_deficiency_runs` zero, `open_margin_call_count` zero, `approved_pledge_capacity_cents` zero, a null `approved_at`, `mtm_run_index` zero, a null `last_mtm_window_end`, and `total_required_collateral_cents`, `total_pledged_collateral_value_cents` and `total_pledged_par_cents` all zero.

Qualification is a documented, cyclical review conversation between the bank's two lines of defence and the state. A `BankCollateralOfficer` files a **qualification review** under a depository, supplying only `requested_pledge_capacity_cents`, the pledge capacity the bank asks the state to authorise; it must be strictly positive, and it must not exceed the statutory ceiling of 500,000,000,000 cents (five billion dollars), a ceiling enforced both when the package is filed and again when the regulator decides it, with `ERR_CAPACITY_ABOVE_STATUTORY_CEILING` on either. The filed review is `SUBMITTED`, its `depository_id` is taken from the path rather than the body, its `submitted_by_user_id` records the filing officer's `X-User-ID`, and its `round_count` is 1.

Attestation is the second line's act. A `BankComplianceOfficer` attests a package in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` status, supplying an `attestation_note`, which drives the review to `ATTESTED` and records the attester's `X-User-ID` as `attested_by_user_id`. The attester's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and a package belonging to a depository whose `approval_status` is already `DENIED` cannot be attested at all. Re-attesting a `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` package is the move that closes the review cycle `ATTESTED → UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO → ATTESTED`, and it additionally increments `round_count` by 1. Rounds are capped: the incremented `round_count` may be at most 3, so a package that has already been through three rounds is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` on the attempt that would produce a fourth.

The regulator's side of the conversation has three moves. Taking an `ATTESTED` package under review drives the review to `UNDER_REVIEW` and simultaneously carries the depository from `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, incrementing the depository's `version`; the depository must be in `DRAFT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` for this to be admissible. Returning the package for more information requires an `info_request_note`, drives the review to `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, and clears `attested_by_user_id` back to null so that the second line must attest afresh — the depository's `approval_status` deliberately stays at `UNDER_REVIEW` throughout. Deciding the package takes a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `decision_note`. An approval requires the review to be `UNDER_REVIEW` with a non-null `attested_by_user_id`; it sets the review to `APPROVED` with `decided_at` stamped from the clock, carries the depository from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED` with `approved_at` stamped, copies `requested_pledge_capacity_cents` into the depository's `approved_pledge_capacity_cents`, and increments the depository's `version`. A denial sets the review to `DENIED` with `decided_at` stamped, carries the depository to `DENIED`, resets `approved_pledge_capacity_cents` to zero, and increments the depository's `version`. `DENIED` is terminal for the depository: no further review may be opened or progressed against it, and attempts are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Suspension is the regulator's emergency brake, and it is the origin of the deepest cascade in the system. Suspending an `APPROVED` depository executes four hops in order. Hop one sets the depository's `approval_status` to `SUSPENDED` and increments its `version`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every deposit account of that depository whose `funding_status` is `PENDING_FUNDING` or `FUNDED` to `RESTRICTED`, records `restricted_reason` as `DEPOSITORY_SUSPENDED` on each, and increments each account's `version`; a restricted account's `required_collateral_cents` is *not* zeroed, because a restricted balance still has to be collateralised. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes `exposure_status` for every public unit that owns an account which just became restricted, and each such unit becomes `ESCALATED`. Hop four, caused by hop three, moves every runoff forecast in `OPEN` status whose `deposit_account_id` belongs to one of those escalated units to `STALE` and sets its `requires_reforecast` flag to true. Note what suspension does *not* touch: an account's `collateral_status` is an independent dimension and is left exactly as it was. `SUSPENDED` is likewise terminal for the depository.

### 3.2. Deposit Accounts: Opening, Funding, Insurance and the Collateral Requirement

A `PublicUnitTreasurer` opens a **deposit account** by naming the `depository_id` of the bank, the `public_unit_id` of its own unit — which must equal its `X-User-ID` — the `deposit_class`, which is `DEMAND` or `TIME_AND_SAVINGS`, and the `pledging_mode`, which is `DEDICATED` or `POOLED`. Three gates guard the opening beyond the ordinary ones: the depository's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED`, its `collateral_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, and its `risk_grade` must not be `D`. A fourth gate enforces coherence with the unit's declared intent: the requested `pledging_mode` must equal the owning public unit's `collateral_program_preference`, and a `POOLED` account may only be opened at a depository whose `pooled_program_id` is non-null; either violation is refused with `ERR_PLEDGING_MODE_MISMATCH`.

At creation the service assigns `group_sequence`, a monotone integer beginning at 1 within the account's **insurance group**, which is the triple of `public_unit_id`, `depository_id` and `deposit_class`. This sequence — never the account's `id` — pins the deterministic order in which insurance coverage is consumed, so the second `DEMAND` account a county opens at a given bank always sits behind the first. The new account's `funding_status` is `PENDING_FUNDING` and its `collateral_status` is `UNSECURED`; `ledger_balance_cents`, `insured_amount_cents`, `uninsured_amount_cents`, `required_collateral_pct_bps`, `required_collateral_cents`, `assigned_collateral_value_cents` and `pooled_allocated_collateral_cents` are all zero; `coverage_ratio_bps` is 10000, because an account with no requirement is by definition fully covered; and `restricted_reason` is null. The account also carries `pool_priority_rank`, derived once from its public unit's `unit_type`: a `SCHOOL_DISTRICT` ranks 1, a `COUNTY` ranks 2, a `MUNICIPALITY` ranks 3 and a `STATE_AGENCY` ranks 4, which is also the field's default.

Funding moves money and triggers the longest recomputation chain in the service. A deposit supplies `amount_cents` and, on an account still in `PENDING_FUNDING`, carries `funding_status` to `FUNDED`; on an account already `FUNDED` it is a self-loop that leaves `funding_status` untouched. Both a deposit and a withdrawal require the depository's `approval_status` to be `APPROVED` and the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the account's `public_unit_id`. Before a deposit is accepted, the owning unit's `total_deposit_balance_cents` plus the deposited `amount_cents` must be at most its `statutory_max_deposit_cents`; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_STATUTORY_DEPOSIT_LIMIT`. A withdrawal supplies `amount_cents` too, is admissible only on a `FUNDED` account, and may not exceed the account's current `ledger_balance_cents`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LEDGER_BALANCE`. Draining an account to zero does *not* return it to `PENDING_FUNDING`: `funding_status` stays `FUNDED`. And because `RESTRICTED` is terminal on the funding dimension, neither a deposit nor a withdrawal is admissible on a restricted account — the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Whenever a balance moves, the service re-derives the whole dependent chain in a fixed order. First the deposited or withdrawn amount is added to or subtracted from `ledger_balance_cents`. Second, **insured-amount determination** is re-run for every member of the touched account's insurance group, in ascending `group_sequence` order, where group membership means an account of that same triple whose `funding_status` is `PENDING_FUNDING`, `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED`. Accounts of another deposit class, another depository or another public unit never share coverage. Third, each touched account's required percentage and requirement are re-derived. Fourth, the account's assigned collateral value is re-derived — for a `DEDICATED` account from its own agreements' allocations, for a `POOLED` account by re-running the pool waterfall of §3.5. Fifth, `coverage_ratio_bps` and `collateral_status` are recomputed for every touched account. Sixth, the owning public unit's `total_deposit_balance_cents`, `total_uninsured_cents` and `exposure_status` are recomputed, along with the depository's `total_required_collateral_cents`. Finally the account's `version` is incremented.

Insured-amount determination itself comes in **two variants**, selected by the depository's `insurance_determination_basis`, and the federal coverage limit in both is 25,000,000 cents — two hundred fifty thousand dollars — per bucket.

- Under `AGGREGATED_BY_UNIT`, the default, one single coverage bucket of 25,000,000 cents is shared by the whole insurance group and consumed in ascending `group_sequence` order. Starting with the full bucket as the remaining coverage, each account in turn takes as its `insured_amount_cents` the lesser of the coverage still remaining and its own `ledger_balance_cents`; that amount is then deducted from the remaining coverage before the next account is considered. Later accounts in the group can therefore end up entirely uninsured.
- Under `PER_ACCOUNT`, every account receives its own 25,000,000-cent bucket and the processing order is irrelevant because nothing is shared: each account's `insured_amount_cents` is simply the lesser of 25,000,000 cents and its own `ledger_balance_cents`. Whenever a group holds more than one funded account, this variant produces strictly smaller uninsured totals than the aggregated variant.

In both variants an account's `uninsured_amount_cents` is its `ledger_balance_cents` less its `insured_amount_cents`, so the two parts always sum back to the ledger balance exactly. No rounding occurs anywhere in this determination — only comparison and subtraction of whole cents.

The **required collateral percentage** is then built for each affected account in four steps and stored as `required_collateral_pct_bps`. Step one reads the depository's capital tier from `capital_ratio_bps` using the inclusive lower bounds already described, and converts the tier into a base percentage: a `WELL_CAPITALIZED` depository requires 10500 basis points of the uninsured balance, an `ADEQUATELY_CAPITALIZED` one requires 11000, and an `UNDERCAPITALIZED` one requires 12500. Step two adds a pledging-mode surcharge keyed on *the account's own* `pledging_mode` rather than on the unit's stated preference: nothing for a `DEDICATED` account and 500 basis points for a `POOLED` one. Step three adds a deficiency surcharge of 1000 basis points when the depository's `consecutive_deficiency_runs` counter stands at 2 or more, and nothing otherwise; inside a mark-to-market run the counter used here is the snapshot taken before the run mutates it, never the post-run value. Step four combines the three additively and then applies a hard cap: the stored percentage is the lesser of 15000 basis points and the sum of the tier, mode and deficiency components. The account's `required_collateral_cents` is then the uninsured amount scaled by that percentage — `uninsured_amount_cents` multiplied by `required_collateral_pct_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. An account whose `funding_status` is `PENDING_FUNDING`, `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED` all carry a requirement; restriction confers no exemption.

Coverage is measured by `coverage_ratio_bps`. When `required_collateral_cents` is zero the ratio is defined as 10000; otherwise it is `assigned_collateral_value_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `required_collateral_cents`, floored to whole basis points.

The account's second status dimension, `collateral_status`, is a pure function of that ratio and of whether any collateral is assigned at all, and it moves entirely independently of `funding_status` — a `RESTRICTED` account still travels between `UNSECURED`, `SECURED` and `DEFICIENT`, and a `FUNDED` account's collateral status changes without any funding transition. The service recomputes this dimension after every operation that can disturb either side of the ratio: creating a pledge allocation, releasing a pledge allocation, posting a new market price, crediting a deposit, debiting a withdrawal, settling a substitution, and curing a margin call, and equally after the allocation releases that agreement termination and asset ineligibility cascade into. Six transitions are defined, and the boundary at 10000 basis points is inclusive on the secured side:

- `UNSECURED → SECURED` when the recomputed `coverage_ratio_bps` is at least 10000.
- `UNSECURED → DEFICIENT` when `assigned_collateral_value_cents` is greater than zero and the ratio is below 10000.
- `SECURED → DEFICIENT` when the ratio falls below 10000 while `assigned_collateral_value_cents` remains greater than zero — the ordinary consequence of a price drop, a requirement increase, an allocation release or a substitution settlement.
- `DEFICIENT → SECURED` when the ratio recovers to at least 10000, whether through a new allocation, a price rise or a withdrawal.
- `SECURED → UNSECURED` and `DEFICIENT → UNSECURED` when every allocation that was covering the account has left `ACTIVE`, so `assigned_collateral_value_cents` is zero while `required_collateral_cents` is still greater than zero.

Each of these six transitions is followed by a recomputation of the owning public unit's `exposure_status`: a unit holding no restricted account drops to `WATCH` when one of its accounts is deficient, and its watch clears back to `NORMAL` only when none of its accounts is deficient and none is restricted.

Public unit reporting is served by a per-account **coverage report**, readable by the account's own treasurer, by a `BankComplianceOfficer` and by a `StateRegulator`. It is a point-in-time projection rather than a stored entity, and it returns exactly ten values: `insured_amount_cents`, `uninsured_amount_cents`, `required_collateral_pct_bps`, `required_collateral_cents`, `assigned_collateral_value_cents`, `coverage_ratio_bps`, `collateral_status`, `pledging_mode`, the depository's `insurance_determination_basis`, and `report_as_of`, which is the logical clock at the moment the report was produced, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp. Accounts themselves are listable with any combination of `depository_id`, `public_unit_id`, `deposit_class`, `funding_status` and `collateral_status` filters, which is how a regulator sweeps for deficiency across a whole bank.

### 3.3. Pledge Documentation: Tri-Party Agreements and the Signature Quorum

Collateral cannot be pledged on a handshake. Every pledge in this service hangs off a **pledge agreement**, a document that names the pledging bank, the custodian that will hold the securities, and the beneficiary the pledge protects. A `BankCollateralOfficer` drafts one by supplying the `depository_id` of its own bank, the `custodian_id` of the intended tri-party safekeeping agent, a `beneficiary_type` of either `DEPOSIT_ACCOUNT` or `POOLED_PROGRAM`, the bank's own document number as `agreement_number`, and the two-letter `governing_law_state` code named in the instrument. The beneficiary discriminator is polymorphic and its companion field is conditional: a `DEPOSIT_ACCOUNT` agreement must supply `deposit_account_id` and must omit `pooled_program_id`, while a `POOLED_PROGRAM` agreement must supply `pooled_program_id`, must omit `deposit_account_id`, and may only name a program of which the drafting depository is already a member, meaning the depository's own `pooled_program_id` equals the named program. Any breach of that coherence rule — a missing companion, a superfluous companion, or a pool the bank does not belong to — is refused with `ERR_BENEFICIARY_TARGET_INVALID`. The `agreement_number` must be unique within the depository, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A drafted agreement begins at `status` `DRAFT` with `signature_count` zero, `amendment_round` zero, all three party stamps (`depository_signed_at`, `public_unit_signed_at`, `custodian_signed_at`) null, both `executed_at` and `terminated_at` null, and `total_pledged_par_cents`, `total_collateral_value_cents` and `active_allocation_count` all zero.

Circulating the draft carries it to `PENDING_SIGNATURES`. Circulation is admissible from `DRAFT` and from `AMENDMENT_REQUESTED`, and it has two cross-entity preconditions beyond the state check: the named custodian's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED` **and** its `tri_party_capable` flag must be true, else `ERR_CUSTODIAN_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and the depository's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_NOT_APPROVED`. Re-circulating an amended agreement is additionally admissible only while `amendment_round` remains within the ceiling of 2. Each circulation increments the agreement's `version`.

Signatures arrive one at a time, each request naming a single `party` from `DEPOSITORY`, `PUBLIC_UNIT` and `CUSTODIAN`. Entitlement is strict, and it is checked against the acting role and, for the entity-backed roles, against ownership as well. The `DEPOSITORY` party may be stamped only by a `BankComplianceOfficer` — the bank's second line signs for the bank, never its first line. The `CUSTODIAN` party may be stamped only by a `Custodian` whose `X-User-ID` equals the agreement's `custodian_id`. The `PUBLIC_UNIT` party may be stamped by a `PublicUnitTreasurer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the `public_unit_id` of the beneficiary deposit account when `beneficiary_type` is `DEPOSIT_ACCOUNT`; on a `POOLED_PROGRAM` agreement, where there is no single beneficiary unit, the `PUBLIC_UNIT` party is stamped instead by a `StateRegulator` acting for the pool's participants. A role attempting a party it is not entitled to is refused with `ERR_SIGNATURE_PARTY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403); a party whose stamp is already set in the current round is refused with `ERR_PARTY_ALREADY_SIGNED` (HTTP 409).

Each accepted signature stamps the matching `_signed_at` field with the logical clock, recomputes `signature_count` as the number of non-null party stamps, and increments the agreement's `version`. While fewer than three stamps are present the agreement self-loops in `PENDING_SIGNATURES`. The signature that supplies the third distinct party — that is, the one arriving when `signature_count` stood at 2 — carries the agreement to `EXECUTED`, sets `signature_count` to 3 and stamps `executed_at` from the clock. Execution is what turns the document into a legal pledge vehicle: until it has happened, any attempt to allocate collateral to the agreement, to request a substitution under it, or to issue a safekeeping receipt against it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`.

Amendment is the counter-move, available to the beneficiary's `PublicUnitTreasurer` or to a `StateRegulator` while the agreement sits in `PENDING_SIGNATURES`. Requesting an amendment carries the agreement to `AMENDMENT_REQUESTED`, increments `amendment_round` by 1, and **voids every signature already collected**: `depository_signed_at`, `public_unit_signed_at` and `custodian_signed_at` are all reset to null and `signature_count` is reset to 0, so the whole quorum must be rebuilt after re-circulation. The incremented `amendment_round` may be at most 2, so the third amendment request is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. This is the documentation cycle `PENDING_SIGNATURES → AMENDMENT_REQUESTED → PENDING_SIGNATURES`, and re-circulation is what closes it.

Termination is the regulator's move against an `EXECUTED` agreement, and it is blocked while any substitution under that agreement is still in flight — that is, while any of its substitution requests is in `SUBMITTED`, `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED`, `CUSTODIAN_ACKED` or `READY_TO_SETTLE` status; the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When termination proceeds, the agreement moves to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` stamped and its `version` incremented, and every one of its allocations still in `ACTIVE` is moved to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`. Each release restores the underlying asset's `pledged_par_cents` and `available_par_cents`, after which the agreement's totals, the depository's pledged totals and the beneficiary's coverage — the dedicated account's assigned value, or the pool waterfall for a pooled agreement — are all recomputed, followed by the affected public units' `exposure_status`. `TERMINATED` is terminal: a terminated agreement accepts no further action.

Agreements are listable with any combination of `depository_id`, `custodian_id`, `deposit_account_id`, `pooled_program_id`, `beneficiary_type` and `status` filters, which is how an officer finds the executed dedicated agreement backing a particular account and how a custodian finds every agreement naming it.

### 3.4. Collateral Inventory, Pricing and Allocation

A `BankCollateralOfficer` registers each security the bank intends to pledge as a **collateral asset**, supplying the `depository_id` of the owning bank, the `custodian_id` of the institution holding it in safekeeping, its `cusip` security identifier, its `asset_type`, and its `par_value_cents`, which must be strictly positive. An optional `market_price_bps` may be supplied at registration — the clean price per 100 of par in basis points, where 10000 means the security trades exactly at par — and it defaults to 10000 when omitted. The depository must be `APPROVED` before any collateral may be registered against it, and the `cusip` must be unique within that depository, a collision being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

The asset type drives the **haircut**, the discount the state applies to market value before crediting collateral. `haircut_bps` is derived at registration from the asset type and is never supplied by a client: a `US_TREASURY` carries 200 basis points, an `AGENCY_DEBENTURE` 300, an `AGENCY_MBS` 800, a `MUNI_GO` 1000, a `MUNI_REVENUE` 1500, and a `LETTER_OF_CREDIT` none at all. `MUNI_REVENUE` occupies a special position: its haircut is defined for completeness, but revenue bonds are **not** an eligible collateral type for public funds in this jurisdiction, so any attempt to pledge one, to substitute into one, or to credit one against a margin call is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_TYPE_INELIGIBLE` — as is any attempt involving an asset whose own `eligibility_status` has been set to `INELIGIBLE`. A newly registered asset has `priced_at` stamped from the logical clock, `pledged_par_cents` at zero, `available_par_cents` equal to its full `par_value_cents`, `eligibility_status` `ELIGIBLE`, `pledge_status` `UNPLEDGED` and `ineligible_reason` null, with its two value mirrors computed as described below.

Valuation runs in **two sequential rounding steps, in this order**, and it is performed both on the whole asset and on each individual allocation. For the asset as a whole, `market_value_cents` is `par_value_cents` multiplied by `market_price_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents; then `collateral_value_cents` is that market value multiplied by the quantity 10000 minus `haircut_bps`, divided by 10000, again rounded half up to whole cents. For an individual allocation, the same two steps run against the pledged slice rather than the full par: the allocation's market value is `allocated_par_cents` multiplied by the asset's `market_price_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up; then `allocated_collateral_value_cents` is that figure multiplied by 10000 minus the asset's `haircut_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. An allocation whose asset has `eligibility_status` `INELIGIBLE` has an `allocated_collateral_value_cents` of exactly zero regardless of price.

Aggregates are assembled by **summing the already-rounded per-allocation values**, never by re-deriving the total from unrounded par, price and haircut. The distinction matters because the two orders can differ by up to one cent per allocation, and this service pins the per-allocation order. Concretely: an agreement's `total_collateral_value_cents` is the sum of `allocated_collateral_value_cents` over its allocations that are `ACTIVE` and whose asset is `ELIGIBLE`; its `total_pledged_par_cents` is the sum of `allocated_par_cents` over all of its `ACTIVE` allocations, whether or not the underlying asset is still eligible; and its `active_allocation_count` is the number of its allocations in `ACTIVE` status. A depository's `total_pledged_collateral_value_cents` is the sum of `total_collateral_value_cents` over its agreements in `EXECUTED` status. So an ineligible asset stops contributing value immediately while its par continues to be counted as committed until the allocation is released.

Two of the mirrors are **interval-aware**, because a pledge occupies par only for the stretch of time it is effective. An asset's `pledged_par_cents` is the sum of `allocated_par_cents` over its `ACTIVE` allocations whose effective interval covers the current logical clock, meaning the allocation's `effective_from` is at or before the clock and its `effective_to` is strictly after it. `available_par_cents` is then `par_value_cents` less `pledged_par_cents`, and it can never go negative even when two `ACTIVE` allocations each committing the full par sit on disjoint intervals. A depository's `total_pledged_par_cents` is likewise the sum of `allocated_par_cents` over the `ACTIVE` allocations of its `EXECUTED` agreements whose intervals cover the current clock. From `pledged_par_cents` the service also derives `pledge_status`: `UNPLEDGED` when nothing is pledged, `PARTIALLY_PLEDGED` when some but not all of the par is pledged, and `FULLY_PLEDGED` when the pledged par equals `par_value_cents` exactly.

Prices arrive from the vendor. A `SystemOperator` or a `BankCollateralOfficer` posts a new `market_price_bps`, which must be strictly positive, against an asset whose `eligibility_status` is still `ELIGIBLE`; the asset self-loops in `ELIGIBLE`, its `priced_at` is re-stamped from the clock, and its `market_value_cents` and `collateral_value_cents` are recomputed. The repricing then propagates: every `ACTIVE` allocation on that asset recomputes its `allocated_collateral_value_cents`; every agreement touched recomputes `total_collateral_value_cents`; dedicated beneficiaries recompute their coverage while pooled agreements re-run the pool waterfall; the depository's `total_pledged_collateral_value_cents` is recomputed; and the asset's `version` is incremented. Because a stale price is not evidence of value, an asset whose `priced_at` is more than 48 hours behind the logical clock may not back a new pledge or a new substitution, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_STALE_COLLATERAL_PRICE`.

A `StateRegulator` may declare an asset ineligible, supplying an `ineligible_reason`. The asset moves `ELIGIBLE → INELIGIBLE`, its `collateral_value_cents` collapses to zero, the reason is stored, and its `version` is incremented. Every `ACTIVE` allocation on that asset is then moved to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `ASSET_INELIGIBLE`; each release restores `pledged_par_cents` and `available_par_cents`, then recomputes the owning agreement, then the beneficiary — a dedicated account's coverage, or the pool waterfall — and finally the affected public units' `exposure_status`. `INELIGIBLE` is terminal for the asset.

Pledging is the act that binds an asset to an agreement. A `BankCollateralOfficer` creates a **pledge allocation** by supplying the `pledge_agreement_id`, the `collateral_asset_id`, the `allocated_par_cents` to commit (strictly positive), the `effective_from` instant at which the pledge begins, and `expected_pledge_agreement_version` as described in §2.6. An optional `effective_to` gives the exclusive end of the occupation interval; when it is omitted the allocation is open-ended and the service stores the sentinel instant `9999-12-31T00:00:00Z` in its place. The interval is half-open: it includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`. Beyond the agreement-executed, depository-approved and version-pinning gates, five domain checks guard the pledge.

- **Tri-party alignment.** The asset's `custodian_id` must equal the agreement's `custodian_id`, else `ERR_CUSTODIAN_MISMATCH`; and the asset's `depository_id` must equal the agreement's `depository_id`, else `ERR_DEPOSITORY_MISMATCH`.
- **Interval sanity.** `effective_from` must be strictly before `effective_to`, and `effective_from` must be at or after the agreement's `executed_at` — a pledge cannot predate the document that authorises it. A violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.
- **Par capacity.** Considering every instant inside the requested half-open interval, the sum of `allocated_par_cents` of the asset's `ACTIVE` allocations that overlap that instant, plus the newly requested `allocated_par_cents`, must not exceed the asset's `par_value_cents`. Two intervals that merely touch — where an existing allocation's `effective_to` equals the new allocation's `effective_from` — do **not** overlap, so back-to-back pledges of the full par are legitimate. Genuine oversubscription is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_OVERSUBSCRIBED`.
- **Custodian concentration.** After the write, the collateral value the depository holds at this custodian, multiplied by 10000, must be at most the depository's `total_pledged_collateral_value_cents` multiplied by that custodian's `concentration_limit_bps`. Exceeding the limit is refused with `ERR_CUSTODIAN_CONCENTRATION_EXCEEDED`.
- **Approved pledge capacity.** The depository's `total_pledged_par_cents` plus the newly requested `allocated_par_cents` must not exceed the `approved_pledge_capacity_cents` the state granted at qualification, else `ERR_PLEDGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A created allocation is `ACTIVE`, with `released_at`, `release_reason` and `created_by_substitution_id` all null — the last of these is populated only on an allocation minted by a settled substitution. Its `allocated_collateral_value_cents` is computed by the two-step valuation above. The asset's `pledged_par_cents` grows by the allocated par and its `available_par_cents` shrinks correspondingly, with `pledge_status` re-derived. The agreement's totals and `active_allocation_count` are updated and **the agreement's `version` is incremented by 1**, which is precisely why a client that has just created one allocation must re-read the agreement before creating a second. Finally the beneficiary's coverage is recomputed: the dedicated account's assigned value, or the pool waterfall across the whole program.

Releasing an allocation is the officer's way of freeing collateral back into the bank's unpledged inventory. An officer-initiated release requires the allocation to be `ACTIVE` and the owning agreement to be `EXECUTED`, and it is blocked while any substitution request naming that allocation as its `outgoing_allocation_id` is still in flight in `SUBMITTED`, `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED`, `CUSTODIAN_ACKED` or `READY_TO_SETTLE` status. A release sets `status` to `RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` from the clock, records `release_reason` as `OFFICER_RELEASE`, and increments the allocation's `version`; it then decreases the asset's `pledged_par_cents` by the allocated par, re-derives `available_par_cents` and `pledge_status`, recomputes the agreement's totals, recomputes the beneficiary (dedicated account coverage, or a fresh pool waterfall run), and finally recomputes the affected public units' `exposure_status`. `RELEASED` and `SUBSTITUTED` are both terminal for an allocation, and the four values `release_reason` can take are `OFFICER_RELEASE`, `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`, `ASSET_INELIGIBLE` and `SUBSTITUTION_SETTLED`.

Inventory is navigable from both directions: assets are listable by `depository_id`, `custodian_id`, `asset_type`, `eligibility_status` and `pledge_status`, and allocations by `pledge_agreement_id`, `collateral_asset_id`, `created_by_substitution_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Dedicated Assignment versus the Pooled Waterfall

There are two ways an account's uninsured balance gets covered, and an account's `pledging_mode` decides which one applies to it. Under **dedicated** pledging, coverage is a direct read of the account's own paperwork: the account's `assigned_collateral_value_cents` is the sum of `allocated_collateral_value_cents` over the `ACTIVE` allocations of the agreements that are in `EXECUTED` status, name that account as their beneficiary, and sit on assets whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`. A dedicated account's `pooled_allocated_collateral_cents` is always exactly zero — it receives nothing from any pool.

Under **pooled** pledging the account has no paperwork of its own; it draws on a shared pot, and the share it receives is decided by a statutory waterfall that is re-run in full every time either side of the pool changes. The pot itself, `aggregate_collateral_value_cents`, is the sum of `allocated_collateral_value_cents` over every allocation that is `ACTIVE`, sits under an agreement in `EXECUTED` status whose `beneficiary_type` is `POOLED_PROGRAM` and whose `pooled_program_id` is this pool, rests on an asset whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, and whose half-open effective interval covers the current logical clock.

The participants are every deposit account whose `pledging_mode` is `POOLED`, whose `funding_status` is either `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED`, and whose depository's `pooled_program_id` is this pool. Accounts still in `PENDING_FUNDING` are **not** participants and receive nothing, even though they may already carry a requirement. Restricted accounts remain participants: suspension does not evict a public unit from the pool's protection.

Participants are then ordered by three keys applied in sequence, and the ordering is the whole substance of the statute. The primary key is `pool_priority_rank` ascending, so school districts are made whole before counties, counties before municipalities, and municipalities before state agencies. Where two participants share a rank, the one with the larger `required_collateral_cents` is served first. Where they still tie, the account created earlier is served first.

Distribution walks that ordered list once, carrying the pot as a running remainder that starts at `aggregate_collateral_value_cents`. Each participant in turn takes as its `pooled_allocated_collateral_cents` the lesser of the remainder and its own `required_collateral_cents`; that amount is also written to its `assigned_collateral_value_cents`; and the remainder is reduced by the amount just taken. Whatever survives the walk becomes the pool's `unallocated_collateral_cents`. Because only comparison and subtraction of whole cents occur, the identity is exact: the pot always equals the sum of the participants' allocated shares plus the unallocated residual, with nothing created and nothing lost. A pool whose pot runs dry mid-walk leaves every remaining participant at a share of zero, which drives those accounts to `UNSECURED` or `DEFICIENT` according to the rules of §3.2.

Two further pool figures fall out of the same walk. `aggregate_required_collateral_cents` is the sum of `required_collateral_cents` over the participants, and `participant_account_count` is how many participants there were. From those the pool's own health indicator is derived: `pool_posture` is `SHORTFALL` when `aggregate_collateral_value_cents` multiplied by 10000 is strictly less than `aggregate_required_collateral_cents` multiplied by the pool's `minimum_pool_pct_bps`, and `ADEQUATE` otherwise. With the default floor of 11000 basis points, a pool holding exactly as much collateral as its participants collectively require is already in `SHORTFALL`, because the statute demands a ten percent cushion above the bare requirement.

The waterfall is re-run whenever a pooled agreement's value or a participant's requirement can have moved. That means after creating an allocation, after releasing one, after posting a new market price, after declaring an asset ineligible, after settling a substitution, after terminating an agreement, after a deposit, after a withdrawal, and after suspending a depository. Each re-run rewrites every participant's share, not only the account that appeared to be involved, which is exactly why a price drop on one bank's bond can move a school district's `collateral_status` at another bank in the same pool. Member depositories of a pool are listable from the pool itself, optionally filtered by `approval_status`, and a pool's read view exposes `aggregate_collateral_value_cents`, `unallocated_collateral_cents` and `pool_posture` so a regulator can see the cushion directly.

### 3.6. Collateral Substitution as a Fork-Join

Banks swap collateral constantly — a bond matures, a better-priced security becomes available, a custodian needs to move a line. Swapping pledged collateral is not a unilateral act, so a substitution in this service is a **fork-join**: an independent compliance branch and an independent custodian branch run in either order, and only when both have completed may the custodian settle. A `BankCollateralOfficer` opens a **substitution request** by naming the `pledge_agreement_id` it operates under, the `outgoing_allocation_id` to be swapped out, the `incoming_asset_id` to be pledged in its place, and the `incoming_par_cents` of that asset to pledge, which must be strictly positive.

The request is admitted only against an agreement in `EXECUTED` status, only at a depository whose `collateral_posture` is not `FROZEN`, and only when the incoming asset passes the collateral-type test and the two alignment tests — its `custodian_id` matching the agreement's `custodian_id` and its `depository_id` matching the agreement's `depository_id` — and carries a price observation no more than 48 hours behind the logical clock. The named outgoing allocation must genuinely belong to the named agreement and must still be `ACTIVE`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_UNDER_AGREEMENT`. An admitted request begins at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` recording the requesting officer's `X-User-ID`, with `compliance_approved_at`, `compliance_approved_by_user_id`, `custodian_acked_at`, `settled_at`, `rejection_reason` and `value_adequacy_delta_cents` all null.

The **compliance branch** is the bank's second line asserting that the bank is not quietly downgrading its collateral. A `BankComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id` — equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — approves the request. This branch, and only this branch, computes and stores `value_adequacy_delta_cents`: the haircut value the incoming pledge would carry, obtained by applying the two-step valuation of §3.4 to `incoming_par_cents` on the incoming asset, less the outgoing allocation's current `allocated_collateral_value_cents`. The delta must be zero or positive, meaning the incoming collateral is worth at least as much as what it replaces; a negative delta is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_VALUE_SHORTFALL`. On success the branch stamps `compliance_approved_at` from the clock, records the approver as `compliance_approved_by_user_id`, and increments the request's `version`. No par moves on this branch. Arriving first, it carries the request `SUBMITTED → COMPLIANCE_APPROVED`; arriving second, against a request already in `CUSTODIAN_ACKED`, it performs exactly the same writes and checks and carries the request `CUSTODIAN_ACKED → READY_TO_SETTLE`.

The **custodian branch** is the safekeeping agent confirming that it can actually take delivery. Only a `Custodian` whose `X-User-ID` equals the agreement's `custodian_id` may acknowledge, and the acknowledgement additionally asserts tri-party custody alignment: the incoming asset's `custodian_id` must equal the agreement's `custodian_id`, else `ERR_CUSTODIAN_MISMATCH`. This branch, and only this branch, stamps `custodian_acked_at` from the clock, and it increments the request's `version`; it never writes `value_adequacy_delta_cents`, which stays null if the custodian acknowledges first. Arriving first, it carries the request `SUBMITTED → CUSTODIAN_ACKED`; arriving second, against a request already in `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED`, it performs the same stamp and the same ownership check and carries the request `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED → READY_TO_SETTLE`.

Settlement is the join's payoff and the second depth-four cascade in the service. Only a `Custodian` whose `X-User-ID` equals the agreement's `custodian_id` may settle, only from `READY_TO_SETTLE`, only while that custodian's own `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, and only when the incoming asset's `eligibility_status` is still `ELIGIBLE` and its `available_par_cents` is at least `incoming_par_cents`; the par-capacity rule of §3.4 applies to the incoming pledge exactly as it does to a directly created allocation, with `ERR_ALLOCATION_OVERSUBSCRIBED` on breach. Given those, four hops run in order.

- **Hop one** moves the outgoing allocation from `ACTIVE` to `SUBSTITUTED`, stamps its `released_at` from the clock, sets its `release_reason` to `SUBSTITUTION_SETTLED`, increments its `version`, and reduces the outgoing asset's `pledged_par_cents` by that allocation's `allocated_par_cents`. In the same hop a brand-new allocation is minted on the incoming asset with `allocated_par_cents` equal to `incoming_par_cents`, `effective_from` set to the logical clock, `effective_to` set to the open-ended sentinel, `created_by_substitution_id` pointing at this request, and `status` `ACTIVE`; the incoming asset's `pledged_par_cents` grows by the same par. The two legs are deliberately equal and opposite, so total par in the system is conserved.
- **Hop two**, caused by hop one, has the pledge agreement recompute `total_pledged_par_cents`, `total_collateral_value_cents` and `active_allocation_count`. When the agreement's `beneficiary_type` is `POOLED_PROGRAM`, the pool additionally recomputes `aggregate_collateral_value_cents` and re-runs the full waterfall of §3.5.
- **Hop three**, caused by hop two, has every participant deposit account whose `assigned_collateral_value_cents` changed recompute `coverage_ratio_bps` and `collateral_status`, so an account slips `SECURED → DEFICIENT` when its ratio drops below 10000.
- **Hop four**, caused by hop three, has every public unit owning an account that just became `DEFICIENT` recompute `exposure_status` to `WATCH`, provided that unit holds no `RESTRICTED` account.

The request itself then moves to `SETTLED` with `settled_at` stamped and its `version` incremented. `SETTLED` is terminal.

Two escape hatches close a request without moving any par. Compliance may **reject** a request that is in `SUBMITTED` or `CUSTODIAN_ACKED` status, supplying a `rejection_reason`; the acting `X-User-ID` must again differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. Rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED`, stores the reason and increments `version`, and changes no allocation and no asset field, because the request never moved par; when rejecting from `CUSTODIAN_ACKED`, the existing `custodian_acked_at` stamp is retained for the audit trail rather than cleared. Note that a request already in `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED` or `READY_TO_SETTLE` cannot be rejected — the attempt meets `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The originating `BankCollateralOfficer`, and only the officer whose `X-User-ID` equals `submitted_by_user_id`, may **withdraw** a request from any of `SUBMITTED`, `COMPLIANCE_APPROVED`, `CUSTODIAN_ACKED` and `READY_TO_SETTLE`, including a late withdrawal on the very brink of settlement; withdrawal sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN` and increments `version`, and because no par movement occurs, every asset's `available_par_cents` plus `pledged_par_cents` remains exactly what it was. `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are both terminal. Requests are listable by `pledge_agreement_id`, `outgoing_allocation_id`, `incoming_asset_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Mark-to-Market Valuation, Deficiency and Risk Grading

Periodically — daily for the busiest banks, less often for small ones — somebody must value the whole arrangement and say whether the bank is short. That act is a **mark-to-market run**, and either a `BankCollateralOfficer` or a `StateRegulator` may execute one against a depository. The request supplies the closed valuation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, a `basis` of `SUPERVISORY` or `INTERNAL`, and `expected_depository_version` as described in §2.6. A run record is immutable once written: it carries `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`, and nothing in the service ever amends one.

Windows are disciplined so that history cannot be rewritten or double-counted. `window_start` must be at or before `window_end`, so a single-instant window is permissible but an inverted one is not; `window_end` may not lie in the future relative to the logical clock; the closed window from `window_start` to `window_end` inclusive must not intersect the closed window of any prior run of the same depository; and `window_start` must fall at least 4 hours after the previous run's `window_end`, giving a mandatory buffer between consecutive windows. Any of these failures is refused with `ERR_MTM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

The run's difficulty is that the collateral requirement it measures depends on a deficiency surcharge derived from a counter that the run itself updates. The service breaks that circularity with an explicit, authoritative evaluation order of seven steps.

**Step one — snapshot.** Before anything is mutated, the service reads the depository's current `consecutive_deficiency_runs` and holds that value for the entire run. The run's `deficiency_surcharge_bps_applied` is 1000 basis points when the snapshot is 2 or more, and zero otherwise. The run's `as_of` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `run_index` is the depository's `mtm_run_index` plus 1.

**Step two — re-derive every account.** For every deposit account of the depository whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED`, the service re-runs insured-amount determination and then the required-collateral-percentage computation of §3.2, using the step-one snapshot as the deficiency counter throughout — never the incremented value.

**Step three — totals.** The run's `total_required_collateral_cents` is the sum of `required_collateral_cents` over exactly those accounts. The run's `total_collateral_value_cents` is the sum of `allocated_collateral_value_cents` over the allocations that are `ACTIVE`, belong to the depository's agreements in `EXECUTED` status, rest on assets whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, and whose half-open effective interval covers the run's `as_of` — that is, whose `effective_from` is at or before `as_of` and whose `effective_to` is strictly after it.

**Step four — deficiency.** `deficiency_cents` is the amount by which the required total exceeds the collateral total, floored at zero, so a surplus reports as zero rather than as a negative number. `deficiency_ratio_bps` is zero when `total_required_collateral_cents` is zero; otherwise it is `deficiency_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `total_required_collateral_cents`, floored to whole basis points.

**Step five — outcome.** The run's `outcome` is `DEFICIENT` when `deficiency_cents` is greater than zero and `SUFFICIENT` otherwise.

**Step six — counter and margin call.** This step holds the only writes to the deficiency counter in the entire service. On a `DEFICIENT` outcome the depository's `consecutive_deficiency_runs` becomes the snapshot plus 1, and a margin call is issued against the depository referencing this run as its `mtm_run_id`, with `required_cure_cents` frozen at the run's `deficiency_cents`, `cured_cents` at zero and `status` `ISSUED`. Its `cure_deadline_at` is the run's `as_of` advanced by a number of hours that depends on how badly the bank is short: 24 hours when `deficiency_ratio_bps` is at least 2000, otherwise 48 hours when it is at least 500, and otherwise 120 hours, which is five full days for a trivial shortfall. Its `breach_deadline_at` is `cure_deadline_at` advanced by a further 24 hours of escalation grace. On a `SUFFICIENT` outcome the counter is instead reset to zero — a single clean run wipes the slate. Either way the depository's `mtm_run_index` is set to the run's `run_index`, its `last_mtm_window_end` is set to the run's `window_end`, and its `collateral_posture` is re-derived: `FROZEN` when `consecutive_deficiency_runs` has reached 3 or more, otherwise `MARGIN_DEFICIENT` when `open_margin_call_count` is 1 or more, otherwise `COMPLIANT`. The depository's `version` is incremented by 1, which is why a client running two consecutive runs must re-read the depository between them.

**Step seven — risk grade.** Three component scores are computed from this run's step-three totals, each expressed in basis points on a zero-to-10000 scale.

- The **coverage component** starts from a capped coverage ratio: 12000 basis points when `total_required_collateral_cents` is zero, otherwise the lesser of 12000 and the value of `total_collateral_value_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `total_required_collateral_cents`, floored to whole basis points. `coverage_component_bps` is then that capped ratio less 9000, multiplied by 10000, divided by 3000, rounded half up, and finally clamped into the range zero through 10000 inclusive. In other words a bank sitting at 90 percent coverage scores zero on this component and one sitting at 120 percent scores the full 10000.
- The **volatility component**, `volatility_component_bps`, is 10000 less the lesser of 10000 and the depository's `consecutive_deficiency_runs` multiplied by 2500 — and here the counter is the value **after** step six, not the snapshot. Four consecutive deficient runs therefore zero this component out.
- The **documentation component** counts paperwork. The denominator is the number of the depository's accounts whose `funding_status` is `FUNDED` or `RESTRICTED`; the numerator is how many of those are the beneficiary of an agreement in `EXECUTED` status, where a `POOLED` account counts as documented when its depository's pool has at least one executed pooled agreement. `documentation_component_bps` is 10000 when the denominator is zero; otherwise it is 10000 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up, and capped at 10000.

The run's `basis` then selects between **two variants** that differ only in which component values are fed into the weighting. Under `SUPERVISORY`, the regulator's view, the three components of this run alone are used, so one bad run moves the grade immediately. Under `INTERNAL`, the bank's view, each component is instead averaged over a trailing window consisting of this run plus the previous two runs of the same depository, taken in descending `run_index` order; where the depository has fewer than three runs in total, the average is taken over however many exist. Each of the three component averages is rounded half up to whole basis points **before** weighting — averaging first, then weighting, is the pinned order — so the same current-run inputs yield a higher, smoothed score whenever the earlier runs were stronger.

Weighting is identical in both variants and involves exactly one further rounding step: `risk_score_bps` is the coverage figure multiplied by 0.50, plus the volatility figure multiplied by 0.30, plus the documentation figure multiplied by 0.20, with the sum rounded half up to whole basis points. From that score `risk_grade_assigned` follows with inclusive lower bounds: `A` at 8500 or above, `B` at 7000 or above, `C` at 5000 or above, and `D` below 5000. Both the score and the grade are copied onto the depository as `risk_score_bps` and `risk_grade`, which is how a bank that lets its collateral rot eventually finds itself unable to open new deposit accounts at all. A depository's runs are listable from the depository with optional `basis` and `outcome` filters, and each run is also readable individually.

### 3.8. Margin Calls, Cure Accounting and the Daily Operations Sweep

A **margin call** is never created by a client; it is born from a deficient mark-to-market run and it carries the identity of that run in `mtm_run_id` along with the `depository_id` of the bank that owes the collateral. Its `required_cure_cents` is frozen at issuance from the issuing run's `deficiency_cents` and never changes afterwards, however the market moves; `cured_cents` starts at zero and accumulates; `outstanding_cents` is always `required_cure_cents` less `cured_cents`, floored at zero. `escalated_at` and `cured_at` are null at issuance. `breach_deadline_at` is computed at issuance as `cure_deadline_at` advanced by 24 hours of escalation grace, and it is re-stamped as `escalated_at` plus 24 hours if and when the sweep actually escalates the call.

Curing is the `BankCollateralOfficer`'s remedy, and the whole point of the cure rules is that **only new collateral counts**. The request supplies `credited_allocation_ids`, the identifiers of the allocations the bank offers against the call. Every one of them must be `ACTIVE`, must sit under an agreement in `EXECUTED` status belonging to the same depository as the call, must rest on an asset whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, and must have been created strictly after the issuing run's `as_of` — collateral that was already pledged when the deficiency was measured cannot be offered a second time to cure it. An offered allocation resting on an ineligible asset or an ineligible asset type is caught by the collateral-type rung and reported as `ERR_COLLATERAL_TYPE_INELIGIBLE`; any other failure of the four conditions is reported as `ERR_CURE_ALLOCATION_INELIGIBLE`, and one bad identifier spoils the whole request.

The credited value is the sum of the offered allocations' `allocated_collateral_value_cents` — the values already rounded per allocation, with no further rounding applied to the sum. Overpayment is not tolerated at all: the tolerance for exceeding the call is zero cents, so a credited value strictly greater than the call's current `outstanding_cents` is refused with `ERR_CURE_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. When the credit is accepted, `cured_cents` grows by the credited value, `outstanding_cents` is re-derived as `required_cure_cents` less `cured_cents` floored at zero, and the call's `version` is incremented. The depository's `open_margin_call_count` is then recounted as the number of its margin calls whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_CURED` or `ESCALATED`, and its `collateral_posture` is re-derived: `FROZEN` when `consecutive_deficiency_runs` stands at 3 or more, otherwise `MARGIN_DEFICIENT` when at least one call is open, otherwise `COMPLIANT`.

Cures may be posted only while the logical clock is strictly before the call's `breach_deadline_at`; a later attempt is refused with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Within that window the call's status follows the size of the credit:

- A credit smaller than `outstanding_cents` carries an `ISSUED` call to `PARTIALLY_CURED`, and a `PARTIALLY_CURED` call self-loops in `PARTIALLY_CURED` — a call may be cured in arbitrarily many partial instalments for as long as `outstanding_cents` stays positive.
- A credit that closes the remaining `outstanding_cents` exactly carries the call to `CURED`, stamping `cured_at` from the clock and setting `outstanding_cents` to zero. This is reachable from `ISSUED`, from `PARTIALLY_CURED` and from `ESCALATED` — a bank that has already been escalated can still redeem itself inside the grace period — and in every case the depository's `open_margin_call_count` and `collateral_posture` are recomputed afterwards.

`CURED` and `BREACHED` are terminal for a margin call; neither accepts a further cure.

Escalation and breach are not client actions. They are performed by the **daily operations sweep**, a `SystemOperator` capability that walks the open calls at the current logical clock and takes exactly two kinds of action. A call in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CURED` status whose `cure_deadline_at` the clock has reached or passed, while still standing before `breach_deadline_at`, is moved to `ESCALATED` with `escalated_at` stamped from the clock and its `version` incremented, after which the owning depository's `collateral_posture` is recomputed. A call already in `ESCALATED` status whose `breach_deadline_at` the clock has reached or passed is moved to `BREACHED` with its `version` incremented, and a breach is the gravest event in the system: the owning depository's `approval_status` is set to `SUSPENDED` if it was `APPROVED` — which fires hops two, three and four of the suspension cascade described in §3.1, restricting accounts, escalating public units and staleifying their open forecasts — and the depository's `collateral_posture` is set to `FROZEN`.

The sweep responds with four counters plus the clock it ran at: `escalated_margin_call_count`, `breached_margin_call_count`, `suspended_depository_count`, `stale_priced_asset_count` and `current_utc`. The last of the counters is purely informational — it reports how many collateral assets have a `priced_at` more than 48 hours behind the logical clock, so operations can chase the pricing vendor, and the sweep mutates nothing on those assets. Margin calls are listable with `depository_id`, `mtm_run_id` and `status` filters, and each is readable individually with its `outstanding_cents`, `cure_deadline_at` and `breach_deadline_at` exposed.

Driving the clock itself is the other operator capability: it takes a single `to_iso8601` instant, sets the logical clock to it, mutates no domain record, and replies with the new `current_utc`. Every deadline, every staleness test and every effective interval in this document reads from that clock, which is what makes the escalation and breach behaviour above exercisable without waiting a day.

### 3.9. Custody Confirmation and Deposit Runoff Planning

The bank's pledge ledger is only as credible as the custodian's independent account of what it holds. A `Custodian` therefore issues a **safekeeping receipt** against an agreement that names it, supplying the `pledge_agreement_id`, its own `receipt_number` for the document, the `reported_par_cents` total it claims to hold under that agreement, and the `reported_asset_count` of distinct securities it claims to hold. Both reported figures may be zero but neither may be negative. The agreement must be in `EXECUTED` status, the issuing custodian must be `APPROVED` and `tri_party_capable`, its `X-User-ID` must equal the agreement's `custodian_id`, and the `receipt_number` must be unique within that agreement, a collision being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Issuance stamps `as_of` from the logical clock and computes the ledger side of the reconciliation at that instant. The covering set is the agreement's allocations that are `ACTIVE` **and** whose half-open effective interval covers `as_of`; allocations in `SUBSTITUTED` or `RELEASED` status are excluded even when their interval would cover it. Over that set, `ledger_par_cents` is the sum of `allocated_par_cents` and `ledger_asset_count` is the number of distinct `collateral_asset_id` values, so two allocations on the same security count once. `discrepancy_par_cents` is then a **signed** figure: `reported_par_cents` less `ledger_par_cents`, negative when the custodian reports less than the bank's ledger claims. The receipt is created with `confirmation_status` `ISSUED`, and `confirmed_by_user_id` and `confirmed_at` null. Where the covering set is empty the arithmetic still applies rather than erroring: `ledger_par_cents` is zero and `ledger_asset_count` is zero, not null.

Audit confirmation is the `StateRegulator`'s act on a receipt still in `ISSUED` status, and the tolerance is exactly zero cents — this is an exact-match reconciliation. When `discrepancy_par_cents` is zero **and** `reported_asset_count` equals `ledger_asset_count`, the receipt moves to `CONFIRMED`; when either the discrepancy is non-zero or the counts disagree, it moves to `EXCEPTION`. Both outcomes stamp `confirmed_at` from the clock, record the confirming regulator's `X-User-ID` as `confirmed_by_user_id`, and increment the receipt's `version`. Note the boundary case that follows from the empty-set rule: a receipt against an agreement holding no active allocations, reporting zero par and zero assets, **confirms** — it does not raise an exception. An exception is recorded on the receipt alone; no depository field changes as a result, and in particular the depository's documentation component is untouched until the next mark-to-market run recomputes it. `CONFIRMED` and `EXCEPTION` are both terminal. Receipts are listable under their agreement with an optional `confirmation_status` filter.

Deposit balances at a public unit are not random; they surge when property taxes land and drain when payroll and debt service go out. A `PublicUnitTreasurer` plans for those swings by filing a **runoff forecast** on one of its own accounts, supplying the `deposit_account_id`, a `cycle_label` of `TAX_COLLECTION`, `DISBURSEMENT` or `DEBT_SERVICE`, a `basis` of `PEAK_WINDOW` or `PERIOD_END`, the `horizon_start` and `horizon_end` bounding the plan, the `projected_windows` array itself, and `expected_deposit_account_version` as described in §2.6.

`projected_windows` is an ordered, non-empty array of objects, each carrying `window_start`, `window_end` and `projected_balance_cents`. Each window is half-open, including its start and excluding its end, and the chain must be exactly contiguous and exactly coextensive with the horizon: every window's `window_start` must be strictly before its `window_end`, each window's `window_end` must equal the next window's `window_start` with no gaps and no overlaps, the first window's `window_start` must equal `horizon_start`, and the last window's `window_end` must equal `horizon_end`. Any break in that chain is refused with `ERR_FORECAST_WINDOW_DISCONTINUITY`, while a horizon whose `horizon_start` is at or after its `horizon_end` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

Projection freezes the account's **current** `required_collateral_pct_bps` into the forecast and applies it window by window. For each window the projected uninsured amount is `projected_balance_cents` less the 25,000,000-cent insurance coverage limit, floored at zero; the window's projected requirement is that uninsured figure multiplied by the frozen percentage, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. The rounding happens per window, **before** any window is selected. The forecast's `projected_peak_balance_cents` is the largest `projected_balance_cents` across the windows.

The `basis` then selects between **two variants** for `projected_required_collateral_cents`. Under `PEAK_WINDOW` the forecast takes the largest per-window projected requirement in the horizon, on the theory that the treasury must be collateralised for its worst moment, so a mid-horizon tax-collection spike sets the plan. Under `PERIOD_END` the forecast takes the projected requirement of the chronologically last window — the one with the greatest `window_start`, and among ties the one with the greatest `window_end` — on the theory that the treasury plans to its closing position; a spike that has already run off by the end of the horizon is therefore invisible, and this variant's projected requirement is never higher than the peak variant's.

The capacity side is identical under both variants. `unpledged_capacity_cents` is the haircut value of the depository's *free* collateral: for every collateral asset of the account's depository whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE` and whose `available_par_cents` is greater than zero, the service applies the same two-step rounding — available par multiplied by `market_price_bps` divided by 10000 and rounded half up, then that figure multiplied by 10000 less `haircut_bps`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up again — and sums the per-asset results. Allocations are not consulted at all here; only unpledged par counts, because already-pledged collateral cannot be pledged again. `pledge_capacity_gap_cents` is then the projected requirement less that capacity, floored at zero, and it is the number a treasurer takes to the bank: if it is positive, the bank does not currently own enough free collateral to cover the forecast cycle.

A filed forecast begins at `status` `OPEN` with `requires_reforecast` false and `superseded_at` null. Filing is also a supersession: any prior forecast for the same account and the same `cycle_label` that stands in `OPEN` or `STALE` status is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the clock, and when the superseded forecast was `STALE`, its `requires_reforecast` flag is cleared back to false as it is retired. Because a forecast is a claim about an account, filing one increments that `DepositAccount`'s `version` by 1. The other way a forecast leaves `OPEN` is the suspension cascade of §3.1: when the owning public unit's `exposure_status` becomes `ESCALATED`, every one of that unit's `OPEN` forecasts moves to `STALE` with `requires_reforecast` set true, which is the system telling the treasurer that the plan rests on assumptions that no longer hold. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. Forecasts are listable under their account with optional `status` and `cycle_label` filters, and each is readable individually.
