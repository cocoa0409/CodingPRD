# Commercial Fitness Equipment Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A commercial fitness equipment manufacturer lives in the gap between two very different clocks. Engineering measures time in cycle counts: a strength frame welded, powder-coated and upholstered this quarter must survive hundreds of thousands of repetitions on a durability rig before anyone is allowed to sell it. Commercial operations measure time in gym openings: a chain wants forty treadmills, twenty racks and a wall of connected consoles delivered into a numbered floor layout on a specific weekend, installed by a trained partner crew, invoiced against a credit line, and then serviced for years afterwards. Today those two clocks are reconciled by spreadsheets, e-mail threads and a serial-number log that nobody trusts. This API replaces all of it with a single system of record.

The system covers the full arc of a product: catalogue definition across cardio, strength and connected equipment; engineering revisions that accumulate durability evidence on shared test rigs and loop back into rework when a specimen fails early; console firmware builds whose connectivity certification is signed off by somebody other than the engineer who submitted it; order configuration with powder-coat, upholstery and branding options priced per line; delivery and installation coordinated against a service partner's calendar and the facility's blackout days; serialized asset registration that carries warranty and service history for the life of the unit; preventive maintenance programs sized from real usage telemetry and settled against a partner's committed hours and spare-parts stock; and finally trade-in, refurbishment and resale into the secondary market.

Two commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that a connected console's firmware support horizon is a contractual promise, not a marketing statement: once a revision's sunset is announced, the support commitment runs for a fixed number of days, and any unit whose mechanical warranty outlives that horizon is flagged as an exposure the business can quantify. The second is segregation of duties: no single actor may both create an artefact and sign it off, and nobody who prices an order may also close it in the field. The specification below is written for the engineers who will build this service, and it is deliberately explicit about arithmetic, ordering and boundary inclusivity, because in this domain a rounding difference of one cent per line multiplied across five hundred units is a real invoice dispute.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelopes, pagination, the logical clock, common properties, precision, concurrency, the validation ladder, referential resolution, cascade discipline and the conserved ledgers.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role Scope

Every request to the API — without exception, including the read-only projections and the clock control endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names something the system does not recognise, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The system recognises exactly six roles, spelled precisely as follows: `ProductEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `OrderPlanner`, `FacilityManager`, `ServicePartnerTech`, and `ServiceOpsAdmin`. Each endpoint is authorised for a specific subset of these roles, listed in §4; a caller whose role is not on an endpoint's list is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

Four of the six roles are operator identities and two are resource-backed identities, and the distinction determines whether `X-User-ID` participates in ownership checks.

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProductEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it is stamped into `submitted_by_user_id` on a firmware certification when a `ProductEngineer` submits one — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. `ProductEngineer` owns product development: models, engineering revisions, durability testing, firmware builds and firmware loading. It may not release, sunset, certify or withdraw anything.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `certified_by_user_id` when the officer certifies a firmware pairing, and as `submitted_by_user_id` if the officer is the one who submitted the certification, but it is not compared to any entity's `id`. `ComplianceOfficer` is the gatekeeper: it releases qualified revisions, certifies and rejects firmware/console pairings, announces sunsets, withdraws builds and declares obsolescence.
- When `X-User-Role` is `OrderPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. `OrderPlanner` owns commercial configuration and money: orders, lines, quoting, confirmation, production release, installation scheduling, trade-in credit, settlement and the secondary market.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceOpsAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`. `ServiceOpsAdmin` runs the service network: partner onboarding, parts distribution, preventive-maintenance program lifecycle, refurbishment intake and scrapping.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facility`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Facility` compare the resource's facility foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: the facility path identifier itself, an order's `facility_id`, a preventive-maintenance program's `facility_id`, or the `facility_id` of the order behind an install job. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). A `FacilityManager` is a gym customer: it may configure, line up and confirm its own orders, request a maintenance program, and read only what belongs to its own facility.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ServicePartnerTech`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServicePartner`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `ServicePartner` compare the resource's partner foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: an install job's `service_partner_id`, a program's `service_partner_id`, or the servicing partner behind a maintenance visit or an asset. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). A `ServicePartnerTech` is a field technician: it owns on-site execution, usage reporting and preventive-maintenance delivery for its own partner only.

Segregation of duties is enforced on the firmware certification sign-off. The `ComplianceOfficer` who certifies or rejects a certification must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded when that certification was created; if they are the same string, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` counts all rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current window.

Ordering is deterministic and must not depend on insertion into a hash map or on database default order. Every list endpoint returns rows ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the row `id` ascending as the tie-break when two rows share a creation timestamp. The serialized asset listing is the single documented exception to the tie-break: it orders by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence_no` ascending, so the units minted from an order's first line appear before the units minted from its second.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — warranty horizons, firmware support windows, training currency, trade-in ageing, obsolescence eligibility — reads a single server-side logical clock rather than the wall clock of the host machine. The clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which is a Saturday, and it never advances on its own. It moves only when the clock-control capability described in §4 is invoked with a target instant supplied as `to_iso8601`; that operation returns the newly effective `current_utc`. Every role may drive the clock, because deterministic test-driving of long-horizon commitments would otherwise be impossible.

Two consequences deserve stating plainly. First, "expired" always means expired relative to the logical clock, so a support horizon that has not been passed by the clock is still live no matter how long the server has been running. Second, all instants persisted by the system — `qualified_at`, `released_at`, `sunset_announced_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `certified_at`, `quoted_at`, `completed_at`, `warranty_starts_at`, `last_revised_at` and every derived horizon — are stamped from the logical clock, in UTC. Every one of those server-stamped instants is rendered in exactly the same ISO 8601 form as `created_at` in §2.5 — UTC, `Z`-suffixed, with exactly three fractional digits — so an instant that falls on a whole second reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; the `current_utc` returned by the clock-control endpoint is rendered the same way. Instants that arrive from a client rather than from the clock — a durability run's and an install job's `window_start` and `window_end`, an order's `requested_install_window_start` and `requested_install_window_end`, a service partner's `training_current_until` and a maintenance visit's `scheduled_date` — are stored and echoed back exactly as the client supplied them; they are parsed for comparison but never re-rendered.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this system — equipment models, engineering revisions, durability test rigs and runs, firmware releases and certifications, facilities, orders, order lines, service partners, install jobs, serialized assets, maintenance programs and maintenance visits — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first created, taken from the logical clock.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the row.

There are no exceptions in this domain: all fourteen resource types carry all four properties, including the ones a client can never create directly. A mutation is any change to a stored field, and that emphatically includes changes to derived fields. When an order's `line_subtotal_cents` is recomputed because a line was voided, when a partner's `committed_hours` is recomputed because an install job aborted, or when an asset's `connectivity_support_status` flips because a build was withdrawn, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented exactly as if a client had edited it directly. This matters because `version` is also the token used for the concurrency checks in §2.7: a cascade that touches a facility will invalidate a client's pinned facility version.

The `serial_number` of a serialized asset is the one identifier in the system that is deliberately *not* random. It is composed from the as-built revision's `revision_code`, a hyphen, and the asset's `sequence_no` rendered as a decimal integer left-padded with zeros to five digits — so the fourth unit ever built of revision `TRX-9A` is `TRX-9A-00004`. Serial numbers are reproducible by construction, which is what makes warranty claims auditable years later.

### 2.6. Precision: Cents, Hours, Cycles and Days

Money is never represented as a floating-point quantity of currency. Every monetary field in this system is an integer count of cents, and every intermediate product is computed with exact decimal arithmetic before being rounded. Where rounding is required, the method is always round half up — a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero, so 12.5 cents becomes 13 cents. The specification states, for each pricing step, whether rounding happens at that step; steps that are not named as rounding points must carry their exact value forward, because rounding twice where the specification rounds once produces off-by-one-cent invoices.

Committed labour hours are decimal quantities carried to two decimal places. A preventive-maintenance program's `annual_labor_hours_committed` is rounded half up to two decimal places, and that rounding is applied once, to the total over all covered assets, not to each asset's contribution. Capacity comparisons on hours are exact decimal comparisons. Chargeable shipping weight is likewise a decimal quantity with two decimal places.

Cycle counts are whole numbers of cycles. Where a cycle contribution is extrapolated by a factor, the extrapolated contribution is truncated toward zero to a whole cycle — that is, the fractional part is discarded — and the contributions are then added as integers. Support and warranty horizons are measured in whole days, and where a day count is derived from the difference between two instants the fractional day is likewise discarded.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Superseding Writes

Three write operations in this system supersede a snapshot that the client must prove it actually read. Each of them therefore requires a version-pinning field in the request body. The field is **not optional** on those three operations: omitting it is a schema violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every other write endpoint in the API takes no version field at all, and supplying one is simply ignored by the pinning machinery.

- Creating an engineering revision supersedes the owning model's revision set, so the caller must prove it read the current catalogue row. The request carries `expected_equipment_model_version` and the server compares it to the owning `EquipmentModel` row's current `version`. If the two differ — because some other writer has advanced the model since the client last read it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) and no revision is persisted. On success the owning model's `version` is incremented by one.
- Quoting an order supersedes the order's previous pricing snapshot, so the caller must prove it priced the configuration it actually read. The request carries `expected_order_version` and the server compares it to the `Order` row's current `version`. On a mismatch the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) and the previous quote is left exactly as it was. On success the order's `version` is incremented by one.
- Applying trade-in credit to an order supersedes the facility's credit-ledger snapshot, so the caller must prove it read the current balance. The request carries `expected_facility_version` and the server compares it to the `Facility` row's current `version`. On a mismatch the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) and the ledger is left untouched. On success the facility's `version` is incremented by one.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

Checks are evaluated in one strict, globally ordered ladder. The **first** check that fails aborts the request immediately: no later check runs, no side effect of any kind is persisted, and the response carries exactly that check's error identifier and status. The ordering is normative because several checks can fail on the same request and only one may surface. Ordered from first to last, the ladder is:

1. **Authentication.** Both context headers present and non-empty, with `X-User-Role` naming a known role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The caller's role appears on the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the request path is resolved, left to right, and the first one that does not resolve produces that resource family's 404 code (§2.9). Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
4. **Facility scope.** A `FacilityManager` caller's `X-User-ID` equals the facility in scope — otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `FacilityManager`.
5. **Partner scope.** A `ServicePartnerTech` caller's `X-User-ID` equals the service partner in scope — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `ServicePartnerTech`.
6. **Segregation of duties.** On firmware-certification certify and on firmware-certification reject — those two operations only — the acting officer differs from the submitter, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
7. **Schema.** Required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values within range, and every numeric bound respected: `quantity` at least 1 and at most 500, `reserved_units` at least 0 and at most 50, `parallel_station_count` at least 1 and at most 8, `visit_interval_days` at least 30 and at most 365, arrays that must be non-empty are non-empty, arrays contain no duplicate members, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that accepts a body or query parameters.
8. **Window sanity.** Wherever a pair of window instants is supplied — a durability run's reservation window, an install job's window, or an order's requested install window — the end must be strictly after the start, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (422). Applies to exactly three creation operations: order creation, durability-run creation and install-job creation.
9. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key carried in the body resolves to an existing row, otherwise that resource family's 404 code (§2.9). Applies to every endpoint carrying a foreign key in its body.
10. **Version pinning.** The supplied `expected_equipment_model_version`, `expected_order_version` or `expected_facility_version` equals the current parent `version` per §2.7, otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly three operations: revision creation, order quoting and trade-in-credit application.
11. **Declared transition.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Three creation operations carry the same class of gate and report the same code: creating a durability run requires the referenced revision to be `IN_TESTING`; creating an install job requires the referenced order to have `config_status` of `CONFIRMED` with `fulfillment_status` either `IN_PRODUCTION` or `STAGED`; creating a maintenance visit requires the referenced program to be `ACTIVE`.
12. **Order is quoted.** Confirming an order requires its `billing_status` to be `QUOTED`; because any configuration change since the last quote resets it to `UNQUOTED`, a stale configuration surfaces here as `ERR_ORDER_NOT_QUOTED` (409). Applies to order confirmation.
13. **Revision released.** A new order line's referenced revision has `status` `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_NOT_RELEASED` (409). Applies to order-line creation.
14. **Firmware certified.** Releasing a revision of a connected model requires at least one `CERTIFIED` certification for that revision, and loading firmware onto a connected asset requires a `CERTIFIED`, non-withdrawn build for that asset's revision. Either failure is `ERR_FIRMWARE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409). Applies to those two operations.
15. **Cycle evidence.** Qualifying a revision requires its `verified_cycle_count` to be greater than or equal to its `min_required_cycles` and requires no durability run of that revision to be outstanding in `RESERVED` or `RUNNING`; either failure is `ERR_CYCLE_TARGET_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to revision qualification.
16. **Rework budget.** `rework_count` is strictly less than the rework limit, otherwise `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to revision rework.
17. **Partner training currency.** The service partner's `training_current_until` is strictly after the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_TRAINING_EXPIRED` (409). This guards six operations: install-job creation, install-job arrival, program activation, program revision, maintenance-visit creation and maintenance-visit completion.
18. **Partner category certification.** The partner's `certified_categories` covers every equipment category involved, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_CERTIFIED_FOR_CATEGORY` (422). This guards three operations: install-job creation (the categories present on the order), program activation and program revision (the categories present among the facility's in-service assets).
19. **Console support live.** A connected asset may be refurbishment-certified or resold only while its `firmware_support_expires_at` is null or strictly after the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_FIRMWARE_SUPPORT_EXPIRED` (409). This guards exactly two operations: refurbishment certification and resale.
20. **Support commitment lapsed.** A revision may be marked obsolete only once its `firmware_support_until` is at or before the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_SUPPORT_WINDOW_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to marking a revision obsolete.
21. **Finish availability.** Every requested finish code is a member of the model's `allowed_finish_codes`, otherwise `ERR_FINISH_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Applies to order-line creation.
22. **Console platform compatibility.** The firmware build's `console_platform` equals the revision's model `console_platform`, and the model must be connected; otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_INCOMPATIBLE` (422). This guards two operations: firmware-certification creation and firmware loading onto an asset.
23. **Certification uniqueness.** No existing certification with `cert_status` of `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED` already covers the same build-and-revision pair, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION` (409). Applies to firmware-certification creation.
24. **Naming uniqueness.** A revision's `revision_code` is unique among its own model's revisions whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`; an equipment model `name`, a test rig `name`, a service partner `name` and a firmware release `version_string` are each globally unique. Any collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVISION_CODE` (409). This guards five creation operations: revision creation, equipment-model creation, test-rig creation, service-partner creation and firmware-release creation.
25. **Supersession graph integrity.** `supersedes_model_id` must not point at the model being created, must not close a cycle in the supersession graph, and the resulting chain must be at most the depth cap deep; otherwise `ERR_SUPERSEDE_CYCLE` (422). Applies to equipment-model creation.
26. **Rig duty class.** The chosen rig's `supported_duty_classes` contains the revision's model `duty_class`, otherwise `ERR_RIG_DUTY_CLASS_UNSUPPORTED` (422). Applies to durability-run creation.
27. **Asset coverage.** The serviced asset's `facility_id` equals the program's `facility_id` and the asset's `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COVERED` (422). Applies to maintenance-visit creation.
28. **Facility active.** The facility in scope has `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED` (409). This guards six operations: order creation, order-line creation, order quoting, order confirmation, program activation and asset resale (where the facility in scope is the buyer).
29. **Order has lines.** The order has at least one line with `status` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES` (422). This guards three operations: order quoting, order confirmation and install-job creation.
30. **One open install job.** The order has no other install job in `SCHEDULED`, `ON_SITE` or `PUNCH_LIST`, otherwise `ERR_INSTALL_JOB_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). Applies to install-job creation.
31. **Blackout weekdays.** Neither endpoint date of the requested install window falls on a blacked-out weekday for the facility, otherwise `ERR_INSTALL_BLACKOUT_DAY` (422). Applies to install-job creation.
32. **Partner calendar conflict.** The requested install window, extended by the travel buffer, does not intersect another open job of the same partner, otherwise `ERR_INSTALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to install-job creation.
33. **Rig station capacity.** The rig has a free station across the requested reservation window, otherwise `ERR_RIG_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to durability-run creation.
34. **Partner hour capacity.** The partner's `committed_hours` plus the load being added is less than or equal to its `certified_hours_capacity`, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). This guards three operations: install-job creation, program activation and program revision.
35. **Spare stock sufficiency.** The partner's `spare_stock_on_hand_units` is greater than or equal to the units the visit wants to hold, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK` (409). Applies to maintenance-visit creation, and to program revision when held visits must re-reserve their units.
36. **Credit limit.** The facility's `open_order_exposure_cents` plus the order's `quoted_total_cents` is less than or equal to its `credit_limit_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to order confirmation.
37. **Trade-in credit availability.** The requested `amount_cents` is at least 1, at most the facility's `tradein_credit_balance_cents`, and at most the order's outstanding total, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRADEIN_CREDIT` (402). Applies to trade-in-credit application.
38. **Trade-in reversibility.** Reversing a trade-in requires the facility's `tradein_credit_balance_cents` to still be at least the asset's `trade_in_valuation_cents`, otherwise `ERR_TRADEIN_CREDIT_ALREADY_APPLIED` (409). Applies to trade-in reversal.

A worked consequence of the ordering: if a `FacilityManager` sends a confirmation for an order belonging to another facility *and* that other facility is suspended *and* the order has no active line, the response is `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, because scope (rung 4) precedes suspension (rung 28) and line presence (rung 29). Likewise a stale `expected_order_version` on a quote surfaces as `ERR_STALE_VERSION` even when the order also has no active line, because version pinning sits at rung 10 and the line check at rung 29.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and resolution stops at the first identifier that does not exist. Body-borne foreign keys are resolved after the schema check, in the same all-or-nothing spirit: an unresolvable reference means the request never reaches business logic. Each resource family has its own 404 identifier, and several families deliberately share one:

- `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` — no equipment model with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_REVISION_NOT_FOUND` — no engineering revision with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_TEST_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` — no durability test rig and no durability test run with the referenced identifier; the testing family shares one code.
- `ERR_FIRMWARE_NOT_FOUND` — no firmware release and no firmware certification with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` — no facility with the referenced identifier, including a `buyer_facility_id` supplied on resale.
- `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` — no order and no order line with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` — no service partner with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_INSTALL_JOB_NOT_FOUND` — no install job with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` — no serialized asset with the referenced identifier.
- `ERR_SERVICE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` — no preventive-maintenance program and no maintenance visit with the referenced identifier.

Relationships are never declared to clients as schema; they are exercised through the payloads described in §3. Filters on list endpoints follow the same naming as the underlying foreign keys, so a caller may narrow a listing by `equipment_model_id`, `model_revision_id`, `test_rig_id`, `firmware_release_id`, `order_id`, `order_line_id`, `installed_firmware_release_id`, `facility_id`, `service_partner_id`, `pm_program_id`, `serialized_asset_id`, `supersedes_model_id`, or by the relevant status field: `status`, `cert_status`, `lifecycle_status`, `config_status`, `fulfillment_status`, `billing_status`, `connectivity_support_status` and `category`. A filter value that is not a legal member of the corresponding enumeration is a schema violation.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Many operations in this domain are not local. Announcing a sunset touches assets, programs and visits; withdrawing a firmware build touches certifications, assets, programs, visits and partner rollups; completing an install job touches the order's fulfilment and billing dimensions, every asset built for it, the programs at that facility and the installing partner's capacity. Three rules govern all of it.

First, cascades are immediate and complete within the request that triggers them. There is no deferred queue, no eventual reconciliation and no background sweep. When the specification in §3 says that a transition also moves child rows, those child rows are moved before the response is written, and the response reflects the post-cascade world.

Second, a cascade is atomic with its trigger. If any rung of the ladder in §2.8 fails — including a precondition that only becomes visible partway through a cascade, such as a held visit that cannot re-reserve its spare units — nothing is persisted at all. The client sees a single error and a completely unchanged system.

Third, every row a cascade touches is mutated in the sense of §2.5: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented. Derived aggregates are recomputed from their definitions rather than adjusted by hand wherever the specification gives a definition, which is why the same numbers are described in §3 as sums over a stated row set rather than as increments. The counters `released_revision_count`, `occupied_station_count`, `certified_revision_count`, `assets_minted_count`, `covered_asset_count`, `service_history_count` and `accrued_service_labor_hours` are all derived in this sense, as are `open_order_exposure_cents`, `committed_hours` and `coverage_status`.

### 2.11. Conserved Ledgers

Two quantities in this system are ledgers, and both are protected by a conservation invariant that must hold after every single mutation.

The **trade-in credit ledger** lives on a facility. `tradein_credit_granted_cents` is the sum of `trade_in_valuation_cents` across that facility's serialized assets whose trade-in has not been reversed — equivalently, across those assets whose `trade_in_valuation_cents` is not null. `tradein_credit_applied_cents` is the sum of `tradein_credit_applied_cents` across that facility's orders. `tradein_credit_balance_cents` is the granted total minus the applied total. No rounding occurs anywhere in this ledger; it is a pure sum of stored integers. After every mutation the balance must be greater than or equal to zero, and the granted total must equal the applied total plus the balance. An operation that would drive the balance negative is refused outright, never silently clamped to zero.

The **spare stock ledger** lives on a service partner. `spare_stock_received_units` is the total of the `units` figures across every parts receipt posted to that partner. `spare_stock_reserved_units` is the sum of `reserved_units` over that partner's maintenance visits whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. `spare_stock_consumed_units` is the sum of `reserved_units` over visits whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. `spare_stock_on_hand_units` is the received total minus the reserved total minus the consumed total. Visits in `ON_HOLD` and visits in `CANCELLED` hold nothing at all. Units are whole numbers with no rounding. After every mutation the on-hand figure must be greater than or equal to zero, and the received total must equal the on-hand total plus the reserved total plus the consumed total.

### 2.12. Two Interval Geometries

The system books two kinds of shared capacity against time windows, and — because the underlying physical constraints genuinely differ — the two use deliberately different interval geometries. A partner install crew needs to drive between sites, so an install job's occupation is half-open at the end and padded with a travel buffer, and two jobs that merely touch at the padded boundary do not collide. A durability rig, by contrast, has to be rigged and unrigged around a specimen, so a run's occupation is treated as closed at both ends and two runs that merely touch at a boundary *do* collide; the rig also has more than one station, so the rig test is an aggregate-capacity count rather than a pairwise exclusion. The exact arithmetic of each geometry is given with the workflow it belongs to, in §3.2 for rigs and §3.7 for install crews. Implementers should not attempt to share one interval-overlap helper between the two.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Catalogue: Models, Supersession and Engineering Revisions

A `ProductEngineer` opens the catalogue by registering an **equipment model**, the commercial product a customer will eventually buy. Registration takes the marketing `name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and globally unique among models), the `category` — exactly one of `CARDIO`, `STRENGTH` or `CONNECTED` — the `duty_class`, which is one of `LIGHT_COMMERCIAL`, `FULL_COMMERCIAL` or `INSTITUTIONAL`, the base `unit_list_price_cents` (a strictly positive integer, before any finish surcharge or duty uplift), the per-unit chargeable `shipping_weight_kg` (a strictly positive number carried to two decimals), and `allowed_finish_codes`, a non-empty list drawn without repetition from `GRAPHITE`, `ARCTIC_WHITE`, `CLUB_RED`, `CUSTOM_BRAND_DECAL` and `PREMIUM_UPHOLSTERY`. Those five codes are the powder-coat, upholstery and branding options the factory can actually run for that frame.

Two optional inputs complete the registration. `console_platform` names the console family and takes one of `CONSOLE_T1`, `CONSOLE_T2` or `CONSOLE_X`; it is required and must be non-null when the `category` is `CONNECTED`, and it must be null for every other category. From the category the server derives the boolean `connected`, which is true exactly when the `category` is `CONNECTED`; that single flag gates firmware certification, the firmware support commitment and the unsupported-console penalty on trade-in. `supersedes_model_id` optionally names the catalogue model this one replaces. The supersession graph must stay acyclic: a model may not supersede itself, may not close a cycle through any chain of predecessors, and the resulting chain may be at most five edges long. Violating any of the three conditions yields `ERR_SUPERSEDE_CYCLE`.

A model's `lifecycle_status` is never supplied by a client; it is derived from its own revisions and takes one of `PRE_RELEASE`, `ACTIVE`, `SUNSET` or `OBSOLETE`. A freshly registered model is `PRE_RELEASE`. The derivation, re-run on every status change of any of the model's revisions, considers the model's revisions whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, and picks the first matching case in this order: if that set is empty, or if no revision in it has reached `RELEASED`, `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED` or `OBSOLETE`, the model is `PRE_RELEASE`; otherwise, if any revision in the set is `RELEASED`, the model is `ACTIVE`; otherwise, if any revision in the set is `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED`, the model is `SUNSET`; otherwise — every remaining revision being `OBSOLETE` — the model is `OBSOLETE`. A model whose revisions have all been driven to `SUPERSEDED` by a successor model is also `SUNSET`. Alongside it, `released_revision_count` counts the model's own revisions whose `status` is `RELEASED`, `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED` or `OBSOLETE`.

Engineering work happens on **model revisions**, and creating one is the first of the three version-pinned writes described in §2.7: the payload names the owning `equipment_model_id`, the engineering `revision_code` (1 to 40 characters), the `target_cycle_count` the revision is being tested to (at least 1000 cycles), an optional `firmware_baseline_version` string identifying the console firmware baseline (which stays null for models that are not connected), and the mandatory `expected_equipment_model_version`. The `revision_code` must be unique among the owning model's revisions whose `status` is not `SUPERSEDED`, so an engineering code freed up by supersession may be reused; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVISION_CODE`. At creation the server derives `min_required_cycles` from the owning model's `duty_class` — this is the qualification gate, and it is 250,000 cycles for `LIGHT_COMMERCIAL`, 500,000 cycles for `FULL_COMMERCIAL` and 900,000 cycles for `INSTITUTIONAL`. A new revision begins at `status` `DRAFT` with `rework_count` at 0, `verified_cycle_count` and `projected_cycle_count` both at 0, and `qualified_at`, `released_at`, `sunset_announced_at` and `firmware_support_until` all null.

A revision's `status` walks the sequence `DRAFT`, `IN_TESTING`, `TEST_FAILED`, `QUALIFIED`, `RELEASED`, `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED`, `OBSOLETE`, `SUPERSEDED`, of which `OBSOLETE` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal. Submitting a revision for testing moves it `DRAFT → IN_TESTING`; the owning model's `lifecycle_status` must not already be `OBSOLETE`, and if the owning model is connected its `console_platform` must be non-null. Failing either precondition is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The submission takes no body.

The rework loop is the cyclic part of this machine and it exists because a frame that cracks at 180,000 cycles gets welded differently and tested again. A `ProductEngineer` may rework a revision from either `IN_TESTING → DRAFT` or `TEST_FAILED → DRAFT`, supplying a `rework_note` describing the design change. Both branches carry identical preconditions and identical effects. The preconditions are that `rework_count` is strictly less than three completed rework loops — a fourth request is `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — and that the revision has no durability run outstanding in `RESERVED` or `RUNNING`, an outstanding run making the transition undeclared and therefore `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The effects are that `rework_count` increases by one, and that every one of the revision's own `COMPLETED` durability runs has its `superseded_by_rework` flag set to true. Because a superseded run is excluded from both cycle-count variants forever, `verified_cycle_count` and `projected_cycle_count` both fall back to 0 — which is precisely the point: a reworked revision re-accumulates its durability evidence from zero rather than inheriting the history of a design that failed.

### 3.2. Durability Testing: Rigs, Runs and Cycle Evidence

Durability evidence is accumulated on **test rigs**, the physical test cells on the lab floor. A rig is registered with a `name` (1 to 80 characters, globally unique), a `parallel_station_count` between 1 and 8 stating how many specimens it can cycle at once, and `supported_duty_classes`, a non-empty list drawn without repetition from `LIGHT_COMMERCIAL`, `FULL_COMMERCIAL` and `INSTITUTIONAL`. The rig also exposes `occupied_station_count`, derived as the number of its own runs currently in `RESERVED` or `RUNNING`.

Booking a **durability test run** names the `model_revision_id` under test, the `test_rig_id` to be reserved, the reservation `window_start` and `window_end`, and the `target_cycles` the run is scheduled to accumulate (at least 1000). The referenced revision must be `IN_TESTING`, the window end must be strictly after the window start, and the rig's `supported_duty_classes` must contain the revision's model `duty_class` — a mismatch there is `ERR_RIG_DUTY_CLASS_UNSUPPORTED`. A run's reservation window is **closed and inclusive at both ends**: for the same rig, the server counts existing runs in `RESERVED` or `RUNNING` whose window intersects the requested one under the inclusive test — the requested start is at or before the existing end, and the existing start is at or before the requested end — and refuses the booking with `ERR_RIG_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that count has already reached the rig's `parallel_station_count`. A new window that starts exactly when an existing window ends therefore *does* collide, which is the deliberate opposite of the install-crew geometry in §3.7. A successful booking creates the run at `status` `RESERVED` with `outcome` `PENDING`, `achieved_cycles` 0, `failure_cycle_count` null and `superseded_by_rework` false, and raises the rig's `occupied_station_count`.

A run's `status` moves through `RESERVED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, the last two being terminal. Starting a run advances it `RESERVED → RUNNING` and requires the owning revision to still be `IN_TESTING`; the side effect is a recomputation of the revision's `projected_cycle_count`, which now picks up the running specimen's extrapolated contribution. Completing a run advances it `RUNNING → COMPLETED` and carries the reported `outcome`, which is `PASSED` or `FAILED`, together with either `achieved_cycles` or `failure_cycle_count`. A `PASSED` completion requires `achieved_cycles` of at least 1 and requires `failure_cycle_count` to be omitted entirely; a `FAILED` completion requires `failure_cycle_count` of at least 1 and no greater than the run's `target_cycles`. A body that breaks either shape is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Completion records the outcome and the corresponding cycle figure, lowers the rig's `occupied_station_count`, and recomputes both of the revision's cycle counts. Then — and only after that recount — the failure branch is evaluated: if the `outcome` was `FAILED`, the recomputed `verified_cycle_count` is still below `min_required_cycles`, and the revision is currently `IN_TESTING`, the revision moves `IN_TESTING → TEST_FAILED`. The ordering matters: a failure late in a long evidence chain that nonetheless leaves the revision above its gate does not fail the revision.

Aborting a run is permitted from either `RESERVED → ABORTED` or `RUNNING → ABORTED`, takes an `abort_reason`, and has no preconditions. Both branches lower the rig's `occupied_station_count`, so the reservation stops blocking overlapping windows. The `RUNNING → ABORTED` branch additionally recomputes the revision's `projected_cycle_count`, because an aborted run contributes nothing to either variant and its earlier extrapolated contribution must be withdrawn.

Cycle evidence is then summarised two ways, and the difference between them is the single most consequential piece of arithmetic in the engineering half of this system. Both variants read the same input set: the revision's durability runs whose `status` is `COMPLETED` or `RUNNING` **and** whose `superseded_by_rework` flag is false. Runs still in `RESERVED`, runs that were `ABORTED`, and runs superseded by a rework loop are excluded from both variants. Both variants walk that set in the same chronological order — by `window_end` ascending compared as instants rather than as strings, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and, should two runs share both, the row `id` ascending as the final deterministic tie-break — and both start an accumulator at zero.

The **verified** variant produces `verified_cycle_count`, and it is the qualification gate. Walking the ordered list, a run whose `status` is `RUNNING` contributes nothing; a run whose `outcome` is `PASSED` contributes its `achieved_cycles`; and a run whose `outcome` is `FAILED` contributes its `failure_cycle_count` and then **halts the walk entirely**. Evidence recorded after the first chronological failure is not verified evidence: later passing runs are ignored until a rework loop clears the history. The **projected** variant produces `projected_cycle_count`, is advisory only, and never gates anything. It walks the same ordered list without any early stop: a `RUNNING` run contributes its `target_cycles` multiplied by four fifths, truncated to a whole cycle; a `PASSED` run contributes its `achieved_cycles` unchanged; and a `FAILED` run contributes half of its `failure_cycle_count`, likewise truncated to a whole cycle. Both accumulators are whole cycle counts.

A read-only qualification report is available for a revision, and it exposes both variants side by side so an engineer can see how much of the projection is real: it returns `verified_cycle_count`, `projected_cycle_count`, `min_required_cycles`, a `completed_run_count` and a `running_run_count` over the same non-superseded input set, and a boolean `qualifies` stating whether the verified figure has reached the gate. The report computes, but never persists a different answer from, the values stored on the revision.

Qualification itself moves a revision `IN_TESTING → QUALIFIED`, takes no body, and is available only to a `ProductEngineer`. It requires `verified_cycle_count` to be greater than or equal to `min_required_cycles` — the projected figure is explicitly *not* a substitute — and requires no run of the revision to be outstanding in `RESERVED` or `RUNNING`. Either failure is `ERR_CYCLE_TARGET_NOT_MET`. On success, `qualified_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

### 3.3. Console Firmware Builds and Connectivity Certification

Connected consoles run firmware, and firmware is versioned independently of the frames it ships on. A `ProductEngineer` registers a **firmware release** with a `version_string` (1 to 40 characters, globally unique), the `console_platform` it targets — `CONSOLE_T1`, `CONSOLE_T2` or `CONSOLE_X` — and an optional `mandatory_security` boolean that defaults to false and marks a security build that connected assets are expected to accept. A release begins at `status` `ACTIVE` with `certified_revision_count` at 0 and `withdrawn_at` null; `WITHDRAWN` is its only other and terminal state. Its `certified_revision_count` counts its own certifications whose `cert_status` is `CERTIFIED`.

Connectivity certification is the many-to-many junction between builds and revisions, and it is the place where segregation of duties bites. Either a `ProductEngineer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may submit a **firmware certification**, naming the `firmware_release_id`, the `model_revision_id` it is being certified against, and a `test_report_ref` of 1 to 80 characters pointing at the connectivity test report. The build's `console_platform` must equal the revision's model `console_platform` and that model must be connected, otherwise the submission is `ERR_PLATFORM_INCOMPATIBLE`; and no certification with `cert_status` `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED` may already exist for the same build-and-revision pair, otherwise it is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION`. Submission stamps `submitted_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` — this is the counterparty for the later duties check — and leaves `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` null. A certification's `cert_status` runs `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED`, `REJECTED` and `REVOKED`, with `REJECTED` and `REVOKED` terminal.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may sign off. Certifying moves `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` and requires three things: that the officer's `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; that the owning firmware release is still `ACTIVE`; and that the target revision's `status` is either `QUALIFIED` or `RELEASED`, a revision in any other state making the transition undeclared and therefore `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Success writes `certified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock, and raises the release's `certified_revision_count` by one. Rejecting moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, takes a `rejection_note`, and carries the same segregation-of-duties requirement as certifying and no other precondition; it has no side effect beyond the status change.

Withdrawing a build is the widest cascade in the system, and it is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `withdraw_reason`. The release must currently be `ACTIVE`; it moves `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN` and `withdrawn_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Then, in the same request, six levels of consequence run. Every one of the release's own certifications whose `cert_status` is `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → REVOKED`, each such move lowering the release's `certified_revision_count` by one; every certification of that release still in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Every serialized asset whose `installed_firmware_release_id` is this release has its `connectivity_support_status` set to `FIRMWARE_REVOKED`, its `firmware_support_expires_at` set to the release's `withdrawn_at`, and its `support_gap_days` recomputed accordingly. Every `ACTIVE` preventive-maintenance program covering any such asset moves `ACTIVE → NEEDS_REVISION` with `needs_revision_reason` set to `FIRMWARE_REVOKED`, and its `annual_labor_hours_committed` is recomputed. Every `SCHEDULED` maintenance visit belonging to those programs moves `SCHEDULED → ON_HOLD` with `held_reason` set to `PROGRAM_NEEDS_REVISION`, releasing its held spare units back to the partner's on-hand stock. Finally, each affected service partner's `committed_hours`, `coverage_status` and spare-stock figures are recomputed.

### 3.4. Release, Sunset and Obsolescence

A `ComplianceOfficer` — never the engineer who built it — releases a qualified revision, moving it `QUALIFIED → RELEASED` with no request body. If the owning model is connected, at least one of the revision's own certifications must be in `CERTIFIED`; without one the release is refused with `ERR_FIRMWARE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A non-connected model has no such requirement. Release stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, raises the owning model's `released_revision_count` by one and re-derives that model's `lifecycle_status`, which typically carries it from `PRE_RELEASE` to `ACTIVE`.

Release is also where the self-referential catalogue graph does its work. If the newly released revision's model carries a non-null `supersedes_model_id`, then every revision of that superseded predecessor model whose `status` is `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → SUPERSEDED` in the same request, and the predecessor model's `lifecycle_status` is then re-derived to `SUNSET`. Superseding a predecessor is thus a consequence of releasing a successor, not an operation a client asks for directly, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: the freed `revision_code` becomes available for reuse within its model, per §3.1.

Announcing a sunset is the point at which the firmware support commitment becomes contractual. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `sunset_reason` and the revision moves `RELEASED → SUNSET_ANNOUNCED`; the transition requires the revision to be `RELEASED`. The server stamps `sunset_announced_at` from the logical clock and sets `firmware_support_until` to exactly 1,825 days — five years — after that instant. The owning model's `lifecycle_status` is re-derived. Then the sunset propagates outward: every serialized asset of this revision whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` has its `firmware_support_expires_at`, `support_gap_days` and `connectivity_support_status` recomputed, landing on either `SUNSET_TRACKED` or `SUPPORT_GAP` per §3.7; every `ACTIVE` program covering any of those assets moves `ACTIVE → NEEDS_REVISION` with `needs_revision_reason` set to `CONSOLE_SUPPORT_LOST`; and every `SCHEDULED` visit of those programs moves `SCHEDULED → ON_HOLD` with `held_reason` `PROGRAM_NEEDS_REVISION`, releasing its held spare units.

Declaring obsolescence closes the revision out. It moves `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED → OBSOLETE`, takes no body, and is permitted only once the revision's `firmware_support_until` is at or before the current logical clock — the five-year commitment must actually have run out. An early attempt is refused with `ERR_SUPPORT_WINDOW_ACTIVE`. The only side effect is a re-derivation of the owning model's `lifecycle_status`, which reaches `OBSOLETE` once every non-superseded revision of the model has.

### 3.5. Configuring a Gym Floor: Facilities, Orders and Lines

A **facility** is a gym or a chain account, created by an `OrderPlanner` or a `ServiceOpsAdmin` with an `account_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `freight_zone` of `Z1`, `Z2`, `Z3` or `Z4`, a `credit_limit_cents` that is zero or greater and caps confirmed-but-unsettled exposure, and an optional `install_blackout_weekdays` list, which defaults to empty and draws without repetition from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU`, `FRI`, `SAT` and `SUN`. A facility starts at `status` `ACTIVE`; suspending it with a `suspend_reason` moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, and a `SUSPENDED` facility can no longer have orders created, lines added, quotes taken, orders confirmed, maintenance programs activated, or refurbished units resold to it — all of those surface `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. Alongside its inputs a facility exposes four derived money figures: `open_order_exposure_cents`, the sum of `quoted_total_cents` over its own orders whose `config_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `billing_status` is `QUOTED` or `INVOICED`; and the three trade-in ledger fields `tradein_credit_granted_cents`, `tradein_credit_applied_cents` and `tradein_credit_balance_cents` defined in §2.11.

An **order** is created by an `OrderPlanner` or by the facility's own `FacilityManager`, naming the `facility_id` and the facility-requested delivery window as `requested_install_window_start` and `requested_install_window_end`, the end strictly after the start. What makes an order unusual is that it carries three *independent* status dimensions rather than one lifecycle. `config_status` starts at `CONFIGURING` and reaches the terminal `CONFIRMED`. `fulfillment_status` starts at `PENDING` and walks `IN_PRODUCTION`, `STAGED`, `INSTALLING` to the terminal `INSTALLED`. `billing_status` starts at `UNQUOTED` and walks `QUOTED`, `INVOICED` to the terminal `SETTLED`. The three advance on different triggers, and several transitions in one dimension are driven automatically by a transition in another.

**Order lines** are the configuration itself. Adding a line — again an `OrderPlanner` or the owning `FacilityManager` — supplies the `model_revision_id` being configured, a `quantity` between 1 and 500 units, a `finish_codes` list, and a `layout_zone_label` of 1 to 40 characters naming the gym floor zone the units are destined for. The referenced revision must have `status` `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_NOT_RELEASED`: a facility may only buy equipment that has actually been signed off. The `finish_codes` list may be empty, must contain no duplicates, and every code in it must be a member of the owning model's `allowed_finish_codes`; a code outside that set is `ERR_FINISH_NOT_ALLOWED`. A line starts at `status` `ACTIVE` with `assets_minted_count` 0; voiding it moves it `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, and a voided line contributes nothing to any subsequent calculation.

Each line is priced at creation, and re-priced whenever the owning order is recomputed. Its `unit_price_cents` is the model's `unit_list_price_cents` plus the surcharges for the selected finishes, added together: `GRAPHITE` adds nothing, `ARCTIC_WHITE` adds 4,500 cents, `CLUB_RED` adds 7,500 cents, `CUSTOM_BRAND_DECAL` adds 12,000 cents and `PREMIUM_UPHOLSTERY` adds 26,000 cents. Its `extended_price_cents` is that unit price multiplied by the `quantity` and then multiplied by one plus the duty uplift for the model's `duty_class` — the uplift being zero for `LIGHT_COMMERCIAL`, six percent for `FULL_COMMERCIAL` and eleven percent for `INSTITUTIONAL` — with the result rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The order's `line_subtotal_cents` is then the sum of `extended_price_cents` over its lines whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Rounding **per line and only then summing** is normative: it can differ by whole cents from rounding a single aggregate product, and the per-line result is the figure that is stored and billed. The order's `chargeable_weight_kg` is computed in the same spirit, as the sum over active lines of the line's `quantity` multiplied by its model's `shipping_weight_kg`.

Adding a line or voiding a line while the order is still `CONFIGURING` invalidates any quote already taken. The order's `billing_status` moves `QUOTED → UNQUOTED` automatically, and in doing so `quoted_total_cents`, `install_amount_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `freight_cents` and `punch_list_credit_cents` are all reset to 0, `price_variant_applied` is reset to `NONE`, and `quoted_at` is reset to null. The recomputed `line_subtotal_cents` and `chargeable_weight_kg` are of course retained, since they describe the new configuration. A planner who changes the floor plan must therefore re-quote before the order can be confirmed; confirming a configuration whose quote has been invalidated surfaces `ERR_ORDER_NOT_QUOTED`.

### 3.6. Quoting, Confirmation and the Credit Gate

Quoting is exclusively an `OrderPlanner` action and it is version-pinned: the request body carries only `expected_order_version`, which must match the order's current `version` per §2.7. Quoting is permitted while `config_status` is `CONFIGURING`, and it is permitted twice: `UNQUOTED → QUOTED` for a first quote, and `QUOTED → QUOTED` for a re-quote after a configuration change, which recomputes and overwrites every provisional pricing field. The first-quote branch additionally requires the order to have at least one `ACTIVE` line, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`. Both branches increment the order's `version` by one on success, which is what makes a second quote issued against a stale pin fail with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Order totals are assembled in eight steps that run in exactly this pinned sequence, because every step consumes the previous step's output and no step may be reordered.

1. `line_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `extended_price_cents` across the order's `ACTIVE` lines, computed per §3.5.
2. An installation hours figure is determined — the one genuinely variant-specific input, described below — and `install_amount_cents` is that hours figure multiplied by the installation labour rate of 8,875 cents per crew hour (88.75 dollars an hour), rounded half up to a whole number of cents.
3. A discount base is formed as `line_subtotal_cents` plus `install_amount_cents`. It is exposed to clients as `discount_base_cents` on the read-only price breakdown.
4. `volume_discount_cents` is the discount base multiplied by the volume discount rate for the tier that base falls into, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The tiers are read on the discount base, each with an inclusive lower bound and an exclusive upper bound: from 0 up to 5,000,000 cents the rate is zero; from 5,000,000 up to 15,000,000 cents it is four percent; from 15,000,000 up to 40,000,000 cents it is seven and a half percent; and at 40,000,000 cents (400,000 dollars) or above it is eleven percent. Because the tier is selected on a base that *includes* installation labour, installation labour can push an order across a tier boundary — that is intended, not an accident of ordering.
5. A discounted base is formed as the discount base minus `volume_discount_cents`. It is exposed as `discounted_base_cents` on the price breakdown.
6. `freight_cents` is zero when the discounted base has reached 20,000,000 cents (200,000 dollars) or more — freight is waived on orders that large. Otherwise it is the order's `chargeable_weight_kg` multiplied by the per-kilogram rate for the facility's `freight_zone`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; the rates are 180 cents per kilogram for `Z1`, 260 for `Z2`, 340 for `Z3` and 520 for `Z4`.
7. `punch_list_credit_cents` is determined — the second variant-specific input.
8. The total is the discounted base plus `freight_cents` minus `punch_list_credit_cents`.

Rounding half up occurs at steps 2, 4 and 6 and nowhere else; steps 3, 5 and 8 are exact integer arithmetic on already-rounded operands.

The two variants differ only in steps 2 and 7, and in where the total lands. The **provisional** variant is what a quote writes. Its installation hours figure is an estimate: the sum over `ACTIVE` lines of the line's `quantity` multiplied by the per-unit installation hours for the model's `category`, which is 1.5 hours for `CARDIO`, 2.5 hours for `STRENGTH` and 3.0 hours for `CONNECTED`. Its `punch_list_credit_cents` is 0, because no install job has reported yet. The total is stored in `quoted_total_cents`, `final_total_cents` stays null, `price_variant_applied` is set to `PROVISIONAL`, and `quoted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The **final** variant is written automatically when the install job completes, as described in §3.7. Its installation hours figure is the completed install job's `actual_labor_hours` — real crew time, not the estimate. Its `punch_list_credit_cents` is 25,000 cents per open remediation item, that is 250.00 dollars multiplied by the job's `punch_list_open_count`. The total is stored in `final_total_cents` while `quoted_total_cents` is retained unchanged so that the provisional-to-final delta stays auditable, and `price_variant_applied` becomes `FINAL`.

A read-only price breakdown is available on an order for an `OrderPlanner`, the owning `FacilityManager` or a `ServiceOpsAdmin`. It restates the assembly step by step, returning `line_subtotal_cents`, `install_amount_cents`, `discount_base_cents`, `volume_discount_cents`, `discounted_base_cents`, `chargeable_weight_kg`, `freight_cents`, `punch_list_credit_cents`, `quoted_total_cents`, `final_total_cents`, `tradein_credit_applied_cents`, `amount_due_cents` and `price_variant_applied`.

Confirmation locks the configuration. It takes no body, is available to an `OrderPlanner` or the owning `FacilityManager`, and moves `config_status` `CONFIGURING → CONFIRMED`. Four preconditions apply: the `billing_status` must be `QUOTED` (`ERR_ORDER_NOT_QUOTED`), the facility's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`), the order must have at least one `ACTIVE` line (`ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`), and the credit gate must clear: the facility's `open_order_exposure_cents` plus this order's `quoted_total_cents` must be less than or equal to the facility's `credit_limit_cents`, with equality explicitly allowed. Exceeding the limit is `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. In the same request, `fulfillment_status` moves `PENDING → IN_PRODUCTION` — production is authorised by confirmation, not by a separate call — and the facility's `open_order_exposure_cents` is recomputed, which is where the newly confirmed order begins consuming the credit line.

Settlement closes the money out. An `OrderPlanner` supplies a `payment_reference` and `billing_status` moves `INVOICED → SETTLED`; the order must currently be `INVOICED`. The single side effect is a recomputation of the facility's `open_order_exposure_cents`, since a settled order no longer counts against the credit limit and the freed headroom immediately becomes available to the next confirmation.

### 3.7. Production, Serialization, Delivery and Installation

Once an order is in production, an `OrderPlanner` reports production complete, moving `fulfillment_status` `IN_PRODUCTION → STAGED`. This is the moment every physical unit acquires an identity. For each `ACTIVE` line, as many **serialized assets** are minted as the line's `quantity`, each starting at `status` `BUILT`. Each asset's `sequence_no` comes from a monotone counter kept per model revision, and the counter is consumed in a fixed order: lines are processed by their `created_at` ascending — and lines that share a `created_at`, which they do whenever the logical clock did not move between the calls that added them, are processed in the order they were added to the order rather than by `id`, so that the §2.3 guarantee holds — and within a line the units are numbered by ascending unit index. Each asset's `serial_number` is then composed as described in §2.5, from the revision's `revision_code`, a hyphen, and `sequence_no` zero-padded to five digits. Each asset records its as-built `model_revision_id`, the owning `facility_id`, the `order_line_id` it was built for, and — until firmware is loaded — a null `installed_firmware_release_id`. Its `connectivity_support_status` is initialised at this point: `NOT_APPLICABLE` for units of a model that is not connected, `SUPPORTED` for units of a connected model. Finally each `ACTIVE` line's `assets_minted_count` is set to the number of units minted from it.

An asset's `status` walks `BUILT`, `IN_SERVICE`, `TRADE_IN_RETURNED`, `REFURB_INTAKE`, `REFURB_CERTIFIED`, `RESOLD` and `SCRAPPED`, the last two being terminal. Loading firmware onto an asset is available to a `ProductEngineer` or a `ServicePartnerTech` at any point in that walk: the payload names the `firmware_release_id`, the build's `console_platform` must match the asset's model `console_platform` and that model must be connected (`ERR_PLATFORM_INCOMPATIBLE`), and there must exist a `CERTIFIED`, non-withdrawn certification pairing that build with the asset's revision (`ERR_FIRMWARE_NOT_CERTIFIED`). Success writes `installed_firmware_release_id` and recomputes the asset's support window.

That support window is derived, never supplied. `warranty_expires_at` is `warranty_starts_at` plus the warranty term for the model's `duty_class` — 365 days for `LIGHT_COMMERCIAL`, 1,095 days (three years) for `FULL_COMMERCIAL`, and 1,825 days (five years) for `INSTITUTIONAL` — and it stays null while the asset is `BUILT`, because the warranty has not started. The console side is evaluated as a chain of cases, first match winning. If the model is not connected, `firmware_support_expires_at` is null, `support_gap_days` is 0, and `connectivity_support_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Otherwise, if the installed release's `status` is `WITHDRAWN`, `firmware_support_expires_at` is that release's `withdrawn_at` and the status is `FIRMWARE_REVOKED`. Otherwise, if the asset's revision has a null `firmware_support_until` — no sunset has been announced — then `firmware_support_expires_at` is null, `support_gap_days` is 0, and the status is `SUPPORTED`, an open-ended commitment. Otherwise `firmware_support_expires_at` takes the revision's `firmware_support_until`, and the status is `SUNSET_TRACKED` when that horizon is at or after `warranty_expires_at`, or `SUPPORT_GAP` when it falls strictly before. `support_gap_days` is 0 whenever either timestamp is null; otherwise it is the number of whole days from `firmware_support_expires_at` to `warranty_expires_at`, discarding any fractional day, and floored at zero so it is never negative. A `support_gap_days` greater than zero is the contractual exposure the business tracks: the console loses firmware support while the mechanical warranty is still running.

Delivery is executed by a service partner crew under an **install job**, created by an `OrderPlanner`. The payload names the `order_id`, the `service_partner_id` whose calendar will be occupied, the `window_start` and `window_end` of the install visit, and the `estimated_labor_hours` of crew time to book — strictly greater than 0 and no more than 400. Creation is heavily gated. The referenced order must have `config_status` `CONFIRMED` with `fulfillment_status` either `IN_PRODUCTION` or `STAGED`, and must have at least one `ACTIVE` line (`ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`); the window end must be strictly after the start; the partner's `training_current_until` must be strictly after the logical clock (`ERR_PARTNER_TRAINING_EXPIRED`); the partner's `certified_categories` must cover every equipment category present on the order (`ERR_PARTNER_NOT_CERTIFIED_FOR_CATEGORY`); the order must have no other install job in `SCHEDULED`, `ON_SITE` or `PUNCH_LIST` (`ERR_INSTALL_JOB_ALREADY_OPEN`); neither the `window_start` calendar date nor the `window_end` calendar date, both evaluated in UTC and both checked inclusively, may fall on a weekday listed in the facility's `install_blackout_weekdays` (`ERR_INSTALL_BLACKOUT_DAY`); the calendar must be free under the geometry below; and the partner's hour capacity must accommodate the booking.

The crew calendar geometry is **half-open and travel-buffered**. Every job carries `travel_buffer_minutes`, a system-set field fixed at 120 minutes — two hours of drive time — appended after `window_end` to form the job's effective occupation, which runs from `window_start` inclusive up to but *excluding* `window_end` plus the buffer. For the same service partner, the requested job conflicts with an existing job in `SCHEDULED`, `ON_SITE` or `PUNCH_LIST` when the requested start falls strictly before that job's effective end **and** that job's start falls strictly before the requested effective end. A new job that starts exactly at an existing job's effective end is therefore accepted: touching endpoints do not collide, which is the deliberate opposite of the rig geometry in §3.2. A genuine intersection is `ERR_INSTALL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The capacity gate reads a partner's derived `committed_hours`, which is the sum of `estimated_labor_hours` over that partner's install jobs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ON_SITE` or `PUNCH_LIST`, plus the sum of `annual_labor_hours_committed` over that partner's maintenance programs whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `NEEDS_REVISION`. Creating the job is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when `committed_hours` plus the new `estimated_labor_hours` would exceed `certified_hours_capacity`; equality is allowed and is not an over-commitment. A successful creation puts the job at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `actual_labor_hours` null, `punch_list_open_count` 0 and `completed_at` null, and the booked hours immediately enter the partner's `committed_hours`.

A job's `status` walks `SCHEDULED`, `ON_SITE`, `PUNCH_LIST`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, the last two terminal. The assigned `ServicePartnerTech` reports arrival, moving the job `SCHEDULED → ON_SITE`; the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `STAGED` and the partner's `training_current_until` must still be strictly after the logical clock. Arrival moves the order `STAGED → INSTALLING` in the same request.

Completion is a fork, and the branch is chosen by the reported `punch_list_open_count`. Both branches require `actual_labor_hours` strictly greater than 0 in the body, and both record `actual_labor_hours` and `punch_list_open_count` on the job. If `punch_list_open_count` is 1 or more, the job moves `ON_SITE → PUNCH_LIST` and nothing else happens at all: the order is neither installed nor invoiced, the assets stay `BUILT`, no warranty starts and no rollup is recomputed. If `punch_list_open_count` is exactly 0, the job moves `ON_SITE → COMPLETED` and the full five-level cascade runs: `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock; the order moves `fulfillment_status` `INSTALLING → INSTALLED` and `billing_status` `QUOTED → INVOICED`, the latter writing the final price variant of §3.6 and setting `amount_due_cents` to `final_total_cents` minus `tradein_credit_applied_cents`, and the facility's `open_order_exposure_cents` is recomputed; every `BUILT` asset belonging to the order's `ACTIVE` lines moves `BUILT → IN_SERVICE` with `warranty_starts_at` stamped from the logical clock and `warranty_expires_at`, `firmware_support_expires_at`, `support_gap_days` and `connectivity_support_status` all derived; every `ACTIVE` maintenance program at the order's facility has its `covered_asset_count` and `annual_labor_hours_committed` recomputed, because the facility just acquired in-service assets; and the installing partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` are recomputed, since the job has stopped consuming its estimated hours.

Resolving the punch list closes the deferred branch. A `ServicePartnerTech` supplies a `resolution_note` and the job moves `PUNCH_LIST → COMPLETED`; the job must currently be `PUNCH_LIST`. `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the `punch_list_open_count` recorded at completion is **retained**, not zeroed — it is what prices `punch_list_credit_cents` into the final total. Then exactly the same cascade as the direct `ON_SITE → COMPLETED` branch runs, with equal effect at every level: the order reaches `INSTALLED` and `INVOICED` at the final price variant, its assets reach `IN_SERVICE` with warranty and support windows derived, the facility's maintenance programs are recomputed, and the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` are recomputed.

A job may be aborted with an `abort_reason` by an `OrderPlanner` or by the assigned `ServicePartnerTech`, from either `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` or `ON_SITE → ABORTED`, with no preconditions on either branch. Both branches release the calendar window and recompute the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status`. The `ON_SITE` branch additionally reverts the order's `fulfillment_status` `INSTALLING → STAGED`, so the order can be rescheduled with a fresh install job; because the aborted job is no longer in an open status, the one-open-job rule no longer blocks that.

### 3.8. Service Network Operations and Preventive Maintenance

A **service partner** is onboarded by a `ServiceOpsAdmin` with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that is globally unique, a `region` of `R_NORTH`, `R_SOUTH`, `R_EAST` or `R_WEST`, a non-empty duplicate-free `certified_categories` list drawn from `CARDIO`, `STRENGTH` and `CONNECTED`, a `certified_technician_count` of at least 1, a `certified_hours_capacity` greater than 0 carried to two decimals as the annual committed-hours ceiling, and a `training_current_until` instant recording when service-network training currency lapses. Training currency is not advisory: every operation that puts a partner in front of equipment requires `training_current_until` to be strictly after the logical clock, and the six guarded operations are listed at rung 17 of §2.8.

A partner's load is summarised by the derived `committed_hours` defined in §3.7 and by `coverage_status`, which is `OVER_COMMITTED` exactly when `committed_hours` is strictly greater than `certified_hours_capacity`, and `ACTIVE` otherwise. Equality is not over-commitment. Note the asymmetry that follows from the definition of `committed_hours`: a program in `NEEDS_REVISION` still consumes capacity, so only termination actually releases a program's hours. Parts distribution is separate: a `ServiceOpsAdmin` posts a receipt of `units` to a partner, which raises `spare_stock_received_units` and, through the ledger arithmetic of §2.11, `spare_stock_on_hand_units`.

A **preventive-maintenance program** is requested by a `ServiceOpsAdmin` or by the covered facility's own `FacilityManager`, naming the covered `facility_id`, the delivering `service_partner_id`, the service `tier` of `BASIC`, `PRO` or `PLATINUM`, and a `visit_interval_days` between 30 and 365 stating the nominal days between preventive visits. A program is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `covered_asset_count` 0, `annual_labor_hours_committed` 0, `needs_revision_reason` null and `last_revised_at` null; no hours are committed until activation. Its `status` walks `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `NEEDS_REVISION` and the terminal `TERMINATED`.

The annual labour commitment is computed from the facility's real installed base and its real usage. First, the number of visits per year is 365 divided by `visit_interval_days`, rounded **up** to a whole number of visits. The covered set is every serialized asset whose `facility_id` equals the program's `facility_id` and whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE`; `covered_asset_count` is the size of that set. For each covered asset, a usage factor is derived from its reported `annual_usage_hours` divided by the nominal norm of 2,000 hours per year, then clamped by a nested pair of bounds: the inner bound raises anything below one up to one, and the outer bound lowers anything above two down to two. Light users are therefore floored at the nominal service burden and heavy users are capped, so usage below 2,000 hours and usage above 4,000 hours are both flat. That asset's hours are then the per-visit base hours for its model `category` — 1.0 hour for `CARDIO`, 0.6 hour for `STRENGTH`, 1.4 hours for `CONNECTED` — multiplied by its usage factor, multiplied by the tier multiplier (1.0 for `BASIC`, 1.25 for `PRO`, 1.6 for `PLATINUM`), multiplied by the visits per year. The asset contributions are summed exactly, and only the sum is rounded, half up, to two decimal places; that figure is `annual_labor_hours_committed`. It is recomputed on activation, on revision, whenever an asset enters or leaves `IN_SERVICE`, whenever a usage report arrives, and whenever a firmware withdrawal cascades through the facility.

Activation is a `ServiceOpsAdmin` action with no body, moving the program `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. It has four preconditions: the facility's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`); the partner's `training_current_until` must be strictly after the logical clock (`ERR_PARTNER_TRAINING_EXPIRED`); the partner's `certified_categories` must cover every equipment category present among the facility's `IN_SERVICE` assets (`ERR_PARTNER_NOT_CERTIFIED_FOR_CATEGORY`); and the partner's existing `committed_hours` plus this program's `annual_labor_hours_committed` must be less than or equal to its `certified_hours_capacity`, equality allowed (`ERR_PARTNER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`). Activation computes `covered_asset_count` and `annual_labor_hours_committed`, then recomputes the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status`.

A program is knocked into `ACTIVE → NEEDS_REVISION` automatically, never by client request, in exactly two situations: a revision covering one of its assets reaches `SUNSET_ANNOUNCED`, which sets `needs_revision_reason` to `CONSOLE_SUPPORT_LOST`, or a firmware release loaded on one of its assets is withdrawn, which sets it to `FIRMWARE_REVOKED`. Either way, every `SCHEDULED` visit of the program moves to `ON_HOLD`, releasing its held spare units. Reviving it is a `ServiceOpsAdmin` action that moves `NEEDS_REVISION → ACTIVE` and may optionally re-specify `visit_interval_days` and `service_partner_id` in the body — a program can be moved to a different partner as part of the fix. Revision requires the partner's `training_current_until` to be strictly after the logical clock, requires the partner's `certified_categories` to still cover the facility's in-service categories, and requires the partner's `committed_hours` plus the **recomputed** `annual_labor_hours_committed` to be within `certified_hours_capacity`. On success `needs_revision_reason` is cleared to null, `last_revised_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `covered_asset_count` and `annual_labor_hours_committed` are recomputed, every `ON_HOLD` visit of the program returns `ON_HOLD → SCHEDULED` and re-reserves its `reserved_units`, and the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` are recomputed. Re-reservation is itself gated: if the partner does not have enough `spare_stock_on_hand_units` to re-reserve a held visit's units, the whole revision request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK` and, per §2.10, nothing at all is persisted.

Termination takes a `terminate_reason` and is reachable from all three non-terminal states: `DRAFT → TERMINATED`, `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and `NEEDS_REVISION → TERMINATED`, none of them with preconditions. Terminating a `DRAFT` program has no side effects, since it never committed anything. Terminating an `ACTIVE` or a `NEEDS_REVISION` program moves every one of its visits in `SCHEDULED` or `ON_HOLD` to `CANCELLED`, releasing their held spare units, and then recomputes the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` — this is the only way a program's hours are given back.

Individual **maintenance visits** are booked by the delivering `ServicePartnerTech`, naming the owning `pm_program_id`, the `serialized_asset_id` to be serviced, a `scheduled_date` for the planned visit instant, and an optional `reserved_units` count between 0 and 50 (defaulting to 0) of spare units to hold from the partner's stock. The program must be `ACTIVE`; the partner's training must be current; the asset's `facility_id` must equal the program's `facility_id` and the asset's `status` must be `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COVERED`; and the partner's `spare_stock_on_hand_units` must be at least the requested `reserved_units`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_STOCK`. A visit starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `labor_hours` null and `held_reason` null, and its `status` walks `SCHEDULED`, `ON_HOLD`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, the last two terminal.

Completing a visit moves it `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` with a reported `labor_hours` that must be strictly greater than 0. It requires the owning program to be `ACTIVE` and the partner's `training_current_until` to be strictly after the logical clock. The reported `labor_hours` is recorded, the visit's `reserved_units` shift from the partner's `spare_stock_reserved_units` into `spare_stock_consumed_units`, and the serviced asset's `service_history_count` and `accrued_service_labor_hours` are recomputed — the latter as the sum of `labor_hours` over that asset's `COMPLETED` visits. Growing service history is not free: as §3.9 explains, each completed visit reduces the asset's later trade-in valuation.

Two other movements affect a visit. It goes `SCHEDULED → ON_HOLD` only automatically, when the owning program is knocked into `NEEDS_REVISION`, which sets `held_reason` to `PROGRAM_NEEDS_REVISION` and releases its `reserved_units` back to on-hand stock; and it returns `ON_HOLD → SCHEDULED` only automatically, when the program is revised, which clears `held_reason` and re-reserves the units. Cancellation reaches `CANCELLED` from either open state. A `ServicePartnerTech` or a `ServiceOpsAdmin` may cancel explicitly with a `cancel_reason`; the same transitions also fire automatically when the serviced asset is traded in or the owning program is terminated. The `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` branch releases the visit's `reserved_units` back to the partner's `spare_stock_on_hand_units`; the `ON_HOLD → CANCELLED` branch releases nothing, because an on-hold visit was already holding nothing.

Usage telemetry closes the loop between the field and the commitment. A `ServicePartnerTech` reports an asset's latest `annual_usage_hours`, and that single number ripples: every `ACTIVE` maintenance program at the asset's facility has its `covered_asset_count` and `annual_labor_hours_committed` recomputed, and the delivering partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` are recomputed with them. A heavily used floor can therefore push a partner into `OVER_COMMITTED` without anybody booking a new job.

### 3.9. Trade-In, Refurbishment and the Secondary Market

Trade-in is where the service history a facility accumulated turns into money. An `OrderPlanner` trades in an asset that is currently `IN_SERVICE`, supplying a `declared_condition_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C`; the asset moves `IN_SERVICE → TRADE_IN_RETURNED`.

The valuation is a decay function evaluated in a fixed order. First the age in whole years is the number of days from the asset's `warranty_starts_at` to the current logical clock, divided by 365 with the fractional part discarded. Then a residual proportion is formed by starting at 0.55, subtracting 0.08 for each whole year of age, and subtracting a further 0.015 for each visit counted in the asset's `service_history_count`; if the result falls below 0.10 it is raised to 0.10. Separately, a console multiplier is 0.75 when the asset's model is connected **and** its `firmware_support_expires_at` is non-null **and** that horizon is at or before the current logical clock — an unsupported console is worth a quarter less — and 1.0 in every other case, including an open-ended commitment where the horizon is null. Finally `trade_in_valuation_cents` is the model's `unit_list_price_cents` multiplied by the residual proportion, multiplied by the condition multiplier for the declared grade (1.0 for `A`, 0.85 for `B`, 0.65 for `C`), multiplied by the console multiplier, with a single round half up to a whole number of cents at the very end. The order of operations matters at the floor: the 0.10 floor is applied to the residual **before** the condition and console multipliers, so a floored asset can still be discounted further by a `C` grade or by an unsupported console, and the effective proportion of list price can legitimately land below 0.10.

Trading in an asset also grants credit and unwinds the asset's service obligations. The valuation is added to the facility's `tradein_credit_granted_cents` and `tradein_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed per §2.11. Because the asset has left `IN_SERVICE`, every `ACTIVE` maintenance program at that facility has its `covered_asset_count` and `annual_labor_hours_committed` recomputed, and then the delivering partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status`. Every `SCHEDULED` visit for that specific asset moves to `CANCELLED`, releasing its held spare units.

A trade-in can be undone, and this compensating back-edge is the only transition in the asset machine that moves backwards. An `OrderPlanner` supplies a `reverse_reason` and the asset moves `TRADE_IN_RETURNED → IN_SERVICE`. The single precondition is that the granted credit must still be unspent: the facility's `tradein_credit_balance_cents` must be greater than or equal to the asset's `trade_in_valuation_cents`, otherwise the reversal is refused with `ERR_TRADEIN_CREDIT_ALREADY_APPLIED`, because credit already spent on an order cannot be clawed back and the ledger is never allowed to go negative. On success the valuation is subtracted from the facility's `tradein_credit_granted_cents` and the balance recomputed, `trade_in_valuation_cents` and `declared_condition_grade` are both cleared back to null, and the facility's program rollups and the partner's `committed_hours` and `coverage_status` are recomputed as the asset re-enters service.

Credit is spent by an `OrderPlanner` applying it to an order, and this is the third version-pinned write of §2.7: the body carries the `amount_cents` to apply and the `expected_facility_version` pinning the facility's credit-ledger snapshot. The requested `amount_cents` must be at least 1, must not exceed the facility's `tradein_credit_balance_cents`, and must not exceed the order's outstanding total — that being `final_total_cents` reduced by the `tradein_credit_applied_cents` already recorded on the order once the order has been invoiced, and `quoted_total_cents` reduced by the same figure before that. A request outside those bounds is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TRADEIN_CREDIT`. Success moves the credit off the facility ledger and onto the order: the order's `tradein_credit_applied_cents` grows by the applied amount, the facility's `tradein_credit_applied_cents` and `tradein_credit_balance_cents` are recomputed, the order's `amount_due_cents` is recomputed as `final_total_cents` minus `tradein_credit_applied_cents`, and the facility's `version` is incremented by one.

Refurbishment is the branching part of the asset machine. A `ServiceOpsAdmin` takes a returned unit into the shop, moving it `TRADE_IN_RETURNED → REFURB_INTAKE` with no body and no precondition beyond the state itself. From `REFURB_INTAKE` the unit forks two ways, and the two branches are symmetric in structure and asymmetric only in what they require. Certifying the refurbishment moves it `REFURB_INTAKE → REFURB_CERTIFIED` and takes a `refurb_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C`, which is stored separately from the `declared_condition_grade` recorded at trade-in so that the shop's assessment can be compared with the customer's declaration; the precondition is that, if the model is connected, `firmware_support_expires_at` must be null or strictly after the current logical clock, otherwise `ERR_FIRMWARE_SUPPORT_EXPIRED` — the business will not certify a console it can no longer patch. Scrapping moves it `REFURB_INTAKE → SCRAPPED`, takes a `scrap_reason`, and has no preconditions and no side effects; `SCRAPPED` is terminal.

From `REFURB_CERTIFIED` the unit forks the same two ways. Reselling moves it `REFURB_CERTIFIED → RESOLD` and takes the `buyer_facility_id`; the buyer facility's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`) and, if the model is connected, `firmware_support_expires_at` must again be null or strictly after the current logical clock (`ERR_FIRMWARE_SUPPORT_EXPIRED`). Resale rewrites the asset's `facility_id` to the buyer facility and resets both `warranty_starts_at` and `warranty_expires_at` to null — a resold unit carries no residual manufacturer warranty — and then recomputes `connectivity_support_status`. `RESOLD` is terminal. Scrapping is equally available here, moving the unit `REFURB_CERTIFIED → SCRAPPED` with a `scrap_reason`, again with no preconditions and no side effects.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence return `201 Created`. Every other operation — state transitions, recomputations, reads and the clock control — returns `200 OK`. Failures return the status paired with their error identifier in §5. Where the table names several roles, any one of them may call the endpoint, subject to the facility-scope and partner-scope ownership rules of §2.1; "Any authenticated role" means all six roles are authorised.
