# Community Health Worker & SDOH Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Health systems that employ community health workers face a coordination problem no clinical record system was designed to solve. A single patient may be screened for social risk with one instrument today and a different one next quarter, referred to a food pantry that declines and must be re-routed to another, visited at home by a worker who then has seventy-two hours to attest what happened, and finally billed — for the very same minutes — either to a managed-care payer or to a philanthropic grant, but never to both. The work is clinical, logistical and fiscal at once, and the failure modes are quiet ones: a referral loop that nobody closed, a pantry whose last food box was promised twice, a grant that overspent its cap by one rounding step, a month of care-management minutes double-counted across two funding streams.

This document specifies the provider-side backend for such a program. The system maintains a patient roster with a weighted social-determinants risk score, drives closed-loop referrals against a real inventory of community resource units, schedules and documents home visits on two independent lifecycles, tracks care-plan goals, captures community health worker time down to the minute, and carves those minutes to exactly one funding source. Around all of that sits an escalation ladder that promotes unattended problems through three tiers, a set of lazy system sweeps that expire acknowledgement deadlines and resource holds, and a derived program report that never stores a single figure.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **conservation**: resource units, grant dollars and billable minutes are partitioned quantities, and every operation — including every cascade an operation sets off — must leave those partitions balanced. The second is **strict ordering**: when a request could fail several ways at once, exactly one error is correct, and it is determined by a single global validation ladder that this document pins rung by rung. Implementers should treat both commitments as testable contracts rather than aspirations.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every route in this specification is mounted under the `/chw` prefix.

### 2.1. Personnel, Roles and Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the first check the server performs on any request, before it looks at the path parameters, the payload or anything else — with one mechanical exception: a URL that matches no declared route at all never reaches the check and is answered `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) whatever headers it carries, and a URL that matches a declared path under a method that path does not declare is answered `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 405. Both are still delivered inside the standard error envelope of §2.2, as is any other framework-level failure.

The five roles are `CHW`, `Supervisor`, `ProgramAdmin`, `BillingSpecialist` and `ResourcePartner`, spelled exactly as shown. Once a caller is authenticated, the server checks that the caller's role appears in the authorized-role list declared for the target endpoint in §4; a caller whose role is absent from that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authorization is a pure role check — it never inspects the payload and never inspects the state of the target row.

A `CHW` is a community health worker. Screening, referral origination and closure, home visits, care-plan goals and encounter time capture belong to this role; program configuration, billing and records holds do not. When `X-User-Role` is `CHW`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CommunityHealthWorker`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CommunityHealthWorker` compare that resource's worker reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `CHW` may drive a home visit's scheduling and attestation only when `X-User-ID` equals the visit's `assigned_chw_id`, and may submit an encounter only when `X-User-ID` equals that encounter's `chw_id`.

A `ResourcePartner` is a community resource organization acting on referrals sent to it. When `X-User-Role` is `ResourcePartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ResourceOrg`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the `resource_org_id` of the `ResourceOffering` named by the referral's `offering_id`. A partner may therefore respond to, and record delivery against, only its own referrals.

The remaining three roles are operator roles whose identifiers are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `Supervisor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — for example as `opened_by_user_id` on an escalation the supervisor opens, or as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on an escalation the supervisor acknowledges — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, the configuration and operations role that owns workers, screening instruments, resource organizations, offerings, eligibility criteria, grant awards, the logical clock and the sweeps; and when `X-User-Role` is `BillingSpecialist`, the revenue-cycle role that owns payer claims, their appeals, grant charge lines and their reversals. A `BillingSpecialist` never creates, submits or voids an encounter — a `CHW` creates and submits encounters, a `Supervisor` voids them, and a `BillingSpecialist` funds them. That split is the program's core segregation of duties, and it is enforced purely through the per-endpoint role lists in §4.

One further segregation rule is enforced at request time rather than by role membership: the operator acknowledging an escalation must not be the operator who opened it. If the acknowledging `X-User-ID` equals the escalation's `opened_by_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Four endpoints answer instead with a **derived** object that is not a persisted entity and therefore carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version`: setting the logical clock, the eligibility check, running a sweep, and the program report. Each carries exactly the fields named for it in §3 and nothing else.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and only for list endpoints; every other response's `meta` carries just `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a payload-domain failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400), as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not parseable as an integer; a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current page — that is, whether `offset` plus the number of rows on this page is still less than `total_count`. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still between calls that set it, many rows share an identical `created_at`; ties are therefore broken by the server-assigned creation sequence, so that rows created earlier in the run always sort earlier, and never by `id`. The one endpoint that departs from this default is the eligibility-criteria listing, which returns an offering's criteria in evaluation order — `evaluation_rank` ascending, with `created_at` ascending and then creation sequence breaking ties between two criteria of the same rank.

The filter parameters each list endpoint declares are matched as exact, case-sensitive string comparisons against the stored value. Filters are never enum-validated: a filter value that is not a member of the field's enumeration simply matches nothing and yields an empty page with `total_count` of 0, rather than `ERR_VALIDATION`. Enum validation is a body-side rule only. An unrecognized query parameter is ignored.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form, with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp. All fifteen entity types in this specification additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. Each entity is serialized with its complete declared field set on every response that returns it, listing and single read alike: a field that has no value renders as an explicit `null` — an unset array field renders as `[]` — and no field is ever omitted from the object because it is empty.

Timestamp rendering is not uniform, and the differences are observable. `created_at`, `updated_at` and `meta.api_request_timestamp` are ISO 8601 UTC values rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Every *other* server-written instant instead reproduces the logical clock's operator string verbatim, digit for digit: if the clock was last set to `2026-03-02T08:00:00Z`, then `sent_at`, `delivered_at`, `closed_at`, `exhausted_since`, `completed_at`, `attested_at`, `submitted_at`, `denied_at`, `appealed_at`, `reversed_at`, `tier_entered_at`, `anchor_screened_at`, `latest_response_at`, a goal's `closed_at` and a patient's `risk_tier_effective_at` all read `2026-03-02T08:00:00Z`, not `2026-03-02T08:00:00.000Z`; had the clock been set with fractional digits, those same fields would carry them. An instant derived by arithmetic from another instant — `ack_deadline_at`, `hold_expires_at` and `review_due_at` — inherits the fractional-digit style of the string it was derived from. An instant supplied by the caller — `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `effective_from`, the clock's own `to_iso8601` — is stored and echoed back exactly as it arrived, and the same holds for the `YYYY-MM-DD` and `YYYY-MM` string fields.

`version` begins at 1 when a row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations applied to it by a cascade fired from a different entity's operation; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. That increment is capped at one per row per request: however many links of a cascade write to the same row, its `version` rises by exactly 1 across the whole request, while `updated_at` is refreshed by each of them. A row **created** by the request is born at `version` 1 and stays at 1 even when a later link of the same request writes to it again.

All temporal reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the machine's real time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock forward or backward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response is HTTP 200 carrying a derived object whose only field is `current_utc`, which echoes the supplied string exactly as it arrived rather than re-rendering it. Setting the clock mutates no entity and runs no sweep — it only changes what "now" means for the next request. Every deadline, cadence window, expiry, attestation window, appeal window and report cut-off described in this document is measured against this clock.

### 2.5. The Validation Priority Ladder

When a request could fail in more than one way, exactly one error is correct. The server evaluates checks in a single global order and stops at the first failure; no later check may mask an earlier one, and no earlier check may be skipped because a later one is cheaper to evaluate. The ladder has seventeen rungs, in this order:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognized (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401).
2. **Role authorization** — the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorized list (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403).
3. **Path resource existence** — every `{id}` segment in the URL resolves, checked left to right. A patient id under the patients collection that does not resolve yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` (404); a referral id under the referrals collection yields `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` (404); every other path id yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
4. **The records-hold write gate** — the resolved gate-subject patient must not be on a records hold (`ERR_PATIENT_RECORDS_HOLD`, 409). This rung sits *above* payload validation; see §2.6.
5. **Payload shape and domain rules** — required fields present, correct JSON types, declared enum members, scalar range and cross-field rules, and uniqueness (`ERR_VALIDATION`, 400). A body that is absent, empty or not parseable as a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so such a request fails on its first missing required field rather than on a parse error; a body field the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected.
6. **Body-referenced existence** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves. A `patient_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND` (404); every other body-supplied reference (`instrument_id`, `offering_id`, `opened_by_chw_id`, `assigned_chw_id`, `second_chw_id`, `chw_id`, `home_visit_id`, `resource_org_id`, `grant_award_id`, `encounter_id`) yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied expected parent version matches the parent's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_OFFERING_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION`, each 412); see §2.7.
8. **Target-entity state precondition** — the target row is in a state that permits the requested transition (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409).
9. **Cross-entity state precondition** — a related row is in the state the operation requires (`ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_RESOURCE_ORG_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, each 409).
10. **Actor identity and segregation of duties** — the caller owns the resource, or is distinct from the party they must be distinct from (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, each 403).
11. **Safety** — the two-person home-visit rule (`ERR_TWO_PERSON_VISIT_REQUIRED`, 422).
12. **The eligibility gauntlet** — the patient clears every criterion attached to the offering, in rank order (`ERR_ELIGIBILITY_INCOME`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_GEOGRAPHY`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT` or `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`, each 422).
13. **Temporal overlap** — visit-slot collision (`ERR_VISIT_SLOT_CONFLICT`, 409) and grant-period collision (`ERR_GRANT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, 409).
14. **Window and count limits** — attestation, amendment, re-route and appeal limits (`ERR_ATTESTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_REROUTE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, each 422).
15. **Funding exclusivity and allowability** — the anti-double-dip rules and grant allowability (`ERR_MINUTES_ALREADY_FUNDED`, 409; `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_ALLOWABLE`, 422).
16. **Inventory capacity** — enough offering units remain (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNITS`, 409).
17. **Fiscal caps and thresholds** — the grant budget cap and the care-management minute threshold (`ERR_GRANT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, 422; `ERR_MINUTE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, 422).

Within a single rung, the relative order of independent checks is immaterial for most pairings, because no two of them can be violated by the same request in a way that produces different errors. Four intra-rung orderings are nonetheless deliberate and observable. First, the five eligibility checks at rung 12 are strictly ordered among themselves by criterion rank, as §3.3 describes. Second, within rung 5 an endpoint's uniqueness check runs last, after every shape, type, enum, range and cross-field rule has passed, so a payload that is both malformed and a duplicate reports the malformation. Third, when more than one body-supplied reference dangles at rung 6, the server reports the first failure in a fixed per-endpoint order: `patient_id` then `instrument_id` on a screening; `patient_id`, then `offering_id`, then `opened_by_chw_id` on a referral; `patient_id`, then `assigned_chw_id`, then `second_chw_id` on a home visit; `patient_id`, then `chw_id`, then `home_visit_id` on an encounter; and `grant_award_id` before `encounter_id` on a grant charge line. Fourth, within rung 9 the resource organization's suspension is evaluated **before** the patient's enrollment, so a referral sent or re-routed to an offering of a `SUSPENDED` organization on behalf of a patient who is not `ENROLLED` reports `ERR_RESOURCE_ORG_SUSPENDED` and never `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED`.

### 2.6. The Records-Hold Write Gate

A patient may withdraw consent, at which point a `Supervisor` places a **records hold** on that patient. The hold is a second, independent state axis on the patient row: `gate_status` is either `OPEN` or `RECORDS_HOLD`, and it evolves entirely separately from the patient's `enrollment_status`. While `gate_status` is `RECORDS_HOLD`, `gate_reason` carries the free-text reason captured when the hold was placed; while `gate_status` is `OPEN`, `gate_reason` is null.

The gate is evaluated at rung 4 of the ladder — after authentication, role authorization and path-id resolution, but **before** payload validation. The ordering is deliberate and observable: a request that is both malformed and aimed at a held patient returns `ERR_PATIENT_RECORDS_HOLD` (HTTP 409), not `ERR_VALIDATION`. Equally, a request aimed at a held patient by a caller whose role is not authorized returns `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, because rung 2 is evaluated first.

**Resolving the gate subject.** Before the gate can be evaluated, the server must decide which patient the request concerns. It resolves the subject in this fixed order, taking the first step that applies:

1. If the endpoint path contains a patients-collection `{id}` segment, the subject is that patient.
2. Otherwise, if the request body carries a `patient_id` field, the subject is that patient.
3. Otherwise, if the path names a row of an entity that itself carries a patient reference — a referral, a home visit, a care-plan goal, an encounter, a payer claim, an escalation or a screening — the subject is that row's patient.
4. Otherwise, if the path names a grant charge line, or the request is a grant-charge-line creation carrying an `encounter_id`, the subject is the patient of the referenced encounter.
5. Otherwise there is no subject at all, and the rung is skipped.

If step 2, 3 or 4 names an identifier that does not resolve to an existing patient, the rung is **skipped** rather than failed — evaluation falls through to rung 5. This is what keeps `ERR_VALIDATION` and the rung-6 not-found errors reachable on malformed or dangling requests.

**Operations the gate blocks.** Exactly nineteen operations are gated. When their resolved subject patient is on a records hold, each returns `ERR_PATIENT_RECORDS_HOLD`:

1. Updating a patient's demographics or driving the enrollment axis.
2. Administering a screening.
3. Sending a referral.
4. Re-routing a declined referral.
5. Scheduling a home visit.
6. Marking a home visit en route.
7. Completing a home visit.
8. Recording a home-visit no-show.
9. Attesting home-visit documentation.
10. Applying a documentation revision to a home visit, under either the amend or the void action.
11. Creating a care-plan goal.
12. Recording goal progress.
13. Closing a goal.
14. Re-opening a goal.
15. Creating an encounter.
16. Submitting an encounter.
17. Creating a payer claim, under either basis.
18. Posting a grant charge line.
19. Opening a manual escalation.

**Operations the gate exempts.** Everything else proceeds normally even while a hold is in force. Specifically: every read endpoint without exception, because a hold restricts writes and never reads; placing or releasing the hold itself, under both the place and the release action, since otherwise a held patient could never be released; creating a patient, which cannot have a gated subject; cancelling a home visit, so an in-flight visit on a newly held patient can always be stood down; voiding an encounter, so corrections remain possible while a hold is in force; acknowledging and resolving escalations, so open escalations remain closeable; the three partner-side referral operations — responding, recording delivery and closing the loop — because the resource organization is a third party and is not bound by the provider's records hold; the three payer-side operations on an already-submitted claim — adjudication, appeal and appeal decision; reversing a grant charge line, which is a compensating action; running a system sweep, together with every cascade a sweep fires; the eligibility-check capability, which computes and stores nothing; and every endpoint with no patient subject at all — worker registration, screening instruments, resource organizations, offerings, eligibility criteria, grant awards, the logical clock and the program report.

One consequence deserves emphasis: **cascades are not gated.** The write gate constrains inbound API writes only. When placing a hold lapses the patient's outstanding referrals and those lapses ripple into offering inventory and open escalations, every one of those writes lands, even though the patient is by then held.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations act on a parent row that other writers are simultaneously moving, and each therefore requires the client to declare the parent version it last observed. The declared field is mandatory on those three endpoints: omitting it is a payload failure at rung 5, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a stale-version rejection at rung 7. No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field of any kind.

Sending a referral requires `expected_offering_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ResourceOffering` named by `offering_id`. Every hold placement, release, expiry and delivery bumps that offering's `version`, so a client working from a stale view of inventory would otherwise place a hold against a unit count that no longer exists. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFERING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-routing a declined referral places a hold on an alternate offering and carries the same field with the same semantics, checked against the alternate offering's current `version`.

Posting a grant charge line requires `expected_grant_award_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `GrantAward` named by `grant_award_id`. Each posted or reversed line moves the award's `committed_cents` and therefore changes both the remaining budget and the rate tier the next line would receive. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Administering a screening requires `expected_patient_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Patient` named by `patient_id`. A screening legitimately supersedes or amends the patient's prior screening and rewrites `risk_score`, `risk_tier` and `risk_tier_effective_at`, so a writer holding a stale view of the patient would silently overwrite a newer risk assessment. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Two Interval Conventions, Deliberately Opposite

This system uses two different interval conventions, and confusing them is the single easiest way to get boundary behaviour wrong.

**Home-visit slots are half-open.** A visit occupies its `scheduled_start` inclusively and its `scheduled_end` exclusively. Two slots for the same worker collide when the new slot's start falls strictly before the existing slot's end *and* the existing slot's start falls strictly before the new slot's end. Touching slots therefore do **not** collide: a visit beginning at exactly the instant another ends is accepted. There is no buffer between visits of any kind.

**Grant award periods are closed.** An award's `period_start` and `period_end` are both inclusive service dates. Two award periods for the same funder collide when the new period's start falls on or before the existing period's end *and* the existing period's start falls on or before the new period's end. Touching dates therefore **do** collide: a new period beginning on the exact day an existing period ends is rejected. The same closed convention governs grant allowability — an encounter is inside an award's period when its service date falls on or after `period_start` and on or before `period_end`.

### 2.9. Minutes, Money and Rounding Discipline

Money is always expressed in whole cents as an integer; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system. Minutes are always whole integers. Wherever a value must be rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two candidates rounds away from zero — and the *order* of rounding relative to summation is pinned per calculation and is never a matter of implementer preference.

Three rounding orders in particular are load-bearing. Encounter billable minutes are rounded **per encounter and then summed**, never summed and then rounded. Grant charge amounts are rounded **twice and independently**, once for the direct amount and once for the indirect amount, never once on a combined total. Mileage reimbursement is computed with **marginal band rates** — each band's rate applies only to the miles falling inside that band, never an average rate applied to all miles — and the result is rounded to whole cents before the cap is applied.

Percentages that this system reports — goal progress and the two report coverage percentages — carry one decimal place and are rounded half up. Risk scores carry two decimal places and are rounded half up. Where a percentage is also clamped to a range, the clamp is applied **after** rounding, never before. All of these figures travel as JSON numbers rather than strings, so trailing zeros are not preserved on the wire: a percentage rounded to 25.00 is transmitted as `25.0`, a weighted score rounded to 62.50 is transmitted as `62.5`, and a fully achieved goal reports `100.0`.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Four quantities in this system are partitioned, and every request — including every cascade a request fires — must leave the partition balanced. These invariants are not advisory; they are the arithmetic contract implementers are held to.

**Offering units.** An offering's `capacity_total` always equals the sum of its `available_units`, its `allocated_units` and its `delivered_units`. Released and expired holds return their units to `available_units` and never leave that three-way partition; `released_units` and `expired_units` are cumulative counters that sit outside it.

**The hold ledger.** An offering's `lifetime_allocated_units` always equals the sum of its `allocated_units`, `delivered_units`, `released_units` and `expired_units`. Every unit ever placed on hold ends in exactly one of those four buckets. `lifetime_allocated_units` only ever increases — it rises when a hold is placed and never falls.

**Grant budget.** An award's `budget_cap_cents` always equals its `committed_cents` plus its `remaining_budget_cents`, where `committed_cents` is the sum of `total_amount_cents` over that award's charge lines whose `status` is `POSTED`. Reversing a line subtracts exactly the amount it added.

**Encounter minutes.** An encounter's `total_minutes` always equals the sum of its `direct_minutes`, `indirect_minutes`, `travel_minutes` and `documentation_minutes`. Furthermore, for every patient-and-service-month pair, the sum of `billable_minutes` over that pair's encounters in status `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED` equals the payer-funded total plus the grant-funded total plus the unfunded total, where each total sums `billable_minutes` over the encounters whose `funding_source` is `PAYER`, `GRANT` and `UNFUNDED` respectively. No encounter's minutes may ever appear in two buckets — that is the anti-double-dip rule — and every compensating action returns minutes to the unfunded bucket with zero drift.

### 2.11. Cascades, Versioning and Nested Path Resolution

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. When they do, every touched row's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as if it had been written directly — but, as §2.4 pins, by 1 for the whole request and not once per link. Placing a records hold that lapses three of a patient's referrals therefore raises that patient's `unfulfilled_referral_count` by three while raising its `version` by one, and a sweep that lapses two referrals holding units on the same offering raises that offering's `version` by one. Cascades run to completion within the originating request — consistency is immediate, never eventual — and their depth is not bounded by convention: placing a records hold, for instance, lapses referrals, which release inventory, which can restore an exhausted offering, which auto-resolves that offering's open escalations, five links deep. §3 describes each cascade at the point where it fires; the rule to carry into all of them is that a secondary effect is as mandatory as the primary one.

For nested paths, path parameters are validated left to right. The eligibility-criteria routes are nested under an offering: the offering id is resolved first, and only then is the criterion body or the listing evaluated. A criterion created through that route takes its `offering_id` from the path, not from the body.

Response bodies for create and modify operations return the affected resource. New resources are created with HTTP 201; modifications of existing resources return HTTP 200. The one operation whose status code deserves early warning is screening administration, which always returns 201 even when the server folds the submission into an existing screening row rather than creating a new one.

Where an operation touches several rows, the one it serializes back is the row named in the paragraphs of §3, and in four cases that is not the row a caller might guess. Re-routing a referral returns the **new child referral** at HTTP 201, not the declined parent it was launched from. Reversing a grant charge line returns the **charge line**, not the award whose budget it restored nor the encounter it unlocked. Voiding an encounter returns the **encounter**, not the charge line the cascade reversed. Placing or releasing a records hold returns the **patient**, not the referrals the cascade lapsed. Creating a payer claim or a grant charge line likewise returns the claim or the line, never the encounters it locked.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Program Roster: Workers, Patients and the Enrollment Axis

A `ProgramAdmin` registers each community health worker with a `full_name` and an `employee_code`. The employee code is the employer's own worker identifier and must be a non-empty string that is unique across all workers; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The worker's `credential_level` may be supplied as `CHW_BASIC`, `CHW_SENIOR` or `CHW_SUPERVISOR`, and defaults to `CHW_BASIC` when omitted. Every worker is created with `employment_status` set to `ACTIVE`; no operation in this specification ever changes that field, so `ACTIVE` is the only value any worker row will ever hold.

Patients are added to the roster by a `CHW`, a `Supervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin`. A patient record requires an `mrn` — the medical record number, a non-empty string unique across all patients — a `full_name`, a `household_size` of at least 1, a `monthly_income_cents` of at least 0, and a `residence_zip` of exactly five numeric characters. Four further attributes are optional at creation. `program_enrollments` is an array of non-empty uppercase program codes the patient already participates in, such as SNAP, WIC or MEDICAID, and defaults to an empty array. `categorical_exclusions` is an array of non-empty uppercase exclusion flag codes on file for the patient, such as PRIOR_FRAUD_FINDING, and likewise defaults to empty. `child_in_household` is a boolean, false by default, recording whether a child under eighteen lives in the household. `address_safety_flag` is a boolean, false by default, recording a documented safety concern at the residence — it is the trigger for the two-person home-visit rule described in §3.5.

A newly created patient starts with `enrollment_status` of `PROSPECTIVE`, `gate_status` of `OPEN`, `risk_tier` of `NONE`, `risk_score` of 0, `risk_tier_effective_at` of null, `gate_reason` of null, `unfulfilled_referral_count` of 0 and `version` 1. Of the roster fields, only the six demographic ones — `household_size`, `monthly_income_cents`, `residence_zip`, `program_enrollments`, `categorical_exclusions`, `child_in_household` — plus `address_safety_flag` may later be updated, and only by a `Supervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin`; the `mrn` and `full_name` are fixed at creation, and an `mrn` or `full_name` supplied in an update body is silently ignored rather than rejected. Any such update increments the patient's `version` by 1 — once per call, even when the same call rewrites several demographic fields *and* drives the enrollment axis, and equally when the body carries neither a demographic change nor an `enrollment_action`, in which case the call is a well-formed no-op that still bumps `version` and refreshes `updated_at`.

The same update operation optionally carries an `enrollment_action`, which drives the patient's enrollment axis through a three-state cycle. `ENROLL` moves a patient from `PROSPECTIVE` to `ENROLLED`. `DISENROLL` moves a patient from `ENROLLED` to `DISENROLLED` and, as a cascade, sets every one of that patient's care-plan goals currently in `ACTIVE` to `DEFERRED`, stamping each goal's `closed_at` with the current logical clock and incrementing each goal's `version` by 1; goals already in `MET`, `NOT_MET` or `DEFERRED` are left entirely untouched, and the cascade opens no escalation of any kind. `REENROLL` moves a patient from `DISENROLLED` back to `ENROLLED` — and closes the cycle — but does **not** reopen the goals that `DISENROLL` deferred; those must be re-opened one at a time by a worker. An enrollment action requested from the wrong starting state is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Enrollment can also arrive automatically: as §3.2 explains, a screening that lands a `PROSPECTIVE` patient at moderate risk or above enrolls them without any operator action, whereas a low-risk screening leaves the patient `PROSPECTIVE` and enrollment then requires the explicit `ENROLL` action.

A `Supervisor` — and only a `Supervisor` — manages the records-hold axis, supplying an `action` of either `PLACE` or `RELEASE` together with an optional `reason`. `PLACE` requires `gate_status` to be `OPEN`, moves it to `RECORDS_HOLD`, writes the supplied reason into `gate_reason` — leaving `gate_reason` null when no `reason` accompanied the request — and increments the patient's `version` by 1. `RELEASE` requires `gate_status` to be `RECORDS_HOLD`, returns it to `OPEN`, clears `gate_reason` back to null, and again increments `version` by 1; requesting either action from the wrong state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both actions are exempt from the write gate they administer.

Placing a hold sets off the deepest cascade in the system, and every link is mandatory. First, the patient's `gate_status`, `gate_reason` and `version` are written as just described. Second — caused by that first link — every referral belonging to this patient whose `status` is `SENT` transitions to `LAPSED` with `lapse_reason` set to `RECORDS_HOLD`. Third, caused by each of those lapses, that referral's hold is released back to its offering: `allocated_units` falls by the referral's `units_requested`, `available_units` rises by the same amount, `released_units` rises by the same amount, and the offering's `version` increments by 1. Fourth, caused by the third link whenever that release lifts an offering's `available_units` from 0 to a positive number, the offering's `availability_state` flips from `EXHAUSTED` back to `AVAILABLE` and its `exhausted_since` is cleared to null. Fifth, caused by the fourth, every escalation whose `source_flag` is `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` and whose `blocking_offering_id` names that offering, and whose `status` is any of `OPEN_TIER_1`, `OPEN_TIER_2` or `OPEN_TIER_3`, transitions to `RESOLVED` with `resolution_reason` set to `RESOURCE_RESTORED`. Riding alongside the second link, each lapse also charges the patient's `unfulfilled_referral_count` exactly as a decline does: the counter rises by 1 for every referral the cascade lapses, and an escalation with `source_flag` of `REFERRAL_UNFULFILLED` opens at the moment the counter reaches exactly 2 — but because the patient row was already written by the first link, none of those counter writes bumps the patient's `version` a second time. Every link is conditional on the one above it, so a hold placed on a patient with no `SENT` referral writes nothing beyond the patient row, a lapse whose offering was never exhausted stops at the third link, and an offering restored while its `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` escalations are already `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `RESOLVED` leaves them exactly as they stand. Releasing the hold later reverses none of this: referrals lapsed by the cascade are not restored, their released units are not re-held, and `RELEASE` fires no cascade at all — it writes only the patient row.

### 3.2. SDOH Screening: Instruments, Weighted Scoring and the Anchoring Fork

Screening instruments are configuration, owned by a `ProgramAdmin`. Each instrument revision carries a `name` such as PRAPARE-Local, a `version_label` such as v2.1, an `effective_from` instant nominally marking when the revision may be used, and a `domain_weights` map from SDOH domain code to integer weight. `effective_from` is required at creation and echoed back verbatim, but it is a recorded attribute only: the server never compares it to the logical clock, so a `PUBLISHED` revision may be used for screening whatever its `effective_from` says, including one dated in the future. The pair of `name` and `version_label` must be unique across all instrument revisions; a duplicate pair is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Weight keys are drawn from `FOOD`, `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORT`, `EMPLOYMENT` and `HEALTH_LITERACY`; at least one key must be present; each weight must be an integer of at least 0; and the weights must sum to exactly 100. An instrument also carries a `rescreen_cadence_days` value of at least 1, which defaults to 90 days and which decides — as this subsection explains below — whether a re-screen amends the patient's existing record or starts a fresh one.

An instrument is created in `DRAFT` and cannot be used for screening in that state. Publishing it moves `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and increments its `version` by 1; retiring it moves `PUBLISHED` to `RETIRED`, again incrementing `version`, and leaves every screening already anchored to that revision untouched with its historical scores intact. A screening that names an instrument in `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

A `CHW` or a `Supervisor` administers a screening by supplying the `patient_id`, the `instrument_id`, a `domain_scores` map and the mandatory `expected_patient_version`. The score map's keys must equal the instrument's weight keys exactly — no missing key, no extra key — and each value must be an integer between 0 and 4 inclusive; any deviation is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Because the key-equality test needs an instrument to compare against, it is applied only when `instrument_id` resolves: a screening naming an unknown instrument passes rung 5 whatever its `domain_scores` contain and is answered `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` at rung 6. The patient must not be on a records hold, and the instrument must be published.

**Computing the weighted score.** Each domain contributes its raw score, divided by the maximum possible domain score of 4, multiplied by that domain's weight from the instrument. Those per-domain contributions are added together across every key in the instrument's weight map, and the running total is rounded half up to two decimal places to produce `weighted_score`, which therefore always lies between 0.00 and 100.00. The risk band follows from the rounded score by descending comparison: a score of at least 75 is `CRITICAL`; otherwise a score of at least 50 is `HIGH`; otherwise a score of at least 25 is `MODERATE`; otherwise the tier is `LOW`. All three thresholds are inclusive on their lower edge — a rounded score of exactly 25.00 is `MODERATE`, exactly 50.00 is `HIGH`, exactly 75.00 is `CRITICAL`.

**The anchoring fork.** Whether a submission creates a new screening row or rewrites the patient's existing one is decided by a two-variant fork. Exactly one screening row per patient is ever "current": the one whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED`. Rows in `SUPERSEDED` never participate, and because at most one current row exists at any time there is no tie to break. If the patient has no current screening at all, the **fresh-anchor** variant applies. If the submitted `instrument_id` differs from the current screening's `instrument_id`, the fresh-anchor variant applies. Otherwise, if the elapsed time from the current screening's `anchor_screened_at` to the current logical clock is strictly less than the instrument's `rescreen_cadence_days` expressed in days, the **amendment-anchor** variant applies; and if that elapsed time is equal to or greater than the cadence, the fresh-anchor variant applies. The cadence window is half-open on its upper end: at exactly the cadence boundary the strict comparison is false and the fresh-anchor variant takes over.

Under **fresh anchor**, a new screening row is created with `anchor_screened_at` and `latest_response_at` both set to the current logical clock and `amendment_count` at 0, and its `status` is `SUBMITTED`. If the patient already had a screening in `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED`, that prior row transitions to `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments by 1; its own `anchor_screened_at`, `weighted_score` and `risk_tier` are frozen as historical record. The patient's `risk_tier_effective_at` re-anchors to the new instant, which restarts the 365-day screening coverage clock the program report uses.

Under **amendment anchor**, no new row is created. The existing row is rewritten in place: `domain_scores` is overwritten, `weighted_score` and `risk_tier` are recomputed, `latest_response_at` advances to the current logical clock, `amendment_count` increments by 1, the row's `version` increments by 1, and its `status` becomes `AMENDED` — a row already in `AMENDED` stays in `AMENDED` and simply increments again, which is how a patient can be amended repeatedly inside one cadence window. Critically, `anchor_screened_at` is **preserved unchanged**, and therefore so is the patient's `risk_tier_effective_at`: an amendment does not restart the coverage clock. The response to an amendment is still HTTP 201, and it carries the existing row's `id` with its incremented `amendment_count` and its untouched `anchor_screened_at`.

Both variants then write the same three fields onto the patient — `risk_score` takes the computed `weighted_score`, `risk_tier` takes the computed band — and increment the patient's `version` by 1, once for the whole request, that single increment also covering the automatic enrolment move described below. The difference is only in `risk_tier_effective_at`: fresh anchor sets it to the new anchor instant, amendment anchor leaves it pointing at the preserved original anchor instant.

Two side effects hang off the computed tier. When the tier is `CRITICAL`, an escalation opens automatically with `source_flag` of `CRITICAL_SDOH_FINDING`, `source_screening_id` naming this screening, `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM`, and `status` of `OPEN_TIER_1`. That duplicate test is scoped to escalations whose `source_screening_id` names *this* row: it opens only when no escalation already exists for this screening in any of `OPEN_TIER_1`, `OPEN_TIER_2`, `OPEN_TIER_3` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, so a repeated critical amendment does not stack duplicates, while a critical amendment made after the prior escalation has been `RESOLVED` opens a fresh one. On the fresh-anchor path the test can never match, because the row is brand new, so a critical fresh anchor always opens an escalation even when the superseded predecessor still carries one. Separately, when the patient's `enrollment_status` is `PROSPECTIVE` and the computed tier is `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, the patient is automatically moved to `ENROLLED` as part of the same request; a `LOW` tier leaves them `PROSPECTIVE`.

Screenings can be read individually and listed. The listing filters on `patient_id`, `instrument_id` and `status`.

### 3.3. The Community Resource Network and the Eligibility Gauntlet

Community resource organizations are registered by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `name`, an `org_type` of `FOOD`, `HOUSING` or `TRANSPORT`, a non-empty `service_zips` array of five-character numeric ZIP codes, and a `contact_email` used as the closed-loop acknowledgement contact — required as a non-empty string, but not checked for address form. `service_zips` is likewise a recorded attribute only: geography matching runs off a `GEOGRAPHY` criterion's own `allowed_zips`, never off the organization's service area, and no operation in this specification ever consults `service_zips`. An organization is created with `status` of `ACTIVE`. A `ProgramAdmin` may later supply an `action` of `SUSPEND` to move `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, or `REINSTATE` to move `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`; either toggle increments the organization's `version` by 1, and requesting a toggle from the wrong state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Suspension is forward-looking only: existing referrals against a suspended organization's offerings are left entirely untouched and may still be responded to, delivered and closed. What suspension blocks is new business — sending or re-routing a referral to an offering whose organization is `SUSPENDED` is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_RESOURCE_ORG_SUSPENDED`.

Each organization publishes one or more **offerings**, the consumable inventory the program draws on. An offering names its `resource_org_id`, which must resolve to a registered organization at rung 6, a `unit_type` of `FOOD_BOX`, `HOUSING_VOUCHER` or `TRANSPORT_RIDE`, and a `capacity_total` of at least 1 which is fixed at creation and never changes. `unit_type` is not constrained by the organization's `org_type`: a `FOOD` organization may publish a `TRANSPORT_RIDE` offering, and creating an offering under a `SUSPENDED` organization is permitted — suspension blocks only the sending and re-routing of referrals. At creation the offering's `available_units` equal its `capacity_total`, while `allocated_units`, `delivered_units`, `released_units`, `expired_units` and `lifetime_allocated_units` all start at 0, `availability_state` is `AVAILABLE`, and `exhausted_since` is null. `available_units` counts what a new referral may still hold; `allocated_units` counts what referrals currently in `SENT` or `ACCEPTED` are holding; `delivered_units` counts what was consumed by referrals that reached `SERVICE_DELIVERED` and never returns to circulation. `released_units` accumulates units handed back by a declined referral or by a referral lapsed under `ACK_SLA_EXPIRED` or `RECORDS_HOLD`, and `expired_units` accumulates units handed back by the lazy hold-expiry sweep; both are cumulative tallies rather than members of the live partition. Closing a referral moves no units in either direction and does not touch the offering row at all: a referral closed out of `DECLINED` released its units at the moment of the decline, and one closed out of `SERVICE_DELIVERED` consumed them at delivery. `lifetime_allocated_units` is a monotonic count of every unit ever placed on hold. `availability_state` is `AVAILABLE` for as long as `available_units` is greater than 0, and flips to `EXHAUSTED` the moment `available_units` reaches 0, at which point `exhausted_since` is stamped with the current logical clock; when units return and `available_units` rises above 0 again, the state flips back to `AVAILABLE` and `exhausted_since` returns to null. Offerings can be read individually and listed, with the listing filtering on `resource_org_id`, `unit_type` and `availability_state`.

Attached to an offering is any number of **eligibility criteria**, created by a `ProgramAdmin` under the offering's own route so that the criterion's `offering_id` comes from the path rather than the body. Each criterion declares a `criterion_type` of `INCOME_BAND`, `GEOGRAPHY`, `HOUSEHOLD_SIZE`, `PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT` or `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`. The criterion type also fixes the criterion's `evaluation_rank`, which is derived by the server and can never be supplied by the client: `INCOME_BAND` is rank 1, `GEOGRAPHY` is rank 2, `HOUSEHOLD_SIZE` is rank 3, `PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT` is rank 4 and `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION` is rank 5. The remaining criterion fields are type-specific and each defaults to null — or, for the array, to empty — when the criterion is of another type: `max_monthly_income_cents` on an income band, `allowed_zips` on a geography criterion, `min_household_size` and `max_household_size` on a household-size criterion, `required_program_code` on a program-enrolment criterion, and `excluded_flag_code` on a categorical exclusion. A field belonging to the criterion's *own* type is optional too: omitting it stores null — or, for `allowed_zips`, an empty array — rather than failing validation, and the criterion is then evaluated with that null in place. An `INCOME_BAND` with no `max_monthly_income_cents`, a `HOUSEHOLD_SIZE` with neither bound, a `PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT` with no `required_program_code` and a `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION` with no `excluded_flag_code` therefore pass trivially for every patient, whereas a `GEOGRAPHY` criterion whose `allowed_zips` is empty fails every patient. `allowed_zips` entries need only be strings; unlike an organization's `service_zips` they are not checked for five-digit numeric form. An `evaluation_rank` or an `offering_id` supplied in the body is ignored rather than rejected, since the rank comes from `criterion_type` and the offering comes from the path.

**Running the gauntlet.** Whenever a patient must be matched to an offering, every criterion attached to that offering is evaluated in a fixed order: `evaluation_rank` ascending, with `created_at` ascending — never `id` — breaking ties between two criteria carrying the same rank. Criteria have no lifecycle and therefore no status filter; all of them participate. Evaluation walks the ordered list and stops at the **first** failure, reporting that criterion's error and never looking at the criteria behind it. An offering with no criteria at all passes trivially, having evaluated zero of them. The five checks, each with the error it raises, are:

- An `INCOME_BAND` criterion passes when the patient's `monthly_income_cents` is less than or equal to the criterion's `max_monthly_income_cents`; income exactly at the band ceiling passes. Failure raises `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_INCOME`.
- A `GEOGRAPHY` criterion passes when the patient's `residence_zip` is a member of the criterion's `allowed_zips`. Failure raises `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_GEOGRAPHY`.
- A `HOUSEHOLD_SIZE` criterion passes when the patient's `household_size` is greater than or equal to `min_household_size` and less than or equal to `max_household_size`; both ends are inclusive, so a household sitting exactly on either bound passes. Failure raises `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE`.
- A `PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT` criterion passes when the criterion's `required_program_code` is a member of the patient's `program_enrollments`. Failure raises `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT`.
- A `CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION` criterion passes when the criterion's `excluded_flag_code` is **not** a member of the patient's `categorical_exclusions`; presence of the flag is what fails. Failure raises `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_CATEGORICAL_EXCLUSION`.

All five failures carry HTTP 422 and all five sit at rung 12 of the ladder, which is why an ineligible patient against a suspended organization reports the suspension (rung 9) rather than the ineligibility, and why an ineligible patient against an exhausted offering reports the ineligibility rather than the shortage (rung 16).

The gauntlet is also exposed on its own, as a dry run that creates nothing. A `CHW`, `Supervisor` or `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `patient_id` against an offering and receives HTTP 200 whatever the outcome — an ineligible patient is a successful answer, not an error. The response reports `eligible` as a boolean, `evaluated_count` as the number of criteria in the ordered list, and three fields describing the stopping point: `first_failing_criterion_id`, `first_failing_criterion_type` and `first_failing_error_id`, which names the error identifier the same failure would have produced on a real referral. When `eligible` is true, all three of those fields are null. This capability has no side effects of any kind, and its listing companion returns an offering's criteria already sorted into evaluation order.

### 3.4. Closed-Loop Referrals: Holding, Responding, Re-Routing and Closing

A referral is the program's promise to a patient and its claim on a partner's inventory, and it is tracked as a closed loop from `SENT` all the way to `CLOSED`. Only a `CHW` may originate one, supplying the `patient_id`, the `offering_id` of the offering whose units are being claimed, the `opened_by_chw_id` of the worker sending it, an `urgency`, a `units_requested` count of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_offering_version`.

Five conditions must hold before a referral comes into existence, and they are checked in ladder order. The organization owning the offering must be `ACTIVE`, or the request fails at rung 9 with `ERR_RESOURCE_ORG_SUSPENDED`. The patient's `enrollment_status` must be `ENROLLED`, or it fails at the same rung with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED` — and because the suspension test is evaluated first within that rung, a request that is wrong on both counts reports the suspension. The patient must clear every eligibility criterion on the offering, in rank order, at rung 12. The offering's `available_units` must be greater than or equal to `units_requested` at rung 16, or the request fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNITS`; requesting exactly the remaining units succeeds and drives the offering to `EXHAUSTED`, while requesting one unit more fails. And the patient must not be on a records hold, checked all the way back at rung 4.

Creating the referral fires a three-link cascade. The referral itself is created in `SENT` with `sent_at` set to the current logical clock, `root_referral_id` equal to its own `id`, `parent_referral_id` null and `reroute_count` 0, and with `ack_deadline_at` and `hold_expires_at` computed as described below; simultaneously the hold is placed on the offering, raising `allocated_units` by `units_requested`, lowering `available_units` by the same amount, raising `lifetime_allocated_units` by the same amount, and incrementing the offering's `version` by 1. The second link fires only when that subtraction brings `available_units` to exactly 0: the offering's `availability_state` becomes `EXHAUSTED` and `exhausted_since` is stamped with the current logical clock. The third link, caused by the second, opens an escalation with `source_flag` of `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`, `blocking_offering_id` naming this offering, `patient_id` naming this referral's patient, `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM` and `status` of `OPEN_TIER_1`.

**Acknowledgement deadlines.** The `urgency` selects how long the partner has to respond: `ROUTINE` allows 72 hours, `URGENT` allows 24 hours, and `EMERGENT` allows just 4 hours. How that allowance is anchored depends on whether the referral is a root or a re-routed child, and this is a genuine fork in behaviour. Under the **fresh-SLA** variant, which applies whenever `parent_referral_id` is null, `ack_deadline_at` is this referral's own `sent_at` plus the urgency's hours, so a root referral always gets the full window. Under the **root-chain-budget** variant, which applies whenever `parent_referral_id` is not null, the whole re-route chain shares one acknowledgement budget: `ack_deadline_at` is the *root* referral's `sent_at` plus the urgency's hours. A re-routed referral therefore inherits an already-consumed window and can be born with `ack_deadline_at` already in the past, in which case the very next lapse sweep lapses it immediately. `hold_expires_at`, by contrast, has no such subtlety in either variant — it is always this row's own `sent_at` plus 168 hours, that is, seven days.

**The partner's response.** A `ResourcePartner` whose `X-User-ID` equals the offering's `resource_org_id` responds to a `SENT` referral with a `decision` of either `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`, optionally supplying a `decline_reason`; a partner acting on someone else's referral is rejected at rung 10 with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. `ACCEPTED` moves the referral to `ACCEPTED` and retains the hold on the offering exactly as it stood, incrementing only the referral's `version` by 1; a `decline_reason` sent alongside an `ACCEPTED` decision is ignored and never stored. `DECLINED` moves the referral to `DECLINED`, records the supplied reason in `decline_reason` — null when none was supplied — and releases the hold: `allocated_units` falls by `units_requested`, `available_units` rises by the same amount, `released_units` rises by the same amount, and the offering's `version` increments by 1. If that release lifts `available_units` from 0 to a positive number, the offering's `availability_state` flips from `EXHAUSTED` back to `AVAILABLE`, `exhausted_since` is cleared to null, and every escalation with `source_flag` of `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` and `blocking_offering_id` naming this offering whose `status` is `OPEN_TIER_1`, `OPEN_TIER_2` or `OPEN_TIER_3` is resolved with `resolution_reason` of `RESOURCE_RESTORED`. When the offering was not exhausted to begin with, none of that restore-and-resolve step runs: `availability_state` and `exhausted_since` are left exactly as they stand and no escalation is touched — and escalations already in `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `RESOLVED` are never swept up by the auto-resolve even when the offering does return to `AVAILABLE`. Finally, a decline increments the patient's `unfulfilled_referral_count` by 1; when that count reaches exactly 2, an escalation opens with `source_flag` of `REFERRAL_UNFULFILLED`, `source_referral_id` naming this referral, `opened_by_user_id` of `SYSTEM` and `status` of `OPEN_TIER_1`.

**Lapsing.** A referral can leave the loop unfulfilled by two different routes, and they charge different counters. A `SENT` referral whose `ack_deadline_at` the logical clock has passed — strictly after, never at — is lapsed by the referral-lapse sweep with `lapse_reason` of `ACK_SLA_EXPIRED`. A `SENT` referral belonging to a patient who has just been placed on a records hold is lapsed by that cascade with `lapse_reason` of `RECORDS_HOLD`. Both routes release the hold into `released_units` exactly as a decline does — `allocated_units` down, `available_units` up, `released_units` up, offering `version` up — and both apply the same restore-and-auto-resolve step if `available_units` rises from 0, and both increment the patient's `unfulfilled_referral_count` with the same escalation at exactly 2. The third route is different: an `ACCEPTED` referral whose `hold_expires_at` the clock has passed is lapsed by the hold-expiry sweep with `lapse_reason` of `HOLD_EXPIRED`, and its units are returned into `expired_units` rather than `released_units` — `allocated_units` falls, `available_units` rises, `expired_units` rises, and the offering's `version` increments. That route too increments `unfulfilled_referral_count` and opens the same escalation at exactly 2. Holds expire lazily: nothing expires on a read, and nothing expires until a sweep is run.

**Delivery and closure.** A `ResourcePartner` owning the offering records delivery on an `ACCEPTED` referral, which moves it to `SERVICE_DELIVERED`, stamps `delivered_at` with the current logical clock, and consumes the hold: `allocated_units` falls by `units_requested` and `delivered_units` rises by the same amount, with the offering's `version` incrementing by 1. `available_units` is emphatically **not** raised — delivered units leave the pool permanently, which is precisely why they sit in their own partition member. A `CHW` or a `Supervisor` then closes the loop by supplying an `outcome_note` — a mandatory non-empty string — which moves a referral from `SERVICE_DELIVERED` to `CLOSED` or from `DECLINED` to `CLOSED`, stamping `closed_at` with the current logical clock and storing the note. Closure carries no ownership requirement and writes only the referral row: the offering, the patient's counters and every escalation are untouched. Only a `CLOSED` referral whose `delivered_at` is non-null counts as a genuinely closed loop in the program report; a referral closed out of `DECLINED` is counted as closed-without-delivery.

**Re-routing.** When a partner declines, a `CHW` may re-route the referral to an alternate offering by supplying that alternate's `offering_id`, an `urgency`, a `units_requested` and the alternate's `expected_offering_version`. Re-routing carries every precondition that originating a referral carries, evaluated at the same rungs against the alternate offering: the patient must not be on a records hold at rung 4, the supplied version must match the alternate offering's current `version` at rung 7, the patient's `enrollment_status` must still be `ENROLLED` at rung 9, the alternate offering's organization must be `ACTIVE` at the same rung, the patient must clear every eligibility criterion attached to the alternate offering in rank order at rung 12, and the alternate offering's `available_units` must be at least `units_requested` at rung 16. Two preconditions are specific to re-routing and sit between those: the referral being re-routed must be in `DECLINED`, checked at rung 8, and the chain's `reroute_count` must be strictly less than 2, checked at rung 14 — a chain already re-routed twice cannot be re-routed a third time and is rejected with `ERR_REROUTE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The declined row itself is not re-stated — it stays `DECLINED` for audit. Instead a **new** referral is created in `SENT` with `parent_referral_id` naming the declined row, `root_referral_id` inherited from the declined row's own `root_referral_id`, `reroute_count` one higher than the chain's current value, and `opened_by_chw_id` copied from the declined row rather than taken from the acting caller — re-routing carries no ownership requirement of its own, so any `CHW` may re-route any `DECLINED` referral while the chain keeps recording its original opener. That incremented `reroute_count` is then written onto every **pre-existing** referral row sharing the same `root_referral_id`, incrementing each of those rows' `version` by 1, so the chain's re-route budget is readable from any member; the new child already carries the incremented value and, being a row born in this request, is returned at `version` 1. The response is HTTP 201 carrying that new child, never the declined parent. The new row places a hold on the alternate offering exactly as a root referral does, including the exhaustion cascade and its escalation, and computes its `ack_deadline_at` under the root-chain-budget variant. This is the referral machine's cycle: the chain returns to `SENT` on a fresh row while its declined predecessor is retained.

Referrals can be read individually and listed, with the listing filtering on `patient_id`, `offering_id`, `opened_by_chw_id`, `parent_referral_id`, `root_referral_id` and `status` — the last two together let an operator pull an entire re-route chain or find every child of a given parent.

### 3.5. Home Visits: Two Lifecycles on One Row

A home visit carries **two independent state axes** that evolve separately and never constrain each other's transitions. `scheduling_status` tracks where the visit is in the field, moving among `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED`. `documentation_status` tracks the paperwork, moving among `DRAFT`, `ATTESTED`, `AMENDED` and `VOIDED`. Billing depends on the second axis only: an encounter linked to a visit can be submitted only once that visit's documentation is `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`.

A `CHW` or a `Supervisor` schedules a visit by supplying the `patient_id`, the `assigned_chw_id` of the worker who will own and attest it, a `scheduled_start`, a `scheduled_end`, and optionally a `second_chw_id`, which stores null when omitted. `assigned_chw_id`, and `second_chw_id` when supplied, must each resolve to a registered worker at rung 6. The patient must be `ENROLLED`, or the request is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. `scheduled_end` must be strictly after `scheduled_start`, and the resulting duration must be at least 15 minutes and at most 240 minutes, both bounds inclusive; a shorter or longer slot is `ERR_VALIDATION`. When `second_chw_id` is supplied it must differ from `assigned_chw_id`, or the payload is likewise rejected.

Two further checks then apply in ladder order. At rung 11, the safety rule: when the patient's `address_safety_flag` is true, a `second_chw_id` is **mandatory**, and omitting it is rejected with `ERR_TWO_PERSON_VISIT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The visit records the outcome of that rule in `two_person_required`, copied from the patient's `address_safety_flag` at creation. At rung 13, the slot-collision rule: for each worker named on the new visit — in the `assigned_chw_id` role or the `second_chw_id` role, since both consume that worker's time — the server looks for an existing visit naming that same worker in either role whose `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `COMPLETED`. A collision exists when the new slot's `scheduled_start` falls strictly before the existing slot's `scheduled_end` and the existing slot's `scheduled_start` falls strictly before the new slot's `scheduled_end`, and it is rejected with `ERR_VISIT_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the interval is half-open, a new visit starting at exactly the instant an existing one ends is accepted. Visits already in `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` never participate in the check at all. A newly created visit begins with `scheduling_status` of `SCHEDULED`, `documentation_status` of `DRAFT`, `amendment_count` 0, and `completed_at`, `attested_at` and `visit_note` all null.

Along the scheduling axis, the assigned worker departs — moving `SCHEDULED` to `EN_ROUTE` — and later completes the visit, moving `EN_ROUTE` to `COMPLETED` and stamping `completed_at` with the current logical clock, which is the instant that starts the 72-hour attestation clock. Both of those transitions require the acting `CHW`'s `X-User-ID` to equal `assigned_chw_id`; anyone else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A no-show can be recorded from either `SCHEDULED` or `EN_ROUTE`, and a cancellation can likewise be taken from either `SCHEDULED` or `EN_ROUTE`; unlike departure and completion, neither of those two operations carries an ownership requirement, and both are open to a `CHW` or a `Supervisor`. A visit in `NO_SHOW` keeps `documentation_status` at `DRAFT` forever and can never be attested, since attestation requires `COMPLETED`. A visit in `CANCELLED` stops participating in slot-overlap checks, freeing that worker's time. Cancellation is one of the few write operations exempt from the records-hold gate, precisely so that an in-flight visit on a newly held patient can always be stood down.

Along the documentation axis, the assigned worker attests the visit by supplying a `visit_note`. Attestation requires `documentation_status` to be `DRAFT`, requires `scheduling_status` to be `COMPLETED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `assigned_chw_id`, and requires the elapsed time from `completed_at` to the current logical clock, measured as an exact duration rather than in calendar units, to be no more than 72 hours — exactly 72 hours still attests, and anything beyond is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_ATTESTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). A successful attestation sets `documentation_status` to `ATTESTED` and stamps `attested_at`.

Afterwards, documentation is revised through a single operation that takes an `action` of `AMEND` or `VOID` and an optional `visit_note`. The two actions have different owners, and the mismatch is caught at rung 10: `AMEND` requires the caller to be a `CHW` whose `X-User-ID` equals `assigned_chw_id`, while `VOID` requires the caller to be a `Supervisor`; a caller who fails the applicable test gets `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). `AMEND` applies from `ATTESTED`, moving documentation to `AMENDED`, overwriting `visit_note` and incrementing `amendment_count` by 1; it also applies from `AMENDED` back to `AMENDED` as a self-loop, again overwriting the note and again incrementing the count, so a visit can be amended any number of times. `visit_note` is optional on the revision, and its absence is not the same as a null: when the field is missing from the body the stored note is left exactly as it was, and only when it is present and null is the note cleared. `attested_at` is never re-stamped by an amendment. Every amendment must fall within 30 days — exactly 720 hours of elapsed time — measured from the **original** `attested_at`, never from the most recent amendment; past that, the request is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). `VOID` applies from either `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`, ignores any `visit_note` sent with it, sets `documentation_status` to `VOIDED`, and cascades: every encounter whose `home_visit_id` names this visit and whose `status` is `DRAFT` is itself voided, incrementing that encounter's `version` by 1 while leaving its `funding_source` at `UNFUNDED`; encounters on the visit in `SUBMITTED`, `LOCKED` or `VOIDED` are left entirely untouched, and the cascade goes no further than those encounter rows.

Visits can be read individually — the read returns both axes — and listed, with the listing filtering on `patient_id`, `assigned_chw_id`, `second_chw_id`, `scheduling_status` and `documentation_status`.

### 3.6. Care-Plan Goals and Measured Progress

A care-plan goal makes a social-need intervention measurable. A `CHW` or a `Supervisor` creates one for an `ENROLLED` patient — a patient in any other enrollment state is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED` — by supplying the `patient_id`, a `domain` of `FOOD`, `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORT`, `EMPLOYMENT` or `HEALTH_LITERACY`, a `direction`, a `baseline_value` measured at creation, a `target_value` that counts as full achievement, and optionally a `review_cycle_days` interval of at least 1 day which defaults to 30.

The `direction` declares which way progress runs and constrains the target accordingly. Under `INCREASE_GOOD` — think monthly grocery-budget dollars, or kept-appointment counts — `target_value` must be strictly greater than `baseline_value`. Under `DECREASE_GOOD` — think missed medication doses, or emergency-department visits — `target_value` must be strictly less than `baseline_value`. Violating either constraint is `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new goal is created `ACTIVE` with `current_value` initialized to `baseline_value`, `progress_pct` of 0.0, `reopen_count` 0, `closed_at` null, and `review_due_at` set to the current logical clock plus `review_cycle_days` days.

A `CHW` records a new measurement by supplying a `current_value`; `baseline_value` and `target_value` are immutable after creation, so the measurement is the only moving input. Each measurement recomputes `progress_pct` under the goal's direction variant and resets `review_due_at` to the current logical clock plus `review_cycle_days` days. Recording progress carries no state precondition and no ownership check: any `CHW` may record against any goal, and a goal already closed in `MET`, `NOT_MET` or `DEFERRED` accepts the measurement just as an `ACTIVE` one does, recomputing `progress_pct` and resetting `review_due_at` while leaving `status`, `closed_at` and `reopen_count` exactly where they stand. Recording progress never re-opens a goal. Under `INCREASE_GOOD`, the raw percentage is 100 multiplied by the amount the current value has risen above baseline, divided by the total rise from baseline to target. Under `DECREASE_GOOD`, both the numerator and the denominator invert: the raw percentage is 100 multiplied by the amount the current value has fallen below baseline, divided by the total fall from baseline to target. The raw percentage is then rounded half up to one decimal place, and only then clamped into the range 0.0 through 100.0 — rounding first, clamping second. The inversion matters: a baseline of 40, a target of 20 and a current value of 30 yields 50.0 under `DECREASE_GOOD`, whereas the identical triple under `INCREASE_GOOD` produces a negative raw value that clamps to 0.0.

Closure takes an `outcome` of `MET`, `NOT_MET` or `DEFERRED`, and all three apply only from `ACTIVE`, stamping `closed_at` with the current logical clock. `MET` additionally requires `progress_pct` to be exactly 100.0; a goal short of full achievement cannot be marked met and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `NOT_MET` carries a consequence: it opens an escalation with `source_flag` of `GOAL_NOT_MET`, `patient_id` naming this goal's patient, `status` of `OPEN_TIER_1` and — unlike the system-opened escalations elsewhere in this document — `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting caller's `X-User-ID`, because a person made this call. `DEFERRED` is the quietest of the three and opens nothing; it is also the outcome the disenrollment cascade writes onto a patient's active goals.

Any closed goal can be re-opened, which is the goal machine's cycle. Re-opening applies from `MET`, from `NOT_MET` or from `DEFERRED` — checked at rung 8 — and in all three cases requires the patient's `enrollment_status` to be `ENROLLED`, checked at rung 9 and rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED`, which is why a goal deferred by a disenrollment cascade can only be revived after the patient has been re-enrolled. Because rung 8 precedes rung 9, re-opening a goal that is already `ACTIVE` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the patient is disenrolled. Re-opening sets `status` back to `ACTIVE`, clears `closed_at` to null, increments `reopen_count` by 1, and recomputes `review_due_at` as the current logical clock plus `review_cycle_days` days. Goals can be read individually with their derived `progress_pct`, and listed with filters on `patient_id`, `status` and `domain`.

### 3.7. Capturing Community Health Worker Time

Every minute a worker spends is recorded on an **encounter**, and encounters are the atoms both funding streams draw from. Only a `CHW` may create one, supplying the `patient_id` of an `ENROLLED` patient — any other enrollment state is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_ENROLLED` — the `chw_id` of the worker whose time this is, an `encounter_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and four minute buckets: `direct_minutes` for face-to-face or telephonic time with the patient, `indirect_minutes` for time spent on the patient's behalf without them present, `travel_minutes` for time getting to or from the encounter, and `documentation_minutes` for writing it up. Each of the four must be an integer of at least 0, and their sum must not exceed 480 minutes; a larger total is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Two optional inputs complete the payload: `travel_miles`, a number of at least 0 carried to one decimal place and defaulting to 0, and `home_visit_id`, naming the scheduled visit this encounter documents — left null for telephonic or field time not tied to a scheduled visit. `travel_miles` is stored and echoed back exactly as supplied; the server neither rounds nor quantizes it, and only the reimbursement derived from it is rounded. `home_visit_id`, when supplied, need only resolve to an existing home visit at rung 6: creation neither requires that visit to belong to the same patient nor inspects either of its state axes, which are examined only at submission.

Creation derives five values. `service_month` is the first seven characters of `encounter_date`, in `YYYY-MM` form, and is the monthly bucket every billing rule works in. `total_minutes` is the sum of all four minute buckets. `raw_billable_minutes` is the sum of `direct_minutes`, `indirect_minutes` and `documentation_minutes` — travel time is worked time but it is never billable time, so it is excluded here even though it counts toward `total_minutes`. `billable_minutes` is `raw_billable_minutes` rounded to the nearest 6-minute increment, half up: the raw figure is divided by 6, that quotient is rounded half up to a whole number, and the result is multiplied back by 6. Because the rounding is half up on the quotient, a raw value three above a multiple of six always rounds upward — a raw 21 becomes 24, a raw 27 becomes 30. Finally, `travel_reimbursement_cents` is computed from `travel_miles` under the per-encounter variant of the mileage rule.

**Mileage reimbursement.** Rates are marginal, never average, and there are three bands. The first 10 miles are paid at 67 cents per mile. Miles beyond the first 10 and up to 30 — that is, at most 20 miles' worth — are paid at 55 cents per mile. Every mile beyond 30 is paid at 40 cents per mile. The three band amounts are added together, the gross is rounded half up to whole cents, and the result is capped at 5000 cents. Under the **per-encounter** variant used at encounter creation, the calculation is applied to this one encounter's `travel_miles` and the 5000-cent cap binds on that single encounter. Under the **monthly aggregated** variant used by the program report, the same band walk is applied **once** to the sum of the month's `travel_miles`, so later miles fall into cheaper bands and the cap binds once on the monthly figure rather than per row. The two variants genuinely diverge: two separate 10-mile encounters produce 670 plus 670, or 1340 cents, per encounter, but the same 20 aggregate miles produce 10 miles at 67 plus 10 miles at 55, or 1220 cents, when computed monthly.

An encounter is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `funding_source` `UNFUNDED`, `funding_claim_id` null and `submitted_at` null. The owning worker — the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the encounter's `chw_id`, or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — then submits it, which moves `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at`. When the encounter carries a non-null `home_visit_id`, that visit's `documentation_status` must be `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED` at rung 9, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Submission does not change `funding_source`, which stays `UNFUNDED`; what it does is make the encounter eligible for the final billing input set.

From `SUBMITTED`, an encounter moves to `LOCKED` when its minutes are carved to a funding source — either by a final payer claim, which also sets `funding_source` to `PAYER` and writes the claim's `id` into `funding_claim_id`, or by a grant charge line, which sets `funding_source` to `GRANT`. Either way the encounter's `version` increments by 1. The reverse move exists too, and it is the encounter machine's cycle: a compensating action — reversing a grant charge line, or an appeal decision that upholds a denial — returns a `LOCKED` encounter to `SUBMITTED`, resets `funding_source` to `UNFUNDED`, clears `funding_claim_id` to null and increments `version` by 1. The minutes land back in the unfunded bucket with zero drift, and may then be carved again, to either funding source.

Voiding is a `Supervisor`'s privilege and is exempt from the records-hold gate, since corrections must remain possible while a hold is in force. An optional `reason` may accompany the request; it is accepted for the caller's convenience but is not persisted on the encounter, which carries no reason field. A `DRAFT` encounter and a `SUBMITTED` encounter both void directly to `VOIDED`, running no cascade of any kind and touching no charge line or award; an already-`VOIDED` encounter is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every void, whatever the starting status, also clears `funding_claim_id` back to null. A `LOCKED` encounter may be voided **only** when its `funding_source` is `GRANT`; a payer-locked encounter must have its claim resolved first, and attempting to void one is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding a grant-locked encounter runs a three-link cascade: the encounter's `status` becomes `VOIDED` and its `funding_source` returns to `UNFUNDED`; the charge line whose `encounter_id` names this encounter and whose `status` is `POSTED` is reversed, taking a `reversed_at` of the current logical clock, a `reversal_reason` of `ENCOUNTER_VOIDED` and a `status` of `REVERSED`; and the award behind that line has its `committed_cents` reduced by the line's `total_amount_cents`, its `remaining_budget_cents` raised by the same amount, and its `version` incremented by 1. A voided encounter keeps all of its minute fields but is excluded from every billing input set thereafter. Encounters can be read individually with all derived minute and money fields, and listed with filters on `patient_id`, `chw_id`, `home_visit_id`, `funding_claim_id`, `service_month`, `status` and `funding_source`.

### 3.8. Payer Billing: The Monthly Rollup and the Appeal Tree

A `BillingSpecialist` — and nobody else — creates a monthly care-management claim by supplying the `patient_id`, the `service_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and a `basis` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`. The basis is the fork that decides both what the claim counts and whether it touches anything.

**The input set.** Under either basis, the candidate encounters are those whose `patient_id` matches the claim's, whose `service_month` matches the claim's, and whose `funding_source` is `UNFUNDED`. The basis then filters by status. `PROVISIONAL` includes encounters in `DRAFT` **or** `SUBMITTED`. `FINAL` includes **only** encounters in `SUBMITTED`, which makes a final claim's minutes strictly smaller than the provisional estimate whenever any draft time exists for that month. Encounters in `VOIDED` or `LOCKED` never participate under either basis.

**The rollup.** The order of operations is pinned and is not interchangeable. First, each encounter in the input set is reduced to its own rounded billable figure — the sum of its `direct_minutes`, `indirect_minutes` and `documentation_minutes`, with travel excluded, divided by 6, rounded half up, and multiplied back by 6. Second, those **already-rounded** per-encounter values are added together to give `claimed_minutes`; the aggregate is never formed by summing raw minutes and rounding once at the end, and the two orders give different answers. `claimed_encounter_count` is simply how many encounters were in the input set. Third, the claim's `billing_code` is chosen by threshold: `claimed_minutes` of at least 120 selects `CM-120`; otherwise `claimed_minutes` of at least 60 selects `CM-60`; and anything below 60 is not billable at all and is rejected at rung 17 with `ERR_MINUTE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422) — exactly 60 minutes qualifies for `CM-60`, and 59 does not. A patient-and-month pair with no qualifying encounter at all yields `claimed_minutes` of 0 and is rejected on that same rung, so no claim row is ever created empty. Fourth, `units` is `claimed_minutes` divided by 15 and truncated downward to a whole number — floor, never rounding — and `allowed_amount_cents` is `units` multiplied by 2150 cents. Because ordering within the input set cannot change a plain sum of independently rounded values, the aggregate is order-independent.

**Anti-double-dip.** Before a `FINAL` claim is allowed to carve anything, rung 15 checks two things about the patient-and-month pair. No encounter for that pair may already have `funding_source` of `GRANT`, and no earlier `FINAL` claim for that pair may exist in `SUBMITTED`, `PAID`, `APPEALED` or `OVERTURNED`. Either condition rejects the request with `ERR_MINUTES_ALREADY_FUNDED` (HTTP 409). A prior claim in `UPHELD` does **not** block, because upholding a denial compensates the claim and releases its minutes back to the unfunded bucket. This check does not apply to `PROVISIONAL` claims, which carve nothing.

**What creation does.** A `FINAL` claim moves every encounter in its input set from `SUBMITTED` to `LOCKED`, setting each one's `funding_source` to `PAYER` and its `funding_claim_id` to the new claim's `id`. A `PROVISIONAL` claim mutates **no** encounter whatsoever: nothing locks, `funding_source` stays `UNFUNDED`, and the same minutes remain claimable later. Both bases create the claim in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `denial_reason`, `denied_at` and `appealed_at` all null.

The adjudication and appeal tree runs only on final claims. Every one of adjudication, appeal and appeal decision first requires the claim's `basis` to be `FINAL`; a provisional claim is an estimate and can never leave `SUBMITTED`, so any such attempt is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Adjudication takes an `outcome` of `PAID` or `DENIED` on a `SUBMITTED` claim. `PAID` moves the claim to `PAID` and leaves its encounters `LOCKED` with `funding_source` `PAYER`, ignoring any `denial_reason` sent alongside it and leaving both `denial_reason` and `denied_at` null. `DENIED` moves the claim to `DENIED`, records the supplied `denial_reason` — null when none was supplied — and stamps `denied_at` with the current logical clock — that instant anchors the appeal window — while the encounters likewise stay `LOCKED`, pending the appeal.

A `DENIED` claim may be appealed, moving it to `APPEALED` and stamping `appealed_at`, provided the elapsed time from `denied_at` to the current logical clock is no more than 60 days — exactly 60 times 24 hours, measured as a duration rather than in calendar units; exactly 60 days still appeals, and beyond that the request is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The appeal is then resolved with a `decision` of `OVERTURNED` or `UPHELD`, and the two branches are opposites. `OVERTURNED` moves the claim to `OVERTURNED` and changes nothing about the encounters — they stay `LOCKED` with `funding_source` `PAYER`, because the payer has conceded. `UPHELD` moves the claim to `UPHELD` and **compensates**: every encounter whose `funding_claim_id` names this claim *and* which still stands in `LOCKED` returns to `SUBMITTED`, with `funding_source` reset to `UNFUNDED`, `funding_claim_id` cleared to null and `version` incremented by 1, while `submitted_at` keeps its original value rather than being re-stamped; an encounter that has since left `LOCKED` is passed over untouched. The month's minute distribution is thereby restored to exactly what it was before the claim. The month is thereby claimable again — which is precisely why a prior `UPHELD` claim does not block a new final claim. Claims can be read individually and listed with filters on `patient_id`, `service_month`, `status` and `basis`.

### 3.9. Grant Funding: Awards, Rate Tiers and Charge Lines

The second funding stream is philanthropic. A `ProgramAdmin` creates a grant award with a `funder_name`, a `period_start` and a `period_end` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form where `period_end` is on or after `period_start`, and a `budget_cap_cents` of at least 1. Two optional inputs shape how the award prices work: `indirect_rate_pct`, a number between 0 and 100 inclusive that defaults to 10, and `allowable_cost_categories`, a non-empty subset of `DIRECT`, `INDIRECT` and `DOCUMENTATION` that defaults to all three. Travel is never an allowable cost category no matter what that list contains. The award is created with `status` `OPEN`, `committed_cents` of 0 and `remaining_budget_cents` equal to `budget_cap_cents`.

Award periods are closed intervals, and they may not overlap within a funder. At rung 13, a new award is rejected with `ERR_GRANT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when an existing award with the same `funder_name` and `status` of `OPEN` has a period that touches or overlaps the new one — that is, when the new `period_start` falls on or before the existing `period_end` and the existing `period_start` falls on or before the new `period_end`. `funder_name` is matched as an exact, case-sensitive string, and an award already in `CLOSED` never blocks a new period however completely it overlaps. Because the convention is closed rather than half-open, a new period beginning on the exact day an existing period ends **is** rejected. A `ProgramAdmin` may later close an award, moving `OPEN` to `CLOSED` and incrementing its `version` by 1; a `CLOSED` award accepts no further charge lines, and posting to one is rejected at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A `BillingSpecialist` charges one encounter's minutes to one award by supplying the `grant_award_id`, the `encounter_id`, and the mandatory `expected_grant_award_version`. The encounter must be in `SUBMITTED` — a `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` encounter is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and its `funding_source` must be `UNFUNDED`. An encounter already carved to a payer claim, or to another grant line, has a `funding_source` of `PAYER` or `GRANT` and is rejected at rung 15 with `ERR_MINUTES_ALREADY_FUNDED` (HTTP 409). Then, still at rung 15 but after the funding check, comes allowability: the encounter's `encounter_date` must fall inside the award's closed period — on or after `period_start` and on or before `period_end` — and the encounter must carry at least one minute in a category the award's `allowable_cost_categories` lists, where `DIRECT` corresponds to `direct_minutes`, `INDIRECT` to `indirect_minutes` and `DOCUMENTATION` to `documentation_minutes`. An encounter dated outside the period, or one whose only minutes are `travel_minutes`, is rejected with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_ALLOWABLE` (HTTP 422).

**Pricing the line.** The evaluation order is pinned, and one step in particular is easy to get wrong. First, `charged_minutes` is copied from the encounter's `billable_minutes` — the already-rounded figure, not the raw one. Second, the rate tier is selected from the award's `committed_cents` **as it stands before this line posts**, even though this very line is about to move that figure: when `committed_cents` is below 50000 cents, `hourly_rate_cents` is 4200; when `committed_cents` is 50000 cents or more, `hourly_rate_cents` is 3800. The tier break is inclusive on the upper tier — a `committed_cents` of exactly 50000 selects the 3800-cent rate. Third, `direct_amount_cents` is `charged_minutes` multiplied by `hourly_rate_cents`, divided by 60, and rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, `indirect_amount_cents` is `direct_amount_cents` multiplied by the award's `indirect_rate_pct`, divided by 100, and rounded half up to whole cents — indirect is a percentage of the direct amount alone, never of any combined figure. Fifth, `total_amount_cents` is `direct_amount_cents` plus `indirect_amount_cents`. Rounding happens twice and independently, once on each component; rounding a combined total in a single step gives a different figure and is wrong.

**The budget gate.** Sixth in that order, and at rung 17 of the ladder, the award's `committed_cents` plus this line's `total_amount_cents` must not exceed `budget_cap_cents`. A line that would breach the cap is rejected outright with `ERR_GRANT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); it is never trimmed to fit the remaining budget. Seventh and last, on success, the award's `committed_cents` rises by `total_amount_cents` and its `remaining_budget_cents` is recomputed as `budget_cap_cents` minus the new `committed_cents`, with the award's `version` incrementing by 1. The line itself is created with `status` `POSTED`, `reversed_at` null and `reversal_reason` null, and the encounter it charges transitions from `SUBMITTED` to `LOCKED` with `funding_source` of `GRANT`.

Reversal is the compensating action, available to a `BillingSpecialist` and exempt from the records-hold gate. Reversing a `POSTED` line stamps `reversed_at` with the current logical clock and moves `status` to `REVERSED`; the line's `reversal_reason` takes the supplied value when one is given and defaults to `MANUAL_REVERSAL` when it is omitted, while the value `ENCOUNTER_VOIDED` is what the void cascade of §3.7 writes. The award's `committed_cents` falls by the line's `total_amount_cents`, its `remaining_budget_cents` rises by exactly the same amount and its `version` increments by 1 — a reversal subtracts precisely what the posting added, with no drift. The encounter returns from `LOCKED` to `SUBMITTED` with `funding_source` back to `UNFUNDED` and `funding_claim_id` null, making its minutes claimable again by either stream — but only when it is still standing in `LOCKED`. An encounter that has already been voided, or has otherwise left `LOCKED`, is passed over untouched while the money still returns to the award; that is exactly the state the void cascade of §3.7 leaves behind, where the encounter is set to `VOIDED` before the line is reversed. Note that `committed_cents` always counts only lines in `POSTED`; reversed lines are excluded from it, and therefore also from the tier selection the next line will see. Awards can be read individually with their committed and remaining budgets, and charge lines can be read individually or listed with filters on `grant_award_id`, `encounter_id` and `status`.

### 3.10. Escalations, Lazy Sweeps and Program Reporting

**Escalations** are the program's attention mechanism, and they fan in from five sources. Four of them are written by cascades described elsewhere in this document: `CRITICAL_SDOH_FINDING` when a screening lands at critical risk, with `source_screening_id` naming that screening; `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` when a hold drives an offering's `available_units` to 0, with `blocking_offering_id` naming that offering; `REFERRAL_UNFULFILLED` when a patient's `unfulfilled_referral_count` reaches 2, with `source_referral_id` naming the referral that pushed it there; and `GOAL_NOT_MET` when a care-plan goal is closed unmet. The fifth is `MANUAL`, opened by a `CHW` or a `Supervisor` supplying a `patient_id` and an optional `note`. On a manual escalation the `source_flag` is always `MANUAL` and `opened_by_user_id` is the caller's own `X-User-ID`; on a cascade-opened escalation the `note` is null and `opened_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM` — with the single exception of the goal-not-met cascade, which records the acting caller's `X-User-ID` because a human closed the goal. Each of the three polymorphic source references is null unless its own `source_flag` is the one that writes it. Every escalation, however it arises, is born at `status` `OPEN_TIER_1` with `current_tier` 1, `promotion_count` 0, `acknowledged_by_user_id` null, `resolution_reason` null, `tier_entered_at` set to the current logical clock and `ack_deadline_at` computed from that instant.

**The tier ladder.** An escalation's acknowledgement deadline is `tier_entered_at` plus the hours its tier allows: 48 hours at tier 1, 24 hours at tier 2, and 8 hours at tier 3. When the escalation-promotion sweep runs and finds an escalation in `OPEN_TIER_1` whose `ack_deadline_at` the logical clock is **strictly** past, that escalation moves to `OPEN_TIER_2`, its `current_tier` becomes 2, its `tier_entered_at` is re-stamped with the current logical clock, its `promotion_count` increments by 1, and its `ack_deadline_at` is recomputed as the new `tier_entered_at` plus 24 hours. The same sweep moves an over-deadline `OPEN_TIER_2` escalation to `OPEN_TIER_3`, setting `current_tier` to 3, re-stamping `tier_entered_at`, incrementing `promotion_count` and recomputing `ack_deadline_at` with 8 hours. Because each promotion re-stamps the entry instant, every tier gets a full, fresh window. Tier 3 is the ceiling: an `OPEN_TIER_3` escalation that is past its deadline is left entirely untouched by any later sweep — no state change, no field change and no `version` bump. A sweep run at exactly `ack_deadline_at` promotes nothing, because the comparison is strictly after.

**Acknowledgement and resolution.** A `Supervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin` acknowledges an escalation from `OPEN_TIER_1`, `OPEN_TIER_2` or `OPEN_TIER_3`, in each case requiring that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the escalation's `opened_by_user_id` — an operator may not acknowledge their own escalation, and doing so is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Acknowledgement sets `status` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, records the acting identifier in `acknowledged_by_user_id`, and freezes `current_tier` at whichever tier the escalation had reached — 1, 2 or 3 respectively; `tier_entered_at`, `ack_deadline_at` and `promotion_count` are all left exactly where the last promotion put them, and an `ACKNOWLEDGED` escalation is never promoted again by any sweep. An `ACKNOWLEDGED` escalation is then resolved by a `Supervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin`, moving `status` to `RESOLVED` with `resolution_reason` of `MANUAL_RESOLUTION` and leaving `acknowledged_by_user_id` and every tier field untouched; resolving an escalation that is not `ACKNOWLEDGED` — one still open, or one already `RESOLVED` — is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. There is also a system path to resolution that skips acknowledgement entirely: when an offering that had gone `EXHAUSTED` returns to `AVAILABLE`, every escalation with `source_flag` `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` and `blocking_offering_id` naming that offering is auto-resolved from `OPEN_TIER_1`, from `OPEN_TIER_2` or from `OPEN_TIER_3` alike, taking `resolution_reason` of `RESOURCE_RESTORED`, leaving `acknowledged_by_user_id` null and leaving `current_tier`, `tier_entered_at`, `ack_deadline_at` and `promotion_count` at whatever values they already held. Escalations already in `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `RESOLVED`, and escalations carrying any other `source_flag`, are never swept up by it. Both acknowledgement and resolution are exempt from the records-hold gate, so open escalations always remain closeable. Escalations can be read individually and listed with filters on `patient_id`, `source_flag`, `status`, `source_referral_id`, `source_screening_id` and `blocking_offering_id`.

**Lazy sweeps.** Nothing in this system expires on a read. Deadlines and holds are only enforced when a `ProgramAdmin` runs a sweep, naming a `sweep_type` of `REFERRAL_LAPSE`, `OFFERING_HOLD_EXPIRY` or `ESCALATION_PROMOTION`. A run of `REFERRAL_LAPSE` visits referrals in `SENT` whose `ack_deadline_at` the clock is strictly past and lapses each with `lapse_reason` of `ACK_SLA_EXPIRED`, returning their units into `released_units`. A run of `OFFERING_HOLD_EXPIRY` visits referrals in `ACCEPTED` whose `hold_expires_at` the clock is strictly past and lapses each with `lapse_reason` of `HOLD_EXPIRED`, returning their units into `expired_units`. A run of `ESCALATION_PROMOTION` promotes open escalations as described above, visiting only rows in `OPEN_TIER_1` and `OPEN_TIER_2`; `OPEN_TIER_3`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `RESOLVED` escalations are skipped outright. Within each sweep type, rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, with the server-assigned creation sequence breaking the ties that the standing logical clock inevitably produces. Every sweep is exempt from the records-hold gate, as is every cascade it fires. The response echoes the `sweep_type` and reports four counters: `referrals_lapsed`, the number of referrals moved to `LAPSED` by this run; `holds_expired`, the number of accepted holds this run expired; `escalations_promoted`, the number of tier promotions this run applied; and `units_returned`, the total number of offering units this run returned to `available_units`. Counters that a given sweep type cannot affect are reported as 0 — an escalation-promotion run reports zero lapsed referrals, zero expired holds and zero units returned. The response is HTTP 200 carrying that derived five-field object rather than any entity, and a sweep that matches no row writes nothing at all: no `version` anywhere moves, and all four counters read 0.

**The program report.** A `Supervisor`, `ProgramAdmin` or `BillingSpecialist` requests a derived program summary for a `service_month` under a `denominator_basis` of either `ENROLLED_ONLY` or `EVER_ENROLLED`. Both arrive as query-string parameters and both are mandatory, validated at rung 5: a missing or malformed `service_month`, or a missing or off-enum `denominator_basis`, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). `denominator_basis` is the one query parameter in this specification that *is* enum-checked, in contrast with the list filters of §2.3. Nothing in this report is stored on any entity; every figure is recomputed on read, and the response is a derived object carrying the eight figures below and nothing else — no `id`, no `version` and no pagination fields.

The denominator is the report's pivot and its dual variant. Under `ENROLLED_ONLY`, it is the set of patients whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED`. Under `EVER_ENROLLED`, it is the set of patients whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED` **or** `DISENROLLED`. Patients still in `PROSPECTIVE` are excluded under both variants. `patient_denominator_count` reports the size of that set, and every other figure in the report restricts itself to patients inside it. The choice genuinely changes the answers: disenrolling a patient shrinks the `ENROLLED_ONLY` denominator and can therefore *raise* the coverage percentage without a single screening being performed, whereas under `EVER_ENROLLED` that same patient stays in the denominator and drags coverage down if their screening is stale.

`screening_coverage_pct` counts the denominator patients who have a screening in `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED` whose `anchor_screened_at` is no more than 365 days — exactly 365 times 24 hours of elapsed time — before the current logical clock, each such patient counting once however many of their screenings qualify, and screenings in `SUPERSEDED` never counting at all. It then reports 100 multiplied by that count, divided by `patient_denominator_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place. An empty denominator yields 0.0 rather than a division error. This is where the anchoring fork of §3.2 pays off: an amendment preserves `anchor_screened_at` and so does not refresh coverage, while a fresh anchor restarts the 365-day clock.

`referral_loop_closure_pct` looks at the denominator patients' referrals whose `status` is `SERVICE_DELIVERED`, `CLOSED`, `DECLINED` or `LAPSED` — the resolved ones — and counts how many of those are in `CLOSED` with a non-null `delivered_at`. It reports 100 multiplied by that count, divided by the number of resolved referrals, rounded half up to one decimal place, or 0.0 when no referral has resolved.

The minute figures scope to the requested `service_month` and to encounters in `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED`; `DRAFT` and `VOIDED` encounters never count. `payer_minutes` sums `billable_minutes` over those encounters whose `funding_source` is `PAYER`, `grant_minutes` over those whose `funding_source` is `GRANT`, and `unfunded_minutes` over those whose `funding_source` is `UNFUNDED`. Those three figures are whole integers, already rounded at the encounter level, and by the minute-conservation invariant of §2.10 they add up to the month's total billable minutes with nothing left over. `travel_reimbursement_monthly_cents` applies the monthly aggregated variant of the mileage rule to the sum of `travel_miles` over that same encounter set. Finally, `open_escalation_count` counts the denominator patients' escalations whose `status` is `OPEN_TIER_1`, `OPEN_TIER_2` or `OPEN_TIER_3`, of every `source_flag` alike. Only the four minute-and-mileage figures are scoped to the requested `service_month`; `screening_coverage_pct`, `referral_loop_closure_pct` and `open_escalation_count` ignore it entirely and range over all time.
