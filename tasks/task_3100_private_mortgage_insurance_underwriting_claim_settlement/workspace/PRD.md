# Private Mortgage Insurance Underwriting and Claim Settlement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Private mortgage insurance sits between a lender's origination desk and the secondary market: it lets a borrower buy a home with a small down payment while transferring part of the lender's default exposure to a master insurer. This document specifies the API surface that a master insurer — referred to throughout as the insurer — exposes to the lenders, servicers and internal risk staff who participate in that transfer. The product scope runs the full arc of a certificate of coverage: an application arrives from a lender, is priced off a rate card, is underwritten on either a delegated or a non-delegated path, becomes an issued loan with an annual or single premium, is monitored month by month by the servicer that collects the payments, is eventually cancelled or terminated on a milestone or on delinquency, and — when the worst happens — produces a claim whose settlement is shared with a reinsurance counterparty.

The hard part is not any single step; it is that the same loan is simultaneously a pricing object, an amortizing balance, a delinquency timeline, a rescission-liability record and a ceded-risk unit, and each of those views can invalidate a decision taken from another. A lender that read a loan yesterday to size an exposure contribution may find that a servicer filed a delinquency report overnight, that the loan crossed a milestone, or that a cancellation notice was applied against it — and the insurer must refuse the stale action rather than price a picture of the book that no longer exists. Loss-mitigation evidence, rescission relief that attaches after a statutorily-defined run of clean payment cycles, and treaty capacity that must be respected per claim but capped in aggregate all impose the same discipline: numbers computed once must stay consistent with the state that produced them, and any operation that would contradict a frozen figure must be refused with a specific error rather than silently accepted.

Finally, the economics are deliberately double-edged. Premium is an insurer liability when a loan cancels — the unearned part is refunded on evidence — while coverage and reinsurance are an insurer cost when a loan defaults. A single API therefore has to reconcile a borrower-facing refund schedule, a lender-facing exposure cap, and a reinsurer-facing cession ledger over the same population of loans, and it must do so without ever letting the borrower's premium arithmetic, the lender's exposure arithmetic and the treaty's aggregate arithmetic drift apart.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The following specifications hold across every resource and every operation in the API. They are stated once here and are assumed by every workflow described in §3; where a workflow appears to contradict one of these principles, the principle governs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting party, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which that party acts. A request that omits either header, or presents a malformed value, is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Even with valid headers, an operation is only admissible to the roles enumerated for it; a caller whose role is well-formed but not permitted for the operation is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Note in particular that a borrower is barred from submitting a coverage application: the submission operation is restricted to the `Lender` role.

Five roles are recognized, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `MasterPolicyAdmin`, `Underwriter`, `Lender`, `Servicer`, and `Borrower`.

`MasterPolicyAdmin` acts as an opaque operator identity. When `X-User-Role` is `MasterPolicyAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it, but it is never compared to the `id` of any lender, servicer or other row. The same is true of `Underwriter` and of `Borrower`: both are opaque roles whose identifiers are not resolved against any table. A `Borrower` has no write operations at all.

`Lender` is an entity-backed identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Lender`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Lender` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a lender — a coverage application's `lender_id`, a loan's `lender_id`, a claim's parent loan, a curtailment dispute's `lender_id` — compare that foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A lender may therefore submit coverage applications, self-approve them when it holds delegated authority, file claims against its own loans, settle and seek reconsideration on them, and file coverage disputes. `Servicer` is likewise entity-backed: when `X-User-Role` is `Servicer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Servicer` row, and the same comparison applies to a delinquency report's `servicer_id`. A servicer may file monthly delinquency reports only as the loan's designated servicer.

The exclusive and shared operation sets are binding. Configuration and issuance belong to `MasterPolicyAdmin` alone: master policies, rate cards, reinsurance treaties and their cancellation, servicers and their suspension, lenders, loan issuance, the rescission-relief record, dispute review and decision, portfolio snapshots, and the logical clock are all `MasterPolicyAdmin` operations. `Underwriter` owns the non-delegated underwriting queue (taking an application into underwriting, requesting information, approving and declining), owns claim review, approval and denial, and is the only role that files independent validation reports against a loan. Exposure reconciliation, manual reinsurance cessions and portfolio snapshots are shared between `MasterPolicyAdmin` and `Underwriter`. Amortization milestones are shared between `MasterPolicyAdmin` and `Servicer`. Cancellation notices are shared between `Lender` and `Servicer`. Read access to collections and single resources is broadly held by `MasterPolicyAdmin`, `Underwriter`, `Lender` and `Servicer`, with `Borrower` limited to reading its own loan, its claims, and the master policy and rate card governing it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, and any supplied value must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive. `offset` is the number of leading items to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a workflow states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its items ordered by creation timestamp ascending. Paginated list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the standard envelope, alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`. The list endpoints that accept a `status` filter — master policies, reinsurance treaties, servicers, lenders, coverage applications, claims, coverage disputes, reinsurance cessions and portfolio snapshots — apply it before pagination, so `total_count` reflects the filtered set. The collection reads that hang off a loan (its cancellation notices, its delinquency reports, its claims, its external reports and its coverage disputes) are scoped to that loan and take no `limit`/`offset` of their own.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and Audit Fields

Every resource in this API is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID; no resource prepends a type prefix to that identifier, and clients must never construct one. Alongside `id`, every resource carries a `created_at` instant, an `updated_at` instant and an integer `version`. Both instants are ISO 8601 values in UTC written with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`. `version` begins at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly one every time the row is mutated — a state transition, a recomputed derived field, a refreshed flag, or an update to a nested collection all count. `updated_at` moves with it. No resource in this API is immutable after creation: all of the configuration records, party records, transaction records and aggregates carry the complete quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Two consequences deserve emphasis because they are easy to miss. First, recomputing a derived field is a mutation in its own right even when the client did not ask for it: when a delinquency report causes the loan's `delinquency_streak` to be recalculated, the loan is mutated, so the loan's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances. Second, a report record that has no state of its own still advances its own version when its single creation transition is recorded.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Temporal Evaluation

Time in this API is explicit rather than ambient. The server maintains a logical clock whose value at startup is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`; until an operator moves it, the clock reads that default and never falls back to wall-clock time. A `MasterPolicyAdmin` may set the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant through the administrative clock capability, which responds with the new `current_utc`. Every temporal rule in this document — event instants, delinquency effective dates, cancellation effective dates, the thirty-day and sixty-day deferrals, anniversary cycles, claim filing bars and treaty in-force windows — is evaluated against this logical clock and not against real time. Advancing the clock is not a passive operation: moving it forward can satisfy conditions that fire system-driven transitions, so a clock advance may itself close a pending cancellation, supersede a portfolio snapshot, or otherwise leave resources in a state the caller did not explicitly request.

### 2.6. Money, Rate and Rounding Conventions

Monetary amounts are always integer cents, and rates are never currency. Where a rate is expressed in basis points it is an integer number of hundredths of a percent, and where a rate is expressed as a fraction it is a decimal number. Three rounding behaviours appear in this document and each is pinned to the specific quantity that uses it, so an implementation may not choose a rounding mode globally. The default for a value described as "rounded half up" is the nearest value with ties resolved upward. Division that yields a fraction of a cent for a coverage base or a curtailment deduction is floored rather than rounded. Division that yields a fraction of a cent for a borrower's periodic premium is ceilinged. Percentages and indices reported to four decimal places — the validated loan-to-value ratio, the exposure utilisation percentage and the lender concentration index — are rounded half up at the fourth decimal place. Loan-to-value comparisons against the band boundaries of 85, 90 and 95 percent are made against values carried to four decimal places.

A configuration rate supplied at input is preserved at its input precision; only computed results are subject to the rounding rules above. This distinction matters for the policy's curtailment factors, tolerance and contribution rate, all of which are fractional inputs used as exact multipliers.

### 2.7. The Premium Schedule Ledger

Each borrower-paid monthly loan carries a premium schedule of twelve periods for the policy year. Three period states exist: a period that has been paid, a period that has been billed, and a period that has been voided. The schedule is a closed ledger and two invariants hold at all times, both before and after any cancellation. First, the sum of all paid period amounts plus the sum of all billed period amounts plus the sum of all voided period amounts always equals the loan's annual premium. Second, regardless of how many periods have been paid, the sum of the billed period amounts plus the sum of the voided period amounts always equals the loan's annual premium as well. Cancelling coverage moves periods from the billed column into the voided column; because the total is preserved by that move, both invariants continue to hold. A cancellation that leaves the schedule summing to less than the loan's annual premium has drifted and is a defect, not an outcome.

The twelve periods are not twelve equal instalments. The annual amount is the rounded figure, and the individual periods are derived from it in a fixed order that the schedule must respect: the borrower is billed eleven periods of the ceiling-derived monthly amount, and the twelfth period absorbs whatever remainder is left so that the twelve periods sum exactly to the annual amount. The twelfth period is consequently the only period whose amount may differ from the other eleven, and it may be larger or smaller than them.

### 2.8. Nested Resource Retrieval

Several operations address a resource through a path that names a single collection and a single identifier. Where a request names a resource that itself belongs to a larger one — a cancellation notice, a delinquency report, a claim, an external report or a coverage dispute under a loan, or a settlement option under a claim — the server validates the identifiers in the order they appear in the path, left to right, and rejects the request at the first identifier that does not correspond to an existing resource, with the not-found error named for that specific resource type. A request for a dispute under a loan that does not exist is refused with the loan's not-found code rather than the dispute's. Where a path names a resource that does not exist at all, the entity-specific not-found code is used: `ERR_LENDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MASTER_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SNAPSHOT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` for an external report or a delinquency report. A referenced identifier supplied inside a request body rather than in the path is checked the same way and, if it does not exist or belongs to a different resource than the one it is named alongside, is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations in this API are guarded by a version check against the loan that the write is really about, because in each case the request's arithmetic is read off the loan row and a loan that has moved on since the client last read it cannot be acted upon from that reading. Settling a claim carries `expected_loan_version`; reconciling a lender's exposure contribution carries `expected_loan_version`; and manually recording a reinsurance cession carries `expected_loan_version`. In each of the three cases the supplied value must equal the target loan's current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The value is a required part of the request body and is never optional. Settling a claim is the case where staleness is most likely in practice, because a loan's version legitimately advances between a claim's approval and its settlement — any delinquency report, amortization milestone or cancellation notice landing on the loan in that interval mutates the loan row — so a client holding a view of the loan taken before those events will be refused rather than allowed to settle against a superseded picture of the loan. No other operation in this API accepts a version field, and operations not listed here must not require one.

### 2.10. Coverage, Claim and Dispute State Families

The exact state strings in this API are enumerated here so that no workflow invents its own spellings.

A coverage application begins at `SUBMITTED` and may reach `IN_UNDERWRITING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` or `WITHDRAWN`. `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal for the application.

A loan's coverage is described on two independent axes. The coverage axis, carried on the loan as `coverage_status`, takes the values `ACTIVE`, `PENDING_CANCELLATION` and `CANCELLED`; `CANCELLED` is terminal for that axis. The amortization axis, carried on the loan as `amortization_status`, takes the values `IN_AMORTIZATION`, `AT_78` and `AT_80`. Reaching a milestone never by itself ends coverage, and ending coverage never resets the milestone axis. A loan also carries a `termination_reason` drawn from `NONE`, `BORROWER_REQUEST`, `LTV_MILESTONE` and `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, and a `rescission_relief_status` drawn from `NONE`, `PROVISIONAL` and `GUARANTEED`.

A delinquency report is created in the single state `FILED` and never leaves it; the record is append-only and the loan timeline it feeds is what moves.

A claim begins at `FILED` and moves through `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `SETTLED` and `RESCINDED`. Only `SETTLED` and `RESCINDED` are terminal, and even those are reachable more than once: a denied claim can be re-opened, so denial is not terminal. A claim's settlement option kind is one of `CASH`, `THIRD_PARTY_SALE`, `DEED_IN_LIEU` or `DIL`, and each claim carries four priced options bearing those four kinds.

A curtailment dispute begins at `OPEN`, may move to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and ends at `RESOLVED`. Its recorded decision is one of `UPHELD`, `DENIED`, `RESCISSION_GRANTED` or `RESCISSION_DENIED`, and it is absent while the dispute is still open. Its grounds are one of `ORIGINATION_OMISSION`, `OCCUPANCY_MISREPRESENTATION`, `VALUATION_ERROR` or `LOSS_MITIGATION_EXCUSE`.

A cancellation notice begins at `SUBMITTED` and is immediately driven to either `APPLIED` or `REJECTED`, both terminal. Its request kind is `BORROWER_REQUEST` or `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, and the requesting party recorded against it is `BORROWER` or `SERVICER`.

A lender and a servicer both begin `ACTIVE`. A lender's only declared transition is to `SUSPENDED`. A servicer may likewise become `SUSPENDED`. A reinsurance treaty is `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`. A reinsurance cession is `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, and a rejected cession records exactly one of `NO_TREATY`, `TREATY_NOT_IN_FORCE_AT_EVENT` or `AGGREGATE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` as its reason; an accepted cession records no reason. A portfolio snapshot is `COMPUTED` and may become `SUPERSEDED`; each of its segments is `WITHIN_LIMITS` or `BREACHED`.

Two enumerated inputs describe borrower and property characteristics. A coverage application, a rate card and an underwritten loan each carry a credit grade of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`. A premium plan is `BPM` for borrower-paid monthly, `SPM` for single premium or `LPM` for lender-paid. A rate card's loan-to-value band is `831_TO_85`, `851_TO_90` or `901_TO_95`. An independent validation report records the occupancy it observed as `OWNER_OCCUPIED`, `SECOND_HOME` or `INVESTMENT`. A policy's required servicer loss-mitigation standard is `PARTICIPATION` or `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE`, and a servicer's own capability is described with the same two values; a servicer's tier is `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`. The loss-mitigation activities a servicer may report in a month are `OUTREACH_SENT`, `INTAKE_COMPLETE`, `EVALUATION_COMPLETE`, `PROGRAM_ACCEPTED` and `COLLATERAL_SALE_LISTING`.

### 2.11. Underwriting Paths and Rate Card Pinning

Two underwriting paths exist and each has its own operation. The non-delegated path puts an underwriter in the loop; the delegated path lets a lender that holds delegated authority bind coverage itself without an underwriter review step. `ERR_PATH_MISMATCH` is returned whenever a caller invokes the path that does not match the lender's authority in either direction: an underwriter taking a lender's delegated application through the non-delegated path is refused, and a lender attempting the delegated path without delegated authority is refused. An application's `underwriting_path` is not settled at submission; the submission records `NON_DELEGATED` as a placeholder only, and whichever underwriting transition the application actually takes overwrites it with `NON_DELEGATED` or `DELEGATED` accordingly. A lender returning requested information sends the application back down the non-delegated path.

Approval on either path pins a rate card, and the pinning rule is the same on both. The card named by `rate_card_id` must belong to the same master policy as the application — a card from a different policy is refused with `ERR_RATE_CARD_MISMATCH`. It must also actually price the application: a card that does not carry the application's requested `coverage_percent` or does not carry the application's `premium_plan` is refused with `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The rate that the card carries becomes the application's `rate_bps_applied`, frozen at that moment, and the resulting loan inherits it.

### 2.12. Order of Evaluation and Request Rejection Discipline

When more than one rule could reject the same request, the rules are evaluated in a fixed precedence and the first one that fails determines the response. The order is as follows, and it is binding rather than illustrative.

Authentication is checked first: absent or malformed identity headers yield `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before anything else is examined. Role permission is checked second, so a caller whose role is not permitted receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` and never learns whether the request would otherwise have been well-formed. Existence checks come third: a body-supplied foreign key that does not resolve yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, and a path-named subject that does not resolve yields the entity-specific not-found code from §2.8, both ahead of any state or business rule. Fourth comes the state check: a transition requested from a state that does not declare it yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On the two coverage-application underwriting operations, the path-versus-authority check follows the state check with `ERR_PATH_MISMATCH`.

Only after those structural gates does the request reach the business layer, and the business gates are themselves ordered. The rescission-relief bar (`ERR_RESCISSION_BARRED_RELIEF_ATTACHED`) is evaluated before the claim filing bar (`ERR_CLAIM_TIME_BARRED`), which in turn precedes the loss-mitigation gate (`ERR_CLAIM_BLOCKED_LOSS_MITIGATION`). The lender exposure cap check (`ERR_LENDER_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED`) follows. The rate-card eligibility checks (`ERR_RATE_CARD_MISMATCH` and `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`) precede the underwriting-decline check (`ERR_UNDERWRITING_DECLINED`), so a card that does not price the application is reported as a card problem rather than a decline. Cancellation admissibility (`ERR_CANCELLATION_NOT_PERMITTED` and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`) precedes the waiver gate (`ERR_WAIVER_NOT_AVAILABLE`). Coverage-in-force (`ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_ACTIVE`) is evaluated before the open-dispute referential checks (`ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_DISPUTE` and `ERR_CURTAILMENT_DISPUTE_OPEN`), which precede the settlement-option checks (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_OPTION_UNKNOWN` and `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_SALE_INSUFFICIENT`), which precede the treaty checks (`ERR_TREATY_NOT_IN_FORCE` and `ERR_TREATY_CAP_EXHAUSTED`). The optimistic-concurrency check (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`) is evaluated last, after every other business gate, so a request that is both stale and otherwise invalid reports the substantive problem. The remaining business gates follow in the order they are numbered in §1.4: milestone reachability (`ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`), duplicate delinquency period (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD`), and the reporting-servicer checks (`ERR_SERVICER_INACTIVE` then `ERR_SERVICER_NOT_DESIGNATED`).

Below all of these sits body-shape validation. A required field that is absent, a value of the wrong type, a negative monetary amount, or an event instant set in the future is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). In practice a malformed body is detected as the request is deserialized and therefore surfaces before the gates above; the numbered precedence applies to well-formed requests whose fields are individually valid but whose business conditions are not met.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Lender and a Servicer onto the Book, and the Terms They Operate Under

Nothing can be priced or issued until the insurer has recorded the parties and the contract that govern them. A `MasterPolicyAdmin` establishes a master policy by supplying its `policy_number`, the `anniversary_utc` instant that anchors the policy's rescission-relief cycle counting, and a set of commercial terms. The policy carries a `coverage_percent_default` — the coverage percentage applied when an application pins none — and that value may only ever be 12, 25 or 30; it defaults to 25. It carries two curtailment factors, a `delegated_curtailment_rate` applied to a claim whose loan was written on the delegated path and a `nondelagated_curtailment_rate` applied to one written on the non-delegated path. It carries an `ltv_variance_tolerance`, a fraction compared against the loan-to-value movement that independent validation may reveal. It carries a `servicer_loss_mitigation_standard`, either `PARTICIPATION` or `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE`, which is one of the two inputs that decide what a monthly delinquency report must contain. It carries a `rescission_relief_cycles` count — the number of consecutive payment-free-of-delinquency anniversary cycles a loan must survive before the guaranteed relief attaches — a `min_third_party_net_bps` floor expressed in basis points, applied to every third-party-sale settlement option, and a `two_year_effective_flag` that gates whether the second relief rule is available to loans sourced from this policy at all. Finally it carries an `exposure_contribution_rate`, the fraction of each loan's coverage base that the loan contributes to its lender's exposure reconciliation. A policy created through this operation starts `ACTIVE`; the API declares no suspension or termination operation for a master policy, so no value other than `ACTIVE` is reachable for its `status`.

Lenders arrive under the same administrative authority, described by a `lender_name`, a `delegated_underwriting` flag that determines which underwriting path they may use, and a `policy_exposure_cap_cents` ceiling on the aggregate coverage base they may have in force. A lender is created `ACTIVE` and its `status` changes only in one circumstance: a failed exposure reconciliation, discussed in §3.11, drives it to `SUSPENDED`. Servicers are recorded with a `servicer_name`, a `tier` of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, and a `loss_mitigation_capability` of `PARTICIPATION` or `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE` — the highest standard that servicer has been approved to perform. A servicer is created `ACTIVE`, may be driven to `SUSPENDED` by the administrative suspension operation, and each such change advances the record's `version`. The tier matters beyond labelling: a `TIER_1` or `TIER_2` servicer's tier standard is `PARTICIPATION`, while a `TIER_3` servicer's tier standard is `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE`.

A lender initiates coverage by submitting a coverage application. The payload names the `lender_id` submitting it, the `master_policy_id` it is issued under, and the `rate_card_id` it should be priced off; identifies the borrower through the lender's own `borrower_reference`; and states the economics — `loan_amount_cents` for the note amount, `property_value_cents` for the origination appraised value exclusive of any financed premium, `credit_grade` as the lender reports it, the requested `coverage_percent`, the `premium_plan`, a `borrower_paid` flag, and a `financed_premium` flag. It also states the property's five-digit FIPS `county_code`, the note `term_months`, and optionally the `borrower_reserves_months` documented at origination, which defaults to zero when the lender supplies none. The application is created at `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_utc` set to the logical clock and `resubmission_count` at zero. Because the borrower cannot act on its own behalf here, a submission attempted under the `Borrower` role is refused outright.

From `SUBMITTED`, an application forks. An `Underwriter` may take it into non-delegated underwriting, which moves it to `IN_UNDERWRITING`, stamps `underwritten_utc` from the logical clock, and records `underwriting_path` as `NON_DELEGATED`. That operation is refused with `ERR_PATH_MISMATCH` when the application's lender holds delegated authority, because a delegated lender's file does not belong in the underwriter queue. Alternatively, a `Lender` whose institution holds delegated authority may approve the application directly from `SUBMITTED`, skipping the review step entirely; this records `underwriting_path` as `DELEGATED`, sets `approved_coverage_percent` to the requested `coverage_percent` without any underwriting step-down, pins `rate_bps_applied` from the card, and computes the coverage base. A lender without delegated authority attempting this path is refused with the same `ERR_PATH_MISMATCH`. Before a lender's application may be withdrawn, it may be withdrawn from `SUBMITTED` or from `INFO_REQUESTED`; in either case it reaches `WITHDRAWN`.

The non-delegated path has a genuine return cycle rather than a one-way ratchet. From `IN_UNDERWRITING` an `Underwriter` may send the file back to the lender by requesting information: the application moves to `INFO_REQUESTED`, `resubmission_count` increases by one, and a free-text `info_requested` note is carried on the request. The lender answers by re-submitting borrower information, which returns the application to `IN_UNDERWRITING` and re-establishes the non-delegated path; that request may carry a refreshed `borrower_reserves_months`. The cycles may repeat, and `resubmission_count` records how many times the file has bounced. An `Underwriter` may also decline the file from `IN_UNDERWRITING`, moving it to `DECLINED` and recording a `decline_reason`. Every one of these transitions advances the application's `version`.

Approval on the non-delegated path is where the coverage-step-down rule lives, and it is the only place in the API where the underwritten coverage percentage differs from what the lender asked for. An `Underwriter` approving an application out of `IN_UNDERWRITING` begins from the lender's requested `coverage_percent` and reduces it by five percentage points for each of two conditions that holds. The first is that the application's `credit_grade` is worse than the credit band the pinned rate card prices. The second is that the application's `borrower_reserves_months` is less than that card's `min_borrower_reserves_months`. Each failing condition costs five points, each is applied once regardless of how badly the condition fails, and the two reductions are added together rather than compounded — so an application that fails both is reduced by ten points, neither more nor less. The reduced figure is a floor-guarded quantity: if the result would fall below the lowest coverage percentage the master policy permits, the application is approved at that minimum rather than declined. The approved figure is written to `approved_coverage_percent`, the card's rate is pinned into `rate_bps_applied`, `underwritten_utc` is stamped, and the coverage base is computed from the approved percentage rather than the requested one. Note that this operation carries no request body of its own beyond the approval itself.

One refusal cuts across all of these approval paths. When the loan-to-value ratio computed at submission exceeds 95.0 percent, or when the requested `coverage_percent` lies outside the set the master policy permits, the application is declined rather than approved, and the refusal carries `ERR_UNDERWRITING_DECLINED` (HTTP 422). The percentage-set clause applies on the decline operation as well as on both approval operations.

### 3.2. Pricing: Loan-to-Value Banding, Rate Card Lookup and the Coverage Base

Underwriting does not negotiate a price; it looks one up. The insurer publishes rate cards, each owned by a master policy and each priced for a combination of credit grade, loan-to-value band, coverage percentage and premium plan. An administrator creates a card by supplying its owning `master_policy_id`, the `credit_grade` band it prices, the `ltv_band` it prices, the `coverage_percent` it prices, the `premium_plan` it prices, and the `rate_bps` it charges. A card may also impose a `min_borrower_reserves_months` minimum, which defaults to zero when no reserve requirement is stated; a card with the default imposes nothing.

Loan-to-value is a percentage and it is computed from the note amount as the lender reports it over the origination appraised value, multiplied by one hundred. The note amount already includes any financed single premium, while the denominator stays the appraised `property_value_cents` and is never adjusted for the premium — which means financing a premium raises the ratio and can lift a loan into a higher band than the same base mortgage would have occupied with an un-financed premium. The ratio is carried to four decimal places, rounded half up, and the band comparison is made against that value. Three bands exist. A ratio at or below 85.0000 percent falls in `831_TO_85`. A ratio strictly above 85.0000 and at or below 90.0000 falls in `851_TO_90`. A ratio strictly above 90.0000 and at or below 95.0000 falls in `901_TO_95`. Each band therefore includes its upper bound and excludes its lower bound. A ratio strictly above 95.0000 has no band, and an application that computes one is declined with `ERR_UNDERWRITING_DECLINED` rather than approved.

Given the band, the lookup selects the single card matching the application's master policy, credit grade, band, coverage percentage and premium plan. The rate on the matched card is what the application pins.

The coverage base is the dollar amount the policy actually covers, and it is derived from the note amount and the coverage percentage that survives underwriting. It is always an integer number of cents obtained by multiplying the note amount by the coverage percentage, dividing by one hundred, and discarding any fractional cent — the base is floored and never rounded up, so a base that computes to 8499999.6 cents is recorded as 8499999 cents. On the delegated path the coverage percentage used is the one the lender requested. On the non-delegated path it is the approved percentage after the step-down rule of §3.1. The resulting loan instead records this figure as `loan_amount_cents` on the loan and derives the base from it: the loan's `coverage_base_upb_cents` is `loan_amount_cents` multiplied by the approved `coverage_percent`, divided by one hundred, and floored. The application records the same figure as `coverage_base_upb_cents`, and the loan eventually issued from the application inherits it as the original-item coverage base, which becomes the ceiling that claim arithmetic is measured against.

One special case in the base arithmetic concerns a financed single premium. Where the premium is not financed, and where the premium is financed under a single-premium plan, the base is computed the same way, off the note amount exactly as the lender reported it. The base is not inflated by the financed premium and is not separately recalculated for it; the pricing consequence of financing is the loan-to-value band movement described above, and nothing else.

### 3.3. Issuing Coverage: Premium Plans, the Instalment Schedule and the Single-Premium Annuity

An approved application becomes a live certificate when a `MasterPolicyAdmin` issues the loan. Issuance names the originating `application_id`, designates the servicing organization through `servicer_id`, and elects a reinsurance treaty through `reinsurance_treaty_id`; that last identifier may name a treaty that is not yet in force, because cession is tested at settlement time against the treaty's window rather than at issuance. Roughly the whole pricing and party picture is inherited from the approved application rather than restated: the coverage percentage in force, the `premium_plan`, whether the borrower bears the premium, the `underwriting_path`, the pinned `rate_bps_applied`, the original-item `coverage_base_upb_cents`, the `credit_grade`, the `approval_credit_grade` frozen at approval, the `master_policy_id`, the `lender_id`, the `county_code` and the `term_months`. Issuance also denormalizes the application's `lender_id` and `master_policy_id` onto the loan so that exposure aggregation does not have to walk back through the application, and it copies the amortized starting balances: the `current_upb_cents` opens at the application's `loan_amount_cents`, and the `current_coverage_base_cents` — the part of the coverage base still outstanding — opens at the note amount multiplied by the coverage percentage, divided by one hundred, floored.

The new loan opens with coverage axis `ACTIVE`, amortization axis `IN_AMORTIZATION`, a `termination_reason` of `NONE`, an absent `cancellation_effective_utc`, a `claim_blocked` flag of false, an `aggregate_ceded_cents` of zero, a `rescission_relief_cycle_index` of zero and a `rescission_relief_status` of `NONE`. Its `milestone_78_reached_utc` and its `last_loss_mitigation_deficient_utc` are absent. Two of its derived values are also absent until an independent validation report arrives: both `verified_property_value_cents` and `verified_loan_amount_cents` are null at issuance and stay null until a report establishes them.

The premium depends entirely on the plan. A `BPM` plan — borrower-paid monthly — is the case the instalment schedule governs, and it applies only when `borrower_paid` is true. Its annual premium, held on the loan as `annual_premium_cents`, is the original-item coverage base multiplied by the applied rate in basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent, and it is that single rounded annual figure that everything downstream uses. The borrower is then billed eleven periods of the periodic amount, held as `monthly_period_cents`, plus one final true-up period, held as `final_period_cents`. The periodic amount is the annual premium divided by twelve, with the result ceilinged to a whole cent, so a quotient that is not a whole number of cents is rounded up. The twelfth period is not that same amount: it is the annual premium less eleven times the periodic amount, absorbing whatever remainder the ceiling division left behind. The rounding order is pinned and must not be re-derived. The annual figure is rounded once; the periodic amount is derived from that already-rounded annual figure; and the final period absorbs the difference. Summing twelve independently rounded periods, or re-rounding the annual amount for each period, produces a different result and is wrong. The canonical illustration is a loan whose coverage base is 8500000 cents priced at 62 basis points: the annual premium rounds to 52700 cents, the periodic amount is the ceiling of 52700 divided by twelve, or 4392 cents, and the final period is 52700 less eleven times 4392, or 4388 cents — the eleven regular periods total 48312 and the schedule totals 52700. Rounding each period independently against the unrounded annual would instead produce twelve periods of 4391 cents, eight cents light of the true total; and distributing the remainder any other way would produce a schedule that totals correctly but carries a different twelfth instalment. Only the pinned reading is acceptable. This same arithmetic is recomputed on loan creation, on cancellation and whenever an amortization milestone is recorded, and the schedule ledger invariants of §2.7 hold throughout.

An `LPM` plan — lender-paid — is defined by `borrower_paid` being false. Every period under it bills zero, and the applied rate is forced to zero at approval, so the loan is a zero-premium loan. It is admissible only where the master policy supplies rate cards priced for it. The zero premium has no effect whatsoever on the claim side of the loan: the curtailment factor applied to a claim still comes from the loan's underwriting path, and the deductible rate still comes from the loan's approval credit grade, exactly as they would for any other loan. Only the premium is zero.

An `SPM` plan — single premium — is charged once rather than monthly, and the loan's `single_premium_cents` carries the result while remaining zero for `BPM` and `LPM` loans. Whether the premium is financed decides the formula. Where `financed_premium` is true, the premium is financed into the note and is therefore an annuity over the term: the coverage base is multiplied by the applied rate in basis points, divided by ten thousand, multiplied by the note term expressed in months divided by twelve, and multiplied by the present-value factor; the result is ceilinged to a whole cent. Where `financed_premium` is false, the borrower pays a one-time premium computed as the plain single premium: the coverage base multiplied by the applied rate in basis points, divided by ten thousand, ceilinged to a whole cent. Both forms ceiling rather than round. The term used in the financed form is twelve times the number of years the loan runs; expressed in months as this API states it, the note term is 240 months, which is twenty years, and the present-value factor applied to the annuity is 0.92.

One further accrual attaches to a borrower-paid monthly loan sitting under a policy that charges the premium on an annual basis. Simple interest runs on the whole annual premium at the policy's premium-interest rate, which is a master-policy constant rather than a loan field, and the result is the annual premium multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. The reference policy carries a rate of 0.0450, so an annual premium of 52700 cents accrues 2372 cents of interest. Only borrower-paid monthly loans accrue this interest.

### 3.4. Monthly Servicer Reporting and the Loss-Mitigation Gate

Once a loan is in force the servicing organization carries the reporting obligation, and the report it files each month is what turns a performing certificate into a delinquent one. A `Servicer` files a monthly delinquency report naming the `loan_id` it concerns and its own `servicer_id`, the reporting month as an `as_of_period` in year-month form, a `delinquency_class` of `A` or `B`, a `months_delinquent` count of contractual payments past due, and a `loss_mitigation_activities` list. Class A is a report of one or more payments past due; class B reports no past-due payment and is the record that closes a delinquency run.

Each entry in the `loss_mitigation_activities` list describes work the servicer has done and carries three parts: an `activity_kind`, an `outcome`, and an `activity_utc`. The `activity_kind` is one of `OUTREACH_SENT`, `INTAKE_COMPLETE`, `EVALUATION_COMPLETE`, `PROGRAM_ACCEPTED` or `COLLATERAL_SALE_LISTING`. The list is the evidence the report offers, and it is what the gate below reads.

Two admissibility rules guard the filing itself. The reporting servicer must be `ACTIVE`; a report from a suspended servicer is refused with `ERR_SERVICER_INACTIVE`. The reporting servicer must also be the loan's own designated servicer; a report from any other organization is refused with `ERR_SERVICER_NOT_DESIGNATED`. A third rule guards the timeline: only one report may exist per loan per reporting month, so filing a report whose `as_of_period` duplicates one already on file for that loan is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD`.

The report's effective instant is derived rather than supplied. It is the fifth calendar day of the report's `as_of_period` month at midnight UTC, and this is the instant at which the report enters the loan's timeline — not the instant it was filed, which is recorded separately as the `reported_utc` stamp. Everything downstream, including the anniversary-cycle windows of §3.6 and the deficiency timestamp used by cancellation, is measured from the effective instant.

Reports never change state. Each is created in `FILED` and remains there; the record itself is append-only, and the loan timeline it feeds is the only thing that moves. That does not mean the report is inert with respect to its own audit fields — its creation is its one transition and its `version` advances with it.

The gate that the report applies is the loss-mitigation participation standard, and it is a set-containment test rather than a score. The applicable standard for a loan is whichever is the greater of two standards: the master policy's `servicer_loss_mitigation_standard` and the servicing organization's own tier standard, where a `TIER_1` or `TIER_2` servicer's tier standard is `PARTICIPATION` and a `TIER_3` servicer's tier standard is `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE`. `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE` is the more demanding of the two and therefore dominates whenever the two disagree, so a loan whose policy requires only `PARTICIPATION` but whose `TIER_3` servicer is held to `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE` is gated at the complete standard. The standard names a set of required activities. `PARTICIPATION` requires that the report's completed activities include `OUTREACH_SENT` and `INTAKE_COMPLETE`. `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE` requires that they include `OUTREACH_SENT`, `INTAKE_COMPLETE`, `EVALUATION_COMPLETE` and `PROGRAM_ACCEPTED`. `COLLATERAL_SALE_LISTING` is the one activity that satisfies the `PARTICIPATION` standard on its own, without the intake steps; it does not satisfy `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE` by itself. The four graded activities also form an ordered ladder — `OUTREACH_SENT`, then `INTAKE_COMPLETE`, then `EVALUATION_COMPLETE`, then `PROGRAM_ACCEPTED` — which matters when a report's evidence is being read against a standard. The report's `participation_standard_met` field records whether the set of completed activities in the report contains every activity the applicable standard requires.

The consequence of a failed standard depends on the class of the report. A class B report carries no delinquency consequence at all. A class A report whose `participation_standard_met` is false sets the loan's `claim_blocked` flag to true and stamps the loan's `last_loss_mitigation_deficient_utc` with that report's effective instant. The `claim_blocked` flag is deliberately sticky: it is never cleared by any later report, however complete that later report's evidence, and the only way it comes off is the explicit administrative waiver described in §3.6. A loan carrying `claim_blocked` true is refused at claim approval with `ERR_CLAIM_BLOCKED_LOSS_MITIGATION`, discussed further in §3.8.

Filing a report also recomputes the loan's delinquency run. The run, held on the loan as `delinquency_streak`, is the number of consecutive class A reports counted from the newest report backward before the first class B report is encountered. The ordering that defines "newest" is by effective instant descending, and ties on that instant are broken by creation time ascending, with a further tie on identifier ascending purely for stability. A class B report stops the count wherever it falls, so a loan with a class A report, a class B report and another class A report has a run of one. The same recomputation refreshes the loan's rescission-relief status per §3.6, and, where the conditions of §3.5 are met, may automatically suspend and cancel the loan's coverage. Every one of these consequences mutates the loan, so its `updated_at` and its `version` move with the report.

### 3.5. Two Independent Axes: Amortization Milestones and Coverage Termination

A loan's life is described on two axes that never interfere with each other. The amortization axis tracks the balance; the coverage axis tracks whether the certificate is still in force. Reaching a milestone never by itself ends coverage, and ending coverage never resets the milestone axis. An administrator or the loan's servicer records amortization progress by submitting the loan's `current_upb_cents` against the milestone operation, which updates the balance and re-evaluates where the loan sits on the amortization axis.

The milestone rule is a threshold on the balance measured against the property value asserted at origination. The operation is refused outright with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED` when the submitted balance still exceeds eighty percent of the loan's `origination_property_value_cents` — that is, when the balance is greater than the floor of 0.80 times that value. Once the balance has come down far enough, the loan takes the deepest milestone it has reached: it is at `AT_78` when the balance is at or below the floor of 0.78 times the origination property value, and at `AT_80` when the balance is above that 78 percent line but at or below the 80 percent line. The axis is monotonic and never regresses, so a loan already at `AT_78` stays there on a later report even if the newly submitted balance would only qualify it for `AT_80`. The first time a loan enters `AT_78`, the logical clock is stamped into `milestone_78_reached_utc` and is never revisited afterward.

The first entry into `AT_78` has a second consequence that the client does not request. Because the balance has reached the seventy-eight percent amortized line, the loan's coverage is scheduled to end automatically: the coverage axis moves from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_CANCELLATION`, the `termination_reason` becomes `LTV_MILESTONE`, and the `cancellation_effective_utc` is set to whichever is later — the current logical clock, or `milestone_78_reached_utc` plus thirty days. The thirty-day deferral is what gives the borrower a statutory notice window even when the milestone was reached well in the past. This automatic transition is skipped when a cancellation is already pending on the loan.

A second automatic termination trigger runs off the delinquency timeline rather than the balance. When a loan's `delinquency_streak` reaches three or more and the loan's `claim_blocked` flag is true, the coverage axis moves from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_CANCELLATION` with a `termination_reason` of `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, and the `cancellation_effective_utc` is set to the loan's `last_loss_mitigation_deficient_utc` plus sixty days. This transition too is skipped when a cancellation is already pending, and it also records a cancellation notice against the loan, of kind `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE` and with a requesting party of `SERVICER`, so that the termination has a documentary trail.

Both automatic triggers feed a single arbitration. The server evaluates every termination trigger that is satisfied and takes the earliest effective instant across all of them, and the loan's `termination_reason` is the label of the trigger that produced that earliest instant. The three labels and their instants are: `LTV_MILESTONE`, effective at the later of the current logical clock and `milestone_78_reached_utc` plus thirty days; `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, effective at `last_loss_mitigation_deficient_utc` plus sixty days; and `BORROWER_REQUEST`, effective at the later of the notice's `applied_utc` and its `notice_utc` plus thirty days. Where two triggers land on exactly the same instant, the tie is resolved by a fixed label precedence: `LTV_MILESTONE` beats `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, which beats `BORROWER_REQUEST`. Instants are compared at one-second resolution. One thing that is emphatically not a termination trigger is a lender's exposure-cap breach; that affects the lender's own status and nothing about the loan's coverage.

Once the coverage axis is at `PENDING_CANCELLATION`, it resolves when the logical clock reaches the `cancellation_effective_utc`: at that point the axis moves to `CANCELLED`, the associated cancellation notice's status becomes `APPLIED`, and the loan is mutated once more. The one way back from `PENDING_CANCELLATION` is administrative: a `MasterPolicyAdmin` may revoke the pending termination, which returns the coverage axis to `ACTIVE` and clears `cancellation_effective_utc` to null. This revocation is the operation that doubles as the loss-mitigation waiver, and the waiver's own gating is described in §3.6.

### 3.6. Cancellation on Notice, the Refund Computation and the Rescission-Relief Guarantee

A lender or a servicer may file a cancellation notice against a loan's coverage directly, rather than waiting for an automatic trigger. The notice names the `loan_id`, a `request_kind` of `BORROWER_REQUEST` or `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE`, the `requested_by_role` of `BORROWER` or `SERVICER`, the `notice_utc` instant the notice is given, the `applied_utc` instant at which termination is requested to take effect, and an optional `evidence_cents` amount supporting the refund, which defaults to zero. Filing a notice against a loan whose coverage axis is `ACTIVE` moves that axis to `PENDING_CANCELLATION`, and the notice itself is created and immediately driven by the system to `APPLIED` where it is admissible, or to `REJECTED` where it is not.

Two preconditions govern admissibility, and each has its own refusal. A notice whose `request_kind` is `SERVICER_LOSS_MITIGATION_FAILURE` requires the loan's `claim_blocked` flag to be true; a servicer-loss-mitigation cancellation of a loan that has not actually failed its loss-mitigation standard is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_NOT_PERMITTED`. The `evidence_cents` the notice supplies must also reach a computed maximum refund; an amount below that maximum is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. The maximum refund is itself the quantity the cancellation is about, so it deserves stating in full.

The maximum refund is the proportion of the original coverage base that has already amortized away. Take the original-item coverage base and subtract from it the current coverage base — the part of the base still outstanding, which is the current unpaid principal balance multiplied by the coverage percentage and divided by one hundred, floored. Divide that difference by the original-item coverage base, multiply by ten thousand, and round half up to a whole cent. That is the maximum refund. The borrower-paid and lender-paid plans refund on the same basis, so an `LPM` loan is not exempt from the computation. The refund actually recorded on the notice, held as `refund_cents`, is then the smallest of three figures: the `evidence_cents` the notice supplied, the maximum refund, and — as a floor — zero, so the recorded refund can never be negative. Where the computed maximum refund is zero, the notice is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_NOT_PERMITTED` rather than applied with a zero refund; a factor of zero is not a cancellation ground.

The effective instant of a notice-based termination is clamped forward rather than accepted as requested. The notice's `effective_utc` is the later of the requested `applied_utc` and the notice's `notice_utc` plus thirty days. A borrower who asks for termination a week after giving notice therefore gets a termination thirty days out, not seven. Filing the notice also voids the loan's premium instalments whose period covers that effective instant; per the schedule ledger invariants of §2.7 the voided periods move out of the billed column and the schedule's total is preserved exactly.

Layered over all of this is the rescission-relief guarantee, which is the insurer's statutory protection against having to rescind coverage for an origination defect after a long enough run of clean payments. The loan's standing is held as `rescission_relief_status`, one of `NONE`, `PROVISIONAL` or `GUARANTEED`, and the counting is done in anniversary cycles rather than in calendar time. For each class A delinquency report on the loan, that report's cycle is the three-hundred-and-sixty-five-day window ending at the report's effective instant, running from the instant three hundred and sixty-five days before the effective instant up to and including it. A cycle is free of delinquency when it contains no class A report other than the one that defines the window — which has the slightly counter-intuitive consequence that a class A report reporting no past-due amount is its own lone class A report and its cycle counts as free. A cycle that contains any additional class A report is not free. The cycle index is then the count of consecutive free cycles found by walking the loan's class A reports newest first and stopping at the first report whose cycle is not free.

Two rules turn that count into a status. Rule one: the status becomes `PROVISIONAL` as soon as the cycle index reaches one or more. Rule two: the status becomes `GUARANTEED` when all three of the following hold — the cycle index has reached the master policy's `rescission_relief_cycles` figure, the policy's `two_year_effective_flag` is true, and every one of the consecutive free cycles counted by that index has its defining effective instant falling after the policy's `anniversary_utc` instant and no later than that instant plus seven hundred and thirty days. Where either rule's conditions fail, the status is `NONE`. `GUARANTEED` is absorbing: once relief has attached it never falls back to `PROVISIONAL` or to `NONE`. The relief is recomputed whenever a delinquency report is filed, whenever an anniversary boundary is crossed, and whenever the administrative relief operation is invoked, and it gates the rescission decision discussed in §3.11 — a loan whose relief is `GUARANTEED` cannot be rescinded.

The administrative relief operation itself does two separable things depending on the `relief_status` supplied. A `relief_status` of `ATTACHED` records the master insurer's attached relief against the loan. A `relief_status` of `REVOKED` is the loss-mitigation waiver: it clears the loan's `claim_blocked` flag, but only when the master policy's `servicer_loss_mitigation_standard` is `PARTICIPATION` and an `waiver_evidence_code` is supplied and is an attached code. A waiver attempted on a policy whose standard is `PARTICIPATION_COMPLETE`, or without an attached evidence code, is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. This is the sole path by which a sticky `claim_blocked` flag can be cleared, and it is administrative precisely because it is the only relief from the gate that otherwise blocks the loan's claims forever. Note that the `claim_blocked` flag itself remains untouched by a `REVOKED` call that fails its gate.

Independent validation is the last piece of the origination-integrity picture, and it is filed by an underwriter or an administrator against a loan. The report supplies externally established figures — a `verified_loan_amount_cents` and a `verified_property_value_cents` — together with an `occupancy_reported` value of `OWNER_OCCUPIED`, `SECOND_HOME` or `INVESTMENT`, an `independent_validation_completed_utc` instant, and an optional `post_origination_value_cents` which defaults to zero and represents any value observed after origination; a report predating the loan carries zero there, which suppresses the occupancy/value-reset pathway entirely. From those inputs the report computes a `validated_ltv` of one hundred times the verified loan amount over the verified property value, carried to four decimal places and rounded half up, and a boolean `occupancy_materially_differs` that is true exactly when the reported occupancy is `INVESTMENT`.

The validation then reviews whether the insurer's ability to rescind persists. The validated loan-to-value is compared to the loan-to-value computed at origination, taken as one hundred times the reported note amount over the origination appraised value. The difference between the two, expressed as a fraction of the original figure, is the variance fraction. The variance exceeds the policy's tolerance when that fraction is strictly greater than the master policy's `ltv_variance_tolerance` — strictly, not at or above. A value-reset pathway is present when `post_origination_value_cents` exceeds the loan's `origination_property_value_cents`. The insurer's ability to rescind persists when the variance does not exceed tolerance and it is not the case that the occupancy materially differs while the value-reset pathway is present; those two clauses are conjunctive, and the outcome is that rescission is permitted whenever that combined condition does not hold. Two loans with identical occupancy and post-origination value evidence therefore diverge purely on whether the validated ratio moved by more than the tolerance. Concretely, at a tolerance of 0.05, a validated ratio of 87.0588 against an original of 85.0000 gives a variance fraction of 0.024221 and the ability to rescind persists, while a validated ratio of 92.0000 against the same original gives 0.082353 and rescission is permitted. Filing the report writes the verified figures onto the loan as `verified_property_value_cents` and `verified_loan_amount_cents` — the fields that were null until now — and recomputes the rescission review, mutating the loan.

### 3.7. Filing a Claim and Taking It Through Review

When a covered loan ends in a loss, the lender files a claim. The filing names the `loan_id` the claim is against, the `event_kind` — `FORECLOSURE`, `SHORT_SALE` or `DEED_IN_LIEU` — and the `event_completed_utc` instant at which the title-transferring event completed. It then states the arithmetic inputs as five numbered items: `unpaid_principal_cents` for the unpaid principal balance at the event, `accrued_interest_cents` for the interest claimed, `allowable_advances_cents` for the advances claimed, `gross_proceeds_cents` for the gross sale or liquidation proceeds, and `expenses_of_sale_cents` for the expenses of the sale. The claim is created at `FILED` with `filed_utc` stamped from the logical clock, and it also receives a server-minted `trace_number` used purely for path addressing rather than for any ranking. Its `curtailment_rate_applied`, `settlement_option_kind` and `settlement_amount_cents` are all absent at this point — the rates are frozen and the options priced only when the claim is approved.

An `Underwriter` takes the claim into review, moving it from `FILED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`. One absolute bar applies here: the claim must have been filed no later than the loan's `term_months` after the event instant. The bar is a whole-month period measured from the event instant to the claim's filing instant, and a claim filed outside it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_TIME_BARRED`. A loan whose note term in months is 240 is therefore barred once more than 240 months have elapsed between the title-transferring event and the filing.

Review is a two-way door. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the lender may seek reconsideration, which returns the claim to `FILED` and re-enters it in the review queue; that request must supply a `reason`. The same reconsideration operation is available from `DENIED`, where it re-opens the denial and returns the claim to `FILED` — which is to say that a denial is not the end of a claim's life, and a lender that has been refused may put the claim back into the queue with an explanation. Reconsideration may also carry optional `additional_documentation`.

While the claim sits in `UNDER_REVIEW` the underwriter either approves it or denies it. Denial moves it to `DENIED` and records a `denial_reason`; approval is the operation that does all the work, and it is described in §3.8. Both are refusals-and-grants rather than partial outcomes: an approval either succeeds outright or returns one of the gate errors in §3.8, and it never leaves the claim in an intermediate arithmetic state.

### 3.8. The Claim Computation: Two Ceilings, a Curtailment Factor and a Credit-Grade Deductible

Approving a claim is the point at which the entire claim arithmetic is computed and frozen. The computation proceeds through a fixed sequence of quantities, each derived from the last, and the order is not a matter of interpretation.

The first pair of quantities caps what the lender may claim on the interest and advances items. Permitted interest, held on the claim as `permitted_interest_cents`, is the lesser of the claimed `accrued_interest_cents` and fifteen percent of the unpaid principal, computed by multiplying the unpaid principal by 0.15 and discarding any fractional cent. Permitted advances, held as `permitted_advances_cents`, is the lesser of the claimed `allowable_advances_cents` and one hundred and ten percent of the unpaid principal, computed by multiplying the unpaid principal by 1.10 and discarding any fractional cent. Both percentage caps are floored, not rounded. The net unpaid balance, `net_unpaid_balance_cents`, is then the unpaid principal plus the permitted interest plus the permitted advances.

The recoverable amount, `recoverable_amount_cents`, is what is left of that balance after the liquidation proceeds are credited. The net proceeds are the `gross_proceeds_cents` less the `expenses_of_sale_cents`, and they are floored at zero so that a sale that cost more than it raised does not increase the claim. The recoverable amount is the net unpaid balance less those floored net proceeds, itself floored at zero. Formally it is the larger of zero and the net unpaid balance minus the larger of zero and the gross proceeds minus the expenses of sale.

The coverage ceiling, `coverage_ceiling_cents`, is then applied, and it is applied before any curtailment. The ceiling is the loan's original-item `coverage_base_upb_cents` exactly as it stood when the claim was filed — the original-item ceiling, not a re-derived one. The covered claim amount, `covered_claim_amount_cents`, is the smaller of the recoverable amount and that ceiling. This ordering is normative: the ceiling caps the recoverable amount first, and only the capped figure is curtailed. Applying the curtailment factor to the raw recoverable amount and then ceilinging the result is wrong and produces a different number; a claim with a covered amount of 8500000 cents, a curtailment factor of 0.40 and a deductible rate of 0.10 comes to a net amount of 4590000 cents under the pinned order, while curtailing first and ceiling afterwards comes to 4600800 cents for the same claim, a divergence of 10800 cents.

Curtailment is the insurer's reduction of the claim for defects in the origination, and the factor is chosen from the loan's underwriting path. A loan written on the delegated path has its claim curtailed at the master policy's `delegated_curtailment_rate`; a loan written on the non-delegated path at the policy's `nondelagated_curtailment_rate`. The curtailed amount is the covered claim amount multiplied by that `curtailment_rate_applied` with any fractional cent discarded, and the post-curtailment amount, `post_curtailment_cents`, is the covered claim amount less the curtailed amount. A lender-paid loan occupies no special carve-out here: its underwriting-path curtailment factor applies exactly as it would to any other loan, and only its applied rate, and therefore its premium, is zero.

A second reduction follows, and it is keyed to credit quality rather than to path. The claim deductible, `claim_deductible_cents`, is the post-curtailment amount multiplied by the deductible rate for the loan's `approval_credit_grade` — the credit grade underwritten into the coverage and frozen at the loan's creation, not the grade on the claim — with any fractional cent discarded. The applicable rates are five percent for grade `A`, ten percent for grade `B`, fifteen percent for grade `C` and twenty percent for grade `D`. The net claim amount, `net_claim_amount_cents`, is the post-curtailment amount less the deductible.

Two of these quantities are frozen on the claim rather than recomputed each time. The `curtailment_rate_applied` is taken from the loan's underwriting path at the moment of approval and frozen there, and the deductible rate is likewise keyed to the loan's frozen approval credit grade. Re-running the computation — which settlement does, and which a coverage-dispute decision does — recomputes every derived amount but never re-freezes either rate; the frozen rates stay as they were at approval even if the loan's path or grade fields were somehow to move afterwards.

Precision throughout this computation is exact integer arithmetic with a single rule: every multiplication that could produce a fraction of a cent is floored, and no step is rounded half up. That applies to the two percentage caps, to the curtailment deduction, and to the deductible.

Approval also seeds the claim's four priced settlement options, one for each of the four kinds, and it is gated. Four conditions are checked, and they are ordered. Coverage must have been in force at the claim's event instant, meaning the loan's coverage axis was `ACTIVE` then; otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The loan's `claim_blocked` flag must be false at the same instant; otherwise the refusal is `ERR_CLAIM_BLOCKED_LOSS_MITIGATION`. The filing bar of §3.7 is re-asserted here as well. Only when those gates pass does the arithmetic run, the rates freeze, the four option rows are created, and the claim advance to `APPROVED` with its `version` incremented.

### 3.9. The Four Settlement Options and the Recovery Waterfall

Approval prices four ways of closing a claim, and the lender chooses one of them at settlement. The four kinds are `CASH`, `THIRD_PARTY_SALE`, `DEED_IN_LIEU` and `DIL`, each carried on its own priced option row recording the `claim_id` it belongs to, the `option_kind`, and the `computed_amount_cents` the option would pay against the claim's current arithmetic. Three of the four are driven off the net claim amount and are simple; the fourth runs a recovery waterfall and is not.

`CASH` pays the claim's net claim amount outright. `DEED_IN_LIEU` pays the same net claim amount. `THIRD_PARTY_SALE` pays the net claim amount less a share of it, computed by multiplying the net claim amount by the policy's `min_third_party_net_bps` floor in basis points, dividing by ten thousand, discarding any fractional cent, and subtracting that from the net claim amount, with the result floored at zero. The subtracted share is therefore the policy's basis-point floor applied to the net amount, and a policy whose floor is zero would make `THIRD_PARTY_SALE` equal to `CASH`.

`DIL` is the option that behaves differently, because its amount is driven not by the net claim amount but by a coverage-percent reinstatement credit running through a recovery waterfall. The waterfall starts by taking the net proceeds — the gross proceeds less the expenses of sale, floored at zero — and adding to them a coverage credit, which is the coverage base basis multiplied by the loan's coverage percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent. Total recoveries are then capped at the net unpaid balance: they are the smaller of the net unpaid balance and the sum just computed. Two things about the credit deserve care. The base it multiplies, held as `coverage_ceiling_basis_cents`, is the loan's `origination_property_value_cents` while `verified_property_value_cents` is null and the loan's `verified_property_value_cents` once an independent validation report has established it — so the credit changes basis the moment a validation report lands on the loan. The `DIL` amount is then the smaller of the coverage ceiling and the net unpaid balance less total recoveries, floored at zero. The practical effect is that `DIL` can pay materially more than `CASH` on identical claim data: with a net unpaid balance of 41000000 cents and a twenty-five percent credit against a 40000000-cent basis, total recoveries come to 18000000 cents, `DIL` pays the full 8500000-cent ceiling while `CASH` pays 7650000 cents, and the two options differ by 8500000 cents without a single input changing. An institution choosing an option that restores the coverage percentage is choosing a different number, not a different form of the same number.

Selecting an option at settlement is gated twice. The option named by `settlement_option_kind` must be one of the four rows priced on the claim; anything else is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_OPTION_UNKNOWN`. And a `THIRD_PARTY_SALE` selection must clear a proceeds floor: the net proceeds — gross proceeds less expenses of sale, floored at zero — must be at least eighty-five percent of the claim's net amount, with that threshold taken as the floor of 0.85 times the net amount. A sale that recovered less than that is refused with `ERR_THIRD_PARTY_SALE_INSUFFICIENT`, because a third-party sale that leaves the insurer paying a figure the sale did not support is not an admissible disposition of the claim.

Re-pricing is the rule at settlement, not pricing once and reusing. Whatever the four option rows held when approval seeded them, settlement recomputes the entire claim arithmetic against the loan and claim state as it stands at that instant — the net claim amount, the deductible, the post-curtailment amount, the covered claim amount, the recoverable amount, the net unpaid balance, and both the permitted interest and permitted advances — and then sets `settlement_amount_cents` to the recomputed figure for the selected option. The frozen `curtailment_rate_applied` is not re-derived, but every amount built on top of it is. A claim approved months before settlement therefore settles at the numbers that are true when it settles.

### 3.10. Reinsurance Cessions and Treaty Capacity

Settling a claim under some options also creates a cession to the loan's reinsurance treaty, and the rules for that cession are narrow enough to state exactly. Cessions arise only from settlements whose option is `CASH` or `THIRD_PARTY_SALE`; a settlement under `DEED_IN_LIEU` or `DIL` creates no cession at all. The treaty is the one named by the loan's `reinsurance_treaty_id`, and the ceded amount is zero when that reference is absent, when the treaty's status is not `ACTIVE`, or when the treaty is not in force at the claim's event instant. In-force window means the treaty's `inception_utc` is at or before the claim's `event_completed_utc` and that instant is strictly before the treaty's `expiration_utc`; the inception bound is inclusive and the expiration bound is exclusive.

Where a treaty applies, the per-claim ceded amount is the settlement amount less the treaty's `retention_per_claim_cents`, confined to the range from zero up to the treaty's per-claim capacity. That capacity is the treaty's `exhaustion_per_claim_cents` less its `retention_per_claim_cents`. This is a clamp rather than a test: a settlement below the retention cedes nothing, a settlement above the retention cedes the excess, and the excess can never exceed the capacity.

Aggregate capacity is respected by capping, not by rejecting. Before the cession is accepted, the loan's running `aggregate_ceded_cents` plus the per-claim ceded amount is tested against the treaty's `aggregate_ceded_cap_cents`; where the sum would exceed the cap, the per-claim ceded is reduced so that the sum lands exactly at the cap rather than the cession being refused. The accepted cession's `ceded_amount_cents` is that capped figure, and its `retained_amount_cents` is the settlement amount less the ceded figure. An accepted cession also advances the loan's `aggregate_ceded_cents` by the ceded amount, so the loan moves with the cession and both the loan's and the claim's versions advance with a settlement.

A cession may also be recorded directly, rather than arising from a settlement, by an underwriter or an administrator. This manual path differs from the synthesized one in a way that matters: the manual cession carries its own `ceded_amount_cents` supplied by the caller rather than deriving it from the settlement arithmetic, so the two paths agree only when the caller-supplied amount happens to equal what the allocation would have computed. The manual path is also where the treaty's refusals bite. A manually recorded cession whose requested amount exceeds the treaty's remaining aggregate capacity is refused outright with `ERR_TREATY_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, whereas a synthesized cession in the same situation is merely capped — a distinction the API makes deliberately, since a caller stating an amount is asking for that amount while a settlement is asking for a disposition. A cession that cannot be accepted for a treaty-related reason is still recorded, with status `REJECTED` and a `rejected_reason` of `NO_TREATY`, `TREATY_NOT_IN_FORCE_AT_EVENT` or `AGGREGATE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` as applicable; an accepted cession carries no reason. Alongside the amount fields, a cession records the `claim_id` it was made against, the `loan_id` it belongs to, the `treaty_id` applied, and the `aggregate_ceded_after_cents` — the loan's ceded total once this cession has been applied.

Two arithmetic invariants bind the cession ledger and the exposure ledger together. The loan's `aggregate_ceded_cents` accumulates the accepted cessions made against that loan's claims and never exceeds the referenced treaty's aggregate cap. And a reversal of a settlement — described in §3.11 — must leave both the loan's ceded total and the lender's exposure contribution exactly where they stood before the settlement it undoes, which it achieves by voiding the previously accepted cession and subtracting precisely the ceded amount that settlement had added.

### 3.11. Settling a Claim, Disputing a Curtailment and Rescinding a Claim

A lender settles an approved claim by naming one of the four priced options and supplying both expected versions: `expected_claim_version` for the claim being settled and `expected_loan_version` for the loan behind it. Both are required. The `expected_claim_version` must equal the claim's current version exactly as the loan's must equal the loan's, and each mismatch is a stale-version refusal. The loan's check is the one of §2.9 framed in the loan's own terms — the loan legitimately moves between a claim's approval and its settlement, since a delinquency report, a milestone record or a cancellation notice each mutates it, and a settlement presented from a stale reading of the loan is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than applied against a superseded picture. Because the settlement both mutates the loan and mutates the claim, the loan's version advances once more as a result of the settlement itself, in addition to whatever movement the check was guarding against.

Settlement is admissible from `APPROVED` and from nowhere else. Beyond the two version checks and the option checks of §3.9, one further referential gate applies: no open curtailment dispute may be targeting the claim at the moment of settlement, and settlement attempted while one is open is refused with `ERR_CURTAILMENT_DISPUTE_OPEN`. There is exactly one exception to that gate, and it is narrow: an open dispute whose `grounds` are `LOSS_MITIGATION_EXCUSE` does not block settlement, because a dispute asserting that the loss-mitigation gate itself was misapplied has no bearing on the arithmetic being settled. A dispute filed on any of the other three grounds — `ORIGINATION_OMISSION`, `OCCUPANCY_MISREPRESENTATION` or `VALUATION_ERROR` — blocks settlement while it remains open, because each of those contests figures that the settlement would be freezing.

A lender contests a curtailment or an origination defect by filing a coverage dispute. The filing names the `loan_id`, the contesting `lender_id`, the `target_claim_id` of the curtailment being contested on that same loan, the `grounds`, and an `asserted_coverage_percent` — the coverage percentage the lender argues should have applied. The dispute opens at `OPEN` with `filed_utc` stamped from the logical clock and with neither a `decision` nor a `decision_utc` recorded. Only one open dispute may target a claim at a time; a second filing against a claim that already has one open is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_DISPUTE`. An administrator takes the dispute into review, moving it to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and then records the decision, which moves it to `RESOLVED` and stamps both the `decision` and the `decision_utc`. The decision supplied must be one of `UPHELD`, `DENIED`, `RESCISSION_GRANTED` or `RESCISSION_DENIED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_DECISION_INVALID`. A decision of `RESCISSION_GRANTED` carries an additional precondition of its own: the targeted claim must currently be in `DENIED` or in `SETTLED`, and a rescission decision against a claim in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The decision request also carries a `decision_reason`.

The four decisions do quite different things, and each must be treated with the same specificity. `UPHELD` is the lender's case succeeding on the arithmetic: the insurer re-runs the whole claim computation against the targeted claim and adds to that claim's net amount whatever positive difference the recomputation produced — that is, the amount by which the recomputed net claim amount exceeds the claim's frozen net claim amount, floored at zero — so a dispute can raise a claim's figure but can never lower it. `DENIED` and `RESCISSION_DENIED` leave the claim's arithmetic as it stands. `RESCISSION_GRANTED` is the heaviest outcome and it reaches beyond the claim into the loan. It drives the targeted claim into `RESCINDED`, drives the loan's coverage axis into `CANCELLED`, and clears the loan's `termination_reason` to `NONE` and its `rescission_relief_status` to `NONE`. It is barred absolutely on a loan whose relief has attached: a `RESCISSION_GRANTED` decision attempted where the loan's `rescission_relief_status` is `GUARANTEED` is refused with `ERR_RESCISSION_BARRED_RELIEF_ATTACHED`, which is the statutory protection of §3.6 doing its job. Both the loan and the claim are mutated by the decision, so both versions advance.

Where the rescission targets a claim that had already been settled, one more duty falls on it. Reversing a settlement must leave the loan's `aggregate_ceded_cents` exactly where it stood before the settlement, and it must leave the lender's exposure contribution exactly where it stood before any claim on the loan settled. The mechanism is the reversal of the prior settlement's own cession: the previously accepted cession is voided and the loan's ceded total is reduced by precisely the ceded amount that settlement had added, which restores the earlier figure rather than approximating it. A reversal that leaves either total drifting from its pre-settlement value is a defect.

### 3.12. Lender Exposure, Portfolio Concentration and Reinsurance Configuration

An underwriter or an administrator reconciles a lender's aggregate in-force exposure by targeting the lender and supplying `expected_loan_version` together with the `loan_id` being reconciled. The expected version behaves as in §2.9: the reconciliation reads its contribution off the loan row, so a loan that has been amortized, cancelled or newly claimed against since the client last read it cannot be reconciled from that stale reading, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Each loan's contribution is its running `aggregate_ceded_cents` plus its coverage base multiplied by the master policy's `exposure_contribution_rate`, rounded half up to a whole cent. The lender's contribution is the sum of its loans' contributions, rounded half up once, taken across the loans whose coverage axis is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_CANCELLATION` — a loan whose coverage is `CANCELLED` contributes nothing at all. The canonical illustration: a policy carrying a contribution rate of 0.35 makes an 8500000-cent coverage base contribute 2975000 cents, while a policy carrying a rate of 1.0 contributes the loan's entire coverage base. Every loan in the reference book sits under a policy whose rate is 1.0, so a loan's contribution equals its coverage base, and a two-loan example of 8500000 cents each reconciles to a lender contribution of 17000000 cents.

Two thresholds govern the outcome. Utilisation is the lender's contribution as a percentage of its `policy_exposure_cap_cents`, carried to four decimal places and rounded half up. Reconciliation is refused with `ERR_LENDER_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED` when the contribution strictly exceeds the cap — strictly, so a contribution exactly equal to the cap is permitted and a contribution one cent over is not — and a refused reconciliation additionally drives the lender's status to `SUSPENDED` as it returns that error against the lender. Where the reconciliation succeeds, it records a warn flag when utilisation has reached eighty percent, and the response carries the `lender_id` reconciled, the `lender_contribution_cents`, the `used_pct` and that `warn` boolean. The two-loan example above, against a cap that yields 14.1667 percent utilisation, reconciles with a warn of false. A successful reconciliation also stamps the reconciled loan as reconciled at the current logical clock and advances the loan's version.

Portfolio concentration is computed on demand over the whole in-force book. An administrator or underwriter cuts a snapshot by declaring the `snapshot_utc` instant it is for and a `cluster_in_force_threshold_cents` — the aggregate in-force coverage base within a single geography at which that geography breaches concentration. The in-force population is the loans whose coverage axis is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_CANCELLATION`. Geography totals, held on the snapshot as `geography_totals`, are the sums of the in-force coverage base by the loan's five-digit FIPS `county_code`, which is carried on the coverage application's property record and denormalized onto the loan. One segment is emitted per county present in the in-force book, and each is `BREACHED` when that county's total is greater than or equal to the snapshot's threshold — the comparison is inclusive at the threshold — and `WITHIN_LIMITS` otherwise; the per-segment results are held as `segment_statuses`. Segments are ordered by their total descending, with ties broken by county code ascending. The snapshot also reports the `lender_concentration_index`: the largest share of any single lender's aggregate in-force coverage base over the whole-book aggregate, computed as a fraction and carried to four decimal places rounded half up, and reported as null when the in-force book is empty. Its `aggregate_ceded_cents` is the sum of the in-force loans' running ceded totals. A newly computed snapshot is created `COMPUTED`; any previously computed snapshot for the same reporting basis is driven to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer one is computed, so at most one snapshot per basis is live at a time. An empty book is a valid snapshot: it yields a null concentration index and no segments.

Reinsurance terms are a configuration act of the same authority. An administrator creates a treaty by supplying its `treaty_number`, the `inception_utc` and `expiration_utc` bounds of its effective window, its `retention_per_claim_cents` attachment point, its `exhaustion_per_claim_cents` point, and its `aggregate_ceded_cap_cents` ceiling on the total ceded across all loans it covers. The per-claim capacity used by the cession arithmetic of §3.10 is the exhaustion point less the retention point. A treaty is created `ACTIVE` and may be cancelled by an operation that moves it to `CANCELLED` and advances its version; a treaty may alternatively carry the status `EXPIRED` once its window has passed. Ceding is tested against the treaty's status and its in-force window at the moment of cession, so cancelling a treaty stops all further cessions against it even for loans already referencing it.
