# Athletics Academic Eligibility API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

University athletics departments carry a compliance obligation that is unusual in its combination of breadth and precision: every student-athlete who competes must be certified eligible for the specific term in which the competition falls, and that certification has to be defensible years later against a rulebook that changes between catalog years. Today the work is spread across registrar spreadsheets, advisor notebooks, travel itineraries mailed to instructors, and an annual academic-progress-rate submission assembled by hand from all three. When an audit asks why a particular athlete was allowed to travel in a particular week, nobody can reconstruct the credit count, the grade-point average, or the study-hall obligation that was in force at the moment the decision was made.

This document specifies the Athletics Academic Eligibility service, an HTTP API mounted under `/athletics` that owns the whole chain: the academic catalogue of terms, degree programs and courses; the degree-applicability rules that decide whether a given course counts toward a given major in a given catalog year; enrollments and their graded outcomes; the progress-toward-degree benchmark evaluation that produces a pass or a fail; the certification workflow with its deliberate split between the advisor who requests and the officer who decides; the appeals and waivers that can restore a denied athlete; the academic-support machinery of risk tiers, tutors, assignments and study-hall hours; the missed-class budget consumed by competition travel and acknowledged by instructors; the impermissible-assistance incident register that disciplines tutors and can withhold support outright; and the academic-progress-rate close checkpoint that freezes a term and awards its retention and eligibility points.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **every derived number is a stored, inspectable field with one specified formula and one specified rounding rule** — a credit count, a risk score or a study-hall obligation is never recomputed differently by two callers. Second, **the term is the unit of finality**: while a term is open its certifications, appeals, outcomes and travel charges remain mutable and its ledgers keep re-balancing, and once the term is closed for academic-progress-rate reporting every write bound to it is refused. Readers implementing this specification should expect the interesting difficulty to lie not in any single endpoint but in the cascades: one recorded grade moves a grade-point average, a credit audit, a progress percentage, a risk score, a study-hall obligation and a support status, in that order, on the same request.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operator's role. If either header is absent, or is present but holds an empty string or nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is not one of the ten role names below, or is a valid role name that is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authoritative role list per endpoint is the table in §4.

The ten roles are `Registrar`, `AcademicAdvisor`, `CertificationOfficer`, `LearningSpecialist`, `Instructor`, `AppealsCommittee`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `AthleticsOperations`, `Tutor`, and `StudentAthlete`.

- The `Registrar` owns the academic catalogue and student registration: terms, degree programs, courses, degree-applicability rules, student-athlete records, enrollments and graded outcomes.
- An `AcademicAdvisor` records major declarations, opens missed-class requests, notifies instructors, submits certifications for decision, and files and revises appeals on a student's behalf.
- A `CertificationOfficer` creates eligibility certifications and decides them — certify, deny or revoke — and may also file appeals.
- A `LearningSpecialist` owns tutor onboarding, tutor assignments and support-session logging.
- An `Instructor` acknowledges or disputes the missed-class notifications raised against the courses it teaches.
- The `AppealsCommittee` opens review on, grants, or denies eligibility appeals; it never posts a certification action of its own, because a certification returns to service only through the grant cascade.
- The `ComplianceOfficer` closes terms for academic-progress-rate reporting, investigates impermissible-assistance incidents and disciplines tutors.
- `AthleticsOperations` schedules competition events and drives the travel lifecycle.
- A `Tutor` acts on its own behalf, logging sessions against its own assignments.
- A `StudentAthlete` acts on its own behalf and may only read its own record and summary.

Identity resolution differs by role and is not a matter of convention. When `X-User-Role` is `Registrar`, `AcademicAdvisor`, `CertificationOfficer`, `LearningSpecialist`, `Instructor`, `AppealsCommittee`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `AthleticsOperations`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands provenance — as `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a certification, `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an appeal, `reported_by_user_id` on a compliance incident, `logged_by_user_id` on a support session — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. The single exception among the opaque roles is `Instructor`, whose `X-User-ID` is compared against the opaque `instructor_user_id` recorded on the `Course` and copied onto the `MissedClassRequest`; an instructor acknowledging or disputing a request whose `instructor_user_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_COURSE_INSTRUCTOR` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `Tutor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tutor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Tutor` compare the resource's tutor reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a tutor logging a support session against an assignment whose `tutor_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TUTOR` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `StudentAthlete`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StudentAthlete` row; reading a student-athlete row or an eligibility summary whose path identifier differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWN_RECORD` (HTTP 403).

Two decisions are deliberately split between operators. The user who requested a certification may not be the user who decides it, and the user who submitted an appeal may not be the user who decides that appeal: the recorded `decided_by_user_id` must differ from the corresponding `requested_by_user_id` or `submitted_by_user_id`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Note that this is an identity comparison and not a role comparison — two different `CertificationOfficer` operators satisfy it, and the same operator wearing the same role twice does not.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in the whole system read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it neither follows nor constrains the stored timestamps of the resource in `data`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer at all, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry four pagination fields inside `meta` alongside the request identifier and timestamp: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). The `data` field of a list response is an array and `response_type` is `"array"`.

Unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise in §4, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections carry a domain-natural ordering instead, and where they do the fallback tiebreaker is still `created_at` ascending: terms are ordered by `term_index` ascending; degree programs by `code` ascending; courses by `subject_code` ascending then `course_number` ascending; degree-applicability rules by `effective_catalog_year` ascending; student-athletes by `entry_term_index` ascending; support sessions by `start_at` ascending; competition events by `departure_at` ascending; and eligibility appeals by `round_number` ascending. Where two rows tie on every one of those keys — routine rather than exotic, because `created_at` is written from the logical clock of §2.4 and two rows created without moving that clock share the instant exactly — the row created first sorts first. Ordering keys that are strings compare as strings, so a course with `course_number` `1000` precedes one numbered `190`.

Every list endpoint additionally accepts equality filters on the fields named in its §4 description, and those filters compose conjunctively. The available filters are: `term_type` and `apr_status` on terms; `catalog_year` on degree programs; `subject_code` and `is_remedial` on courses; `degree_program_id`, `course_id`, `effective_catalog_year` and `applicability` on degree-applicability rules; `declared_program_id`, `sport_code`, `risk_tier`, `eligibility_state` and `support_status` on student-athletes; `student_athlete_id`, `course_id`, `term_id`, `status` and `credit_source` on enrollments; `student_athlete_id`, `term_id`, `status` and `certification_type` on certifications; `status` and `subject_code` on tutors; `tutor_id`, `enrollment_id` and `status` on tutor assignments; `student_athlete_id`, `tutor_assignment_id` and `session_type` on support sessions; `term_id`, `sport_code` and `status` on competition events; `competition_event_id`, `enrollment_id` and `status` on missed-class requests; `certification_id`, `predecessor_appeal_id`, `status` and `appeal_type` on appeals; and `student_athlete_id`, `tutor_id`, `discovered_session_id`, `status` and `severity` on compliance incidents. A filter value arrives as a string and is compared against the field's stored type: a boolean field accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case, an integer or decimal field is compared numerically, and an empty value or the word `null` matches the rows whose field is null. A value that cannot be read as the field's type matches no row at all, so the endpoint answers with an empty page rather than an error, and a query parameter that is neither `limit`, `offset` nor one of the filters declared for that endpoint is ignored. One filter in that list is not an equality test on a stored field of the same name: `subject_code` on tutors names no tutor field and instead matches a tutor whose `subject_codes` array contains the supplied value exactly, so the comparison is case-sensitive and never numeric, and an empty `subject_code` matches no tutor at all rather than matching a null.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Client-supplied values for any of these four properties are ignored.

`SupportSession` is the one exception. A logged session is an immutable audit record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its response payload. Every other entity — `Term`, `DegreeProgram`, `Course`, `DegreeApplicabilityRule`, `StudentAthlete`, `Enrollment`, `EligibilityCertification`, `Tutor`, `TutorAssignment`, `CompetitionEvent`, `MissedClassRequest`, `EligibilityAppeal` and `ComplianceIncident` — carries all four.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before the clock has been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Every deadline, window, expiry, ordering and "has the term ended yet" question in this specification is evaluated against that logical clock, and the fields that record when something happened — `outcome_recorded_at`, `decided_at` on both certifications and appeals, `notified_at`, `acknowledgement_deadline_at` — are written from it, as are the `created_at` and `updated_at` of every row. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; it modifies no entity and re-runs no calculation, and it is available to every role. Any instant is accepted, including one earlier than the clock's present reading, so the clock may be wound backwards as freely as forwards; and because the capability owns no resource, it answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object carrying the single key `current_utc`, holding the adopted instant in the three-fractional-digit UTC rendering above, rather than with any stored row.

The academic calendar itself is ordered by `term_index` and not by dates. `term_index` is the sole ordering key for every chronological comparison in this specification: which term precedes another, whether a tutor's training has lapsed, whether a remedial course still counts, which term is the "latest" term holding an enrollment. Dates are used only for window containment — a term's `[start_date, end_date)` window, an event's `[departure_at, return_at)` window, a session's `[start_at, end_at)` window — and in every one of those pairs the opening instant is inclusive and the closing instant is exclusive. Any supplied instant pair must satisfy the strict ordering that the opening instant precedes the closing instant; equal or inverted instants are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). That check applies to `start_date` against `end_date` on term registration, `departure_at` against `return_at` on competition-event scheduling, and `start_at` against `end_at` on support-session logging.

### 2.5. Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every stored numeric field has a fixed number of decimal places that does not vary with the caller. Such a field is rendered in JSON as an unquoted number carrying exactly that many decimal digits, trailing zeros included, so a credit figure of twelve is rendered `12.00` and never `12` or `"12.00"`. The scales are:

- Credit quantities and credit-derived sums are stored at 2 decimal places. This covers `degree_applicable_credits`, `degree_applicable_credits_counted` on both the enrollment and the certification, `transfer_credits_accepted`, and `credit_relief_applied`. It equally covers the registrar's credit inputs and ceilings, each quantised to two places as it is stored: a course's and an enrollment's `credit_hours`, an enrollment's `earned_credit_hours`, a program's `total_required_credits` and `max_elective_credits_countable`, and a rule's `max_countable_credits`.
- `cumulative_gpa` and `gpa_at_certification` are stored at 2 decimal places, as is a program's `min_progress_gpa` benchmark constant. An empty input set yields 0.00 rather than null.
- `progress_pct` and `required_progress_pct` are stored at 4 decimal places. `progress_pct` is stored on the student row and on the certification alike; `required_progress_pct` is a field of the certification and of the on-demand summary of §3.6 only, and is not stored on the student row at all. They are proportions, not percentages: a student who has completed a quarter of the degree holds 0.2500, not 25.
- `risk_score` is stored at 2 decimal places. Each of its four components is rounded to 2 decimal places before it is weighted, and the weighted sum is rounded once at the end.
- `required_study_hall_hours` is stored at 1 decimal place.
- Missed-class quantities — `missed_class_units_allowance`, `missed_units_gross`, `missed_units`, `missed_class_units_charged`, `missed_class_units_remaining` and `charged_units_total` — are stored at 2 decimal places, and the per-meeting rounding described in §3.8 happens before summation, not after.
- `hours_credited` and `logged_hours` are stored at 2 decimal places, as are the contracted tutoring-hour figures: an assignment's `weekly_hours`, and a tutor's `max_weekly_hours` and `assigned_weekly_hours`. A tutor registered with the default ceiling therefore reports `max_weekly_hours` as `20.00`, and a fresh tutor reports `assigned_weekly_hours` as `0.00`.
- `squad_apr` is an integer between 0 and 1000 inclusive, obtained by rounding at 0 decimal places.
- Point counters — `apr_points_possible`, `apr_points_earned`, `apr_points_forfeited`, `penalty_points`, `penalty_points_assessed` and `substantiated_penalty_points` — hold whole numbers; no fractional penalty is ever stored.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names nothing produces that entity's own 404 error before any later segment, body field or business rule is examined. The path identifiers in use are `term_id`, `degree_program_id`, `course_id`, `rule_id`, `student_athlete_id`, `enrollment_id`, `certification_id`, `tutor_id`, `assignment_id`, `session_id`, `event_id`, `request_id`, `appeal_id` and `incident_id`. Once the path resolves, foreign-key identifiers carried in the request body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields.

Each entity owns a distinct 404 code: `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEGREE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICABILITY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_ATHLETE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TUTOR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPORT_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPETITION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MISSED_CLASS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_COMPLIANCE_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`. A body-borne reference resolves to the same code as its path counterpart: an unknown `discovered_session_id` yields `ERR_SUPPORT_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `competition_event_id` yields `ERR_COMPETITION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `predecessor_appeal_id` yields `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`.

A well-formed body is one that parses as a JSON object, supplies every required field, carries only declared values in enum fields, keeps every numeric field inside its declared range, and names a declared action in `action` where an action is expected. Types are checked as strictly as ranges are. A string field must carry a string, and no string field anywhere in this specification accepts an empty or whitespace-only value. A boolean field must carry a true JSON boolean, so the string `"true"` is malformed. An integer field accepts an integer or a whole-valued number such as `3.0` and nothing else; a decimal field accepts a number or a numeric string such as `"3.50"`; and a boolean is never accepted where a number is expected. An instant field accepts any ISO 8601 value — a trailing `Z` or an explicit offset is honoured and normalised to UTC, a value carrying no zone at all is read as UTC, and a plain calendar date is read as midnight UTC of that day — while an unparseable value is malformed. A request that sends no payload where a body is required, or whose payload is not a JSON object at its top level, is malformed too; the request's `Content-Type` is never inspected. Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. Anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — including the conditionally required companion fields, which are required exactly when their action is requested and are rejected as malformed when supplied alongside any other action: `grade` accompanies `RECORD_GRADE`, `severity` accompanies `SUBSTANTIATE`, `granted_credit_relief` accompanies `GRANT`, `term_id` accompanies `COMPLETE_TRAINING`, and `terminated_reason` accompanies `TERMINATE`.

### 2.7. The Validation Ladder

Requests are validated in strictly ascending priority bands. The **first** violated rule decides the response, and no later rule is evaluated — so when a single request breaks several rules at once, the reported error is always the one from the lowest-numbered band. Rules that share a band are mutually exclusive in practice because they guard different endpoints. The ladder is:

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the ten declared role names and that role appears in the called endpoint's authorised list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Ownership scoping.** Three checks share this band, each on its own endpoints. `ERR_NOT_COURSE_INSTRUCTOR` (403) when an instructor acknowledging or disputing a missed-class request is not the request's recorded `instructor_user_id`. `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TUTOR` (403) when a tutor logging a support session is not the tutor behind the named assignment. `ERR_NOT_OWN_RECORD` (403) when a student-athlete reads either the single student-athlete record or the eligibility summary of an id other than its own — both of those two read endpoints, not just one.
4. **Existence.** Every path identifier resolves, left to right, then every body-borne foreign key resolves in declared field order — the entity-specific 404 codes of §2.6.
5. **Request shape.** The body is well-formed as described in §2.6 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that takes a body or query parameters.
6. **Instant pairs.** Supplied window bounds satisfy strict ordering — `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (422). Applies to term registration, competition-event scheduling and support-session logging.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_student_athlete_version` matches the student's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to certification creation only. Because it sits above every state check, a stale version on a request that would *also* have failed a state precondition reports the 412, not the state error.
8. **Term reporting freeze.** The governing term is still open for academic-progress-rate reporting — `ERR_APR_TERM_CLOSED` (409). Applies to seven operations: certification creation, certification status actions, appeal creation, appeal status actions, enrollment-outcome recording, missed-class-request creation, and the `APPROVE_TRAVEL` action on a competition event. On the first six the freeze is evaluated in this band, ahead of every state check; on `APPROVE_TRAVEL` alone it is evaluated after the `SCHEDULED` precondition, so an approval attempted from any other status against a closed term reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than the freeze. No other competition-event action and no missed-class-request status action consults the governing term at all.
9. **State legality.** Three checks share this band. `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409) when the requested action is not a declared transition out of the target's current state — this covers every status-changing endpoint, the academic-progress-rate close checkpoint and enrollment-outcome recording, and it covers every attempt made from a terminal state and every repeat of an already-applied action. `ERR_APR_TERM_NOT_ENDED` (409) when a term close is requested while the logical clock is still before the term's `end_date`. `ERR_APR_PRIOR_TERM_OPEN` (409) when a term close is requested while an earlier reporting term remains open.
10. **Cross-entity state.** `ERR_EVENT_NOT_TRAVEL_APPROVED` (409) when a new missed-class request names a competition event that is not travel-approved; because this band outranks every business rule below it, a request that targets a merely scheduled event *and* would exceed the student's missed-class allowance reports the event error, not the allowance error. The same code also answers a `NOTIFY_INSTRUCTOR` action — a first notification and a re-notification alike — whose event has left `TRAVEL_APPROVED`; there, however, it is evaluated only after the request's own status precondition, so it never displaces `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422) when an appeal is filed against, or decided against, a certification that is neither denied nor revoked — this guards appeal creation and the `SUBMIT`, `GRANT`, `DENY` and `REQUEST_REVISION` actions on an appeal, while `OPEN_REVIEW` and `WITHDRAW` are deliberately exempt and stay available whatever status the linked certification holds.
11. **Safety and duty separation.** `ERR_TUTOR_NOT_TRAINED` (422) when an assignment is created for, or activated or reinstated on, an untrained tutor, and equally when a tutoring session is logged against one — reported even when every referential and capacity condition holds. `ERR_TUTOR_TRAINING_EXPIRED` (422) for those same four operations when the tutor's training has lapsed or the tutor is suspended or terminated, and on session logging also when the session's term of record lies beyond the tutor's `training_valid_through_term_index`. `ERR_IMPERMISSIBLE_ASSISTANCE_BLOCK` (422) when a tutoring session is logged for a student whose support is withheld or against a tutor implicated in a substantiated top-severity incident involving that same student; on that endpoint the two training codes are evaluated before it, so an expired tutor logging for a withheld student reports the training error. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409) on the certify, deny, appeal-grant, appeal-deny and revision-request decisions taken by the same operator who requested or submitted them.
12. **Referential business rules.** `ERR_TUTOR_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (422) on assignment creation — deliberately ordered *after* the training checks, so an untrained tutor who also lacks the subject clearance reports the training error. `ERR_MAJOR_NOT_DECLARED` (422), `ERR_TRANSFER_CREDIT_SOURCE_INVALID` (422) and `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TYPE_TERM_MISMATCH` (422) on certification creation. `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_NOTIFIABLE` (422) on missed-class-request creation.
13. **Temporal business rules.** `ERR_MAJOR_DECLARATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (422) on major declaration. `ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION` (422) on instructor notification. `ERR_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422) on instructor acknowledgement and dispute. `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422) and, in the same band, `ERR_TRANSFER_RESIDENCY_NOT_MET` (422) on transfer-certification creation.
14. **Uniqueness and round limits.** `ERR_STUDENT_SESSION_OVERLAP` (409) and `ERR_TUTOR_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409) on session logging. `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409) on enrollment creation. `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICABILITY_RULE` (409) on applicability-rule creation. `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409) on appeal creation, on the revision-request and resubmit actions, and on missed-class re-notification.
15. **Budget and capacity.** Evaluated last, so any state, safety, referential, temporal or uniqueness violation on the same request wins over these. `ERR_MISSED_CLASS_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (422) on missed-class-request creation. `ERR_TUTOR_WEEKLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (422) and `ERR_TIER_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422) on assignment creation, activation and reinstatement.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Certification Creation

Certification creation is the one write in this system that is allowed to invalidate somebody else's work: a new certification for a student and term pair pushes any existing draft for that same pair into a superseded state and re-freezes the credit audit. A client that holds a stale picture of the student would therefore supersede a draft it never saw and freeze an out-of-date credit snapshot in the same request.

To prevent that, certification creation requires a non-optional `expected_student_athlete_version` field carrying the `version` value of the `StudentAthlete` as the client last observed it. The field is an integer field with a declared minimum of 0, so a negative or non-integer value never reaches the comparison at all and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` from the request-shape band. If the value does equal an integer of at least 0 but does not equal the student's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. This is a stale-version comparison and nothing else: it does not depend on how closely two requests fall together in time, only on whether the student row has advanced since the client read it. Since almost every workflow in this specification increments `StudentAthlete.version` — recording a grade, declaring a major, charging or releasing missed-class units, activating or terminating an assignment, deciding a certification or an appeal, substantiating an incident, closing a term — a client that wants to create a certification must re-read the student immediately beforehand. No other endpoint takes a version field; certification creation is the only one.

### 2.9. The Term as Reporting Boundary

A `Term` carries its own state in `apr_status`, which starts `OPEN` and moves once and only once to `CLOSED` at the academic-progress-rate close checkpoint. `CLOSED` is terminal for the term.

While a term is `OPEN`, everything bound to it stays live: certifications may be created, submitted, decided, revoked and restored; appeals may be filed and decided; enrollment outcomes may be recorded; missed-class requests may be opened. The moment the term closes, every one of those writes is refused with `ERR_APR_TERM_CLOSED`, and the certifications and appeals bound to that term are frozen with their snapshot fields exactly as the close left them. The freeze is scoped by the term the *write* is bound to — the term on the certification, the term of the appealed certification, the enrollment's term, the term of the competition event behind a missed-class request — and not by the term the operator happens to be working in.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Three quantities in this system are ledgers, and each satisfies an equality that must hold after every single request, not merely eventually.

**The missed-class budget.** For every student, `missed_class_units_allowance` equals `missed_class_units_charged` plus `missed_class_units_remaining` at all times. `missed_class_units_charged` is the sum of `missed_units` over that student's missed-class requests in the holding statuses `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `DISPUTED`; requests in `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` release their units. Whenever a request enters or leaves the holding set, the charged total and the remaining total move by the same amount in opposite directions, restoring the equality within the same request.

**The academic-progress-rate points ledger.** For every student, `apr_points_possible` equals `apr_points_earned` plus `apr_points_forfeited` exactly, after every close. Points are never rolled back once awarded or forfeited: revoking a certification after a close changes the student's eligibility standing but leaves all three counters untouched, so the equality survives.

**Tutor capacity.** For every tutor, `assigned_weekly_hours` equals the sum of `weekly_hours` over that tutor's assignments whose status is `ACTIVE` — no more and no less — after every assignment transition, every cascade and every tutor status change. A proposed assignment holds no capacity, a suspended assignment releases the capacity it held, and reinstatement re-reserves it. Releases floor at zero; capacity is never negative.

### 2.11. Cascades, Ordering and Version Semantics

Many operations here touch more than one entity. Three rules govern how those cascades behave. First, they are **immediate and in-request**: there is no deferred queue, and a response reflects the fully settled state of every entity the request touched. Second, when a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows — the assignments of a tutor being suspended, the missed-class requests of a cancelled event — the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, which matters because a capped or floored accumulator can otherwise produce different totals. Third, `version` and `updated_at` move on every row a cascade actually mutates, not merely on the row named in the path; a request that terminates three assignments and drops a tutor's assigned hours has advanced four rows.

The recomputation chain deserves its own statement because it recurs throughout §3. When an input to the academic picture changes, the derived fields are refreshed in this order: the credit audit and transfer-credit acceptance first, then the progress-benchmark evaluation, then the risk score and its tier band, then the study-hall requirement, and only then is `support_status` re-derived from the freshly written tier. Running that chain out of order — deriving `support_status` from a stale `risk_tier`, for instance — yields observably different stored values. Not every request enters the chain at the top, and where it does not, only the fields from its entry point downward are written back. A request that moves a single input — a missed-class charge or release, an incident substantiation, an incident remediation, an assignment transition — enters at the risk-score step or at the study-hall step. The risk-score entry point recomputes the credit audit and the benchmark internally, always under the default variant selection and never under a variant some certification once resolved, but persists only `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `required_study_hall_hours` and `support_status`, leaving `cumulative_gpa`, `completed_full_time_terms`, `transfer_credits_accepted`, `degree_applicable_credits` and `progress_pct` exactly as they stood — so a stored `progress_pct` frozen under one variant can coexist with a `risk_score` computed from another. The study-hall entry point writes only `required_study_hall_hours` and `support_status`, reading the `risk_tier` already on the row.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Academic Catalogue

Nothing in this system can be certified until the registrar has described the calendar and the curriculum. A term is registered with a registrar term `code` such as `2026FA` — four digits followed by `FA`, `SP` or `SU`, unique across all terms — together with a `term_index`, a `term_type` of `FALL`, `SPRING` or `SUMMER`, a `catalog_year`, and the window bounds `start_date` and `end_date`. The `term_index` is a monotonic ordinal of the term in the academic calendar, must be at least 1, and is unique across all terms; a duplicate `code` or a duplicate `term_index` is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and that uniqueness check is made before the `start_date`/`end_date` ordering check, so a duplicate registration carrying an inverted window reports the 400 rather than the 422. `catalog_year` must be at least 2000 and identifies the degree catalog in force during the term, which one of the two credit-audit variants reads. Two optional inputs shape how the term behaves later: `full_time_credit_minimum`, the completed resident credit hours a student must hold in this term for it to count as a completed full-time term, which defaults to 12 and must be at least 1; and `is_apr_reporting_term`, which defaults to true and decides whether the term participates in point accounting at all. A newly registered term is `OPEN`, with `apr_points_possible` and `apr_points_earned` both 0 and `squad_apr` null. The two window bounds are echoed back normalised to the three-fractional-digit UTC rendering of §2.4, so a `start_date` supplied as a plain date comes back as that date's midnight instant.

A degree program is registered with a unique `code` such as `KINE-BS` — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a human-readable `name`, the `catalog_year` in which the program record was published, and `total_required_credits` — the degree-applicable credit hours needed to graduate, which must be greater than zero and which serves as the denominator of every progress proportion. Three benchmark constants may be supplied and otherwise default: `min_progress_gpa`, the minimum cumulative grade-point average for a passing benchmark, defaults to 2.0 and must fall between 0 and 4.0 inclusive; `major_declaration_deadline_term_count`, the number of completed full-time terms after which the student must have declared this major, defaults to 4 and must be at least 1; and `max_elective_credits_countable`, the ceiling on the total elective-classified credits the audit may count for this program, defaults to 12 and may not be negative.

Courses carry both a catalogue identity and a weekly meeting pattern, because the same record has to answer "does this count toward the degree" and "how much class does the athlete miss when the team flies out on Thursday". A course is registered with a `subject_code` prefix such as `KINE`, a `course_number` such as `190`, carried as a string rather than a number and therefore compared as one wherever it orders or filters a list — the pair is unique across all courses, and a duplicate pair is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `title`, `credit_hours` of record greater than 0 and at most 12, and an `instructor_user_id`, which is the opaque operator identifier of the instructor of record rather than a foreign key to any stored row and which is required and may not be empty, so no stored course can ever lack a notification recipient. The meeting pattern is optional and defaults sensibly: `meeting_days` holds ISO weekday numbers, where 1 is Monday through 7 is Sunday, and defaults to an empty list, meaning the course never meets and can never generate a missed meeting; `meeting_start_minute_utc` is the number of minutes after midnight UTC at which each meeting begins, defaults to 600 (that is, 10:00 UTC), and must lie between 0 and 1439 inclusive; and `meeting_duration_minutes` is the length of one meeting, defaults to 50 and must be greater than 0 and at most 480. A course may also be flagged `is_remedial`, defaulting to false, which changes how its credits are treated by the audit in a way §3.3 describes.

Degree applicability is expressed as a junction between one program and one course for one catalog year, and it is the pivot on which the whole credit audit turns. Each rule names a `degree_program_id`, a `course_id` and an `effective_catalog_year`, and classifies the pairing through `applicability`: `CORE` means the course counts in full, `ELECTIVE` means it counts but against the program's elective ceiling, and `NOT_APPLICABLE` means it counts zero. At most one rule may exist for a given program, course and catalog-year triple; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICABILITY_RULE`. An optional `max_countable_credits` places a per-rule ceiling on how many credits this course may contribute; it defaults to null, may not be negative when supplied, and when it is null the enrollment's full earned credit hours are eligible. Because publishing a rule retroactively changes what already-completed coursework is worth, declaring one immediately re-runs, for every student whose declared program is the rule's program and in `created_at` ascending order, transfer-credit acceptance and the credit audit, followed by the progress-benchmark evaluation, the risk score with its tier band, the study-hall requirement and the re-derivation of `support_status` for each of them. Those students' derived fields are rewritten in place: notwithstanding the general cascade rule of §2.11, this one cascade advances neither the affected students' `version` nor their `updated_at`.

### 3.2. Students, Enrollments and Graded Outcomes

A student-athlete is registered with a `full_name`, a `sport_code` drawn from `MBB`, `WBB`, `FB`, `BASE`, `SOC` and `TRACK` — the squad the student competes for and the cohort key for point reporting — an `entry_term_index` of at least 1 naming the term ordinal in which the student first enrolled, and an `entry_catalog_year` recording the catalog in force at entry. Optionally, `transfer_status` may be set to `INCOMING_TRANSFER` for a student whose record includes external coursework; it defaults to `NATIVE`. Also optionally, `missed_class_units_allowance` sets the season budget of missed-class units the student may be charged; it may not be negative and defaults to 9.0 units. A fresh record has no declared major, so `declared_program_id` and `major_declared_term_index` are both null; `eligibility_state` is `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `support_status` is `SUPPORT_NORMAL`, `risk_tier` is `TIER_1`, `missed_class_units_charged` is 0 and `missed_class_units_remaining` equals the allowance. Every derived figure starts at zero on the fresh row — `cumulative_gpa` 0.00, `degree_applicable_credits` 0.00, `transfer_credits_accepted` 0.00, `progress_pct` 0.0000, `risk_score` 0.00, `required_study_hall_hours` 0.0, and `completed_full_time_terms`, `apr_points_possible`, `apr_points_earned`, `apr_points_forfeited` and `substantiated_penalty_points` all 0 — and the student row carries no `required_progress_pct`, `benchmark_result` or `failure_reasons` field of its own: the benchmark verdict lives on the certification and in the on-demand summary of §3.6.

Declaring a major is a small operation with large consequences. A registrar or an academic advisor records it by supplying the `declared_program_id` being declared and the `term_id` of the term in which the declaration is filed; the system writes `major_declared_term_index` from that term's ordinal. The deadline is enforced against the program's own rule: the declaration term's ordinal must not exceed the student's `entry_term_index` plus the program's `major_declaration_deadline_term_count` minus one, so a student who entered in term 3 declaring against a program with the default deadline of four terms may still declare in terms 3, 4, 5 and 6, and a declaration in term 7 is rejected with `ERR_MAJOR_DECLARATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A successful declaration re-runs the credit audit, transfer-credit acceptance, the progress-benchmark evaluation, the risk score and the study-hall requirement, and advances the student's `version`. Once that chain has settled, `eligibility_state` is re-derived from the governing certification exactly as §3.4 describes — so where the student was already `PROVISIONALLY_CERTIFIED` on the strength of a certified certification filed before any major existed, writing a non-null `declared_program_id` moves `eligibility_state` from `PROVISIONALLY_CERTIFIED` to `CERTIFIED`, while a student with no non-superseded certification at all keeps the eligibility standing it already held. Declaring a major may be repeated to change programs, subject to the same deadline.

An enrollment binds a `student_athlete_id`, a `course_id` and a `term_id`. Its `credit_hours` may be supplied — greater than 0 and at most 12 — and when omitted the referenced course's `credit_hours` is copied at creation. Its `credit_source` is `RESIDENT` for coursework taken at this institution and `TRANSFER` for external coursework on an incoming transfer's record, defaulting to `RESIDENT`. Resident enrollments are created in `ENROLLED` with `earned_credit_hours` of 0, `degree_applicable_credits_counted` of 0, `is_full_time_countable` false, `grade` and `grade_points` null and `outcome_recorded_at` null; transfer rows are created directly in `COMPLETED`, and because no grade will ever be recorded against them their `earned_credit_hours` is set equal to their `credit_hours` at creation while `grade`, `grade_points` and `outcome_recorded_at` stay null, `degree_applicable_credits_counted` is 0 and `is_full_time_countable` is false. A `credit_source` of `TRANSFER` is accepted whatever the student's `transfer_status` says — the pairing is not checked here, and it is the acceptance waterfall of §3.3 that declines to count transfer coursework for a `NATIVE` student. At most one non-terminal enrollment may exist for a given student, course and term triple, the non-terminal statuses being `ENROLLED` and `INCOMPLETE`; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`.

The registrar records the terminal fate of an enrollment through a single outcome operation whose `action` is one of `RECORD_GRADE`, `MARK_INCOMPLETE`, `DROP` or `WITHDRAW`; `grade` is supplied with `RECORD_GRADE` and is forbidden with the other three. Recording a grade takes an enrollment from `ENROLLED` to `COMPLETED`, and equally takes one from `INCOMPLETE` to `COMPLETED` — a course left unfinished can be graded later, and both edges run the identical recomputation chain. The supplied `grade` must be one of `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` or `F`; the system writes `grade_points` from the fixed scale where `A` is 4.00, `B` is 3.00, `C` is 2.00, `D` is 1.00 and `F` is 0.00, sets `earned_credit_hours` to the enrollment's `credit_hours` when `grade_points` is greater than 0.00 and to 0 otherwise (an `F` is attempted but never earned), sets `is_full_time_countable`, and stamps `outcome_recorded_at` from the logical clock.

`MARK_INCOMPLETE` moves an `ENROLLED` row to `INCOMPLETE`, leaving `earned_credit_hours` at 0 so the term cannot become a completed full-time term through that row. `DROP` moves an `ENROLLED` row to `DROPPED`, and `WITHDRAW` moves either an `ENROLLED` or an `INCOMPLETE` row to `WITHDRAWN`; both set `earned_credit_hours` to 0, `DROP` additionally zeroes `degree_applicable_credits_counted`, and both run the same tutoring cascade — every tutor assignment on that enrollment whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_reason` set to `ENROLLMENT_DROPPED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each formerly `ACTIVE` assignment releasing its `weekly_hours` from the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours`. Because the released tutor hours change the student's tutoring credit, the study-hall requirement re-runs and `support_status` is re-derived. Every one of the four actions stamps `outcome_recorded_at` from the logical clock, the three non-grade actions included. All four actions are refused with `ERR_APR_TERM_CLOSED` once the enrollment's term has closed, and all four are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` from `COMPLETED`, `DROPPED` or `WITHDRAWN`, which are terminal.

Two aggregates are rebuilt from the enrollment set on every outcome. The first is `cumulative_gpa`: over the student's enrollments that are `COMPLETED`, carry `credit_source` of `RESIDENT` and hold a grade among `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` and `F`, take the sum of each row's `grade_points` multiplied by its attempted `credit_hours`, divide by the sum of those same attempted credit hours, and round half up to two decimal places. With an empty input set the value is 0.00 rather than null. The second is `completed_full_time_terms`: for each term, add up the attempted `credit_hours` of the student's `COMPLETED` resident enrollments in that term, and count the term when that sum reaches the term's `full_time_credit_minimum`. The comparison is inclusive, so a term with exactly 12 attempted credits against a minimum of 12 counts. `completed_full_time_terms` is the number of distinct terms that qualify, and `is_full_time_countable` is set true on each `COMPLETED` resident enrollment whose term qualified and false otherwise. Enrollments in `ENROLLED`, `DROPPED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `INCOMPLETE` contribute to neither aggregate. Every one of the four outcome actions then runs the recomputation chain of §2.11 for the student in the order that section states, so `cumulative_gpa`, `completed_full_time_terms`, `transfer_credits_accepted`, `degree_applicable_credits`, `progress_pct`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `required_study_hall_hours` and `support_status` are all refreshed from the new enrollment set — a `DROP` or a `WITHDRAW` may therefore move the risk tier and the support dimension even where no tutor assignment was involved.

### 3.3. The Degree-Applicability Credit Audit

The credit audit answers one question — how many of this student's earned credits actually move the student toward the declared degree — and its answer, `degree_applicable_credits`, is the numerator of every progress judgement in the system. Its input set is the student's enrollments that are `COMPLETED` and whose `grade_points` are greater than 0.00, meaning a grade of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`; each such enrollment is joined to the applicability rule whose program is the student's `declared_program_id`, whose course is the enrollment's `course_id`, and whose `effective_catalog_year` is the year the active variant selects. Enrollments in `ENROLLED`, `DROPPED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `INCOMPLETE`, and rows graded `F` with `grade_points` of 0.00, are excluded outright.

Which catalog year is read is the audit's **variant**, and the two variants are genuinely different answers rather than presentation choices. Under `CATALOG_AT_ENTRY` the year read is the student's `entry_catalog_year`, so the rules the student enrolled under are honoured for life. Under `CATALOG_AT_CERTIFICATION` the year read is the `catalog_year` of the term the certification governs, so a course whose classification changed between catalog years resolves to the newer rule row. The variant is resolved by a strict ladder when a certification is created: a `TRANSFER` certification is forced to `CATALOG_AT_CERTIFICATION` whatever the request says; an `INITIAL` certification is forced to `CATALOG_AT_ENTRY` just as unconditionally, so an `audit_variant` supplied on either of those two types is still validated against the two declared variant names — a third value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and then ignored; otherwise, for a `CONTINUING` certification, an explicitly supplied `audit_variant` is honoured; and when `audit_variant` is omitted the variant is `CATALOG_AT_ENTRY` when the student's `major_declared_term_index` is null or is at most `entry_term_index`, and `CATALOG_AT_CERTIFICATION` when the major was declared later than entry. The resolved value is stored on the certification's `audit_variant` field. When the audit runs outside any certification — for the on-demand summary of §3.6 — and the caller asks for `CATALOG_AT_CERTIFICATION`, the year read is the `catalog_year` of the term with the greatest `term_index` that holds at least one enrollment of this student, and where the student holds no enrollment at all the student's `entry_catalog_year` is read instead.

Given a variant, the audit proceeds in a fixed order that no implementation may rearrange, because the caps interact.

1. **Classify.** For each input enrollment, take `applicability` from the matched rule. When no rule row exists for the triple of declared program, course and effective catalog year, the enrollment is treated as `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
2. **Apply the per-course ceiling.** Each enrollment's countable figure is its `earned_credit_hours`, except that when the matched rule carries a non-null `max_countable_credits` the countable figure is whichever of the two is smaller.
3. **Sum the core.** Add the countable figures of every row classified `CORE`; that total enters the result in full.
4. **Sum the electives against the program ceiling.** Walk the rows classified `ELECTIVE` in the order term ordinal ascending, then course `subject_code` ascending, then `course_number` ascending, then enrollment `created_at` ascending, adding each countable figure while the running elective total stays at or below the program's `max_elective_credits_countable`. The row that would carry the running total past the ceiling contributes only the headroom that remains, and every later elective row contributes zero. The elective total is that capped running sum.
5. **Sum the remedial credits under their own cap.** Remedial rows are excluded from the core and elective sums entirely and handled here instead. Add the countable figures of rows whose course carries `is_remedial` true and whose term ordinal is at most the student's `entry_term_index` plus one, and cap that total at 6 credits; remedial rows in any later term contribute zero. The classification of step 1 is not consulted here at all: a remedial row counts under this cap whatever its `applicability` says, a row matched by no rule and therefore treated as `NOT_APPLICABLE` included. The cap is filled in the same order as the elective walk, the crossing row contributing only the headroom that remains and every later remedial row contributing zero.
6. **Add accepted transfer credit.** The transfer total is the student's `transfer_credits_accepted`, computed as described below.
7. **Total and round.** `degree_applicable_credits` is the core total plus the capped elective total plus the capped remedial total plus the transfer total, rounded half up to two decimal places.
8. **The undeclared-major path.** While `declared_program_id` is null — reachable only for an `INITIAL` certification — no applicability rule can match, so the core, elective and remedial totals are all 0.00 and `degree_applicable_credits` is exactly `transfer_credits_accepted`.

Every enrollment records what it contributed the last time the audit ran for its student, in `degree_applicable_credits_counted`, and the `is_full_time_countable` flag of §3.2 is rewritten alongside it on every enrollment of that student. Both rewrites are silent: they advance neither the enrollment's `version` nor its `updated_at`, notwithstanding the general cascade rule of §2.11, so a sibling enrollment's recorded contribution and countability can change with no visible mutation on its row whatever. The only enrollment whose `version` and `updated_at` move is the one the request itself names — the row whose outcome is being recorded — and a major declaration or a rule publication, which names no enrollment, moves none of them. The audit re-runs whenever an enrollment outcome is recorded, whenever a major declaration is filed, whenever an applicability rule is published, and at certification creation, where its output is frozen onto the certification rather than merely stored on the student.

Transfer credit is accepted through a **waterfall** rather than a single sum, because the two buckets have separate ceilings and the overflow of one does not spill into the other. The input set is the student's enrollments whose `credit_source` is `TRANSFER` and whose status is `COMPLETED`, joined to the applicability rule for the declared program at the catalog year the active audit variant selected; resident enrollments are excluded. Order those rows by term ordinal ascending, then `subject_code` ascending, then `course_number` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Fill the first bucket from the `CORE`-applicable rows, each contributing its `earned_credit_hours` or its rule's `max_countable_credits` when that is smaller and non-null, while the running bucket total stays at or below 40.00 credits, with the crossing row contributing only the remaining headroom. Only once the first bucket is exhausted or has no further rows, fill the second bucket the same way from the `ELECTIVE`-applicable rows against a ceiling of 20.00 credits. Rows classified `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and every row that arrives after its bucket's ceiling is reached, contribute zero, and the remainder is discarded rather than moved to the other bucket. `transfer_credits_accepted` is the smaller of 60.00 credits and the sum of the two bucket totals, rounded half up to two decimal places. For a student whose `transfer_status` is `NATIVE` the figure is 0.

A separate residency gate rides alongside the same computation. When the student's `transfer_status` is `INCOMING_TRANSFER` and the student holds fewer than one term with a `COMPLETED` resident enrollment, a `TRANSFER` certification is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_RESIDENCY_NOT_MET` — but the accepted-credit figure itself is still computed and stored, because the number is needed for advising even when certification is not yet available.

### 3.4. Progress Benchmarks and the Certification Lifecycle

The progress-toward-degree benchmark converts the audit's credit figure into a pass or a fail, and it is evaluated in six steps.

Where the student has no declared major — the provisional path — the evaluation short-circuits: `progress_pct` and `required_progress_pct` are both 0.0000, the grade-point test compares `cumulative_gpa` against a provisional floor of 1.80 rather than the program's `min_progress_gpa`, steps one through five are skipped, and `benchmark_result` is `PASS` exactly when that provisional grade-point test passes.

Otherwise: `progress_pct` is `degree_applicable_credits` divided by the program's `total_required_credits`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The benchmark itself keys off the count of completed full-time terms: for a student with fewer than two such terms `required_progress_pct` is 0.0000 — the first two full-time terms are a grace period during which no credit progress is demanded — and from the second completed full-time term onward `required_progress_pct` is ten percent of the degree per completed full-time term, expressed as 0.10 multiplied by that count, never exceeding 1.0000, and rounded half up to four decimal places. The grade-point test passes when `cumulative_gpa` is at least the program's `min_progress_gpa`. The progress test passes when `progress_pct` is at least `required_progress_pct`; the comparison is inclusive, so a `progress_pct` exactly equal to `required_progress_pct` is a pass. `benchmark_result` is `PASS` when both tests pass and `FAIL` otherwise, and on a failure `failure_reasons` carries the codes of the tests that failed in a fixed order — `PROGRESS_BELOW_BENCHMARK` first, then `GPA_BELOW_MINIMUM` — and is an empty list on a pass. Finally, where waiver relief has been granted for the certification under evaluation, `credit_relief_applied` takes the `granted_credit_relief` of the appeal whose status is `GRANTED` and whose `round_number` is the highest, ties broken in favour of the later `created_at` and then of the later-inserted of two appeals sharing that instant. Only `GRANTED` appeals are candidates, so a subsequent denial, withdrawal or revision never displaces a grant, and where the certification carries no granted appeal the figure is 0.00. The progress proportion is then recomputed with the audit credits plus that relief, and the certification's own `degree_applicable_credits_counted` is written as the audit figure plus the same relief.

Certification is a two-operator workflow by design. A `CertificationOfficer` creates the certification, naming the `student_athlete_id`, the `term_id` of the competition term it governs, a `certification_type`, and the `expected_student_athlete_version` of §2.8; `audit_variant` may be supplied to override the default variant selection. `certification_type` must match the term and the student: `INITIAL` is valid only when the term's ordinal equals the student's `entry_term_index`, `CONTINUING` only when the term's ordinal is strictly greater than `entry_term_index`, and `TRANSFER` only for a student whose `transfer_status` is `INCOMING_TRANSFER`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TYPE_TERM_MISMATCH`. A `CONTINUING` or `TRANSFER` certification additionally requires a non-null `declared_program_id`, failing which the create is rejected with `ERR_MAJOR_NOT_DECLARED`, while an `INITIAL` certification is accepted with a null declared program and takes the provisional path instead. A `TRANSFER` certification also requires at least one enrollment with `credit_source` of `TRANSFER` and status `COMPLETED`, failing which the create is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_CREDIT_SOURCE_INVALID`; there is no converse condition, so an `INITIAL` or a `CONTINUING` certification is created whatever transfer coursework the record happens to hold. These creation conditions share a band but are not mutually exclusive, so their relative order is fixed: a create that breaks several of them at once reports `ERR_MAJOR_NOT_DECLARED` first, then `ERR_TRANSFER_CREDIT_SOURCE_INVALID`, then `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TYPE_TERM_MISMATCH`, and last of all `ERR_TRANSFER_RESIDENCY_NOT_MET`.

Creation puts the certification in `DRAFT` and freezes the academic picture onto it: the audit and the benchmark evaluation run, and `degree_applicable_credits_counted`, `required_progress_pct`, `progress_pct`, `gpa_at_certification`, `completed_full_time_terms_counted`, `benchmark_result` and `failure_reasons` are written. `completed_full_time_terms_counted` counts only the completed full-time terms whose ordinal is strictly less than the certification term's own ordinal — the term being certified is not itself counted. `requested_by_user_id` is set to the creating operator's `X-User-ID`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` remain null, `credit_relief_applied` is 0, and the resolved variant is stored in `audit_variant`. Creating a certification moves every existing `DRAFT` row for that same student and term to `SUPERSEDED`, in `created_at` ascending order, which advances each such row's `version`, keeps its snapshot fields frozen exactly as they were, and permanently closes it to further transitions. Only `DRAFT` rows are swept: a row already `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED` or `RESTORED` for that same pair is left exactly as it stands, so several non-superseded certifications for one student and term may coexist and the newest draft never supersedes itself. `SUPERSEDED` is the certification's only terminal state. The create also writes the freshly computed picture onto the student row itself — `cumulative_gpa`, `completed_full_time_terms`, `transfer_credits_accepted`, `degree_applicable_credits`, `progress_pct`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier` and `required_study_hall_hours`, all computed under the variant the certification just resolved — and re-derives `support_status` from the tier so written; `eligibility_state` is not touched by a create. The student's `version` increments once on the create.

An `AcademicAdvisor` then submits the draft, taking the certification `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. The division of labour described here and in the two paragraphs below is procedural rather than enforced: the certification status endpoint authorises `CertificationOfficer` and `AcademicAdvisor` alike and narrows none of its four actions by role, so an advisor may also certify, deny or revoke and an officer may also submit. Submission refreshes the snapshot: the audit and the benchmark evaluation re-run and rewrite `degree_applicable_credits_counted`, `progress_pct`, `required_progress_pct`, `gpa_at_certification`, `completed_full_time_terms_counted`, `benchmark_result`, `failure_reasons` and `credit_relief_applied`, and the certification's own `version` increments by one while the student row is left entirely untouched, so a submit moves no student field and no student `version`. A later appeal grant, as §3.5 describes, refreshes that same snapshot once more. Submission is refused while the student's `support_status` is `SUPPORT_WITHHELD` — an athlete under a withheld-support finding cannot be advanced toward eligibility at all. A `CONTINUING` submission is refused unless the student has a non-null `declared_program_id`, and is also refused when the student's `completed_full_time_terms` has grown beyond the program's `major_declaration_deadline_term_count`, which is the point at which an undeclared or late-declared major becomes disqualifying rather than merely overdue. A `TRANSFER` submission is refused unless the student holds at least one completed full-time term counted from resident enrollments alone. Any of these precondition failures, and any submit attempted from a status other than `DRAFT`, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` unless one of the more specific codes named in §2.7 applies first.

A `CertificationOfficer` who is *not* the requester then decides. Certifying takes the certification `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` and requires `benchmark_result` to be `PASS`; a submitted certification carrying `FAIL` cannot be certified — the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and because that check sits in the state band it is reported ahead of the duty-separation check, so even the requester's own attempt on a failing certification reports the state error — and must be denied or repaired through appeal. Denying takes it `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and imposes no benchmark condition. Both decisions write `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock, both require the deciding operator to differ from `requested_by_user_id` on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, both require the governing term to be open, and both advance the student's `version` by one because both re-derive the student's `eligibility_state`.

That re-derivation is the second of the student's two independent state dimensions and reads only from the **governing certification** — the non-superseded certification of the highest term ordinal that exists for the student; where two non-superseded certifications sit in that same term, the one with the later `created_at` governs, and the later-inserted of two sharing that instant wins. When the governing certification becomes `CERTIFIED`, `eligibility_state` moves from `NOT_CERTIFIED` to `CERTIFIED` if the student has a non-null `declared_program_id`, and to `PROVISIONALLY_CERTIFIED` if the declared program is still null; the later filing of a major then lifts `PROVISIONALLY_CERTIFIED → CERTIFIED` as described in §3.2. When the governing certification becomes `DENIED` or `REVOKED`, `eligibility_state` moves `CERTIFIED → INELIGIBLE`. When it becomes `RESTORED`, `eligibility_state` moves `INELIGIBLE → RESTORED_BY_WAIVER`, and a subsequent revocation moves it back `RESTORED_BY_WAIVER → INELIGIBLE`. None of these re-derivations touches `support_status`: the two dimensions live on the same row and evolve independently, and a certification decision never moves the support dimension.

Revocation is available to a `CertificationOfficer` from either `CERTIFIED` or `RESTORED`, and it is also driven as a system cascade when a top-severity impermissible-assistance finding is substantiated against the student. In both cases the certification moves to `REVOKED`, the student's `eligibility_state` is re-derived to `INELIGIBLE`, and the student's `version` increments. The open-term requirement binds only the operator's own `REVOKE`, where it is checked ahead of the `CERTIFIED`-or-`RESTORED` precondition; the substantiation cascade of §3.9 revokes whatever the certification's term status says and is never refused for a closed term. Revocation records no decision of its own — `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` keep whatever the earlier certify or deny wrote there — and it is the one certification action exempt from `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, so the very operator who requested or decided the certification may revoke it. Because a revoked certification can be restored by a granted appeal and a restored certification can be revoked again, the cycle `CERTIFIED → REVOKED → RESTORED → REVOKED` may repeat any number of times for as long as the term stays open. `RESTORED` is never requested directly by any operator: it is produced only by the appeal-grant cascade of §3.5.

### 3.5. Appeals, Waivers and Restoration

An appeal is the only route back from a denial. An `AcademicAdvisor` or a `CertificationOfficer` files one against a certification by naming its `certification_id`, an `appeal_type` drawn from `PROGRESS_WAIVER`, `MISSED_CLASS_EXCEPTION`, `TRANSFER_EXCEPTION` and `ASSISTANCE_REINSTATEMENT`, and a `rationale` narrative supporting the waiver. Optionally the filing carries `requested_credit_relief`, the degree-applicable credits the committee is asked to waive, which defaults to 0 and must be between 0 and 12 inclusive. Optionally it also carries a `predecessor_appeal_id`, naming the earlier round this filing continues; when present, `round_number` is the predecessor's `round_number` plus one, and when absent `round_number` is 1. The predecessor need only exist — it is not required to hang off the same certification, or to sit in any particular status — and it is the one body-borne reference in this specification resolved *after* the filing's own scalar fields have been shape-checked, so a body that names an unknown `predecessor_appeal_id` and also carries a malformed `appeal_type`, `rationale` or `requested_credit_relief` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, while an unknown `certification_id` still reports its 404 ahead of every shape check. The appeal is created in `DRAFT` with `granted_credit_relief` of 0, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` null, and `submitted_by_user_id` set to the filing operator's `X-User-ID`. The linked certification must be `DENIED` or `REVOKED` — an appeal against a certification that is neither has nothing to except and is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, which guards both the filing and every later decision on the appeal.

Each certification supports at most three appeal rounds. A filing whose predecessor chain has already reached `round_number` 3 is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, and so is a revision request or a resubmission on an appeal already sitting at `round_number` 3.

The advisor submits the draft, taking the appeal `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and setting `round_number` to 1 — overwriting whatever a predecessor chain gave the row at filing time, so an appeal filed at round 3 sits at round 1 the moment it is submitted; the appealed certification's term must still be open, as it must for every subsequent step, `OPEN_REVIEW` and `WITHDRAW` included. As with certifications, the split of moves between advisor and committee is procedural rather than enforced: the appeal status endpoint authorises `AppealsCommittee` and `AcademicAdvisor` alike for all six of its actions and narrows none of them by role. The committee then opens review, taking the appeal `SUBMITTED → COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, which changes nothing beyond the status and `updated_at`. From `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` the committee has exactly three moves, and all three require the deciding member to differ from `submitted_by_user_id` where a decision is recorded.

Denying an appeal takes it `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → DENIED`, writes `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, and leaves the appealed certification in whatever status it already held. Requesting a revision takes the appeal `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and writes `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` for that round; it is available only while `round_number` is below 3. The advisor may then resubmit, taking the appeal `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, which increments `round_number` by one and clears `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` so that the next round starts undecided; this edge closes the revision cycle, and a fourth submission is refused because `round_number` would pass the three-round ceiling. An advisor may also withdraw an appeal outright from `DRAFT`, from `SUBMITTED` or from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, moving it to `WITHDRAWN`, after which it accepts no further transitions. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the appeal's three terminal states.

Granting is the interesting one, because it is the system's only cascade that *improves* a student's standing. The committee supplies `granted_credit_relief`, which must be a number of at least 0 and may not exceed the appeal's `requested_credit_relief`; a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`. That ceiling carries no upper bound of its own beyond the request it is measured against, and it is checked last of the grant's three conditions — after the transition's own legality and after duty separation — so an out-of-range figure offered by the appeal's own submitter reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The grant takes the appeal `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → GRANTED` and writes `granted_credit_relief`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`. Because relief was written, the appealed certification's whole snapshot is refreshed: `credit_relief_applied` is rewritten by the winning-appeal selection of §3.4 rather than incremented — the field is a selection and never a running total, so two grants of three credits on successive rounds leave it at 3.00 and never 6.00 — `degree_applicable_credits_counted` becomes the audit figure plus that relief, and the progress-benchmark evaluation re-runs on that certification with the relief folded into the credit figure. That refresh, and the `version` increment that accompanies it, happen on every grant whether or not the benchmark flips. If and only if the re-evaluated `benchmark_result` is now `PASS`, the certification transitions out of its failed state — `DENIED → RESTORED` or `REVOKED → RESTORED` — and because the certification became `RESTORED`, the full recomputation chain of §2.11 runs for the student and rewrites `cumulative_gpa`, `completed_full_time_terms`, `transfer_credits_accepted`, `degree_applicable_credits`, `progress_pct`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `required_study_hall_hours` and `support_status`, after which `eligibility_state` is re-derived to `RESTORED_BY_WAIVER`. That chain runs under the default variant selection and folds in no relief at all, so the waiver moves the certification's frozen credit figure but never the student row's own `degree_applicable_credits`. The student's `version` increments by one. Where the relief is insufficient to flip the benchmark, the appeal still reaches `GRANTED` and the relief is still recorded, but the certification keeps its status — only its refreshed snapshot and advanced `version` record the attempt — and the student row is not touched at all, so no student field and no student `version` moves; the restoration is conditional on the benchmark, not on the committee's goodwill. Restoration also requires the governing term to still be open.

### 3.6. Academic Risk, the Study-Hall Obligation and the Support Dimension

Academic support is allocated by a risk score rather than by intuition, and the score is a weighted blend of four signals, each expressed on a 0-to-100 scale before weighting. Its inputs are the student's grade-point average, the gap between achieved and required progress, the share of the missed-class budget already consumed, and the student's substantiated impermissible-assistance history — specifically, incidents whose status is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REMEDIATED`, with incidents still `REPORTED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, and those found `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, excluded entirely.

The score has its own **variant**, which decides which grade-point average feeds the first component. Under `TERM_SNAPSHOT` the grade component is the credit-weighted grade-point average over only the enrollments in the student's highest-ordinal term that holds at least one `COMPLETED` enrollment, so a single bad term dominates. Where that input set is empty — no term of the student holds a `COMPLETED` enrollment at all, or the selected term contributes no graded row — the term-snapshot average is 0.00 rather than null, under the same empty-input convention as §2.5, and the grade component is scored from that 0.00 like any other value. Under `CUMULATIVE` the grade component is the lifetime `cumulative_gpa` over every completed enrollment, so an improving student scores lower risk than the term snapshot would suggest and a declining student scores higher. Selection is not free: `TERM_SNAPSHOT` applies when `risk_variant` is omitted and the student's `completed_full_time_terms` is below two, and also when `TERM_SNAPSHOT` is explicitly requested while that count is still below two; `CUMULATIVE` applies when it is explicitly requested, and is **forced** once `completed_full_time_terms` reaches two even if `TERM_SNAPSHOT` was asked for.

The four components are computed as follows. The grade component takes the shortfall of the variant-selected average below a risk floor of 2.30 grade points, treating any surplus as a shortfall of 0.00; that shortfall is divided by 2.30 and multiplied by 100.00, capped at 100.00 — so an average of 2.30 or better scores 0.00 and an average of 0.00 scores the full 100.00. The progress component takes the shortfall of `progress_pct` below `required_progress_pct`, treating any surplus as 0.0000, multiplies it by 400.00, and caps the result at 100.00 — meaning a student a quarter of the degree behind the benchmark is already at the cap. The travel component divides `missed_class_units_charged` by `missed_class_units_allowance`, multiplies by 100.00 and caps at 100.00; where the allowance is itself 0.00 no division is attempted and the component is 100.00 when anything at all has been charged and 0.00 when nothing has. The incident component sums `penalty_points_assessed` over the included incidents, multiplies by 25.00 and caps at 100.00, so four accumulated penalty points saturate it. Each of the four is rounded half up to two decimal places before weighting. `risk_score` is then the grade component weighted at 0.40, plus the progress component at 0.25, plus the travel component at 0.20, plus the incident component at 0.15 — the four weights sum to 1.00 — with the weighted sum rounded half up to two decimal places once at the end.

`risk_tier` bands the score: `TIER_1` below 20.00, `TIER_2` from 20.00 up to but not including 40.00, `TIER_3` from 40.00 up to but not including 65.00, and `TIER_4` at 65.00 and above. Each lower bound is inclusive and each upper bound is exclusive, so a score of exactly 40.00 is `TIER_3` and never `TIER_2`.

The study-hall obligation, `required_study_hall_hours`, is derived from the tier in four ordered steps, and the order is load-bearing. First, the base weekly hours come from the tier: no hours at all for `TIER_1`, 4.0 for `TIER_2`, 8.0 for `TIER_3` and 12.0 for `TIER_4`. Second, a travel offset is added for the class time already lost: one extra hour for every three whole missed-class units charged, discarding any fraction, capped at 4 additional hours. Third — and this is the step most easily got wrong — the tutoring credit ceiling is selected from the **gross** figure of base plus travel offset, not from the figure after any credit is applied: the ceiling is 4.0 hours when the gross figure is 10.0 hours or more, and 2.0 hours otherwise. Fourth, the tutoring credit itself is half an hour of relief per contracted weekly tutoring hour, summed over the student's `ACTIVE` tutor assignments only — assignments in `PROPOSED`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` contribute nothing — and then limited to the ceiling just selected. `required_study_hall_hours` is the gross figure minus that credit, floored at 0.0, rounded half up to one decimal place.

`support_status` is the student's other independent state dimension, and it is a pure function of `risk_tier`, `apr_points_forfeited` and substantiated incidents; it is recomputed after every mutation that touches those inputs and is never moved by a certification decision. From `SUPPORT_NORMAL` a student moves to `SUPPORT_MANDATED` when the tier lands in `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, provided `apr_points_forfeited` is below 2 and no top-severity incident against the student has been substantiated or remediated; the study-hall obligation is rewritten on the move. A student in `SUPPORT_MANDATED` returns to `SUPPORT_NORMAL` when the score falls back to `TIER_1` under the same two provisos. A student escalates to `SUPPORT_PROBATION` — from `SUPPORT_MANDATED` or directly from `SUPPORT_NORMAL` — when the tier reaches `TIER_4` or `apr_points_forfeited` reaches 2, again provided no top-severity substantiated incident exists; that escalation can be driven by a recomputed score or by the points forfeited at a term close. From `SUPPORT_PROBATION` a student steps back to `SUPPORT_MANDATED` when the tier returns to `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` and `apr_points_forfeited` is below 2. Every one of these moves rewrites `required_study_hall_hours`. None of them is available at all, however, while any top-severity incident sits on the student's record — a `REMEDIATED` one just as much as a `SUBSTANTIATED` one: such a student keeps whatever status it holds however far its tier or its forfeited points move, and the single way out of that freeze is the remediation exit from `SUPPORT_WITHHELD` described next.

`SUPPORT_WITHHELD` overrides all of it. From any of the other three statuses, a student whose record acquires a `SUBSTANTIATED` incident of top severity moves straight to `SUPPORT_WITHHELD`, which blocks certification submission and blocks every tutoring session log for that student. The way out is remediation: once every top-severity incident for the student has been moved to `REMEDIATED`, the student leaves `SUPPORT_WITHHELD` for `SUPPORT_MANDATED`, provided the tier sits in `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` and `apr_points_forfeited` is below 2, and the study-hall obligation is rewritten on the way out.

Any authorised reader may ask for the whole picture on demand rather than inferring it from stored fields. That summary capability recomputes the credit audit, the benchmark evaluation, the risk score and the study-hall requirement, optionally taking `audit_variant` to choose the catalog year and `risk_variant` to choose the grade basis — either parameter carrying a value that is not one of its declared variant names is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and returns `student_athlete_id`, the two resolved variant names under the keys `audit_variant` and `risk_variant`, `degree_applicable_credits`, `progress_pct`, `required_progress_pct`, `benchmark_result`, `failure_reasons`, `cumulative_gpa`, `completed_full_time_terms`, `transfer_credits_accepted`, `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `required_study_hall_hours`, `missed_class_units_charged`, `missed_class_units_remaining`, `apr_points_possible`, `apr_points_earned`, `apr_points_forfeited` and the student's `version`. When both variant parameters are omitted, so that the default selection rules are in force, the recomputed results are persisted back onto the student row, together with the audit's per-enrollment contributions and a fresh re-derivation of `support_status`; that write is silent, in that neither the student's `version` nor its `updated_at` advances and the `version` the summary reports is the one the row already held. When a caller overrides a variant, the summary answers the hypothetical without disturbing stored state.

### 3.7. Tutors, Assignments and Session Logging

A `LearningSpecialist` registers a tutor with a `full_name`, a `subject_codes` list naming the course subject prefixes the tutor is cleared to support (defaulting to an empty list, which clears the tutor for nothing), and optionally `max_weekly_hours`, the ceiling on the total contracted hours the tutor may hold at once, which defaults to 20 and must be greater than 0 and at most 40. A new tutor starts `UNTRAINED` with `penalty_points` of 0, `assigned_weekly_hours` of 0, and both `training_completed_term_index` and `training_valid_through_term_index` null. Only a `TRAINED` tutor may hold active assignments or have sessions logged against them.

Compliance training is recorded against a term: the specialist supplies the `term_id` in which training was completed — or the compliance officer does, since the tutor status endpoint authorises `LearningSpecialist` and `ComplianceOfficer` alike for all four of its actions and narrows none of them by role — and the system writes `training_completed_term_index` from that term's ordinal and `training_valid_through_term_index` as that ordinal plus the three-term validity span minus one — that is, the ordinal plus two, so training completed in term 5 remains valid through terms 5, 6 and 7. Completing training moves the tutor `UNTRAINED → TRAINED`, and the identical action moves a lapsed tutor `EXPIRED → TRAINED`, rewriting both training ordinals from the newly supplied term and closing the `TRAINED → EXPIRED → TRAINED` cycle. Those two are the only statuses the action accepts: `COMPLETE_TRAINING` requested for a tutor already `TRAINED`, or for one `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The term named for training is merely read for its ordinal and need not be open. Sessions logged in a term whose ordinal exceeds `training_valid_through_term_index` are refused with `ERR_TUTOR_TRAINING_EXPIRED`, even while the tutor's own status still reads `TRAINED`.

Lapsing is not an action anybody takes; it is a cascade of the term close. When a reporting term is closed, every tutor whose status is `TRAINED` and whose `training_valid_through_term_index` is at or below the closed term's ordinal moves `TRAINED → EXPIRED`, and each of that tutor's `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` assignments whose enrollment belongs to the closed term moves to `COMPLETED` with `terminated_reason` set to `TERM_CLOSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` reduced by each formerly active assignment's `weekly_hours`.

Suspension arrives either by the specialist's or the compliance officer's explicit action or as a cascade once `penalty_points` reaches three, and it applies from `TRAINED` or from `EXPIRED`, in both cases landing on `SUSPENDED`. Suspension is destructive to capacity: every `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` assignment of the tutor moves to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_reason` set to `TUTOR_SUSPENDED`, in `created_at` ascending order, `assigned_weekly_hours` is decreased by each formerly active assignment's `weekly_hours` down to a floor of 0, and because the tutoring credit of every affected student just fell, the study-hall requirement re-runs for each of those students and each one's `support_status` is re-derived. Reinstatement moves the tutor `SUSPENDED → TRAINED` and is permitted only once every incident naming that tutor with status `SUBSTANTIATED` has been moved to `REMEDIATED` — a reinstatement blocked by an unremediated substantiated finding, whatever that finding's severity, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; it does **not** restore capacity — `assigned_weekly_hours` stays at its post-termination value and new assignments must be created to rebuild the load — and it takes no `term_id`, leaving `training_completed_term_index` and `training_valid_through_term_index` exactly where they stood, so a tutor reinstated after its validity span has elapsed may hold assignments again and still fail a session log in the current term. From `SUSPENDED` the tutor may instead be moved to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal: the tutor accepts no further transitions and may not be named on any new assignment.

An assignment is proposed by naming a `tutor_id`, an `enrollment_id` and `weekly_hours` greater than 0 and at most 10. It is created `PROPOSED` with `suspension_count` of 0, `logged_hours` of 0 and a null `terminated_reason`, and it reserves no capacity at all until it is activated. Five conditions guard creation and, with one exception, guard activation and reinstatement equally: the tutor must not be `UNTRAINED`, or the request fails with `ERR_TUTOR_NOT_TRAINED`; the tutor must not be `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, or it fails with `ERR_TUTOR_TRAINING_EXPIRED`; the tutor's `subject_codes` must contain the `subject_code` of the course behind the assignment's enrollment, or it fails with `ERR_TUTOR_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` — this one guards creation only; the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` plus the new `weekly_hours` must not exceed the tutor's `max_weekly_hours`, or it fails with `ERR_TUTOR_WEEKLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`; and the sum of `weekly_hours` over the student's own `ACTIVE` assignments plus the new hours must not exceed the student's tier ceiling, or it fails with `ERR_TIER_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That tier ceiling is 2.0 hours for `TIER_1`, 4.0 for `TIER_2`, 8.0 for `TIER_3` and 10.0 for `TIER_4` — the least-at-risk students are deliberately allowed the least tutoring, because tutoring hours are a scarce compliance-sensitive resource.

Activating an assignment takes it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and additionally requires the tutor to be `TRAINED` and the referenced enrollment to still be `ENROLLED`. On activation the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` increases by the assignment's `weekly_hours`, the study-hall requirement re-runs for the student so that `required_study_hall_hours` reflects the new tutoring credit, `support_status` is re-derived, and the student's `version` increments by one. Suspending an assignment takes it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, increments `suspension_count` by one, decreases the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` by the assignment's `weekly_hours`, and re-runs the study-hall requirement and the support re-derivation for the student. Reinstating takes it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, requires the tutor to be `TRAINED` and the capacity ceiling to still admit the hours, and re-reserves the capacity with the same downstream recomputation. This closes the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle, which may repeat freely; `suspension_count` is monotonic and is never decremented by a reinstatement.

Two terminal outcomes exist. Termination is available from `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and writes `terminated_reason`, which accompanies the action as a required field, accepts any one of the five declared reasons — `TUTOR_SUSPENDED`, `SPECIALIST_REQUEST`, `TERM_CLOSED`, `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED` and `ENROLLMENT_DROPPED` — and is stored verbatim without being checked against the circumstances: the specialist's explicit action conventionally supplies `SPECIALIST_REQUEST`, while the cascades supply `TUTOR_SUSPENDED` when the tutor was suspended, `ENROLLMENT_DROPPED` when the enrollment was dropped or withdrawn, and `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED` when a substantiated finding forced it. Completion is available from `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`; the term-close cascade that produces it writes `terminated_reason` of `TERM_CLOSED`, whereas an explicit `COMPLETE` takes no reason at all — the field is rejected if supplied with that action — and leaves `terminated_reason` null. In both cases the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` decreases by the assignment's `weekly_hours` only when the previous status was `ACTIVE` — a `PROPOSED` assignment never held capacity, so terminating one leaves `assigned_weekly_hours` untouched — and after the release the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` again equals the sum of `weekly_hours` over that tutor's remaining `ACTIVE` assignments. Both terminal moves re-run the study-hall requirement for the assignment's student, re-derive `support_status`, and advance the student's `version` by one. Every one of the five assignment transitions in fact ends the same way — the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` is rewritten as the sum of `weekly_hours` over its remaining `ACTIVE` assignments, and the student's study-hall requirement and `support_status` are re-derived from the unchanged `risk_tier`, the risk score itself never being recomputed here — and each of the five advances the assignment's, the tutor's and the student's `version` by one even where the recomputed figures come out identical, as they do when a merely `PROPOSED` assignment is completed or terminated.

Support sessions are the immutable record of what actually happened in the study centre. A `LearningSpecialist` or a `Tutor` logs one by naming the `student_athlete_id`, a `session_type` of either `STUDY_HALL` for monitored independent work or `TUTOR_SESSION` for supervised tutoring, and the interval bounds `start_at` (inclusive) and `end_at` (exclusive). `tutor_assignment_id` is required for a `TUTOR_SESSION` and must be omitted for a `STUDY_HALL`, which is by definition unsupervised. On logging, `hours_credited` is the length of the interval in minutes divided by 60, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `term_index_of_record` is the ordinal of the unique term whose `[start_date, end_date)` window contains `start_at`; a session whose start falls in no term at all is rejected. `logged_by_user_id` records the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, and where the acting role is `Tutor` that identifier must equal the `id` of the `Tutor` row behind the named assignment — a tutor's own resource identifier is its operator identifier for this comparison — failing which the log is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TUTOR`. That ownership check is made only once both body references have resolved, so a `Tutor` naming an assignment that does not exist reports `ERR_TUTOR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` rather than the 403, and a `Tutor` logging a `STUDY_HALL` session names no assignment and is never blocked by the check at all. When the session names an assignment, that assignment's `logged_hours` increases by `hours_credited` and the assignment's `version` advances; `logged_hours` is the running sum of credited hours across the assignment's sessions, held at two decimal places. Nothing else is recomputed by a log: the risk score, the study-hall requirement and `support_status` are all left alone — the tutoring credit of §3.6 is driven by contracted `weekly_hours` and never by hours actually logged — so no student, tutor or term row is touched and no other row's `version` moves.

Four rules bound a session. It must lie inside its term of record — `start_at` at or after the term's `start_date` and `end_at` at or before the term's `end_date`, the closing instant itself being accepted here even though the term's own window is half-open elsewhere — failing which the log is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_TERM`; the same error answers a `start_at` that falls inside no term's `[start_date, end_date)` window at all, which is why a session starting exactly at some term's `end_date` belongs to no term rather than to that one. No two sessions of the same student may overlap, whatever their `session_type`: the windows are half-open, so a session beginning exactly when another ends is accepted while a single minute of true overlap is rejected with `ERR_STUDENT_SESSION_OVERLAP`. Two sessions whose assignments belong to the same tutor must be separated by at least 20 minutes of clear time between the earlier session's `end_at` and the later session's `start_at`, and that applies across students rather than only within one; a gap of exactly 20 minutes is accepted, a gap of 10 minutes is rejected with `ERR_TUTOR_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, and two same-tutor sessions that actually overlap are rejected with that same code. A `STUDY_HALL` session names no assignment and so is bound by the overlap rule but never by the buffer. And a `TUTOR_SESSION` may not be logged for a student whose `support_status` is `SUPPORT_WITHHELD`, nor against an assignment whose tutor holds a `SUBSTANTIATED` top-severity incident involving that same student; either case is rejected with `ERR_IMPERMISSIBLE_ASSISTANCE_BLOCK`.

### 3.8. Competition Travel and Missed-Class Accounting

`AthleticsOperations` schedules a competition event by naming the travelling `sport_code`, the `term_id` containing the travel window, an `event_type`, and the window bounds `departure_at` (inclusive) and `return_at` (exclusive). The named term is read for its own dates when missed meetings are later enumerated, but the travel window is never checked to fall inside those dates: an event whose departure or return lies outside its term is scheduled without complaint, and simply generates no missed meeting for the part of the window that falls outside. The `event_type` decides how expensive the trip is academically, through a derived `missed_class_unit_multiplier`: `REGULAR_SEASON` and `EXHIBITION` both carry a multiplier of 1.0, `CONFERENCE_CHAMPIONSHIP` carries 0.5 because half the missed time is forgiven, and `POSTSEASON` carries 0.0 — postseason travel is exempt and consumes no budget at all. The event is created `SCHEDULED` with `charged_units_total` of 0 and that derived multiplier, which is stored at one decimal place and so renders `1.0`, `0.5` or `0.0`.

Approving travel takes the event `SCHEDULED → TRAVEL_APPROVED` and requires the event's term to still be open — a closed term is `ERR_APR_TERM_CLOSED`, reported only once the `SCHEDULED` precondition itself holds, as §2.7 records; only from `TRAVEL_APPROVED` may missed-class requests be filed. Departure takes it `TRAVEL_APPROVED → IN_TRAVEL` and requires the logical clock to have reached `departure_at`, an early departure being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; once the event leaves `TRAVEL_APPROVED`, no new missed-class request may be created against it. Completion takes it `IN_TRAVEL → COMPLETED` and requires the logical clock to have reached `return_at` on the same code; charged units stay charged and the event accepts no further transitions. Departure, completion and cancellation consult the event's term not at all, so all three remain available after that term has closed. Cancellation is available from `SCHEDULED` and from `TRAVEL_APPROVED` and lands on `CANCELLED`. Cancelling a merely `SCHEDULED` event releases nothing, because no request can exist yet. Cancelling a `TRAVEL_APPROVED` event releases everything: each missed-class request of that event in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `VOIDED` in `created_at` ascending order; each affected student's `missed_class_units_charged` decreases by that request's `missed_units` while `missed_class_units_remaining` increases by the same amount; the event's `charged_units_total` becomes 0 and the event's own `version` advances exactly once for the whole cancellation, whatever the released units came to; the risk score re-runs for each affected student and rewrites `risk_score` and `risk_tier`; the study-hall requirement re-runs and rewrites `required_study_hall_hours`; `support_status` is re-derived; and each affected student's `version` increments by one. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for an event.

An `AcademicAdvisor` opens a missed-class request by naming the `competition_event_id` and the `enrollment_id` whose meetings will be missed. The event must be `TRAVEL_APPROVED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_TRAVEL_APPROVED`. The enrollment must belong to the same student as every other request already on the event's roster — and that roster is every request ever filed against the event whatever its status, so a `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` row still binds the event to the student it named — and its course's `instructor_user_id` must be non-empty so that notification has a recipient, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_NOTIFIABLE`, the roster test being made first. Nothing else about the enrollment is examined: neither its own `term_id` nor its status is consulted, because the travel window, the dates that bound the meeting enumeration and the term whose freeze band 8 checks all come from the event, so a request may be opened against an enrollment of another term or one already dropped or withdrawn. The request is created in `DRAFT` with `dispute_count` of 0 and with `instructor_user_id`, `notified_at`, `acknowledgement_deadline_at` and `instructor_response_note` all null.

Creation computes the charge from the course's meeting pattern and the travel window, in a fixed order. First, enumerate every occurrence of the course's meetings between `departure_at` and `return_at`: an occurrence exists on each date whose ISO weekday appears in the course's `meeting_days`, beginning at `meeting_start_minute_utc` minutes after midnight UTC and running for `meeting_duration_minutes`, and only occurrences whose whole meeting interval lies inside the term's window are considered at all — a containment test that accepts the closing instant, so a meeting ending exactly at the term's `end_date` still counts even though that window is half-open elsewhere. A weekday listed more than once in `meeting_days` yields one occurrence per calendar date and not several. An occurrence counts as missed when its meeting interval overlaps the travel window by a strictly positive duration; because both windows are half-open, a meeting that begins exactly at `return_at` does not count, and neither does one that ends exactly at `departure_at`. Second, `missed_meeting_count` is the number of counted occurrences. Third, each counted occurrence yields its own unit figure: take the length in whole minutes of the intersection between the meeting interval and the travel window, divide by 60 minutes — one missed-class unit is one hour of overlap — and round half up to two decimal places. Fourth, `missed_units_gross` is the sum of those already-rounded per-occurrence figures; the rounding is deliberately applied per occurrence and only then summed, and summing raw overlap minutes first and rounding once at the end is **not** the specified order and will produce a different answer. Fifth, `missed_units` is `missed_units_gross` multiplied by the event's `missed_class_unit_multiplier`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and that is the amount charged.

The charge posts immediately: the student's `missed_class_units_charged` increases by `missed_units`, `missed_class_units_remaining` becomes the `missed_class_units_allowance` minus the new charged total, and the event's `charged_units_total` increases by `missed_units` while the event's `version` advances by one, a charge of 0.00 units included. Because the charged figure changed, the risk score and the study-hall requirement re-run and `support_status` is re-derived, and the student's `version` increments by one. The charge may not overdraw the budget: when the computed `missed_units` exceeds the student's `missed_class_units_remaining`, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSED_CLASS_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. That budget check is the last check the request faces, so a request that is defective for any other reason reports the other reason.

Notification is the advisor's next step and moves the request `DRAFT → PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`. The division of labour described in this section and the two below it is procedural rather than enforced, exactly as it is for certifications: the request status endpoint authorises `AcademicAdvisor` and `Instructor` alike and narrows none of its four actions by role, so an instructor may notify or cancel and an advisor may acknowledge or dispute — and because the ownership check of §2.1 fires only when the acting role is `Instructor`, an advisor acknowledging or disputing is never measured against the recorded `instructor_user_id` at all. Notification requires the event to still be `TRAVEL_APPROVED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_TRAVEL_APPROVED`, and it requires lead time — a requirement that binds this first notification out of `DRAFT` and no other: notification must occur at least 7 days before the event's `departure_at`, with the boundary inclusive, so notification exactly 7 days ahead is accepted and notification 6 days and 23 hours ahead is rejected with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION`. Notification writes `notified_at` from the logical clock, sets `acknowledgement_deadline_at` to 72 hours — three days — after `notified_at`, and copies `instructor_user_id` from the enrolled course.

The instructor named on the request, and no other instructor, may respond. Acknowledging moves it `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT → ACKNOWLEDGED` and stores the supplied `instructor_response_note`; disputing moves it `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT → DISPUTED`, increments `dispute_count` by one and likewise stores `instructor_response_note`. That note is optional on both of those actions and is written from the request exactly as it arrives, so an omitted note leaves the field null and clears whatever an earlier response had put there; supplied instead with `NOTIFY_INSTRUCTOR` or with `CANCEL` it is a malformed body and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both require the logical clock to be at or before `acknowledgement_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself is accepted, anything later is rejected with `ERR_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` — and both leave the charged units charged, because an instructor's disagreement about the notification does not refund class time the athlete will still miss. After a dispute the advisor may re-notify, moving the request `DISPUTED → PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT` and rewriting `notified_at` and `acknowledgement_deadline_at` from the current clock; this closes the `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT → DISPUTED → PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT` cycle. Re-notification carries no lead-time requirement of its own — the 7-day rule is never applied a second time, however close to departure the dispute arrives — but it does require the event to still be `TRAVEL_APPROVED`, which is checked first, and it is bounded at two disputes: a re-notification requested while `dispute_count` already stands at 2 is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, so two disputes may be answered and a third can never be reached.

Cancellation by the advisor is available from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `DISPUTED` alike, moves the request to `CANCELLED`, and requires the parent event to be `SCHEDULED` or `TRAVEL_APPROVED`, failing which the cancel is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — so a request whose event has departed can no longer be cancelled and its units stay charged. All four cancellation edges run the identical release chain: the student's `missed_class_units_charged` decreases by the request's `missed_units` and `missed_class_units_remaining` increases by the same amount, restoring the identity that allowance equals charged plus remaining exactly; the event's `charged_units_total` decreases by the same `missed_units` and the event's `version` advances by one; the risk score and the study-hall requirement re-run and `support_status` is re-derived; and the student's `version` increments by one. `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are the request's two terminal states, and the void produced by an event cancellation runs precisely the same release chain as an explicit cancellation.

### 3.9. Impermissible Assistance, Tutor Discipline and the Term Close

Anyone close to the academic-support operation — a `ComplianceOfficer`, a `LearningSpecialist` or an `AcademicAdvisor` — may report a suspected case of impermissible academic assistance. The report names the `student_athlete_id` who received the assistance, an `incident_type` drawn from `IMPERMISSIBLE_WORK_COMPLETION`, `UNAUTHORIZED_EXAM_ASSISTANCE`, `UNTRAINED_TUTOR_SESSION` and `EXCESSIVE_TUTOR_HOURS`, and a `narrative` describing the conduct. Two references are optional: `tutor_id` names the implicated tutor and is null where no tutor is involved, and `discovered_session_id` names the logged session in which the conduct was observed and is null where the report is not session-based. Each of those two is honoured only when it arrives as a string: a value of any other JSON type is neither resolved against the store nor rejected as malformed, but simply ignored, and the incident is created with that reference null — a deliberate narrowing of §2.6's type ladder that applies to these two optional references and to nothing else in this specification. The incident is created `REPORTED` with `penalty_points_assessed` of 0, a null `severity`, and `reported_by_user_id` set from the reporter's `X-User-ID`.

Investigation belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` alone. Opening an investigation moves the incident `REPORTED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and changes nothing beyond the status and `updated_at`. From `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` there are exactly two exits. Finding the report unfounded moves it `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, which is terminal, leaves `penalty_points_assessed` at 0, leaves `severity` null, and propagates no penalty to either the student or the tutor — no row but the incident's own is written or advanced. Substantiating it moves the incident `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → SUBSTANTIATED` and requires a `severity` of `LEVEL_I`, `LEVEL_II` or `LEVEL_III`, which converts directly into points: one point for `LEVEL_I`, two for `LEVEL_II`, three for `LEVEL_III`, written to `penalty_points_assessed`.

Substantiation is the widest cascade in this specification, and it fires in order. The assessed points are added to the student's `substantiated_penalty_points`, and where the incident names a tutor, the same points are added to that tutor's `penalty_points`. If the tutor's `penalty_points` has thereby reached three, the tutor moves `TRAINED → SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED → SUSPENDED`; a tutor already `UNTRAINED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` accumulates the points with no status change at all. Where the tutor did become suspended, each of that tutor's assignments in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_reason` of `TUTOR_SUSPENDED` in `created_at` ascending order, and the tutor's `assigned_weekly_hours` drops by each formerly active assignment's `weekly_hours`. Because those assignments left `ACTIVE`, the study-hall requirement re-runs for every affected student — the tutoring credit fell, so `required_study_hall_hours` rises — and each affected student's `support_status` is re-derived. Where the severity is `LEVEL_III`, the reported student's `support_status` becomes `SUPPORT_WITHHELD` and the student's governing certification moves `CERTIFIED → REVOKED` or `RESTORED → REVOKED`, which re-derives `eligibility_state` to `INELIGIBLE`; that revocation is not gated on the certification's term, so a term already `CLOSED` does not stop it. Finally the risk score re-runs for the student and the student's `version` increments by one — except that a reported student who also held a `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE` assignment with the tutor this same request suspended is touched by both legs of the cascade, and that student's `version` therefore advances twice on the one request.

Remediation moves a substantiated incident `SUBSTANTIATED → REMEDIATED`, which is terminal. Points are never rolled back: `penalty_points_assessed`, the student's `substantiated_penalty_points` and the tutor's `penalty_points` all stay exactly where they were, and remediated incidents continue to feed the risk score. What remediation does unlock is the support dimension — once every top-severity incident for the student is `REMEDIATED`, `support_status` is re-derived out of `SUPPORT_WITHHELD` as described in §3.6 — and tutor reinstatement, which requires every substantiated incident naming that tutor to have been remediated. Mechanically, remediation re-runs the study-hall requirement for the reported student and re-derives that student's `support_status`, advancing the student's `version` by one; it recomputes neither the risk score nor the tutor's penalty figure, and it leaves the tutor row and every assignment untouched.

A tutor's `penalty_points` is not a plain running sum forever; it decays — but the decay is computed and written at one moment only, the academic-progress-rate close, and never when an incident is substantiated. The included incidents are those naming the tutor with status `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REMEDIATED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; incidents that are `REPORTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` contribute nothing. An incident's own term is the `term_index_of_record` of the support session named in its `discovered_session_id` — the incident carries no term of its own — so an incident that names no session, or names one holding no term of record, never decays at all whatever its age. Where that term of record lies more than two below the `term_index` of the newest `CLOSED` reporting term, the incident contributes one point less than its `penalty_points_assessed`, floored at zero; the reduction is a single point however many terms have elapsed since, and the span is measured on `term_index` alone, counting the intervening terms whether or not they were reporting terms. `penalty_points` is that decayed sum, always a whole number, and only the close writes it: between closes the field is the undecayed running total that each substantiation added its points to. The suspension threshold reads that running total at the moment of substantiation — at three or more points a `TRAINED` or `EXPIRED` tutor is suspended, while at exactly two points the tutor keeps its current status — and the close's recomputation, which can only lower the figure, suspends nobody and unsuspends nobody. Reinstatement touches neither the curve nor the total: a suspended tutor reinstated as §3.7 describes returns to `TRAINED` with `penalty_points` unchanged and with `training_completed_term_index` and `training_valid_through_term_index` exactly as the suspension left them, and `COMPLETE_TRAINING` is not a legal move out of `SUSPENDED`, so no single action both clears a suspension and refreshes the training ordinals.

The academic-progress-rate close is the system's checkpoint operation, run by a `ComplianceOfficer` once per reporting term, and it is where a term stops being editable. Four conditions guard it: the term must still be `OPEN`, it must carry `is_apr_reporting_term` true, the logical clock must have reached the term's `end_date` — earlier is `ERR_APR_TERM_NOT_ENDED` — and every reporting term with a lower `term_index` must already be `CLOSED`, failing which the close is refused with `ERR_APR_PRIOR_TERM_OPEN`. Closing a term that is already `CLOSED`, or one that is not a reporting term, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The cohort for the close is every student whose `entry_term_index` is at or below the term's `term_index` and who holds at least one enrollment in that term with status `ENROLLED`, `COMPLETED` or `INCOMPLETE`, processed in the order `entry_term_index` ascending then `created_at` ascending. A student with no enrollment in the term, or whose only enrollments in it were dropped or withdrawn, is excluded from both the numerator and the denominator — not counted as a forfeit, simply absent.

Each cohort student contributes exactly 2 possible points, one for eligibility and one for retention. The eligibility point is earned when the student holds a certification for this term whose status is `CERTIFIED` or `RESTORED`, and is forfeited otherwise. The retention point depends on a **variant** determined by the next term in `term_index` order — the term of lowest `term_index` above this one, whatever its `is_apr_reporting_term` flag says, so a non-reporting summer term standing next in sequence is the term the variant reads. Under the `PROJECTED` variant, which applies when that next term exists and is still `OPEN`, the retention point is awarded on intent: at least one enrollment with status `ENROLLED` in the next term suffices. Under the `OBSERVED` variant, which applies when the next term is already `CLOSED`, the retention point requires realised attendance: at least one enrollment with status `COMPLETED` in that next term, so a student who enrolled and then dropped everything forfeits the point the projected variant would have granted. Where no next term row exists at all, the retention point is awarded by default.

Each student's `apr_points_earned` then increases by the sum of the two point tests, `apr_points_possible` increases by 2, and `apr_points_forfeited` is set so that `apr_points_possible` equals `apr_points_earned` plus `apr_points_forfeited` exactly after the close. The term's own `apr_points_possible` and `apr_points_earned` are the cohort sums, and `squad_apr` is the term's earned points divided by its possible points, multiplied by 1000 and rounded half up to a whole number; where the term's `apr_points_possible` is 0, `squad_apr` is null rather than 0. Every cohort student's `support_status` is then re-derived, in the cohort order, because `apr_points_forfeited` has moved — by exactly the pure function of §3.6 and by nothing else, so a student who earned neither of the two points has thereby forfeited at least two and, wherever that function admits a move at all, escalates to `SUPPORT_PROBATION` rather than to `SUPPORT_MANDATED`, reaching two forfeited points being what drives the escalation. Notwithstanding §3.6, this is the one support re-derivation that rewrites no `required_study_hall_hours`: the close moves the support dimension alone. Each cohort student's `version` advances by one for the points and that re-derivation together, and again should the training-expiry cascade below reach one of that student's assignments.

The close finishes by expiring lapsed tutor training and its assignments as §3.7 describes, then moving the term `OPEN → CLOSED`, and only after that recomputing the decayed penalty figure for every tutor in the system rather than for a selected few. The order is load-bearing: because the term is already `CLOSED` when the decay is measured, the term just closed is itself the newest closed reporting term the decay reads. A tutor whose recomputed figure equals the figure already stored is not written to and its `version` does not move; a tutor whose figure falls has the new value stored and its `version` advanced by one. From that instant every certification and appeal bound to the term is frozen and every later write against it is refused with `ERR_APR_TERM_CLOSED`.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every error identifier the API can return, together with the HTTP status code it is served with. The conditions that raise each identifier, and the order in which competing conditions are evaluated, are specified in §2.7 and in the workflow narratives of §3.
