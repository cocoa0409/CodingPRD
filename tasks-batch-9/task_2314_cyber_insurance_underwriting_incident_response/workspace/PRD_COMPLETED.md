# Cyber Insurance Underwriting & Incident Response API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Cyber insurance is unusual among lines of business in that the insurer sells not only indemnity but also a response capability. A single ransomware event obliges the carrier to pay a business interruption loss, to reimburse a forensics firm, to fund breach notification for hundreds of thousands of records, and to defend third-party claims — all at once, all against a tower of sublimits whose remaining capacity nobody can see without an authoritative ledger. Today those four obligations live in four disconnected systems: an underwriting workbench that scores security controls, a policy administration system that holds limits, a spreadsheet of pre-approved panel vendor rates, and a claims file. The result is predictable. Coverage gets confirmed against a policy whose warranty controls have lapsed; a forensics firm bills at a rate nobody negotiated; the aggregate limit is exceeded because two adjusters were reading stale copies of the same tower.

This document specifies a single API, mounted under `/cyber`, that closes the loop from application to renewal. It carries the underwriting side — a mandatory control catalog, applicant attestations, external attack-surface scanning that verifies or contradicts those attestations, a weighted posture score, a tiered premium and a bindable quote — and the response side — incident notification, coverage confirmation against named tower sections, panel vendor selection under concurrency and sector constraints, hour logging against authorized caps, business interruption loss quantification, and a strict waterfall that erodes sublimits and the policy aggregate in a pinned order. Above both sits the portfolio view: a systemic aggregation model that asks what a shared cloud-provider outage would cost the whole book, and a renewal engine that reprices from the realised loss ratio, the aggregation load, and whatever controls the insured actually remediated.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is that money and capacity are conserved: every dollar of loss is either absorbed by a retention, allocated to a named section, deducted as coinsurance, or explicitly recorded as unallocated — nothing evaporates, and every reversal restores exactly what it consumed. The second is that duties are segregated: the underwriter who prices a risk may not be the one who binds it, and the adjuster who hires a forensics firm may not be the one who accepts the proof of loss that pays that firm. Both commitments are enforced by the server, not by convention.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, the logical clock, numeric precision, stale-read protection, cascade discipline, path resolution, and the fixed order in which validations fire.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it acts. Both must be present and non-empty; a missing or blank header is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the request body is parsed at all. The single exception is the logical-clock endpoint used to drive tests, which accepts requests without either header and never validates `X-User-Role` at all, so any role value — including one outside the five named below — is accepted there.

Five roles exist, and their spelling is exact: `Underwriter`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `PortfolioActuary`, `PanelAdmin`, and `Insured`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of these five, or that is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see §4), is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This check runs before any identifier in the URL is resolved, so an unauthorised role calling an endpoint with a path id that does not exist still receives 403, never 404.

The four operator roles carry opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `PortfolioActuary`, or `PanelAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded in two places where segregation of duties depends on it: the `Underwriter` who issues a live quote has its `X-User-ID` written to the application's `quoted_by_user_id`, and the `ClaimsAdjuster` who authorises a panel deployment has its `X-User-ID` written to the engagement's `authorized_by_user_id`.

The fifth role is account-bound. When `X-User-Role` is `Insured`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting insured account; the value returned when an insured account is registered is precisely what that policyholder then presents as `X-User-ID`. Ownership checks compare the resource's owning account to `X-User-ID`: directly through `insured_account_id` on an application or a policy, and transitively for coverage sections, incident claims, vendor engagements, proofs of loss, claim payments, and renewal assessments, each of which reaches an insured account through its parent chain. A read or write by an `Insured` against a row belonging to another account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Ownership is evaluated **after** existence, so a foreign row that does exist yields 403, not 404.

Segregation of duties is a separate, narrower rule and is described in §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with 20 characters drawn from upper-case letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Alone among the timestamps this API emits it is read from real time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it does not move when a caller sets the clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` skips a number of rows from the front of the result set and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or that falls outside those bounds, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; so is a filter value that falls outside the enumerated domain the filter draws on.

Unless a specific endpoint declares its own ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several endpoints do declare their own ordering — the control catalog is returned by `weight` descending then `control_key` ascending, the panel register by `tier` ascending then `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd` ascending then `created_at` ascending, a policy's tower by `erosion_priority` ascending, and an application's attestations by `control_key` ascending — and where such an ordering is declared it replaces the default. Because the logical clock does not advance on its own, rows routinely share a `created_at`; every ordering in this API, the default and the declared ones alike, breaks such ties on the order in which the rows were created, so equal keys never reorder between calls.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope. An `Insured` caller's list is always force-filtered to its own account, and an `Insured` that supplies an `insured_account_id` filter naming a different account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` rather than being silently narrowed.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields, and the Immutable Catalog

Each persisted row is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Almost every entity in this system is mutable and therefore also carries two further system-managed fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every change to the row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation; a row that one request mutates more than once — a claim that moves twice inside a single cascade, say — still advances exactly one version for that request. This applies to insured accounts, applications, control attestations, external scans, policies, coverage sections, panel vendors, incident claims, vendor engagements, proofs of loss, claim payments, renewal assessments, and systemic event scenarios. A mutation includes a change to a derived field: when a cascade rewrites a computed score, a remaining sublimit, or a counter on some other row, that other row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented.

The one exception is the underwriting control catalog. A catalog entry is immutable once created — there is no endpoint that amends it — and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a control requirement row entirely.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Epoch Processing

The server does not read wall-clock time. Every deadline, policy period boundary, waiting period, notification window, quote expiry, renewal window, and modelling instant is evaluated against a logical clock. Before any call is made the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it changes only when a caller sets it explicitly by supplying `current_utc`; the clock endpoint echoes back the instant it now holds as a `current_utc` field carrying the caller's own string verbatim rather than a renormalised rendering of it. It never falls back to real time.

A companion administrative capability advances the system epoch at whatever instant the clock currently holds and performs three sweeps in one request: quoted applications whose quote has expired, in-force policies whose period has matured, and renewal assessments whose remediation deadline has passed. The three sweeps run in exactly that order, and an assessment that carries no `remediation_due_at` at all is never swept. Its response reports `expired_application_count`, `expired_policy_count`, `non_renewed_assessment_count`, and `run_at`. Every row the sweep touches has its `version` incremented by 1.

### 2.6. Monetary Values, Ratios, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are computed with decimal arithmetic and stored rounded to exactly 2 decimal places using the round-half-up method. Monetary values are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, never as strings, with at most 2 decimal places. A monetary field supplied in a request with more than 2 decimal places, or with a negative value where the field is declared non-negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two derived ratios are held at finer precision: the policy loss ratio and the renewal premium adjustment percentage are each rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The control posture score is a percentage figure rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Rounding order is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Where a calculation in §3 pins the order in which intermediates are rounded — components rounded to cents before they are combined, a percentage cap computed against an already-rounded figure, a floor applied after rounding rather than before — that order must be reproduced exactly. Summing raw intermediates and rounding once at the end produces a different answer and is wrong.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Quotes, Bindings, Proofs, and Renewals

Four write operations sit on top of records that other writers legitimately advance, and in each case no state precondition can catch a caller working from an out-of-date read. Each of these four requires the client to declare the `version` it last observed on the governing parent record. The field is **not optional** — it must be present in the request body, and it must be an integer, a missing or non-integer field being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than one of the 412s below — and its value must equal that parent's current `version` at the moment the request is served. If it does not, the write is refused and nothing is mutated.

- `POST /cyber/applications/{application_id}/quote` requires `expected_application_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Application`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-quoting is a legitimate supersede — it overwrites `premium_usd`, `quoted_by_user_id`, and `quote_expires_at` — so the operation cannot be refused on state grounds, and this check is the only guard that stops an `Underwriter` from overwriting a quote from a view of the application that no longer holds.
- `POST /cyber/applications/{application_id}/bind` requires `expected_application_version` against the same `Application`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A quoted application remains bindable after a re-quote has replaced the premium, so no state precondition catches a binder still holding the earlier quote; this check is what prevents a policy being issued at a premium the application no longer carries.
- `POST /cyber/incident-claims/{claim_id}/proofs-of-loss` requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `IncidentClaim`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An amended proof legitimately supersedes the proof already under review, so state preconditions permit the write; this check is what stops an insured filing an amendment built on a claim view that a coverage change or an engagement change has since invalidated.
- `POST /cyber/renewal-assessments/{assessment_id}/issue-renewal-quote` requires `expected_policy_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Policy` under review. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every posted or reversed payment and every systemic model run that flags the policy advances the policy's `version` and moves the loss ratio the renewal premium is built from, while the assessment itself sits still in `CONDITIONS_MET` — so no state check can detect the stale read.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in the four bullets above must not require one and must not reject a request for the absence of one.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Many writes in this system touch more than one row. A completed scan rewrites attestations, then rewrites the application, then rewrites the insured account's open-remediation counter. An accepted proof of loss writes the proof, moves the claim twice, creates payment rows, erodes sections and the policy aggregate, and can terminate vendor engagements and decrement panel counters. Cancelling a policy closes claims, terminates their engagements, and decrements panel counters.

Three principles govern all of it. First, cascades run to completion inside the same request: consistency is immediate, and a caller never observes a half-propagated state. Second, every row a cascade mutates — not only the row the caller addressed — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Third, cascades are conditional where §3 says they are conditional: a hop that is described as applying only to children in certain states must skip children in every other state rather than sweeping the whole set. The individual cascades, hop by hop, are specified in §3.

### 2.9. Path Resolution Order

When a URL contains an identifier, that identifier must resolve to an existing row, and when it contains more than one, resolution proceeds strictly left to right — the outer segment first. The request fails on the *first* identifier that does not resolve, with the 404 error belonging to that identifier's type: `insured_account_id` yields `ERR_INSURED_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `application_id` yields `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `scan_id` yields `ERR_EXTERNAL_SCAN_NOT_FOUND`, `policy_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `section_id` yields `ERR_COVERAGE_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `claim_id` yields `ERR_INCIDENT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `engagement_id` yields `ERR_VENDOR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `proof_id` yields `ERR_PROOF_OF_LOSS_NOT_FOUND`, `payment_id` yields `ERR_CLAIM_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `assessment_id` yields `ERR_RENEWAL_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `scenario_id` yields `ERR_SYSTEMIC_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`, and `vendor_id` yields `ERR_PANEL_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`. A single control attestation is also directly addressable by an `attestation_id`; because the catalogue in §5 declares no attestation-specific identifier, an `attestation_id` that resolves to no row reports `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`.

The same discipline applies to identifiers supplied in a request body rather than the path. An `insured_account_id` naming no account, an `application_id` naming no application, a `policy_id` naming no policy, a `systemic_event_scenario_id` naming no scenario, and a directed `panel_vendor_id` naming no panel firm each yield the corresponding 404. A `covered_section_keys` entry naming a `section_key` that the claim's policy tower does not contain yields `ERR_COVERAGE_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several checks could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated determines which error the client sees, and that order is fixed. Checks run in this sequence, and the first one to fail terminates the request:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty on every endpoint other than the logical-clock setter; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), raised before the body is parsed.
2. **Role authorisation** — `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and is permitted for this endpoint; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Raised before any path identifier is resolved.
3. **Path existence** — every identifier in the URL resolves, left to right, as described in §2.9; otherwise the matching 404.
4. **Account scope** — an `Insured` caller may only touch rows owned by its own account; otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Deliberately after existence, so a foreign row that exists is 403.
5. **Segregation of duties** — the two rules in §2.11; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
6. **Body existence** — identifiers and keys supplied in the request body resolve, as described in §2.9; otherwise the matching 404.
7. **Schema** — an absent or empty request body is read as an empty object, so the fields it lacks are reported here rather than as a distinct missing-body failure, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; required fields present, enum values inside their declared domains, non-negative numerics, monetary fields at most 2 decimals, ratio fields inside range (`coinsurance_pct` at most 0.50, `gross_profit_margin_pct` at most 1.00, `footprint_pct` at most 1.00), timestamps in ISO 8601 UTC — a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric offset, and no zone marker at all are all accepted, an unzoned value being read as UTC, and the value a row keeps is the caller's own spelling rather than a renormalised rendering — string-array fields free of duplicate entries, interval fields correctly ordered (`period_start` before `period_end`, `outage_start_at` before `outage_end_at`, `deployment_start_at` before `deployment_end_at`), `waiting_period_hours` greater than 0 and required for a `BUSINESS_INTERRUPTION` or `DEPENDENT_BI` section and absent — null — for every other section key, and `requested_aggregate_limit_usd` at least the 250,000 minimum; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
8. **Stale-read protection** — the declared version field carries the parent's current `version`, per §2.7; otherwise the matching 412.
9. **Own state** — the addressed row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared in §3; terminal states admit no transition at all. Otherwise the matching 409: `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SCAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PROOF_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`, or `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`.
10. **Cross-entity state** — states of *other* rows gate the write (409): notifying an incident requires its policy in `INFORCE` with `limit_status` other than `EXHAUSTED`; authorising a vendor engagement requires the claim in `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` or `RESPONSE_ACTIVE` and, when a firm is directed by id, that firm's `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_ACTIVE`; submitting a proof of loss requires the claim in `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `PROOF_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`, so a claim that never needed a panel vendor can still file; opening a renewal assessment requires the policy in `INFORCE`.
11. **Control-based coverage gates** (422), themselves internally ordered: first the latest-scan-completed check, then the recomputed mandatory gap, then the minimum verified score, then the warranty lapse, then the mandatory attestation coverage. The five errors are `ERR_SCAN_NOT_COMPLETED`, `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_GAP`, `ERR_CONTROL_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_LAPSE`, and `ERR_MANDATORY_ATTESTATION_MISSING`; each is described where the workflow it guards is described in §3.
12. **Clock-relative gates** (422) — `ERR_INCIDENT_OUTSIDE_POLICY_PERIOD`, `ERR_LATE_NOTIFICATION`, `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_SATISFIED`, `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, and `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`.
13. **Coverage-shape gates** (422) — `ERR_DEPENDENT_BI_NOT_COVERED` and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COVERAGE_SECTION`.
14. **Structural and overlap constraints** (422) — `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTESTATION`, `ERR_INVALID_TOWER_STRUCTURE`, `ERR_VENDOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANEL_VENDOR`.
15. **Money and limit constraints, checked last** (422) — `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_PRE_APPROVED`, `ERR_AUTHORIZED_HOURS_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_AGGREGATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LIMIT_INCREMENT`, and `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`.

The practical consequence worth internalising: a request that is both unauthorised and malformed reports the authorisation failure, and a request that would both breach a sublimit and be filed by the wrong role reports the role failure. Later checks never mask earlier ones.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two writes are refused when the acting principal has already played the conflicting part in the same file, and both carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403):

- Binding an application is refused when `X-User-ID` equals the application's `quoted_by_user_id`. The underwriter who priced the risk may not be the one who commits the carrier to it.
- Accepting a proof of loss is refused when `X-User-ID` appears as `authorized_by_user_id` on **any** vendor engagement of the same incident claim. The adjuster who hired the response firms may not be the one who signs off the indemnity that pays them.

Both checks precede any state or business validation on those two operations, per §2.10.


## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Underwriting Control Catalog

Everything the carrier believes about a risk begins with the control catalog. An `Underwriter` populates it one control at a time, naming a `control_key` drawn from a fixed vocabulary of five security controls: `MFA_PRIVILEGED`, `OFFLINE_BACKUPS`, `EDR_COVERAGE`, `EMAIL_FILTERING`, and `INCIDENT_RESPONSE_PLAN`. A `control_key` may appear in the catalog at most once; a second entry for a key already present is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Each entry declares three things about how the control is scored and enforced. `min_level_required` is the lowest maturity the carrier will accept and is one of `NONE`, `PARTIAL`, or `FULL`. `weight` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 100 expressing how much the control contributes to the posture score. `is_mandatory` is a boolean that defaults to false; when it is true, a verified maturity below `min_level_required` blocks both quoting and binding outright rather than merely depressing the score. A fourth field, `remediation_grace_days`, records how many days the insured is granted to fix this control at renewal; it defaults to 30 and must be at least 0 and at most 365.

Catalog entries are permanent. There is no amendment operation, and consequently a control requirement row exposes neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Permanence has a consequence worth stating plainly: because the posture score's denominator is the sum of *every* catalog weight, adding a control changes the denominator of every score computed from that moment on, and adding a mandatory control can open a gap on an application that had none. The catalog is readable by all five roles, returned in `weight` descending then `control_key` ascending order, and can be narrowed to mandatory entries alone with an `is_mandatory` filter.

### 3.2. From Applicant to Submitted Application

An `Underwriter` registers a prospective insured before any application exists. The applicant record carries a `legal_name` of between 1 and 200 characters, an `industry_code` from `HEALTHCARE`, `FINANCIAL`, `RETAIL`, `MANUFACTURING`, `PUBLIC_SECTOR`, or `TECHNOLOGY`, an `annual_revenue_usd` strictly greater than zero, and a `record_count` of held personal records of at least zero. An optional `primary_dependency_key` names the applicant's single largest technology dependency and is one of `CSP_ALPHA`, `CSP_BETA`, `MSSP_DELTA`, or `NONE`, defaulting to `NONE`. The account also exposes a computed `open_remediation_count`, which starts at 0 and tracks how many of this applicant's applications currently sit in remediation. The `id` returned by registration is the value that policyholder will present as `X-User-ID`. Accounts can be listed and filtered by `industry_code` or by `primary_dependency_key`, and can be read individually including the live remediation counter.

Applications are opened either by an `Underwriter` on the applicant's behalf or by the `Insured` directly, in both cases by naming the `insured_account_id` the submission belongs to. An `Insured` naming any account other than its own is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` before that identifier is resolved at all, so a foreign id that names no account still yields 403 rather than 404. Three commercial terms are fixed at that moment. `requested_aggregate_limit_usd` is the aggregate the applicant wants; it must be at least 250,000 and must be a whole multiple of 250,000. Those two rules fail differently and the distinction matters: a figure below the 250,000 floor is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas a figure at or above the floor that is not a whole multiple of the 250,000 increment — 300,000, say — is refused later in the sequence with `ERR_INVALID_LIMIT_INCREMENT`. `requested_retention_usd` is the self-insured amount the applicant will absorb per claim; it must be at least zero and no greater than `requested_aggregate_limit_usd`. `dependent_bi_definition` fixes the breadth of dependent business interruption wording sought and is one of `NAMED_ONLY`, `NAMED_PLUS_SUPPLY_CHAIN`, or `BROAD_SYSTEM_FAILURE`.

A new application opens in `DRAFT` with `verified_control_score`, `indicative_premium_usd`, `premium_usd`, `quoted_by_user_id`, `quote_expires_at`, and `declined_reason` all null, `mandatory_gap_count` at 0 and `mandatory_gap_control_keys` empty, and `attested_control_score` already run over the catalog against an empty attestation set — which makes it 0.00, not null. Over its life it moves through the underwriting states `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDERWRITING`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and `QUOTED`, and comes to rest in one of three terminal states: `BOUND`, `DECLINED`, or `EXPIRED`. Attempting any transition from a state where that transition is not declared, or any transition at all out of a terminal state, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. Applications are readable individually — exposing both control scores, both premium figures, and the mandatory-gap detail — and as a list filterable by `insured_account_id` and by `status`.

Against a draft application the applicant attests its controls, one row per control. An attestation names the `control_key` being attested, which must match a control requirement that exists in the catalog — a key absent from the catalog is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked before the `DRAFT` state gate — and the `attested_level` the applicant claims, one of `NONE`, `PARTIAL`, or `FULL`. Only one attestation may exist for a given application and control pairing; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTESTATION`. Each attestation write recomputes the application's `attested_control_score` and increments the application's `version` by 1. An attestation also carries a `verified_level`, which is computed rather than supplied and stays null until an external scan has completed. Attestations can be listed under their application in `control_key` ascending order, read individually, or swept across the whole book by underwriting and actuarial roles with `application_id` and `control_key` filters.

Submitting the application moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and there is one precondition: an attestation row must exist for every catalog control whose `is_mandatory` is true. A submission with any mandatory control unattested is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_ATTESTATION_MISSING`. On success the server recomputes `attested_control_score` on the attested basis, writes `indicative_premium_usd` from the attested-basis pricing run, and increments the application's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. External Scanning and the Verification Cascade

Attestation is a claim; verification is evidence. An `Underwriter` orders an external attack-surface scan against an application in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDERWRITING`, or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, naming the provider in `scan_vendor_name` (1 to 120 characters) and optionally recording the provider's own `scan_score`, a figure between 0 and 100 inclusive. That score is stored for the file and is deliberately never used in premium arithmetic. The scan is created in `PENDING` with `findings` an empty array, `critical_finding_count` at 0 and `completed_at` null, and ordering it moves the application nowhere at all — a scan influences underwriting only when its result is returned. Scans are listed under their application in `created_at` ascending order with a `status` filter, read individually, and swept flat across applications by underwriting and actuarial roles with `application_id` and `status` filters.

Returning the result supplies an `outcome` of either `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`, and may also restate `scan_score`, which then overwrites the figure recorded when the scan was ordered. The scan must be in `PENDING`; a second result on the same scan is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SCAN_STATE`. Both outcomes stamp `completed_at` from the logical clock and increment the scan's `version` by 1, both are terminal for the scan, and in both cases the response body is the scan row rather than the application.

A `FAILED` outcome does nothing else. Any `findings` supplied alongside it are still schema-validated — a malformed entry, an unknown `control_key`, or a repeated one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even on a failing scan — and then ignored, no attestation acquires a verified level, and the application remains in `SUBMITTED` waiting for another attempt.

A `COMPLETED` outcome carries the evidence and drives a four-hop cascade. The evidence arrives in `findings`, an array whose entries each pair a `control_key` with a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH`, or `CRITICAL` and an `observed_level` of `NONE`, `PARTIAL`, or `FULL`. Every `control_key` in the array must match a catalog control requirement, and at most one entry may appear per `control_key` — two entries for one key are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A control absent from the array altogether is treated as observed at `FULL`: silence from the scanner is not evidence against the applicant. The completion also computes `critical_finding_count`, the number of `findings` entries whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`, which starts at 0 when nothing critical was seen. The cascade then proceeds:

1. The scan itself moves from `PENDING` to `COMPLETED` with `findings`, `critical_finding_count`, and `completed_at` stored.
2. Because the scan completed, every control attestation on that application has its `verified_level` rewritten to the lower of its `attested_level` and the level the scan observed for that control, comparing on the ordering where `NONE` is lowest, `PARTIAL` is next, and `FULL` is highest. Each attestation touched has its `version` incremented by 1.
3. Because the attestation rows changed, the application's `verified_control_score`, `mandatory_gap_count`, and `mandatory_gap_control_keys` are recomputed on the verified basis, and the application moves from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDERWRITING`.
4. If the recomputed `mandatory_gap_count` is greater than zero, the application does not rest in `UNDERWRITING` at all: in the same request it continues to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and its `premium_usd` is cleared to null. Because the application entered `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, the owning insured account's `open_remediation_count` is incremented by 1 and that account's `version` is incremented by 1.

The application's `version` is incremented by 1 for the request as a whole.

Remediation is a revision loop rather than a dead end. From `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` the `Insured` or an `Underwriter` re-attests the failed controls and the application returns to `SUBMITTED`. The request body carries `attestations`, a list of revised control-and-level pairs, and every `control_key` it names must already appear in the application's `mandatory_gap_control_keys` — the loop reopens only the controls that actually failed. That list must be non-empty; a `control_key` outside `mandatory_gap_control_keys` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, raised after the state gate; and a named control that has no attestation row yet has one created. As on the original submission, an attestation row must exist for every catalog control whose `is_mandatory` is true, or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_ATTESTATION_MISSING`. The revision has a deliberately wide blast radius. Each named control's `attested_level` is updated; `verified_level` is reset to null on *every* attestation of the application, not merely the resubmitted ones, because the evidence they were derived from is now stale; `verified_control_score` is set to null, `mandatory_gap_count` back to 0, `mandatory_gap_control_keys` back to an empty list, and `premium_usd` back to null, since every arrival in `SUBMITTED` clears the bindable premium; `attested_control_score` and `indicative_premium_usd` are recomputed; the owning account's `open_remediation_count` is decremented by 1 with that account's `version` incremented by 1; and the application's own `version` is incremented by 1. The practical consequence is that the full circuit `SUBMITTED` → `UNDERWRITING` → `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` → `SUBMITTED` leaves the application with no verified evidence whatsoever, so a fresh scan must be ordered and completed before the risk can be quoted again.

### 3.4. Posture Scoring and Premium Construction

**Scoring the control posture.** Maturity levels are converted to numeric factors before anything is weighted: `NONE` counts as 0.0, `PARTIAL` as 0.5, and `FULL` as 1.0. The score is then a weighted percentage over the whole catalog. The numerator is the sum, taken across every control requirement row in the catalog, of that requirement's `weight` multiplied by the factor of the level established for its `control_key`. The denominator is the sum of `weight` across every catalog row — the catalog has no status field, so there is no filtering and every row participates. The score is 100 multiplied by the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The division is performed once, on the exact weights; per-control contributions are never rounded before being summed. An empty catalog leaves the denominator at zero, in which case the score is simply 0.00 and the gap list is empty — no division is attempted and no error is raised.

What differs between the two variants is how the level for each control is established.

- On the **attested basis**, the level for a control is simply the `attested_level` of the application's attestation for that control, or `NONE` when the application has no attestation row for it. Scan findings are ignored entirely, so this figure never drops because a scanner contradicted the applicant. This variant writes `attested_control_score`, and it is refreshed when an attestation is created, when the application is submitted, and when controls are resubmitted after remediation.
- On the **verified basis**, a completed scan is required. The governing scan is the single latest one: among the application's scans whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, take them in `completed_at` descending order and, where two share an instant, `created_at` descending order, and use the first. Failed and pending scans are ignored no matter how recent. For each control, the level the scan observed is the lowest `observed_level` among that scan's `findings` entries for that `control_key` on the ordering `NONE` below `PARTIAL` below `FULL`, and is `FULL` when the array holds no entry for it. The level used for scoring is then the lower of the `attested_level` and that observed level on the same ordering. Because it takes the lower of the two, identical attestations always yield a verified score that is lower than or equal to the attested score, and the verified basis can expose mandatory gaps the attested basis conceals. This variant writes `verified_control_score`, is produced by hop 3 of the scan-completion cascade, and is the figure the mandatory-gap gate and the bindable premium both consume.

The gap detail is produced by the same routine. `mandatory_gap_control_keys` lists the `control_key` of every catalog row whose `is_mandatory` is true and whose established level factor is strictly below the factor of its own `min_level_required`, ordered by `weight` descending and then `control_key` ascending. `mandatory_gap_count` is simply the number of entries in that list. A dedicated computed read returns both variants side by side as `attested_score` and `verified_score`, together with `mandatory_gap_count`, `mandatory_gap_control_keys`, the `min_score_for_coverage` threshold of 70, and a boolean `quotable`. On that read `verified_score` is null until a completed scan exists, while the gap detail is computed on the verified basis regardless — absent a completed scan that basis coincides with the attested one, since every control is then treated as observed at `FULL` — and `quotable` is true exactly when the application's latest scan by `created_at` is `COMPLETED`, no mandatory gap is open, and `verified_score` is non-null and at least 70.

**Pricing the risk.** The premium is built in two stages: an unmodified layer-based gross premium, then a multiplicative modifier reflecting controls, retention, and dependency concentration. The gross stage works as follows.

1. The base rate per million of limit is read from the applicant's `industry_code`: 9,000 for `HEALTHCARE`, 8,000 for `FINANCIAL`, 7,000 for `RETAIL`, 6,000 for `MANUFACTURING`, 7,500 for `PUBLIC_SECTOR`, and 5,500 for `TECHNOLOGY`.
2. A revenue tier factor is applied: 1.00 when `annual_revenue_usd` is at most 50,000,000; 1.15 when it is above 50,000,000 and at most 250,000,000; and 1.35 when it is above 250,000,000.
3. The requested aggregate is expressed in millions — `requested_aggregate_limit_usd` divided by 1,000,000 — and priced in three layers. The portion up to and including the first million is charged at a factor of 1.00; the portion above the first million up to and including the fifth million is charged at 0.60; and everything above the fifth million is charged at 0.35. Concretely, the layer units are the lesser of the millions figure and 1, multiplied by 1.00; plus the greater of zero and the quantity (the lesser of the millions figure and 5, less 1), multiplied by 0.60; plus the greater of zero and the millions figure less 5, multiplied by 0.35.
4. The gross premium is the base rate multiplied by the revenue tier factor multiplied by the layer units, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. This rounding happens before any modifier touches the figure.

The modifier stage combines three independent adjustments into a single multiplier. The control adjustment is negative 0.15 when the governing score is at least 90; negative 0.05 when it is at least 75 and below 90; positive 0.10 when it is at least 70 and below 75; and positive 0.30 when it is below 70. The retention adjustment is negative 0.10 when `requested_retention_usd` is at least 250,000; negative 0.05 when it is at least 100,000 and below 250,000; and zero otherwise. A dependency load of positive 0.08 applies when the applicant's `primary_dependency_key` is any value other than `NONE`, and zero when it is `NONE`. The multiplier is 1 plus the control adjustment plus the retention adjustment plus the dependency load. The premium is the gross premium multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and then floored at 7,500.00 — the rounding is applied first and the floor second, so a computed 7,499.996 rounds to 7,500.00 and a computed 6,000.00 becomes 7,500.00.

Only the applicant record and the application participate; no other entity contributes. The two pricing runs differ solely in which score they consume and which field they write. The **indicative run** fires at submission, consumes `attested_control_score`, and writes `indicative_premium_usd` — a figure issued before any scan can contradict the applicant. The **bindable run** fires at quote issuance, consumes `verified_control_score`, and writes `premium_usd`. The two diverge whenever a scan finding pushes the score across one of the control-adjustment bands.

### 3.5. Quoting, Declining, Binding, and the Coverage Tower

An `Underwriter` issues a bindable quote against an application in `UNDERWRITING`, taking it to `QUOTED`. Quoting is also legal against an application already in `QUOTED`, in which case it is a self-loop supersede: the previous `premium_usd`, `quoted_by_user_id`, and `quote_expires_at` are overwritten by the new quote. Because supersede is legitimate, no state check protects the file, and `expected_application_version` is the only thing standing between a careless re-quote and a lost update, as §2.7 sets out.

Three coverage gates then fire, in this internal order:

1. The application's latest scan by `created_at` descending must be in `COMPLETED`. A newer scan that is still `PENDING`, or one that came back `FAILED`, blocks the quote even when an earlier scan completed successfully — the newest attempt governs, and an application carrying no scan at all fails the same way. Failure is `ERR_SCAN_NOT_COMPLETED`.
2. The verified-basis mandatory gap is **recomputed at quote time** against the control catalog exactly as it stands in that request, and the recomputed count must be zero. This is stricter than reading the stored `mandatory_gap_count`: a mandatory control added to the catalog after the scan completed has no attestation and no observed level, so it opens a gap and blocks the quote even though the stored count still reads 0. Failure is `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_GAP`.
3. The verified score recomputed in that same pass must be at least the minimum score for coverage, which is 70. A score strictly below 70 with no mandatory gap present is refused with `ERR_CONTROL_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

None of the three gates writes anything: the quote-time recomputation is read-only, so a quote refused at any of them leaves the application exactly as it was — its `status`, its stored `verified_control_score`, `mandatory_gap_count`, and `mandatory_gap_control_keys`, and its `version` all unchanged.

On success the recomputed verified-basis figures are persisted onto the application — `verified_control_score` takes the recomputed score, `mandatory_gap_count` is set to 0 and `mandatory_gap_control_keys` to the empty list — the bindable pricing run writes `premium_usd` from that same recomputed score, `quoted_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, `quote_expires_at` is set to the current logical clock instant plus 30 days, and the application's `version` is incremented by 1. A quote left unbound does not linger indefinitely: when the epoch sweep runs at or after `quote_expires_at`, a `QUOTED` application moves to `EXPIRED` with `premium_usd` cleared to null and its `version` incremented by 1.

Declining is available from `UNDERWRITING`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, or `QUOTED`. The `declined_reason` supplied by the underwriter is required and must be a non-empty string; it is stored verbatim, `premium_usd` is cleared to null, and — when the application was sitting in `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — the owning account's `open_remediation_count` is decremented by 1. The application's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Binding.** Binding takes a `QUOTED` application to `BOUND` and mints the policy. It carries `expected_application_version` per §2.7 and is subject to the quote-versus-bind duty split of §2.11. Two further gates apply, in this order: the application's stored `mandatory_gap_count` must be zero, or the bind is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_GAP`; and then the current clock instant must be strictly before `quote_expires_at`, since binding at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`.

The request supplies the contract terms the quote did not fix. `period_start` and `period_end` bound the policy period, with `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, and both are stored on the policy exactly as the caller spelled them rather than renormalised; the period is half-open, covering `period_start` and every instant up to but not including `period_end`. `bi_baseline_method` fixes which revenue baseline the tower will use to quantify business interruption and is either `TRAILING_12M` or `SAME_PERIOD_PRIOR_YEAR`. An optional `named_dependency_keys` array schedules dependencies by name for dependent business interruption cover and is a subset of `CSP_ALPHA`, `CSP_BETA`, and `MSSP_DELTA`. And `coverage_sections` describes the tower itself, one entry per coverage part.

Before the policy is created the server checks that this insured account has no existing policy in `INFORCE` whose period overlaps the requested one. Because both periods are half-open, a new period beginning exactly at an incumbent's `period_end` does not overlap and is allowed; any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.

Each tower entry names a `section_key` from `FIRST_PARTY_RESPONSE`, `BUSINESS_INTERRUPTION`, `DEPENDENT_BI`, `THIRD_PARTY_LIABILITY`, and `CYBER_EXTORTION`, unique within the policy. `sublimit_usd` is the loss-side cap for that part and must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than the policy aggregate. `erosion_priority` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 99 that fixes the section's place in the allocation order, ascending, and must be unique within the policy. `coinsurance_pct` is the insured's share of each allocated loss dollar on that section; it defaults to 0.00 and must be at least 0 and at most 0.50. `waiting_period_hours` is the time deductible: it is required and strictly greater than zero, and at most 168, for `BUSINESS_INTERRUPTION` and `DEPENDENT_BI`, and must be null for every other section key — supplying it on a non-interruption section, or omitting it on an interruption section, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Structurally the tower must include a `FIRST_PARTY_RESPONSE` section and must not repeat a `section_key` or an `erosion_priority`; any of those three faults is `ERR_INVALID_TOWER_STRUCTURE`. Fiscally, the sum of `sublimit_usd` across the `BUSINESS_INTERRUPTION` and `DEPENDENT_BI` sections must not exceed the policy aggregate, and no single section's `sublimit_usd` may exceed it either; a breach is `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_AGGREGATE`.

The policy is minted with `lifecycle_status` `INFORCE` and `limit_status` `INTACT`. Its `application_id` records the submission it came from and its `insured_account_id` is copied from that application. `aggregate_limit_usd` is copied from `requested_aggregate_limit_usd` and `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` starts equal to it; `retention_usd` is copied from `requested_retention_usd`; `premium_usd` is copied from the application's `premium_usd` and becomes the earned-premium denominator of the loss ratio; `dependent_bi_definition` is copied from the application. Each tower section is created, in the order the request listed them, with `sublimit_remaining_usd` equal to its `sublimit_usd`. `warranty_control_keys` is pinned to the set of catalog keys whose `is_mandatory` is true at that instant, sorted `control_key` ascending, and later catalog edits never disturb the snapshot. `lapsed_warranty_control_keys` starts empty, `warranty_status` starts `COMPLIANT`, and `aggregation_flagged` starts false. The application's `version` is incremented by 1.

The policy carries **two independent state dimensions**, and neither constrains the other. `lifecycle_status` describes the contractual life of the contract and runs from `INFORCE` to one of the terminal states `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`, or `NON_RENEWED`; `cancelled_at` and `cancellation_reason` are populated only on the cancellation path. `limit_status` describes the erosion of aggregate capacity and moves between `INTACT`, `PARTIALLY_ERODED`, and `EXHAUSTED` purely as a function of payment activity; it has no terminal state and can travel in both directions. An in-force policy matures when the epoch sweep runs at or after `period_end`, at which point `lifecycle_status` becomes `EXPIRED` and the policy's `version` is incremented by 1. Policies are readable individually and as a list filterable by `insured_account_id`, `application_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `limit_status`, and `warranty_status`. A policy's tower is readable under the policy in `erosion_priority` ascending order — exactly the order allocation walks — with a `section_key` filter, individual sections are readable by `section_id`, and underwriting and actuarial roles can search sections flat across policies with `policy_id` and `section_key` filters.

### 3.6. Warranty Reporting, Incident Notification, and Coverage Confirmation

Mandatory controls are not merely an underwriting hurdle; they are warranted for the life of the policy. An `Underwriter` records a mid-term warranty report by supplying `lapsed_control_keys`, the set of warranted controls now reported below their `min_level_required`. The field is required though it may be empty — an empty report is how a lapse is cleared — its entries are drawn from the five control keys and need not be a subset of the policy's `warranty_control_keys`, and no policy state gates the report at all, so a cancelled or expired policy still accepts one. The report rewrites `lapsed_warranty_control_keys`, sorted `control_key` ascending, and recomputes `warranty_status`, which reads `COMPLIANT` while the lapsed list is empty and `LAPSED` otherwise. The policy's `version` is incremented by 1. A `LAPSED` policy is still in force and still receives notifications, but two operations are barred while it stays lapsed — coverage confirmation on a claim and issuance of a renewal quote — each refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_LAPSE`.

**Notification.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` or the `Insured` notifies an incident by naming the responding `policy_id` and describing the event. `incident_type` categorises it as `RANSOMWARE`, `SYSTEM_FAILURE`, `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE`, `BEC`, or `DATA_THEFT`, and that category later determines which tower section a business interruption loss may erode. `incident_discovered_at` records when the insured found out. `outage_start_at` and `outage_end_at` bound the interruption window and are required whenever `incident_type` is `RANSOMWARE`, `SYSTEM_FAILURE`, or `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE`, with `outage_end_at` strictly after `outage_start_at`. `dependency_key` names the third-party dependency that failed, defaults to `NONE`, is drawn from `CSP_ALPHA`, `CSP_BETA`, `MSSP_DELTA`, and `NONE`, and is required when `incident_type` is `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE` — where `NONE` remains an accepted value, though under a `NAMED_ONLY` policy it then fails the named-dependency check below. `affected_record_count` sizes the notification workload, defaults to 0, and must be at least zero. An optional `systemic_event_scenario_id` tags the claim to a modelled systemic event and, when supplied, must name an existing scenario.

Three gates guard intake. The policy must be in `INFORCE` with `limit_status` other than `EXHAUSTED`, or the notification is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. `incident_discovered_at` must fall inside the half-open policy period — at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end` — or the notification is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_OUTSIDE_POLICY_PERIOD`. And the notification must be timely: `notified_at` is stamped from the logical clock at acceptance, and the elapsed time from `incident_discovered_at` to `notified_at` must be no more than 30 days, a longer delay yielding `ERR_LATE_NOTIFICATION`. A `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE` additionally requires that the policy tower actually contains a `DEPENDENT_BI` section, and, when the policy's `dependent_bi_definition` is `NAMED_ONLY`, that the claim's `dependency_key` appears among the policy's `named_dependency_keys`; a broader definition of `NAMED_PLUS_SUPPLY_CHAIN` or `BROAD_SYSTEM_FAILURE` responds without the dependency having been scheduled by name. Failure here is `ERR_DEPENDENT_BI_NOT_COVERED`.

The claim opens in `NOTIFIED` with `reserved_amount_usd`, `retention_absorbed_usd`, `paid_total_usd`, and `unallocated_loss_usd` all at 0.00, `proof_review_round` at 0, `covered_section_keys` empty, `reservation_of_rights` false, and `denial_reason` null. Its lifecycle runs through `NOTIFIED`, `TRIAGE`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `PROOF_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, and `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` to one of the three terminal states `SETTLED`, `DENIED`, and `CLOSED_NO_COVERAGE`. Any transition not declared for the claim's current state, and any attempt to act on a terminal claim at all, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Claims are readable individually and as a list filterable by `policy_id`, `systemic_event_scenario_id`, `status`, and `incident_type`.

**Triage.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` moves the claim from `NOTIFIED` to `TRIAGE`; the policy must still be in `INFORCE`. The claim's `version` is incremented by 1.

**The fork out of triage.** From `TRIAGE` the adjuster either confirms coverage or denies the claim, and the two branches have deliberately disjoint effects.

Confirming coverage moves the claim from `TRIAGE` to `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`. The adjuster supplies `covered_section_keys`, a non-empty subset of the section keys present on the responding policy's tower — every entry is checked against that tower and an entry naming a section the tower does not contain is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`. That tower check runs first, ahead of the remaining schema checks and of the claim's own state gate, and it catches every entry the tower does not carry — including a string outside the five-key section vocabulary, which reports the same 404 rather than a schema error. `initial_reserve_usd` is required, is non-negative, and sets the opening case reserve, and an optional `reservation_of_rights` boolean, defaulting to false, records that coverage is confirmed subject to a reservation. Confirmation requires the policy in `INFORCE` with `limit_status` other than `EXHAUSTED`, requires `warranty_status` to be `COMPLIANT` rather than `LAPSED`, and is refused with `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` when the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` stands at 0.00. A `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE` claim is re-checked here against the named-dependency rule. On success `covered_section_keys` and `reservation_of_rights` are stored, `reserved_amount_usd` is set from `initial_reserve_usd`, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. That stored set of keys is load-bearing for the rest of the file: every later allocation is intersected with it.

Denying the claim is available from `TRIAGE`, `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `PROOF_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED` and takes the claim to `DENIED`. The `denial_reason` is required and non-empty and is stored, `reserved_amount_usd` is zeroed to 0.00, and every vendor engagement of that claim whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED`, with the corresponding panel firm's `active_engagement_count` decremented once per terminated engagement and that firm's `version` incremented. The claim's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Cancellation.** An `Underwriter` cancels an `INFORCE` policy mid-term, supplying a required non-empty `cancellation_reason`; cancelling a policy that is not `INFORCE` — a second cancellation, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. Cancellation runs a four-hop close-out:

1. `lifecycle_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, `cancelled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `cancellation_reason` is stored.
2. Because the policy was cancelled, every incident claim on it whose `status` is `NOTIFIED`, `TRIAGE`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, or `RESPONSE_ACTIVE` moves to `CLOSED_NO_COVERAGE` with `reserved_amount_usd` zeroed to 0.00 and its `version` incremented by 1. Claims that have already reached `PROOF_REVIEW` or gone beyond it — `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`, `SETTLED`, `DENIED` — are left entirely untouched, because the indemnity determination on those files is already under way.
3. Because those claims closed, each of their vendor engagements whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED`.
4. Because those engagements terminated, each affected panel firm's `active_engagement_count` is decremented once per terminated engagement and its `version` is incremented.

The policy's own `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.7. The Response Panel: Empanelment, Selection, and Deployment

A `PanelAdmin` owns the response panel. Empanelling a firm records its `vendor_name` (1 to 160 characters) and its `vendor_type`, the discipline it practises, from `FORENSICS`, `LEGAL_COUNSEL`, `NOTIFICATION`, `PR_CRISIS`, and `RANSOM_NEGOTIATION`. Commercially it records a `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd` strictly greater than zero — the ceiling rate negotiated with the firm — and a `per_record_notification_rate_usd`, the ceiling per-record price for notification mailings, which defaults to 0.00 and must be at least zero. `specialty_sectors` is a non-empty subset of the six industry codes the firm is empanelled for; an insured whose `industry_code` is absent from that list makes the firm ineligible for its claims. `tier` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 3 expressing panel preference, where 1 is most preferred. `capacity_concurrent_engagements` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 20 capping how many engagements' buffered deployment windows may overlap at one instant. `status` records panel standing as `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `RETIRED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`. A computed `active_engagement_count`, starting at 0, is the conserved slot quantity: it is the number of engagements on that firm whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE`. Empanelment takes effect immediately: from that moment the firm is in the candidate pool the panel selector ranks over.

Panel administration is ongoing. Amending a firm may change `status`, `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd`, `per_record_notification_rate_usd`, `specialty_sectors`, `tier`, or `capacity_concurrent_engagements`, and increments the firm's `version` by 1. Amendments affect future eligibility and future ranking only; a rate already pinned on an existing engagement is never rewritten. The panel register is readable by all five roles, ordered `tier` ascending, then `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — the very order the selector applies — and filterable by `vendor_type` and `status`; individual firms are readable by `vendor_id` including the live `active_engagement_count`.

**Authorising a deployment.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` authorises a panel deployment against a claim in `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` or `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`; the first authorisation advances the claim from `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` to `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, and subsequent ones are a self-loop within `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`. The policy's `limit_status` must not be `EXHAUSTED`, and an authorisation against a policy whose `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` stands at 0.00 is refused with `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` — both conditions raise that same error, and it is the last check the authorisation makes, after the firm has been chosen. The request names the `vendor_type` wanted — which also decides which tower sections this engagement's costs may erode — a `deployment_start_at` and a `deployment_end_at` with the end strictly after the start, and an `authorized_hours_cap` strictly greater than zero and at most 5000.

The adjuster may either direct a specific firm or let the panel selector choose. Supplying `panel_vendor_id` directs a firm and sets `selection_mode` to `DIRECTED`; omitting it invokes the selector and sets `selection_mode` to `PANEL_SELECTED`. A directed firm must exist and its `status` must be `ACTIVE` — a `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` firm is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. Direction bypasses the selector's remaining filters entirely: the firm's own `vendor_type` need not equal the requested one and its `specialty_sectors` need not contain the insured's `industry_code`, and the engagement records the `vendor_type` the request asked for either way. A directed authorisation may also supply `directed_rate_usd`, which must not exceed the firm's `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd`; a higher figure is refused with `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_PRE_APPROVED`. The figure is checked and then discarded — it is never persisted and never becomes the engagement's `approved_rate_usd`, and supplied without a `panel_vendor_id` it is ignored outright. And a directed firm is still subject to concurrency: when its colliding count at the requested window already reaches its `capacity_concurrent_engagements`, the authorisation is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Collision is defined once and used both for the directed check and inside the selector. For a candidate firm and a requested window, the colliding count is the number of engagements on that firm whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` and whose **buffered** window intersects the requested window. A buffered window runs from the engagement's `deployment_start_at` to its `deployment_end_at` extended by a changeover buffer of 24 hours, reflecting the day a firm needs to stand down one deployment before standing up the next. Both the buffered window and the requested window are treated as closed intervals, so windows that merely share an endpoint do collide.

The selector itself runs in three steps. First, the candidate pool is filtered to firms whose `vendor_type` equals the requested type, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `specialty_sectors` contains the `industry_code` of the insured behind the claim's policy, and whose colliding count at the requested window is strictly less than their `capacity_concurrent_engagements`. Second, the surviving candidates are ranked by `tier` ascending, then by `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and the first is the winner; a firm's `id` never participates in the ordering. Third, if the candidate pool is empty, the authorisation fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANEL_VENDOR` — the server never relaxes a filter to find a firm.

The engagement is then created in `AUTHORIZED` against the claim, recording `incident_claim_id` and the chosen firm as `panel_vendor_id`. Two rates are pinned at that instant and never move again: `approved_rate_usd` from the chosen firm's `pre_approved_hourly_rate_usd` and `approved_per_record_rate_usd` from its `per_record_notification_rate_usd`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stamped as `authorized_by_user_id`, which is what later disqualifies that adjuster from accepting the claim's proof of loss. `incurred_hours` and `records_notified` start at 0 and `incurred_amount_usd` at 0.00. The chosen firm's `active_engagement_count` is incremented by 1 and its `version` incremented by 1, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. Engagements are readable individually and as a list filterable by `incident_claim_id`, `panel_vendor_id`, `vendor_type`, and `status`.

**Logging work.** The adjuster logs work incrementally, supplying `hours` and optionally `records_notified` for that increment. `hours` is required and merely non-negative, so a zero-hour increment is accepted and still advances the engagement, and `records_notified` is an optional non-negative integer defaulting to 0. The first log moves the engagement from `AUTHORIZED` to `ACTIVE`; every later log is a self-loop within `ACTIVE`. Logging against an engagement that has reached `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. Cumulative hours are capped: the engagement's `incurred_hours` after adding the increment must not exceed `authorized_hours_cap`, and a log that would breach the cap is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZED_HOURS_EXCEEDED` and changes nothing.

The money is recomputed from cumulative totals, never accumulated incrementally. `incurred_hours` becomes the previous cumulative hours plus the submitted `hours`, and `records_notified` becomes the previous cumulative records plus the submitted increment. `incurred_amount_usd` is then the sum of two independently rounded terms: cumulative `incurred_hours` multiplied by `approved_rate_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; plus cumulative `records_notified` multiplied by `approved_per_record_rate_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Each term is rounded before the addition — rounding once after summing the raw products gives a different answer and is wrong — and the rates used are always the ones pinned on the engagement, never the firm's current values. The engagement's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Closing an engagement.** Completing an engagement takes it from `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`. The claim must be in `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `PROOF_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`, and when `incurred_amount_usd` is greater than zero the policy's `limit_status` must not be `EXHAUSTED`. The eligible-section intersection of §3.8 is verified before anything at all is mutated and ahead of that limit check, so a completion with no responding section reports `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COVERAGE_SECTION` even on an exhausted policy; and an engagement whose `incurred_amount_usd` is 0.00 still runs the waterfall, which absorbs no retention and writes no payment row. Completion is the second of the two allocation triggers in the system: the engagement's `incurred_amount_usd` is pushed through the tower waterfall of §3.8 with a `loss_source` of `VENDOR_ENGAGEMENT`, against the sections eligible for its discipline. That mapping is fixed: `FORENSICS`, `NOTIFICATION`, and `PR_CRISIS` costs are eligible against `FIRST_PARTY_RESPONSE` only; `LEGAL_COUNSEL` costs are eligible against `FIRST_PARTY_RESPONSE` and `THIRD_PARTY_LIABILITY`; and `RANSOM_NEGOTIATION` costs are eligible against `CYBER_EXTORTION` only. The firm's `active_engagement_count` is decremented by 1 and its `version` incremented by 1, the section and policy remaining figures and `limit_status` are updated exactly as on the payment cascade, and the engagement's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Termination.** An engagement in `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `TERMINATED` by the server, never by a direct request, in three circumstances: the policy is cancelled, the claim is denied, or the policy's aggregate is exhausted. Termination runs no allocation at all — the engagement's `incurred_amount_usd` is retained on the record as an unpaid figure, which is the carrier's audit trail of work done but not indemnified. The firm's `active_engagement_count` is decremented by 1 and its `version` incremented by 1, and the engagement's `version` is incremented by 1. A later reversal that restores capacity does not resurrect a terminated engagement.

### 3.8. Proof of Loss, Indemnity Quantification, Allocation, and Reversal

**Filing.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` or the `Insured` files a proof of loss against a claim in `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `PROOF_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`. The wide entry set is deliberate: a claim that never needed a panel vendor never passed through `RESPONSE_ACTIVE` and must still be able to file. The request carries `expected_claim_version` per §2.7, and the compensable hours computed against the eligible business interruption section must be strictly greater than zero — a proof whose entire outage sits inside the waiting period is refused with `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_SATISFIED`. That same error covers the cases where no interruption section can respond at all, so a `BEC` or `DATA_THEFT` claim — which maps to no section — and a claim whose mapped section is absent from the tower can never file. At filing the mapped section need only exist on the tower; the further restriction to the claim's `covered_section_keys` is applied only at acceptance.

The financial submission comprises `claimed_bi_loss_usd`, the amount the insured asserts, which is at least zero and is recorded purely for comparison and never used in the assessed figure; `trailing_12m_revenue_usd`, strictly greater than zero, the revenue of the twelve months preceding the outage; `prior_year_same_period_revenue_usd` and `prior_year_same_period_hours`, both strictly greater than zero, describing the matching calendar window one year earlier; `gross_profit_margin_pct`, strictly greater than zero and at most 1, the margin applied to lost revenue; an optional `extra_expense_usd` defaulting to 0.00 and at least zero; and an optional `mitigation_savings_usd` defaulting to 0.00 and at least zero.

The proof is created in `UNDER_REVIEW`, and any earlier proof of the same claim sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented by 1 — a claim never has two live proofs. The claim moves to `PROOF_REVIEW`, whether it came from `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, or from `INFO_REQUESTED` after an information request, and its `version` is incremented by 1. A proof's own lifecycle is `UNDER_REVIEW`, then either `INFO_REQUESTED` or one of the two terminal states `ACCEPTED` and `SUPERSEDED`. Proofs are readable individually with every quantification component exposed, and as a list filterable by `incident_claim_id` and `status`.

**Requesting more information.** An adjuster who is not satisfied requests further information, supplying an `info_request_note`. The proof must be in `UNDER_REVIEW` — a proof in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROOF_STATE` — and the claim must be in `PROOF_REVIEW`. The proof moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, the claim moves from `PROOF_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, the note is stored, the claim's `proof_review_round` is incremented by 1, and both the proof's and the claim's `version` are incremented by 1. The response body is the proof row, on this path and on the denial path below alike. The insured then files a revised proof, which supersedes the previous one and returns the claim to `PROOF_REVIEW`.

The loop is bounded at two rounds. Requesting information is permitted only while the claim's `proof_review_round` is strictly below 2. When `proof_review_round` already stands at 2 before the request, the round is not granted; instead the proof moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the claim moves from `PROOF_REVIEW` to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` recorded as `PROOF_INSUFFICIENT` and `reserved_amount_usd` zeroed to 0.00, every vendor engagement of the claim in `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED` with each affected firm's `active_engagement_count` decremented, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1 while its `proof_review_round` is left standing at 2. This is what stops the negotiation running forever.

**Acceptance and quantification.** Accepting a proof requires the proof in `UNDER_REVIEW`, the claim in `PROOF_REVIEW`, and the policy in `INFORCE`; a policy whose `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` stands at 0.00 refuses acceptance with `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. The response-versus-adjustment duty split of §2.11 applies. The compensable hours are re-derived at this point and must still be strictly greater than zero, so an acceptance whose recomputed `compensable_hours` collapses to zero — because the override changed nothing about the waiting period but the section's `waiting_period_hours` now swallows the whole outage — is refused with `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_SATISFIED`. The adjuster may supply a `baseline_method_override`; `baseline_method_applied` records the variant actually used, which is the override when one is supplied and the policy's `bi_baseline_method` otherwise.

Quantification uses exactly one proof — the single one under review, since superseded and accepted proofs of the same claim contribute nothing — together with its claim's outage window and one coverage section. That section is fixed by `incident_type`: `RANSOMWARE` and `SYSTEM_FAILURE` map to `BUSINESS_INTERRUPTION`, `DEPENDENT_OUTAGE` maps to `DEPENDENT_BI`, and `BEC` and `DATA_THEFT` map to no interruption section at all. The mapped key is then restricted to the claim's `covered_section_keys`, so a section that exists on the tower but was not confirmed as responding does not qualify and acceptance is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COVERAGE_SECTION`, raised after the waiting-period re-derivation and before the exhausted-aggregate check. The arithmetic then proceeds:

1. `outage_hours` is the elapsed time from `outage_start_at` to `outage_end_at` expressed in hours, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
2. `compensable_hours` is the greater of zero and `outage_hours` less the eligible section's `waiting_period_hours`.
3. The hourly revenue baseline depends on `baseline_method_applied`. Under `TRAILING_12M` it is `trailing_12m_revenue_usd` divided by 8,760 — the hours in a 365-day year — which is a flat annual average that smooths seasonality and usually produces the lower indemnity. Under `SAME_PERIOD_PRIOR_YEAR` it is `prior_year_same_period_revenue_usd` divided by `prior_year_same_period_hours`, taken from the matching prior-year window, so a seasonal peak raises every downstream component and the assessed indemnity with it.
4. `bi_component_usd` is `compensable_hours` multiplied by the hourly revenue baseline multiplied by `gross_profit_margin_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
5. `mitigation_component_usd` is the lesser of `mitigation_savings_usd` and `bi_component_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the credit can never exceed the component it offsets.
6. `extra_expense_component_usd` is the lesser of `extra_expense_usd` and 25 percent of `bi_component_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The cap is computed from the **already rounded** `bi_component_usd`, not from the raw product behind it.
7. `assessed_indemnity_usd` is `bi_component_usd` less `mitigation_component_usd` plus `extra_expense_component_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Each component is rounded to cents before it is combined with the others. Summing raw components and rounding once at the end is explicitly wrong and will produce a different indemnity.

Acceptance then runs a six-hop cascade:

1. The proof moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `ACCEPTED` with `baseline_method_applied`, `outage_hours`, `compensable_hours`, and every quantification component written, and its `version` incremented by 1.
2. Because the proof was accepted, the claim moves from `PROOF_REVIEW` to `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`.
3. Because the claim was approved, the tower waterfall runs over `assessed_indemnity_usd` with a `loss_source` of `PROOF_OF_LOSS`, creating one payment row per eroded section, updating the claim's `retention_absorbed_usd`, `paid_total_usd`, and `unallocated_loss_usd`, zeroing `reserved_amount_usd` to 0.00, and moving the claim on to `SETTLED`.
4. Because those payments posted, each affected section's `sublimit_remaining_usd` and the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` are decremented and `limit_status` is recomputed.
5. If `limit_status` became `EXHAUSTED`, every vendor engagement in `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` on **any** claim of that policy — not merely this claim — moves to `TERMINATED`.
6. Because those engagements terminated, each affected firm's `active_engagement_count` is decremented once per engagement and its `version` incremented.

The claim's `version` is incremented by 1 for the request.

**The tower allocation waterfall.** One routine allocates loss for both triggers. Its input is a loss amount — `assessed_indemnity_usd` when the trigger is an accepted proof, or `incurred_amount_usd` when the trigger is a completed engagement — and a set of eligible section keys, which is the single interruption-section key for the claim's `incident_type` in the proof case, or the discipline's section list in the engagement case. In either case the eligible set is intersected with the claim's `covered_section_keys`, and if that intersection is empty the allocation fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COVERAGE_SECTION`.

Retention comes first, once per claim, off the top, before any section is touched. The retention still available is the greater of zero and the policy's `retention_usd` less the claim's `retention_absorbed_usd` so far. The amount absorbed by this allocation is the lesser of that available retention and the loss amount, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; it is added to the claim's `retention_absorbed_usd`, which therefore never exceeds the policy's `retention_usd`. What remains to allocate is the loss amount less the absorbed retention, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The remainder then walks the eligible sections of the policy in `erosion_priority` ascending order, breaking ties on `created_at` ascending. For each section in turn, the walk stops as soon as nothing remains. The loss allocated to a section is the lesser of the remainder and that section's `sublimit_remaining_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; a section whose allocation would be zero or less is skipped without producing a zero-value payment row at all. From the allocated figure, `coinsurance_deduction_usd` is the allocated loss multiplied by the section's `coinsurance_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the payable amount is the allocated loss less that deduction, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; and `net_paid_usd` is the lesser of the payable amount and the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` at that point, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The order of the four constraints is pinned: the retention deduction precedes coinsurance, coinsurance precedes the sublimit cap, and the sublimit cap precedes the aggregate cap.

A payment row is written for each section actually eroded, recording its `incident_claim_id`, the `coverage_section_id` of the section it eroded, the `loss_source` that produced it — `PROOF_OF_LOSS` or `VENDOR_ENGAGEMENT` — a `gross_amount_usd` equal to the allocated loss, the `coinsurance_deduction_usd`, the `net_paid_usd`, and a `payment_status` of `POSTED`. `retention_applied_usd` carries the retention absorbed by this allocation on the **first** payment row of the allocation only and 0.00 on every subsequent row, so the retention is visible exactly once and never double-counted. After each row the section's `sublimit_remaining_usd` is reduced by the allocated loss, the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` is reduced by `net_paid_usd`, the claim's `paid_total_usd` is increased by `net_paid_usd`, and the remainder still to allocate is reduced by the allocated loss.

Whatever the waterfall could not place — because eligible sublimits ran out, or the aggregate did — is added to the claim's `unallocated_loss_usd`. Nothing is silently dropped. Finally the policy's `limit_status` is recomputed from remaining capacity alone: `INTACT` when `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` equals `aggregate_limit_usd`, `EXHAUSTED` when it stands at zero, and `PARTIALLY_ERODED` in every other case. Framed as transitions, an allocation that leaves remaining capacity strictly above zero and strictly below the full aggregate carries the policy from `INTACT` to `PARTIALLY_ERODED`; one that leaves it at exactly zero carries the policy from `PARTIALLY_ERODED` to `EXHAUSTED`, or, when the whole aggregate was consumed in a single allocation, straight from `INTACT` to `EXHAUSTED` with the same engagement-termination cascade attached; and each such move increments the policy's `version` by 1. An allocation that moves no aggregate capacity at all — every `net_paid_usd` coming out at zero, or nothing being placeable — leaves the policy's `version` and `limit_status` untouched, while the claim's `version` still advances.

The remaining figures are, by construction, derivable: `sublimit_remaining_usd` is a section's `sublimit_usd` less the sum of `gross_amount_usd` over the `POSTED` payment rows on that section; `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` is the policy's `aggregate_limit_usd` less the sum of `net_paid_usd` over the `POSTED` payment rows across all of that policy's claims; and a claim's `paid_total_usd` is the sum of `net_paid_usd` over its own `POSTED` rows. Reversed rows never re-enter the ledger. Payment rows are readable individually and as a list in `created_at` ascending order, filterable by `incident_claim_id`, `coverage_section_id`, `loss_source`, and `payment_status` — and since payment rows are created only by cascade, that list is their only discovery channel.

**Reversal.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` reverses a posted payment row, supplying a required non-empty `reversal_reason`, which is stored on the row — a payment row carries that field null until it is reversed. The row must be in `POSTED`; a row already `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`. The policy must not be `CANCELLED`, or the reversal is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. Reversal is a precise compensating action: the section's `sublimit_remaining_usd` is increased by the row's `gross_amount_usd`, the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` is increased by its `net_paid_usd`, and `limit_status` is recomputed — which can carry the policy from `EXHAUSTED` back to `PARTIALLY_ERODED` when remaining capacity ends up strictly above zero and strictly below the full aggregate, or from `PARTIALLY_ERODED` back to `INTACT` when it ends up equal to the full aggregate. On the claim, `paid_total_usd` is decreased by `net_paid_usd`, `retention_absorbed_usd` is decreased by `retention_applied_usd` but floored at 0.00, and `unallocated_loss_usd` is reset to 0.00; a claim that had reached `SETTLED` returns to `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`, though its `reserved_amount_usd` is not reinstated. The `version` of the payment row, the coverage section, the policy, and the incident claim are each incremented by 1. What reversal does **not** do is resurrect engagements that exhaustion terminated — the capacity comes back, the deployments do not.

### 3.9. Renewal Conditions, Premium Adjustment, and Portfolio Aggregation

**Opening renewal conditions.** An `Underwriter` opens a renewal assessment against a policy inside the renewal window, which begins 90 days before the policy's `period_end`. The policy must be in `INFORCE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`, and that state gate is checked first; only then is the window itself tested, an assessment opened while the clock is still earlier than `period_end` less 90 days being refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The assessment names `required_control_keys`, the subset of the five controls the insured must improve to earn a renewal quote — the field is required though it may be empty, its entries are drawn from the fixed five-key vocabulary rather than from whatever the live catalog currently holds, and the list is stored sorted `control_key` ascending — and an optional `remediation_due_at` deadline for the evidence, stored exactly as the caller spelled it. A non-empty requirement list creates the assessment in `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`; an empty list means there is nothing to remediate, and the assessment is created directly in `CONDITIONS_MET` with `verified_control_keys` empty. Either way `verification_round` starts at 0, `submitted_control_keys` and `verified_control_keys` start empty, and `loss_ratio`, `premium_adjustment_pct`, `renewal_premium_usd`, and `non_renewal_reason` all start null. Assessments are readable individually — exposing `loss_ratio`, `renewal_premium_usd`, and `premium_adjustment_pct` — and as a list filterable by `policy_id` and `status`. The assessment lifecycle runs `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONS_MET`, and terminates in `RENEWAL_QUOTED` or `NON_RENEWED`; a transition not declared for the current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`.

**The remediation negotiation.** The `Insured`, or an `Underwriter` acting for it, submits evidence by supplying `submitted_control_keys`, which moves the assessment from `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` to `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`. The field is required though it may be empty, an empty submission being a legitimate move. Every submitted key must appear in `required_control_keys`, one that does not being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a state or coverage error, and that membership test is applied after the `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` state gate. The submitted set is stored sorted `control_key` ascending and the assessment's `version` is incremented by 1.

An `Underwriter` then verifies, supplying `verified_control_keys` — the controls it accepts as genuinely remediated. That field is required though it may be empty, and its entries are constrained only to the five-key vocabulary: a key that appears in neither `required_control_keys` nor `submitted_control_keys` is accepted, is merged into the verified set, and still earns its control credit at renewal pricing. The newly verified set is merged with whatever was already verified, held sorted `control_key` ascending, and `verification_round` is incremented by 1. Three outcomes follow from that merged set:

- When the merged verified set covers every entry in `required_control_keys`, the assessment moves from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` to `CONDITIONS_MET`.
- When it covers only part of the requirements and `verification_round` after the increment is still below 2, the assessment returns to `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` so the insured can submit more evidence. This is the negotiation cycle `CONDITIONS_ISSUED` → `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` → `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`.
- When `verification_round` already stood at 2 before this pass and the required controls are still not all verified, the negotiation is over: the assessment moves to `NON_RENEWED` with `non_renewal_reason` recorded as `CONDITIONS_NOT_MET`, and the policy's `lifecycle_status` becomes `NON_RENEWED` with the policy's `version` incremented by 1.

The assessment's `version` is incremented by 1 on every verification pass. A second path to non-renewal is temporal: when the epoch sweep runs at or after an assessment's `remediation_due_at` and the assessment is still sitting in `CONDITIONS_ISSUED`, it moves to `NON_RENEWED` with `non_renewal_reason` recorded as `REMEDIATION_DEADLINE_MISSED`, the policy's `lifecycle_status` becomes `NON_RENEWED`, and the assessment's `version` is incremented by 1. Note that the policy's `lifecycle_status` may travel to `NON_RENEWED` from either `INFORCE` or `EXPIRED` — a policy whose period matured while the renewal negotiation was still running is still capable of being non-renewed.

**The loss ratio.** Renewal pricing rests on how the expiring policy actually performed. The paid figure is the sum of `net_paid_usd` over every payment row whose `payment_status` is `POSTED` and whose claim belongs to this policy; reversed rows are excluded. The outstanding reserve is the sum of `reserved_amount_usd` over every claim of the policy whose `status` is not one of `SETTLED`, `DENIED`, or `CLOSED_NO_COVERAGE` — closed files hold no reserve. The incurred figure is the paid figure plus the outstanding reserve, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the loss ratio is the incurred figure divided by the policy's `premium_usd`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Every claim of the policy participates regardless of notification date; the window is the whole policy, not a calendar period, and because the sums are order-insensitive no tie-break is needed. A computed read exposes these figures directly as `paid_total_usd`, `outstanding_reserve_usd`, `incurred_usd`, and `loss_ratio`, alongside `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` and `limit_status`.

**Pricing the renewal.** A `PortfolioActuary` issues the renewal quote against an assessment in `CONDITIONS_MET`, taking it to `RENEWAL_QUOTED`. The request carries `expected_policy_version` per §2.7. The policy's `lifecycle_status` must be `INFORCE` or `EXPIRED`, and its `warranty_status` must be `COMPLIANT`; a `LAPSED` policy is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_CONTROL_LAPSE`. The evaluation order below is pinned, because the control credit is capped as a share of the very premium it reduces — the pre-credit premium is computed first, the credit is capped against that figure, and only then is it subtracted. The calculation is never iterated to a fixed point.

1. The earned premium is the policy's `premium_usd`, which is never zero because the original pricing floored every premium at 7,500.00.
2. The loss ratio is computed as described above.
3. A loss-ratio tier adjustment is selected: negative 0.05 when the loss ratio is at most 0.30; positive 0.05 when it is above 0.30 and at most 0.60; positive 0.20 when it is above 0.60 and at most 1.00; and positive 0.45 when it is above 1.00.
4. An aggregation load of positive 0.10 applies when the policy's `aggregation_flagged` is true, and zero when it is false.
5. The pre-credit premium is the earned premium multiplied by the quantity one plus the tier adjustment plus the aggregation load, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
6. The raw control credit is the number of entries in the assessment's `verified_control_keys` multiplied by 750. The credit actually granted is the lesser of that raw figure and 12 percent of the rounded pre-credit premium, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Controls the insured submitted but the underwriter did not verify earn nothing.
7. `renewal_premium_usd` is the greater of the pre-credit premium less the credit, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the floor of 7,500.00.
8. `premium_adjustment_pct` is `renewal_premium_usd` less the earned premium, divided by the earned premium, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. `loss_ratio` is stored on the assessment at 4 decimal places alongside it.

The policy's `version` is incremented by 1 and the assessment's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Portfolio aggregation.** Cyber's defining hazard is correlation: one cloud provider failing takes a whole book of insureds down at once. A `PortfolioActuary` drafts a systemic scenario naming the `dependency_key` assumed to fail — one of `CSP_ALPHA`, `CSP_BETA`, or `MSSP_DELTA` — an `assumed_outage_hours` strictly greater than zero and at most 8760, a `footprint_pct` strictly greater than zero and at most 1 expressing the share of dependent insureds assumed impacted, a `portfolio_capacity_usd` strictly greater than zero being the capacity the portfolio holds for that dependency, and `qualifying_definitions`, a non-empty subset of `NAMED_ONLY`, `NAMED_PLUS_SUPPLY_CHAIN`, and `BROAD_SYSTEM_FAILURE` naming which dependent-BI wordings respond in this scenario. The `qualifying_definitions` list is stored in the order the caller supplied rather than sorted. The scenario is created in `DRAFT` with `modeled_policy_count` at 0, `capacity_breached` false, and both `modeled_gross_exposure_usd` and `modeled_at` null, and is readable individually or as a list filterable by `dependency_key` and `status`.

Running the model moves the scenario from `DRAFT` to `MODELED`, and re-running it is a legitimate self-loop within `MODELED` that overwrites the previous outputs; no state gate applies to the run at all, so neither starting state is ever refused. The population is assembled at the current logical clock instant: a policy is included when its `lifecycle_status` is `INFORCE`, when its `period_start` is at or before the current instant and its `period_end` strictly after it, when the insured account behind it has a `primary_dependency_key` equal to the scenario's `dependency_key`, when the policy's `dependent_bi_definition` is among the scenario's `qualifying_definitions`, and when the policy's tower actually contains a `DEPENDENT_BI` section. Policies whose `lifecycle_status` is `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED`, or `NON_RENEWED` are excluded even when their period covers the instant. The included policies are taken in `created_at` ascending order; the sum is order-insensitive, but the order is pinned regardless.

For each included policy, working from its `DEPENDENT_BI` section: the insured's hourly revenue is `annual_revenue_usd` divided by 8,760, the hours in a 365-day year; the compensable duration is the greater of zero and `assumed_outage_hours` less that section's `waiting_period_hours`; the modelled loss is the compensable duration multiplied by the hourly revenue multiplied by a modelled margin of 60 percent, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the section exposure is the lesser of the modelled loss and that section's `sublimit_remaining_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the figure after retention is the greater of zero and the section exposure less the policy's `retention_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places; and the modelled paid figure is the lesser of two quantities — the figure after retention multiplied by the quantity one less the section's `coinsurance_pct` and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the policy's `aggregate_limit_remaining_usd` — itself rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Erosion already suffered is therefore respected: the model reads remaining sublimit and remaining aggregate, never the original limits.

The already-rounded per-policy modelled paid figures are summed. `modeled_policy_count` is the number of included policies. `modeled_gross_exposure_usd` is that sum multiplied by `footprint_pct` and rounded half up to 2 decimal places — the footprint is applied **once**, to the summed figure; applying it per policy before summing is explicitly wrong. `capacity_breached` is true when `modeled_gross_exposure_usd` is strictly greater than `portfolio_capacity_usd`, and equality is not a breach. `modeled_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the scenario's `version` is incremented by 1.

A breach has portfolio consequences: every policy included in that run has `aggregation_flagged` set to true and its `version` incremented by 1, which in turn attaches the ten-percent aggregation load to its renewal pricing. Flags are sticky across re-runs — a policy that falls out of the included population on a later run keeps the `aggregation_flagged` value an earlier run gave it.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/cyber` prefix. A request that successfully creates a new resource returns HTTP 201; every other successful request — reads, state transitions, amendments, and cascade-bearing operations that modify existing resources — returns HTTP 200.
