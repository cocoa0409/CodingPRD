# Mobile Veterinary Diagnostics & Imaging API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mobile veterinary diagnostics practice sells two things that look identical on an invoice and are nothing alike in operations. The first is a van: a technician, a calibrated modality unit bolted into the back of it, and a day's worth of stops at referring small-animal and equine clinics, where images are acquired on site and the van drives on. The second is a network connection: a referring clinic uploads images it acquired itself, and the only thing our practice supplies is the specialist who reads them. Both pipelines converge on the same reading queue, the same report, the same turnaround promise and the same per-study bill to the clinic — and both diverge sharply in what can go wrong. A van can be out of service, arrive late, run out of shift minutes, or carry a modality unit whose calibration lapsed overnight. A telerad upload has no van, no stop, no trip fee and no equipment at all.

This document specifies the API that runs both pipelines end to end: referral intake and triage, day-route construction with capacity ceilings and emergency STAT insertion, on-site acquisition and image quality control, equipment QC with its retroactive blast radius, specialist assignment under privilege and workload constraints, the preliminary/final/addendum reporting chain with peer review, a turnaround clock that runs on business hours for routine work and on the wall clock for emergencies, and per-study billing against either a prepaid block of studies or a per-study list price.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **quality beats revenue, except where money has already been closed**: a failed equipment QC check reaches backwards in time and condemns every study that unit acquired since its last passing check, retracting published reports and reversing charge lines along the way — unless one of those charge lines sits on an invoice that has already been finalized, in which case the safety action is refused outright rather than allowed to rewrite a closed book. Second, **cost is a property of the day, not of the study**: a single trip fee is charged once per stop and shared among the studies actually acquired there, so removing work from a stop makes the surviving work more expensive. Implementers should expect several rules in this document to read as counter-intuitive on first pass; each of them is deliberate, and each is stated in full.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every path in this document is mounted at the server root. There is no API version prefix and no URL nesting: `RouteStop`, `Study`, `ChargeLine` and every other collection is addressed directly (for example `/route-stops/{stop_id}`), and ownership is carried by identifier fields inside the payload rather than by the shape of the URL.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the actor's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `Scheduler`, `BillingAdmin`, `ClinicUser`, `FieldTech` and `Specialist`.

- A **`PlatformAdmin`** is the operations administrator. They own the reference data — clinics, mobile units, modality equipment, technicians and specialists — plus peer-review sampling and the server's logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record or log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A **`Scheduler`** staffs the routing and intake desk: they build day routes, triage incoming referrals, book appointments and offer reads to named specialists. When `X-User-Role` is `Scheduler`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and not compared to any entity's `id`.
- A **`BillingAdmin`** runs the revenue desk: contracts, prepaid blocks, invoices and the clinic billing hold. When `X-User-Role` is `BillingAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and not compared to any entity's `id`.
- A **`ClinicUser`** is staff at a referring clinic. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinic`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Clinic` compare the resource's clinic identifier to `X-User-ID`, following the chain from a study up through its appointment to its referral's `clinic_id` where necessary.
- A **`FieldTech`** is a travelling technician. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldTech`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks compare the route's `technician_id`, or the technician identifier carried in the request body, to `X-User-ID`.
- A **`Specialist`** is a reading radiologist or cardiologist. When `X-User-Role` is `Specialist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Specialist`; ownership checks compare the assignment's `specialist_id`, the report's `author_specialist_id` or the peer review's `reviewer_specialist_id` to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership scoping applies to **write** endpoints only, and only when the acting role is one of the three entity-backed roles. Read endpoints are not ownership-scoped: any authenticated actor holding an authorized role may read any resource the endpoint exposes. The exhaustive ownership rules are enumerated at rung 3 of the validation ladder in §2.8, and a violation is always reported as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative in §3 states otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters are all optional; supplying several narrows the result set by conjunction, and filters that name an identifier match on exact equality. Each list collection accepts its own filters in addition to `limit` and `offset`. Contracts filter on `clinic_id` and on the computed `status`. Equipment filters on `assigned_unit_id`, `modality` and the computed `calibration_status`. Routes filter on `mobile_unit_id`, `technician_id`, `service_date` and `status`. Route stops filter on `route_id`, `clinic_id`, `bumped_by_stop_id` and `status`. Referrals filter on `clinic_id`, `status` and `study_kind`. Appointments filter on `referral_id`, `route_stop_id` and `status`. Studies filter on `appointment_id`, `equipment_id`, `technician_id`, `repeat_of_study_id`, `workflow_status`, `qc_status`, `tat_status`, `billing_status` and `priority`. Read assignments filter on `study_id`, `specialist_id` and `status`. Reports filter on `study_id`, `author_specialist_id`, `supersedes_report_id`, `kind` and `status`. Equipment QC checks filter on `equipment_id`, `performed_by_technician_id` and `result`. Peer reviews filter on `report_id`, `reviewer_specialist_id` and `status`. Invoices filter on `clinic_id` and `status`. Charge lines filter on `invoice_id`, `study_id`, `kind` and `status`. Block ledger entries filter on `contract_id` and `study_id`. Notifications filter on `clinic_id`, `study_id` and `kind`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and Timestamps

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id` on creation, and identifiers are never reused.

Resources also carry a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. `created_at` is set once when the record is first persisted and never changes. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the record. `version` begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations produced indirectly by a cascade from some other resource. Where a workflow performs several distinct mutation steps against the same record inside one request, each step bumps the version.

Three record types are append-only historical facts and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all: the equipment quality-control record `QcCheck`, the prepaid-block movement record `BlockLedgerEntry`, and the outbound `Notification`. Every other resource type in the system carries the full quartet.

All timestamps in requests and responses are ISO 8601 in UTC. Server-generated instants are rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Datetime values supplied by a client are interpreted as instants and compared as instants; a client-supplied value that carries a different textual form denotes the same moment and must compare equal to it. Two fields, `service_date` on a route and the `period_start` / `period_end` pair on an invoice, are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form interpreted in UTC rather than instants.

Resources are addressed by a single path variable naming their own identifier: `clinic_id`, `contract_id`, `unit_id`, `equipment_id`, `technician_id`, `specialist_id`, `route_id`, `stop_id`, `referral_id`, `appointment_id`, `study_id`, `assignment_id`, `report_id`, `qc_check_id`, `peer_review_id`, `invoice_id`, `charge_line_id`, `entry_id` and `notification_id`. Path variables are resolved left to right; the first one that names nothing produces that resource type's not-found error before any later variable or any body field is examined. Only once all path variables resolve are body-referenced identifiers checked, and those are checked in the order the endpoint's input fields are listed.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Two Ways of Counting Minutes

The server keeps a **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` — a Friday — and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. Every deadline, every calibration window, every cancellation lead-time tier, every contract validity test and every business-hours accrual is evaluated against this clock and nothing else. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, carried in a field named `to_iso8601`, and answers with the new value under the key `current_utc`. Moving the clock does not itself write to any record; it changes what the next read of a contract's status, a modality unit's calibration status and a study's turnaround figures computes.

All instants and all minute-of-day values in this system are UTC. No local-time conversion is ever applied anywhere, for any clinic, in any calculation. A day has 1440 minutes, and a minute-of-day value therefore runs from 0 through 1440 inclusive; any minute-valued field outside that span is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Two distinct minute-counting functions run through the whole specification, and confusing them is the single easiest way to get turnaround wrong.

**Wall minutes between two instants.** Given a start instant and an end instant, the wall-minute count is the number of whole minutes from the start to the end: take the difference in seconds, divide by 60 and truncate toward zero, and treat any negative result as zero. The calendar is ignored entirely — nights, weekends and holidays all count.

**Business minutes between two instants.** Business minutes are counted only inside a specific clinic's opening windows. Each `Clinic` publishes three calendar fields: `business_open_minute`, the minute of the UTC day at which its window opens, defaulting to 480 (that is, 08:00 UTC); `business_close_minute`, the minute at which the window closes, defaulting to 1020 (17:00 UTC); and `business_days`, the list of weekday indices on which the window is open at all, defaulting to Monday through Friday, where index 0 denotes Monday and index 6 denotes Sunday. A clinic's opening minute must be at least 0 and strictly less than its closing minute, and the closing minute must be at most 1440; `business_days` must be a non-empty list of distinct integers each between 0 and 6 inclusive. To count business minutes from a start instant to an end instant: if the end is at or before the start the answer is zero; otherwise walk every calendar date from the UTC date of the start through the UTC date of the end inclusive, skip any date whose weekday index is not in the clinic's `business_days`, and for each remaining date form that date's window as midnight UTC plus the opening minute through midnight UTC plus the closing minute. Intersect that window with the interval from the start instant to the end instant — the intersection begins at the later of the two starts and ends at the earlier of the two ends — and when the intersection is non-empty add the whole minutes it contains, computed as its length in seconds divided by 60 and truncated toward zero. The total across all dates is the answer.

**Advancing an instant by a number of business minutes** is the same calendar walked forwards. Advancing by zero minutes yields the start instant unchanged. Otherwise, take the successive business windows at or after the start instant — each one a date whose weekday index is in the clinic's `business_days`, whose window end lies strictly after the running cursor — and for each such window let the effective start be the later of the cursor and the window's opening instant. If the whole minutes available between that effective start and the window's closing instant are at least the minutes still owed, the answer is the effective start advanced by the minutes still owed. Otherwise subtract the available minutes from the amount still owed, move the cursor to that window's closing instant, and continue with the next window.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Precision

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer cents**. There are no fractional currency values anywhere in a request or a response.

Wherever a monetary result is not already a whole number of cents, it is rounded **half up** — a value of exactly one half rounds away from zero, upwards. This applies to the per-study administrative fee and to every percentage-derived cancellation fee. Rounding happens exactly where each calculation says it happens and nowhere else: in particular, a study's administrative fee is one single rounding applied to that study's subtotal rather than a sum of separately rounded per-component fees, and an invoice total is a plain sum of already-rounded line amounts rather than a re-rounding of an unrounded aggregate.

Minute-valued and count-valued results are whole integers. Where a duration is derived from two instants it is truncated toward zero, never rounded.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Four write operations mutate a parent record whose state the client must have observed first. Each of them takes a mandatory version field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent. The field is **not optional**: omitting it is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. If the supplied value does not equal the parent record's current `version` at the moment the request is processed, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and nothing is written.

- Inserting a stop into a route (`POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`) requires `expected_route_version`, matched against the target `Route`'s current `version`. Another writer may have inserted a STAT stop that legitimately bumped a routine stop and re-sequenced the route, so a client working from an older view of the route holds a stale picture of its remaining capacity.
- Offering a read on a study (`POST /read-assignments`) requires `expected_study_version`, matched against the target `Study`'s current `version`. A fresh offer legitimately supersedes a prior one once that prior offer has been declined, so on identical state the natural product semantics would let a second create succeed; concurrency control on the study's version is what makes the stale-view case deterministic.
- Posting a study's charges to an invoice (`POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/post-study`) requires `expected_invoice_version`, matched against the target `Invoice`'s current `version`. Posting re-prices trip-fee shares across the studies already carried by the invoice, so a writer holding a stale invoice view would post against an allocation that no longer exists.
- Topping up a prepaid block (`POST /contracts/{contract_id}/block-purchase`) requires `expected_contract_version`, matched against the target `ClinicContract`'s current `version`. Block draws arising from study postings bump the contract's version, so a top-up computed against a stale remaining balance would over- or under-purchase.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a version reason.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

There is exactly **one** validation ladder in this system, fifty rungs deep, and every request climbs it in strictly ascending rung order. **The first rung that fails decides the response; no later rung is evaluated.** This matters constantly: a request that is wrong in four different ways reports only the lowest-numbered failure, and a test that expects a rung-30 error must satisfy rungs 1 through 29 first. Several rungs can emit more than one error identifier; where they do, the rung states its own internal ordering, and that ordering is exhaustive — no field outside the rung's stated list routes to those codes.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Ownership scope**, for entity-backed roles, exhaustively as follows — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rung applies to every write endpoint whose acting role is `ClinicUser`, `FieldTech` or `Specialist`.
   - `ClinicUser`: `X-User-ID` must equal the `Clinic` identifier that owns the targeted referral, appointment or study — for a study, reached through its appointment to its referral's `clinic_id`.
   - `FieldTech` dispatching or completing a route, arriving at or departing a stop, or recording an appointment no-show: `X-User-ID` must equal the owning `Route`'s `technician_id`.
   - `FieldTech` acquiring images: `X-User-ID` must equal the `technician_id` carried in the request body — a technician may only acquire as himself. Whether that technician also staffs the route is a **separate** question, decided at rung 24.
   - `FieldTech` recording an equipment QC check: `X-User-ID` must equal the `performed_by_technician_id` carried in the request body.
   - `Specialist` accepting, declining or handing back a read assignment: `X-User-ID` must equal that `ReadAssignment`'s `specialist_id`.
   - `Specialist` publishing or retracting a report: `X-User-ID` must equal the `Report`'s `author_specialist_id`. A `PlatformAdmin` is exempt on retraction.
   - `Specialist` submitting a peer review: `X-User-ID` must equal that `PeerReview`'s `reviewer_specialist_id`.
   - `Specialist` placing a turnaround hold on a study: `X-User-ID` must equal the `specialist_id` of an `ACCEPTED` read assignment on that study.
   - Recording the image-quality verdict on a study carries **no** ownership clause at all: any `FieldTech` may record it, including on a telerad study that no technician ever touched.
4. **Existence.** Path-parameter resources exist, validated left to right, and then body-referenced identifiers exist, validated in the order they appear in the endpoint's input list. Each entity has its own 404 code: `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPECIALIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`. Seven record types have no dedicated code and use `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` instead: `MobileUnit`, `Technician`, `QcCheck`, `PeerReview`, `ChargeLine`, `BlockLedgerEntry` and `Notification`. All are HTTP 404.
5. **Request shape.** Every required input field of the endpoint is present and correctly typed; `limit` lies between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is at least 0; every minute-of-day field is an integer between 0 and 1440 inclusive; a peer-review score is an integer. A calibration window whose end is at or before its start, or whose span exceeds 30 days, also fails here. Failures report `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
6. **Closed-enum membership**, reported as `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). This list is **exhaustive — no other field in the system routes to this code**: a clinic's `status`; a contract's `kind`; a mobile unit's `service_status`; an equipment record's `modality`; each member of a technician's `certified_modalities`; each member of a specialist's `modality_privileges`; each member of a specialist's `species_privileges`; a specialist's `status`; a route stop's `stop_priority`; a referral's `species`, `study_kind`, `requested_modality` and `priority`; a study's `modality`, `hold_reason` and `repeat_cause`; the `qc_result` body field on the image-QC verdict; the `billing_hold_status` body field; the `result` body field on an equipment QC check; and a report's `kind`. Everything else that fails a value check falls to rung 5 and reports `ERR_VALIDATION`.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_route_version`, `expected_study_version`, `expected_invoice_version` or `expected_contract_version` equals the parent record's current `version`, per §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`, `POST /read-assignments`, `POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/post-study` and `POST /contracts/{contract_id}/block-purchase`.
8. **Write gate G1 — finalized-invoice freeze**, `ERR_INVOICE_FINALIZED` (409). See §2.9.
9. **Write gate G2 — clinic billing hold**, `ERR_CLINIC_BILLING_HOLD` (409). See §2.9.
10. **Write gate G3 — unit out of service**, `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_SERVICE` (409). See §2.9.
11. **Write gate G4 — equipment calibration**, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` or `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CALIBRATION_LAPSED` (both 409). See §2.9.
12. **Write gate G5 — completed route freeze**, `ERR_ROUTE_FROZEN` (409). See §2.9.
13. **Pipeline applicability.** Internal order: the mobile-only verb is checked first. Acquiring images against a telerad study raises `ERR_NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_TELERAD` (422); uploading images against a mobile study raises `ERR_STUDY_KIND_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire` and `POST /studies/{study_id}/upload`.
14. **Appointment shape matches the referral's pipeline**, on `POST /appointments`: a mobile referral requires a `route_stop_id`, and its absence raises `ERR_MOBILE_REQUIRES_ROUTE_STOP` (422); a telerad referral must omit it, and supplying one raises `ERR_TELERAD_HAS_NO_ROUTE_STOP` (422).
15. **Referring clinic is active.** The `Clinic`'s `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CLINIC_INACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /referrals`, `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/accept` and `POST /appointments`.
16. **Contract is active.** The clinic must have exactly one `ClinicContract` whose computed `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/post-study` and `POST /contracts/{contract_id}/block-purchase`.
17. **Route and stop state preconditions** for the requested transition. Internal order: the route is checked before its stop. A route in a from-state that does not admit the transition raises `ERR_ROUTE_INVALID_STATE` (409); a stop in such a state raises `ERR_STOP_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every route and route-stop action endpoint.
18. **Referral and appointment state preconditions.** Internal order: the referral is checked before the appointment. `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE` (409) and `ERR_APPOINTMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /referrals/{referral_id}/accept`, `/reject`, `/return` and `/resubmit`, to `POST /appointments`, and to `POST /appointments/{appointment_id}/cancel` and `/no-show`.
19. **Study workflow precondition.** Internal order: a study already in a terminal workflow state — `CANCELLED` or `NOT_PERFORMED` — raises `ERR_STUDY_TERMINAL` (409); any other wrong from-state raises `ERR_STUDY_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every study action endpoint.
20. **Study parallel-axis preconditions.** Internal order: first, a hold requested on a study whose `tat_status` is not `ACCRUING`, or a resume requested on a study whose `tat_status` is not `PAUSED`, raises `ERR_TAT_CLOCK_STATE_INVALID` (409); second, a STAT escalation requested on a study whose `priority` is already `STAT` raises `ERR_ALREADY_STAT` (409). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/hold`, `/resume` and `/escalate-stat`.
21. **Read-assignment state precondition** — accept and decline require `OFFERED`, hand-back requires `ACCEPTED`; otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409).
22. **Report and peer-review state preconditions.** Internal order: the report is checked before the peer review. Publishing requires the report to be `DRAFT` and retracting requires it to be `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE` (409). A peer-review submission requires the peer review to be `SAMPLED`, and sampling or submission requires the underlying report to be in `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETRACTED`; otherwise `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /reports/{report_id}/publish`, `POST /reports/{report_id}/retract`, `POST /peer-reviews` and `POST /peer-reviews/{peer_review_id}/submit`.
23. **Technician eligibility**, two limbs with a fixed internal order. The **status** limb applies on `POST /routes` (against the `technician_id` supplied for the route), `POST /routes/{route_id}/dispatch`, `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire` and `POST /qc-checks`: a `Technician` whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` raises `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INACTIVE` (409). The **certification** limb applies **only** on `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire`, tested against the study's `modality`, and on `POST /qc-checks`, tested against the equipment's `modality` — never on `POST /routes`, because a route names no modality: a technician whose `certified_modalities` does not contain the relevant modality raises `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422). Because a technician's `status` is fixed at creation and no endpoint mutates it, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INACTIVE` is reached by naming an inactive technician on a fresh request, not by deactivating one mid-flight.
24. **Technician staffs this route.** The `technician_id` supplied in the acquire body must equal the stop's owning `Route`'s `technician_id`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ON_ROUTE` (409). A certified, active technician who is simply not staffing this route is refused here, not at rung 3.
25. **Stop has been arrived at.** The study's `RouteStop` must be in `ARRIVED` before any image is acquired at it, otherwise `ERR_STOP_NOT_ARRIVED` (409). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire`.
26. **Equipment is on the right van.** The equipment's `assigned_unit_id` must equal the stop's route's `mobile_unit_id`, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_ON_UNIT` (422). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire`.
27. **Equipment modality matches.** The equipment's `modality` must equal the study's `modality`, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_MODALITY_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire`.
28. **Equipment QC is not overdue.** The equipment's `usage_count_since_qc` must be strictly less than 3, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_QC_DUE` (409). Applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire`.
29. **Specialist is active.** The named `Specialist`'s `status` must be `ACTIVE` — a specialist who is `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` raises `ERR_SPECIALIST_INACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /read-assignments` and `POST /peer-reviews`.
30. **Modality privilege.** The study's `modality` must be a member of the specialist's `modality_privileges`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIALIST_MODALITY_NOT_PRIVILEGED` (422). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
31. **Species privilege.** The study's `species` must be a member of the specialist's `species_privileges`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIALIST_SPECIES_NOT_PRIVILEGED` (422). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
32. **STAT eligibility.** A study whose `priority` is `STAT` may only be offered to a specialist whose `stat_eligible` flag is true, otherwise `ERR_SPECIALIST_NOT_STAT_ELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
33. **Read-attempt cap.** The study's `read_attempt_count` must be strictly less than 3, otherwise `ERR_READ_ATTEMPT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
34. **Specialist read capacity.** The specialist's `open_read_count` must be strictly less than 2, otherwise `ERR_SPECIALIST_READ_CAPACITY` (409). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
35. **Study is read-ready** — its `workflow_status` is `ACQUIRED` and its `qc_status` is `ACCEPTED`; otherwise `ERR_STUDY_NOT_READ_READY` (409). Applies to `POST /read-assignments`.
36. **Authoring entitlement.** The acting specialist must either hold an `ACCEPTED` read assignment on the study being reported, or already be the `author_specialist_id` of a report on that study whose status is `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETRACTED`; otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_SPECIALIST` (403). The second branch exists so that an addendum can be authored after the final publication has already moved the original assignment to `COMPLETED`; without it the addendum path would be unreachable. Applies to `POST /reports`.
37. **Report sequence rule** as specified in §3.7 — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /reports/{report_id}/publish`.
38. **Addendum window.** An addendum may be published only while the logical clock is strictly before the final report's `published_at` plus 14 days; otherwise `ERR_ADDENDUM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to `POST /reports/{report_id}/publish`.
39. **Segregation of duties.** A peer review's `reviewer_specialist_id` must differ from the report's `author_specialist_id`, otherwise `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_SELF` (422). Applies to `POST /peer-reviews`.
40. **Stop geometry**, evaluated on `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`. For a STAT stop the payload's `service_minutes` is overridden to 30 before this rung and every later rung runs. Internal order: first, a payload `service_minutes` below 15 raises `ERR_STOP_SERVICE_MINUTES_TOO_SHORT` (422) — this limb inspects the value the client supplied, even on a STAT stop whose effective service time was overridden; second, a `planned_arrival_minute` below the route's `shift_start_minute`, or a `planned_arrival_minute` plus effective service minutes exceeding the route's `shift_end_minute`, raises `ERR_STOP_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW` (422).
41. **Stop window conflict** — the proposed stop's buffer-expanded minute interval must not overlap another counted stop on the same route, otherwise `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`.
42. **Clinic already scheduled that day** — the clinic must not already host a counted route stop on the same `service_date`, across every route and every mobile unit, otherwise `ERR_CLINIC_STOP_ALREADY_SCHEDULED` (409). Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`.
43. **Route stop-count ceiling**, otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). For a STAT stop this rung fires only after the bump fallback has been exhausted. Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`.
44. **Route shift-minutes ceiling**, otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_SHIFT_MINUTES_EXCEEDED` (409). Checked strictly after the stop-count ceiling, so a request that breaches both reports the stop-count code. Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`.
45. **Route has stops.** A route must carry at least one `PLANNED` stop before it may be published, otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_HAS_NO_STOPS` (422). Applies to `POST /routes/{route_id}/publish`.
46. **Repeat eligibility**, on `POST /studies/{study_id}/repeat`. Internal order: first, a source study whose `repeat_count` has reached 2 raises `ERR_REPEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409); second, the supplied `repeat_cause` must be supported by the source study — `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE` requires the study's equipment to be in `QUARANTINED`, `QC_FAILURE` requires the study's `qc_status` to be `REPEAT_REQUIRED` or `NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, and `CLINICIAN_EXTRA_VIEWS` requires the study's `workflow_status` to be `FINALIZED` with `qc_status` equal to `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_REPEAT_CAUSE_NOT_SUPPORTED` (422).
47. **Invoice scope match**, on `POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/post-study`. Internal order: first, the study's clinic must equal the invoice's `clinic_id`, otherwise `ERR_CLINIC_MISMATCH` (422); second, the study's `acquired_at` date — or, for a cancellation-fee-only posting, its `updated_at` date — must fall inside the invoice's `period_start` through `period_end` range inclusive, otherwise `ERR_STUDY_OUTSIDE_INVOICE_PERIOD` (422).
48. **Study is postable** — its `workflow_status` is `FINALIZED`, or its `cancellation_fee_cents` is greater than zero for a fee-only posting; otherwise `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FINALIZED` (409).
49. **Study is billable** — a study whose `billing_status` is `NON_BILLABLE` may never be posted, and the attempt raises `ERR_STUDY_NON_BILLABLE` (422).
50. **Study is not already posted** — a study whose `billing_status` is `POSTED` may not be posted again until its lines have been reversed, and the attempt raises `ERR_STUDY_ALREADY_POSTED` (409).

### 2.9. The Ordered Write Gates

Rungs 8 through 12 form an ordered block of five **write gates**. They sit after authentication, authorization, ownership, existence, request shape, enum membership and concurrency control, and **before every rung from 13 upwards**. When any gate fires, no rung at or above 13 is evaluated at all, so those rungs' errors never surface for that request. Gate order is absolute and independent of how many gates a single request trips: a request that trips both G2 and G4 always reports `ERR_CLINIC_BILLING_HOLD`.

**G1 — the finalized-invoice freeze (`ERR_INVOICE_FINALIZED`, 409).** No charge line belonging to an `Invoice` whose `status` is `FINALIZED` may be created, re-priced or reversed, whether by a direct posting or by any cascade. This bites hardest on cascades: recording an equipment QC check whose `result` is `FAIL` is **rejected outright** with `ERR_INVOICE_FINALIZED` when any study inside its retroactive blast radius still carries `ACTIVE` charge lines on a finalized invoice. A billing freeze therefore blocks a safety action, the freeze is absolute, and there is no override path. G1 also guards direct posting and finalization: `POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/post-study` and `POST /invoices/{invoice_id}/finalize` against an already-finalized invoice both report it, as does every cascade leg that would touch a finalized invoice's lines.

**G2 — the clinic billing hold (`ERR_CLINIC_BILLING_HOLD`, 409).** Intake writes for a clinic whose `billing_hold_status` is `HOLD` are refused. Exactly five operations are guarded: `POST /referrals`, `POST /appointments`, `POST /studies`, `POST /studies/{study_id}/escalate-stat` and `POST /studies/{study_id}/repeat`. Work already in flight is untouched — acquisition, image QC, reading, reporting and billing all continue normally for a held clinic.

**G3 — unit out of service (`ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, 409).** A route whose `MobileUnit` has a `service_status` other than `IN_SERVICE` — that is, `OUT_OF_SERVICE` or `MAINTENANCE` — refuses exactly three operations: `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`, `POST /routes/{route_id}/publish` and `POST /routes/{route_id}/dispatch`. Creating the route itself, unpublishing it and completing it are not gated by G3.

**G4 — equipment calibration (409).** Acquiring images with a modality unit whose `calibration_status` is anything other than `VALID` is refused, and this gate applies to `POST /studies/{study_id}/acquire` alone. Its internal order is fixed: a `QUARANTINED` unit reports `ERR_EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` in preference to everything else; an `EXPIRED` or `UNCALIBRATED` unit reports `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CALIBRATION_LAPSED`.

**G5 — the completed-route freeze (`ERR_ROUTE_FROZEN`, 409).** Once a `Route`'s `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, no write against that route or any of its stops is accepted. Eight operations are guarded: `POST /routes/{route_id}/stops`, `/publish`, `/unpublish`, `/dispatch` and `/complete`, plus `POST /route-stops/{stop_id}/arrive`, `/depart` and `/cancel`.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Six quantities are conserved, and each must hold after **every** request, including every cascaded side effect that request triggered. An implementation that satisfies each rule in isolation but breaks one of these after a cascade is wrong.

1. **Invoice money.** An invoice's `total_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over the charge lines whose `invoice_id` is that invoice and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. A `REVERSED` line keeps its `amount_cents` value for audit but is excluded from the sum.
2. **Block conservation.** A contract's `block_purchased_studies` equals its `block_consumed_studies` plus its `block_remaining_studies`; and `block_consumed_studies` equals the negation of the sum of `delta_studies` over every block ledger entry whose `contract_id` is that contract.
3. **Route time partition.** A route's `shift_end_minute` minus its `shift_start_minute` equals its `planned_occupied_minutes` plus its `slack_minutes`, where `planned_occupied_minutes` is the sum of `drive_minutes_from_prev` plus `service_minutes` over the route's stops whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`.
4. **Turnaround partition.** A study's `tat_elapsed_minutes` equals its `tat_accrued_minutes` plus its `tat_paused_minutes`, exactly.
5. **Study disposition partition.** An appointment's `ordered_studies_count` equals the sum of its `pending_studies_count`, `acquired_studies_count`, `cancelled_studies_count` and `not_performed_studies_count`.
6. **Single live report.** At most one `Report` on any given study may be in `PUBLISHED` at a time; publishing a new report supersedes the previously published one on the same study.

### 2.11. Derived Values, Cascades and Read-Time Recomputation

A large fraction of the fields in this system are **derived**: they are not stored decisions but functions of other records, and they must be correct at the moment they are read rather than at the moment they were last written. In particular, a contract's `status`, a modality unit's `calibration_status`, a route's `stop_count`, `planned_occupied_minutes`, `actual_occupied_minutes` and `slack_minutes`, a route stop's `sequence` and `actual_service_minutes`, an appointment's five disposition counters, a specialist's `open_read_count` and `workload_score`, an open invoice's `total_cents` and `posted_study_count`, a contract's `block_consumed_studies` and `block_remaining_studies`, and every turnaround figure on a study are all recomputed on read. Reading a study additionally evaluates the turnaround breach test described in §3.8, which can move a study into `BREACHED` with no client having written anything.

Any implementation that caches a derived value at write time and serves the cached copy later will diverge — most visibly on a study's `trip_share_cents`, which changes when a *sibling* study at the same stop leaves the billable set, an event that touches no field of the study whose price moves.

When an operation mutates more than one record, every affected record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, cascade-driven mutations included. The cascades themselves are described where the business events that trigger them are described: the six-hop stop-bump cascade in §3.3, the trip-share re-allocation cascade in §3.5 and §3.9, and the deepest one — the seven-hop equipment-quarantine cascade — in §3.6.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Clinics, Contracts, Vans, Modality Units and People

Nothing happens until a `PlatformAdmin` has populated the reference data. Registering a referring clinic captures its `name` and an `address_zone` — a coarse geographic label kept purely for human readability, never consulted by any decision the server makes — together with the three calendar fields described in §2.5. A clinic's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; a suspended clinic may not submit new referrals, have referrals accepted, or be given appointments, and each such attempt is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_INACTIVE`. A clinic also carries a `billing_hold_status` of `CLEAR` or `HOLD`, initialised to `CLEAR` at registration and settable **only** by the `BillingAdmin`'s billing-hold capability, which takes a `billing_hold_status` value in the body and toggles gate G2 for that clinic. Placing a hold requires the clinic to be `CLEAR`, lifting one requires it to be `HOLD`, and the pair may be cycled any number of times. Placing a hold leaves in-flight work entirely alone.

Pricing is governed by a `ClinicContract`, created by the `BillingAdmin` against a `clinic_id`. The contract's `kind` is the pricing discriminator and is one of `PREPAID_BLOCK` or `PER_STUDY_LIST`. Its window runs from `valid_from` inclusive to `valid_until` exclusive, and a `suspended` boolean — defaulting to false — lets an operator switch it off without moving the dates. From those three inputs the contract's `status` is computed, never stored: it is `EXPIRED` whenever the logical clock is at or after `valid_until` or strictly before `valid_from`; otherwise it is `SUSPENDED` when `suspended` is true; otherwise it is `ACTIVE`. A prepaid-block contract tracks `block_purchased_studies` — optionally seeded at creation and 0 for a per-study-list contract — alongside two derived counters, `block_consumed_studies` and `block_remaining_studies`, with the remaining balance initialised at creation to the purchased figure. Topping up a block adds a supplied `additional_studies` count to both `block_purchased_studies` and `block_remaining_studies`, and is subject to the concurrency rule of §2.7 and to rung 16's requirement that the clinic have exactly one contract computing to `ACTIVE`. That rung is phrased as a property of the clinic rather than of the contract being topped up, so it admits two readings on a top-up — that the clinic must have exactly one active contract, whichever contract is targeted, or that the targeted contract must itself be the active one — and this document does not choose between them.

A `MobileUnit` is a van, registered with a `name` (its call sign) and two independent capacity ceilings: `max_stops`, the hard ceiling on how many non-cancelled, non-bumped stops one day route on this van may carry, which defaults to 3 and must be at least 1; and `max_shift_minutes`, the hard ceiling on a route's shift span on this van, which defaults to 300 minutes — five hours — and must be at least 60. Its `service_status` is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `OUT_OF_SERVICE` or `MAINTENANCE`, defaults to `IN_SERVICE`, and is changed by a `PlatformAdmin`'s service-status capability carrying a `service_status` value; anything other than `IN_SERVICE` trips gate G3 for every route on that van.

Modality units are registered as `Equipment` with a `modality` — one of `RADIOGRAPHY`, `ULTRASOUND`, `CT` or `ECHO` — a human-readable `serial_label`, and an optional `assigned_unit_id` naming the van it is bolted into. Equipment may sit unassigned in the depot, and a separate assign capability sets or clears `assigned_unit_id` (supplying null detaches it). A newly registered unit has never been calibrated: its `calibration_valid_from` and `calibration_valid_until` are null, its `usage_count_since_qc` is 0, its `last_passing_qc_at` is null and its `quarantined_at` is null. Its `calibration_status` is computed on every read, in this fixed order of tests: it is `QUARANTINED` whenever `quarantined_at` is not null; otherwise it is `UNCALIBRATED` whenever `calibration_valid_from` is null; otherwise it is `EXPIRED` whenever the logical clock is strictly before `calibration_valid_from` or at or after `calibration_valid_until`; otherwise it is `VALID`.

Calibrating a unit is a `PlatformAdmin` action that supplies `calibration_valid_from` and `calibration_valid_until`. The end must be strictly after the start, and the span between them may be at most 30 days; violating either is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. **The supplied window need not contain the logical clock.** Calibrating with a window that lies entirely in the past is accepted and leaves the unit computing `EXPIRED` immediately — only a window containing the clock yields `VALID`. Calibration always clears `quarantined_at` to null and resets `usage_count_since_qc` to 0, which is what makes the equipment axis cyclic: `UNCALIBRATED → VALID`, `VALID → EXPIRED`, `EXPIRED → VALID`, `VALID → QUARANTINED` and `QUARANTINED → VALID` are all reachable, and there is no terminal state on this axis. Studies already condemned by a past failure are **not** un-condemned by recalibration; that demotion is permanent.

People come in two kinds. A `Technician` carries a `full_name`, a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, and a non-empty list of distinct `certified_modalities` drawn from the four modality values — the set of modalities this person may acquire on and may run equipment QC for. A `Specialist` carries a `full_name`, a `status` of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, a non-empty list of distinct `modality_privileges` drawn from the four modalities, a non-empty list of distinct `species_privileges` drawn from `CANINE`, `FELINE`, `EQUINE`, `BOVINE` and `EXOTIC`, and a `stat_eligible` boolean defaulting to false which decides whether emergency work may be offered to them. Both of a specialist's load figures, `open_read_count` and `workload_score`, start at 0 and are derived thereafter, as §3.7 describes.

### 3.2. Referral Intake and Triage

Work enters the system when a `ClinicUser` submits a referral on behalf of their own clinic. The payload names the `clinic_id`, the `patient_name` of the animal, its `species` — one of `CANINE`, `FELINE`, `EQUINE`, `BOVINE` or `EXOTIC` — and the `study_kind`, which is the pipeline discriminator and is either `MOBILE` (a travelling technician will acquire the images at a route stop) or `TELERAD` (the clinic will upload images it acquired itself). The referring clinician also states a `requested_modality` from the four modality values, and may state a `priority` of `ROUTINE` or `STAT`, defaulting to `ROUTINE`. Free-text `clinical_history` is optional at submission — this is deliberate, because history is the single thing referring clinics most often forget, and the triage loop below exists to chase it. A new referral is created in `SUBMITTED`. A clinic on billing hold cannot submit at all: gate G2 refuses with `ERR_CLINIC_BILLING_HOLD`. A suspended clinic is refused at rung 15 with `ERR_CLINIC_INACTIVE`.

Triage belongs to the `Scheduler`, who has three exits from `SUBMITTED`. **Accepting** moves the referral `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and requires two things beyond the from-state: the referral's `clinical_history` must not be null, and the referring clinic's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The clinic-status requirement is rung 15's, reported as `ERR_CLINIC_INACTIVE`. The missing-history requirement is stated by the scaffold as a precondition of the transition without nominating a code of its own; it can be read either as a request-shape defect on the acceptance call, reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`, or as a from-state defect on the referral, reported as `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`. Both readings are consistent with the rest of this document and neither is prejudged here.

**Returning** the referral moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and records a `return_reason` supplied in the body. A return is a request for more information, not a rejection, and — unlike almost every other clinic-visible event in this system — **no notification row is appended for a return**. The clinic answers by resubmitting: that action is available to the `ClinicUser` whose `X-User-ID` matches the referral's `clinic_id`, requires the referral to be in `RETURNED`, carries a `clinical_history` value that **overwrites** whatever was there before, and clears `return_reason` back to null as it moves the referral `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`. This is a genuine cycle with no cap: a referral may be returned and resubmitted any number of times.

**Rejecting** moves the referral `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and records a `reject_reason`. `REJECTED` is terminal. The only other terminal state on this axis is `SCHEDULED`, which the referral reaches without anyone acting on it directly: booking an appointment against an `ACCEPTED` referral moves it `ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED` as a side effect.

### 3.3. Building the Day: Routes, Stops and the STAT Bump

A `Scheduler` builds a day route by naming a `mobile_unit_id`, a `technician_id`, a `service_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form interpreted in UTC, and a shift window given as `shift_start_minute` and `shift_end_minute`, both minutes of the UTC day, with the start at least 0, strictly less than the end, and the end at most 1440. The named technician must be `ACTIVE` — rung 23's status limb applies here and reports `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INACTIVE` — but their certifications are **not** checked, because a route names no modality. A route is created in `DRAFT` with `stop_count` at 0, `planned_occupied_minutes` at 0 and `slack_minutes` equal to the whole shift span. Note that the shift window may legally be longer than the van's `max_shift_minutes`; that ceiling binds only when stops are inserted.

Inserting a stop names the `clinic_id` to visit, the `planned_arrival_minute` at which the van is scheduled to arrive, the `service_minutes` reserved on site, the `drive_minutes_from_prev` needed to reach it from the previous stop or from the depot for the first one, an optional `stop_priority` of `ROUTINE` or `STAT` defaulting to `ROUTINE`, and the `expected_route_version` required by §2.7. Drive minutes must be at least 0. A stop is created in `PLANNED`, is stamped with a `trip_fee_cents` of 12500 — one trip fee per stop, no matter how much work happens there — and carries a `cancel_reason` that stays null until it is cancelled or bumped, plus a `bumped_by_stop_id` that stays null unless a STAT insertion displaces it.

Four geometry and uniqueness rules guard the insertion, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8 rungs 40 through 44, after the write gates. **First**, a STAT stop's service time is forced to 30 minutes, overriding whatever the payload asked for — the stop's persisted `service_minutes` reads 30 — and that override is applied before rung 40 and every later rung runs; nevertheless rung 40's first limb inspects the value the client actually supplied, so a STAT stop whose payload asked for 10 minutes is still refused with `ERR_STOP_SERVICE_MINUTES_TOO_SHORT` even though its effective service time would have been 30. A routine stop must reserve at least 15 minutes. **Second**, the stop's window — from its `planned_arrival_minute` up to but excluding that minute plus its effective service minutes — must sit wholly inside the route's shift window, or `ERR_STOP_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW` is raised. **Third**, that window, expanded by a 10-minute buffer on **both** sides, must not overlap the window of any other counted stop on the same route; a proposed stop conflicts with an existing stop exactly when the proposed start is earlier than the existing end plus 10 minutes **and** the existing start is earlier than the proposed end plus 10 minutes, and the collision reports `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A gap of exactly 10 minutes between one stop's end and the next stop's start is legal; a gap of 9 minutes is a conflict. Throughout this subsection a **counted** stop means one whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`; `BUMPED` and `CANCELLED` stops are never counted. **Fourth**, a clinic may host at most one counted route stop per `service_date`, and that uniqueness spans **every route and every mobile unit**, not just this one. This last rule uses whole-calendar-day granularity with no buffer and no minute arithmetic at all — deliberately different interval semantics from the buffered minute overlap above — and a collision reports `ERR_CLINIC_STOP_ALREADY_SCHEDULED`.

Capacity is then tested against the van's two independent ceilings. Let the counted stops be those already on the route; the route's occupied minutes are the sum over them of `drive_minutes_from_prev` plus `service_minutes`. The proposed stop's own load is its effective service minutes (30 for a STAT stop, the payload figure otherwise) plus its `drive_minutes_from_prev`. The prospective stop count is the current counted total plus one. The buffer allowance is 10 minutes multiplied by one fewer than that prospective count, or zero if that would be negative. The minute budget is the **smaller** of the route's own shift span and the van's `max_shift_minutes`. The count test passes when the prospective count is at most the van's `max_stops`; the minute test passes when the current occupied minutes plus the proposed load plus the buffer allowance is at most the budget. Both ceilings admit equality — a route may sit exactly on either limit. **The count ceiling is tested before the minute ceiling**, so a request that breaches both reports `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_CAP_EXCEEDED`, never `ERR_ROUTE_SHIFT_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`.

When both tests pass, the stop is inserted. When one fails and the proposed stop is `ROUTINE`, the insertion is refused — with `ERR_ROUTE_STOP_CAP_EXCEEDED` if the count test was the one that failed, otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_SHIFT_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`. When one fails and the proposed stop is `STAT`, the emergency gets to displace routine work instead. The server picks a victim, bumps it, recomputes the counted set, the occupied minutes and both tests, and inserts if they now pass; it repeats this at most as many times as the van's `max_stops`. **Victim selection** considers only stops on this route whose `status` is `PLANNED` and whose `stop_priority` is `ROUTINE`; stops that are `EN_ROUTE`, `ARRIVED`, `SERVICED`, `BUMPED` or `CANCELLED` are never victims, and neither is another STAT stop. Among the eligible stops the one with the **latest** `planned_arrival_minute` is sacrificed first — the last routine visit of the day is the cheapest to lose — with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. Identifiers are never part of the ordering. When no eligible victim remains, the insertion is refused with the same two codes and the same precedence as the routine case.

Bumping a stop moves it `PLANNED → BUMPED` and sets off a six-hop cascade. **Hop 1** stamps the victim's `bumped_by_stop_id` with the id of the newly inserted STAT stop and writes the literal string `BUMPED_BY_STAT_INSERTION` into its `cancel_reason`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, moves every appointment bound to that stop whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` to `CANCELLED_BY_PROVIDER`, stamping `cancelled_at` with the logical clock. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, moves every study under those appointments whose `workflow_status` is `ORDERED` to `NOT_PERFORMED`, writes the literal string `STOP_BUMPED` into its `cancel_reason`, sets its `cancellation_fee_cents` to 0 and its `billing_status` to `NON_BILLABLE`. Provider-caused cancellation costs the clinic nothing, regardless of how little lead time was left. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3, moves every `ACTIVE` charge line belonging to one of those studies whose invoice is still `OPEN` to `REVERSED`, recording the reversal reason `STOP_BUMPED`. **Hop 5**, caused by hop 4, recomputes `total_cents` and `posted_study_count` on every invoice thereby touched. **Hop 6**, caused by hop 2, appends one notification of kind `APPOINTMENT_CANCELLED` per cancelled appointment plus one notification of kind `STOP_BUMPED` for the stop's clinic. Finally, every remaining stop on the route is re-sequenced. If any of the charge lines reachable at hop 4 sits on an invoice that is already `FINALIZED`, gate G1 refuses the whole insertion with `ERR_INVOICE_FINALIZED` before anything is written.

Stop `sequence` numbers are derived, never supplied. After every stop insert, bump, cancel or arrival, the route's counted stops are ordered by `planned_arrival_minute` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and numbered 1, 2, 3 and so on in that order. `BUMPED` and `CANCELLED` stops keep whatever `sequence` they last held and are skipped by the renumbering.

The route itself moves through four states. Publishing takes it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, requires the van to be `IN_SERVICE` (gate G3) and requires at least one stop — publishing an empty route is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_HAS_NO_STOPS`. Publication stamps `published_at` with the logical clock and refreshes `stop_count`, `planned_occupied_minutes` and `slack_minutes`. Unpublishing takes it `PUBLISHED → DRAFT`, clears `published_at` back to null, and is refused while any stop on the route is `EN_ROUTE`, `ARRIVED` or `SERVICED`. This unpublish edge is what makes the route axis non-linear: a route may be republished any number of times. Dispatching takes it `PUBLISHED → DISPATCHED`; it is the `FieldTech`'s action, requires the acting technician to be the route's own `technician_id` and to be `ACTIVE`, requires the van to be `IN_SERVICE` (gate G3), stamps `dispatched_at`, and fans out to move **every** `PLANNED` stop on the route to `EN_ROUTE`. Completing takes it `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`, is likewise the staffing technician's action, is refused while any stop is still `EN_ROUTE` or `ARRIVED`, stamps `completed_at` and recomputes `actual_occupied_minutes` from the serviced stops. `COMPLETED` is terminal, and reaching it arms gate G5: every subsequent write against that route or any of its stops fails with `ERR_ROUTE_FROZEN`.

Two occupancy figures live on a route and they disagree on identical data by design. `planned_occupied_minutes` sums `drive_minutes_from_prev` plus the reserved `service_minutes` over counted stops. `actual_occupied_minutes` sums `drive_minutes_from_prev` plus `actual_service_minutes` over stops whose `status` is `SERVICED` only. A stop reserved for 45 minutes but serviced in 20 contributes 45 to the first and 20 to the second. `slack_minutes` is always the route's shift span minus `planned_occupied_minutes`, and `stop_count` is simply the number of counted stops.

### 3.4. Appointments, Arrival and the Day in the Field

Booking an appointment is the `Scheduler`'s act of realising an accepted referral. The payload names the `referral_id` and, for a mobile referral only, the `route_stop_id` at which the visit will happen. Rung 14 enforces the shape: a mobile referral without a `route_stop_id` is refused with `ERR_MOBILE_REQUIRES_ROUTE_STOP`, and a telerad referral that supplies one is refused with `ERR_TELERAD_HAS_NO_ROUTE_STOP`. The referral must be in `ACCEPTED`, the clinic must be `ACTIVE`, and the clinic must not be on billing hold. Booking creates the appointment in `SCHEDULED`, initialises its five disposition counters to 0, and moves the referral `ACCEPTED → SCHEDULED`.

An appointment's five counters are derived from the studies beneath it and recomputed after every study creation, acquisition, upload, cancellation, not-performed marking, repeat, or cascade that changes a study's `workflow_status`. `ordered_studies_count` is the total number of studies carrying this `appointment_id`, with no status filter at all — cascade-created replacement studies included. `pending_studies_count` counts those whose `workflow_status` is `ORDERED` or `REPEAT_PENDING`. `acquired_studies_count` counts those in `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED` or `FINALIZED`. `cancelled_studies_count` counts those in `CANCELLED`. `not_performed_studies_count` counts those in `NOT_PERFORMED`. Those four partitions must sum exactly to the total, as §2.10 requires.

On the day itself, the staffing technician drives the route. Arriving at a stop moves it `EN_ROUTE → ARRIVED`, requires the owning route to be `DISPATCHED` and the acting technician to be that route's `technician_id`, stamps `arrived_at` with the logical clock, and fans out to move every appointment on that stop whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. Departing moves the stop `ARRIVED → SERVICED`, stamps `departed_at`, and derives `actual_service_minutes` as the whole minutes between `arrived_at` and `departed_at` — a figure that reads 0 for as long as `departed_at` is null. Departure fans out to move every appointment on the stop whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED`, and it refreshes the owning route's `actual_occupied_minutes`. Departure is **refused while any study under the stop's appointments is still in `ORDERED`**: every ordered study must first be acquired, cancelled, or explicitly marked not-performed. The scaffold states that precondition on the stop's own transition without nominating a dedicated code for it; under rung 17 it surfaces as `ERR_STOP_INVALID_STATE`, and no other code in this document covers the case.

A `Scheduler` may cancel a stop outright. Cancellation is admitted from `PLANNED` and from `EN_ROUTE`, requires a `cancel_reason` in the body, and is refused once the owning route is `COMPLETED` (gate G5). Cancelling a stop moves every appointment on it whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` to `CANCELLED_BY_PROVIDER`, moves every study under those appointments whose `workflow_status` is `ORDERED` to `NOT_PERFORMED` with `cancellation_fee_cents` set to 0 and `billing_status` set to `NON_BILLABLE`, re-sequences the route's remaining stops, refreshes the route's `stop_count`, `planned_occupied_minutes` and `slack_minutes`, and appends one notification of kind `APPOINTMENT_CANCELLED` per cancelled appointment. Cancellation from `EN_ROUTE` behaves identically and is likewise treated as provider-caused, so every study it touches carries a zero fee.

Appointments can also be cancelled directly, and **the acting role decides what it costs**. The same capability is shared by `ClinicUser` and `Scheduler`, takes a `cancel_reason`, and is admitted while the appointment is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. When a `ClinicUser` cancels, the appointment moves to `CANCELLED_BY_CLINIC`, every study still in `ORDERED` moves to `NOT_PERFORMED`, and **each such study's `cancellation_fee_cents` is computed by the clinic-caused fee rules of §3.9**. When a `Scheduler` cancels, the appointment moves to `CANCELLED_BY_PROVIDER`, every study still in `ORDERED` moves to `NOT_PERFORMED` with `cancellation_fee_cents` of 0 and `billing_status` of `NON_BILLABLE`, and one notification of kind `APPOINTMENT_CANCELLED` is appended for the clinic. In both cases `cancelled_at` is stamped and `cancel_reason` is recorded. A `ClinicUser` may only cancel appointments belonging to their own clinic, per rung 3.

The fourth exit is the no-show, recorded by the staffing `FieldTech` with an optional `cancel_reason`. It moves the appointment to `NO_SHOW` and marks every study still in `ORDERED` as `NOT_PERFORMED` with a fee equal to 100 percent of the study's modality list price, as §3.9 spells out. Two preconditions are stated for it: the appointment's `status` must be `SCHEDULED`, and the appointment's route stop must be in `ARRIVED`. Readers should note that arriving at a stop moves that stop's `SCHEDULED` appointments to `IN_PROGRESS`, so the two preconditions can only hold together for an appointment that entered `SCHEDULED` after the stop had already been arrived at. This document states both preconditions as written and does not resolve the tension between them.

The appointment axis has four terminal states — `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED_BY_CLINIC`, `CANCELLED_BY_PROVIDER` and `NO_SHOW` — and they differ from one another only in what money changes hands.

### 3.5. Ordering Studies, Acquisition, Upload and Image Quality Control

A `ClinicUser` orders a study under an appointment by naming the `appointment_id`, the `modality` for this particular study and the `body_region` being imaged. The study's `modality` need not equal the referral's `requested_modality` — a clinician may ask for radiography and get an ultrasound ordered instead. Three values are copied from the parent referral at creation and are not client-supplied: `study_kind`, which is immutable thereafter; `species`; and `priority`. A study also carries a `priority_source` of `NATIVE` or `UPGRADED`, initialised to `NATIVE`, meaning the priority was fixed at order time. Ordering is refused by gate G2 when the clinic is on billing hold.

Every study runs on **four independent status axes**, and no axis constrains another except where this document says so. `workflow_status` starts at `ORDERED` and ranges over `ORDERED`, `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED`, `FINALIZED`, `REPEAT_PENDING`, `CANCELLED` and `NOT_PERFORMED`, of which the last two are terminal. `qc_status` starts at `PENDING_QC` and ranges over `PENDING_QC`, `ACCEPTED`, `REPEAT_REQUIRED` and `NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, of which `NON_DIAGNOSTIC` is terminal on that axis. `tat_status` starts at `ACCRUING` and ranges over `ACCRUING`, `PAUSED`, `MET` and `BREACHED`, the last two terminal. `billing_status` starts at `UNBILLED` and ranges over `UNBILLED`, `POSTED`, `REVERSED` and `NON_BILLABLE`, the last terminal. A newly ordered study has `image_count` 0, `acquired_at` null, `escalated_at` null, `repeat_count` 0, `read_attempt_count` 0, `repeat_of_study_id` null, `repeat_cause` null, `hold_reason` null, `cancel_reason` null, `equipment_id` and `technician_id` null, `price_basis` null, and every money field at 0.

**Mobile acquisition.** The staffing technician acquires images on site, supplying the `equipment_id` used, the `technician_id` doing the work, and the `image_count` captured. This verb is mobile-only: attempting it on a telerad study is refused at rung 13 with `ERR_NOT_APPLICABLE_TO_TELERAD`. It is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. Gate G4 requires the named unit's `calibration_status` to be `VALID`, reporting `ERR_EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` for a quarantined unit and `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CALIBRATION_LAPSED` for an expired or never-calibrated one. Rung 3 requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the body's `technician_id`; rung 23 requires that technician to be `ACTIVE` and to hold the study's `modality` among their `certified_modalities`; rung 24 separately requires that same `technician_id` to be the owning route's `technician_id`, so a perfectly certified technician who is simply not on this route is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ON_ROUTE` rather than with an ownership error. Rung 25 requires the stop to be `ARRIVED`. Rung 26 requires the equipment's `assigned_unit_id` to be the stop's route's `mobile_unit_id`. Rung 27 requires the equipment's `modality` to equal the study's `modality`. Rung 28 requires the equipment's `usage_count_since_qc` to be strictly below 3.

That usage counter is the workhorse of the equipment-safety story. A modality unit may perform three acquisitions between passing quality checks: the first three succeed, because on entry the counter reads 0, then 1, then 2; the fourth is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_QC_DUE` because the counter reads exactly 3. The comparison is *at or above* the cap, not *strictly above* it. A successful acquisition increments the counter by one. A passing equipment QC check resets it to 0, and so does recalibration.

A successful acquisition moves the study `ORDERED → ACQUIRED`, stamps `acquired_at` with the logical clock, records the supplied `equipment_id`, `technician_id` and `image_count`, refreshes the appointment's disposition counters, and re-allocates the trip-fee share across every billable study at that stop — the newcomer joins the split, so its siblings' shares **fall**.

**Telerad upload.** A `ClinicUser` whose `X-User-ID` matches the study's clinic uploads images for a telerad study, supplying only the `image_count`. This verb is telerad-only: attempting it on a mobile study is refused at rung 13 with `ERR_STUDY_KIND_MISMATCH`. Upload moves the study `ORDERED → ACQUIRED`, stamps `acquired_at` and records `image_count`, and leaves `equipment_id` and `technician_id` null — this edge skips every route, van, equipment and technician hop in the system. The appointment's disposition counters are refreshed.

**Image quality control** is the study-level verdict on the pictures themselves, and it is distinct from the equipment-level check described in §3.6. Any `FieldTech` may record it — this operation carries no ownership clause whatsoever, and it applies equally to a telerad study that no technician ever touched. The body carries a single `qc_result` field, one of `ACCEPTED`, `REPEAT_REQUIRED` or `NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, and the study must be in `ACQUIRED` with `qc_status` still `PENDING_QC`.

An `ACCEPTED` verdict moves the quality axis `PENDING_QC → ACCEPTED`, leaves the workflow axis alone, and makes the study read-ready. A `REPEAT_REQUIRED` verdict moves the quality axis `PENDING_QC → REPEAT_REQUIRED`; a `NON_DIAGNOSTIC` verdict moves it `PENDING_QC → NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, which is terminal on that axis. Both of the latter two also move the workflow axis `ACQUIRED → REPEAT_PENDING` and append a notification of kind `STUDY_REPEAT_REQUIRED` for the clinic, and both refresh the appointment's disposition counters.

Moving to `REPEAT_PENDING` has a money consequence that surprises people, and the scaffold spells its four hops out under the `REPEAT_REQUIRED` verdict specifically. **Hop 1**: the study leaves its stop's billable set, because that set admits only studies in `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED` or `FINALIZED`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1: the trip-fee share is re-allocated across every remaining billable sibling at the same stop, and their `trip_share_cents` **rise**, because the same 12500-cent trip fee is now split fewer ways. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2: every `ACTIVE` charge line of kind `TRIP_FEE_SHARE` or `ADMIN_FEE` belonging to those siblings on an `OPEN` invoice is re-priced **in place** — the line stays `ACTIVE` and its `amount_cents` changes. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3: every invoice thereby touched recomputes its `total_cents`. Because membership of the billable set is defined purely by workflow state, the same arithmetic consequence follows a `NON_DIAGNOSTIC` verdict, whose branch the scaffold describes only by its state change; readers should treat the enumerated four-hop cascade as attached to the move into `REPEAT_PENDING` and note that the scaffold itself enumerates it only on the `REPEAT_REQUIRED` branch.

**Not performed.** A `FieldTech` may record that an ordered study could not be performed at all, supplying a `cancel_reason`. This moves the study `ORDERED → NOT_PERFORMED`, sets `billing_status` to `NON_BILLABLE`, recomputes the trip shares of the remaining billable siblings at the same stop, and refreshes the appointment's disposition counters. The same transition is also reached by fan-out from an appointment cancellation or a stop bump.

### 3.6. Equipment Quality Control, Quarantine and the Retroactive Cascade

Equipment quality control is a separate discipline from image quality control, and it is the sharpest instrument in this system. A `FieldTech` records a check against a modality unit by naming the `equipment_id`, the `performed_by_technician_id` who ran it, a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, and an optional free-text `note`. The check is stamped with a `performed_at` instant taken from the logical clock and carries an `affected_study_count`. A QC check is an append-only historical fact: it has an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else — no `updated_at`, no `version`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the body's `performed_by_technician_id`, that technician must be `ACTIVE`, and — this is the certification limb of rung 23 — that technician must hold the **equipment's** `modality` among their `certified_modalities`, not the modality of any study. A technician who is not certified on that modality cannot condemn the unit's past work, and is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

A `PASS` sets the unit's `last_passing_qc_at` to the logical clock and resets its `usage_count_since_qc` to 0. Its `affected_study_count` is always 0. A passing check leaves `calibration_status` where it is — from `VALID` the unit stays `VALID` — and its real effect is forward-looking: **it narrows the retroactive blast radius of every future failure** to studies acquired at or after this instant.

A `FAIL` quarantines the unit and reaches backwards. The **blast radius** is computed first. Take the cutoff to be the unit's `last_passing_qc_at`, or, when that is null because the unit has never passed a check, the unit's own `created_at`. The affected studies are those whose `equipment_id` is this unit, whose `acquired_at` is **at or after** the cutoff, and whose `workflow_status` is not `CANCELLED`, `NOT_PERFORMED` or `REPEAT_PENDING`; they are processed in `created_at` ascending order. The lower bound is inclusive: a study acquired at exactly the cutoff instant **is** swept in, and a study acquired one minute earlier is not. That boundary is what separates "the check that cleared it" from "the check that condemned it". The number of studies swept in becomes the check's `affected_study_count`.

Before anything is written, gate G1 is consulted: if any study inside the blast radius still carries `ACTIVE` charge lines on an invoice whose `status` is `FINALIZED`, the entire failing check is **rejected outright** with `ERR_INVOICE_FINALIZED`. A closed book beats a safety recall, absolutely and with no override.

Otherwise the cascade runs, seven numbered hops deep and six entity hops wide, from the check through equipment, study, charge line and invoice on one leg, and through the block ledger to the contract on the other.

- **Hop 1.** The unit's `quarantined_at` is set to the logical clock, so its computed `calibration_status` becomes `QUARANTINED`. This is reachable from `VALID` and from `EXPIRED` alike, and the cascade is identical from either.
- **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1. Every study in the blast radius moves its `qc_status` to `REPEAT_REQUIRED` and its `workflow_status` to `REPEAT_PENDING`. On the quality axis this is a genuine re-entry edge: a study already sitting at `ACCEPTED` is retroactively demoted `ACCEPTED → REPEAT_REQUIRED`, which is what makes that axis non-linear. On the workflow axis the same edge exists from `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED` and `FINALIZED` — the last of these is the deepest, because it is the one that reaches the billing leg.
- **Hop 2b**, caused by hop 2. Every report on those studies whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED` with `retract_reason` set to the literal string `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE`.
- **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2. Every read assignment on those studies whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `REVOKED`.
- **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3. Each affected specialist recomputes `open_read_count` and `workload_score`, both of which move downward.
- **Hop 4b**, caused by hop 2. Every `ACTIVE` charge line on those studies whose invoice is `OPEN` moves to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` set to `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE`, and each such study moves its billing axis `POSTED → REVERSED`.
- **Hop 4c**, caused by hop 2. The condemned studies leave their stops' billable sets, so the trip-fee share is re-allocated across every surviving billable sibling at those stops, and each sibling's `ACTIVE` `TRIP_FEE_SHARE` and `ADMIN_FEE` charge lines are re-priced in place. The survivors get **more** expensive.
- **Hop 5**, caused by hop 4b. Every invoice thereby touched recomputes `total_cents` and `posted_study_count`.
- **Hop 6**, caused by hop 4b. For each reversed study that had drawn a block unit, a block ledger entry with `delta_studies` of positive 1 and `reason` of `REFUND_QC` is appended, and its contract's `block_consumed_studies` decreases while its `block_remaining_studies` increases by the same amount.
- **Hop 7**, caused by hop 2. One notification of kind `STUDY_REPEAT_REQUIRED` is appended per affected study, addressed to that study's clinic.

Recalibrating a quarantined unit clears `quarantined_at` back to null and resets `usage_count_since_qc` to 0, returning it `QUARANTINED → VALID`. Studies already condemned are **not** un-condemned; reports already retracted stay retracted; charge lines already reversed stay reversed. The retroactive demotion is permanent, and recalibration only reopens the unit for future work.

### 3.7. The Reading Queue, the Reporting Chain and Peer Review

Reads are **offered, never auto-assigned**. A `Scheduler` names both sides explicitly — the `study_id` and the `specialist_id` — together with the `expected_study_version` required by §2.7; the server never picks a specialist itself and maintains no fallback pool. Four matching axes are checked in a fixed order, and they are rungs 29 through 32: the specialist's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SPECIALIST_INACTIVE`; the study's `modality` must be a member of the specialist's `modality_privileges`, or `ERR_SPECIALIST_MODALITY_NOT_PRIVILEGED`; the study's `species` must be a member of the specialist's `species_privileges`, or `ERR_SPECIALIST_SPECIES_NOT_PRIVILEGED`; and a study whose `priority` is `STAT` may only go to a specialist whose `stat_eligible` is true, or `ERR_SPECIALIST_NOT_STAT_ELIGIBLE`. Two counters then gate the offer: the study's `read_attempt_count` must be below 3 (`ERR_READ_ATTEMPT_CAP_EXCEEDED`) and the specialist's `open_read_count` must be below 2 (`ERR_SPECIALIST_READ_CAPACITY`). Finally the study must be read-ready — `workflow_status` of `ACQUIRED` **and** `qc_status` of `ACCEPTED` — or `ERR_STUDY_NOT_READ_READY`.

A successful offer creates a `ReadAssignment` in `OFFERED`, stamps its `offered_at` with the logical clock, leaves `responded_at` and `decline_reason` null, increments the study's `read_attempt_count` by 1, records that new value as the assignment's `attempt_number` — a 1-based index — and moves the study `ACQUIRED → ASSIGNED`.

Three attempts per study is a hard ceiling and **no exit gives an attempt back**. Attempts 1, 2 and 3 are created because the counter reads 0, 1 and 2 on entry; the fourth offer is refused because the counter reads exactly 3. Declining consumes the attempt. So does handing back. So does a revocation driven by the equipment cascade.

A `Specialist` responds to their own offers. **Accepting** moves the assignment `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_at`, and moves the study `ASSIGNED → READ_IN_PROGRESS`. **Declining** moves it `OFFERED → DECLINED`, stamps `responded_at` and the supplied `decline_reason`, and returns the study `ASSIGNED → ACQUIRED` — still read-ready, still eligible for another offer, one attempt poorer. **Handing back** an accepted read moves it `ACCEPTED → HANDED_BACK`, stamps `responded_at` and `decline_reason`, and returns the study `READ_IN_PROGRESS → ACQUIRED`; it is refused if the handing-back specialist already has a `PUBLISHED` report on that study. Any `DRAFT` report they wrote is left untouched — drafts do not block reassignment. Those two return edges, decline and hand-back, are what make the study's workflow axis cyclic. The remaining exits are `COMPLETED`, reached when a final report is published on the study, and `REVOKED`, reached only through the equipment-quarantine cascade of §3.6. Each of the six statuses `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `HANDED_BACK`, `COMPLETED` and `REVOKED` is a valid value of an assignment's `status`, and the last four are terminal.

Two load figures hang off a specialist and they deliberately measure different things. `open_read_count` is simply the number of that specialist's assignments whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`; `DECLINED`, `HANDED_BACK`, `COMPLETED` and `REVOKED` never count. `workload_score` is weighted and its counting basis depends on the specialist: a specialist whose `stat_eligible` is true is scored on a **pipeline** basis, counting assignments in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`, on the theory that an emergency reader must answer offers fast and the offer counts against them the moment it lands; a specialist whose `stat_eligible` is false is scored on a **committed** basis, counting only assignments in `ACCEPTED`. Whichever set is counted, each assignment contributes 2 when its study's `priority` is `STAT` and 1 when it is `ROUTINE`. On identical data — one outstanding offer for a routine study and nothing else — the pipeline basis scores 1 and the committed basis scores 0, while `open_read_count` reads 1 in both cases. Both figures are recomputed after every assignment creation, acceptance, decline, hand-back, completion or revocation.

**Reports.** The reading specialist drafts a report naming the `study_id`, the report `kind` — `PRELIMINARY`, `FINAL` or `ADDENDUM` — and the `findings` body text. The `author_specialist_id` is taken from `X-User-ID`; clients never supply it. To draft at all, the acting specialist must satisfy rung 36: they must hold an `ACCEPTED` assignment on that study, **or** already be the author of a report on that study whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETRACTED`. The second branch is what makes addenda reachable, since publishing the final report moves the original assignment to `COMPLETED` and thereby removes the first branch. A new report is created in `DRAFT` with `published_at`, `retracted_at`, `retract_reason` and `supersedes_report_id` all null, and is stamped with a `report_version`: one more than the number of reports already on that study, **counting rows in every status including `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED` and `RETRACTED`**, assigned at creation in `created_at` ascending order, so the versions form a contiguous 1-based sequence per study.

Publishing requires the report to be `DRAFT` and the acting specialist to be its `author_specialist_id`. The **sequence rule** then decides admissibility. A `PRELIMINARY` report requires the study's `workflow_status` to be `READ_IN_PROGRESS`; it needs no predecessor report. A `FINAL` report requires the study's `workflow_status` to be `READ_IN_PROGRESS` or `PRELIM_REPORTED`. An `ADDENDUM` requires a report on the same study whose `kind` is `FINAL` and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and requires the logical clock to be **strictly before** that report's `published_at` plus 14 days — publishing at exactly the fourteen-day mark is closed, publishing one minute earlier is open. Anything that fails the shape test raises `ERR_REPORT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`; a late addendum raises `ERR_ADDENDUM_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Publication moves the report `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` with the logical clock, sets `supersedes_report_id` to the id of the report that was `PUBLISHED` on this study immediately before — or null when there was none — and moves that predecessor `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`, which keeps at most one live report per study. A superseded report changes no clinical content and stays readable. The study's workflow axis then advances according to the kind published: a preliminary moves it `READ_IN_PROGRESS → PRELIM_REPORTED`; a final moves it `READ_IN_PROGRESS → FINALIZED` or `PRELIM_REPORTED → FINALIZED`; an addendum leaves it unchanged. Publishing a final additionally moves the owning assignment `ACCEPTED → COMPLETED` and settles the turnaround axis as §3.8 describes. Every publication appends a notification of kind `REPORT_PUBLISHED` for the clinic.

An addendum published on a study that has already been posted to an `OPEN` invoice creates **no** new charge line and re-prices nothing — that read was already billed. And if the invoice has since been finalized, the addendum still publishes, because gate G1 guards charge lines rather than clinical content.

**Retraction** moves a report `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED`, stamping `retracted_at` and the supplied `retract_reason`. Either the report's own author or a `PlatformAdmin` may retract. When the retracted report was the study's final, the study's workflow axis rewinds: `FINALIZED → PRELIM_REPORTED` if a `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` preliminary report exists on that study, and `FINALIZED → READ_IN_PROGRESS` otherwise. Any peer review on the retracted report that is still `SAMPLED` is left in place and may still be submitted. `SUPERSEDED` and `RETRACTED` are the report axis's terminal states.

**Peer review** is an independent quality axis with no billing or workflow consequence at all. A `PlatformAdmin` samples a report by naming the `report_id` and the `reviewer_specialist_id`. The reviewer must be `ACTIVE`, the underlying report must have actually been published — that is, be in `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETRACTED`, since sampling one that was never published raises `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_INVALID_STATE` — and segregation of duties requires the reviewer to differ from the report's `author_specialist_id`, with self-review refused as `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_SELF`. The review is created in `SAMPLED` with a null `score` and a null `outcome_note`. The named reviewer, and only they, then submits an integer `score` between 1 and 5 inclusive along with an optional `outcome_note`; a score outside that range is `ERR_VALIDATION`. A score of 2 or lower moves the review `SAMPLED → DISCORDANT`; a score of 3 or higher moves it `SAMPLED → CONCUR`. A peer review's `status` therefore takes exactly three values — `SAMPLED`, `CONCUR` and `DISCORDANT` — of which `CONCUR` and `DISCORDANT` are terminal. A discordant outcome has no billing and no workflow consequence whatsoever; peer review is a quality axis that records disagreement without acting on it.

### 3.8. The Turnaround Clock and STAT Escalation

Turnaround is the promise the practice sells, and it runs on two different clocks depending on urgency. A routine study is given **240 minutes measured only inside the referring clinic's business windows** — four working hours, which across a default Monday-to-Friday 08:00-to-17:00 UTC calendar means the clock stops every evening and does not run at all at weekends. A STAT study is given **60 minutes measured on the wall clock**, nights and weekends included. That budget is the study's `tat_target_minutes`, and it is recomputed on every read of the study and after every mutation that can change the study's `priority`.

The clock is measured from the study's `tat_anchor_at`, and which instant that is depends on `priority_source`. For a `NATIVE` study — one whose priority was fixed at order time — the anchor is the study's `created_at`, so the whole order-to-report span is measured. For an `UPGRADED` study — one raised to `STAT` after the fact — the anchor is the study's `acquired_at`, or, when the study has not been acquired yet, its `escalated_at`.

`tat_elapsed_minutes` is the number of minutes between the anchor and the logical clock's current value, counted in the study's **accrual mode**: wall minutes when the study's `priority` is `STAT`, business minutes against the referring clinic's calendar when it is `ROUTINE`. `tat_paused_minutes` is the number of minutes the clock spent stopped, counted in the **same** mode so the partition invariant cannot drift: it is the sum, over every closed hold-and-resume window on the study, of the minutes that window contains, plus — when the study is currently `PAUSED` — the minutes between `pause_started_at` and the logical clock's current value. `tat_accrued_minutes` is `tat_elapsed_minutes` less `tat_paused_minutes`, and §2.10 requires the three to reconcile exactly. Once a study's `tat_status` becomes `MET` or `BREACHED`, `tat_elapsed_minutes` freezes at whatever it held at that instant and never advances again.

`tat_deadline_at` is the wall-clock instant at which accrued turnaround would reach the target. For a `STAT` study it is the anchor advanced by `tat_target_minutes` plus `tat_paused_minutes` ordinary minutes. For a `ROUTINE` study it is the anchor advanced by that same sum of **business** minutes, using the forward walk over the clinic's opening windows described in §2.5.

Breach is evaluated on every read of a study and after every mutation of it. If the study's `tat_status` is already `MET` or `BREACHED` nothing changes, since both are terminal. Otherwise, if `tat_accrued_minutes` is **strictly greater than** `tat_target_minutes`, the study moves `ACCRUING → BREACHED`. The comparison is strict: a routine study sitting at exactly 240 accrued business minutes is still `ACCRUING`, and one at 241 is `BREACHED`. The first time a study enters `BREACHED`, one notification of kind `TAT_BREACHED` is appended for its clinic. The opposite settlement happens at publication: when a final report is published and the study's `tat_accrued_minutes` is less than or equal to `tat_target_minutes` at that instant, the study moves `ACCRUING → MET` and the clock stops; when it is greater, the study moves `ACCRUING → BREACHED` instead.

**Holding the clock.** A reading `Specialist` may pause the clock pending input from the clinic, supplying a `hold_reason` of either `MISSING_HISTORY` or `ADDENDUM_QUESTION`. A hold requires the study's `tat_status` to be `ACCRUING` and its `workflow_status` to be one of `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS` or `PRELIM_REPORTED`; it moves the clock `ACCRUING → PAUSED` and stamps `pause_started_at` with the logical clock. Rung 3 additionally requires the acting specialist to be the `specialist_id` of an `ACCEPTED` read assignment on that study — a requirement that readers should note is satisfiable only in `READ_IN_PROGRESS` and `PRELIM_REPORTED`, since a study in `ACQUIRED` or `ASSIGNED` has no accepted assignment on it. Both rules are enforced, neither one relaxing the other, and each is evaluated at its own rung of the single ladder — the rung-3 ownership clause before the rung-19 workflow precondition. A specialist holding no `ACCEPTED` assignment on the study is therefore refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` whatever the study's `workflow_status` happens to be.

**Resuming** is the clinic's move, available to the `ClinicUser` whose `X-User-ID` matches the study's clinic. It requires `tat_status` to be `PAUSED`, moves the clock `PAUSED → ACCRUING`, adds the minutes of the now-closed pause window — counted in the study's accrual mode — to `tat_paused_minutes`, and clears both `pause_started_at` and `hold_reason` back to null. The resume payload may carry a `clinical_history` value; the scaffold declares the field on the request without declaring an effect for it, so it can be read either as supplementary text the server records or as an input the server accepts and discards, and this document does not choose between those readings. A study may be held and resumed any number of times, and `tat_paused_minutes` accumulates across all of them. A hold requested on a study whose `tat_status` is not `ACCRUING`, or a resume on one whose `tat_status` is not `PAUSED`, is refused with `ERR_TAT_CLOCK_STATE_INVALID`.

**STAT escalation** is the clinic's emergency lever and it is the most counter-intuitive operation in the system. It is available to the `ClinicUser` who owns the study, takes no body fields, and is permitted from **any non-terminal workflow state** — `ORDERED`, `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED`, `FINALIZED` and `REPEAT_PENDING` all admit it; only `CANCELLED` and `NOT_PERFORMED` do not, and those raise `ERR_STUDY_TERMINAL`. A study whose `priority` is already `STAT` raises `ERR_ALREADY_STAT`. A clinic on billing hold cannot escalate at all, because gate G2 guards this operation.

Escalation sets `priority` to `STAT`, sets `priority_source` to `UPGRADED`, stamps `escalated_at` with the logical clock, re-anchors `tat_anchor_at` to the study's `acquired_at`, re-targets `tat_target_minutes` to 60 wall minutes, adds the 4500-cent STAT surcharge to the study's price, and appends a notification of kind `STAT_UPGRADED`. Priority is a ratchet: it is raised to `STAT` and never lowered again.

The consequence is that **turnaround is not monotone in elapsed time**. Re-anchoring discards the time that passed before acquisition, but the target shrinks from 240 business minutes to 60 wall minutes, and for any study acquired more than 60 wall minutes ago the shrink is larger than the discard. Such a study is `BREACHED` the instant it is upgraded, with no time having passed at all: the clock jumps `ACCRUING → BREACHED` as a direct consequence of the escalation. The same jump happens from a paused clock — escalating while `PAUSED` moves the study `PAUSED → BREACHED` if the retargeted `tat_accrued_minutes` exceeds the retargeted target. The breach is written onto the same `tat_status` axis the pause was holding, so `PAUSED` is gone: `pause_started_at` is retained rather than cleared, but the clinic's later resume no longer finds the `PAUSED` state that rung 20 demands and is refused with `ERR_TAT_CLOCK_STATE_INVALID`. A study that breaches while held stays `BREACHED` and is never resumed.

### 3.9. Repeats, Cancellations, Pricing and the Money Trail

**Repeats.** When images have to be taken again, the `ClinicUser` who owns the study creates a replacement, supplying a single `repeat_cause` of `QC_FAILURE`, `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE` or `CLINICIAN_EXTRA_VIEWS`. A study may spawn at most two replacements: a source whose `repeat_count` has reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REPEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The stated cause must be supported by the source study's own condition — `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE` requires the study's acquiring equipment to be in `QUARANTINED`, `QC_FAILURE` requires the study's `qc_status` to be `REPEAT_REQUIRED` or `NON_DIAGNOSTIC`, and `CLINICIAN_EXTRA_VIEWS` requires the study to be `FINALIZED` with `qc_status` of `ACCEPTED` — and an unsupported claim is refused with `ERR_REPEAT_CAUSE_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Gate G2 refuses the whole operation for a clinic on billing hold.

A successful repeat creates a fresh study whose `repeat_of_study_id` points at the source, increments the source's `repeat_count` by 1, refreshes the appointment's disposition counters, and — when the source was sitting in `REPEAT_PENDING` — closes the source out by moving it `REPEAT_PENDING → NOT_PERFORMED` now that its replacement exists. **The cause decides who pays**, and it is a pure branch with no arithmetic: a repeat caused by `QC_FAILURE` or `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE` is provider fault and the replacement is created with `billing_status` of `NON_BILLABLE`, so its base price, trip share, surcharge and administrative fee are all 0, it never draws a block unit and it never joins its stop's trip-share allocation. A repeat caused by `CLINICIAN_EXTRA_VIEWS` is the clinician's own request: the identical verb produces a replacement with `billing_status` of `UNBILLED`, priced exactly like any first-time study, which does draw a block unit when one is available and does join its stop's trip-share allocation — thereby lowering every sibling's share.

**What a cancellation costs.** A study's `cancellation_fee_cents` is computed at three moments: when a clinic cancels the study directly, when a `ClinicUser` cancels the whole appointment, and when a technician records a no-show. It is priced off the **list** table, always, never off a contract's block rate, because a cancelled study draws no block unit: radiography 18000 cents, ultrasound 26000, CT 52000, echo 34000. The branches are evaluated in this order.

1. If the cause is provider-side — a stop bump, a stop cancellation, or an appointment moved to `CANCELLED_BY_PROVIDER` — the fee is 0, irrespective of lead time.
2. If the study's `study_kind` is `TELERAD`, the fee is 0: there is no stop, no lead time and no trip.
3. If the trigger is a no-show, the fee is 100 percent of the list price, rounded half up — that is, the list price itself.
4. Otherwise, take the stop's scheduled arrival instant to be midnight UTC on the route's `service_date` advanced by the stop's `planned_arrival_minute`, and take the **lead** to be the wall minutes from the logical clock's current value to that arrival instant.
   - If the stop's route is `DISPATCHED`, lead time is ignored entirely: the fee is 100 percent of list, **plus the stop's entire `trip_fee_cents`** when this was the last study still in `ORDERED` at that stop — that is, when the count of other studies at the stop still in `ORDERED`, excluding this one, is zero. The van is already out, so a cancellation twenty hours ahead of the printed arrival time is billed exactly like one made two minutes ahead.
   - Otherwise, when the lead is at least 1440 minutes — a full day — the fee is 0.
   - Otherwise, when the lead is at least 240 minutes, the fee is 50 percent of list, rounded half up.
   - Otherwise the fee is 100 percent of list.

Boundaries are inclusive on the at-least side: a lead of exactly 1440 minutes is free, and a lead of 1439 minutes costs half.

**Cancelling a study.** The owning `ClinicUser` cancels an ordered study with a `cancel_reason`, and the operation requires the study's `workflow_status` to be `ORDERED` and the study's route stop not to be `SERVICED`. The fee is computed by the rules above and stored on `cancellation_fee_cents`; the study moves `ORDERED → CANCELLED`; its `billing_status` is set to `NON_BILLABLE` when the computed fee is 0, and is **left at `UNBILLED` when the fee is positive**, so that the fee can later be posted as a cancellation charge line. The appointment's disposition counters are refreshed. There is a genuine tension in the source material about whether cancelling an ordered study moves any sibling's trip share, and this document renders both readings without choosing: on one reading, cancellation recomputes the trip shares of the surviving billable studies at the same stop and raises them, which is how the operation's own summary describes it; on the other, an `ORDERED` study was never a member of the stop's billable set — which begins at `ACQUIRED` — so re-deriving that set changes nothing and every sibling's share is left exactly where it was. The same tension attaches to marking a study not-performed from `ORDERED`, and to a `ClinicUser` cancelling an appointment.

**How a study is priced.** Six figures make up a study's price and their evaluation order is **pinned**, because the dependency looks circular: the administrative fee depends on the trip share, the trip share depends on which studies at the stop are billable, and billability depends on whether a study was cancelled — which is itself priced off the list table rather than off the contract's block rate.

1. Resolve the study's clinic's single contract whose computed `status` is `ACTIVE` and read its `block_remaining_studies`. When no such contract exists, posting fails with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. (The scaffold attaches that failure to posting; it does not state what the read-only pricing view answers for a clinic with no active contract, and this document leaves that open rather than inventing a behaviour.)
2. Compute `base_price_cents` and, with it, `price_basis`. When the study's `billing_status` is `NON_BILLABLE`, `price_basis` is `NON_BILLABLE` and the base price is 0. Otherwise, when the contract's `kind` is `PREPAID_BLOCK` **and** its `block_remaining_studies` is greater than 0, `price_basis` is `BLOCK_RATE` and the base price is the block rate for the study's modality: 14000 cents for radiography, 20000 for ultrasound, 42000 for CT, 27000 for echo. Otherwise — a per-study-list contract, or a prepaid-block contract with nothing left in the block — `price_basis` is `LIST_PRICE` and the base price is the list rate for the modality: 18000, 26000, 52000 and 34000 cents respectively. The boundary is exact: with one study left in the block the next study prices at the block rate, and the study after it, with the block at zero, prices at list. Identical study, two different numbers, decided by a counter.
3. Compute `trip_share_cents`. A telerad study's share is structurally 0 — there is no route stop, no van and no trip to allocate. For a mobile study, form the stop's **billable set**: every study whose appointment is bound to that route stop, whose `workflow_status` is one of `ACQUIRED`, `ASSIGNED`, `READ_IN_PROGRESS`, `PRELIM_REPORTED` or `FINALIZED`, and whose `billing_status` is not `NON_BILLABLE`, sorted by `created_at` ascending. Studies in `ORDERED`, `CANCELLED`, `NOT_PERFORMED` or `REPEAT_PENDING` are excluded, as are non-billable ones. If that set is empty, no trip fee is billed at all and the share is 0; if the study being priced is not itself in the set, its share is 0. Otherwise let the set's size be the divisor: the base share is the stop's `trip_fee_cents` divided by that size and rounded **down** to a whole cent, and the leftover — the trip fee less the base share multiplied by the size — is handed out one extra cent at a time to the earliest-created members. Concretely, a stop with three billable studies allocates 4167, 4167 and 4166 cents, never 4167 three times; the rounding order is pinned this way precisely so that the allocation sums to exactly the stop's single 12500-cent trip fee and the invoice-money invariant holds.
4. Compute `stat_surcharge_cents`: 4500 cents when the study's current `priority` is `STAT` — whether natively or by upgrade — and 0 otherwise.
5. Compute `admin_fee_cents`, strictly **after** the trip share is allocated: 3 percent of the sum of `base_price_cents`, `trip_share_cents` and `stat_surcharge_cents`, rounded half up. That is **one** rounding applied to the study's subtotal, not a sum of separately rounded per-component fees.
6. `total_price_cents` is the plain sum of `base_price_cents`, `trip_share_cents`, `stat_surcharge_cents` and `admin_fee_cents`. `cancellation_fee_cents` is **never** part of that sum; a cancelled study is priced solely by the cancellation rules above.

The trip-fee split is where this system's pricing stops being monotone. Two billable studies at a stop each carry 6250 cents of trip share. Take one of them out of the billable set — the reachable way is an image-QC verdict of `REPEAT_REQUIRED`, which moves it to `REPEAT_PENDING` — and the survivor's share **rises** to 12500, carrying its administrative fee and its `total_price_cents` up with it. Removing work makes the remaining work cost more, because the trip fee is no longer shared. Any implementation that caches a study's `trip_share_cents` at acquisition time gets this wrong.

A read-only pricing view exposes the whole decomposition for one study without posting anything. It answers with `price_basis`, `base_price_cents`, `trip_share_cents`, `stat_surcharge_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `total_price_cents`, `cancellation_fee_cents` and `billable_studies_at_stop`, the last being the size of the billable set the trip share was divided across.

**Invoices and charge lines.** A `BillingAdmin` opens a billing period for a clinic by naming the `clinic_id` and the inclusive date range `period_start` through `period_end`. The invoice is created in `OPEN` with `total_cents` at 0, `posted_study_count` at 0, and `finalized_total_cents` and `finalized_at` both null.

Posting a study to an invoice names the `study_id` and the `expected_invoice_version` required by §2.7. Beyond concurrency and gate G1, five checks apply in ladder order: the clinic must have exactly one contract computing to `ACTIVE`; the study's clinic must be the invoice's `clinic_id`, or `ERR_CLINIC_MISMATCH`; the study's billable date must fall inside the invoice's inclusive period — its `acquired_at` date for an ordinary posting, or its `updated_at` date for a cancellation-fee-only posting — or `ERR_STUDY_OUTSIDE_INVOICE_PERIOD`; the study must be postable, meaning its `workflow_status` is `FINALIZED` or its `cancellation_fee_cents` is greater than 0 for a fee-only posting, or `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FINALIZED`; a study whose `billing_status` is `NON_BILLABLE` may never be posted, and the attempt raises `ERR_STUDY_NON_BILLABLE`; and a study whose `billing_status` is already `POSTED` raises `ERR_STUDY_ALREADY_POSTED` until its lines have been reversed.

An ordinary posting creates up to four charge lines against the study, each carrying an `invoice_id`, a `study_id`, a `kind`, an `amount_cents` and a `status` that begins at `ACTIVE` with a null `reversal_reason`: one line of kind `STUDY_READ` for the base price; one of kind `TRIP_FEE_SHARE` when the study's `trip_share_cents` is greater than 0; one of kind `STAT_SURCHARGE` when the study's `priority` is `STAT`; and one of kind `ADMIN_FEE`. A cancellation-fee-only posting instead creates a single line of kind `CANCELLATION_FEE`. The study's billing axis moves `UNBILLED → POSTED`, and the invoice recomputes `total_cents` and `posted_study_count` — the latter being the number of distinct studies carrying at least one `ACTIVE` line on that invoice.

Posting is also where a prepaid block is drawn down. When the clinic's contract has `kind` of `PREPAID_BLOCK`, its `block_remaining_studies` is greater than 0, the study's `billing_status` is not `NON_BILLABLE`, and the posting is a full read posting rather than a cancellation-fee-only one, the server appends a block ledger entry naming the `contract_id` and the `study_id`, with `delta_studies` of negative 1, a `reason` of `DRAW`, and a `balance_after_studies` equal to the contract's remaining balance less one. The contract's `block_consumed_studies` rises by 1 and its `block_remaining_studies` falls by 1. Block ledger entries are append-only historical facts carrying only an `id` and a `created_at` — no `updated_at`, no `version` — and the contract's two derived counters are always re-derivable by replaying that ledger in `created_at` ascending order. Because postings are processed one request at a time, the draw order is exactly the order in which posting requests arrive. The boundary is greater-than-zero: with one study left in the block the study draws, and the next one, finding zero, does not draw and is priced at list.

The billing axis is re-entrant. A study that has been posted can be driven `POSTED → REVERSED` by a cascade — the equipment-quarantine cascade of §3.6 or the stop-bump cascade of §3.3 — provided the owning invoice is still `OPEN`. Every `ACTIVE` charge line for that study moves to `REVERSED` with a `reversal_reason` recorded, and when a `DRAW` entry exists for that study a matching block ledger entry with `delta_studies` of positive 1 and `reason` of `REFUND_QC` is appended, restoring the contract's balance. A reversed study can then be posted again, on the same invoice or a different `OPEN` one, moving `REVERSED → POSTED` and creating a fresh set of `ACTIVE` lines; the reversed lines stay on the invoice for audit and keep their `amount_cents` but are excluded from the total. The fourth value on this axis, `NON_BILLABLE`, is terminal and is reached by a cancellation with a zero fee, by a stop bump, or by creating a repeat whose cause is `QC_FAILURE` or `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINE`.

An invoice's `total_cents` behaves differently on either side of finalization. While the invoice is `OPEN` it is re-derived on every read as the plain sum of `amount_cents` over that invoice's `ACTIVE` lines, so a cascade reversal changes it immediately; it is a sum of already-rounded line amounts and never a re-rounding of an unrounded aggregate. Finalizing the invoice moves it `OPEN → FINALIZED`, snapshots the live total into `finalized_total_cents`, stamps `finalized_at`, and thereafter `total_cents` simply returns that frozen snapshot. Gate G1 then refuses any later write that would create, re-price or reverse a line on that invoice, so the live and frozen readings can never disagree by accident — they disagree only because finalization deliberately stopped the clock.

**Notifications.** Every clinic-visible event appends an append-only `Notification` carrying the `clinic_id` it is addressed to, an optional `study_id` — null for stop-level notices that concern no single study — a `kind`, and a human-readable `message`. Notifications have an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else: no `updated_at` and no `version`. Six kinds exist and each has exactly one origin described elsewhere in this section: `STOP_BUMPED` when a STAT insertion displaces a routine stop, `APPOINTMENT_CANCELLED` once per appointment cancelled by a stop bump, a stop cancellation or a provider-side appointment cancellation, `STUDY_REPEAT_REQUIRED` on an image-QC verdict that sends a study to `REPEAT_PENDING` and once per study condemned by an equipment quarantine, `REPORT_PUBLISHED` on every report publication, `TAT_BREACHED` the first time a study enters `BREACHED`, and `STAT_UPGRADED` on a STAT escalation. Returning a referral to the clinic for more information appends **no** notification at all.
