# Airline Passenger Rights & Refund Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airline's customer compliance office sits between the operations centre and the regulator. When a flight is cancelled, when a bag arrives a day and a half after its owner, when a paid seat assignment evaporates in an equipment swap, or when an oversold cabin forces a passenger off the aircraft, the carrier owes money — and it owes that money on a clock, in a currency the passenger is entitled to choose, whether or not the passenger ever thinks to ask. Today that obligation is tracked in spreadsheets stitched together from operational feeds, and the office discovers a missed deadline only when the regulator asks about it. The product described here replaces that patchwork with a single system of record for passenger-rights compliance.

The system has three jobs. First, it must **detect** obligations: a cancellation, a significant arrival delay, a late-delivered bag, an undelivered ancillary service and a denied boarding each mint refund or compensation obligations automatically, without waiting for a passenger request, because proactive issuance is the legal standard rather than a courtesy. Second, it must **value and settle** them: every obligation carries a computed principal, a payment deadline derived from the form of payment originally collected, an election window in which the passenger may choose cash or travel credit, and a two-person settlement chain in which no single officer can both create and pay a refund. Third, it must **report**: tarmac-delay occupations, disability-accommodation complaints and consumer complaints all carry statutory response deadlines, and the office files periodic reports whose metrics must be frozen at submission and amendable afterwards without losing the audit trail.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. Money is conserved: the total the carrier admits it owes on a booking is always exactly the sum of that booking's live obligations, and a compensating void must return the ledger to the value it held before the obligation was minted — no residual drift. And defaults never favour the carrier: travel credit is a choice the passenger makes, never a choice the system makes on their behalf, so an elapsed election window resolves to cash even when the carrier's own configuration would permit otherwise.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every route in this specification is mounted under the `/compliance` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §2.8. The subsections below define the contracts that hold across all of them.

### 2.1. Compliance Office Roles and Caller Identity

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the caller is acting under. If either header is missing or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the five recognised roles, which are spelled exactly: `ComplianceAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `OpsController`, `RegulatorAuditor`, and `Passenger`. A recognised role that is simply not authorised for the endpoint it called is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403); §4 lists the authorised roles for each endpoint.

A `ComplianceAdmin` is the head of the customer compliance office. The role owns policy configuration, obligation voids, tarmac contingency-plan filings, regulator reports, the compliance sweep and the logical clock, and it may additionally perform every `ComplianceOfficer` and `OpsController` write as an override — with one deliberate exception. Initiating settlement is reserved to `ComplianceOfficer` alone, so that no single person can drive an obligation from creation through payment. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the case worker: they mint obligations, record elections, initiate settlements and work consumer complaints, but they cannot void an obligation, cannot file a tarmac report, cannot submit a regulator filing, and cannot complete a settlement they themselves initiated. An `OpsController` owns operational truth about flights — segment creation, disruption declarations, departure/arrival/restore events and tarmac occupation records — and has no authority over money, complaints or filings. A `RegulatorAuditor` is strictly read-only across the entire surface, including filed reports and the complaint activity log, and holds no write capability of any kind.

For `ComplianceAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `OpsController` and `RegulatorAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule demands an actor trail — as `created_by_user_id` and `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` on a refund obligation, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a regulator report, and as `actor_user_id` on a complaint activity row — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. For `Passenger`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting passenger record, the value returned when that passenger was registered; ownership checks on any resource that references a passenger compare the resource's passenger reference to `X-User-ID`.

Passenger ownership is enforced as follows. When `X-User-Role` is `Passenger`, reading a passenger requires that the addressed passenger id equal `X-User-ID`; reading bookings — the collection, a single booking, or any of its sub-resource collections for flight segments, checked bags, consumer complaints — requires that the booking's `passenger_id` equal `X-User-ID`; reading refund obligations, whether as a list or singly, and electing on one, require that the obligation's booking belong to that passenger; and filing a consumer complaint requires that the `booking_id` in the body resolve to a booking whose `passenger_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check runs **before** the existence checks of §2.5, so that a passenger cannot use 404-versus-403 responses to probe another passenger's resource ids.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset` or a non-integer `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list endpoint's description in §4 states a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The four pagination fields — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — appear in the `meta` object of every list response, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, narrow the result set before pagination is applied. Bookings may be filtered by `passenger_id`, `policy_profile_id` and `disruption_posture`. A booking's segment list accepts `status`; its checked-bag list accepts `status`; its complaint list accepts `category` and `status`. A segment's tarmac-event list accepts `status` and `phase`. Ancillary purchases may be filtered by `booking_id`, `flight_segment_id`, `service_type` and `delivery_status`. Denied boarding records may be filtered by `booking_id`, `flight_segment_id`, `category` and `status`. Refund obligations may be filtered by `booking_id`, by any of the four source references `flight_segment_id`, `checked_bag_id`, `ancillary_purchase_id` and `denied_boarding_record_id`, and by `basis`, `issuance_mode`, `election_status` and `settlement_status`. A complaint's activity log accepts `activity_type`. A policy profile's report list accepts `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every resource is identified by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix; clients never supply an `id` for a resource they are creating. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was written.

All entities except the complaint activity trail additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each request that changes the row's persisted field values. The complaint activity trail is append-only and immutable: an activity row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because nothing ever modifies it.

`version` is not merely informational. It is the token clients present when writing against a ledger parent (§2.6), and it is the value the regulator report pins at submission time. Whenever a request advances a row's `version`, it also refreshes that row's `updated_at`. A request that recomputes a booking's derived ledger fields advances `Booking.version` exactly once for the whole request, however many rows the cascade touched on the way — a cancellation that suspends three sibling segments, voids two ancillary services and mints three obligations still advances the booking's `version` by 1, not by 8.

### 2.5. Request Validation Precedence

Because many requests can fail for several reasons at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following sequence, and the first failure determines the response:

1. **Header presence and role recognition** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Endpoint authorisation** — the caller's role must appear among the endpoint's authorised roles; otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Passenger ownership** — the §2.1 ownership rules; otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence** — every id embedded in the request path must resolve to a stored row, and path ids are resolved strictly left to right, so the outer segment's 404 wins over the inner one's. Each entity has its own code: a policy profile yields `ERR_POLICY_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, a passenger `ERR_PASSENGER_NOT_FOUND`, a booking `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, a flight segment `ERR_FLIGHT_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an ancillary purchase `ERR_ANCILLARY_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND`, a checked bag `ERR_CHECKED_BAG_NOT_FOUND`, a denied boarding record `ERR_DENIED_BOARDING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, a tarmac delay event `ERR_TARMAC_DELAY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, a refund obligation `ERR_REFUND_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, a consumer complaint `ERR_CONSUMER_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, and a regulator report `ERR_REGULATOR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. All are HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence** — every foreign-key id supplied in the request body must resolve to a stored row, checked in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint, using the same per-entity codes as the previous step.
6. **Request shape** — the body must carry every required field for the endpoint, must carry no unknown fields, must use the correct JSON type for each field, must keep every enum-typed value inside its declared value set, must express cent amounts as non-negative integers, and must express every timestamp as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. Format rules apply here too: `carrier_code` is exactly two uppercase alphanumeric characters, `record_locator` is exactly six uppercase alphanumeric characters, `tag_number` is exactly ten characters, `sequence_index` is at least 1, `origin_iata` and `destination_iata` are each exactly three uppercase letters, `flight_number` is between 3 and 7 characters, `email` must be syntactically valid and contain an at-sign, `full_name` is between 1 and 120 characters, and a complaint `summary` is between 1 and 500 characters. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
7. **Pagination bounds** — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, as described in §2.3.
8. **Conditionally required fields** — fields that become mandatory because of another field's value, rejected with HTTP 422. Reporting a failed settlement outcome without a `failure_reason` yields `ERR_FAILURE_REASON_REQUIRED`. Answering a complaint with a refund-granted disposition without a `granted_amount_cents` of at least 1 yields `ERR_GRANT_AMOUNT_REQUIRED`. Resolving a checked bag as delivered without `delivered_at_utc`, resolving it as lost without `loss_declared_at_utc`, and declaring a delay disruption without `revised_arrival_utc` each yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
9. **Ledger-parent version match** — the optimistic-concurrency guards of §2.6, rejected with HTTP 412.
10. **State of the addressed resource** — the row named in the path must be in a state from which the requested transition exists. Per entity: `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID` for a flight segment, `ERR_ANCILLARY_STATE_INVALID` for an ancillary purchase, `ERR_BAG_STATE_INVALID` for a checked bag, `ERR_DENIED_BOARDING_STATE_INVALID` for a denied boarding record, `ERR_TARMAC_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` for a tarmac delay event, `ERR_OBLIGATION_STATE_INVALID` for a refund obligation whose election or settlement axis is wrong for the requested action, `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` for a consumer complaint, and `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` for a regulator report. All are HTTP 409.
11. **Action-specific state gates**, in this order: the election prerequisite on settlement initiation (`ERR_ELECTION_REQUIRED`), the void-reason compatibility rule (`ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_VOIDABLE`), and the worsening requirement on a re-declared delay (`ERR_DELAY_NOT_WORSENED`). All are HTTP 409.
12. **Cross-entity state gates**, in this order: the source-disruption check on settlement initiation (`ERR_SOURCE_DISRUPTION_RESCINDED`), the open-complaint check on report submission (`ERR_OPEN_COMPLAINT_IN_PERIOD`), and the operationally-closed-out check on tarmac report filing (`ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`). All are HTTP 409.
13. **Regulatory safety gates**, in this order: segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403), the officer credit-consent requirement (`ERR_CREDIT_CONSENT_REQUIRED`), the disability resolution-code requirement (`ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_RESOLUTION_REQUIRED`), the voluntary disclosure floor (`ERR_VOLUNTARY_BELOW_DISCLOSURE_FLOOR`), and the corrective-action requirement on a violating tarmac report (`ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`) — the last four all HTTP 422.
14. **Temporal gates**, in this order: `ERR_TARMAC_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_AFTER_DEADLINE`, `ERR_DELIVERY_BEFORE_CHECKIN`, `ERR_ANSWER_BEFORE_RECEIPT`, `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_BEFORE_SCHEDULED` and `ERR_REVISED_ARRIVAL_INVALID`. All are HTTP 422.
15. **Uniqueness, overlap and limit constraints**, in this order: `ERR_TARMAC_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then the uniqueness family (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CARRIER_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSENGER_EMAIL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD_LOCATOR`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEQUENCE_INDEX`), then `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION_FOR_SOURCE`, then `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. All are HTTP 409.
16. **Referential consistency**, in this order: `ERR_SEGMENT_BOOKING_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BASIS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` and `ERR_CONSENT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. All are HTTP 422.
17. **Fiscal checks, evaluated last** — `ERR_NO_REFUND_HEADROOM` and `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_HEADROOM`, both HTTP 422. Money is the last thing checked, so a request that is both malformed and unaffordable reports the malformation.

### 2.6. Ledger-Parent Version Guards

Two write operations require the client to prove that it is acting on a current view of a parent record, because in both cases the value the client is about to claim depends on aggregate state that other writers move.

Minting a refund obligation requires the non-optional field `expected_booking_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target booking. If that value does not equal the booking's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is fiscal: the amount a request-based obligation may claim is bounded by the booking's remaining refund headroom (§2.7), and that headroom moves every time a disruption cascade mints a proactive obligation or an administrator voids one. A client whose view of the booking predates such a movement would compute its claim against superseded totals, so the stale view is refused outright rather than silently clamped to whatever is left.

Opening a regulator report requires the non-optional field `expected_policy_profile_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target policy profile. A mismatch with the profile's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The compliance sweep rewrites the profile's `open_violation_count` and thereby advances the profile's `version`, and every prior report creation increments `report_count` and advances it again; a client holding an older profile version is provably filing against a superseded compliance picture, so the stale version is refused rather than accepted with recomputed counts.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, none of the action endpoints that advance a state machine — declaring a disruption, resolving a bag, finalizing a denied boarding record, electing, initiating or completing a settlement, voiding, working a complaint, submitting or amending a report — accepts or requires an expected-version input.

### 2.7. Money, Minutes and Boundary Conventions

All monetary quantities in this system are integer cents. Field names ending in `_cents` are always whole numbers; no monetary field is ever fractional, and no monetary sum performs intermediate rounding — integer additions are exact. Wherever a rule multiplies a money amount by a percentage or divides it, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent, and the rounding is applied at the point the rule states, never deferred. Two rules pin their rounding order explicitly and must not be reordered: the per-segment proration of a fare (§3.6) rounds the per-segment share first and multiplies afterwards, and the statutory denied-boarding ladder (§3.4) rounds the fare multiple before comparing it to the statutory cap.

Elapsed time appears in three shapes. Minutes are whole numbers obtained by taking the difference between two instants in seconds and truncating toward zero — that is, flooring — so a difference of 179 minutes and 59 seconds is 179 minutes. Hours appear only as the baggage delivery delay, `delivery_delay_hours`, which is computed from the raw second difference and rounded half up to two decimal places. Days appear only as deadline offsets, and day arithmetic is exact 24-hour addition on the UTC instant: there is no calendar-month logic, no business-day skipping, and no truncation to midnight, so a deadline of seven days after an instant falls at the identical time of day seven days later.

Boundary inclusivity varies by rule and is deliberate. A significant delay is determined with an at-or-above comparison, so an arrival delay of exactly the configured threshold **is** significant. A baggage delivery delay is likewise compared at-or-above, so a delay of exactly 12.00 hours on a domestic itinerary **is** late. A tarmac deplaning violation, by contrast, uses a strict comparison: occupying the tarmac for exactly the deadline number of minutes is compliant, and one minute beyond it is a violation. Deadline breach flags — the refund settlement deadline, the complaint acknowledgement deadline and the complaint answer deadline — all use strict comparisons against the logical clock, so at the exact deadline instant nothing is yet breached. Reporting periods are inclusive on both ends, while a tarmac event's occupation interval is inclusive at its start and exclusive at its end; the two geometries differ on purpose, which is why two consecutive reporting periods may not share a boundary instant while two consecutive tarmac events on the same segment may.

### 2.8. The Logical Compliance Clock

Every deadline, election window, breach flag and auto-close decision in this system is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that compliance behaviour is reproducible. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. A `ComplianceAdmin` may move the clock to an explicit instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and advances no `version` — it only changes the instant that every subsequent deadline, breach, election-window and auto-close evaluation reads.

Note the division of labour between the clock and request payloads. Domain instants that describe when something happened in the world — when a disruption was declared, when a bag was delivered, when a complaint was received or answered — are supplied by the caller in the request body, because the compliance office frequently records them after the fact. Instants that describe when the system itself acted — `settlement_initiated_at_utc` and `settled_at_utc` — are taken from the logical clock at the moment of the call.

### 2.9. The Policy Profile as the Tunable Rulebook

Thresholds the compliance office is allowed to tune live on a **policy profile**, one per carrier, pinned to each booking so that a change of policy never retroactively rewrites an itinerary already in flight. A profile is identified to the outside world by its `carrier_code`, which must be unique across all profiles. Every remaining configuration field is optional at creation and takes a regulatory default when omitted: `domestic_significant_delay_minutes` defaults to 180 (three hours) and `international_significant_delay_minutes` to 360 (six hours); `credit_card_refund_deadline_days` defaults to 7 and `other_method_refund_deadline_days` to 20; `bag_delay_threshold_hours_domestic` defaults to 12 and `bag_delay_threshold_hours_international` to 30; `tarmac_deplane_deadline_minutes_domestic` defaults to 180 and `tarmac_deplane_deadline_minutes_international` to 240 (three and four hours respectively); `complaint_ack_deadline_days` defaults to 30, `complaint_answer_deadline_days_general` to 60 and `complaint_answer_deadline_days_disability` to 30; `travel_credit_default_permitted` defaults to false; and `travel_credit_election_bonus_pct` defaults to 0.10. Each of the integer thresholds must be at least 1, and the bonus fraction must be at least 0 and at most 0.5.

A profile is configuration, not a workspace: it is created once and is never edited through the public surface. Its two computed counters nonetheless move. `report_count` increases by 1 each time a regulator report is opened against the profile, and `open_violation_count` is rewritten by the compliance sweep (§3.9). Both start at 0, and the profile's `version` starts at 1 and advances whenever either counter's value changes — which is precisely why report creation is guarded by a version check (§2.6).

Statutory constants that the carrier may **not** tune are not represented on the profile at all; they are fixed in the rules that use them, and are stated in §3.4 (the denied-boarding ladder), §3.6 (the five-day election window) and §3.9 (the complaint auto-close window and reopen cap).

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties on the Money Chain

Paying a refund takes two different people. When a settlement is initiated, `X-User-ID` must differ from the obligation's `created_by_user_id` — the officer who minted a request-based obligation may not be the one who starts moving the money. When a settlement outcome is reported, `X-User-ID` must differ from the obligation's `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` — the officer who started the payment may not be the one who confirms it succeeded or failed. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Both comparisons are against persisted actor ids, so they hold across sessions rather than only within one. `created_by_user_id` is written from `X-User-ID` when an officer mints an obligation directly and is left null for obligations minted by a system cascade, which means a cascade-created obligation can be initiated by anyone authorised. `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` is written on each initiation and cleared back to null when a settlement fails, so the officer who initiated a failed attempt is free to initiate the retry — but whoever initiates that retry is then barred from completing it. Because `ComplianceAdmin` deliberately lacks the initiate-settlement capability, the two-person chain cannot be collapsed by a single administrator using override authority.

### 2.11. Conserved Ledgers and Cascade Discipline

Two quantities in this system are conserved exactly, with zero tolerance, and both must hold at the end of every request.

The first is the booking's obligated-money ledger. A booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents` must always equal the sum of `amount_cents` over that booking's refund obligations whose settlement axis is anything other than voided, summed in `created_at` ascending order. The practical consequence is compensating symmetry: if an obligation of some amount is minted and then voided, the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents` after the void equals its value before the create, exactly, with no residual drift, and the booking's `disruption_posture` returns to the value it held before the create. Voiding never deletes a row — the row persists with a voided settlement axis and a recorded `void_reason`, and is excluded from the sum.

The second is the passenger's travel-credit balance. A passenger's `travel_credit_balance_cents` must always equal the sum of `payable_total_cents` over every obligation on that passenger's bookings whose settlement method is travel credit and whose settlement axis reads settled. It is never negative. Here too the symmetry is exact: a credit obligation that is settled and then reversed leaves the balance at precisely its pre-settlement value, including the goodwill bonus portion. The two conserved quantities are genuinely different — the first tracks money the carrier still owes, the second tracks credit already issued — and the goodwill bonus is inside the second while being deliberately outside the first.

Cascade discipline supports both. When one operation touches several rows, the writes happen in a fixed order, siblings are processed in a deterministic order (`sequence_index` ascending for flight segments, `created_at` ascending everywhere else), and the parent booking's derived fields are recomputed once, at the end, from the rows that exist at that moment — including rows written earlier in the same request. Those derived booking fields are: `itinerary_scope`, which reads `INTERNATIONAL` when at least one of the booking's segments is flagged international and `DOMESTIC` otherwise; `segment_count`, the number of segments on the booking; `unused_segment_count`, the number of those segments whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED`, `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED_UNUSED`; `travel_commenced`, true when at least one segment's status is `DEPARTED` or `ARRIVED`; `collected_total_cents`, the booking's `total_fare_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` over all of its ancillary purchases plus the sum of `fee_cents` over all of its checked bags, counting every such row regardless of its delivery outcome; `total_refund_obligated_cents` as defined above; `earliest_refund_deadline_utc`, the smallest `deadline_utc` among the booking's obligations whose settlement axis is neither settled nor voided, null when that set is empty, with ties resolved by `created_at` ascending and never by `id`; and `disruption_posture`. The posture is resolved in this order: it is `NORMAL` when the booking has no segments at all; otherwise, if the booking has at least one non-voided obligation, it is `CLOSED_OUT` when every obligation of the booking is settled or voided and `REFUND_OBLIGATED` when at least one is not; otherwise it is `DISRUPTED` when any of the booking's segments carries a disruption type other than none; otherwise `NORMAL`. One further booking counter, `suppressed_obligation_count`, is not recomputed at all — it is a running total incremented by cascades that wanted to mint a proactive obligation and could not, and it only ever grows.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Compliance Record

Before any obligation can exist, the office needs a passenger, an itinerary and the legs that make it up. Registering a passenger records an `email`, which doubles as the natural key and must be unique across all passengers — a second registration of the same address is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSENGER_EMAIL` — a `full_name` as printed on the ticket, and optionally `requires_disability_assistance`, which defaults to false and marks the traveller as having requested accommodation on file. A newly registered passenger starts with `travel_credit_balance_cents` at 0 and `open_complaint_count` at 0; the latter is maintained as the count of that passenger's complaints, across all of their bookings, whose status is `RECEIVED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. The `id` returned by registration is the value the traveller presents as `X-User-ID` when acting under the `Passenger` role.

A booking is one ticketed itinerary. It names its traveller through `passenger_id` and pins its rulebook through `policy_profile_id`, so that the thresholds in force are the ones that were current when the ticket was sold. It carries a `record_locator` — a six-character locator, unique across all bookings, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD_LOCATOR` — a `total_fare_cents` of at least 1 representing the fare actually collected for the whole itinerary excluding ancillaries and bag fees, and a `payment_method` drawn from `CREDIT_CARD`, `CASH_OR_CHECK`, `TRAVEL_CREDIT` and `MILES`. The form of payment matters far downstream: it selects the settlement deadline and it constrains the instrument a refund can be paid on. A fresh booking begins with `itinerary_scope` reading `DOMESTIC`, `collected_total_cents` equal to its `total_fare_cents`, `total_refund_obligated_cents` at 0, `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` null, `disruption_posture` reading `NORMAL`, `segment_count` and `unused_segment_count` at 0, `travel_commenced` false and `suppressed_obligation_count` at 0.

Legs are added to the booking one at a time, each at an explicit `sequence_index` that is at least 1 and unique within the booking — a repeat index is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEQUENCE_INDEX`. The index is not decoration: it is the itinerary's travel order and therefore the propagation order of every cascade that walks a booking's legs. Each leg records a `flight_number` of 3 to 7 characters, an `origin_iata` and a `destination_iata`, an `is_international` flag defaulting to false, a `scheduled_departure_utc` and a `scheduled_arrival_utc` that must be strictly later than the departure, and a `segment_fare_cents` of at least 0 representing the fare value the carrier's own proration table allocates to that leg. Those per-leg allocations need not add up to the booking's `total_fare_cents`, and the difference between the two views is exactly what makes the twin valuation rules of §3.6 diverge. A newly added leg's `booking_id` is the booking it was added under, its `status` reads `SCHEDULED`, its `disruption_type` reads `NONE`, its `delay_minutes` is 0, its `is_significant_delay` is false, and its `cumulative_tarmac_minutes` and `exempted_tarmac_event_count` are both 0. Adding a leg immediately refreshes the booking's `segment_count`, `unused_segment_count` and `itinerary_scope` — one international leg makes the whole itinerary international — and advances the booking's `version` by 1.

### 3.2. Declaring Disruption and the Cancellation Cascade

An `OpsController` (or a `ComplianceAdmin` acting as override) declares disruption on a leg by supplying a `disruption_type` of either `DELAY` or `CANCELLATION` together with the `declared_at_utc` instant of the declaration. A declaration is only accepted on a leg whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED`; on a leg that has already been cancelled, has departed or has arrived, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`. As a matter of record-keeping practice the office declares disruption within a window running from 30 days before the leg's `scheduled_departure_utc` to 7 days after its `scheduled_arrival_utc`. Nothing requires the declaration instant to fall after the scheduled departure — a disruption may legitimately be declared in advance.

A `DELAY` declaration must carry `revised_arrival_utc`, and omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The revised arrival must be strictly later than the leg's `scheduled_arrival_utc`, and a value at or before it is rejected with `ERR_REVISED_ARRIVAL_INVALID`. Declaring a delay drives the leg `SCHEDULED → DELAYED`, sets `disruption_type` to `DELAY`, records `revised_arrival_utc`, stamps `disruption_declared_at_utc` from the supplied `declared_at_utc`, and advances the leg's `version` by 1. Along the way it recomputes two derived facts. `delay_minutes` is the difference between `revised_arrival_utc` and `scheduled_arrival_utc`, expressed in whole minutes with the remainder dropped. `is_significant_delay` is then true when `delay_minutes` is at or above the threshold that governs this leg — the international threshold from the pinned policy profile when the leg's own `is_international` flag is true, and the domestic threshold otherwise. Note that it is the **per-leg** flag that selects the threshold, not the booking-level `itinerary_scope`, so a domestic leg sitting inside an international itinerary is measured against the three-hour domestic figure.

Whether a delay costs the carrier money follows directly. When `is_significant_delay` becomes true and no non-voided fare obligation already points at this leg, the declaration mints one refund obligation for the leg on a `FARE` basis in proactive issuance mode, valued by the proactive full-itinerary rule of §3.6, and then recomputes the booking's ledger fields and advances the booking's `version` by 1. When `is_significant_delay` comes out false, no obligation is created; the booking's `disruption_posture` is still recomputed — reaching `DISRUPTED` when the booking has no obligations at all — and the booking's `version` still advances by 1.

Delays get worse, and a leg already in `DELAYED` may be re-declared. Such a re-declaration is a self-cycle `DELAYED → DELAYED` and requires a `revised_arrival_utc` strictly later than the one already stored, so that the recomputed `delay_minutes` is strictly greater than the stored value; walking a delay backwards is refused with `ERR_DELAY_NOT_WORSENED`. On re-declaration `delay_minutes` and `is_significant_delay` are recomputed, and if the delay newly crosses the threshold a fare obligation is minted exactly as on the first declaration. If a fare obligation already exists for the leg, its `amount_cents` is **not** recomputed — the first valuation stands.

A delay can also be walked back entirely. Recording the `RESTORED` operation on a leg drives it `DELAYED → SCHEDULED`, and is accepted only when the leg's `status` is `DELAYED` and its `disruption_type` is `DELAY`; otherwise `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Restoration clears `disruption_type` back to `NONE`, sets `revised_arrival_utc` to null, resets `delay_minutes` to 0 and `is_significant_delay` to false, sets `disruption_declared_at_utc` to null, and advances the leg's `version` by 1; the booking's `disruption_posture` and `unused_segment_count` are recomputed and the booking's `version` advances by 1. Crucially, restoration does **not** void any obligation the delay produced. The obligation survives, and it is instead blocked at the settlement gate described in §3.7 — the carrier must decide explicitly whether to pay it or void it, rather than having the row silently disappear.

Cancellation is the deepest cascade in the system, and its links run in a fixed order. A `CANCELLATION` declaration must **not** carry `revised_arrival_utc`. **Link one:** the leg moves to `CANCELLED`, its `disruption_type` is set to `CANCELLATION`, its `disruption_declared_at_utc` takes the supplied `declared_at_utc`, and its `version` advances by 1; if the leg was in `DELAYED`, its `delay_minutes` resets to 0, its `revised_arrival_utc` to null and its `is_significant_delay` to false. **Link two-a,** caused by link one: every sibling leg of the same booking with a `sequence_index` greater than the cancelled leg's and a `status` of `SCHEDULED` becomes `DISRUPTED_UNUSED`, processed in ascending `sequence_index` order. Each such sibling keeps its `disruption_type` at `NONE` but copies `disruption_declared_at_utc` from the cancelled leg, so that an obligation naming a downstream sibling still has a well-defined clock start. **Link two-b,** also caused by link one: every ancillary purchase attached to the cancelled leg whose `delivery_status` is `PENDING_DELIVERY` becomes `NOT_PROVIDED` with `non_provision_reason` set to `SERVICE_CANCELLED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. **Link three-a,** caused by link one: one refund obligation on a `FARE` basis in proactive issuance mode, naming the cancelled leg, is minted per the proactive full-itinerary valuation and clamped to the booking's remaining headroom; if the clamp yields zero the obligation is skipped and the booking's `suppressed_obligation_count` increases by 1 instead. **Link three-b,** caused by link two-b: one refund obligation on an `ANCILLARY` basis in proactive issuance mode at fee face value is minted for each ancillary that just became `NOT_PROVIDED`, in the same ascending `created_at` order, each clamped against the headroom left by every obligation created earlier in this same request; each such ancillary's `refund_obligation_created` becomes true. An ancillary whose clamp yields zero is skipped instead: no obligation row is written, that ancillary's `refund_obligation_created` stays false, and the booking's `suppressed_obligation_count` increases by 1. **Link four,** caused by links three-a and three-b: the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents`, `earliest_refund_deadline_utc`, `unused_segment_count` and `disruption_posture` (which reaches `REFUND_OBLIGATED`) are recomputed, and the booking's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole request. Cancelling a leg that already carries a non-voided fare obligation does not mint a second one and does not raise a duplicate error — the cascade simply skips that obligation and increases `suppressed_obligation_count` by 1.

Operational progress is recorded through the same operational channel, supplying an `operation` of `DEPARTED`, `ARRIVED` or `RESTORED` together with the `occurred_at_utc` instant. Recording `DEPARTED` is accepted from `SCHEDULED` or from `DELAYED`, and additionally requires that every sibling leg with a smaller `sequence_index` already be in `DEPARTED`, `ARRIVED`, `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED_UNUSED` — an itinerary cannot depart its second leg while its first is still merely scheduled; otherwise `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Departure writes `actual_departure_utc` from `occurred_at_utc`, advances the leg's `version` by 1, sets the booking's `travel_commenced` to true, recomputes `unused_segment_count` and `disruption_posture`, and advances the booking's `version` by 1. Recording `ARRIVED` is accepted only from `DEPARTED` and requires `occurred_at_utc` strictly after `actual_departure_utc`; it writes `actual_arrival_utc`, moves the leg `DEPARTED → ARRIVED` — the leg's terminal state — and advances the leg's `version` by 1. Arrival also bubbles downward: every ancillary purchase on that leg still in `PENDING_DELIVERY` becomes `DELIVERED`, because a service flown as sold is a service delivered, and no obligation is created for it. The booking's `unused_segment_count` and `disruption_posture` are then recomputed and its `version` advances by 1.

### 3.3. Paid Extras: Ancillary Services and Checked Baggage

Passengers pay for things beyond the seat itself, and each of those charges is refundable on its own terms. An ancillary purchase records a paid optional service attached to both a booking and one specific leg: it carries `booking_id`, `flight_segment_id`, a `service_type` drawn from `SEAT_SELECTION`, `WIFI`, `ENTERTAINMENT`, `PRIORITY_BOARDING` and `LOUNGE_ACCESS`, and an `amount_cents` of at least 1. The named leg must belong to the named booking; a mismatch is `ERR_SEGMENT_BOOKING_MISMATCH`. A new purchase begins with `delivery_status` reading `PENDING_DELIVERY` and `refund_obligation_created` false, and recording it refreshes the booking's `collected_total_cents` and advances the booking's `version` by 1 — the charge enters the collected-money side of the ledger the moment it is recorded, whether or not the service is ever rendered.

A service that was sold but not provided owes its money back at face value. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `ComplianceAdmin` records non-provision by supplying a `non_provision_reason` — one of `EQUIPMENT_SWAP`, `SYSTEM_OUTAGE`, `SEAT_REASSIGNED` or `SERVICE_CANCELLED` — together with the `occurred_at_utc` instant of the non-provision. The purchase must be in `PENDING_DELIVERY`; a purchase already `DELIVERED` or already `NOT_PROVIDED` is refused with `ERR_ANCILLARY_STATE_INVALID`, and so is a purchase whose parent leg has already reached `ARRIVED`, because a flown leg's services are settled by the arrival bubble of §3.2 rather than by hand. Recording non-provision drives `PENDING_DELIVERY → NOT_PROVIDED`, stores the reason, advances the purchase's `version` by 1, and mints one refund obligation on an `ANCILLARY` basis in proactive issuance mode at fee face value — its gross entitlement is exactly the purchase's `amount_cents` — clamped to the booking's remaining headroom, with the obligation's clock start taken from the supplied `occurred_at_utc`. The purchase's `refund_obligation_created` becomes true, the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents`, `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` and `disruption_posture` are recomputed, and the booking's `version` advances by 1.

Non-provision is reversible in one specific circumstance, and it is the only compensating move on this entity. When an obligation on an `ANCILLARY` basis is voided for the reason `CREATED_IN_ERROR`, the purchase it named returns `NOT_PROVIDED → PENDING_DELIVERY`, its `non_provision_reason` is cleared to null, its `refund_obligation_created` returns to false, and its `version` advances by 1. This restores the pre-cascade picture exactly, which is what allows the obligated-money ledger of §2.11 to balance to the cent after an erroneous cascade is undone.

Checked bags follow a parallel but distinct arc. A bag records its `booking_id`, a `tag_number` of exactly ten characters that must be unique across all bags — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_NUMBER` — a `fee_cents` of at least 0 representing the fee collected from the passenger, the `checked_at_utc` instant the bag was accepted at the counter, and a `scheduled_delivery_utc` that must be strictly after the check-in instant and represents the contractual delivery moment, normally the published arrival of the final leg. A new bag begins with `status` reading `IN_TRANSIT`, `delivery_delay_hours` at 0 and `fee_refund_obligated` false; recording it refreshes the booking's `collected_total_cents` and advances the booking's `version` by 1.

A bag is closed out exactly once, by supplying a `resolution` of either `DELIVERED` or `DECLARED_LOST`. The bag must be `IN_TRANSIT`; resolving an already-resolved bag is `ERR_BAG_STATE_INVALID`. A `DELIVERED` resolution requires `delivered_at_utc` and a `DECLARED_LOST` resolution requires `loss_declared_at_utc`; omitting the timestamp the resolution calls for is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Whichever instant is supplied must be at or after the bag's `checked_at_utc`; an earlier instant is `ERR_DELIVERY_BEFORE_CHECKIN`.

On a `DELIVERED` resolution the system computes `delivery_delay_hours` as the difference between `delivered_at_utc` and `scheduled_delivery_utc`, taken from the raw second difference, converted to hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. That figure is compared against the bag-delay threshold that governs the itinerary — and here, unlike the significant-delay rule, it is the **booking-level** `itinerary_scope` that selects the threshold, because a bag travels the whole itinerary rather than one leg: the international figure when the scope reads `INTERNATIONAL`, the domestic figure otherwise. The comparison is at-or-above, so a bag delivered exactly 12.00 hours late on a default domestic itinerary is late. When the delay falls short of the threshold, the bag moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, `delivered_at_utc` and `delivery_delay_hours` are stored, the bag's `version` advances by 1, and no obligation is created.

When the delay reaches the threshold the cascade runs in three links. **Link one:** the bag moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED_LATE`, `delivered_at_utc` and `delivery_delay_hours` are stored, and the bag's `version` advances by 1. **Link two,** caused by link one: one refund obligation on a `BAGGAGE_FEE` basis in proactive issuance mode at fee face value is minted, its gross entitlement being exactly the bag's `fee_cents`, clamped to the booking's remaining headroom, with the obligation's clock start taken from `delivered_at_utc`; the bag's `fee_refund_obligated` becomes true. If the bag's `fee_cents` is 0, or if the headroom clamp yields 0, no obligation row is written and the booking's `suppressed_obligation_count` increases by 1 instead. **Link three,** caused by link two: the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents`, `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` and `disruption_posture` — which reaches `REFUND_OBLIGATED` — are recomputed and the booking's `version` advances by 1.

A `DECLARED_LOST` resolution moves the bag `IN_TRANSIT → DECLARED_LOST`, stores `loss_declared_at_utc`, and leaves `delivery_delay_hours` at 0 because no delivery ever occurred; the bag's `version` advances by 1. It then mints the fee obligation on exactly the same terms as the late-delivery branch — a lost bag always owes its fee back, regardless of how many hours elapsed — with the obligation's clock start taken from `loss_declared_at_utc` since there is no delivery instant, and the booking's ledger fields are recomputed with the booking's `version` advancing by 1. Voiding a `BAGGAGE_FEE` obligation clears the bag's `fee_refund_obligated` back to false but deliberately leaves the bag's own `status` untouched: the bag really was late or really was lost, and rewriting that fact would falsify the operational record.

### 3.4. Denied Boarding: Categories, the Statutory Ladder and Informed Consent

When a cabin is oversold, somebody does not fly. The compliance office records each such incident against the booking and the leg it happened on, declaring a `category` of either `VOLUNTARY` — the passenger accepted an offer to give up the seat — or `INVOLUNTARY`. The record carries `oversale_confirmed`, which defaults to true and states whether the carrier confirmed the leg really was oversold; `written_disclosure_provided`, which defaults to false and states whether the passenger received the written statement of denied-boarding rights before agreeing; a `one_way_fare_cents` of at least 1, being the one-way fare to the passenger's first stopover or final destination and the base of every compensation figure; a `substitute_arrival_utc` giving the arrival instant of the substitute transportation actually offered; and, when the category is `VOLUNTARY`, a `voluntary_agreed_amount_cents` of at least 0 recording what the volunteer agreed to accept. That last field is required for a volunteer and forbidden for an involuntary denial. The `flight_segment_id` must belong to the supplied `booking_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGMENT_BOOKING_MISMATCH`, and the `substitute_arrival_utc` must be at or after the named leg's `scheduled_arrival_utc`, or it is `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_BEFORE_SCHEDULED`.

A newly recorded incident sits in `RECORDED` with `statutory_compensation_cents` at 0, `compensation_cents` at 0 and `effective_category` unset, and with `substitute_delay_minutes` computed immediately as the difference between the `substitute_arrival_utc` and the leg's `scheduled_arrival_utc` in whole minutes with the remainder dropped, never below 0.

Finalizing the record is where the money is decided. A `ComplianceOfficer` (or `ComplianceAdmin`) supplies the `finalized_at_utc` instant; a record not in `RECORDED` is refused with `ERR_DENIED_BOARDING_STATE_INVALID`. Finalization first walks the statutory ladder, which is a capped multiple of the one-way fare stepped by how much later the substitute transportation gets the passenger there. The first rung is free: a substitute delay of at most 60 minutes — one hour, inclusive — carries a statutory compensation of 0. The second rung runs from 61 minutes up to and including a scope-dependent ceiling of 120 minutes (two hours) on a domestic leg or 240 minutes (four hours) on a leg flagged international; here the statutory figure is twice the `one_way_fare_cents`, rounded half up, but never more than 77,500 cents (775 dollars). Beyond that ceiling — one minute past two hours domestically, one minute past four hours internationally — the statutory figure is four times the `one_way_fare_cents`, rounded half up, but never more than 155,000 cents (1,550 dollars). In both paid rungs the rounding is applied to the fare multiple **before** the cap comparison, not after it. This ladder is stored as `statutory_compensation_cents` on every record, including volunteers', because it is the baseline against which a volunteer's waiver is judged. Note that the ladder reads the record's own `one_way_fare_cents` and never the booking's `total_fare_cents`, and that it selects its ceiling from the **leg's** `is_international` flag.

Finalization then resolves `effective_category`. It stays `VOLUNTARY` only when the declared `category` is `VOLUNTARY` **and** `oversale_confirmed` is true **and** `written_disclosure_provided` is true; in every other combination it resolves to `INVOLUNTARY`. The logic is consent: a volunteer who was never shown the written statement of their rights, or who gave up a seat on a flight the carrier will not even confirm was oversold, cannot have given informed consent, so the waiver is void and the statutory treatment applies.

The two outcomes have deliberately different side-effect sets. When the declared and effective categories **agree**, the record moves `RECORDED → FINALIZED`. On an involuntary record `compensation_cents` is set to `statutory_compensation_cents`. On a genuinely voluntary record the disclosure floor is enforced first: the agreed `voluntary_agreed_amount_cents` must be at least half of `statutory_compensation_cents`, computed as the statutory amount multiplied by 0.50 and rounded half up, and an agreed amount below that floor is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTARY_BELOW_DISCLOSURE_FLOOR`; when it clears the floor, `compensation_cents` is set to the agreed amount, which may sit anywhere at or above the floor. When the declared and effective categories **differ** — most commonly a `VOLUNTARY` declaration whose `written_disclosure_provided` is false — the record moves `RECORDED → RECATEGORIZED` instead: `effective_category` is written as `INVOLUNTARY`, `compensation_cents` is set to `statutory_compensation_cents` and the agreed amount is ignored entirely, and the disclosure floor check is **not** applied on this branch, since there is no valid waiver left to measure. Both branches write `finalized_at_utc` from the supplied value and advance the record's `version` by 1.

Either branch then mints one refund obligation on a `DENIED_BOARDING` basis in proactive issuance mode, whose gross entitlement is the resolved `compensation_cents`, with its clock start taken from `finalized_at_utc`. Statutory denied-boarding compensation is **exempt from the booking's refund headroom entirely** — it is compensation for a service failure rather than a refund of money collected, so it is neither clamped by the headroom nor counted against it when later obligations compute theirs. When `compensation_cents` comes out as 0, because the substitute delay landed on the free rung, no obligation is written and the booking's `suppressed_obligation_count` increases by 1 instead. Finally the booking's ledger fields are recomputed and its `version` advances by 1.

### 3.5. Tarmac Delay Occupation, Deplaning Deadlines and Contingency Reporting

A tarmac delay event records one occurrence of an aircraft holding passengers on the ground on one leg. It names its `flight_segment_id`, a `tarmac_start_utc` — the instant the door closed on a `DEPARTURE`-phase delay or the instant of touchdown on an `ARRIVAL`-phase delay — and a `phase` of either `DEPARTURE` or `ARRIVAL`. Whichever phase is declared, the start instant must be at or after the leg's `scheduled_departure_utc`; an earlier instant is never a tarmac occupation and is refused with `ERR_TARMAC_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Occupations on one leg may not overlap. A closed event occupies the interval from its `tarmac_start_utc` inclusive to its `interval_end_utc` exclusive; an event still open occupies everything from its `tarmac_start_utc` onward without end, and therefore blocks every later event on the same leg until it is closed. A new event whose interval intersects any existing occupation is refused with `ERR_TARMAC_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Because the interval end is exclusive, merely touching is permitted: a new event may start at the exact instant a previous one ended. A new event begins in `DELAY_ACTIVE` with `interval_end_utc` null, `tarmac_minutes` at 0 and `is_violation` false, and — importantly — with its `deadline_minutes` resolved and frozen at creation time from the leg's `is_international` flag and the booking's pinned policy profile: the international deplaning deadline for an international leg, the domestic one otherwise. Freezing the deadline on the row means a later policy profile has no retroactive effect on an occupation already under way.

An open occupation is closed in exactly one of two ways, and the two branches diverge. Deplaning closes it normally: an `OpsController` supplies `deplane_completed_utc`, the instant the last passenger was off the aircraft, which must be strictly after `tarmac_start_utc` or the request is `ERR_TARMAC_WINDOW_INVALID`. The event moves `DELAY_ACTIVE → DEPLANED`, its `interval_end_utc` is set to the deplaning instant, its `tarmac_minutes` becomes the difference between `interval_end_utc` and `tarmac_start_utc` in whole minutes with the remainder dropped, and `is_violation` becomes true when `tarmac_minutes` is **strictly greater** than the event's `deadline_minutes` — occupying the tarmac for exactly the deadline is compliant. The event's `version` advances by 1, the leg's `cumulative_tarmac_minutes` grows by the event's `tarmac_minutes`, and the leg's `version` advances by 1.

Claiming an exemption closes it abnormally: an `OpsController` supplies an `exemption_reason` of `SAFETY`, `SECURITY` or `ATC_DIRECTIVE` together with `exemption_declared_at_utc`. That instant must be strictly after `tarmac_start_utc`, or `ERR_TARMAC_WINDOW_INVALID`, and it must also fall at or before the deadline — the whole minutes between the start and the declaration, remainder dropped, must not exceed the event's `deadline_minutes`. An exemption invoked once the deplaning deadline has already elapsed is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_AFTER_DEADLINE`: a carrier cannot retroactively excuse a deadline it has already blown. The event moves `DELAY_ACTIVE → EXEMPTION_INVOKED`, its `interval_end_utc` is set to the declaration instant, its `tarmac_minutes` is computed the same way as on the deplaning branch, and its `is_violation` is forced to **false** regardless of how many minutes elapsed, because a valid safety, security or air-traffic-control ground cures the breach. Its `version` advances by 1. The leg-level accounting differs from the deplaning branch in a way worth stating twice: the leg's `exempted_tarmac_event_count` grows by 1 — it counts every event on the leg carrying a non-null `exemption_reason` — while the leg's `cumulative_tarmac_minutes` is **not** increased, because exemption-closed minutes are excluded from the leg's accumulated tarmac time even after the event is later reported. The leg's `version` advances by 1. Either way, an event that is not `DELAY_ACTIVE` cannot be closed again: a second deplaning or exemption attempt is `ERR_TARMAC_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Both branches join at the filing. A `ComplianceAdmin` — and only a `ComplianceAdmin` — files the contingency-plan report by supplying a `report_reference`, the `filed_at_utc` instant, and, conditionally, a `corrective_action_summary`. The event must be either `DEPLANED` or `EXEMPTION_INVOKED`, and filing against an event that is still open or already filed is `ERR_TARMAC_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`. The parent leg must additionally be operationally closed out — its `status` must be `DEPARTED`, `ARRIVED` or `CANCELLED` — since a contingency-plan report on a flight still shown as scheduled or merely delayed would be premature; filing while the leg is `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED` is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_STATE_INVALID`. On the deplaning branch, an event whose `is_violation` is true requires a narrative `corrective_action_summary`, and omitting it is `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`; a compliant deplaned event needs no narrative. On the exemption branch a `corrective_action_summary` is never required, because the exemption already forced `is_violation` to false. Filing moves the event to `REPORT_FILED` — its terminal state, reached from either fork — storing `report_reference`, the `corrective_action_summary` when one was supplied, and `report_filed_at_utc` from the supplied `filed_at_utc`, and advancing the event's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Minting a Refund Obligation: Valuation, Headroom and Deadlines

A **refund obligation** is one refund the carrier owes for one cause. Most obligations are minted by the cascades already described — that is what proactive issuance means, and the obligation list is the only discovery channel for them. But an officer may also mint one directly, and that path is where the valuation rules are most visible. A direct create names its `booking_id`, a `basis` drawn from `FARE`, `ANCILLARY`, `BAGGAGE_FEE`, `DENIED_BOARDING` and `GOODWILL_GRANT`, an `issuance_mode` of either `PROACTIVE` or `REQUEST_BASED`, the `expected_booking_version` guard of §2.6, and exactly one source reference matched to the basis: `flight_segment_id` for `FARE`, `checked_bag_id` for `BAGGAGE_FEE`, `ancillary_purchase_id` for `ANCILLARY`, `denied_boarding_record_id` for `DENIED_BOARDING`, and none at all for `GOODWILL_GRANT`. Supplying the wrong reference for the declared basis, supplying more than one, omitting the one that is required, or naming a source row that belongs to a different booking are all `ERR_BASIS_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The narrower check fires first: when a `flight_segment_id` is supplied and the leg it names belongs to a different booking than the `booking_id` in the body, the answer is `ERR_SEGMENT_BOOKING_MISMATCH`. At most one non-voided obligation may exist per basis-and-source pair — one per leg for `FARE`, one per bag for `BAGGAGE_FEE`, one per ancillary purchase for `ANCILLARY`, one per denied-boarding record for `DENIED_BOARDING`, and one per answering complaint for `GOODWILL_GRANT` — and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION_FOR_SOURCE`.

The **gross entitlement** is computed first, and for a fare refund the issuance mode picks between two genuinely different valuations. In proactive mode the variant is `PROACTIVE_FULL_ITINERARY`: when the booking's `travel_commenced` is false, `gross_entitlement_cents` is the booking's whole `total_fare_cents`; when travel has commenced, it is the sum of `segment_fare_cents` over the booking's legs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED`, `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED_UNUSED`, added in ascending `sequence_index` order with no intermediate rounding since each per-leg fare is already whole cents. Legs that are `DEPARTED` or `ARRIVED` are flown value and are excluded. This variant values the remaining itinerary and never prorates by leg count. In request-based mode the variant is `REQUEST_BASED_UNUSED_PRORATION`, and it prorates evenly: when the booking's `segment_count` is 0 the gross entitlement is 0 and no division is attempted, and otherwise the per-leg share is the booking's `total_fare_cents` divided by its `segment_count` and rounded half up, and the gross entitlement is that share multiplied by the booking's `unused_segment_count`. The rounding order is pinned and must not be rearranged: round the per-leg share **first**, then multiply by the unused-leg count. Multiplying first and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and yields a different cent figure on fares that do not divide evenly. Because the carrier's own per-leg allocations need not sum to the ticketed fare, the two variants routinely produce different numbers from identical data — which is exactly why the difference is worth showing an officer before they commit.

Non-fare bases copy a face value rather than computing one. Under the variant `FEE_FACE_VALUE`, an `ANCILLARY` obligation's gross entitlement is the referenced purchase's `amount_cents` and a `BAGGAGE_FEE` obligation's is the referenced bag's `fee_cents`; both ignore `segment_count`, `travel_commenced` and every fare field entirely. Under the variant `GOODWILL_GRANT_AMOUNT`, the gross entitlement is the `granted_amount_cents` an officer entered when answering a complaint, and no source charge exists at all. Partial refunds of a fee are not representable: the obligation is always the full face value of the charge collected. Denied-boarding obligations take their gross entitlement from the ladder of §3.4 under the variant `INVOLUNTARY_STATUTORY` or `VOLUNTARY_NEGOTIATED`. Whichever rule ran, the variant that produced the figure is recorded on the row as `variant_applied`.

**Headroom** is then evaluated, and the five steps run in exactly this sequence. Step one computes the gross entitlement as above. Step two computes the booking's headroom over **strictly pre-existing** obligations only — the row being created is not yet persisted and contributes nothing to its own headroom. The money collected on the booking is its `total_fare_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` over all of its ancillary purchases plus the sum of `fee_cents` over all of its checked bags, counting every such row whatever its delivery outcome. The money already consumed is the sum of `amount_cents` over the booking's obligations whose settlement axis is not voided **and** whose `basis` is not `DENIED_BOARDING`, added in ascending `created_at` order; statutory denied-boarding compensation is excluded because it compensates a service failure rather than refunding money collected. `headroom_at_creation_cents` is the collected figure minus the consumed figure, and it is stored on the row as the audit trail of what was available at the moment of creation. Step three clamps or rejects, and the two issuance modes react differently to the same exhausted ledger. A `DENIED_BOARDING` obligation is exempt: its `amount_cents` is its gross entitlement, unclamped. A proactive obligation is clamped down: its `amount_cents` is the smaller of its gross entitlement and the headroom — but a proactive create against a booking whose headroom is 0 or negative is refused outright with `ERR_NO_REFUND_HEADROOM`. A request-based obligation is never clamped: if its gross entitlement exceeds the headroom, the request is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_HEADROOM`, because a passenger-initiated claim that the ledger cannot support is an error to be corrected rather than a number to be silently reduced. Step four persists the row with its `gross_entitlement_cents`, `headroom_at_creation_cents` and `amount_cents`, with `credit_bonus_cents` at 0 and `payable_total_cents` equal to `amount_cents` — the goodwill bonus is added later, at election, not now. Step five, and only then, recomputes the booking's derived ledger fields, re-reading the persisted rows **including** the one just written, and advances the booking's `version` by 1.

Every obligation carries two deadlines, both measured from its `obligation_start_utc` — the instant the underlying failure became a matter of record. That start instant is basis-dependent: for a `FARE` obligation it is the referenced leg's `disruption_declared_at_utc`; for a `BAGGAGE_FEE` obligation it is the referenced bag's `delivered_at_utc`, or, when the bag was declared lost and therefore has no delivery instant, its `loss_declared_at_utc`; for an `ANCILLARY` obligation it is the `occurred_at_utc` recorded when the service was marked not provided; for a `DENIED_BOARDING` obligation it is the record's `finalized_at_utc`; and for a `GOODWILL_GRANT` obligation it is the answering complaint's `answered_at_utc`. A request-based create supplies the instant the request reached the office as `request_received_utc`, and when that value is absent the logical clock's current instant is used. From the start instant, `deadline_utc` is the settlement deadline: the number of days configured for credit-card payments when the booking's `payment_method` is `CREDIT_CARD`, and the number configured for every other form of payment otherwise — seven days against twenty days under the regulatory defaults, which is why the form of payment collected at booking time still matters months later. And `election_deadline_utc` is five days after the start instant, this election window being a statutory constant the carrier cannot tune. Day arithmetic is exact 24-hour addition, as §2.7 requires. A newly minted obligation begins with `election_status` reading `PENDING_ELECTION`, `settlement_status` reading `UNSETTLED`, `settlement_attempt_count` at 0, `deadline_breached` false, and, for a direct create, `created_by_user_id` set from the caller's `X-User-ID`; obligations minted by a cascade leave `created_by_user_id` null. `deadline_breached` becomes true only when the logical clock has passed `deadline_utc` strictly while the obligation's settlement axis is neither settled nor voided, and the flag is written by the sweep of §3.9 rather than on read.

Before committing to a number, an officer can ask the system what each variant would produce. A read-only exposure view evaluates **both** fare variants against the booking's current state without persisting anything, and reports, for the addressed `booking_id`: `proactive_full_itinerary_cents`, the value the proactive rule would produce right now; `request_based_proration_cents`, the value the request-based rule would produce right now; `variant_delta_cents`, the proactive figure minus the request-based figure; `obligated_total_cents`, mirroring the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents`; `remaining_headroom_cents`, computed exactly as step two above; `collected_total_cents`; `segment_count`; `unused_segment_count`; and `travel_commenced`. Nothing is written and no `version` moves.

### 3.7. Two Axes: Electing the Instrument and Moving the Money

A refund obligation advances along two independent tracks, and keeping them separate is what lets a passenger take their time choosing an instrument while the carrier's clock keeps running. The **election axis**, `election_status`, runs from `PENDING_ELECTION` to either `ELECTED_CASH` or `ELECTED_CREDIT`. The **settlement axis**, `settlement_status`, runs from `UNSETTLED` to `INITIATED` and then to either `SETTLED` or `FAILED`, with `FAILED` returning to `INITIATED` on retry, and with `VOIDED` reachable from `UNSETTLED`, `FAILED` and `SETTLED` alike. `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal outcomes. The axes move without disturbing each other: the automatic default described in §3.9 advances the election axis while the settlement axis stays at `UNSETTLED`, and a retry after failure advances the settlement axis while leaving `election_status` and `election_source` exactly as they were. The one point of contact is a gate, not a merge — the settlement axis cannot leave `UNSETTLED` until the election axis has left `PENDING_ELECTION`.

Electing is done by supplying an `election` of either `CASH` or `TRAVEL_CREDIT`. The obligation must have `election_status` reading `PENDING_ELECTION` **and** `settlement_status` reading `UNSETTLED`; an obligation already elected, or one whose money has already started moving, is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_STATE_INVALID`. A `Passenger` may elect only on an obligation whose booking is their own, per the ownership rules of §2.1. Whoever elects, `election_source` records who chose: `PASSENGER` when the caller acted under the `Passenger` role and `OFFICER_ON_BEHALF` when a compliance role acted for them.

Electing `CASH` sets `election_status` to `ELECTED_CASH`, leaves `credit_bonus_cents` at 0 and `payable_total_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, resolves the `settlement_method`, and advances the obligation's `version` by 1. The instrument is a projection of how the ticket was paid for: a `CREDIT_CARD` booking resolves to `ORIGINAL_PAYMENT`, a `CASH_OR_CHECK` booking to `CASH_OR_CHECK`, a `MILES` booking to `MILES`, and a booking originally paid for in travel credit resolves back to `TRAVEL_CREDIT` even on a cash election — a passenger cannot launder credit into cash by electing cash.

Electing `TRAVEL_CREDIT` sets `election_status` to `ELECTED_CREDIT` and `settlement_method` to `TRAVEL_CREDIT`, and adds the goodwill bonus: `credit_bonus_cents` is the obligation's `amount_cents` multiplied by the profile's `travel_credit_election_bonus_pct` and rounded half up — one tenth of the principal under the regulatory default — and `payable_total_cents` becomes `amount_cents` plus that bonus. The obligation's `version` advances by 1. The booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents` is deliberately **not** changed by the bonus: goodwill sits outside the obligated-money ledger and outside the consumed figure that headroom subtracts, so electing credit never shrinks the headroom available to obligations minted later.

Because moving a passenger onto travel credit gives up their right to cash, a compliance role electing `TRAVEL_CREDIT` on the passenger's behalf must cite a consent artefact: `consent_complaint_id` becomes mandatory, and omitting it is `ERR_CREDIT_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. The complaint it names must exist, or `ERR_CONSUMER_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, and must belong to the same booking as the obligation, or `ERR_CONSENT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. When supplied, its id is stored on the obligation as `credit_consent_reference`. A `Passenger` electing credit for themselves needs no such artefact — their own request is the consent.

Initiating settlement is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and requires a `processor_reference` identifying the payment instruction. Four gates guard it, evaluated in this order. The settlement axis must read `UNSETTLED` or `FAILED`, or the answer is `ERR_OBLIGATION_STATE_INVALID`. The election axis must have left `PENDING_ELECTION`, or the answer is `ERR_ELECTION_REQUIRED` — the carrier may not decide unilaterally what instrument to pay on. For an obligation on a `FARE` basis, the leg that caused it must still be disrupted: its `status` must be `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED_UNUSED`, or it must be `DELAYED` with `is_significant_delay` true; a leg whose disruption was rescinded by a restore blocks settlement of the obligation it produced, with `ERR_SOURCE_DISRUPTION_RESCINDED`. And `X-User-ID` must differ from the obligation's `created_by_user_id`, or the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the settlement axis moves `UNSETTLED → INITIATED` (or `FAILED → INITIATED` on a retry), `settlement_attempt_count` increases by 1, `settlement_initiated_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock's current instant, `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` is written from `X-User-ID`, and the obligation's `version` advances by 1. On a retry the previously recorded `last_failure_reason` is retained for audit.

Reporting the processor's answer requires an `outcome` of either `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED`, and may be done by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `ComplianceAdmin` — but not by whoever initiated the attempt, whose `X-User-ID` matching `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The settlement axis must read `INITIATED`, or `ERR_OBLIGATION_STATE_INVALID`. A `SUCCEEDED` outcome moves the axis `INITIATED → SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at_utc` from the logical clock, and advances the obligation's `version` by 1; when the `settlement_method` is `TRAVEL_CREDIT`, the passenger's `travel_credit_balance_cents` grows by the obligation's `payable_total_cents` — bonus included — and the passenger's `version` advances by 1. The booking's `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` and `disruption_posture` are then recomputed, the posture reaching `CLOSED_OUT` once every obligation of the booking is settled or voided, and the booking's `version` advances by 1. A `FAILED` outcome requires a `failure_reason` of `CARD_DECLINED`, `ACCOUNT_CLOSED` or `PROCESSOR_TIMEOUT`, omitting which is `ERR_FAILURE_REASON_REQUIRED`; it moves the axis `INITIATED → FAILED`, stores the reason as `last_failure_reason`, clears `settlement_initiated_by_user_id` back to null so the retry is unblocked for that officer, and advances the obligation's `version` by 1. A failed settlement leaves the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents` unchanged — failing to pay does not extinguish the debt.

Voiding is a `ComplianceAdmin` power, and the acceptable `void_reason` depends on where the settlement axis stands. From `UNSETTLED` or `FAILED` the reason must be `CREATED_IN_ERROR`, `DUPLICATE` or `PASSENGER_WITHDREW`; from `SETTLED` the only acceptable reason is `SETTLEMENT_REVERSED`, which records that the processor clawed the money back. Any other pairing — `SETTLEMENT_REVERSED` on an obligation that was never settled, an ordinary reason on a settled obligation, or any void of an already-voided obligation — is `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_VOIDABLE`. A void sets the settlement axis to `VOIDED`, stores the `void_reason`, advances the obligation's `version` by 1, and recomputes the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents` (now excluding this row), `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` and `disruption_posture`, advancing the booking's `version` by 1 — restoring the conserved ledger of §2.11 exactly. Three compensating effects ride along. A `CREATED_IN_ERROR` void of an `ANCILLARY` obligation returns the referenced purchase to `PENDING_DELIVERY` with its `non_provision_reason` cleared and its `refund_obligation_created` back to false. A void of a `BAGGAGE_FEE` obligation clears the referenced bag's `fee_refund_obligated` to false while leaving the bag's own `status` alone. And a `SETTLEMENT_REVERSED` void of an obligation settled as travel credit subtracts the obligation's `payable_total_cents` back out of the passenger's `travel_credit_balance_cents` and advances the passenger's `version` by 1, leaving the balance at precisely its pre-settlement value; the obligation's `settled_at_utc` is retained for audit rather than cleared.

### 3.8. Consumer Complaints, Disability Matters and the Activity Trail

Complaints arrive against a booking and carry statutory response clocks of their own. Filing one records the `booking_id`, a `category` drawn from `REFUND`, `BAGGAGE`, `ANCILLARY_SERVICE`, `DENIED_BOARDING`, `DISABILITY` and `OTHER`, a `channel` of `WEB`, `EMAIL`, `PHONE` or `MAIL`, the `received_at_utc` instant the complaint reached the carrier, and a `summary` of the passenger's stated grievance between 1 and 500 characters. A `Passenger` may file only against a booking of their own; a `ComplianceOfficer` or `ComplianceAdmin` may file on anyone's behalf, typically for complaints that arrived by post or telephone.

Both deadlines are derived at filing from the booking's pinned policy profile. `ack_due_utc` is the profile's acknowledgement window added to `received_at_utc` — 30 days by default, for every category alike. `answer_due_utc` is the substantive-answer window added to `received_at_utc`, and here the category matters: a `DISABILITY` complaint gets the shortened disability window of 30 days by default, while every other category gets the general window of 60 days. Under the defaults a disability matter's answer is therefore due at exactly the same instant its acknowledgement is, which is the point — accommodation failures cannot be parked. A newly filed complaint sits in `RECEIVED` with `info_requested_count` and `reopen_count` at 0 and both `ack_breached` and `answer_breached` false, appends an activity row of type `RECEIVED` to its trail, and refreshes the passenger's `open_complaint_count`, advancing the passenger's `version` by 1.

Working a complaint is a chain of four officer actions, each with its own chronology rule. Acknowledging supplies `acknowledged_at_utc` and drives `RECEIVED → ACKNOWLEDGED`; the instant must be at or after `received_at_utc`. Requesting more information supplies an `info_request_note` and a `requested_at_utc` at or after `acknowledged_at_utc`, drives `ACKNOWLEDGED → INFO_REQUESTED`, and increases `info_requested_count` by 1 — but pointedly does **not** extend `answer_due_utc`, because asking the passenger a question does not buy the carrier more time. Answering supplies `answered_at_utc` at or after `received_at_utc`. Reopening supplies `reopened_at_utc` at or after `answered_at_utc`. Any of these chronology rules being violated is `ERR_ANSWER_BEFORE_RECEIPT` (HTTP 422), and an action attempted from the wrong status — acknowledging something not `RECEIVED`, requesting information on something not `ACKNOWLEDGED`, answering something that is neither `ACKNOWLEDGED` nor `INFO_REQUESTED`, or reopening something not `ANSWERED` — is `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Each of the four appends an activity row of the matching type: `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ANSWERED` and `REOPENED` respectively. Acknowledging leaves the passenger's `open_complaint_count` untouched, since the complaint is still open; answering and reopening both refresh it and advance the passenger's `version` by 1.

The answer is the substantive act, and it carries the most conditions. It records a `disposition` of `REFUND_GRANTED`, `REFUND_DENIED`, `POLICY_EXPLAINED` or `ACCOMMODATION_PROVIDED`. A complaint whose `category` is `DISABILITY` additionally requires an `accessibility_resolution_code` of `ASSISTANCE_PROVIDED`, `EQUIPMENT_REPAIRED`, `TRAINING_ISSUED` or `ESCALATED_TO_CRO`; answering a disability matter without one is `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_RESOLUTION_REQUIRED`. A `REFUND_GRANTED` disposition requires a `granted_amount_cents` of at least 1, and omitting it or supplying less is `ERR_GRANT_AMOUNT_REQUIRED`. Answering runs a three-link chain. **Link one:** the complaint moves to `ANSWERED` with its `disposition`, `answered_at_utc`, `accessibility_resolution_code` and `granted_amount_cents` stored, its `version` advanced by 1, an `ANSWERED` activity row appended and the passenger's `open_complaint_count` refreshed. **Link two,** caused by link one and only when the disposition is `REFUND_GRANTED`: one refund obligation is minted on a `GOODWILL_GRANT` basis in **request-based** issuance mode under the variant `GOODWILL_GRANT_AMOUNT`, its gross entitlement being the `granted_amount_cents` just recorded, with all four source references left null and its clock start taken from `answered_at_utc`, and it is subject to the headroom rules of §3.6 — being request-based, a grant that exceeds the booking's remaining headroom is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_HEADROOM` rather than quietly reduced. Only one such grant may exist per complaint: a second attempt to grant money on the same complaint is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION_FOR_SOURCE`. **Link three,** caused by link two: the booking's `total_refund_obligated_cents`, `earliest_refund_deadline_utc` and `disruption_posture` are recomputed and the booking's `version` advances by 1.

Reopening exists because passengers write back. It drives `ANSWERED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, increases `reopen_count` by 1, and re-derives `answer_due_utc` from the `reopened_at_utc` instant plus the category's answer window — the disability window for a disability matter, the general window otherwise — so the clock restarts rather than resuming. The complaint's `ack_due_utc` is **not** re-derived, its `answer_breached` flag is reset to false, and its `disposition` and `accessibility_resolution_code` are retained rather than cleared. Each reopen also supplies a `reopen_reason`. Reopening is capped: a complaint whose `reopen_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. Because the grant obligation from the first answer already exists, a second answer on a reopened complaint does not mint a duplicate — the same duplicate guard blocks it.

Everything that happens to a complaint is written to an append-only **activity trail**, one row per event, immutable once written and never updated. Each row names its `complaint_id`, an `activity_type` — `RECEIVED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ANSWERED`, `REOPENED`, `AUTO_CLOSED`, or `DEADLINE_BREACH_FLAGGED` reserved for a sweep-recorded deadline breach — the `occurred_at_utc` domain instant of the event taken from the triggering payload timestamp, or from the logical clock for rows the sweep generates, the `actor_role` of the caller (recorded as the literal `system` for sweep-generated rows), the `actor_user_id` of the caller (null for sweep-generated rows), and a short machine-readable `detail` such as the disposition, the reopen reason, or the name of the breached deadline field. The trail is read in ascending `occurred_at_utc` order, tie-broken by ascending `created_at`, and is visible to compliance roles and the regulator auditor but not to the passenger.

### 3.9. The Compliance Sweep and Regulator Filings

Deadlines do not enforce themselves. A `ComplianceAdmin` runs the **compliance sweep** for one carrier by naming the `policy_profile_id`; the sweep considers every booking pinned to that profile and every obligation, complaint and tarmac event reachable from those bookings, over all history up to the logical clock's current instant. It runs five phases in exactly this order, processing rows within each phase in ascending `created_at` order.

**Phase one — refund deadline flags.** For every obligation in scope, `deadline_breached` becomes true when the logical clock has passed `deadline_utc` strictly and the obligation's `settlement_status` is neither `SETTLED` nor `VOIDED`.

**Phase two — election auto-default.** For every obligation whose `election_status` still reads `PENDING_ELECTION` and whose `election_deadline_utc` the logical clock has strictly passed, the election axis advances `PENDING_ELECTION → ELECTED_CASH` with `election_source` recorded as `AUTO_DEFAULT` and the `settlement_method` resolved from the booking's `payment_method` exactly as in §3.7. `ELECTED_CREDIT` is **never** the automatic default, regardless of what `travel_credit_default_permitted` says on the profile — defaulting a passenger into travel credit is the prohibited practice this phase exists to prevent, and the flag's purpose is to make that prohibition explicit rather than to license an exception. More strongly: while `travel_credit_default_permitted` is false, the system may never write `ELECTED_CREDIT` at all without an affirmative election recorded through the election action of §3.7. The settlement axis is untouched by this phase and stays at `UNSETTLED`.

**Phase three — complaint deadline flags.** For every complaint in scope, `ack_breached` becomes true when the logical clock has strictly passed `ack_due_utc` while `acknowledged_at_utc` is still null, and `answer_breached` becomes true when the clock has strictly passed `answer_due_utc` while the complaint's `status` is neither `ANSWERED` nor `CLOSED`.

**Phase four — complaint auto-close.** Every complaint sitting in `ANSWERED` whose `answered_at_utc` the logical clock has passed by strictly more than 30 days — the statutory reopen window, which the carrier cannot tune — moves `ANSWERED → CLOSED`, its terminal state, appending an activity row of type `AUTO_CLOSED` and refreshing the passenger's `open_complaint_count`. Complaints are processed here in ascending `received_at_utc` order, tie-broken by ascending `created_at`.

**Phase five — profile rollup.** The profile's `open_violation_count` is rewritten as the sum of three counts in scope: tarmac delay events whose `is_violation` is true, refund obligations whose `deadline_breached` is true, and consumer complaints whose `ack_breached` or `answer_breached` is true. Phases one and three must precede this phase precisely because it counts the flags they set. The profile's `version` advances by 1 only when `open_violation_count` changes value.

The sweep advances the `version` of every obligation and complaint it actually mutates, and it is **idempotent**: running it twice at the same logical-clock instant produces no additional field changes and no additional `version` increments anywhere. Its response reports the work done: the `policy_profile_id` swept, `deadline_breach_flagged_count`, `election_auto_defaulted_count`, `complaint_ack_breach_flagged_count`, `complaint_answer_breach_flagged_count`, `complaint_auto_closed_count`, the resulting `open_violation_count`, and the profile's resulting `policy_profile_version`.

Filings are the office's outward face. A `ComplianceAdmin` opens a report by naming the `policy_profile_id`, a `period_start_utc`, a `period_end_utc` strictly later than the start, and the `expected_policy_profile_version` guard of §2.6; a start at or after the end is `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. Reporting periods for one profile may not intersect, and because both ends are **inclusive**, two periods that share a single boundary instant do intersect — an overlap is `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new report opens in `DRAFT` with all seven metrics at 0, `policy_version_pinned` at 0 and `amendment_count` at 0; opening it also increases the profile's `report_count` and advances the profile's `version` by 1, which is why two reports cannot be opened back to back against one stale profile version.

Submitting freezes the picture. The report must be in `DRAFT` or `AMENDING`, or the answer is `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. The supplied `submitted_at_utc` must be at or after the report's `period_end_utc` — a period cannot be filed before it closes — and an earlier instant is `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`. Every consumer complaint inside the report's scope and period must have reached `ANSWERED` or `CLOSED`; an open complaint anywhere in the window blocks the filing with `ERR_OPEN_COMPLAINT_IN_PERIOD`. The scope is every booking pinned to the report's profile and the rows hanging off those bookings, and the window is closed on both ends — a row counts when its own domain timestamp is at or after `period_start_utc` and at or before `period_end_utc`, and it is always the domain timestamp that is tested, never `created_at`. On that basis the seven metrics are computed and frozen onto the row: `complaint_count`, the number of complaints in scope whose `received_at_utc` falls in the window; `disability_complaint_count`, how many of those same complaints have `category` `DISABILITY`; `late_answer_count`, how many of those same complaints carry `answer_breached` true; `tarmac_violation_count`, the number of tarmac delay events in scope, reached through their leg's booking, whose `tarmac_start_utc` falls in the window and whose `is_violation` is true; `involuntary_denied_boarding_count`, the number of denied-boarding records in scope whose `status` is `FINALIZED` or `RECATEGORIZED`, whose `finalized_at_utc` falls in the window, and whose `effective_category` is `INVOLUNTARY` — so a recategorized volunteer counts here, which is the whole point of recategorization; `refund_obligation_cents_total`, the sum of `amount_cents` over obligations in scope whose `obligation_start_utc` falls in the window and whose settlement axis is not voided, added in ascending `created_at` order; and `overdue_refund_count`, the number of obligations in scope whose `obligation_start_utc` falls in the window and whose `deadline_breached` is true. Alongside them `policy_version_pinned` captures the profile's `version` as observed during this submit, and the report moves to `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at_utc` stored and `submitted_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, its `version` advancing by 1.

Filings get corrected. Amending a `SUBMITTED` report moves it `SUBMITTED → AMENDING`, increases `amendment_count` by 1 and stores the supplied `amendment_reason`, advancing the report's `version` by 1; the supplied `amended_at_utc` must be at or after the report's `submitted_at_utc`, or `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`, and amending anything that is not `SUBMITTED` is `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. While a report sits in `AMENDING` its frozen metrics and its `policy_version_pinned` are **retained**, not cleared — the regulator's last-seen numbers remain readable until they are replaced. Submitting again from `AMENDING` recomputes all seven metrics from current data, refreezes them, refreshes `policy_version_pinned`, and returns the report to `SUBMITTED`, subject to the same open-complaint and submission-instant preconditions as the first submit. This cycle may repeat indefinitely; a regulator report has no terminal state.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/compliance` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint. Endpoints that create a new resource answer with HTTP 201; endpoints that modify an existing resource, run a sweep, set the clock or read data answer with HTTP 200.
