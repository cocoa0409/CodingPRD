# Hotel Channel Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hotel's revenue does not come from one price list. It comes from a lattice: public rates for the brand site, negotiated rates for corporate accounts, opaque rates for discount intermediaries, and packaged rates that bundle non-room value — each of them mapped onto a different set of online travel agencies, global distribution systems, wholesalers and the brand's own booking engine, each of those channels charging a different commission, serving a different set of geographic markets, and pushing back a different set of connectivity failures. Today the revenue distribution team maintains that lattice in spreadsheets and partner extranets. Rate changes reach some channels and not others; a stop-sell placed at 09:00 is still selling rooms at 11:00 on a wholesaler whose connection silently degraded overnight; allotments carved out for a partner sit unconsumed while the shared pool sells out; and nobody can answer, on demand, which channel actually produced the best net average daily rate last month once commission is subtracted.

This document specifies the Hotel Channel Distribution API, the system of record that replaces those spreadsheets. It owns rate plan architecture including derived rates that offset a parent plan, the mapping of plans onto channels with the partner's own rate codes, restriction management across minimum-stay, closed-to-arrival and stop-sell with automatic propagation to every verified mapping, inventory allocation over a pooled availability model with explicit overbooking control, connectivity health monitoring that degrades and suspends misbehaving partner connections, promotion loading with member, package and geo-targeted offers under a two-person approval rule, content and image parity enforcement before a promotion may reach a channel, and the analytics — commission cost ratio, net average daily rate, booking pace — that the team uses to decide where the next room night should be sold.

Two properties of the design are load-bearing and are worth stating before the details. First, **every derived value is derived**: pooled availability, batch state, parity verdicts, resolved base amounts and channel ledgers are computed from their inputs on every event that changes those inputs, and can never be supplied by a client. Second, **time is explicit**: the service reads every date and instant from a logical clock that a platform operator sets, never from wall-clock time, so that horizon checks, freeze windows and pace comparisons are reproducible.

The API is mounted under `/distribution`, with a single additional operational path outside that mount for driving the logical clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including every read — carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator and `X-User-Role` names the capability set that operator is exercising. If either header is absent, or is present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five declared roles, the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) is returned — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a permission problem. Once the role is recognised but is not among the roles the target operation admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `RevenueDirector`, `ChannelOpsEngineer` and `ChannelPartner`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator. It onboards properties and their distribution channels, retires connectivity permanently, drives the logical clock, and reads everything.
- A `RevenueManager` owns commercial content and inventory: rate plans and their repricing, the sellable calendar, allotments, restrictions, and promotion authoring.
- A `RevenueDirector` is the second pair of eyes on promotions — it approves or rejects what a manager submitted — and consumes the analytics that drive channel mix decisions.
- A `ChannelOpsEngineer` owns the connectivity surface: partner connections, heartbeats, freeze windows, channel mappings, content parity, observed-rate surveillance, and the dispatch of rate and restriction payloads.
- A `ChannelPartner` is an external distribution channel acting on its own bookings.

For `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `RevenueDirector` and `ChannelOpsEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a promotion submission stores it in `submitted_by_user_id`, an approval stores it in `approved_by_user_id` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Two `RevenueDirector` requests carrying different `X-User-ID` values are two different people for the purposes of the segregation-of-duties rule described in §3.5, and that is the only place these identifiers are compared to each other.

For `ChannelPartner` the identity is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Channel` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Channel` compare the resource's channel foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a partner booking a stay must submit a `channel_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, and a partner cancelling a booking must target a reservation whose `channel_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. A violation of either is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A partner's reservation reads are silently scoped to its own channel — it sees only reservations whose `channel_id` matches its `X-User-ID`, and it has no access at all to the pricing surface, the inventory surface or the analytics reads.

One further authorization boundary is not about which endpoint may be called but about which *field* may be sent. Overriding a system-owned decision is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403) in exactly two situations: a `ChannelPartner` sending `override_overbooking` as true when booking, and any human role at all attempting to lift a restriction whose `origin` is `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD`. Only `RevenueManager` and `RevenueDirector` may send `override_overbooking` true; only the system lifts a guard restriction, and it does so when the day it guards stops being oversold.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint in this API accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` sets the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` sets how many rows to skip before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless an endpoint's own narrative names a different order — the ranked channel-mix read is the one place where a domain-specific ordering replaces it. Every list response carries all four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain after the current page.

Filters offered by a list endpoint are additive: supplying two filters narrows to rows matching both, and supplying none returns every row the caller may see.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource this API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID. Identifiers are never prefixed with a type marker and are never supplied by a client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, read from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that resource. Whenever the specification below says an operation "bumps the version" of an entity, it means both things: `version` increases by 1 and `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant. Entities carrying `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` are `Property`, `RoomType`, `InventoryDay`, `Channel`, `ChannelConnection`, `RatePlan`, `ChannelMapping`, `ChannelAllocation`, `Restriction`, `DistributionBatch`, `RateDistributionJob`, `Promotion` and `Reservation`.

`ConnectivityFreezeWindow` is the single exception. A freeze window is immutable once declared: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its serialized form.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service keeps a **logical clock** and reads every date and every instant from it. A fresh server starts with the clock at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the clock never falls back to wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock to any instant by supplying `current_utc`; the response echoes `current_utc` and no stored entity is modified by the move. Everything time-dependent then reads the new instant: subsequent `created_at`, `updated_at`, `lifted_at`, `cancelled_at`, `dispatched_at`, `applied_at`, `suspended_at`, `recalled_at`, `superseded_at` and `last_heartbeat_at` values, every booking-horizon check, every freeze-window evaluation, and every analytics window and pace computation.

Where a rule speaks of "the clock's date" it means the calendar date part of the logical clock instant, in UTC.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in minor currency units — cents — and every field carrying money is named with a `_cents` suffix. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no floating-point amount is ever accepted or returned. Each `Property` declares the currency those cents are expressed in through `currency_code`, which must be exactly three uppercase letters and defaults to `USD`; the API performs no currency conversion.

All proportional quantities — commissions, overbooking allowances, rate offsets, discounts, channel adjustments, cost ratios and pace indices — are expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is one hundred percent and 100 basis points is one percent. A field carrying a proportion is named with a `_bp` suffix. Applying a proportion of `x` basis points to an amount means multiplying that amount by `x` and dividing by 10000.

Rounding is **half up** everywhere a division must produce an integer: the quotient is computed exactly and, when the fractional remainder is exactly one half, the result rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Two places deliberately do **not** round half up, and both are called out again where they are used: the overbooking uplift on a room type's physical capacity is **floored** so a fractional room is never sold, and the promotion stacking cap is **floored** so the cap can never exceed the proportion it expresses. Every calculation in §3 states its own rounding points explicitly; where a calculation rounds per night rather than once on a total, rounding once on the total is a different and incorrect answer.

### 2.7. Ordered Request Validation

Validation is **strictly ordered**. For any request, the checks below are evaluated in the order given, the first violated check decides the entire response, and **no side effect of any kind is applied** when a check fails — no entity is created, no state is advanced, no counter moves, no version is bumped, no distribution batch is queued. A request that would violate several checks reports only the earliest one. The ordering is part of the contract, and the deliberate consequences of it are noted where they matter.

1. **Headers present.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` is one of `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `RevenueDirector`, `ChannelOpsEngineer`, `ChannelPartner`, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Role permitted.** The role in `X-User-Role` is listed among the roles the target endpoint admits, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. **Path identifiers resolve.** Every identifier segment in the path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right in path order across properties, room types, inventory days, channels, channel connections, rate plans, channel mappings, allotments, restrictions, promotions, distribution batches, distribution jobs and reservations. This applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter, and the failure carries the code for the entity that failed to resolve, each with HTTP 404: `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESTRICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Channel ownership.** When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelPartner`, `X-User-ID` must equal the channel the request acts on: the `channel_id` in the body when booking a stay, and the booked reservation's `channel_id` when cancelling. This check guards exactly those two operations — stay creation and stay cancellation — and fails with `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403).
6. **Override authority.** The caller may not override a system-owned decision. A `Restriction` whose `origin` is `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` is not liftable by any human role, and only `RevenueManager` and `RevenueDirector` may send `override_overbooking` true — a `ChannelPartner` never may. This check guards exactly two operations, restriction lifting and stay creation, and fails with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
7. **Request shape.** The body carries every required field; every field has its declared JSON type; every enum-typed value is a declared member of that enum; every amount and every count is a non-negative integer; every calendar date is a well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD` date; every instant is a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant; a stay's `departure_date` is strictly after its `arrival_date`; `end_date` is greater than or equal to `start_date`; and `ceiling_amount_cents` is greater than or equal to `floor_amount_cents`. This check applies to every creating and modifying endpoint and to every query parameter of every read. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The analytics window pair is the one deliberate exception to the ordering-comparison clauses above: `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` are shape-checked here only for presence and well-formedness, and their *relative order* is not a check-7 concern but belongs to check 22 — so a reversed analytics window is `ERR_ANALYTICS_WINDOW_INVALID` (422) and never `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. **Body identifiers resolve.** Every identifier referenced from the request *body* resolves: `channel_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `room_type_id`, `parent_rate_plan_id` and `channel_mapping_id`. This check guards mapping creation, restriction creation, allotment creation, stay creation and rate plan creation, and fails with the 404 that names the unresolved identifier: `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND` for `channel_id`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for `rate_plan_id` or `parent_rate_plan_id`, `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_NOT_FOUND` for `room_type_id` and `ERR_CHANNEL_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND` for `channel_mapping_id`, all HTTP 404.
9. **Single-property scope.** Every entity named in one request belongs to the same property: the channel, the room type, the rate plan and the reservation must all share one `property_id`. This check guards mapping creation, restriction creation, allotment creation and stay creation, and fails with `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409).
10. **Version echo.** The submitted `expected_room_type_version` or `expected_rate_plan_version` equals the parent entity's current `version`, as specified in §2.8. This check guards allotment creation and rate plan repricing, and fails with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_TYPE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_RATE_PLAN_VERSION`, both HTTP 412. Because it sits above every state and business check, a stale echo is reported even when the request would also have breached the overbooking ceiling or found the target in the wrong state.
11. **Transition admitted by current state.** The target resource is in a state that admits the requested transition, per the state machines described throughout §3. This check guards every state-transition endpoint and fails with `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MAPPING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CONNECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RESTRICTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_RETRYABLE` or `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, all HTTP 409.
12. **Connection sellable.** The `ChannelConnection` that carries the operation is sellable, meaning its `connection_state` is `HEALTHY` or `DEGRADED`; `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED` are refused. This check guards mapping verification and stay creation, and fails with `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_SELLABLE` (409). Because it precedes the capacity checks, a booking on an unsellable connection reports `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_SELLABLE` rather than `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, even when the requested night is also full.
13. **Segregation of duties.** The `RevenueDirector` approving or rejecting a promotion is not the operator recorded in that promotion's `submitted_by_user_id`. This check guards promotion approval and promotion rejection, and fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Note the inversion against check 11: because the state check is evaluated first, a self-submitted promotion that is no longer `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
14. **Content parity.** Every `VERIFIED` `ChannelMapping` of the promotion's rate plan has `parity_state` equal to `IN_PARITY` before the promotion is loaded to the channels. This check guards promotion loading and fails with `ERR_CONTENT_PARITY_BLOCKED` (409).
15. **Distribution prerequisites exist.** The prerequisites the operation distributes through are present: at least one `VERIFIED` `ChannelMapping` for the plan being loaded; a `ChannelConnection` for the channel being mapped or booked; and an `InventoryDay` row for every date the operation touches. This check guards promotion loading, mapping creation, stay creation and allotment creation, and fails with `ERR_CHANNEL_MAPPING_MISSING`, `ERR_CHANNEL_CONNECTION_MISSING` or `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_OPEN`, all HTTP 409.
16. **Inside the booking horizon.** Requested dates lie inside the property's booking horizon. For a stay, `arrival_date` must be strictly after the clock's date and `departure_date` must be no later than the clock's date plus `booking_horizon_days`; failure is `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_HORIZON` (422). For a restriction, an allotment or an inventory day, `start_date`, `end_date` and `stay_date` must fall inside that same horizon, and a freeze window must start no earlier than the logical clock; failure is `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON` (422). This check guards stay creation, restriction creation, allotment creation, inventory-day creation and freeze-window creation.
17. **Uniqueness, depth and overlap.** Four families of constraint, all HTTP 409. Uniqueness — one `ChannelMapping` per combination of `rate_plan_id`, `channel_id` and `room_type_id`; one `Channel` per property and channel `code`; one `RoomType` per property and room-type `code`; one `RatePlan` per property and plan `code`; one `Promotion` per rate plan and promotion `code`; one `InventoryDay` per `room_type_id` and `stay_date`; and at most one non-retired `ChannelConnection` per channel — each violation returning `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Derivation depth within the permitted maximum, returning `ERR_DERIVATION_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. No two `ACTIVE` restrictions sharing the same `room_type_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `restriction_type` and `origin` may have overlapping closed date ranges, returning `ERR_RESTRICTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. And no two freeze windows on one connection may overlap once the pre-buffer is applied, returning `ERR_FREEZE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. This check guards mapping creation, channel creation, room-type creation, rate-plan creation, promotion creation, inventory-day creation, connection creation, restriction creation and freeze-window creation. Because check 16 precedes it, a restriction that both falls outside the horizon and overlaps an existing window reports `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`, not `ERR_RESTRICTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
18. **Rate eligibility.** The rate plan is eligible for this channel and this guest, all failures HTTP 422: an `OPAQUE` plan requires the channel's `allows_opaque_rate` to be true, else `ERR_OPAQUE_RATE_NOT_ALLOWED`; a member-only plan or a `MEMBER_RATE` promotion requires `member_flag` true and the channel's `allows_member_rate` true, else `ERR_MEMBER_RATE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; a `NEGOTIATED` plan requires the request's `negotiated_account_code` to equal the plan's `negotiated_account_code`, else `ERR_NEGOTIATED_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; and when a channel's `geo_market_codes` list is non-empty, or a `GEO_OFFER` promotion is in play, the request's `geo_market_code` must be one of the served markets, else `ERR_GEO_MARKET_NOT_SERVED`. This check guards stay creation and the quoted-rate read.
19. **Sell restrictions.** No `ACTIVE` restriction blocks the stay, tested in the fixed order stop-sell first, then closed-to-arrival, then minimum stay, failing with `ERR_STOP_SELL_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL` or `ERR_MIN_STAY_NOT_MET`, all HTTP 422. This check guards stay creation only. Because it precedes check 20, a stay that is both stop-sold and over capacity reports `ERR_STOP_SELL_ACTIVE`, not `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.
20. **Capacity.** Capacity is not breached on any touched date, all failures HTTP 409. A booking drawn from the shared pool needs at least one unit of shared pool on every night, else `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED`; a pool booking whose unit lands in the overbooking band on any night needs `override_overbooking` true, else `ERR_OVERBOOK_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`, and because the ceiling test runs first a night already at the ceiling reports `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED` rather than the override error; and a new allotment must satisfy the aggregate capacity check on every date of its closed range, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CEILING`. This check guards stay creation and allotment creation.
21. **Distribution not frozen.** No freeze window of the target connection covers the logical clock once the pre-buffer is applied. This check guards batch dispatch and promotion loading, and fails with `ERR_CONNECTION_FROZEN` (409).
22. **Analytics window well formed.** `window_end_date` is greater than or equal to `window_start_date`, the window is no longer than the property's `mix_window_days`, and a pace request's `target_stay_date` is strictly after the clock's date. This check guards the channel performance read, the channel-mix read and the pace read, and fails with `ERR_ANALYTICS_WINDOW_INVALID` (422).
23. **At least one eligible channel.** At least one channel qualifies for the mix ranking. This check guards the channel-mix read only, and fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHANNEL` (409).

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Room Types and Rate Plans

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both of them read a parent aggregate, make a decision from what they read, and then write a child that the parent's own state constrains. In both cases the client must echo back the `version` of the parent entity it last observed, and the echo is a **required, non-optional** field of the request body. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field of any kind, and supplying one where it is not specified is simply an unrecognised body field.

**Allotting units against a room type.** Creating a `ChannelAllocation` under a room type requires the body to carry `expected_room_type_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that `RoomType`. The server compares the submitted value with the room type's current `version` at the moment the allotment is evaluated. When the two differ, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_TYPE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all happens: no `ChannelAllocation` row is created, no predecessor allotment is superseded, and no `InventoryDay` is recomputed. When the two match, the allotment is created, the aggregate capacity check of §3.8 runs, any same-key predecessor allotment moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the covered `InventoryDay` rows are recomputed, and the room type's `version` is incremented by 1 — which spends the echoed value, so a second allotment attempt reusing the same echo is stale. The room type's `version` is likewise incremented by 1 on every accepted room-type modification, so a modification interleaved between a client's read and its allotment attempt invalidates the echo.

**Repricing a rate plan.** Modifying an `ACTIVE` `RatePlan` requires the body to carry `expected_rate_plan_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that plan. When the echoed value differs from the plan's current `version`, the modification is refused with `ERR_STALE_RATE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412): no field changes, no descendant plan is re-derived, and no `DistributionBatch` or `RateDistributionJob` is created. When the echoed value matches, the submitted fields are applied, `resolved_base_amount_cents` is re-derived on this plan and on every descendant plan, one rate distribution batch plus its child jobs are created, and the plan's `version` is incremented by 1. Because activation and closure also increment a plan's `version`, a caller that read a plan while it was `DRAFT` and attempts to reprice it after activation is refused on the version check rather than on the state check — the version check sits above the state check in §2.7.

### 2.9. Derived State, Cascades and Conservation

A large part of this system is derived rather than stored-and-trusted, and the derivations cascade. Three rules govern all of them.

**Derived fields are never inputs.** An `InventoryDay`'s `pooled_state`, `overbooking_ceiling_units`, `allocated_units`, `committed_units`, `shared_pool_units` and `physical_capacity_units`; a `DistributionBatch`'s `batch_state`, `total_jobs`, `applied_jobs`, `rejected_jobs` and `abandoned_jobs`; a `ChannelMapping`'s `parity_state`, `mismatch_state` and `mismatch_delta_cents`; a `RatePlan`'s `derivation_depth` and `resolved_base_amount_cents`; a `RoomType`'s `effective_overbooking_pct_bp`; a `Reservation`'s `departure_date`, `nightly_breakdown`, `gross_amount_cents`, `commission_amount_cents`, `net_amount_cents` and `loyalty_credit_cents`; a `ChannelAllocation`'s `consumed_units`; a `ConnectivityFreezeWindow`'s `effective_starts_at`; and a `Channel`'s ledger fields `gross_revenue_cents`, `commission_accrued_cents`, `net_revenue_cents`, `room_nights_sold`, `bookings_count`, `cancellations_count` and `mismatch_alert_count` are all computed by the server. A client that submits any of them is submitting an unrecognised field, and its value has no effect.

**A cascade re-derives eagerly and completely.** When an operation changes an input to a derived value, every dependent value is recomputed as part of the same request, before the response is returned — consistency here is immediate, never eventual. Cascades in this domain run deep. A single rejected distribution job can increment its connection's failure streak, suspend the connection, recall every allotment that connection held, recompute every inventory day those allotments covered, lift the automatic stop-sell guard on any day that stops being oversold, and queue a fresh restriction batch with its own child jobs — five levels from one rejection. Every entity touched anywhere along such a chain has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed; the fact that a change reached an entity indirectly does not exempt it from the version bump. Touched means the entity's own stored values actually moved: an entity that a cascade recomputes to exactly the derived values it was already holding has not been touched by that cascade, and takes neither a `version` bump nor an `updated_at` refresh from it. A directly requested modification is a different matter — it bumps the entity it names whether or not the submitted values differ from the stored ones.

**Two quantities are conserved.** For every inventory day, at every moment after every allotment creation, supersede, release, recall, booking and cancellation, the overbooking ceiling equals the sum of held units, committed units and shared pool units. And for every channel ledger, at every moment after every booking and every cancellation, gross revenue equals net revenue plus accrued commission. Where the specification below describes reversing an operation, it means restoring these identities exactly, with no residue and no drift.

### 2.10. Nested Paths, Scoping and Status Codes

Resource paths in this API are shallow by design: no path carries two identifier segments. A child resource is created through its parent's path — a room type under a property, an inventory day or an allotment under a room type, a channel under a property, a connection or freeze window under a channel or connection, a rate plan under a property, a promotion under a rate plan — and is thereafter addressed directly by its own identifier. Where a path does carry an identifier, that identifier is resolved before anything else in the body is examined, per check 4 of §2.7. Where a body carries identifiers, they are resolved left to right in the order the specification lists them for that operation, and only after the body's shape has been validated.

Cross-entity references must stay inside a single property. Any request that names a channel, a room type and a rate plan together — mapping creation, restriction creation, allotment creation, stay creation — requires all of them to share one `property_id`, and reports `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH` when they do not. A reservation's own `property_id` is not supplied by the client at all: it is derived from the booking channel.

Successful creation of a new resource returns HTTP 201. Every other successful operation, including every state transition, every modification and every read, returns HTTP 200. A transition endpoint returns 200 and the updated resource even though it changes state, because it does not mint a new row. The response body always follows the envelope of §2.2, with `data` holding the affected resource for single-resource operations and an array of resources for list reads.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Property, Its Room Types and Its Sellable Calendar

Everything in this system hangs off a **property**. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying a `code` and a `name`, and optionally the six policy defaults that the rest of the specification keeps coming back to. The `code` is the operator-facing property code: 3 to 16 characters drawn only from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen, and unique across every property in the system — a second property claiming a code already in use is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The `name` is free text of 1 to 120 characters. `currency_code` names the ISO-4217 currency all of this property's cent amounts are denominated in; it must be exactly three uppercase letters and defaults to `USD` when omitted.

The four remaining property inputs are policy dials, and each has a default that applies when the field is omitted. `overbooking_pct_bp` is the property-wide overbooking allowance expressed in basis points of physical capacity, must lie between 0 and 5000 inclusive, and defaults to 0 — a property that says nothing sells no more rooms than it has. `booking_horizon_days` is how far into the future a stay may be sold or restricted, counted forward from the logical clock's date; it must lie between 1 and 730 inclusive and defaults to 365, so a property that says nothing sells a year out. `mix_window_days` caps the length in days of the trailing stay-date window that channel-mix ranking will accept; it must lie between 1 and 90 inclusive and defaults to 30. `rate_mismatch_tolerance_cents` is the absolute cent tolerance within which an observed partner rate is still considered in sync, must be zero or greater, and defaults to 0 — meaning by default any deviation at all is an alert. `required_image_count` is the minimum number of images a channel mapping must publish before it counts as being in content parity; it must be zero or greater and defaults to 0.

A property's serialized form carries `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `code`, `name`, `currency_code`, `overbooking_pct_bp`, `booking_horizon_days`, `mix_window_days`, `rate_mismatch_tolerance_cents` and `required_image_count`. Properties may be listed, page by page, or read one at a time by any of `PlatformAdmin`, `RevenueManager`, `RevenueDirector` or `ChannelOpsEngineer`; registering one is a `PlatformAdmin` privilege alone.

**Room types** are the sellable products. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `RevenueManager` creates one under a property with a `code` of 2 to 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters, unique within the owning property (a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`); a `name` of 1 to 120 characters; `physical_capacity_units`, the number of physical rooms of this type, between 1 and 500 inclusive; `master_description_hash`, the fingerprint of the property-owned descriptive content for this room type, being 4 to 64 characters of lowercase letters and digits; and `master_image_count`, the number of images in the master content set, between 0 and 40 inclusive. The room type's own `property_id` is taken from the creation path and is never supplied in the body.

One optional field on a room type deserves its own sentence because its default is not a number. `overbooking_pct_bp` on a room type is an **override** of the property-level allowance, again in basis points and again constrained to 0 through 5000 — but it may also be null, and null is what it defaults to. Null means *inherit*, and the resolved value the system actually uses is exposed as the computed field `effective_overbooking_pct_bp`: the room type's own `overbooking_pct_bp` when that is non-null, and the owning property's `overbooking_pct_bp` otherwise. A room type override of 0 is therefore meaningfully different from no override at all — the first forces no overbooking on this product even when the property permits it, the second follows the property.

A room type serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `property_id`, `code`, `name`, `physical_capacity_units`, `overbooking_pct_bp`, `master_description_hash`, `master_image_count` and `effective_overbooking_pct_bp`, and its `version` starts at 1.

Only a `RevenueManager` may modify a room type afterwards, and every field of the modification is optional: `name`, `physical_capacity_units`, `master_description_hash`, `master_image_count` and `overbooking_pct_bp`. The modification is where the cascade discipline of §2.9 first shows itself, and it has three consequences beyond the field writes themselves. The room type's `version` is incremented by 1, which invalidates any `expected_room_type_version` echo a client is holding — see §2.8, because this is the interleaving the concurrency rule exists to catch. Every `InventoryDay` of that room type re-mirrors `physical_capacity_units` from the room type and recomputes `overbooking_ceiling_units`, `allocated_units`, `committed_units`, `shared_pool_units` and `pooled_state` by the arithmetic of §3.8. And when — and only when — `master_description_hash` changes, every `ChannelMapping` of that room type recomputes its `parity_state`, because the master content those mappings are compared against has moved underneath them.

Room types may be listed by all four internal roles, optionally narrowed to one property with the `property_id` filter, or read one at a time.

**The sellable calendar** is explicit. A stay date does not exist for sale until a `RevenueManager` opens it, one night at a time, by creating an `InventoryDay` under a room type with a single input: `stay_date`, a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. A room type may hold only one inventory day per stay date, and a second attempt at the same date is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The date must lie inside the property's booking horizon, or the request is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`. Nothing else is supplied: the day's `room_type_id` comes from the creation path, `physical_capacity_units` is mirrored live from the room type, and `overbooking_ceiling_units`, `allocated_units`, `committed_units`, `shared_pool_units` and `pooled_state` are all computed on the spot by the pool arithmetic of §3.8. An inventory day serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `room_type_id`, `stay_date`, `physical_capacity_units`, `overbooking_ceiling_units`, `allocated_units`, `shared_pool_units`, `committed_units` and `pooled_state`, where `pooled_state` is always one of `OPEN`, `TIGHT`, `EXHAUSTED` or `OVERSOLD`.

The consequence of an explicit calendar is that operations refuse to touch dates nobody opened. An allotment whose closed date range covers a date with no inventory day, and a booking whose stay covers such a date, are both refused with `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_OPEN`. Inventory days may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `room_type_id`, `stay_date_from`, `stay_date_to` and `pooled_state`, or read individually — the single-day read is the operator's window onto the live pool arithmetic for one night, and it recomputes nothing, it merely reports what the last mutating event left behind.

### 3.2. Rate Plan Architecture, Derivation Chains and Repricing

A **rate plan** is the commercial product a channel actually sells. Only a `RevenueManager` may author one, and it is created under a property with a `code` of 2 to 16 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and the hyphen, unique within that property — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Its `property_id` comes from the creation path. Its `rate_class` declares the commercial family and is exactly one of `PUBLIC`, `NEGOTIATED`, `OPAQUE` or `PACKAGE`; the class is what later gates which channels may carry the plan.

Two amounts are always required on creation and they bound everything the plan can ever quote. `floor_amount_cents` is the lower clamp and `ceiling_amount_cents` the upper clamp; both must lie between 1000 and 1000000 inclusive, and `ceiling_amount_cents` must be greater than or equal to `floor_amount_cents`, or the creation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Where the plan's *starting* amount comes from is the interesting part, because a plan may either stand alone or **derive** from another plan. A standalone plan supplies its own nightly `base_amount_cents`, an integer between 1000 and 1000000 inclusive, and leaves `parent_rate_plan_id` null — which is its default. A derived plan does the opposite: it supplies `parent_rate_plan_id`, the identifier of the plan it takes its base from, and must **not** supply `base_amount_cents`. Stated as a rule: `base_amount_cents` is required when `parent_rate_plan_id` is null and forbidden when it is non-null, and a request that gets this the wrong way round is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `parent_rate_plan_id` that does not resolve to an existing plan is `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.

A derived plan states *how* it offsets its parent through `offset_kind`, which is one of `NONE`, `PERCENT` or `AMOUNT` and defaults to `NONE`. When `offset_kind` is `PERCENT` the plan must supply `offset_bp`, a signed basis-point offset between -5000 and 5000 inclusive; when it is `AMOUNT` the plan must supply `offset_amount_cents`, a signed cent offset between -500000 and 500000 inclusive. Both fields default to null, and each is required only by its own offset kind — supplying neither where one is demanded is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

**Resolving the base amount.** Every plan carries a computed `resolved_base_amount_cents`, and it is derived by walking the chain of ancestors reached through `parent_rate_plan_id`. For a plan whose `parent_rate_plan_id` is null, the resolved base amount is simply its own `base_amount_cents`. For a plan whose offset kind is `PERCENT`, the resolved base amount is the parent's own resolved base amount multiplied by the quantity ten thousand plus this plan's `offset_bp`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent. For a plan whose offset kind is `AMOUNT`, the resolved base amount is the parent's resolved base amount plus this plan's `offset_amount_cents`, with no rounding needed because both operands are already whole cents. A plan's own `base_amount_cents` is ignored entirely whenever `parent_rate_plan_id` is non-null. The parent's *state* is irrelevant to this arithmetic: a `DRAFT` parent still supplies the value used to derive a child's stored amount. Re-derivations always run parent before child, so re-deriving the root of a chain settles every descendant in a single pass with no iteration.

Alongside it sits `derivation_depth`, which is 0 when `parent_rate_plan_id` is null and otherwise one more than the parent's own `derivation_depth`. Derivation chains are capped: a plan whose depth would exceed **two** is refused at creation with `ERR_DERIVATION_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. In practice a root plan sits at depth 0, a plan derived from it at depth 1, a plan derived from *that* at depth 2, and an attempt to derive from a depth-2 plan is the one that is refused.

Three further optional fields complete the plan. `package_inclusion_cents` is the non-commissionable inclusion value added to every night of this plan — a breakfast, a parking credit, a spa voucher — between 0 and 200000 inclusive, defaulting to 0; §3.6 and §3.8 both come back to it, because it sits outside the price clamp and outside the commission base. `member_only` is a boolean defaulting to false, marking a plan that may only be sold to a booking that identifies an authenticated loyalty member. `negotiated_account_code` is the corporate account code a booker must quote, null by default, and 2 to 16 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens when present; it is **required** when `rate_class` is `NEGOTIATED`, and its absence on such a plan is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A rate plan is born in `plan_state` `DRAFT` with `version` 1, and its serialized form is `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `property_id`, `code`, `rate_class`, `parent_rate_plan_id`, `base_amount_cents`, `offset_kind`, `offset_bp`, `offset_amount_cents`, `floor_amount_cents`, `ceiling_amount_cents`, `package_inclusion_cents`, `member_only`, `negotiated_account_code`, `plan_state`, `derivation_depth` and `resolved_base_amount_cents`.

**Activation** takes a plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and only a `RevenueManager` may perform it. Two preconditions guard it. The plan must currently be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. And when the plan is derived — when `parent_rate_plan_id` is non-null — the parent plan must itself already be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` again. On success `resolved_base_amount_cents` is recomputed by the derivation walk above and the plan's `version` is incremented by 1. Activation queues no distribution work of its own.

**Repricing** an `ACTIVE` plan is the plan's self-loop: the state before and after is `ACTIVE`, and repricing can never move a plan out of `ACTIVE`. Only a `RevenueManager` may reprice, the body must carry `expected_rate_plan_version` per §2.8, and every other field is optional: `base_amount_cents`, `offset_bp`, `offset_amount_cents`, `floor_amount_cents`, `ceiling_amount_cents` and `package_inclusion_cents`. Three preconditions are evaluated: the plan must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`; the echoed `expected_rate_plan_version` must equal the plan's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_RATE_PLAN_VERSION` at HTTP 412; and the resulting `ceiling_amount_cents` must remain greater than or equal to the resulting `floor_amount_cents` once the submitted fields are applied, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Per §2.7 these three land in a fixed order: the ceiling-versus-floor shape check is priority 7 and therefore decides first, the stale-version check is priority 10 and decides next, and the state check is priority 11 and decides last — so a repricing of a `DRAFT` plan that also carries a stale echo reports `ERR_STALE_RATE_PLAN_VERSION`, not `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`.

An accepted repricing does four things, and the third of them is the reason repricing exists. The submitted fields are written. `resolved_base_amount_cents` is re-derived on this plan and on every plan whose `parent_rate_plan_id` chain reaches it, parent before child — regardless of what `plan_state` those descendants are in. Each descendant plan whose `resolved_base_amount_cents` the walk actually **moves** is an entity the request touched and therefore takes its own `version` increment of 1 and its own `updated_at` refresh, per §2.9; a descendant the walk recomputes to exactly the amount it was already holding is left entirely alone, `version` included. One `DistributionBatch` is created carrying `payload_kind` `RATE`, `source_ref_kind` `RATE_PLAN`, `source_ref_id` set to this plan's identifier and `batch_state` `QUEUED`. And that batch creation itself immediately fans out one `RateDistributionJob` per `ChannelMapping` whose `rate_plan_id` is this plan **or any descendant plan of it** and whose `mapping_state` is `VERIFIED`, taking those mappings in ascending order of the mapping's own `created_at` and numbering `sequence_index` from 1. The plan's `version` is then incremented by 1. Nothing is dispatched yet — §3.10 describes how a queued batch actually reaches the partners.

**Closure** takes a plan `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, is again a `RevenueManager` operation, and requires the plan to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. `CLOSED` is terminal: no transition leaves it. Closure has one cascade — every `ChannelMapping` whose `rate_plan_id` is this plan and whose `mapping_state` is `DRAFT`, `VERIFIED` or `ERRORED` moves to `DISABLED` — and then increments the plan's `version` by 1.

Plans may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `property_id`, `parent_rate_plan_id`, `rate_class` and `plan_state`, or read one at a time; the single-plan read is where an operator sees the resolved base amount that the derivation walk produced.

### 3.3. Channels, Connectivity Health and Partner Freeze Windows

A **channel** is one commercial route to market, and only a `PlatformAdmin` registers one, under a property. Its `code` is 2 to 16 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, unique within the property (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` otherwise), and its `property_id` is taken from the creation path. `channel_kind` places it in a distribution family and is exactly one of `OTA_PUBLIC`, `OTA_OPAQUE`, `GDS`, `DIRECT_BRAND` or `WHOLESALER`. `commission_bp` is the commission the channel charges, in basis points of the commissionable amount, between 0 and 5000 inclusive, and is required.

Five channel inputs are optional, and every one of them has a stated default. `sell_adjustment_bp` is a signed channel-level adjustment to the sell rate, applied after promotions, constrained to -3000 through 3000 inclusive and defaulting to 0. `allows_member_rate` decides whether member-only rate plans may be sold here and defaults to false. `allows_opaque_rate` decides whether plans whose `rate_class` is `OPAQUE` may be sold here and defaults to false. `geo_market_codes` lists the geographic markets this channel serves, each element being 2 to 8 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens; it defaults to the **empty array**, and an empty array carries a specific meaning — the channel serves *every* market. `requires_content_parity` decides whether promotion loading on this channel demands mappings that are in content parity, and defaults to true.

A channel also carries a lifetime revenue ledger, every field of which starts at zero on registration and is maintained by the system thereafter: `gross_revenue_cents`, `commission_accrued_cents`, `net_revenue_cents`, `room_nights_sold`, `bookings_count`, `cancellations_count` and `mismatch_alert_count`. §3.9 and §3.11 specify exactly how each moves. A channel serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `property_id`, `code`, `channel_kind`, `commission_bp`, `sell_adjustment_bp`, `allows_member_rate`, `allows_opaque_rate`, `geo_market_codes`, `requires_content_parity` and those seven ledger fields. Channels may be listed by all four internal roles with the filters `property_id` and `channel_kind`, or read one at a time — the single-channel read is where the lifetime ledger is inspected.

**Connectivity** is modelled separately from the channel, because a commercial relationship outlives any one technical session. A `ChannelOpsEngineer` opens a `ChannelConnection` under a channel by supplying `protocol` — one of `XML_PUSH`, `JSON_PUSH` or `ARI_PULL` — and `endpoint_url`, the partner endpoint the property pushes to, being 8 to 200 characters and required to begin with the literal prefix `https://`. Optionally it supplies `heartbeat_interval_minutes`, the cadence agreed with the partner, constrained to 5 through 1440 inclusive and defaulting to 15. The connection's `channel_id` comes from the creation path. A channel may hold only **one** connection that is not `RETIRED`; a second attempt while a live one exists is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

A new connection begins in `connection_state` `PENDING` with `consecutive_failures` 0, `last_heartbeat_at` null, `last_failure_code` null and `suspended_at` null. Its serialized form is `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `channel_id`, `protocol`, `endpoint_url`, `heartbeat_interval_minutes`, `connection_state`, `consecutive_failures`, `last_heartbeat_at`, `last_failure_code` and `suspended_at`. `connection_state` is one of `PENDING`, `HEALTHY`, `DEGRADED`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED`. `last_failure_code` is null or one of `TIMEOUT`, `AUTH_REJECTED`, `SCHEMA_ERROR`, `MAPPING_REJECTED` or `UNKNOWN`.

**Sellability** is the concept the rest of the system consumes. A connection is *sellable* when its `connection_state` is `HEALTHY` or `DEGRADED`, and only then; `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED` are not sellable. Verifying a mapping and booking a stay both require a sellable connection and are refused with `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_SELLABLE` otherwise, and the channel-mix analytics of §3.11 exclude non-sellable channels outright.

**Activation** moves a connection `PENDING → HEALTHY`. It is a `ChannelOpsEngineer` operation taking no body, requires the connection to be in `PENDING` (otherwise `ERR_CONNECTION_STATE_INVALID`), sets `consecutive_failures` back to 0 and `last_failure_code` back to null, and increments the connection's `version` by 1.

**Heartbeats** are how the partner's health is reported, and they are also a `ChannelOpsEngineer` operation. The body carries `status`, which is either `OK` or `ERROR`, and optionally `failure_code`, one of `TIMEOUT`, `AUTH_REJECTED`, `SCHEMA_ERROR`, `MAPPING_REJECTED` or `UNKNOWN`; when `status` is `ERROR` and no `failure_code` is supplied, `UNKNOWN` is recorded. Every heartbeat of either status refreshes `last_heartbeat_at` to the current logical-clock instant and increments the connection's `version` by 1. A `RETIRED` connection accepts nothing at all — `RETIRED` is terminal, and any operation against it is `ERR_CONNECTION_STATE_INVALID`.

An `OK` heartbeat clears the failure streak: it sets `consecutive_failures` to 0 and `last_failure_code` to null. When the connection was `DEGRADED` this also moves it `DEGRADED → HEALTHY`, closing the health cycle; when it was already `HEALTHY` the state is unchanged; when it was `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` the streak fields are cleared but the state does not move, because only activation leaves `PENDING` and only revalidation leaves `SUSPENDED`.

An `ERROR` heartbeat is a **failure event**, and a rejected distribution job on this channel is the other failure event — the two are interchangeable for everything that follows. Every failure event increments `consecutive_failures` by exactly 1 and records `last_failure_code`. Where the connection lands depends on the resulting streak, and the suspension threshold is **three** consecutive failures:

- From `HEALTHY`, the first failure raises the streak to 1 and moves the connection `HEALTHY → DEGRADED`.
- From `DEGRADED`, a failure that leaves the streak strictly below three — that is, the second failure, leaving it at 2 — is a self-loop: the connection stays `DEGRADED` with its streak and `last_failure_code` updated. This is the single intermediate step between degradation and suspension, and it is why the same trigger from the same state can land in two different states.
- From `DEGRADED`, a failure that brings the streak to three or more moves the connection `DEGRADED → SUSPENDED`, and this is the head of the deepest cascade in the system, described immediately below.

**Suspension cascades.** When a connection enters `SUSPENDED`, `suspended_at` is set to the logical clock and then four things happen in order. Every `ChannelAllocation` whose `channel_id` is this channel and whose `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` moves to `RECALLED` with `recalled_at` set to the logical clock. Each such recall recomputes every `InventoryDay` of that allotment's room type whose `stay_date` falls inside the allotment's closed `start_date`-through-`end_date` range: held units drop, `shared_pool_units` rises by the same amount, and `pooled_state` is re-derived. Each inventory day whose re-derived `pooled_state` leaves `OVERSOLD` lifts its automatic stop-sell guard, moving that `Restriction` `ACTIVE → LIFTED` with `lift_reason` `AUTO_GUARD_CLEARED`, which itself creates a restriction distribution batch and its child jobs. Finally the connection's `version` is incremented by 1. Every entity touched along that chain has its own `version` incremented, per §2.9.

**Revalidation** brings a `SUSPENDED` connection back to `DEGRADED`. It is a `ChannelOpsEngineer` operation with no body, requires the connection to be `SUSPENDED` (otherwise `ERR_CONNECTION_STATE_INVALID`), and puts the connection on probation: `consecutive_failures` is set to **two**, one below the suspension threshold, so that a single further failure re-suspends it immediately. Revalidation explicitly does **not** restore recalled allotments — those units are gone, and the operator must carve new allotments if the partner needs them again. The connection's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Retirement** is permanent and is a `PlatformAdmin` privilege. It may be applied to a connection in `PENDING`, `HEALTHY`, `DEGRADED` or `SUSPENDED`; anything else, which in practice means an already-`RETIRED` connection, is `ERR_CONNECTION_STATE_INVALID`. Retirement disables the channel's commercial surface: every `ChannelMapping` on the channel whose `mapping_state` is `DRAFT`, `VERIFIED` or `ERRORED` moves to `DISABLED`, and every `ChannelAllocation` on the channel whose `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` moves to `RECALLED`, which in turn recomputes the covered inventory days exactly as a suspension does. The connection's `version` is incremented by 1, and `RETIRED` admits no further transition.

**Freeze windows** let a partner declare that it will refuse pushes for a period. A `ChannelOpsEngineer` declares one under a connection with `starts_at`, the inclusive start instant, and `ends_at`, the exclusive end instant, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, and `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at` or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `reason` is optional, is one of `MAINTENANCE`, `CERTIFICATION` or `INCIDENT`, and defaults to `MAINTENANCE`. The window's `connection_id` comes from the creation path. A window may not start earlier than the logical clock, and a window falling outside the property's booking horizon is likewise refused; both failures are `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`.

Because partners stop accepting traffic slightly before their announced start, every window carries a computed `effective_starts_at` equal to `starts_at` minus a **one-hour pre-buffer** — sixty minutes earlier than announced — and it is the buffered interval, from `effective_starts_at` inclusive to `ends_at` exclusive, that actually blocks distribution. Two windows on one connection may not overlap once that pre-buffer is applied, and a second overlapping declaration is refused with `ERR_FREEZE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A freeze window is immutable: it serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `connection_id`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `reason` and `effective_starts_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`, as §2.4 states. While a buffered window covers the logical clock, batch dispatch and promotion loading against that connection are refused with `ERR_CONNECTION_FROZEN`.

Connections may be listed by all four internal roles with the filters `channel_id` and `connection_state`, or read one at a time, and the freeze windows declared on one connection may be listed page by page by those same four roles.

### 3.4. Mapping Plans onto Channels: Verification, Content Parity and Rate Surveillance

A **channel mapping** is the junction that makes a rate plan sellable: it joins one rate plan, one channel and one room type, and records the partner's own code for that product. A `ChannelOpsEngineer` creates one by supplying `rate_plan_id`, `channel_id`, `room_type_id` and `external_rate_code`, the partner-side rate code, which is 2 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits, the underscore and the hyphen. Optionally the mapping also declares the content it publishes to that channel: `content_description_hash`, the fingerprint of the descriptive content, being null or 4 to 64 characters of lowercase letters and digits, and `content_image_count`, the number of images published, being null or an integer between 0 and 40 inclusive. Both content fields default to **null**.

Three refusals guard mapping creation beyond the shape checks. The channel must already have a `ChannelConnection` — connectivity must exist before a product is mapped onto it — and a channel with no connection at all yields `ERR_CHANNEL_CONNECTION_MISSING`. The plan, the channel and the room type must share one `property_id`, or the request is `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. And a channel may carry any given combination of `rate_plan_id`, `channel_id` and `room_type_id` only once; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

A mapping is born in `mapping_state` `DRAFT`, which is one of `DRAFT`, `VERIFIED`, `ERRORED` or `DISABLED`. Its surveillance fields all start empty: `last_push_at` null, `last_pushed_rate_cents` null, `observed_rate_cents` null, `observed_stay_date` null, `mismatch_state` `UNKNOWN` and `mismatch_delta_cents` null. Its full serialized form is `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `rate_plan_id`, `channel_id`, `room_type_id`, `external_rate_code`, `mapping_state`, `content_description_hash`, `content_image_count`, `parity_state`, `last_push_at`, `last_pushed_rate_cents`, `observed_rate_cents`, `observed_stay_date`, `mismatch_state` and `mismatch_delta_cents`.

**Content parity** is evaluated on mapping creation, on every content update, on every verification, and on every promotion load attempt. The verdict lands in `parity_state` and the rule has exactly two conditions, both of which must hold: the mapping's `content_description_hash` must be **exactly equal**, as a string, to the room type's `master_description_hash`, and the mapping's `content_image_count` must be **greater than or equal to** the property's `required_image_count` — being exactly at the floor passes. When both hold, `parity_state` is `IN_PARITY`; in every other case it is `OUT_OF_PARITY`, including when either content field is still null. `parity_state` is one of `UNKNOWN`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY`, and the evaluation above never produces `UNKNOWN`. A `ChannelOpsEngineer` updates the published content with an optional `content_description_hash` and an optional `content_image_count`; the update recomputes `parity_state` immediately and increments the mapping's `version` by 1. Note the asymmetry deliberately built into §3.1: changing the room type's `master_description_hash` also re-evaluates every mapping of that room type, because parity is a comparison and either side of it can move.

**Verification** moves a mapping to `VERIFIED` from either `DRAFT` or `ERRORED` — the second of those closes the cycle `VERIFIED → ERRORED → VERIFIED`, which is how a mapping recovers after a partner rejection. It is a `ChannelOpsEngineer` operation with no body, and three preconditions guard it, each with its own code. The mapping's `mapping_state` must be `DRAFT` or `ERRORED`, otherwise `ERR_MAPPING_STATE_INVALID` — which is in particular what a `DISABLED` mapping gets, since `DISABLED` is terminal. The mapping's rate plan must be in `plan_state` `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RATE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` — a draft product is not verifiable. And the mapping's channel must have a connection whose `connection_state` is `HEALTHY` or `DEGRADED`, otherwise `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_SELLABLE`. On success `parity_state` is recomputed and the mapping's `version` is incremented by 1.

The reverse edge is system-driven, never operator-driven. When a distribution job on this mapping resolves to `REJECTED` with a `failure_reason` of `MAPPING_NOT_VERIFIED` or `PARITY_BLOCKED`, and the mapping is `VERIFIED` at that instant, the mapping moves `VERIFIED → ERRORED`, the mapping's channel connection has its `last_failure_code` set to `MAPPING_REJECTED`, and the mapping's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Disabling** a mapping is permanent. A `ChannelOpsEngineer` may disable a mapping whose `mapping_state` is `DRAFT`, `VERIFIED` or `ERRORED`; an already-`DISABLED` mapping yields `ERR_MAPPING_STATE_INVALID`. Disabling cascades three levels: every `RateDistributionJob` on this mapping whose `job_state` is `QUEUED` or `REJECTED` moves to `ABANDONED` with `failure_reason` set to `MAPPING_DISABLED`, each such abandonment recomputes its parent `DistributionBatch`'s counters and re-derives that batch's `batch_state` by the roll-up rule of §3.10, and finally the mapping's `version` is incremented by 1. Closing a rate plan and retiring a connection both reach the same `DISABLED` state for their mappings, by the routes described in §3.2 and §3.3.

**Rate surveillance** answers a question every distribution team asks: is the partner actually selling what we pushed? A `ChannelOpsEngineer` reports what the channel is showing by supplying `observed_rate_cents`, the partner-side sell rate seen, and `observed_stay_date`, the stay date it was seen for. Both are required. Recording an observation stores both values on the mapping, re-evaluates the mismatch verdict, re-counts the channel's `mismatch_alert_count`, and increments the mapping's `version` by 1.

The mismatch verdict compares the observed rate against the rate this system last distributed, held in `last_pushed_rate_cents`. When either `last_pushed_rate_cents` or `observed_rate_cents` is null, `mismatch_state` is `UNKNOWN` and `mismatch_delta_cents` stays null. Otherwise `mismatch_delta_cents` is the signed difference obtained by subtracting the distributed rate from the observed rate — positive when the partner is selling high, negative when it is selling low — and `mismatch_state` is `MISMATCHED` when the **absolute value** of that delta is **strictly greater** than the property's `rate_mismatch_tolerance_cents`, and `IN_SYNC` otherwise. A delta exactly equal to the tolerance is `IN_SYNC`; only exceeding it raises the alert. `mismatch_state` is one of `UNKNOWN`, `IN_SYNC` or `MISMATCHED`, and no history of past observations is retained — each report replaces the last. A channel's `mismatch_alert_count` is simply the number of that channel's mappings whose `mismatch_state` is currently `MISMATCHED`.

One subtlety governs what `last_pushed_rate_cents` holds, and it matters because it is the number every mismatch verdict is measured against. The distributed rate is the **promotion-free** rate: take the plan's `resolved_base_amount_cents`, multiply by the quantity ten thousand plus the channel's `sell_adjustment_bp`, divide by ten thousand and round half up to a whole cent; clamp the result upward to the plan's `floor_amount_cents` if it falls below, and downward to the plan's `ceiling_amount_cents` if it rises above; then add the plan's `package_inclusion_cents`. No promotion of any kind participates. This is deliberately *not* the same number as the guest-facing quote of §3.6, which does stack promotions — the surveillance baseline is the rate card, not the offer. The same promotion-free number is written to `last_pushed_rate_cents` by every applied rate or promotion distribution job, alongside `last_push_at`, and the mismatch verdict is re-evaluated at that moment too.

Mappings may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `rate_plan_id`, `channel_id`, `room_type_id`, `mapping_state` and `mismatch_state`, or read one at a time with their parity and mismatch state attached.

### 3.5. Promotions: Authoring, Two-Person Approval and Channel Loading

Promotions are the offers layered on top of a rate plan, and a `RevenueManager` authors them under the plan they discount. A promotion needs a `code` of 2 to 16 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, unique within the owning rate plan (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` for a repeat); a `promo_kind` naming the eligibility family, exactly one of `MEMBER_RATE`, `PACKAGE_INCLUSION` or `GEO_OFFER`; a `discount_kind` of either `PERCENT` or `AMOUNT`; and the closed stay-date window it applies to, given as `start_date` and `end_date`, where `end_date` must be on or after `start_date` and both must fall inside the property's booking horizon — a window outside the horizon is `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`. The promotion's `rate_plan_id` comes from the creation path.

The discount itself is carried by whichever field matches the kind. A `PERCENT` promotion requires `discount_bp`, a basis-point discount off the running amount between 1 and 5000 inclusive; an `AMOUNT` promotion requires `discount_amount_cents`, an absolute cent discount between 1 and 200000 inclusive. Both fields are null by default, and supplying neither where one is demanded — or the wrong one for the declared kind — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `geo_market_codes` lists the markets the offer is eligible in, each element 2 to 8 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens; it defaults to the empty array, but a `GEO_OFFER` promotion **must** supply a non-empty list, and an empty one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A promotion is unusual in this system because it carries **two independent state axes**, and neither one reads the other. `approval_status` is the commercial axis and runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED` or `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, with `REJECTED → DRAFT` closing the loop; its permitted values are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`. `distribution_status` is the technical axis and runs `NOT_LOADED → LOADING → LOADED` or `NOT_LOADED → LOADING → LOAD_FAILED`, with `LOAD_FAILED → LOADING` closing the loop; its permitted values are `NOT_LOADED`, `LOADING`, `LOADED` and `LOAD_FAILED`. A new promotion starts at `DRAFT` and `NOT_LOADED` with `revision_count` 0, `submitted_by_user_id` null, `approved_by_user_id` null and `reject_reason` null. It serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `rate_plan_id`, `code`, `promo_kind`, `discount_kind`, `discount_bp`, `discount_amount_cents`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `geo_market_codes`, `approval_status`, `distribution_status`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `reject_reason` and `revision_count`. Every transition on the approval axis leaves `distribution_status` untouched, and every transition on the distribution axis leaves `approval_status` untouched.

**Submission** moves the approval axis `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and either a `RevenueManager` or a `RevenueDirector` may perform it. Two preconditions apply: the promotion's `approval_status` must be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`; and when `discount_kind` is `PERCENT`, `discount_bp` must not exceed **three thousand basis points — thirty percent**, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That thirty-percent figure is the same ceiling that caps total stacked discounting in §3.6; it is checked here so an offer that could never be honoured in full is caught at submission rather than at quote time. On success the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` and the promotion's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Approval and rejection** are `RevenueDirector` operations, and this is where segregation of duties bites. Both require the promotion's `approval_status` to be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`. Both also require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the value stored in `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. A director is permitted to submit a promotion — the submission endpoint admits the director role — but that same director can then never approve or reject it, so moving a self-submitted promotion to `APPROVED` requires two distinct director identities. Per §2.7 the state check is evaluated **before** the duties check, so a promotion that is no longer `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID` even when the caller is also the original submitter.

Approval moves the axis `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records the approver's `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id`, makes the promotion eligible for sell-rate resolution from that instant onward, and increments `version` by 1. Rejection moves the axis `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, takes an optional `reason` in the body, stores that text in `reject_reason` — or stores null when no reason was supplied — and increments `version` by 1. Both branches are otherwise identical in their preconditions and in their failure codes.

**Revision** is how a rejected offer comes back. A `RevenueManager` returns the promotion `REJECTED → DRAFT`; the promotion must be in `REJECTED`, otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID`. Revision clears both `submitted_by_user_id` and `reject_reason` back to null — which is precisely what allows the *next* submitter to be a different person, and equally what allows the original rejecting director to approve a revised offer without tripping the duties check — increments `revision_count` by 1, and increments `version` by 1.

**Loading** is the distribution axis, it is a `ChannelOpsEngineer` operation, and it does **not** consult the approval axis at all. A `DRAFT` promotion may be loaded to channels, and an `APPROVED` promotion may sit forever at `NOT_LOADED`; the two axes exist separately because commercial sign-off and technical delivery are different concerns with different owners. Loading moves `distribution_status` from `NOT_LOADED` or from `LOAD_FAILED` into `LOADING`, and four preconditions guard it. The `distribution_status` must be `NOT_LOADED` or `LOAD_FAILED`, otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_STATE_INVALID` — an already-`LOADING` or `LOADED` promotion is refused. At least one `ChannelMapping` whose `rate_plan_id` matches the promotion's rate plan must be in `mapping_state` `VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_MAPPING_MISSING`. Every such `VERIFIED` mapping whose channel has `requires_content_parity` true must have `parity_state` `IN_PARITY`, otherwise `ERR_CONTENT_PARITY_BLOCKED`; a mapping on a channel that has switched `requires_content_parity` off does not block the load regardless of its parity verdict. And no buffered freeze window of the target connection may cover the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_CONNECTION_FROZEN`.

On success the load creates one `DistributionBatch` with `payload_kind` `PROMOTION`, `source_ref_kind` `PROMOTION`, `source_ref_id` set to this promotion's identifier and `batch_state` `QUEUED`, and that batch immediately fans out one `QUEUED` `RateDistributionJob` per `VERIFIED` mapping of the plan. The promotion's `version` is incremented by 1. Nothing has reached a partner yet — the batch still has to be dispatched.

What happens next on the distribution axis is entirely system-driven and is decided by the batch. When the promotion's batch settles to `APPLIED`, the promotion moves `LOADING → LOADED` and is live on every channel the batch reached, with its `version` incremented by 1. When the batch settles to `FAILED` or to `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, the promotion moves `LOADING → LOAD_FAILED`, again with `version` incremented by 1 — a partial success on the distribution axis is a failure, because a promotion live on some channels and absent from others is a parity problem, not a win.

From `LOAD_FAILED` there are two distinct routes back to `LOADING`, and they differ in what they create. A fresh load call by a `ChannelOpsEngineer` creates a *further* promotion batch with its own child jobs, exactly as the first load did. Alternatively, retrying any child job of the promotion's *existing* batch — a job whose parent batch has `source_ref_kind` `PROMOTION` and `source_ref_id` equal to this promotion — also pulls the promotion `LOAD_FAILED → LOADING`, but creates **no** new batch: the existing batch is re-opened by the retry and re-derives to `DISPATCHING` on the next dispatch. Either way the promotion's `version` is incremented by 1. This second route is why a retried promotion batch can still reach `LOADED` without a second load call.

Promotions may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `rate_plan_id`, `approval_status` and `distribution_status`, or read one at a time with both axes exposed.

### 3.6. Resolving the Sell Rate, Stacking Discounts and Rate Eligibility

The sell rate a guest sees is not stored anywhere. It is resolved, for one rate plan on one channel for one stay night, every time it is needed — by the quoted-rate read that a `RevenueManager`, a `RevenueDirector` or a `ChannelOpsEngineer` uses to inspect the price, and independently per night while a booking is being priced. The resolution runs in **six ordered steps**, and the order is load-bearing: swapping any two of them changes the answer.

**Step one — the starting amount.** The running amount begins at the plan's `resolved_base_amount_cents`, the value the derivation walk of §3.2 produced. That same figure is remembered separately as the *base* for the whole resolution, because step three measures the total discount against it.

**Step two — promotion stacking.** Promotions apply sequentially and compound, each one working on the amount the previous one left. They are applied in a **fixed order by kind**: `MEMBER_RATE` first, then `GEO_OFFER`, then `PACKAGE_INCLUSION`. At most one promotion of each kind participates; when several promotions of the same kind are eligible, the one with the earliest `created_at` is chosen and the rest are ignored. For each participating promotion, a `PERCENT` promotion replaces the running amount with the running amount multiplied by the quantity ten thousand minus that promotion's `discount_bp`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent; an `AMOUNT` promotion simply subtracts that promotion's `discount_amount_cents` from the running amount.

A promotion is **eligible** for a given night when all of the following hold: its `rate_plan_id` is the plan being quoted; its `approval_status` is `APPROVED`; and the closed interval from its `start_date` to its `end_date` inclusive covers the stay night being priced. `distribution_status` is **not** consulted — whether the offer has reached the channels technically has no bearing on what the price is. Two kinds carry an extra condition: a `GEO_OFFER` promotion is eligible only when the request's `geo_market_code` appears in that promotion's own `geo_market_codes`, and a `MEMBER_RATE` promotion is eligible only when the request's `member_flag` is true. Because eligibility is evaluated per night, a promotion whose window covers only part of a stay changes some nights and leaves others at the undiscounted amount.

**Step three — the stacking cap.** The cap applies to the **total** discount after all promotions have been applied, never to any individual promotion. Take the total discount as the base from step one minus the running amount after step two. Compute the cap as the base multiplied by three thousand, divided by ten thousand, **floored** to a whole cent — thirty percent of the base, rounded down. If the total discount exceeds that cap, replace the total discount with the cap. Then set the running amount back to the base minus the (possibly reduced) total discount. Capping the total rather than each promotion is observably different: two promotions that individually respect the thirty-percent ceiling can together breach it, and it is the combined effect that is pulled back.

**Step four — the channel adjustment.** The running amount is multiplied by the quantity ten thousand plus the channel's `sell_adjustment_bp`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent. Because `sell_adjustment_bp` is signed, this step may raise or lower the amount, and a channel with the default adjustment of 0 leaves it unchanged.

**Step five — the plan clamp.** The running amount is clamped into the plan's own band: if it is below `floor_amount_cents` it becomes `floor_amount_cents`, and if it is above `ceiling_amount_cents` it becomes `ceiling_amount_cents`. A deeply discounted amount therefore rises back to the floor, and an aggressively uplifted amount falls back to the ceiling.

**Step six — the package inclusion.** Finally the plan's `package_inclusion_cents` is added to the running amount, and the result is the sell amount for that night. This addition sits deliberately **outside** the clamp of step five: a package inclusion may carry the final quote above `ceiling_amount_cents`, because the inclusion is bundled value rather than room rate.

The **quoted-rate read** exposes all of this for one mapping and one date. It requires `stay_date` and takes three optional guest attributes that drive eligibility: `member_flag`, a boolean defaulting to false; `geo_market_code`, the market the booker is transacting from, defaulting to null; and `negotiated_account_code`, the corporate account code being quoted, defaulting to null. The response carries `channel_mapping_id`, the echoed `stay_date`, `base_amount_cents` holding the step-one starting amount, `promotion_discount_cents` holding the total discount actually applied after the cap, `discount_cap_cents` holding the cap computed in step three, `channel_adjusted_cents` holding the amount after step four, `clamped_cents` holding the amount after step five, `sell_amount_cents` holding the final step-six amount, and `applied_promotion_codes`, an array of the `code` values of the promotions that participated, listed in the fixed kind order in which they were applied.

**Rate eligibility** is enforced on both the quoted-rate read and on booking, and every one of its failures carries HTTP 422. A plan whose `rate_class` is `OPAQUE` may be quoted or booked only on a channel whose `allows_opaque_rate` is true, otherwise `ERR_OPAQUE_RATE_NOT_ALLOWED`. A plan whose `member_only` is true, and any resolution in which a `MEMBER_RATE` promotion is claimed, requires `member_flag` to be true and requires the channel's `allows_member_rate` to be true; a failure of either is `ERR_MEMBER_RATE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A plan whose `rate_class` is `NEGOTIATED` requires the request's `negotiated_account_code` to equal the plan's own `negotiated_account_code` exactly, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATED_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. And when a channel's `geo_market_codes` list is non-empty, the request's `geo_market_code` must appear in it, otherwise `ERR_GEO_MARKET_NOT_SERVED`; a channel with the default empty list serves every market and never raises this error.

### 3.7. Restrictions and Their Automatic Propagation

Restrictions are how the revenue team closes down demand it does not want. A `RevenueManager` places one by supplying `room_type_id`, a `restriction_type` of `MIN_STAY`, `CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL` or `STOP_SELL`, and the closed date range it covers as `start_date` and `end_date`, where `end_date` must be on or after `start_date`. Two fields are optional. `rate_plan_id` narrows the restriction to a single plan and defaults to **null**, which means the restriction applies to *every* plan on that room type. `value_nights` carries the minimum number of nights demanded, must lie between 2 and 14 inclusive when present, and defaults to null; it is **required** when `restriction_type` is `MIN_STAY` and must be absent for the other two types, and getting either wrong is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The requested range must fall inside the property's booking horizon, or the placement is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`. Overlap is policed per scope: no two `ACTIVE` restrictions sharing the same `room_type_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `restriction_type` and `origin` may have overlapping closed date ranges, and a second one is refused with `ERR_RESTRICTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Note the ordering consequence recorded in §2.7 — a placement that is both outside the horizon and overlapping reports the horizon failure, because the horizon check is evaluated first.

Every restriction placed by an operator is born with `origin` `MANUAL` and `restriction_state` `ACTIVE`, with `lifted_at` and `lift_reason` both null; `origin` is never supplied in the body. Its serialized form is `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `room_type_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `restriction_type`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `value_nights`, `origin`, `restriction_state`, `lifted_at` and `lift_reason`. `origin` is either `MANUAL` or `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD`; `restriction_state` is either `ACTIVE` or `LIFTED`, and `LIFTED` is terminal; `lift_reason` is null, `MANUAL_LIFT` or `AUTO_GUARD_CLEARED`.

Placement propagates immediately. Creating a restriction also creates one `DistributionBatch` with `payload_kind` `RESTRICTION` and `batch_state` `QUEUED`, which fans out one `QUEUED` `RateDistributionJob` per `VERIFIED` `ChannelMapping` on the restriction's room type. This is the "automated propagation" the team asked for: an operator closes a date once and the payload is queued for every channel that carries the product.

**Lifting** a restriction moves it `ACTIVE → LIFTED`, and who may do it depends entirely on `origin`. A `RevenueManager` may lift a restriction whose `origin` is `MANUAL`; its `origin` must be `MANUAL`, otherwise `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED` — because a restriction whose `origin` is `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` is system-owned and no human role may lift it — and the restriction must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RESTRICTION_STATE_INVALID`. Those two preconditions are evaluated in the global order of §2.7, where override authority is check 6 and the state transition is check 11, so the `origin` test decides first: a lift aimed at an `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` restriction that has already been lifted reports `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED`, not `ERR_RESTRICTION_STATE_INVALID`. A manual lift sets `lifted_at` to the logical clock and `lift_reason` to `MANUAL_LIFT`, creates one `QUEUED` restriction distribution batch that fans out one job per `VERIFIED` mapping on the room type exactly as placement did, and increments the restriction's `version` by 1.

The same `ACTIVE → LIFTED` edge is also reached by a second, entirely different trigger. When an inventory day that an automatic guard restriction protects stops being oversold, the system lifts that restriction itself: the restriction must be `ACTIVE` and its `origin` must be `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD`, and the lift sets `lifted_at` to the logical clock and `lift_reason` to `AUTO_GUARD_CLEARED`, creates a restriction distribution batch with its child jobs exactly as a manual lift does, and increments the restriction's `version` by 1. The two triggers differ in actor, in precondition and in the recorded `lift_reason`, and they are the reason `lift_reason` exists as a separate field.

**How restrictions actually block a booking** is more subtle than a single date test, because the three types read *different* dates of the same stay. A restriction is in scope for a booking when its `restriction_state` is `ACTIVE`, its `room_type_id` matches the booking's room type, and either its `rate_plan_id` is null or it equals the booking's rate plan. `LIFTED` restrictions never apply. Both `MANUAL` and `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` origins block bookings identically — `origin` governs only who may lift them, never whether they bite. Restriction windows are closed intervals, holding both endpoints.

- A scoped `ACTIVE` `STOP_SELL` restriction blocks the booking when its window covers **any** night of the stay, where the nights are every date from `arrival_date` inclusive up to `departure_date` exclusive. The refusal is `ERR_STOP_SELL_ACTIVE`.
- A scoped `ACTIVE` `CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL` restriction blocks the booking when its window covers the `arrival_date` specifically. A stay may pass through a closed-to-arrival date in the middle of its span without objection. The refusal is `ERR_CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL`.
- `MIN_STAY` restrictions are combined rather than tested one at a time. Take every scoped `ACTIVE` `MIN_STAY` restriction whose window covers the `arrival_date`, and take the **largest** `value_nights` among them — the strictest window wins, and overlapping windows are never summed. When no such restriction exists the effective minimum is zero. If the booking's `nights` is **less than** that effective minimum, the booking is refused with `ERR_MIN_STAY_NOT_MET`.

Within the restriction check, the three types are tested in the fixed order `STOP_SELL`, then `CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL`, then `MIN_STAY`, so a stay that trips two of them reports the earlier one in that order.

Restrictions may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `room_type_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `restriction_type` and `restriction_state`, or read one at a time.

### 3.8. Allotments, the Shared Availability Pool and Overbooking Control

Availability in this system is **pooled**, not partitioned. Every room type's nightly capacity sits in one shared pool that any channel may sell from, and an allotment is a *carve-out*: a block of units held back for one named channel over a closed date range, removed from the shared pool for as long as it is held and unconsumed.

**The pool arithmetic.** For a given inventory day the numbers are derived in this order. First the overbooking allowance: `effective_overbooking_pct_bp` is the room type's `overbooking_pct_bp` when that is non-null, and the property's `overbooking_pct_bp` otherwise. Then the ceiling: `overbooking_ceiling_units` is `physical_capacity_units` plus the product of `physical_capacity_units` and `effective_overbooking_pct_bp` divided by ten thousand, **floored** to a whole unit — so a fractional room is never sold, and a small capacity with a modest allowance may yield no uplift at all. Then the held units: `allocated_units` is the sum, over every `ChannelAllocation` of this room type whose `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` and whose closed `start_date`-through-`end_date` range contains this `stay_date`, of that allotment's `allocated_units` minus its `consumed_units` — that is, of its **unconsumed** held units. Allotments in `RELEASED`, `RECALLED` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing. Then the sold units: `committed_units` is the count of `CONFIRMED` reservations of this room type whose stay covers this date, where a stay covers every date from `arrival_date` inclusive up to `departure_date` exclusive; `CANCELLED` reservations contribute nothing. Finally `shared_pool_units` is `overbooking_ceiling_units` minus `allocated_units` minus `committed_units`.

`pooled_state` is then derived by taking the **first** matching clause of four, in this order: `OVERSOLD` when `committed_units` is strictly greater than `physical_capacity_units`; otherwise `EXHAUSTED` when `shared_pool_units` is exactly zero; otherwise `TIGHT` when `allocated_units` plus `committed_units` is greater than or equal to `physical_capacity_units`; otherwise `OPEN`. The clause order matters — a day may satisfy more than one of these descriptions, and the earliest listed wins.

These five derived numbers are recomputed on inventory-day creation and on every event that changes held or sold units for the day: allotment creation, supersede, release and recall, and reservation creation and cancellation. Across all of those events the conservation identity of §2.9 holds exactly — the ceiling always equals held plus committed plus shared pool. A booking that draws from an allotment moves one unit from held to committed and leaves the shared pool untouched; a booking that draws from the pool moves one unit from the shared pool to committed. Either way the sum is preserved.

An inventory day's posture therefore moves through a cycle of its own accord, never by direct instruction. `OPEN → TIGHT` happens when a confirmed booking or a new allotment raises held plus committed to at least `physical_capacity_units` while it remains below `overbooking_ceiling_units`; the day's derived numbers are recomputed and its `version` is incremented by 1. `TIGHT → EXHAUSTED` happens when a further confirmed booking raises held plus committed to exactly `overbooking_ceiling_units`, at which point `shared_pool_units` becomes zero and `pooled_state` becomes `EXHAUSTED`, with `version` incremented by 1. `EXHAUSTED → OVERSOLD` happens when, with the ceiling reached, `committed_units` alone exceeds `physical_capacity_units`; this is the transition that triggers the overbooking guard described below, and it too increments `version` by 1. `OVERSOLD → TIGHT` happens when a cancellation or an allotment release brings `committed_units` back down to at most `physical_capacity_units` while held plus committed remains at or above `physical_capacity_units`; the guard restrictions protecting that day are lifted with `lift_reason` `AUTO_GUARD_CLEARED`, and `version` is incremented by 1. And `TIGHT → OPEN` happens when a cancellation, an allotment release or an allotment recall brings held plus committed back below `physical_capacity_units`, again recomputing the derived numbers and incrementing `version` by 1.

**The overbooking guard.** When an inventory day reaches `OVERSOLD`, the system protects the property from itself. It creates one `Restriction` with `restriction_type` `STOP_SELL`, `origin` `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` and `restriction_state` `ACTIVE`, covering that single `stay_date`, **for every `ACTIVE` rate plan on the property** — one restriction per plan. Each of those creations in turn creates a restriction `DistributionBatch`, and each batch fans out one `RateDistributionJob` per `VERIFIED` `ChannelMapping` on the room type. One oversold night can therefore queue a substantial amount of distribution work, and that is intentional: the stop-sell has to reach every channel that could sell the night again. The guard restrictions are lifted only by the system, when the day leaves `OVERSOLD`, per §3.7.

**Carving an allotment.** A `RevenueManager` creates one under a room type by supplying `channel_id`, the channel the units are held for; `allocated_units`, between 1 and 500 inclusive; `start_date` and `end_date`, the inclusive first and last stay dates of the allotment window, where `end_date` must be on or after `start_date` and the whole range must fall inside the property's booking horizon; and `expected_room_type_version`, the concurrency echo required by §2.8. The allotment's `room_type_id` comes from the creation path, and the channel must belong to the same property as the room type, or the request is `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Every date in the range must already have an `InventoryDay`, or the request is `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_OPEN`. A range extending outside the horizon is `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`.

A new allotment starts with `consumed_units` 0, `allocation_state` `OPEN`, and `superseded_at` and `recalled_at` both null. It serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `room_type_id`, `channel_id`, `allocated_units`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `consumed_units`, `allocation_state`, `superseded_at` and `recalled_at`. `allocation_state` is one of `OPEN`, `EXHAUSTED`, `RELEASED`, `RECALLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, of which the last three are terminal. `consumed_units` is derived, not supplied: it is the **peak per-night count**, over the dates of the allotment's own closed window, of the `CONFIRMED` reservations that drew their unit from **this** allotment. The draw source is the one fixed on each reservation when it was admitted, per the booking-admission rule below: a stay that drew from the shared availability pool is never charged to any allotment, and a stay that drew from a *different* allotment is never charged to this one. That is what makes "starts with `consumed_units` 0" hold literally — carving a new block over nights the same channel has already sold out of the shared pool leaves the new block at 0, and those earlier stays stay in `committed_units` where they already were. The count is recomputed while the allotment is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`; once an allotment has reached one of the terminal states `RELEASED`, `RECALLED` or `SUPERSEDED` its `consumed_units` is frozen at the value it last held and no later booking or cancellation moves it again.

**Overlapping allotments are legal**, and the capacity check that guards creation is therefore an *aggregate, per-date* check rather than a pairwise one. It runs as follows. First identify the predecessor: the existing `ChannelAllocation` with exactly the same `channel_id`, `start_date` and `end_date` as the request whose `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`, if one exists — that block is being replaced and its units leave the pool *before* the new request is measured. Then, for each date in the requested closed range, add up three quantities: the unconsumed held units of every other `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` allotment of the room type covering that date, the committed units on that date, and the units the request is asking for. If that sum is **strictly greater** than the date's `overbooking_ceiling_units`, the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CEILING`. A sum exactly equal to the ceiling is **accepted** — the boundary is inclusive. And a date in the range that has no inventory day at all is refused with `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_OPEN`.

An accepted allotment does four things in order. Any same-key predecessor moves `OPEN → SUPERSEDED` or `EXHAUSTED → SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` set to the logical clock and its own `version` incremented by 1 — which is exactly why re-carving the identical block for the same channel is always accepted, since the predecessor's units have already left the pool by the time the aggregate check runs. The new allotment row is created. Every covered `InventoryDay` recomputes `allocated_units`, `shared_pool_units` and `pooled_state`. And the owning room type's `version` is incremented by 1, spending the `expected_room_type_version` echo.

**Releasing** an allotment returns its unconsumed units to the shared pool. It is a `RevenueManager` operation with no body, admits an allotment whose `allocation_state` is `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED`, and refuses one already `RELEASED`, `RECALLED` or `SUPERSEDED` with `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID`. On success the allotment moves `OPEN → RELEASED` or `EXHAUSTED → RELEASED`, and every `InventoryDay` of the room type whose `stay_date` falls in the allotment's closed range is recomputed — held units drop by the released amount, `shared_pool_units` rises by the same amount, and `pooled_state` is re-derived — and the allotment's `version` is incremented by 1.

Two further allotment transitions are system-driven. `OPEN → EXHAUSTED` happens when a confirmed booking raises the allotment's recomputed `consumed_units` to equal its `allocated_units`, and `EXHAUSTED → OPEN` happens when a cancellation drops recomputed `consumed_units` back below `allocated_units`; each increments the allotment's `version` by 1, and together they close the cycle between the two states. `OPEN → RECALLED` and `EXHAUSTED → RECALLED` happen when the allotment's channel connection enters `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED`: `recalled_at` is set to the logical clock, the covered inventory days are recomputed exactly as for a release, and the allotment's `version` is incremented by 1. Recalled units are never restored automatically, as §3.3 states.

**Booking admission** consumes all of this, and it is evaluated per night. The booking's *draw source* is the allotment when the booking channel holds an `OPEN` `ChannelAllocation` on the booked room type covering **every** night of the stay with at least one unconsumed unit on each of those nights; when more than one such allotment qualifies the draw comes from the **earliest-created** one; and when none qualifies the draw source is the shared pool. Which allotment — or the pool — supplied the unit is fixed on the reservation at admission and is what `consumed_units` counts thereafter; it is not itself a serialized field of the reservation. An allotment draw is **always admitted** — the allotment already booked the capacity risk when it was carved — and it never consumes an overbooking override. A pool draw is admitted only when two conditions hold on every night. `shared_pool_units` must be at least 1 on that night, otherwise the booking is refused with `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. And when the unit being sold lands in the **overbooking band** on any night — meaning that on that night `allocated_units` plus `committed_units` is already greater than or equal to `physical_capacity_units`, so the room being sold is one the property does not physically have free — the request must carry `override_overbooking` true, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_OVERBOOK_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`. The ceiling test is evaluated before the override test, so a night already at the ceiling reports `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED` even when no override was sent.

`override_overbooking` is an optional boolean on a booking request, defaults to false, and may only be sent true by a `RevenueManager` or a `RevenueDirector` — a `ChannelPartner` sending it true is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The resulting `overbooking_overridden` flag on the reservation is set true **only when the override was actually consumed**: sending `override_overbooking` true for a stay whose every night still has room below physical capacity leaves `overbooking_overridden` false.

Allotments may be listed by all four internal roles with any combination of the filters `room_type_id`, `channel_id` and `allocation_state`, or read one at a time.

### 3.9. Booking a Stay, Charging It and Cancelling It

A booking is the point where every earlier decision is cashed in: the rate plan's derivation chain, the channel's mapping and connection health, the approved promotions, the active restrictions and the availability pool all have to agree before a `Reservation` row exists.

**Inputs.** A `RevenueManager` or a `ChannelPartner` books by supplying `channel_id`, the channel the booking arrived on; `rate_plan_id`, the plan sold; `room_type_id`, the room type sold; `arrival_date`, the first night of the stay in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; and `nights`, the number of consecutive nights booked, between 1 and 14 inclusive, one unit of inventory per night. Four inputs are optional. `member_flag` says whether the booker is an authenticated loyalty member and defaults to false. `geo_market_code` is the geo market the booker transacted from, defaults to null, and when non-null is 2 to 8 characters matching `^[A-Z0-9-]+$`. `negotiated_account_code` is the corporate account code the booker quoted, defaults to null, and when non-null is 2 to 16 characters matching `^[A-Z0-9-]+$`. `override_overbooking` defaults to false and is governed by §3.8. A `ChannelPartner` may book only on the channel whose id equals its `X-User-ID`; booking on any other channel is `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`.

`property_id` is not supplied — it is derived from the booking channel — and `departure_date` is computed as `arrival_date` plus `nights`. The guest does not occupy `departure_date`; the stay is the half-open interval from `arrival_date` inclusive to `departure_date` exclusive, and every capacity, restriction and analytics rule in this document uses that same convention.

**Admission checks.** In the global order of §2.7 a booking request must clear, among the generic checks, these domain gates. The channel, rate plan and room type must all exist, or `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_NOT_FOUND` respectively. The channel, the rate plan and the room type must share one `property_id`, or `ERR_PROPERTY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The channel must have a `ChannelConnection`, or `ERR_CHANNEL_CONNECTION_MISSING`, and that connection's `connection_state` must be `HEALTHY` or `DEGRADED` — `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED` are all refused with `ERR_CONNECTION_NOT_SELLABLE`. Every night of the stay must have an `InventoryDay`, or `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_NOT_OPEN`. `arrival_date` must be strictly after the logical clock's date and `departure_date` must be no later than the clock's date plus the property's `booking_horizon_days`, or `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`. The plan and guest must be eligible for the channel: an `OPAQUE` plan requires the channel's `allows_opaque_rate` to be true, or `ERR_OPAQUE_RATE_NOT_ALLOWED`; an eligible `MEMBER_RATE` promotion requires `member_flag` true, or `ERR_MEMBER_RATE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; a `NEGOTIATED` plan requires the request's `negotiated_account_code` to equal the plan's `negotiated_account_code`, or `ERR_NEGOTIATED_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; a `GEO_OFFER` promotion requires `geo_market_code` to be one of its `geo_markets`, or `ERR_GEO_MARKET_NOT_SERVED`. No `ACTIVE` restriction may block the stay, tested in the fixed order `STOP_SELL` → `CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL` → `MIN_STAY` with `ERR_STOP_SELL_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CLOSED_TO_ARRIVAL` and `ERR_MIN_STAY_NOT_MET`. And capacity must hold on every touched night per §3.8, with `ERR_OVERBOOKING_CEILING_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_OVERBOOK_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`.

**Charging.** Once admitted, the stay is priced **night by night**, and each night is priced independently through the full sell-rate resolution of §3.6 — so a promotion whose window covers only part of the stay changes some nights and leaves the others alone. For each night `d` from `arrival_date` up to but excluding `departure_date`, the night's `sell_rate_cents` is the resolved sell rate for that night; the night's `commissionable_cents` is that amount **minus the plan's `package_inclusion_cents`**, because the package inclusion is never commissionable; and the night's `commission_cents` is that night's `commissionable_cents` multiplied by the channel's `commission_bp`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent.

The reservation totals are then `gross_amount_cents`, the sum of every night's `sell_rate_cents`; `commission_amount_cents`, the sum of the **per-night rounded** commissions; and `net_amount_cents`, `gross_amount_cents` minus `commission_amount_cents`. The rounding discipline here is load-bearing and observable: the commission is rounded once per night and the rounded amounts are then summed. Rounding a single commission off the gross total instead produces a different figure — it can differ by a cent or more — and is not the specified behaviour. `nightly_breakdown` is stored on the reservation as one object per night, each carrying `stay_date`, `sell_rate_cents`, `commissionable_cents` and `commission_cents`, ordered by `stay_date` ascending.

**The loyalty credit** is computed after the charge breakdown and in a pinned order. First take `gross_amount_cents` **with package inclusions counted in**. Then pick `tier_bp`: 0 when `gross_amount_cents` is below `LOYALTY_TIER_1_FLOOR_CENTS`, which is 50000; 400 when it is at least 50000 and below `LOYALTY_TIER_2_FLOOR_CENTS`, which is 70000; and 600 when it is at least 70000. Then take `commission_amount_cents` and `net_amount_cents` as already computed. Finally `loyalty_credit_cents` is `net_amount_cents` multiplied by `tier_bp`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent — rounded exactly once, at this last step. The tier is keyed on **gross** while the credit is applied to **net**, and the credit is terminal: it never re-enters `gross_amount_cents`, `net_amount_cents` or `commission_amount_cents`, and it is not deducted from anything. Keying the tier on net, or dropping package inclusions out of the tier key, would put some stays in the wrong tier.

**Side effects of a confirmed booking.** The reservation is created with `reservation_state` `CONFIRMED`, `cancelled_at` null and `version` 1. The booking channel's lifetime ledger is updated in place: `gross_revenue_cents`, `commission_accrued_cents` and `net_revenue_cents` each rise by the reservation's corresponding total, `room_nights_sold` rises by `nights`, and `bookings_count` rises by 1. Every `InventoryDay` of the room type whose `stay_date` falls in the stay recomputes `committed_units`, `allocated_units`, `shared_pool_units` and `pooled_state`. An allotment draw increments the consuming `ChannelAllocation`'s `consumed_units` and can move it `OPEN → EXHAUSTED`. A day that reaches `OVERSOLD` creates the automatic `STOP_SELL` guard restrictions of §3.8, each of which queues a `RESTRICTION` batch and its jobs. And `overbooking_overridden` is set true only when the override was actually consumed.

**Cancelling** is the exact reverse. A `RevenueManager` or the owning `ChannelPartner` cancels with no request body; a `ChannelPartner` whose `X-User-ID` is not the reservation's `channel_id` is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`. The reservation's `reservation_state` must be `CONFIRMED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_CANCELLABLE` — so a second cancellation of the same reservation always fails, and `CANCELLED` is terminal. On success `reservation_state` moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, `cancelled_at` is set to the logical clock, and the reservation's `version` is incremented by 1.

The reversal touches four places. The channel's ledger money is reversed exactly: `gross_revenue_cents`, `commission_accrued_cents` and `net_revenue_cents` each fall by the reservation's corresponding total and `room_nights_sold` falls by `nights`, so that `gross_revenue_cents` continues to equal `net_revenue_cents` plus `commission_accrued_cents` over the surviving `CONFIRMED` reservations. `cancellations_count` is incremented by 1 while **`bookings_count` is left unchanged** — it counts every reservation ever created on the channel, cancelled ones included — which is why `bookings_count` minus `cancellations_count`, not `bookings_count` alone, is the count of live bookings. Every `InventoryDay` of the room type covering the stay recomputes: `committed_units` drops by 1, either `shared_pool_units` rises by 1 or the consuming allotment's `consumed_units` falls by 1 depending on where the booking drew from, and `pooled_state` is re-derived. A consuming allotment that falls below its `allocated_units` moves `EXHAUSTED → OPEN`. And an `InventoryDay` that leaves `OVERSOLD` lifts its `AUTO_OVERBOOK_GUARD` restrictions with `lift_reason` `AUTO_GUARD_CLEARED`, each of which queues a `RESTRICTION` batch and its jobs — a single cancellation can therefore produce a cascade five levels deep.

A reservation serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `property_id`, `channel_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `room_type_id`, `arrival_date`, `nights`, `departure_date`, `member_flag`, `geo_market_code`, `negotiated_account_code`, `override_overbooking`, `reservation_state`, `nightly_breakdown`, `gross_amount_cents`, `commission_amount_cents`, `net_amount_cents`, `loyalty_credit_cents`, `overbooking_overridden` and `cancelled_at`. Reservations may be listed by all five roles with any combination of the filters `property_id`, `channel_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `room_type_id` and `reservation_state`, or read one at a time with the full nightly breakdown; in both cases a `ChannelPartner` sees only the reservations of its own channel.

### 3.10. Distribution Batches, Job Dispatch and Retry

Nothing in this system pushes to a channel synchronously. Every event that changes what a channel should be selling — a rate plan repricing, a restriction placed or lifted, a promotion loaded — creates one `DistributionBatch`, and that batch immediately fans out one `RateDistributionJob` per `VERIFIED` `ChannelMapping` on the affected room type or plan. Batches and jobs are never created directly by a caller; there is no creation endpoint for either. They are created by the system, dispatched by a `ChannelOpsEngineer`, and read by the four internal roles.

**Batch shape.** A batch carries `property_id`, the property whose channels it pushes to; `payload_kind`, one of `RATE`, `RESTRICTION` or `PROMOTION`, describing what is being pushed; `source_ref_kind`, one of `RATE_PLAN`, `RESTRICTION` or `PROMOTION`, naming which entity type created it; and `source_ref_id`, the id of the triggering row. `source_ref_id` is polymorphic and deliberately not a declared foreign key: it resolves in `RatePlan.id` when `source_ref_kind` is `RATE_PLAN`, in `Restriction.id` when it is `RESTRICTION`, and in `Promotion.id` when it is `PROMOTION`. A batch also carries `batch_state`, which starts at `QUEUED`; the four counters `total_jobs`, `applied_jobs`, `rejected_jobs` and `abandoned_jobs`; and `dispatched_at`, the logical-clock instant of the most recent dispatch call, initially null. A batch serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `property_id`, `payload_kind`, `source_ref_kind`, `source_ref_id`, `batch_state`, `total_jobs`, `applied_jobs`, `rejected_jobs`, `abandoned_jobs` and `dispatched_at`.

**Job shape.** A job carries `batch_id`, the batch that fanned it out; `channel_mapping_id`, the mapping it pushes to; `sequence_index`, a 1-based position assigned at fan-out in the mappings' `created_at` ascending order; `payload_kind`, mirroring the parent batch; `payload_rate_cents`, the public quote being pushed, which is populated for `RATE` and `PROMOTION` jobs and is null for `RESTRICTION` jobs; `job_state`, starting at `QUEUED`; `attempt_count`, starting at 0 and counting the number of times the job has entered `DISPATCHED`; `failure_reason`, starting null; and `applied_at`, starting null. A job serializes as `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version`, `batch_id`, `channel_mapping_id`, `sequence_index`, `payload_kind`, `payload_rate_cents`, `job_state`, `attempt_count`, `failure_reason` and `applied_at`. `job_state` is one of `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED`, `APPLIED`, `REJECTED` or `ABANDONED`, of which only `ABANDONED` is terminal — an `APPLIED` job can never be moved again by any specified path, but `ABANDONED` is the only state the model declares terminal.

**Batch state is derived, never set.** Let `open` be the count of child jobs in `QUEUED` or `DISPATCHED`. Then `applied_jobs` is the count in `APPLIED`, `rejected_jobs` the count in `REJECTED`, `abandoned_jobs` the count in `ABANDONED`, and `total_jobs` the number of children, fixed at fan-out and never changed afterwards. At every moment `applied_jobs + rejected_jobs + abandoned_jobs + open` equals `total_jobs`. `batch_state` is then: `QUEUED` when the batch has never been dispatched; `DISPATCHING` when `open` is at least 1 and a dispatch is executing on this batch; `APPLIED` when `open` is 0 and `applied_jobs` equals `total_jobs`; `FAILED` when `open` is 0 and `applied_jobs` is 0; `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` when `open` is 0 and `applied_jobs` is strictly between 0 and `total_jobs`; and otherwise **unchanged** — that last clause matters, because when `open` is at least 1 outside a dispatch the batch keeps whatever settled state it already had. Because `ABANDONED` counts as terminal-unsuccessful, a batch whose remaining children are all abandoned settles to `FAILED` rather than hanging in `DISPATCHING` forever, and `abandoned_jobs` is reported separately from `rejected_jobs` precisely so an operator can tell a retryable failure from a dead one. The rollup is recomputed at fan-out, on dispatch, on every child job resolution, on job retry and on job abandonment.

**Dispatching.** A `ChannelOpsEngineer` dispatches a batch with no request body. The batch's `batch_state` must be `QUEUED`, `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` or `FAILED`, and `total_jobs` must be at least 1, and at least one child job must currently be in `QUEUED`; any of these failing is `ERR_BATCH_NOT_DISPATCHABLE`. A dispatch is also refused with `ERR_CONNECTION_FROZEN` while a freeze window of the target connection, with its one-hour pre-buffer applied, covers the logical clock. On admission `dispatched_at` is set to the logical clock, `batch_state` moves to `DISPATCHING`, and the batch's `version` is incremented by 1.

Dispatch then walks the `QUEUED` children **strictly sequentially, in ascending `sequence_index`**. For each job in turn: the job moves `QUEUED → DISPATCHED` and its `attempt_count` is incremented by 1 and its `version` by 1. The job's mapping and that mapping's channel connection are then examined, and the three failure reasons are tested **in this fixed order**. If the connection's `connection_state` is `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED`, the job moves `DISPATCHED → REJECTED` with `failure_reason` `CONNECTION_UNAVAILABLE`. Otherwise, if the mapping's `mapping_state` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, the job is `REJECTED` with `failure_reason` `MAPPING_NOT_VERIFIED`. Otherwise, if the mapping's `parity_state` is `OUT_OF_PARITY`, the job is `REJECTED` with `failure_reason` `PARITY_BLOCKED`. Otherwise the job moves `DISPATCHED → APPLIED`.

An `APPLIED` job sets `applied_at` to the logical clock, leaves `failure_reason` null, sets the target mapping's `last_pushed_rate_cents` to the promotion-free distributed rate of §3.4 and refreshes the mapping's `last_push_at` and `mismatch_state`, increments the parent batch's `applied_jobs` by 1, and increments the job's `version` by 1. An `APPLIED` job never resets the connection's `consecutive_failures`; only an `OK` heartbeat does that.

A `REJECTED` job leaves `applied_at` null, sets `failure_reason` as above, increments the parent batch's `rejected_jobs` by 1, increments the target connection's `consecutive_failures` by 1 and sets its `last_failure_code` — which may itself drive the connection `HEALTHY → DEGRADED` or `DEGRADED → SUSPENDED` per §3.3 — and, when the `failure_reason` is `MAPPING_NOT_VERIFIED` or `PARITY_BLOCKED` and the mapping is still `VERIFIED`, moves that mapping `VERIFIED → ERRORED`. The job's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Rejection side effects land before the next job is dispatched.** This ordering is observable and intentional: an early rejection that pushes a shared connection's `consecutive_failures` to the suspension threshold of 3 suspends that connection, so every later job on the same channel in the *same* dispatch is rejected with `CONNECTION_UNAVAILABLE` rather than with its own mapping-specific reason. Dispatching the same jobs in a different order would attribute different reasons to different mappings, which is exactly why `sequence_index` is fixed at fan-out and dispatch never reorders. And because that suspension recalls the connection's allotments and recomputes inventory, one dispatch call can ripple all the way to guard restrictions and further batches.

After each child resolution the batch counters and `batch_state` are re-derived. Once no child is left open the batch settles from `DISPATCHING` to one of three outcomes, each of which increments the batch's `version` by 1. `DISPATCHING → APPLIED` when `applied_jobs` equals `total_jobs`; when `source_ref_kind` is `PROMOTION` this moves the source promotion's `distribution_status` `LOADING → LOADED`. `DISPATCHING → PARTIALLY_APPLIED` when `applied_jobs` is at least 1 and `rejected_jobs` is at least 1 and the two together equal `total_jobs`; when `source_ref_kind` is `PROMOTION` this moves the source promotion's `distribution_status` `LOADING → LOAD_FAILED`. And `DISPATCHING → FAILED` when `applied_jobs` is 0 and every child has been rejected; a `PROMOTION` source again moves `LOADING → LOAD_FAILED`. The join outcome depends on the *mix* of child results, not on their count.

**Retrying.** A `ChannelOpsEngineer` retries a single job with no request body. The job's `job_state` must be `REJECTED` and its `attempt_count` must be strictly less than `MAX_JOB_ATTEMPTS`, which is 3; either failing is `ERR_JOB_NOT_RETRYABLE`. On success the job moves `REJECTED → QUEUED`, `applied_at` and `failure_reason` are cleared back to null, and **`attempt_count` is preserved** — it is never reset — so the reject-and-retry cycle terminates after at most three attempts on any one job. The parent batch decrements `rejected_jobs` by 1 and re-derives its counters; when the parent batch's `source_ref_kind` is `PROMOTION` and that promotion's `distribution_status` is `LOAD_FAILED`, the promotion is pulled back `LOAD_FAILED → LOADING`. The job's `version` is incremented by 1.

A retry is the one child change that does **not** move the parent's state: it decrements `rejected_jobs` and leaves the settled `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` or `FAILED` state in place. That is what makes the batch dispatchable again, because a `QUEUED` child now exists; the following dispatch call is what moves the parent back to `DISPATCHING`. This closes the `DISPATCHING → PARTIALLY_APPLIED → DISPATCHING` cycle, and `FAILED → DISPATCHING` works identically.

**Abandonment** is system-driven. When the target `ChannelMapping` is disabled while a job is `QUEUED` or `REJECTED`, that job moves to `ABANDONED` with `failure_reason` `MAPPING_DISABLED`, the parent batch recomputes its counters treating `ABANDONED` as terminal-unsuccessful, `batch_state` is re-derived, and the job's `version` is incremented by 1. An abandoned job can never be retried or dispatched again. The `failure_reason` enum also admits the values `CONNECTION_UNHEALTHY` and `FREEZE_WINDOW_ACTIVE`; no dispatch, rejection or abandonment path specified in this document writes either of them, so in practice a job's `failure_reason` is null, `CONNECTION_UNAVAILABLE`, `MAPPING_NOT_VERIFIED`, `PARITY_BLOCKED` or `MAPPING_DISABLED`.

Batches may be listed by the four internal roles with any combination of the filters `property_id`, `batch_state` and `payload_kind`, or read one at a time with their child counters. Jobs may be listed by the same four roles with any combination of the filters `batch_id`, `channel_mapping_id` and `job_state`, or read one at a time. A `ChannelPartner` may not read batches or jobs at all.

### 3.11. Analytics: Channel Performance, Booking Pace and Channel Mix

Three read-only analyses close the loop. All three are computed from `CONFIRMED` reservations at request time — nothing here is stored except the channel's lifetime ledger — and all three refuse a malformed window with `ERR_ANALYTICS_WINDOW_INVALID` at priority 22: `window_end_date` must be on or after `window_start_date`, the window's inclusive length in days must not exceed the property's `mix_window_days`, and a pace request's `target_stay_date` must be strictly after the logical clock's date.

**Channel performance** is available to a `RevenueManager` or `RevenueDirector`. It takes the required query parameters `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, and the optional `basis`, which is `STAY` or `BOOKED` and **defaults to `STAY`**. It returns `channel_id`, `basis`, `window_start_date`, `window_end_date`, `gross_amount_cents`, `commission_amount_cents`, `net_amount_cents`, `room_nights`, `net_adr_cents`, `commission_cost_ratio_bp` and `health_score_bp`.

The `basis` chooses which reservations and which nights the window selects, and the two views deliberately return different numbers from the same data. On the **stay basis** — the occupancy view — a reservation contributes only those nights that fall on or after `window_start_date` and on or before `window_end_date`, and contributes only those nights' amounts: `gross_amount_cents` accumulates each in-window night's `sell_rate_cents`, `commission_amount_cents` accumulates each in-window night's `commission_cents`, and `room_nights` counts one per in-window night. A reservation's `created_at` is irrelevant, so a stay booked long before the window still counts. On the **booked basis** — the production view — a reservation contributes when the **date part of its `created_at`** falls within the closed window, and then contributes **all** of its nights and its whole gross and commission, including nights that fall outside the window; stay dates are irrelevant.

From the selected amounts, `net_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` minus `commission_amount_cents`; `commission_cost_ratio_bp` is `commission_amount_cents` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `gross_amount_cents`, and rounded half up to a whole basis point when `gross_amount_cents` is greater than zero, and **null** otherwise; and `net_adr_cents` is `net_amount_cents` divided by `room_nights`, rounded half up to a whole cent, when `room_nights` is greater than zero, and **null** otherwise. The ADR division is rounded exactly once, at the end — never per reservation. An empty window therefore reports gross 0, commission 0, net 0, `room_nights` 0, `commission_cost_ratio_bp` null and `net_adr_cents` null.

`health_score_bp` on the same response is the connectivity health score, and it is **not stored** on `ChannelConnection`; it is computed on this read. Let `resolved` be the count of `RateDistributionJob` rows on this channel whose `job_state` is `APPLIED` or `REJECTED`, and `applied` the count of those whose `job_state` is `APPLIED`. Then `health_score_bp` is `applied` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `resolved`, and rounded half up to a whole basis point when `resolved` is greater than zero, and null otherwise. Only terminally resolved jobs count: `QUEUED`, `DISPATCHED` and `ABANDONED` jobs are excluded from both numerator and denominator, so retrying a `REJECTED` job back to `QUEUED` removes it from the denominator and raises the score. The score is a pure ratio and is unrelated to `consecutive_failures`, which is what actually drives `connection_state` per §3.3: an `ERROR` heartbeat or a `REJECTED` job increments `consecutive_failures` by 1, an `OK` heartbeat resets it to 0 while an `APPLIED` job does not, reaching `CONNECTION_FAILURE_SUSPEND_THRESHOLD` of 3 suspends the connection, and a revalidation leaves it at 2 — one short of the threshold.

The channel's **lifetime ledger**, by contrast, is stored on the `Channel` row and maintained on every booking and cancellation as described in §3.9: `gross_revenue_cents` is the sum of `gross_amount_cents` over `CONFIRMED` reservations on the channel, `commission_accrued_cents` the sum of their `commission_amount_cents`, `net_revenue_cents` the difference of those two, `room_nights_sold` the sum of their `nights`, `bookings_count` the count of reservations ever created on the channel, `cancellations_count` the count of those later cancelled, and `mismatch_alert_count` the count of the channel's `ChannelMapping` rows whose `mismatch_state` is `MISMATCHED`. The money identity — gross equals net plus commission — holds on both the stored ledger and the windowed read after every booking and every cancellation. A cancellation removes a reservation's whole contribution and restores the ledger exactly to its pre-booking values, with no residue and no drift; `bookings_count` and `cancellations_count` are monotonic counters and are never reversed.

**Booking pace** for one future stay date is available to a `RevenueManager` or `RevenueDirector`. It takes the required `target_stay_date` and the optional `baseline`, which is `ROLLING` or `STLY` and **defaults to `ROLLING`**. It returns `room_type_id`, `target_stay_date`, `baseline`, `as_of_date`, `days_out`, `target_room_nights`, `baseline_room_nights`, `pace_index_bp` and `pace_state`.

`as_of_date` is the date part of the logical clock. A `target_stay_date` on or before `as_of_date` is refused with `ERR_ANALYTICS_WINDOW_INVALID`. `days_out` is `target_stay_date` minus `as_of_date` in whole days, and is therefore at least 1. The building block is a two-way filtered count: *on-the-books* for a date `d` at cutoff `cutoff_date` is the number of `CONFIRMED` reservations of this room type whose stay covers `d` — meaning the reservation's `arrival_date` is on or before `d` and its `departure_date` is strictly after `d` — **and** whose `created_at` date is on or before `cutoff_date`. `CANCELLED` reservations are excluded from both the target and the baseline. `target_room_nights` is on-the-books for `target_stay_date` at cutoff `as_of_date`.

The baseline differs by variant, and each baseline date carries **its own cutoff** so that every baseline date is measured at the same `days_out` as the target — a like-for-like comparison. On the **rolling** variant, `baseline_room_nights` is the sum, over the `PACE_BASELINE_WINDOW_DAYS` of 7 dates `d` from `target_stay_date` minus 7 through `target_stay_date` minus 1 inclusive, of on-the-books for `d` at cutoff `d` minus `days_out`. Because that denominator is a 7-day total against a single-day numerator, `pace_index_bp` is `target_room_nights` multiplied by seven, then multiplied by ten thousand, then divided by `baseline_room_nights`, and rounded half up to a whole basis point — which compares the target day against the window's daily average. On the **STLY** variant, `baseline_room_nights` is the single value of on-the-books for `target_stay_date` minus `STLY_OFFSET_DAYS` of 364 days, at cutoff that same date minus `days_out`; a 364-day offset lands on the same weekday one year back. Because the denominator is a single day, `pace_index_bp` is `target_room_nights` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `baseline_room_nights`, and rounded half up to a whole basis point, with **no** window multiplier.

`pace_state` is `NO_BASELINE` when `baseline_room_nights` is 0, in which case `pace_index_bp` is null; otherwise `AHEAD` when `pace_index_bp` is strictly greater than `PACE_AHEAD_BP` of 11000, `ON_PACE` when it is at least `PACE_BEHIND_BP` of 9000 and at most 11000, and `BEHIND` when it is strictly below 9000. The boundaries 9000 and 11000 both fall inside `ON_PACE`.

**Channel mix** ranks a property's sellable channels for mix decisions and is available to a `RevenueManager` or `RevenueDirector`. It takes the required `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` and returns `property_id`, `window_start_date`, `window_end_date` and `ranked_channels`. The window is the closed inclusive interval; a window whose end precedes its start, or whose inclusive day count exceeds the property's `mix_window_days`, is refused with `ERR_ANALYTICS_WINDOW_INVALID` — a window exactly equal to `mix_window_days` is accepted.

A channel is **eligible** when it belongs to this property, it has a `ChannelConnection` whose `connection_state` is `HEALTHY` or `DEGRADED`, and its **stay-basis** `room_nights` in the window is at least `MIN_MIX_ROOM_NIGHTS`, which is 1. Both conditions bite independently: a channel whose connection is `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` is excluded even when it holds revenue in the window, and a channel with a `HEALTHY` connection but zero room nights in the window is excluded too. If no channel is eligible, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CHANNEL` at priority 23.

The eligible channels are ranked by stay-basis `net_adr_cents` descending; ties are broken by `commission_bp` ascending, so the cheaper channel wins; and remaining ties are broken by the channel's `created_at` ascending — **never** by `id`. Each entry of `ranked_channels` carries `rank`, starting at 1 and increasing by 1 down the list, together with `net_adr_cents`, `commission_bp`, `commission_cost_ratio_bp` and `room_nights`. The ranking is total and deterministic.

**Setting the clock.** Finally, a `PlatformAdmin` may set the server's logical clock by supplying `current_utc`, an ISO-8601 UTC instant; the response echoes `current_utc`. Every date and instant in this API is read from that clock, which starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a fresh server and never falls back to wall-clock time.
