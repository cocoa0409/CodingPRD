# Small Engine & Outdoor Power Equipment Plant API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An outdoor power equipment manufacturer builds walk-behind mowers, riding mowers, snow throwers and portable generators on a small number of shared engine platforms. That sharing is what makes the plant efficient and what makes it fragile: one emission certification family covers a displacement band, several platforms sit inside that band, and dozens of saleable models — split between the dealer network and mass retail — inherit their regulatory standing from it. A single failed production line audit therefore does not stop one model; it can freeze every platform, suspend every released model, hold every build order and quarantine every unfinished engine downstream of that family. The plant currently tracks these dependencies across spreadsheets, a certification binder and the quality lab's own records, and the reconciliation gap between them is measured in weeks.

This document specifies a single system of record for the plant's regulated flow: emission family definition and durability testing, machining capability of critical components, incoming inspection of carburetors, ignition modules and castings, torque monitoring and hot test on the assembly line, seasonal build planning with pre-season inventory carry, channel positioning between dealer and mass retail, and the service parts commitment owed to the repair network. Every one of those areas is already governed by an external rule — EPA and CARB emission families, ANSI and UL safety standards, the plant's own zero-defect posture on critical castings, the parts-availability promise made to dealers — and the system's job is to make those rules mechanically enforceable rather than advisory.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, regulatory authority is separated from production authority: the engineer who submits an emission family for certification may never be the officer who certifies it, and the inspector who rejects a component lot may never be the officer who grants its deviation. Second, every derived number — a deterioration factor, a process capability index, a carry cost, a channel split, a fill rate — is computed by the server from pinned inputs with a pinned rounding order, so that two people reading the same record read the same number.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All endpoints are mounted at the top level of the API; there is no single owning resource prefix. Collections such as `/certification-families`, `/build-orders` and `/service-parts` are absolute paths.

### 2.1. Plant Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and the role is recognised but that role is not permitted to invoke the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlantAdmin`, `CertificationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `QualityEngineer`, `ProductionPlanner` and `ChannelPartner`.

- `PlantAdmin` owns plant master data — engine platforms, product models, suppliers, assembly lines and service parts — and is the only role permitted to reposition the plant's logical clock.
- `CertificationEngineer` owns emission certification families and durability testing. This role declares a family, schedules and completes its durability runs, and submits it for review; it may never certify a family.
- `ComplianceOfficer` is the regulatory authority. It certifies families, records safety bench tests, grants component deviations, schedules production line audits and records their results.
- `QualityEngineer` owns incoming inspection, machining capability and the assembly line itself: receiving and inspecting lots, opening and closing machining runs, staging engine units, recording torque, running the hot test, releasing, reworking and cancelling units.
- `ProductionPlanner` owns seasonal planning: build orders, release to the floor, and channel inventory positioning.
- `ChannelPartner` is the dealer or mass-retail service counter. It may raise a parts fulfillment request and read the service-part surface; every certification, supplier, machining and production read is closed to it.

Read access is otherwise broad. `PlantAdmin`, `CertificationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `QualityEngineer` and `ProductionPlanner` may call every retrieval endpoint in the system. `ChannelPartner` may read only individual service parts, the service-part list, and the fulfillment requests raised against a part.

Every role in this system carries an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `CertificationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `QualityEngineer`, `ProductionPlanner` or `ChannelPartner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a workflow needs an accountable actor — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a certification family, as `tested_by_user_id` on a safety certification, as `inspected_by_user_id` and `deviation_approved_by_user_id` on a component lot, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a production line audit, and as `requester_user_id` on a parts fulfillment request — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. The two comparisons the server does perform on these recorded identifiers are inequality checks between two operators, described in §2.9, plus one visibility filter: when a `ChannelPartner` lists the fulfillment requests of a part, it sees only rows whose `requester_user_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, while every other role sees all rows.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical plant clock of §2.4 rather than from host wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0, with a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a particular endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock of §2.4 stands still between repositionings, many rows share a `created_at` exactly; wherever this specification orders or selects by `created_at` — including the descending selections of §3.2 and §3.3 — a full tie is broken by the order in which the rows were written, so the earlier-written row sorts first under an ascending order and last under a descending one.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the returned page. Every documented filter is optional and a filter supplied as an empty string counts as absent; a filter value outside a declared enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an identifier-valued filter that matches nothing simply yields an empty page rather than a not-found error.

### 2.4. The Logical Plant Clock

The plant runs on a server-held logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any request is made, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host machine's real time. Every time-sensitive rule in this specification reads that clock: safety-certification expiry, pre-season carry weeks, season-window checks, promised ship dates, the parts-availability commitment year, and every `certified_at`, `completed_at`, `closed_at`, `released_at`, `audit_hold_at` and `allocation_run_at` stamp the server writes. Those clock-derived stamps — together with the `current_utc` the repositioning endpoint answers with and the `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` a durability run echoes back — are rendered in the clock's own canonical form: a whole-second instant as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ` with no fractional part at all, a sub-second instant with three fractional digits. Only `created_at` and `updated_at` always carry three.

A `PlantAdmin` may reposition the clock for test-driving purposes by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, which is normalised to UTC when it carries an offset and read as already being UTC when it carries none; the operation answers with the resulting `current_utc` and mutates no entity. Where a rule compares against "the clock date", it uses only the date component of the logical clock.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Record Versioning

Every resource the API creates carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry three system-managed fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative, such as a derived counter being recomputed or a cascade imposing a hold. A write that would leave every field of a row exactly as it already stood is not a mutation: nothing is rewritten and both `updated_at` and `version` stand unchanged. The two guarded writes of §2.10 are the exception — scheduling a durability run increments the family's `version`, and an allocation run increments the order's `version`, even when the values written are identical to those already there.

There is one deliberate exception. A `SafetyCertification` is an immutable bench-test record: once written it is never modified, and no update endpoint exists for it. Such a record therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision Across the Plant

Wherever a value is derived, its scale and its rounding are fixed. All rounding in this system is **round half up**, and — this is the part that most often goes wrong — **each step of a multi-step computation consumes the previous step's already-rounded value**, never an unrounded intermediate. The single deliberate exception is the `cpk` divisor of §3.5. The scales named below describe the form in which clients supply a value rather than a validation rule: a number supplied with more decimal places than its stated scale is accepted exactly as written, neither rejected nor pre-rounded, and the derivations consume it as supplied.

- A deterioration factor (`deterioration_factor` on a family, `computed_deterioration_factor` on a run) carries 4 decimal places.
- `certified_emission_g_per_kwh` and `compliance_margin_pct` carry 2 decimal places; `compliance_margin_pct` may be negative.
- Defect rates in parts per million (`rolling_defect_ppm`, `defect_ppm`, `scorecard_average_ppm`) are whole integers.
- Sampling quantities are whole integers: the sample fraction is rounded **up** to the next whole piece, the acceptance number is rounded **down**.
- Machining capability statistics carry `mean_mm` at 5 decimal places, `sigma_mm` at 6 decimal places and `cpk` at 4 decimal places; `cpk` alone is formed from the full-precision standard deviation rather than from the 6-decimal `sigma_mm` written to the row, as §3.5 pins. Specification limits `lsl_mm` and `usl_mm` are supplied at 5 decimal places, and each entry of `measurements` likewise.
- Torque band bounds are computed to 2 decimal places, while individual torque readings are supplied at 1 decimal place and compared exactly as supplied, never re-rounded. `torque_spec_nm` is supplied at 1 decimal place and `torque_tolerance_pct` at 2.
- `hot_test_emission_threshold` carries 2 decimal places.
- Money is held in whole cents. `carry_cost_cents`, `build_value_cents` and `total_committed_cents` are integers; the weekly `carry_rate` is carried at 4 decimal places.
- Channel allocation works in whole units, with both raw shares rounded **down**.
- `failure_rate` on a production line audit carries 4 decimal places.
- `fill_rate_pct` on a service part carries 2 decimal places — two served requests out of three total give 66.67.

### 2.7. Emission-Family Authority and the Certification Posture Projection

The emission certification family is the root of authority in this plant. An `EnginePlatform` does not hold an independent regulatory state of its own: its `certification_posture` is a pure projection of the owning family's `status`, recomputed inside the very same request that changes that status. The projection is total and has no other inputs:

- a family in `DRAFT` projects posture `PENDING`;
- a family in `DURABILITY_TESTING`, `DURABILITY_COMPLETE`, `SUBMITTED` or `AUDIT_HOLD` projects posture `RESTRICTED`;
- a family in `CERTIFIED` projects posture `OPEN`;
- a family in `SUSPENDED` projects posture `FROZEN`, which is terminal exactly as the family's `SUSPENDED` is terminal.

Only a platform whose posture is `OPEN` may have production released against it. A release attempt on a platform whose posture is `PENDING`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

Because authority flows downward, so do consequences. When a family loses standing, the effect propagates through platforms to models, from models to build orders, and from build orders to the individual engines and the channel allocation already computed against them; when a family regains standing, part of that propagation is compensated and part deliberately is not. Both directions are specified in detail in §3.8. What matters system-wide is the shape: every one of those secondary rows is genuinely mutated, so every one of them refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`, and a client holding a pre-cascade view of any of them is holding stale data.

### 2.8. Path Resolution Order and the Rule Precedence Ladder

Where an endpoint path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve decides the response. A request naming a production line audit under a build order that does not exist answers `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, never `ERR_LINE_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`.

More generally, an operation may fail several of this specification's rules at once. The rules are evaluated in a fixed order and **the first violated rule decides the response** — later rules are never reported. The ladder, from first to last:

1. **Header authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role is permitted on this operation, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Request shape.** Required body and query fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numbers inside their declared ranges, dates in ISO form. This step also catches `production_week` and `audit_week` that do not fall on a Monday, a `scheduled_end_at` that is not strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, an `expires_on` that is not strictly after `tested_on`, a `usl_mm` that is not strictly greater than `lsl_mm`, a `hot_test_max_rpm` that is not strictly greater than `hot_test_min_rpm`, a `displacement_max_cc` below `displacement_min_cc`, an `msrp_cents` below `standard_cost_cents`, a `defects_found` greater than the lot's `sample_size`, a `units_failed` greater than `units_tested`, and out-of-range pagination. All of these answer `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Existence**, in this order of entity: certification family (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`), engine platform (`ERR_ENGINE_PLATFORM_NOT_FOUND`), durability test run (`ERR_DURABILITY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`), product model (`ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`), supplier (`ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`), component lot (`ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_FOUND`), machining run (`ERR_MACHINING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`), assembly line (`ERR_ASSEMBLY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`), build order (`ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`), engine unit (`ERR_ENGINE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`), production line audit (`ERR_LINE_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`), service part (`ERR_SERVICE_PART_NOT_FOUND`). Each covers both the path identifier and the corresponding body reference where one exists.
5. **Code uniqueness**, answering `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.
6. **Record-version checks** on the two guarded writes, answering `ERR_STALE_FAMILY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_BUILD_ORDER_VERSION` (§2.9).
7. **Target-state preconditions**, in this order: family status (`ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`), durability run status (`ERR_DURABILITY_TEST_STATE_INVALID`), lot status (`ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`), machining run status (`ERR_MACHINING_RUN_STATE_INVALID`), model status (`ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`), build order not planned (`ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_PLANNED`), build order not accepting units (`ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_UNITS`), unit build state (`ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`), audit status (`ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`), audit already open (`ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN`), service part status (`ERR_PART_STATE_INVALID`).
8. **Segregation of duties**, answering `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Safety and compliance**, in this order: supplier suspension (`ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`), safety standard mismatch (`ERR_SAFETY_STANDARD_MISMATCH`), control configuration (`ERR_SAFETY_CONTROL_REQUIRED`), missing safety certification (`ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`), expired safety certification (`ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`), certified emission above the family limit (`ERR_CERTIFIED_EMISSION_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`), compliance margin too low (`ERR_COMPLIANCE_MARGIN_TOO_LOW`), insufficient capability sample (`ERR_CAPABILITY_SAMPLE_INSUFFICIENT`), machining run not closed (`ERR_MACHINING_RUN_NOT_CLOSED`), lot not releasable (`ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_RELEASABLE`), unit conformance (`ERR_UNIT_NONCONFORMING`), line down (`ERR_ASSEMBLY_LINE_DOWN`).
10. **Referential coherence**, in this order: platform posture (`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_CERTIFIED`), platform coherence (`ERR_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`), component category (`ERR_COMPONENT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`), displacement inside the family band (`ERR_DISPLACEMENT_OUT_OF_FAMILY`), channel share coherence (`ERR_CHANNEL_SHARE_MISMATCH`), supersession chain (`ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE`), line product-line support (`ERR_LINE_PRODUCT_LINE_UNSUPPORTED`).
11. **Temporal rules**, in this order: durability window overlap (`ERR_PLATFORM_TEST_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), line changeover conflict (`ERR_LINE_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT`), season window (`ERR_SEASON_WINDOW_MISMATCH`), live availability commitment (`ERR_AVAILABILITY_COMMITMENT_ACTIVE`).
12. **Constraint satisfaction**, in this order: torque reading count (`ERR_TORQUE_READING_COUNT_MISMATCH`), rework limit (`ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), audit sample too small (`ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_TOO_SMALL`), audit sample larger than the population (`ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_EXCEEDS_POPULATION`), open backorders (`ERR_OPEN_BACKORDERS_EXIST`).
13. **Capacity and quota, evaluated last**, in this order: line weekly capacity (`ERR_LINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), build order quantity (`ERR_BUILD_ORDER_QUANTITY_EXCEEDED`), lot depletion (`ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_DEPLETED`), empty allocation pool (`ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_UNITS`).

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: a build order creation that simultaneously names an unsupported product line for the chosen assembly line and exceeds that line's weekly capacity answers `ERR_LINE_PRODUCT_LINE_UNSUPPORTED`, because referential coherence sits above capacity. Likewise, staging a unit against a lot that is both quarantined and depleted answers `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_RELEASABLE`, because a safety rule outranks a quota rule.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this plant may not be taken by the operator who set them up.

The first is emission certification. The `X-User-ID` that submits a family is retained as `submitted_by_user_id`. When a `ComplianceOfficer` later certifies that family, the server compares the certifying `X-User-ID` against `submitted_by_user_id`; if they are the same operator, the certification is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The second is component deviation. The `X-User-ID` that inspects a lot is retained as `inspected_by_user_id`. When a `ComplianceOfficer` grants that lot a deviation acceptance, the server compares the approving `X-User-ID` against `inspected_by_user_id`; if they are the same operator, the deviation is refused with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The approver's identifier is then recorded as `deviation_approved_by_user_id`, so both sides of the decision remain auditable.

### 2.10. Record Versioning Guards on Guarded Writes

Two writes in this system replace a standing decision rather than adding to one, and both require the client to prove it was looking at the current record when it decided. In each case the check is against the parent record's `version` as described in §2.5.

A `POST /durability-tests` request must carry `expected_certification_family_version` — a non-optional input field — holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `CertificationFamily`. Scheduling a new durability run supersedes the family's prior deterioration basis, so if the family's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FAMILY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), preventing the client from displacing a run it never observed. Note that scheduling a run itself increments the family's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a client that schedules two runs back to back must re-read the family between them.

A `POST /build-orders/{id}/allocations/run` request must carry `expected_build_order_version` — likewise non-optional — holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BuildOrder`. A fresh allocation run replaces the standing dealer and mass-retail split, so if the order's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the run is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUILD_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), preventing a client acting on a stale pool size from discarding a split another writer already committed. A successful allocation run increments the order's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where an endpoint is not named in this subsection, submitting a version value is simply not part of its contract.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Two counting invariants must hold after every request that touches them, and both are the reason certain operations look like bookkeeping rather than physical work.

On a component lot, the pieces released to the line are conserved: `accepted_qty` always equals `consumed_qty` plus `remaining_qty`. Staging an engine unit charges one piece to each of the two lots it consumes, adding 1 to each lot's `consumed_qty` and subtracting 1 from each lot's `remaining_qty`. Cancelling that unit reverses exactly that charge, subtracting 1 from `consumed_qty` and adding 1 back to `remaining_qty` on each of the same two lots. Rework, by contrast, does **not** re-charge or refund anything — the engine keeps the lots it was originally built from.

On a build order, the released population is conserved across channels: `built_quantity` always equals `allocated_dealer_units` plus `allocated_mass_retail_units` plus `unallocated_units`. `unallocated_units` is the slack that absorbs whatever the channel caps refuse to take, and the compensating reset described in §3.8 restores the whole population to that slack bucket without touching a single engine.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Emission Certification Families and Shared Engine Platforms

Everything begins with a `CertificationEngineer` declaring an **emission certification family** — the EPA or CARB engine family that a group of platforms will be certified under. The declaration carries a `family_code` of 1 to 40 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and unique across all families; a `model_year` between 2026 and 2035; an `emission_standard` of either `EPA_PHASE3` or `CARB_2024`; a displacement band given as `displacement_min_cc` and `displacement_max_cc`, each between 50 and 1000 with the upper bound at least the lower; a `durability_hours` emission durability period that must be exactly one of 125, 250, 500 or 1000 hours; a `family_emission_limit_g_per_kwh` above zero at 2 decimal places, which is the regulatory ceiling the family will be held to; and a `declared_baseline_emission_g_per_kwh`, also above zero at 2 decimal places, which is the low-hour production baseline that deterioration will later be applied to. It is worth being explicit that the declared baseline is **not** the initial reading of any durability run; the two are independent inputs and the aging calculation uses the declared baseline.

A newly declared family opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `cumulative_audit_failures` at 0, and with `deterioration_factor`, `certified_emission_g_per_kwh`, `compliance_margin_pct`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `certified_at` and `audit_hold_at` all empty. The family's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `DURABILITY_TESTING`, `DURABILITY_COMPLETE`, `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` and `AUDIT_HOLD`, and `SUSPENDED` is terminal. Any operation attempted against a family whose status does not admit it — and every operation on a `SUSPENDED` family — is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`.

A `PlantAdmin` then creates the **shared engine platforms** that live inside the family's band, each naming its owning family through a `certification_family_id` in the payload. A platform carries a `platform_code` of 1 to 30 characters, unique across all platforms; a `displacement_cc` between 50 and 1000 which must fall inside the owning family's declared band inclusively — a displacement below `displacement_min_cc` or above `displacement_max_cc` is refused with `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_OUT_OF_FAMILY`; a nominal fastener torque `torque_spec_nm` above zero at 1 decimal place; a fractional `torque_tolerance_pct` between 0.01 and 0.20 at 2 decimal places, so a value of 0.05 means a band of plus or minus five percent; a `required_fastener_count` between 1 and 12, being the number of torque readings each engine on that platform must report; and a governed-speed window for the hot test given as `hot_test_min_rpm` between 500 and 8000 and `hot_test_max_rpm` strictly above it and at most 8000, both bounds inclusive when the window is applied. The new platform's `certification_posture` is initialised immediately by the projection in §2.7 from the owning family's current status — so a platform created under a `DRAFT` family opens at `PENDING`, and one created under a family already in `DURABILITY_TESTING` opens at `RESTRICTED`.

Families and platforms are both readable individually, and the platforms of a family can be listed from the family, as can the finished-goods models of a platform.

### 3.2. Durability Testing and the Road to Certification

A durability test run occupies a physical platform for a stretch of calendar time in order to measure how emissions deteriorate as an engine ages. A `CertificationEngineer` schedules one by naming the `certification_family_id` whose deterioration basis the run will establish, the `engine_platform_id` that will be occupied, a half-open window bounded by an inclusive `scheduled_start_at` and an exclusive `scheduled_end_at` strictly after it, the `target_hours` the run is planned to accumulate (between 1 and 2000), and the `initial_emission_g_per_kwh` measured at run start on a green engine, above zero at 2 decimal places. The payload also carries the `expected_certification_family_version` guard of §2.10.

Scheduling is admitted only when the family's status is `DRAFT`, `DURABILITY_TESTING` or `AUDIT_HOLD`. The named platform must belong to the named family; if the platform's owning family is a different family, the request is refused with `ERR_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`. The window must not overlap any other run on the same platform whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; overlap is judged on the half-open interval, so two windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending exactly where the next begins — are both accepted, while any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_PLATFORM_TEST_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. When the family is in `AUDIT_HOLD`, one further condition applies: the remediation run's `scheduled_start_at` must be at or after the family's `audit_hold_at`, so that a run predating the hold cannot be used to clear it; an earlier start is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`.

A successful schedule creates the run in `status` `SCHEDULED` with its `df_method` projected from the family's `emission_standard` — `MULTIPLICATIVE` for `EPA_PHASE3` and `ADDITIVE` for `CARB_2024` — leaving `accumulated_hours`, `final_emission_g_per_kwh`, `computed_deterioration_factor` and `completed_at` empty and `extrapolated` false. Three things then happen to the surrounding graph: the family advances to `DURABILITY_TESTING` (from `DRAFT` or from `AUDIT_HOLD`; a family already in `DURABILITY_TESTING` stays there and simply carries an additional scheduled run), the family's `version` increments by 1, and every platform of the family has its `certification_posture` recomputed, landing on `RESTRICTED`. A family may legitimately carry several scheduled runs at once, on different platforms or in non-overlapping windows on one platform. Build orders that were already held by an earlier audit failure stay held through all of this; only re-certification lifts them.

Completing a run supplies the two closing measurements: `accumulated_hours`, the hours actually put on the engine, between 1 and 2000, and `final_emission_g_per_kwh`, the emission measured at run end, above zero at 2 decimal places. Only a run whose status is `SCHEDULED` may be completed; completing a run that is already `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_DURABILITY_TEST_STATE_INVALID`. On completion the run moves to `COMPLETED`, its `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the deterioration arithmetic runs.

That arithmetic proceeds in five pinned steps, and the choice of variant is made once, from the family's `emission_standard`, and recorded as the run's `df_method`.

**Step one — the raw deterioration over the run.** Under the `MULTIPLICATIVE` variant the raw factor is the final emission divided by the initial emission, a dimensionless ratio. Under the `ADDITIVE` variant the raw factor is the final emission minus the initial emission, an offset expressed in grams per kilowatt-hour.

**Step two — extrapolation to the full durability period.** The run's `extrapolated` flag is set true exactly when `accumulated_hours` is strictly less than the family's `durability_hours`, and false otherwise. When the flag is true, the scaling factor is the family's `durability_hours` divided by the run's `accumulated_hours`; when it is false, the scaling factor is 1.

**Step three — applying the scale, which differs between the variants because a ratio is anchored at one while an offset is anchored at zero.** Under `MULTIPLICATIVE`, the deterioration factor is 1 plus the quantity (the raw factor minus 1) multiplied by the scaling factor. Under `ADDITIVE`, the deterioration factor is simply the raw factor multiplied by the scaling factor. The result is then rounded half up to 4 decimal places and written to the run's `computed_deterioration_factor`. This rounding happens **before** the next step consumes it.

**Step four — aging the declared baseline.** Under `MULTIPLICATIVE`, the certified emission is the family's `declared_baseline_emission_g_per_kwh` multiplied by the rounded deterioration factor. Under `ADDITIVE`, the certified emission is the family's `declared_baseline_emission_g_per_kwh` plus the rounded deterioration factor. Either way, the result is rounded half up to 2 decimal places — again, **before** the next step consumes it. Note once more that the run's own `initial_emission_g_per_kwh` plays no part here; only the declared baseline is aged.

**Step five — the headroom under the family limit.** The compliance margin is the family's `family_emission_limit_g_per_kwh` minus the rounded certified emission, divided by that same `family_emission_limit_g_per_kwh`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. It may come out negative when the certified emission exceeds the limit.

The three results are written to the family as `deterioration_factor`, `certified_emission_g_per_kwh` and `compliance_margin_pct`, and the family advances to `DURABILITY_COMPLETE`. Because a family can carry several scheduled runs, two clarifications matter: every completion writes the family's status as `DURABILITY_COMPLETE`, so the first completion is what advances it there while a later completion returns it there from wherever the family had moved on to — `SUBMITTED`, or even `CERTIFIED`, with platform postures recomputed by §2.7 in the same request — a `SUSPENDED` family admitting no completion at all; and the family's stored deterioration figures always come from the *most recently completed* run of that family, ordered by `completed_at` descending and, for ties, by `created_at` descending, with runs still in `SCHEDULED` excluded from consideration entirely. Because the two variants apply the same scaling factor in structurally different places, the same measured readings produce a materially different certified emission depending on whether the family is an `EPA_PHASE3` family or a `CARB_2024` family; that divergence is intentional and must not be smoothed over.

Submission for review is the `CertificationEngineer`'s last act on the family. It is admitted only from `DURABILITY_COMPLETE`, and only when the family's `certified_emission_g_per_kwh` has actually been computed; the family advances to `SUBMITTED` and the submitting operator's identifier is retained as `submitted_by_user_id`.

Certification is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s act, and it is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`. Four conditions must hold, and they are checked in the ladder order of §2.8: the family must be in `SUBMITTED`; the certifying operator must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the family's `certified_emission_g_per_kwh` must be at most its `family_emission_limit_g_per_kwh`, else `ERR_CERTIFIED_EMISSION_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`; and the family's `compliance_margin_pct` must be at least 5.00 percent, else `ERR_COMPLIANCE_MARGIN_TOO_LOW`. A family that is inside the limit but with only, say, three percent of headroom is therefore refused — the plant will not certify to the edge of the regulation. On success the family moves `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, `certified_by_user_id` records the certifying operator, `certified_at` is stamped from the clock, and every platform of the family has its posture recomputed to `OPEN`. The compensating half of the audit cascade also fires here; see §3.8.

### 3.3. Finished-Goods Models: Channel Differentiation and Operator Safety Controls

A **product model** is the saleable machine. A `PlantAdmin` creates one on a shared platform, naming the `engine_platform_id` in the payload, with a `model_code` of 1 to 30 characters unique across all models. Its `product_line` is one of `WALK_MOWER`, `RIDING_MOWER`, `SNOW_THROWER` or `GENERATOR`, and that single choice determines the safety standard the model must be tested under, the operator-presence control it must carry, whether it may have a blade brake at all, and which calendar week counts as the start of its selling season. Its `channel` is `DEALER`, `MASS_RETAIL` or `DUAL`.

Channel differentiation is where dealer and mass retail genuinely diverge. `warranty_months` runs between 12 and 120, but a model sold through the dealer network carries longer coverage: at least 36 months when the `channel` is `DEALER` or `DUAL`, and at least 24 months when it is `MASS_RETAIL`. `standard_cost_cents` is the whole-cent standard build cost per unit and must be at least 1; `msrp_cents` is the suggested retail price and must be at least `standard_cost_cents`. `dealer_share_pct` is the fraction of a build nominally destined for the dealer channel, given between 0.00 and 1.00 at 2 decimal places, and it must agree with the channel: exactly 1.00 for a `DEALER` model, exactly 0.00 for a `MASS_RETAIL` model, and strictly between 0.00 and 1.00 — neither endpoint — for a `DUAL` model. A share that contradicts the channel is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_SHARE_MISMATCH`. Two absorption ceilings complete the picture: `dealer_cap_units` and `mass_retail_cap_units`, each a non-negative whole number, cap how many finished units each channel may take from a single build order. Their asymmetric behaviour is described in §3.8.

Control configuration coherence is enforced at creation time and rejected with `ERR_SAFETY_CONTROL_REQUIRED`. The `operator_presence_type` must be `BAIL` for a `WALK_MOWER`, `SEAT_SWITCH` for a `RIDING_MOWER`, `TWO_HAND` for a `SNOW_THROWER`, and `CO_SHUTOFF` for a `GENERATOR`. The `blade_brake_type` must be `NONE` for a `GENERATOR` — a generator has no blade — and must be either `BLADE_BRAKE_CLUTCH` or `ENGINE_KILL` for every other product line. The warranty floors above are enforced by the same rule. A new model opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `released_at` empty; the lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `RELEASED` and `SUSPENDED`, with no terminal state — a suspended model can come back.

A **safety certification** records one blade-brake and operator-presence bench test against a model, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may record one. It names the `product_model_id`, a `standard_code`, an optional `blade_stop_time_ms`, a mandatory `opc_stop_time_ms`, the `tested_on` date on which the bench test was run and the `expires_on` date through which the certification remains valid, inclusive, which must be strictly after `tested_on`. Both dates are ISO calendar dates. The `standard_code` must match the model's product line — `ANSI_B71_1` for a `WALK_MOWER` or a `RIDING_MOWER`, `ANSI_B71_3` for a `SNOW_THROWER`, and `UL_2201` for a `GENERATOR` — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_STANDARD_MISMATCH`. Blade nullability is likewise dictated by the product line and enforced by the control-coherence rule: `blade_stop_time_ms` must be absent or null for a `GENERATOR` model and must be present and non-null for every other product line.

The bench result is computed by the server, never supplied. A measured blade stop time passes when it is absent altogether or when it is at most 3000 milliseconds; a measured operator-presence response — which for a generator is the carbon-monoxide shutoff response — passes when it is at most 1000 milliseconds. Both bounds are inclusive, so exactly 3000 and exactly 1000 pass. The `result` is `PASSED` when both checks pass and `FAILED` otherwise. Measured times are whole milliseconds between 1 and 10000. The recording operator is retained as `tested_by_user_id`, and the row is immutable thereafter — as noted in §2.5 it therefore carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no way to amend it. A model's certifications can be listed from the model, newest `tested_on` first.

Releasing a model for production is a `PlantAdmin` action, admitted when the model's status is `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED`; releasing an already `RELEASED` model is refused with `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`. The release looks for the model's **latest passing** safety certification — the rows whose `result` is `PASSED`, ordered by `tested_on` descending and, for ties on that date, by `created_at` descending, taking the first. If no passing certification exists at all, the release is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. If one exists but its `expires_on` is earlier than the logical clock's date, the release is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`; an `expires_on` equal to the clock date still counts as valid. A model coming back from `SUSPENDED` — that is, a model taken down by an audit failure — must additionally sit on a platform whose `certification_posture` is `OPEN`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_CERTIFIED`. On success the model moves to `RELEASED` and `released_at` is stamped from the clock.

### 3.4. Supplier Quality and Incoming Inspection

A `PlantAdmin` registers each component supplier for exactly one component family, supplying a `supplier_code` of 1 to 30 characters unique across suppliers, a legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `component_category` of `CARBURETOR`, `IGNITION` or `CASTING`. The supplier's `criticality` follows from that category and is never supplied: a `CASTING` supplier is `CRITICAL`, and a `CARBURETOR` or `IGNITION` supplier is `MAJOR`. Castings are structural, so criticality translates directly into a zero-defect acceptance posture. A new supplier opens with `approval_status` `APPROVED`, `incoming_inspection_level` `NORMAL`, `rolling_defect_ppm` 0, `inspected_lot_count` 0 and `consecutive_accepted_lots` 0. `approval_status` moves among `APPROVED`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`, and `SUSPENDED` is terminal.

Receiving a lot is a `QualityEngineer` action naming the `supplier_id`, a `lot_code` of 1 to 30 characters that must be unique within that supplier (the same lot code under two different suppliers is fine), and a `lot_quantity` of pieces between 1 and 100000. A lot may not be received at all from a supplier whose `approval_status` is `SUSPENDED`; the attempt is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`. On receipt the lot opens in `status` `RECEIVED` with `component_category` copied from the supplier, `accepted_qty`, `consumed_qty` and `remaining_qty` at 0, `defects_found` and `defect_ppm` empty and `deviation_flag` false. The lifecycle runs `RECEIVED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `DEVIATION_ACCEPTED` and `QUARANTINED`.

Receipt is also where the lot's sampling plan is frozen. The supplier's current `incoming_inspection_level` is snapshotted onto the lot as `inspection_level_at_receipt`, and both `sample_size` and `acceptance_number` are computed from that snapshot. A later movement of the supplier's inspection level never re-plans a lot that has already been received. The plan is built as follows. At `SKIP_LOT` the sample size is 0 — the lot is waved through without inspection. At `REDUCED` it is the larger of 5 pieces and two percent of `lot_quantity` rounded up to the next whole piece. At `NORMAL` it is the larger of 8 pieces and five percent of `lot_quantity` rounded up. At `TIGHTENED` it is the larger of 13 pieces and ten percent of `lot_quantity` rounded up. Whatever that produces, the sample size is then capped at `lot_quantity` itself, since one cannot inspect more pieces than were received. The acceptance number — the maximum number of defective pieces in the sample that still permits acceptance — is 0 for a `CRITICAL` supplier, and for a `MAJOR` supplier it is ten percent of the resulting sample size rounded **down** to a whole number.

Inspecting a lot is a `QualityEngineer` action carrying a single input, `defects_found`, the number of defects observed in the sample. Only a lot whose status is `RECEIVED` may be inspected; anything else is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. The count must lie between 0 and the lot's frozen `sample_size` inclusive. The disposition forks on the frozen acceptance number: when `defects_found` is at most `acceptance_number` the lot moves `RECEIVED → ACCEPTED`, and when it is greater the lot moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED`.

The two branches are symmetric in what they write. An acceptance sets `accepted_qty` to `lot_quantity` minus `defects_found`, sets `remaining_qty` equal to that `accepted_qty`, writes the lot's own `defect_ppm` — which is 0 whenever the frozen `sample_size` is zero, as it is for a lot waved through at `SKIP_LOT` — records the inspecting operator as `inspected_by_user_id`, adds 1 to the supplier's `inspected_lot_count`, adds 1 to the supplier's `consecutive_accepted_lots`, and recomputes the supplier's `rolling_defect_ppm`. A rejection sets `accepted_qty` to 0 and `remaining_qty` to 0, writes `defect_ppm` in exactly the same way, records `inspected_by_user_id` in exactly the same way, adds 1 to the supplier's `inspected_lot_count`, **resets** the supplier's `consecutive_accepted_lots` to 0, and recomputes `rolling_defect_ppm`.

The supplier's pooled defect rate is a single ratio taken over the union of all its inspected samples. The population is every lot of that supplier whose status is `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `DEVIATION_ACCEPTED` **and** whose `sample_size` is greater than zero; lots still `RECEIVED`, lots `QUARANTINED`, and lots that were waved through at `SKIP_LOT` with a sample size of zero are excluded, as is any lot carrying no `defects_found` at all because it was deviation-accepted without ever having been inspected. The pooled rate is the sum of `defects_found` across that population, divided by the sum of `sample_size` across the same population, multiplied by one million, rounded half up to a whole number, and stored as `rolling_defect_ppm`; over an empty population the pooled rate is 0. Granting a deviation on a lot does **not** remove that lot's defects from the aggregate — the sample defects still count against the supplier.

The same population supports a second, read-only aggregation that the supplier scorecard exposes side by side with the stored one. That variant weights every lot equally instead of by sample size: for each inspected lot in `created_at` ascending order, its own rate is its `defects_found` divided by its `sample_size`, multiplied by one million, rounded half up to a whole number — the same quantity stored on the lot as `defect_ppm` — and the scorecard figure is the arithmetic mean of those already-rounded per-lot rates, itself rounded half up to a whole number and reported as `scorecard_average_ppm`, or 0 when that population is empty. Because a large clean lot and a small dirty lot count equally in the second variant and unequally in the first, the two figures can diverge sharply, and the scorecard exists precisely so that divergence is visible. Only the pooled figure gates anything: the scorecard read returns `supplier_id`, `rolling_defect_ppm`, `scorecard_average_ppm`, `inspected_lot_count`, `consecutive_accepted_lots`, `approval_status`, `incoming_inspection_level` and `criticality`, and changes nothing.

Once the pooled rate has been recomputed, the supplier's standing and next-lot sampling severity are re-derived together by a ladder. A supplier whose `approval_status` is already `SUSPENDED` is skipped entirely — suspension is terminal and the ladder never runs on it, so its `approval_status` and `incoming_inspection_level` are left exactly as they stand and no quarantine cascade fires, even though the inspection still writes its recomputed `rolling_defect_ppm`, its incremented `inspected_lot_count` and its `consecutive_accepted_lots`. Otherwise: if the recomputed pooled rate is **greater than** 100,000 parts per million, the supplier becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `incoming_inspection_level` becomes `TIGHTENED`; else if the pooled rate is **greater than** 25,000 parts per million, the supplier becomes `PROBATION` and its level becomes `TIGHTENED`; else the supplier becomes `APPROVED` and its level is chosen from the accepted-lot streak — `SKIP_LOT` when `consecutive_accepted_lots` is at least 5, `REDUCED` when it is at least 3, and `NORMAL` otherwise. Both parts-per-million thresholds are strict, so a supplier sitting at exactly 25,000 ppm remains `APPROVED` and one at exactly 100,000 ppm lands on `PROBATION` rather than suspension. A supplier recovering from `PROBATION` returns to `APPROVED` by the same ladder as soon as its pooled rate falls back to at most 25,000 ppm; because that only happens after several clean lots, such a supplier will normally have built an accepted-lot streak of at least 3 by then and will land on `REDUCED` rather than `NORMAL`.

Crossing either threshold is not a bookkeeping change — it stops material. The moment an inspection pushes a supplier from `APPROVED` onto `PROBATION`, or onto `SUSPENDED` from either `APPROVED` or `PROBATION`, three further effects fire in order. First the supplier's own standing and level are written as above. Then **every other lot of that same supplier** whose status is `RECEIVED` or `ACCEPTED` and whose `remaining_qty` is greater than zero moves to `QUARANTINED`; a quarantined lot can no longer be inspected, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, while any attempt to stage a unit from it is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Finally, every engine unit whose `build_state` is `STAGED` and whose `carburetor_lot_id` or `ignition_lot_id` is one of those newly quarantined lots has its `conformance_state` moved to `QUARANTINED`, its `build_state` left exactly where it was.

Deviation acceptance is the escape hatch, and it belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` alone. A lot whose status is `REJECTED` or `QUARANTINED` may be released to the line under deviation; a lot in any other status is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. Two further conditions bind. The approving operator must differ from the lot's `inspected_by_user_id`, per §2.9. And the lot's `component_category` must not be `CASTING` — critical castings are never deviation-accepted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CONTROL_REQUIRED`. On success the lot moves to `DEVIATION_ACCEPTED`, `deviation_flag` becomes true, `deviation_approved_by_user_id` records the approver, and the released quantity is recomputed: `accepted_qty` becomes `lot_quantity` minus `defects_found`, with `remaining_qty` set equal to it. A quarantined lot that was never inspected has no `defects_found` at all, in which case `accepted_qty` is simply the full `lot_quantity`. The supplier's `rolling_defect_ppm` is deliberately **not** revised. The taint travels forward: every engine unit later built from a deviation lot is marked `deviation_sourced` and is hot-tested against a tighter emission ceiling, as §3.7 describes.

### 3.5. Machining Critical Components

Critical rotating and bore geometry is machined in runs, and each run must demonstrate statistical capability before its output may go into an engine. A `QualityEngineer` opens a run naming the `engine_platform_id` whose component is being machined, the `casting_lot_id` feeding it, the `component_type` being cut, and the controlled dimension's specification limits `lsl_mm` and `usl_mm`, each given at 5 decimal places with the lower limit strictly below the upper.

The `component_type` is one of `CRANKSHAFT`, `CYLINDER_BORE` or `CAMSHAFT`, and it fixes the run's `criticality`, which is never supplied: a `CRANKSHAFT` or a `CYLINDER_BORE` run is `CRITICAL`, and a `CAMSHAFT` run is `MAJOR`. The feeding lot must be a `CASTING` lot — pointing the casting slot at a carburetor or ignition lot is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` — and it must be releasable, meaning its status is `ACCEPTED` or `DEVIATION_ACCEPTED`; a lot that is still `RECEIVED`, or `REJECTED`, or `QUARANTINED`, is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_RELEASABLE`. A new run opens in `status` `OPEN` with `measurements` empty, `sample_count` 0, and `mean_mm`, `sigma_mm`, `cpk` and `closed_at` all empty. The lifecycle runs `OPEN`, `CLOSED` and `CAPABILITY_HOLD`.

Closing a run submits the dimensional sample: `measurements`, an array of dimension readings in millimetres, each at 5 decimal places. A run may be closed while its status is `OPEN` or `CAPABILITY_HOLD`; a run already `CLOSED` may not be closed again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_MACHINING_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The sample must contain at least 5 readings, else `ERR_CAPABILITY_SAMPLE_INSUFFICIENT`. Only the sample supplied in the current call is used — readings from an earlier close attempt on the same run are discarded outright, not pooled with the new ones.

The capability arithmetic runs as follows. `sample_count` is the number of readings. `mean_mm` is their arithmetic mean, rounded half up to 5 decimal places. `sigma_mm` is the sample standard deviation computed with the **n minus 1** divisor — that is, the square root of the sum of the squared deviations of each reading from the rounded `mean_mm`, divided by one less than the number of readings — rounded half up to 6 decimal places. The capability index `cpk` is then the smaller of two distances, the upper limit minus the rounded `mean_mm` and that same mean minus the lower limit, divided by three times the sample's **unrounded** standard deviation: this is the one place in the system where a step does not consume the previous step's rounded value, so the 6-decimal `sigma_mm` written to the row reports the dispersion but is not itself the divisor. The quotient is rounded half up to 4 decimal places. A perfectly repeatable sample where `sigma_mm` comes out as zero would divide by zero, so in that single case `cpk` is capped at 9.9999 instead.

The run then forks on the **rounded** `cpk` against the gate its criticality demands: 1.33 for a `CRITICAL` run and 1.00 for a `MAJOR` run. The comparison is inclusive at the gate, so a `MAJOR` run landing on exactly 1.0000 clears. Clearing the gate moves the run to `CLOSED`, and only a `CLOSED` run may supply the critical component of an engine unit. Missing the gate moves the run to `CAPABILITY_HOLD`; the capability figures are written either way, and `closed_at` is stamped from the clock on either outcome. A run sitting in `CAPABILITY_HOLD` may be re-measured and closed again as many times as needed: each attempt overwrites `sample_count`, `mean_mm`, `sigma_mm` and `cpk` with the new sample's figures, and the run either clears the gate and reaches `CLOSED` or stays in `CAPABILITY_HOLD`. `CLOSED` is the end of the road. Staging an engine unit against a run whose status is `OPEN` or `CAPABILITY_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_MACHINING_RUN_NOT_CLOSED`.

### 3.6. Seasonal Production Planning and Line Capacity

Assembly lines are plant master data. A `PlantAdmin` creates one with a `line_code` of 1 to 20 characters unique across lines, a non-empty `supported_product_lines` array drawn from `WALK_MOWER`, `RIDING_MOWER`, `SNOW_THROWER` and `GENERATOR`, a `weekly_capacity_units` between 1 and 100000 stating how many units the line can build in one production week, an optional `changeover_gap_weeks` between 0 and 4 that defaults to 1, and an optional `status` that defaults to `RUNNING` and may otherwise be `DOWN`. The changeover gap is the minimum whole-week separation the line requires between orders of *different* product lines — the time it takes to retool.

A `ProductionPlanner` plans a build order against a released model. The payload names the `product_model_id` being built and the `assembly_line_id` that will be occupied, a `season` of `PRE_SEASON` or `IN_SEASON`, a `production_week` given as the ISO date of the first occupied week which **must** fall on a Monday, a `week_span` between 1 and 8 counting consecutive occupied weeks inclusive of `production_week`, and a `planned_quantity` between 1 and 100000 spread across the whole span. Pre-season builds carry finished inventory forward to the season start and are charged for it; in-season builds are not.

Four gates guard the plan beyond mere existence. The model's status must be `RELEASED`, else `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID` — a `DRAFT` model that was never released and a model suspended by an audit failure are equally unbuildable. The model's `product_line` must appear in the line's `supported_product_lines`, else `ERR_LINE_PRODUCT_LINE_UNSUPPORTED`. The season declaration must agree with the calendar: a `PRE_SEASON` order requires its `production_week` to fall strictly before its product line's season start, and an `IN_SEASON` order requires its `production_week` to fall on or after that season start, with a contradiction refused as `ERR_SEASON_WINDOW_MISMATCH`. The four season starts are fixed dates: walk-behind mowers open on 2027-03-01, riding mowers on 2027-03-15, snow equipment much earlier on 2026-11-02, and generators on 2027-05-03.

The changeover buffer then looks sideways at the line's other commitments. If any other build order whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` sits on the same assembly line, is for a **different** `product_line`, and occupies a week that lies within `changeover_gap_weeks` whole weeks of any week this order would occupy, the plan is refused with `ERR_LINE_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT`. Orders of the same product line never conflict on changeover, however tightly packed.

Capacity is checked last, and it is checked per week rather than in aggregate. An order spreads its `planned_quantity` evenly across its span, contributing to each occupied week a load of `planned_quantity` divided by `week_span`, rounded **up** to a whole unit. For every week this order would occupy, the sum of that per-week load over all orders on the same line and in the same week whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` — this order included — must be at most the line's `weekly_capacity_units`. A breach in even one occupied week refuses the whole plan with `ERR_LINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful plan opens the order in `status` `PLANNED` with `allocation_status` `UNALLOCATED`, all of `staged_quantity`, `built_quantity`, `allocated_dealer_units`, `allocated_mass_retail_units` and `unallocated_units` at 0, and `allocation_run_at`, `held_reason`, `status_before_hold` and `released_at` empty. The order's lifecycle runs `PLANNED`, `RELEASED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `HELD` and `COMPLETED`, with `COMPLETED` terminal.

The order's committed cost is computed at plan time in a pinned order. `build_value_cents` is `planned_quantity` multiplied by the model's `standard_cost_cents`. The weekly `carry_rate` is then selected **from `build_value_cents` alone**: 0.0040 when the build value is below 5,000,000 cents, 0.0030 when it is below 20,000,000 cents, and 0.0020 otherwise — so the tier ceilings are exclusive at the bottom of the next tier and a build value of exactly 20,000,000 cents lands on the 0.0020 rate. `carry_weeks` is 0 for an `IN_SEASON` order; for a `PRE_SEASON` order it is the number of whole weeks from `production_week` to the product line's season start, that is, the day count between the two dates divided by 7 and rounded **down**. `carry_cost_cents` is `build_value_cents` multiplied by `carry_rate` multiplied by `carry_weeks`, rounded half up to whole cents. `total_committed_cents` is `build_value_cents` plus `carry_cost_cents`. That last sum is what the plant commits, but it never feeds back into tier selection: the carry rate is chosen from the build value and only from the build value, however large the resulting total becomes.

Releasing a planned order to the floor is again a `ProductionPlanner` action, and it is where every upstream gate is collected — evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8 rather than in the order they are listed here, so a model that is no longer `RELEASED` outranks an order that is not `PLANNED`, and a missing or expired safety certification outranks a line that is `DOWN`. The order must be in `PLANNED`; an order already `RELEASED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `HELD` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_PLANNED`. The assembly line's `status` must be `RUNNING`, else `ERR_ASSEMBLY_LINE_DOWN`. The model must still be `RELEASED`, else `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`. The model's latest passing safety certification must exist and must still be valid at the clock date, else `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` or `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` respectively, selected and compared exactly as in §3.3. And the model's platform must carry `certification_posture` `OPEN`, else `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_CERTIFIED`. On success the order moves `PLANNED → RELEASED` and `released_at` is stamped from the clock.

Build orders are readable individually and as a list, filterable by `product_model_id`, `assembly_line_id`, `status` and `season`.

### 3.7. Engine Assembly, Torque Monitoring and Hot Test

An engine unit carries **two independent state dimensions**, and keeping them apart is the single most important thing to understand about assembly. `build_state` is physical progress: `STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED`, `HOT_TESTED`, `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED`, with `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` both terminal. `conformance_state` is quality standing: `PENDING`, `CONFORMING`, `NONCONFORMING` or `QUARANTINED`. Neither dimension is derivable from the other. A quarantine cascade moves only the conformance axis and leaves the build axis untouched; a rework moves the build axis backwards and resets the conformance axis; a cancellation moves the build axis to its terminal value and leaves the conformance axis exactly as it was.

Staging a unit is a `QualityEngineer` action that binds four independent upstream records at once. The payload names the `build_order_id` the unit is built against, the `machining_run_id` supplying its critical component, the `carburetor_lot_id` supplying its carburetor, the `ignition_lot_id` supplying its ignition module, and a `serial_number` of 1 to 30 characters unique across all units.

Staging is admitted only against an order whose status is `RELEASED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; an order still `PLANNED`, or `HELD`, or already `COMPLETED`, refuses new units with `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_UNITS`. The machining run must be `CLOSED`, per §3.5. The machining run's `engine_platform_id` must equal the `engine_platform_id` of the model the build order is for — machining cut for one platform cannot be dropped into an engine of another — and a divergence is refused with `ERR_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`. The `carburetor_lot_id` must resolve to a lot whose `component_category` is `CARBURETOR` and the `ignition_lot_id` to a lot whose category is `IGNITION`, with any cross-wiring refused as `ERR_COMPONENT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. Each consumed lot's status must be `ACCEPTED` or `DEVIATION_ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Then two quota checks: staging one more unit must not push the order's `staged_quantity` plus `built_quantity` past its `planned_quantity`, else `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_QUANTITY_EXCEEDED`; and each consumed lot must have `remaining_qty` of at least 1, else `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_DEPLETED`.

A successful staging creates the unit with `build_state` `STAGED` and `conformance_state` `PENDING`, `rework_count` at 0, and `torque_readings`, `out_of_spec_fastener_count`, `measured_rpm`, `measured_emission_g_per_kwh`, `hot_test_emission_threshold` and `released_at` all empty. Its `deviation_sourced` flag is set true when *either* consumed lot carries `deviation_flag` true, and false only when neither does. Each of the two consumed lots has 1 added to `consumed_qty` and 1 subtracted from `remaining_qty`, per §2.11 — note that the bound machining run and its casting lot are not charged in this way; only the carburetor and ignition lots are consumed piecewise. The order's `staged_quantity` rises by 1, and the very first unit staged against a `RELEASED` order advances that order `RELEASED → IN_PRODUCTION`.

Torque monitoring comes next. The line reports `torque_readings`, an array of objects each carrying a `station` integer and a `torque_nm` value at 1 decimal place. The array must contain **exactly** the platform's `required_fastener_count` entries — neither fewer nor more — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_TORQUE_READING_COUNT_MISMATCH`. Recording torque is admitted only when the unit's `build_state` is `STAGED`, else `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`, and only when its `conformance_state` is `PENDING` or `NONCONFORMING`; a unit whose conformance has been moved to `QUARANTINED` cannot be advanced and is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NONCONFORMING`.

The tolerance band is derived from the platform: the lower bound is `torque_spec_nm` multiplied by one minus `torque_tolerance_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the upper bound is `torque_spec_nm` multiplied by one plus `torque_tolerance_pct`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The band is **inclusive** at both ends. `out_of_spec_fastener_count` is the number of readings whose `torque_nm` is strictly below the lower bound or strictly above the upper bound. A platform specified at 22.0 Nm with a tolerance of 0.05 therefore accepts everything from 20.90 through 23.10 inclusive. The unit's `build_state` advances `STAGED → ASSEMBLED` regardless of the count, and its `conformance_state` becomes `NONCONFORMING` when `out_of_spec_fastener_count` is greater than zero, or is left at `PENDING` when every fastener was in band.

The hot test follows, supplying the observed `measured_rpm` (a whole number between 0 and 10000) and the observed `measured_emission_g_per_kwh` (above zero at 2 decimal places). It is admitted only when `build_state` is `ASSEMBLED`, else `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`, and only when `conformance_state` is `PENDING`, else `ERR_UNIT_NONCONFORMING` — a nonconforming unit must be reworked before it can be hot-tested, and a quarantined unit is refused outright.

The unit's `hot_test_emission_threshold` is derived from the certified emission of the family owning its platform, multiplied by a factor and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. For an ordinary unit the factor is 1.10, allowing a ten percent production allowance above the certified value. For a `deviation_sourced` unit the factor is 1.00, so the allowance disappears entirely and the engine must meet the certified emission exactly — this is the price of accepting a deviated component. The unit passes when both of two inclusive comparisons hold: `measured_rpm` lies at or above the platform's `hot_test_min_rpm` and at or below its `hot_test_max_rpm`, and `measured_emission_g_per_kwh` is at most `hot_test_emission_threshold`. The `build_state` advances `ASSEMBLED → HOT_TESTED` either way, and `conformance_state` becomes `CONFORMING` when both comparisons hold and `NONCONFORMING` when either fails.

Releasing a unit to finished goods requires `build_state` `HOT_TESTED`, else `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`; `conformance_state` `CONFORMING`, else `ERR_UNIT_NONCONFORMING`; and a parent order whose status is `RELEASED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, else `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_UNITS`, which by the ladder of §2.8 is reported ahead of either unit check. The unit reaches the terminal `RELEASED` build state with `released_at` stamped from the clock, the order's `built_quantity` rises by 1 while `staged_quantity` falls by 1, and the unit joins the order's allocation pool as slack: `unallocated_units` rises by 1 so that §2.11's channel invariant continues to hold, while `allocated_dealer_units`, `allocated_mass_retail_units`, `allocation_status` and `allocation_run_at` are left exactly as the last allocation run left them. Progress counters are always the plain counts they claim to be: `staged_quantity` is the number of the order's units whose `build_state` is `STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `HOT_TESTED`, `built_quantity` is the number whose `build_state` is `RELEASED`, and a `CANCELLED` unit counts toward neither. When the release brings `built_quantity` up to the order's `planned_quantity`, the order transitions `IN_PRODUCTION → COMPLETED` in the same request; a `COMPLETED` order accepts no further units, though allocation runs remain permitted on it.

Rework closes the conformance cycle. A unit whose `build_state` is `ASSEMBLED` or `HOT_TESTED` and whose `conformance_state` is `NONCONFORMING` may be sent back to staging, provided its `rework_count` is still below 2; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Rework adds 1 to `rework_count`, steps `build_state` back to `STAGED`, resets `conformance_state` to `PENDING`, and clears `torque_readings`, `out_of_spec_fastener_count`, `measured_rpm`, `measured_emission_g_per_kwh` and `hot_test_emission_threshold` back to empty. As §2.11 states, component consumption is not re-charged — the engine keeps its original lots, so a unit can travel `PENDING → NONCONFORMING → PENDING` twice without consuming a single extra piece. A unit whose `conformance_state` is not `NONCONFORMING` may not be reworked, and one whose `build_state` is outside `ASSEMBLED` and `HOT_TESTED` is refused on the state axis instead.

Cancellation is the compensating action for staging. A unit whose `build_state` is `STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `HOT_TESTED` may be scrapped; a unit already `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation moves `build_state` to the terminal `CANCELLED`, leaves `conformance_state` untouched, returns one piece to each of the carburetor and ignition lots by subtracting 1 from `consumed_qty` and adding 1 to `remaining_qty` on each, and subtracts 1 from the order's `staged_quantity`.

Units are readable individually and as a list, filterable by `build_order_id`, `machining_run_id`, `carburetor_lot_id`, `ignition_lot_id`, `build_state` and `conformance_state`.

### 3.8. Channel Inventory Positioning and Production Line Audits

Once an order has released units, a `ProductionPlanner` positions them between the dealer network and mass retail. The allocation pool is exactly the order's `built_quantity` — units whose `build_state` is `RELEASED`. Units still `STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `HOT_TESTED`, units `CANCELLED`, and units belonging to any other order are all excluded. Running allocation on an order whose released pool is empty is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_UNITS`. Beyond that pool check the order's own status gates nothing: a run is admitted on a `RELEASED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `HELD` or `COMPLETED` order alike. The run also carries the `expected_build_order_version` guard of §2.10.

The waterfall runs in three pinned steps. **First, the nominal split with a pinned rounding order.** The raw dealer share is the pool multiplied by the model's `dealer_share_pct`, and the raw mass-retail share is the pool multiplied by one minus `dealer_share_pct`. Both raw shares are rounded **down** to whole units. Because a two-bucket split of a whole pool can strand at most one unit, the remainder — the pool minus the two floored shares — is either 0 or 1. When it is 1, that single unit goes to whichever bucket had the larger fractional part, and when the two fractional parts are equal the unit goes to the **dealer** bucket. Ties are broken by bucket priority in this way and never by entity identifier.

**Second, the mass-retail ceiling, whose overflow spills.** If the mass-retail figure exceeds the model's `mass_retail_cap_units`, the excess above that cap is moved over to the dealer figure and the mass-retail figure is set to the cap. Mass retail simply cannot absorb more than its cap, and the units it refuses are still saleable through dealers.

**Third, the dealer ceiling, whose excess is held back.** If the dealer figure — now possibly inflated by the spill from step two — exceeds the model's `dealer_cap_units`, the excess above that cap becomes `unallocated_units` and the dealer figure is set to the cap. This excess is *not* spilled anywhere; it is held back. The asymmetry between the two caps is deliberate: mass-retail overflow finds a home, dealer overflow does not.

The results are written as `allocated_dealer_units`, `allocated_mass_retail_units` and `unallocated_units`, `allocation_status` becomes `ALLOCATED`, `allocation_run_at` is stamped from the clock, and the order's `version` increments by 1. The conservation invariant of §2.11 holds after every run. A compensating reset is also available to the planner whatever the order's current status and with no version guard of its own: it sets `allocated_dealer_units` and `allocated_mass_retail_units` to 0, sets `unallocated_units` to the full `built_quantity`, returns `allocation_status` to `UNALLOCATED` and clears `allocation_run_at`, all without touching a single engine and while preserving the same invariant.

Production line audits are the compliance side of the same order. A `ComplianceOfficer` schedules one against an order for a specific `audit_week`, given as an ISO date that **must** fall on a Monday. An order may carry at most one open audit: scheduling a second while one is still `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN`. The order's own status places no condition on scheduling, so an order still `PLANNED`, one `HELD`, and one already `COMPLETED` may each be audited. At schedule time two values are frozen onto the audit. Its `certification_family_id` is **resolved by the server, never supplied by the client**, by walking from the build order to its product model, from the model to its engine platform, and from the platform to that platform's certification family, and the resolved family is snapshotted onto the audit row. Its `required_sample_size` is the larger of 2 units and two percent of the order's `built_quantity` rounded **up** to a whole unit, and it is likewise frozen — later releases on the order do not enlarge it. The audit opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `units_tested`, `units_failed`, `max_measured_emission_g_per_kwh`, `failure_rate`, `failure_reason` and `recorded_by_user_id` all empty; the lifecycle runs `SCHEDULED`, `PASSED` and `FAILED`, and both outcomes are terminal.

Recording the result supplies `units_tested`, the number of units actually pulled and tested and at least 1; `units_failed`, between 0 and `units_tested`; and `max_measured_emission_g_per_kwh`, the worst emission observed anywhere in the sample, above zero at 2 decimal places. Only a `SCHEDULED` audit may be recorded — one already `PASSED` or `FAILED` cannot be re-recorded and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`. The sample must be at least as large as the frozen `required_sample_size`, else `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_TOO_SMALL`, and it cannot exceed the order's `built_quantity`, else `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_EXCEEDS_POPULATION`.

The outcome is decided from two independent breaches. `failure_rate` is `units_failed` divided by `units_tested`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. An **emission breach** occurs when `max_measured_emission_g_per_kwh` is strictly greater than the `family_emission_limit_g_per_kwh` of the family snapshotted on the audit. A **rate breach** occurs when `failure_rate` is strictly greater than 0.25; the gate being strict matters, so one failure in four tested — a rate of 0.2500 — passes, while two in four fails. When an emission breach holds, the audit is `FAILED` with `failure_reason` `EMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; the emission breach **outranks** the rate breach, so this reason is recorded even when the rate is also over its gate. Otherwise, when a rate breach holds, the audit is `FAILED` with `failure_reason` `FAILURE_RATE_EXCEEDED`. When neither holds the audit is `PASSED` with `failure_reason` left empty. The recording operator is retained as `recorded_by_user_id` in every case. A passing audit changes nothing outside its own row — in particular the family's status and its `cumulative_audit_failures` are untouched.

A failing audit fires a five-level cascade, and every level of it must land.

- **Level 1 — the audit row.** Its `status` becomes `FAILED`, its `failure_reason` is written as above, and `recorded_by_user_id` is recorded.
- **Level 2 — the family.** Its `cumulative_audit_failures` rises by 1. If the resulting count is still below 2, the family moves to `AUDIT_HOLD` and its `audit_hold_at` is stamped from the clock. If the resulting count reaches 2, the family instead moves to the terminal `SUSPENDED`. A family that had already accumulated one failure, was re-certified, and then failed again therefore goes straight from `CERTIFIED` to `SUSPENDED`; a family already sitting in `AUDIT_HOLD` that fails again does the same.
- **Level 2b — platforms and models.** Every engine platform of that family has its `certification_posture` recomputed by the projection of §2.7, landing on `RESTRICTED` for an `AUDIT_HOLD` family and on the terminal `FROZEN` for a `SUSPENDED` one. Every product model sitting on those platforms whose `status` is `RELEASED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, which blocks new build orders against it.
- **Level 3 — build orders.** Every build order of every model sitting on those platforms — not merely of the models this cascade has just suspended — whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` moves to `HELD`, with its prior status captured in `status_before_hold` and `held_reason` set to `FAMILY_AUDIT_HOLD`. When the family went to `SUSPENDED` instead, the sweep additionally picks up orders that were already `HELD`, and `held_reason` is set to `FAMILY_SUSPENDED`.
- **Level 4 — engines.** For every order newly moved to `HELD`, every engine unit whose `build_state` is `STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `HOT_TESTED` has its `conformance_state` moved to `QUARANTINED`, its `build_state` left exactly where it was. Those units can then neither be hot-tested nor released nor reworked.
- **Level 4b — allocation.** For every order newly moved to `HELD`, `allocated_dealer_units` and `allocated_mass_retail_units` are reset to 0, `unallocated_units` is set to the full `built_quantity`, `allocation_status` returns to `UNALLOCATED` and `allocation_run_at` is cleared — the standing channel position is torn up because the goods can no longer be shipped.

Re-certification of the family is the compensating half, and it is deliberately **partial**. When the family reaches `CERTIFIED` again, every platform's posture returns to `OPEN`; every build order of that family whose status is `HELD` is restored to whatever `status_before_hold` recorded, with both `held_reason` and `status_before_hold` cleared afterwards — so an order held from `PLANNED` returns to `PLANNED`, one held from `RELEASED` returns to `RELEASED`, and one held from `IN_PRODUCTION` returns to `IN_PRODUCTION`; and every engine unit of those restored orders whose `conformance_state` is `QUARANTINED` returns to `PENDING`, with `build_state` again untouched. Two things are pointedly **not** restored. Allocation counters stay where the cascade left them, so the planner must run channel allocation again to re-establish a split. And product models suspended by the cascade are **not** auto-released: because a model release re-checks its safety certification against the clock, a `PlantAdmin` must release each suspended model explicitly. A family in `AUDIT_HOLD` may re-enter durability testing to build a remediation basis, as §3.2 describes, and its held orders stay held throughout — nothing is lifted until the family is `CERTIFIED` once more.

### 3.9. The Service Parts and Repair Network

The plant owes the repair network parts long after a machine leaves the line, and service parts are managed accordingly. A `PlantAdmin` creates one against a platform, naming the `engine_platform_id` it services, a `part_number` of 1 to 30 characters unique across all parts, and a `category` of `CARBURETOR_KIT`, `IGNITION_MODULE`, `BLADE_ASSEMBLY` or `FILTER_KIT`. Stock and promises come with it: a non-negative `on_hand_qty` of pieces available to the network, a `committed_availability_years` of at least 7 years of availability owed after the part's last production, a `last_production_year` between 2020 and 2060, an optional `dealer_lead_time_days` between 1 and 30 defaulting to 2, and an optional `mass_retail_lead_time_days` between 1 and 60 defaulting to 5. Dealers are promised faster shipment than mass retail, and the defaults reflect that.

The server derives `commitment_expires_year` as `last_production_year` plus `committed_availability_years`, and the part opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `superseded_by_part_id` empty, `filled_request_count`, `substituted_request_count` and `backordered_request_count` all 0, and `fill_rate_pct` empty. That last point is worth stressing: a part with no requests at all has no fill rate, not a fill rate of 0.00. The lifecycle runs `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` and `OBSOLETE`, with `OBSOLETE` terminal.

A `ChannelPartner` raises a fulfillment request naming the `service_part_id` wanted, a `requested_quantity` of at least 1, and the requesting `channel` of `DEALER` or `MASS_RETAIL`. The part must be `ACTIVE`; requesting a part that is `SUPERSEDED` or `OBSOLETE` is refused with `ERR_PART_STATE_INVALID`. The request is minted in `BACKORDERED` and settles within the very same call when stock allows: if the part's `on_hand_qty` is at least `requested_quantity`, the request becomes `FILLED`, its `shipped_from_part_id` is set to the part it was requested from, and the part's `on_hand_qty` is reduced by `requested_quantity`. If stock does not cover the request it stays `BACKORDERED` — there are no partial fills. The request's `requester_user_id` records the raising operator, per §2.1. The terminal outcomes are `FILLED` and `SUBSTITUTED`.

The promised date is derived from the logical clock's date component with no weekend or holiday adjustment. A request that is `FILLED` or `SUBSTITUTED` gets `promised_ship_date` equal to the clock date plus the lead time its channel earns — `dealer_lead_time_days` for a `DEALER` request and `mass_retail_lead_time_days` for a `MASS_RETAIL` request, read from the part the pieces actually ship from. A request left `BACKORDERED` gets `promised_ship_date` equal to the clock date plus 21 days.

The availability metric is recomputed on the part after every request and after every supersession. The served count is `filled_request_count` plus `substituted_request_count`; the total is that served count plus `backordered_request_count`. When the total is zero, `fill_rate_pct` is empty. Otherwise it is the served count divided by the total, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Requests are attributed to a part by where the pieces came from: a `FILLED` or `SUBSTITUTED` request counts against its `shipped_from_part_id`, while a `BACKORDERED` request counts against its `service_part_id`.

Supersession is how a part number is replaced, and it is a `PlantAdmin` action naming a `successor_part_id`. The part being superseded must be `ACTIVE` and the named successor must also be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PART_STATE_INVALID`. The successor must sit on the same `engine_platform_id` as the part it replaces, else `ERR_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`. And the resulting chain must be well-formed: the successor may not be the part itself, and walking forward from the successor along `superseded_by_part_id` must never arrive back at the part being superseded, and the resulting chain — one link plus the length of the successor's existing chain — must be at most 5 links deep. Any of those three failures is refused with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE`.

On success three things happen in order. The part moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_part_id` is set to the successor. Then the part's open backorders are worked off from the successor's stock, in strict `created_at` ascending order with no fallback ordering by identifier: for each such request in turn, if the successor's `on_hand_qty` at that moment is at least the request's `requested_quantity`, the request becomes `SUBSTITUTED`, its `shipped_from_part_id` is set to the successor, the successor's `on_hand_qty` is reduced by `requested_quantity`, and its `promised_ship_date` is recomputed from the clock date and the successor's lead time for that request's `channel`. If the successor's remaining stock does not cover a request, that request stays `BACKORDERED` — again no partial fills — but it does **not** block the sweep: processing continues to the next request in `created_at` order, so a later, smaller request can still be substituted while an earlier, larger one waits. Finally, the counters and `fill_rate_pct` are recomputed on **both** parts: the superseded predecessor and the successor.

Retiring a part is the last act. Obsoletion is admitted when the part's status is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and refused with `ERR_PART_STATE_INVALID` when it is already `OBSOLETE`. Two further conditions bind. The plant's parts-availability commitment must have actually run out: the logical clock's year must be **strictly greater** than the part's `commitment_expires_year`, so a part whose commitment expires in the current clock year cannot yet be retired, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AVAILABILITY_COMMITMENT_ACTIVE`. And no backorder may still be open: a `backordered_request_count` above zero refuses the retirement with `ERR_OPEN_BACKORDERS_EXIST`. On success the part reaches the terminal `OBSOLETE`, after which no new fulfillment request against it is accepted.

Service parts are readable individually and as a list, filterable by `engine_platform_id`, by `superseded_by_part_id` to trace supersession pointers, by `status` and by `category`. The requests listable from a part are those attributed to it exactly as its counters are — every request raised against it plus every request that shipped from it, so a substituted backorder is visible both under the part it was raised against and under the successor it shipped from — ordered by `created_at` ascending and filterable by `status`, subject to the `ChannelPartner` scoping described in §2.1.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that mint a new resource — declaring a certification family, creating a platform or model, recording a safety certification, receiving a component lot, opening a machining run, creating an assembly line, planning a build order, scheduling a durability run or a line audit, staging an engine unit, creating a service part, raising a parts request — answer `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state transition, every recording action and the logical-clock repositioning, answer `200 OK`. All retrievals answer `200 OK`. The table below gives only the HTTP contract and authorization; request payloads, response contents and side effects are specified in §2 and §3 and are not repeated here.

| Method | Path | Description | Authorized Role(s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `POST` | `/_clock/set_current_utc` | Repositions the server's logical UTC clock; mutates no entity. | `PlantAdmin` |
| `POST` | `/certification-families` | Declares an emission certification family with its displacement band, durability period, family limit and declared production baseline. | `CertificationEngineer` |
| `GET` | `/certification-families/{id}` | Reads one certification family, including its computed deterioration factor, certified emission, compliance margin and version. | `PlantAdmin`, `CertificationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `QualityEngineer`, `ProductionPlanner` |
