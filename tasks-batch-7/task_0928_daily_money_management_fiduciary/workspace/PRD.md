# Daily Money Management & Bill-Pay Fiduciary API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Daily money management is the unglamorous middle ground between informal family help and full guardianship: a licensed, bonded professional takes over a vulnerable adult's chequebook, pays their bills on time, keeps their benefits from lapsing, and answers to an auditor for every cent. The people served are typically older adults, adults recovering from a health crisis, or adults with a cognitive impairment — precisely the population that scam operators target hardest. A practice that does this work manually accumulates three chronic failure modes: a payment goes out under an authorization that expired last month, a month's books get closed and then quietly amended, and a family member receives a report containing a category of information the client never consented to disclose.

This document specifies the API for a daily-money-management and bill-pay fiduciary platform that closes those three gaps by construction. Client agency is never assumed: every disbursement, every benefit filing, and every family subscription hangs off a dated authorization instrument, and when that instrument is revoked the platform unwinds the client's scheduled commitments in one deterministic cascade rather than leaving orphaned payments in flight. Money is tracked in integer cents on a strictly append-only ledger, so a correction is always a reversing entry and never an edit. Books are closed per account-month, and a closed month refuses further ledger effects — with a short, explicit list of exceptions for the operations that must survive a freeze, such as raising a fraud alert or recording a retroactive benefit award that the agency granted after the fact.

The platform is written for a practice operating under segregation of duties. A bill-pay specialist prepares and releases disbursements but can never approve one; a fiduciary manager or compliance officer supplies second control on the large ones; a compliance officer alone adjudicates fraud and freezes accounts. Over all of it sits an organization-wide surety bond whose committed exposure is a hard ceiling on what the practice may have promised at any instant, and an immutable audit trail that records every write attempt — successful or refused — for later inspection by a regulator, a family member behind the disclosure wall, or the practice's own auditor.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Identity, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds a string that is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else, on every endpoint including the reads and the operational endpoints. Once the caller is authenticated, the platform confirms that the caller's role appears in the authorized-role list published for the requested endpoint in §4; a caller whose role is absent from that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `FiduciaryManager`, `BillPaySpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CareCoordinator`, `Client`, and `FamilyRecipient`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform itself: registering the surety bond, driving the logical clock, and running the periodic checkpoint sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it on the audit trail as `actor_user_id` but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- A `FiduciaryManager` is the licensed daily-money manager: they run intake, issue and revoke authorization instruments, place accounts under management, create budget lines and grant overrides, give second-control approval, stop payments, close and reopen reconciliation periods, and file benefit applications. When `X-User-Role` is `FiduciaryManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (for example as `prepared_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a payment, or as `actor_user_id` on the audit trail) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- A `BillPaySpecialist` prepares, revises, releases and settles disbursements, posts deposits and holds, and imports and matches statement lines. Under segregation of duties this role never appears on the approval endpoint at all. When `X-User-Role` is `BillPaySpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `prepared_by_user_id` on payments it prepares and as `actor_user_id` on the audit trail, but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates fraud alerts and disputes, blocks payees, drives the account fraud posture, reads the audit trail, and may serve as the second approver on a disbursement. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- A `CareCoordinator` is a care-team member who opens coordination requests and acknowledges, declines, completes or escalates them. When `X-User-Role` is `CareCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque care-team member identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is never required to equal any entity's `id`; it is, however, compared against the `assigned_member_user_id` recorded on a coordination request when that request is acknowledged — a comparison of two opaque operator identifiers, not an ownership check against a row.
- A `Client` is the person under service. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Client` compare the resource's client-owning field to `X-User-ID`, and an attempt to act on another client's file is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED`.
- A `FamilyRecipient` is a family member or trusted contact receiving monthly reports through the disclosure wall. When `X-User-Role` is `FamilyRecipient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FamilyRecipient` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FamilyRecipient` compare the resource's recipient-owning field to `X-User-ID`, and generating or reading a report for any other recipient is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED`.

Role membership is the only thing `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` reports, with one deliberate addition: when a `CareCoordinator` acknowledges a coordination request whose `assigned_member_user_id` is somebody else's identifier, that refusal is also `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and is raised at the role rung, not at a later business rung.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering, and Filter Semantics

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two pagination query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many rows come back; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. The `offset` parameter says how many rows to skip and defaults to 0, with a minimum of 0. A supplied value outside the permitted range is clamped to the nearest permitted value rather than rejected: a `limit` of 500 behaves as 100, a `limit` of 0 behaves as 1, and a negative `offset` behaves as 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object; `total_count` counts the rows that survived filtering before pagination was applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`.

Ordering is uniform across every collection in the system: rows come back sorted by their creation timestamp in ascending order — oldest first. Two rows created inside the same millisecond are never reordered arbitrarily; the tie is broken by write order, meaning the row that was persisted first comes first. Identifiers are never used as a tie-break, because they are random UUIDs and carry no ordering information. The audit export follows the same convention, ordered by the instant each entry was appended, oldest first, ties broken by append order.

Filters on list endpoints are exact-match and are combined conjunctively: supplying two filters returns only the rows that satisfy both. A filter value that is syntactically fine but matches nothing — including a status or category filter whose value is not a member of the corresponding enumeration at all — is not an error. Such a request succeeds with HTTP 200, an empty `data` array, `total_count` of 0 and `has_more` false. Filter parameters never route to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`; only the request-body fields listed at rung 5 of §2.9 do.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock, Calendar Dates, and Instant Rendering

Nothing in this system reads the machine's wall clock. Every deadline, window, expiry, cut-off and settlement projection is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any caller has moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint, which responds with a single-field object whose `current_utc` key carries the clock's new reading. Moving the clock performs no sweep of its own — nothing expires, escalates or settles merely because time advanced; the checkpoint endpoint described in §3.9 is what turns elapsed time into state changes.

Two rendering conventions govern every temporal value the API emits, and they must not be conflated.

**Server-written instants** are rendered in ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — the shape `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. The three fractional digits are always present, including when they are all zero. Every one of the following fields uses this rendering: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp`, `executed_at`, `revoked_at`, `released_at`, `closed_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `consent_recorded_at`, `generated_at`, `recorded_at`, and both deadline fields named `ack_due_at`. The value written is the logical clock's reading at the moment the server performed the write, or a whole number of hours added to such a reading in the case of the two `ack_due_at` deadlines. The `current_utc` value returned by the clock endpoint is likewise re-rendered into this canonical shape: a caller who sets the clock to `2026-06-01T09:00:00Z` receives `2026-06-01T09:00:00.000Z` back, because the server renders its own reading rather than echoing the caller's string.

**Calendar dates** are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component and no zone suffix, and **calendar months** are plain `YYYY-MM` strings. Date-valued fields that the caller supplies are stored and echoed back byte-for-byte as the caller wrote them: `effective_date`, `expiry_date`, `requested_send_date`, `value_date`, `line_date`, `determined_on`, `decision_date`, `award_effective_date`, `date_of_birth`, `budget_month`, `period_month` and `report_month` are all caller-supplied and are never re-rendered. `capacity_determined_on` belongs to the same group even though it is a system-sourced field: it holds, byte-for-byte, the `determined_on` date the caller supplied. Date-valued fields the server derives for itself — `activated_on`, `closed_on`, `submitted_on`, `filed_on`, `settled_on`, `projected_settlement_date`, `effective_settlement_date`, `entry_date` when the server dates an entry itself, and `retro_period_month` — are written as the logical clock's own calendar date (or, for the two settlement-date fields, as the computed settlement date, and for `retro_period_month` as the award month) in the same plain `YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM` form.

The current logical date means the calendar date portion of the logical clock's reading in UTC. The current logical instant means the full reading.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Version-Bump Ledger

Every persisted resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Every resource also carries a `created_at` instant recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 each time the platform counts the row as mutated; `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant at exactly the moments `version` increments, and equals `created_at` on a freshly created row. Three entities are deliberately excluded: a `LedgerEntry`, a `FamilyReport` and an `AuditEntry` are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and no endpoint updates or deletes them.

Because a single request in this domain frequently touches half a dozen rows, the rules for which of them count as mutated are pinned exhaustively below. Three principles govern the table, and they are worth stating on their own because they resolve the cases readers most often guess at. First, **a row whose derived aggregate is recomputed does not thereby count as mutated**: recomputing an account's `available_balance_cents`, a budget line's `committed_cents` or variance fields, a bond's exposure figures, a payee's `settled_payment_count`, or a period's `unreconciled_entry_count` moves the number without moving `version` or `updated_at`, unless the table below says otherwise for that specific operation. Second, **a row touched more than once inside one request still increments exactly once** — an account freeze that sweeps nine payments increments the account's `version` by 1, not by 9 (each of the nine payments increments its own `version` by 1). Third, **a rejected request increments nothing**: when any rung of §2.9 refuses a request, no version anywhere moves and no field anywhere is written, apart from the single audit entry the request appends. Reads never write: no GET endpoint increments any version, refreshes any `updated_at`, or performs any deferred recomputation that would be visible as a mutation — the `frozen` flag on a ledger entry is derived at read time from its period's current status and is never stored.

### 2.6. Monetary Representation, Precision, and Rounding

All money in this system is held and returned as **integer cents**. There are no fractional cents anywhere: no field stores them, no calculation emits them, and no response contains them. Every monetary field name in the system ends in `_cents` and its JSON value is a plain integer, never a string and never a decimal.

Only two calculations in the system involve a non-integer factor, and both round with the **round-half-up** convention — a value exactly on the half-cent rounds away from zero, upward. The first is the per-item countable-resource haircut applied to a benefit application's retirement and life-insurance holdings; the second is the prorated allocation used by the period-to-date budget variance. In both cases the rounding point is pinned in §3, and in the resource calculation it is pinned to **per item, then sum** rather than sum-then-round, because the two orders produce different totals whenever more than one rate-bearing item is present.

Signed amounts appear only on ledger entries. A `LedgerEntry` whose `amount_cents` is negative reduces the account's cleared balance; a positive one raises it. Statement lines carry the institution's own sign convention verbatim in their `amount_cents`.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Existence Ordering

Requests that name resources do so in a fixed resolution order: every identifier in the URL path first, left to right, and then every identifier carried in the request body, in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. The first identifier that names no existing row ends the request immediately, before any later identifier is examined and before any business rule runs. Two distinct 404 codes report the failure and the split is by entity type, not by position: a missing `Client` yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing row of any other type — an authorization, an account, a payee, a budget line, a payment, a dispute, a period, a statement line, a ledger entry, an alert, an application, a bond, a recipient, a report, or a coordination request — yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Both carry HTTP 404.

Existence resolution sits above every business gate. A caller who names a client that does not exist learns only that; they learn nothing about the organization's bond status, about any client's freeze state, or about whether a period is closed.

### 2.8. The Four Write Gates and Their Precedence

Four independent gates guard the writing side of this platform, and they compose. Each one refuses on behalf of a different scope, and their relative order is normative — **widest blast radius first** — so that a caller confronted with two simultaneous problems always receives the same answer.

1. **The bond gate (G1)** refuses on behalf of the whole organization. An unbonded fiduciary may lawfully disburse nothing. If no `SuretyBond` is currently `IN_FORCE`, or the `IN_FORCE` bond's coverage window does not contain the current logical date, the refusal is `ERR_BOND_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). The window is inclusive at both ends: the bond is in force on its `effective_date` and still in force on its `expiry_date`. When no bond has ever been registered, this gate fires for the same reason — there is no `IN_FORCE` bond.
2. **The authorization gate (G2a)** refuses on behalf of one client. The *governing* instrument is the one the request names directly through an `authorization_id` field, or — when the request acts on an existing row rather than creating one — the one that row already names: a payment's own `authorization_id` for a revision or a release, the recipient's `authorization_id` for a consent or a report, the application's `authorization_id` for a status change. If that governing `Authorization` is not in status `ACTIVE`, or the current logical date falls outside its `effective_date`-to-`expiry_date` window (again inclusive at both ends), the refusal is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INACTIVE` (HTTP 409). An instrument whose stored status still reads `ACTIVE` but whose `expiry_date` has already passed is refused here immediately; the caller does not have to wait for a checkpoint sweep to relabel it.
3. **The scope gate (G2b)** refuses on behalf of one client's instrument. If the scope matrix of §3.1 marks the requested operation as not permitted for that instrument's `scope_tier`, the refusal is `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).
4. **The account fraud-freeze gate (G3)** refuses on behalf of one account. The account in question is the one the request names through an `account_id`, or the one reached through the row being acted on — the payment's account for a revision, a release or a settlement event, and the disputed payment's account for a provisional credit. If that `ClientAccount` has `fraud_posture` equal to `FROZEN`, the refusal is `ERR_ACCOUNT_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A posture of `WATCH` gates nothing — it is observational only.
5. **The reconciliation-freeze gate (G4)** refuses on behalf of one account-month. If the `ReconciliationPeriod` that would receive this request's ledger effect is `CLOSED` or `LOCKED`, the refusal is `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The receiving period is resolved against the request's own date input when it has one — the `value_date` of an account adjustment, or the period named in the path for a statement-line import or a statement-line disposition — and against the current logical date otherwise, which is the case for scheduling a payment, revising one, recording a settlement event, stopping one, applying a provisional credit and resolving a dispute. When no period covers that date at all, this gate passes.

All five sit **after** authentication, role authorization, existence resolution, request-shape validation and enum-membership validation, and **before** every ordinary business rung. The reason for putting them after the first five checks is a disclosure one: a caller who is not authenticated, who lacks the role, or who names a resource that does not exist must not learn anything about the organization's bond, a client's instrument, or an account's freeze state.

When two gates both apply, the earlier one in the list above wins. A payment prepared under a revoked instrument against a frozen account on a month whose books are closed, with no bond in force, returns `ERR_BOND_EXPIRED` — and returns `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INACTIVE` once a bond is registered, `ERR_ACCOUNT_FROZEN` once the instrument is fixed, and `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` once the account is unfrozen.

**Operations that remain permitted while a period is frozen.** The reconciliation freeze applies only to the operations enumerated at rung 10 of §2.9. Everything below is deliberately outside that list and continues to work normally against a `CLOSED` or `LOCKED` period:

- every read in the system, including the per-client audit-trail export;
- raising a fraud alert, and dispositioning one (acknowledge, confirm, dismiss);
- changing an account's fraud posture, in either direction;
- executing or revoking an authorization instrument;
- opening a coordination request and dispositioning one;
- generating a family report, which is a read-projection wearing a create's clothing;
- driving the reconciliation period's own status axis — the reopen path must survive the freeze it is trying to lift;
- recording an agency decision on a benefit application, including a retroactive award, which avoids writing into the frozen month by the posting rule described in §3.8.

### 2.9. The Validation Priority Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error comes back, and which one is fully determined: the rung with the lowest number wins. The complete ladder, in order, is:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint. This rung also carries the assigned-member check on a coordination-request acknowledgement: a `CareCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the request's `assigned_member_user_id` is refused here.
3. **Existence.** Every resource named in the path, then every resource named in the body, resolved left to right per §2.7. A missing `Client` yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND` (404); every other missing row yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Request shape.** The body carries every required field of the endpoint's input list, each of the declared JSON type. Unknown extra fields are ignored rather than rejected. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). Applies to every endpoint. Several fields are declared optional on their endpoint but become required by the action the request selects, and their absence is refused here with `ERR_VALIDATION` just as a plainly required field's would be: `revocation_reason` when the authorization status request revokes; `return_reason` when the settlement event is a return; `denial_reason` when the recorded outcome is a denial; `award_effective_date` and `award_monthly_amount_cents` when the recorded outcome is an approval; `ledger_entry_id` when the statement-line disposition is a match or a resolve-matched; and `reassign_to_user_id` when the coordination disposition is a decline. A conditionally required field that is present but empty is likewise `ERR_VALIDATION`, raised at rung 15.
5. **Enum membership.** Every enum-typed input field holds a declared member of its enumeration; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422). This rung is a **closed, exhaustive list** — exactly the following fields route here and **no other field routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`**: an authorization's `scope_tier`; an account's `account_type`; a payee's `payee_type`; a payment's `channel`; a dispute's `reason_code`; a fraud alert's `reason_code`; a benefit application's `program`; the `item_type` of every element of a benefit application's `resource_items`; a family recipient's `relationship`; every element of a family recipient's `permitted_categories`; every element of a report request's `requested_categories`; a coordination request's `tier`; the `determination` field on the capacity-determination endpoint; the `to_status` field on the client, authorization and reconciliation-period status endpoints; the `to_posture` field on the fraud-posture endpoint; the `adjustment_type` field on the account-adjustments endpoint; the `decision` field on the approval-decision endpoint; the `event` field on the settlement-event endpoint; the `action` field on the statement-line, fraud-alert, benefit-application-status and coordination-request disposition endpoints; and the `outcome` field on the dispute-resolve and record-decision endpoints. A malformed or out-of-domain value in any field not on this list is `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 4, and a filter value on a query string is neither — see §2.3.
6. **Bond gate (G1).** `ERR_BOND_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, releasing one, and posting an account adjustment.
7. **Authorization gate (G2a).** `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INACTIVE` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, releasing one, drafting a benefit application, driving a benefit application's status axis, enrolling a family recipient, recording consent, and generating a family report.
8. **Scope gate (G2b).** `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, drafting a benefit application, and enrolling a family recipient.
9. **Account fraud-freeze gate (G3).** `ERR_ACCOUNT_FROZEN` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, releasing one, recording a settlement event, posting an account adjustment, and applying a dispute's provisional credit.
10. **Reconciliation-freeze gate (G4).** `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, recording a settlement event, stopping a payment, posting an account adjustment, importing a statement line, disposing of a statement line, applying a provisional credit, and resolving a dispute. It is deliberately **not** applied to the reconciliation-period status endpoint or to any operation on the permitted-while-frozen list in §2.8.
11. **Client active.** The `Client` governing the request has status `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The governing client is the one the request names directly through a `client_id`, or the owner of the row it acts through — the account's client for a payment operation, the recipient's client for a consent or a report, the disputed payment's account's client for a dispute filing. Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, releasing one, drafting a benefit application, driving a benefit application's status axis, enrolling a family recipient, recording consent, generating a family report, opening a coordination request, and filing a dispute.
12. **State gate.** The target row is in a from-state that its transition table permits for this trigger, terminal states included. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every mutating operation that names an existing row in its path. On a `Payment`, which carries two parallel axes, this rung evaluates **only the axis the trigger itself advances**: `settlement_status` for release, settlement events, cancellation and stop-payment; `approval_status` for the approval decision and the revision. The one cross-axis condition that this rung does *not* report is release-requires-approved, which belongs to rung 30; every other cross-axis condition on a payment — notably that an approval decision and a revision both require `settlement_status` to still be `SCHEDULED` — is reported here as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
13. **Payment referential integrity.** The `Payee` and the `BudgetCategory` named on a payment both belong to the same client as the `ClientAccount`, and that budget line's `budget_month` equals the calendar month of the payment's `projected_settlement_date`. Otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to scheduling a payment.
14. **Match referential integrity.** On a match or a resolve-matched disposition, the named ledger entry's `period_id` equals the statement line's `period_id` and its `amount_cents` equals the line's `amount_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to disposing of a statement line.
15. **Value constraints on well-typed fields**, all returning `ERR_VALIDATION` (422): a payment's or revision's or adjustment's `amount_cents` is greater than zero; a budget line's `allocation_cents` is greater than zero; a bond's `coverage_cents` is greater than zero; an account's `opening_balance_cents` is zero or greater; an application's `asserted_monthly_income_cents` is zero or greater and every element's `value_cents` inside `resource_items` is zero or greater; a dispute's `disputed_amount_cents` is greater than zero and no greater than the disputed payment's `amount_cents`; an override's `added_cents` is greater than zero and its `reason` is at least 20 characters long; a fraud-posture request that returns an account to `NORMAL` carries a `reason` at least 20 characters long; and a hold release does not drive the account's `pending_holds_cents` below zero. Applies to scheduling a payment, revising one, placing an account under management, posting an adjustment, changing the fraud posture, creating a budget line, granting an override, registering a bond, drafting a benefit application, and filing a dispute.
16. **Uniqueness.** The triple of a budget line's owning client, `category_name` and `budget_month` is unique, and the pair of a statement line's `period_id` and `external_ref` is unique. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). Applies to creating a budget line and importing a statement line.
17. **Date spans.** An authorization's `expiry_date` is strictly after its `effective_date` and the span between them is at most 730 days, inclusive — a term of exactly 730 days is accepted. A bond's `expiry_date` is strictly after its `effective_date`. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). Applies to drafting an authorization and registering a bond.
18. **Period non-repetition.** No `ReconciliationPeriod` already exists for this account with the same `period_month`. Otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to opening a reconciliation period. Calendar months partition the timeline exactly, so an adjacent month is always legal and only an exact repeat collides.
19. **Duplicate-payment buffer.** No colliding payment exists per the rule in §3.3, or `duplicate_override` was supplied as true. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYMENT_WINDOW` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment only — never to a revision.
20. **Send date not in the past.** A supplied `requested_send_date` is not earlier than the current logical date; the current logical date itself is acceptable. Otherwise `ERR_SEND_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). Applies to scheduling a payment.
21. **Stop-payment window.** The current logical instant is at or before the payment's `created_at` plus 48 hours; exactly 48 hours later still qualifies. Otherwise `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to the stop-payment endpoint only, and never to a system-driven freeze sweep.
22. **Dispute filing window.** The current logical date is at most 60 days after the payment's `settled_on`; exactly 60 days later still files. Otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to filing a dispute.
23. **Reopen window.** A reopen request arrives at or before the period's `closed_at` plus 30 days; exactly 30 days later still reopens. Otherwise `ERR_REOPEN_NOT_PERMITTED` (409). Applies to a period status request whose `to_status` is `REOPENED`. Note that the reopen *count* cap is not checked at this rung — it is enforced structurally, because a period that has already been reopened once closes into `LOCKED` rather than `CLOSED`, and rung 12 then refuses a second reopen with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
24. **Retroactive lookback.** On an approved decision, `decision_date` minus `award_effective_date` is at most 180 days, inclusive, and `award_effective_date` is not after `decision_date`. Otherwise `ERR_RETRO_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to recording an agency decision.
25. **Reroute cap.** A decline disposition arrives while the request's `reroute_count` is still below 2. Otherwise `ERR_REROUTE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to a coordination-request disposition whose `action` is `DECLINE`.
26. **Appeal cap.** An appeal arrives while the application's `appeal_count` is still below 1. Otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to a benefit-application status request whose `action` is `APPEAL`.
27. **Open exceptions.** A close request arrives while the period's `open_exception_count` is zero. Otherwise `ERR_OPEN_EXCEPTIONS` (409). Applies to a period status request whose `to_status` is `CLOSED`. The test is on the count, not on non-emptiness: a period with zero imported lines closes successfully.
28. **Payee not blocked.** The `Payee` named by the payment has status `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_PAYEE_BLOCKED` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment and revising one.
29. **Segregation of duties.** The approver's `X-User-ID` differs from the payment's `prepared_by_user_id`. The same natural person may not prepare and approve the same disbursement even when acting under a different role. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to the approval-decision endpoint.
30. **Second approval present.** The payment being released has `approval_status` equal to `APPROVED`. Otherwise `ERR_SECOND_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to releasing a payment. A payment that is `SCHEDULED` but still `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` therefore returns this code, never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
31. **Capacity to consent.** When the caller's role is `Client`, the governing client's `capacity_determination` reads `RETAINS_CAPACITY`. Otherwise `ERR_CAPACITY_REQUIRED` (403). Applies to recording consent. When the caller is a `FiduciaryManager`, this rung does not apply.
32. **Disclosure ownership.** When the caller's role is `Client`, `X-User-ID` equals the governing client's `id`. When the caller's role is `FamilyRecipient`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `FamilyRecipient` the request names and, on the audit export, that recipient's `client_id` equals the client named in the path. Otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED` (403). Applies to recording consent, generating a family report, and exporting a client's audit entries.
33. **Instrument amount ceiling.** The payment's `amount_cents` is at or below the instrument's per-payment ceiling — 250000 cents under `LIMITED_BILLPAY`, 400000 cents under `REP_PAYEE`, unbounded under `FULL_AGENCY`. Both ceilings are inclusive: exactly the ceiling passes. Otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to scheduling a payment and revising one. Because this is a fiscal rung and the scope matrix at rung 8 is not, a request that breaches both the matrix cell and the ceiling returns `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
34. **Stale parent version.** The optimistic-concurrency field the endpoint requires equals the parent row's current `version` — see §2.10. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to scheduling a payment, disposing of a statement line, and generating a family report.
35. **Bond coverage ceiling.** The `IN_FORCE` bond's `exposure_committed_cents` plus this payment's `amount_cents` is at or below its `coverage_cents`; landing exactly on coverage is permitted. Otherwise `ERR_BOND_COVERAGE_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment and revising one. This aggregate spans the whole organization, so a modest payment for one client can be refused because other clients' committed payments have consumed the headroom.
36. **Budget line capacity.** The line's `committed_cents` plus this payment's `amount_cents` is at or below `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents`; landing exactly on the allocation is permitted. Otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment and revising one.
37. **Account sufficiency.** The account's `available_balance_cents` is at least the payment's `total_charge_cents`; an account holding exactly the total charge may schedule the payment. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE` (409). Applies to scheduling a payment and revising one.
38. **Program limits.** The application's `countable_resources_net_cents` is at or below its program's resource limit **and** its `asserted_monthly_income_cents` is at or below that program's monthly income limit; both boundaries inclusive. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to a benefit-application status request whose `action` is `SUBMIT`.
39. **Provisional credit cap.** The dispute's `disputed_amount_cents` is at or below 500000 cents; exactly 500000 still credits. Otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to applying a provisional credit, and to resolving a dispute with outcome `UPHELD` from status `FILED` — the branch that grants the credit at resolution time.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints in this system require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a parent row before it writes. In each case the field is a **required, non-optional** input, and a mismatch with the parent's live `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). No other endpoint in the system takes a version field of any kind, and supplying one to an endpoint that does not require it is simply ignored as an unknown field.

- Scheduling a payment requires `expected_account_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ClientAccount` named by `account_id`. Two payments against the same account are both legitimate; what makes the second unsafe is that the preparer committed funds against a balance picture that a deposit, a hold, a settlement, a cancellation or an earlier payment has already moved. If the account's `version` has advanced since the preparer read it, the schedule is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- Disposing of a statement line requires `expected_period_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ReconciliationPeriod` that owns the line. Matching or flagging a second, different line is legitimate on its own — but each disposition moves the period's `open_exception_count` and `unreconciled_entry_count`, and a reconciler working from a stale count is exactly the condition that lets a period be closed with an exception still outstanding. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- Generating a family report requires `expected_recipient_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `FamilyRecipient` named by `recipient_id`. Generating a second report is legitimate — but `permitted_categories` is replaced wholesale by every consent call, so a report projected from a stale category set would disclose a category the client has just withdrawn. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).

### 2.11. The Immutable Audit Trail

Every operation that writes — that is, every endpoint whose method is POST, including the clock endpoint and the checkpoint endpoint — appends exactly **one** `AuditEntry`, whether the request succeeded or was refused. The entry is appended after the outcome is known, so a refused request produces an entry whose `outcome` reads `REJECTED` and whose `error_id` carries the code that was returned; a successful request produces `outcome` `SUCCESS` and a null `error_id`. Cascaded side effects never add entries of their own: one request produces one entry, no matter how many rows the cascade touched.

Each entry records the caller as `actor_role` and `actor_user_id`, the request as `operation` — the HTTP method and path of the audited request, written as a single string such as `POST /dmm/payments` — and the instant of the append as `recorded_at`. It names the primary resource the request targeted through a polymorphic pair: `subject_entity`, one of `Client`, `Authorization`, `ClientAccount`, `Payee`, `BudgetCategory`, `Payment`, `Dispute`, `ReconciliationPeriod`, `StatementLine`, `FraudAlert`, `BenefitApplication`, `SuretyBond`, `FamilyRecipient`, `FamilyReport`, `CoordinationRequest` or `System`; and `subject_id`, the identifier of the targeted row. For a path-addressed endpoint `subject_id` is the row named in the path; for a successful create it is the newly created row's `id`; for a refused create, and for the two operational endpoints whose `subject_entity` is `System`, it is null. The entry also carries `client_id`, the client whose file the operation touched, which is null for the clock endpoint, the checkpoint sweep, and surety-bond registration, none of which resolve to a client.

Finally, each entry carries a `disclosure_category` that classifies it for the family disclosure wall. The mapping is a closed function of `subject_entity`: a `Payment`, `Payee` or `Dispute` subject yields `BILL_PAY`; a `BudgetCategory` subject yields `BUDGET`; a `BenefitApplication` subject yields `BENEFITS`; a `FraudAlert` subject yields `FRAUD`; a `CoordinationRequest` subject yields `CARE_TEAM`; a `ClientAccount` subject yields `BALANCES`; and every remaining subject — `Client`, `Authorization`, `ReconciliationPeriod`, `StatementLine`, `SuretyBond`, `FamilyRecipient`, `FamilyReport` and `System` — yields `ADMINISTRATION`.

Audit entries are append-only. No endpoint updates one, no endpoint deletes one, and the reconciliation freeze does not apply to them: the audit trail keeps growing while a period is closed, and it remains readable throughout.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants

Five identities hold at all times. Each is asserted after a request's mutations have all been applied and before its response is serialized, so a caller reading a resource back always sees a consistent picture. All five are stated in integer cents.

1. **Account availability.** An account's `available_balance_cents` equals its `cleared_balance_cents` minus its `pending_holds_cents` minus its `committed_disbursements_cents`. This quantity is conserved across compensating actions: scheduling a payment and then cancelling it leaves `available_balance_cents` exactly as it was before the schedule; settling a payment leaves it unchanged, because `cleared_balance_cents` and `committed_disbursements_cents` both fall by the same `total_charge_cents`; a stop-payment leaves it higher than the mid-flight value by `total_charge_cents` minus 3000 cents; and a return leaves it higher by `total_charge_cents` minus 3500 cents.
2. **The ledger identity.** An account's `cleared_balance_cents` minus its `opening_balance_cents` equals the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every `LedgerEntry` belonging to that account, frozen entries included. Every compensating action appends its own reversing entry rather than deleting the original, so this identity never has to be repaired.
3. **Budget line capacity.** For every budget line, `committed_cents` plus `remaining_cents` equals `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents`. Scheduling a payment and then cancelling it restores both terms exactly.
4. **Organization-wide committed exposure.** Scheduling a payment and then cancelling it leaves the bond's `exposure_committed_cents` identical to its pre-scheduling value. Settling a payment moves the same cents out of the committed term and into the current month's settled term, so `exposure_committed_cents` falls by `amount_cents` while `exposure_settled_inclusive_cents` — being the sum of both terms — does not move at all.
5. **Disclosure closure.** On every generated report, `included_categories` and `withheld_categories` partition `requested_categories` exactly: their union equals `requested_categories` and their intersection is empty, for every recipient and every request.

### 2.13. Serialized Representations

Each entity has exactly one serialized shape, and that shape does not vary with context: a single-resource read, a row inside a list page, and the echo returned by an action endpoint all carry the same field set for a given entity. What varies between endpoints is only *which entity* comes back, and that is fixed per endpoint — the account-adjustments endpoint and the fraud-posture endpoint both echo a `ClientAccount`, not a ledger entry and not the payments a freeze swept; the payee-block endpoint echoes a `Payee`, not the payments it cancelled; the budget-override endpoint echoes a `BudgetCategory`; the statement-line disposition endpoint echoes a `StatementLine`, not the period whose counters it moved; the fraud-alert disposition endpoint echoes a `FraudAlert`, not the account it may have frozen; the authorization status endpoint echoes an `Authorization`, not the payments the revocation cancelled; the client status-change endpoint and the capacity-determination endpoint both echo a `Client`; every payment action endpoint — approval decision, revision, release, settlement event, cancellation and stop-payment alike — echoes the `Payment`; the dispute endpoints echo the `Dispute`; the period status and statement-line import endpoints echo the `ReconciliationPeriod` and the `StatementLine` respectively; the benefit-application endpoints echo the `BenefitApplication`; the consent endpoint echoes the `FamilyRecipient`; the report generation endpoint echoes the newly created `FamilyReport`; and the coordination disposition endpoint echoes the `CoordinationRequest`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Intake, Decisional Capacity, and the Authorization Instrument

A `FiduciaryManager` opens a file for a new client by supplying the person's `full_name`, their `date_of_birth` as a plain calendar date, and the `intake_reference` under which the referral arrived. The new client lands in status `INTAKE` with `capacity_determination` reading `NOT_DETERMINED`, and with `activated_on`, `closed_on` and `capacity_determined_on` all null. Nothing operational can happen yet: almost every business operation in this platform requires the governing client to be `ACTIVE`, and a client cannot become `ACTIVE` until a capacity finding is on file.

Recording that finding is a separate act. The manager submits a `determination` — one of `NOT_DETERMINED`, `RETAINS_CAPACITY`, `DIMINISHED_CAPACITY` or `LACKS_CAPACITY` — together with the `determined_on` date on which the clinician or court made it; these are written to `capacity_determination` and `capacity_determined_on` respectively. The finding matters twice over. It gates the move out of `INTAKE`, and it later decides whether the client themselves may set the family disclosure restrictions described in §3.9.

The client's own lifecycle is a four-state axis driven by a `to_status` value, with an optional `note` for the file. Moving `INTAKE` → `ACTIVE` requires the client to be in `INTAKE` and requires `capacity_determination` to be anything other than `NOT_DETERMINED` — a violation of either is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; on success the current logical date is written to `activated_on`. Moving `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` suspends the file temporarily, and it carries a cascade: every family recipient of that client whose `subscription_status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED`. The reverse move, `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE`, is symmetric — every family recipient of that client whose `subscription_status` is `SUSPENDED` moves back to `ACTIVE`. This cycle is re-entrant and uncapped; a file may be suspended and reinstated any number of times.

Closing a file is the terminal act, and it is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Both paths carry the same precondition and the same consequences, so neither is a shortcut for the other: the move is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` unless the client is in the correct source state, and it is refused with the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE` unless **no** payment on **any** of that client's accounts is still in `settlement_status` `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FLIGHT` — a fiduciary does not walk away from money in transit. When the move succeeds, the current logical date is written to `closed_on`, and every authorization instrument of that client whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is revoked with `revocation_reason` set to `CLIENT_CLOSED`, running the full revocation cascade described below. `CLOSED` is terminal: no later status change is accepted out of it.

**The instrument itself.** Agency in this platform is never implicit; it comes from a dated, signed instrument. A `FiduciaryManager` drafts one by naming the `client_id` it covers, the `scope_tier` it grants, the `instrument_reference` identifying the signed agreement or appointment order, and the `effective_date` and `expiry_date` bounding its force. The instrument is in force from `effective_date` inclusive through `expiry_date` inclusive, lapsing the following day. Its `expiry_date` must be strictly after its `effective_date`, and the span between the two dates may be no more than 730 days — a term of exactly 730 days is accepted, a term of 731 is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A newly drafted instrument sits in `DRAFT` with `executed_at`, `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` null and all three cascade counters at zero.

Executing an instrument moves it `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE`. The move requires the instrument to be in `DRAFT` and requires its client to be in `ACTIVE` or `INTAKE` — a suspended or closed client cannot have a new instrument executed against them, and either violation is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On execution, the current logical instant is written to `executed_at`, and **every other instrument of the same client whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`**. Superseding is a bookkeeping relabel and nothing more: it does **not** run the revocation cascade, because the successor instrument keeps the client's scheduled payments, family subscriptions and pending benefit applications alive. This is the single most important asymmetry in the instrument lifecycle — supersession preserves, revocation destroys.

Revocation moves an instrument `ACTIVE` → `REVOKED` and requires a `revocation_reason` that is present and non-empty. It runs a six-part cascade in one request, and every part of it is caused by the part above:

1. The instrument's own `status` becomes `REVOKED` and the current logical instant is written to `revoked_at`.
2. Caused by step 1: every payment whose governing instrument is this one and whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED`. Payments already `IN_FLIGHT`, `SETTLED`, `RETURNED`, `CANCELLED` or `STOPPED` are untouched — the cascade reaches only the ones that have not yet left the account.
3. Caused by step 2: each of those cancellations decrements the payment's budget line's `committed_cents` by that payment's `amount_cents` and recomputes that line's `remaining_cents`, `variance_full_cents`, `variance_ptd_cents` and `variance_direction`.
4. Also caused by step 2: each cancellation decrements the account's `committed_disbursements_cents` by that payment's `total_charge_cents`, recomputes the account's `available_balance_cents`, and decrements the `IN_FORCE` bond's `exposure_committed_cents` by that payment's `amount_cents`.
5. Every family recipient whose governing instrument is this one and whose `subscription_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED`.
6. Every benefit application whose governing instrument is this one and whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Applications already `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` are left alone.

The instrument then records what it destroyed: `cascaded_payments_cancelled`, `cascaded_recipients_terminated` and `cascaded_applications_withdrawn` are set to the respective counts, which are zero when nothing of that kind was outstanding. `REVOKED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal — no transition leaves them.

Expiry is the fourth exit, and it is a sweep rather than a gate. A lapsed instrument keeps its stored `status` of `ACTIVE` until an operator runs the periodic checkpoint described in §3.9, at which point every `ACTIVE` instrument whose `expiry_date` falls strictly before the current logical date moves to `EXPIRED`. This is pure bookkeeping: the authorization gate at rung 7 already refuses work under a lapsed-but-unswept instrument on the instrument's dates alone, so no operation ever slips through merely because the checkpoint has not run yet.

**What each tier permits.** The `scope_tier` on the instrument — `LIMITED_BILLPAY`, `FULL_AGENCY` or `REP_PAYEE` — is read by the scope gate at rung 8 on every operation that names an instrument. The matrix is:

| Operation | `LIMITED_BILLPAY` | `FULL_AGENCY` | `REP_PAYEE` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schedule or revise a payment to a `RECURRING_BILLER` payee | permitted | permitted | permitted |
| Schedule or revise a payment to an `ESSENTIAL` payee | refused | permitted | permitted |
| Schedule or revise a payment to a `DISCRETIONARY` payee | refused | permitted | refused |
| Draft a benefit application | refused | permitted | permitted |
| Enrol a family recipient | refused | permitted | permitted |
| File a dispute | permitted | permitted | permitted |

Every "refused" cell yields `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED` at rung 8. Separately, each tier carries a per-payment amount ceiling enforced much later, at rung 33: 250000 cents under `LIMITED_BILLPAY`, 400000 cents under `REP_PAYEE`, and no ceiling at all under `FULL_AGENCY`. Both ceilings are inclusive — a payment of exactly 250000 cents under a limited instrument, or exactly 400000 under a representative-payee instrument, is accepted. Because the ceiling is a fiscal rung and the matrix is a scope rung, a request that breaches **both** — a discretionary payment of 900000 cents under a limited instrument — comes back as `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED`, not as `ERR_SCOPE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.2. Accounts Under Management, Payees, and the Monthly Budget

A `FiduciaryManager` places an account under management by naming its `client_id`, the `institution_name` holding the funds, an `account_label` distinguishing it from the client's other accounts, an `account_type` of `CHECKING`, `SAVINGS` or `REP_PAYEE_TRUST`, and the `opening_balance_cents` on the books at the moment management begins. That opening figure must be zero or greater. A new account starts with `cleared_balance_cents` equal to `opening_balance_cents`, `pending_holds_cents` and `committed_disbursements_cents` at zero, `available_balance_cents` therefore equal to the opening balance, `fraud_posture` at `NORMAL`, and `fraud_posture_reason` null.

Four balance figures make up the account's picture and they are not interchangeable. `cleared_balance_cents` is the opening balance plus the signed sum of every ledger entry belonging to the account — every entry, regardless of whether its period has been frozen. `pending_holds_cents` is the running total of withdrawal holds placed minus holds released, and it is floored at zero: a release that would drive it negative is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than clamped. `committed_disbursements_cents` is the sum of `total_charge_cents` across the account's payments whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FLIGHT` — money promised but not yet gone. And `available_balance_cents` is the cleared balance minus the holds minus the commitments; unlike the holds figure it is allowed to go negative, because a fee posted after a commitment was taken can legitimately push it below zero.

A `BillPaySpecialist` or a `FiduciaryManager` moves those figures directly through the adjustments capability, supplying an `adjustment_type` of `DEPOSIT`, `WITHDRAWAL_HOLD` or `HOLD_RELEASE`, an `amount_cents` greater than zero, a `value_date`, and an optional `memo`. A `DEPOSIT` appends a `DEPOSIT` ledger entry of positive `amount_cents` dated the supplied `value_date`, which raises `cleared_balance_cents`; a `WITHDRAWAL_HOLD` raises `pending_holds_cents` and appends no entry; a `HOLD_RELEASE` lowers `pending_holds_cents` and likewise appends no entry. All three recompute `available_balance_cents` and all three echo the updated account. The receiving reconciliation period for the freeze gate is resolved from `value_date` in every case, not from the current logical date — which means a deposit back-dated into a closed month is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` even though today's month is wide open.

**Payees.** Before money can go anywhere, the recipient must be registered. A `BillPaySpecialist` or `FiduciaryManager` registers one by naming the `client_id` it serves, the `payee_name`, a `payee_type` of `RECURRING_BILLER`, `ESSENTIAL` or `DISCRETIONARY`, and the `remittance_reference` — the account or invoice number the practice quotes when remitting. A new payee is `ACTIVE` with `blocked_reason` null and `settled_payment_count` at zero. That counter is the practice's memory of the relationship: it rises by one each time a payment to this payee settles and falls by one when a settled payment is later returned, and it feeds the novelty component of fraud scoring in §3.5.

Blocking is a compliance act reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `blocked_reason`. The payee moves `ACTIVE` → `BLOCKED`, which is terminal — there is no unblock. Blocking also sweeps: every payment to that payee whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, each cancellation releasing the account's commitment by `total_charge_cents`, the budget line's `committed_cents` by `amount_cents`, and the bond's `exposure_committed_cents` by `amount_cents`. Payments already in flight or settled are not touched. Once a payee is `BLOCKED`, any attempt to schedule or revise a payment to them is refused with `ERR_PAYEE_BLOCKED` at rung 28.

**Budget lines.** A client's spending plan is expressed one month at a time. A `FiduciaryManager` creates a line by naming the `client_id`, a `category_name` such as `UTILITIES` or `GROCERIES`, the `budget_month` the allocation governs as a `YYYY-MM` string, and the `allocation_cents` — which must be greater than zero. The combination of owning client, `category_name` and `budget_month` must be unique; a repeat is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 16. A new line starts with `override_granted_cents` and `override_count` at zero, `last_override_reason` null, and `committed_cents` and `settled_cents` at zero.

When a month runs tight, the manager may grant an override rather than let a bill go unpaid, supplying the `added_cents` to grant and a `reason` explaining why. The reason must be at least 20 characters long — a bare "over budget" is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and `added_cents` must be greater than zero. The grant raises `override_granted_cents` by `added_cents`, increments `override_count` by one, records the text in `last_override_reason`, and recomputes the line's derived figures.

Three derived figures ride on every budget line and they answer three different questions. `committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` — principal only, service fee excluded — over the line's payments whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_FLIGHT` or `SETTLED`; cancelled, stopped and returned payments are excluded from it entirely. `settled_cents` is the same sum narrowed to payments whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`. And `remaining_cents` is `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents` minus `committed_cents`; it never goes negative, because rung 36 refuses any payment that would drive it below zero.

Two variance figures ride alongside them, and they deliberately disagree. **The full-period variance**, `variance_full_cents`, is `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents` minus `committed_cents` — the whole month's allocation measured against money that has merely been committed. **The period-to-date variance**, `variance_ptd_cents`, prorates instead. Let the number of days in `budget_month` be the month's own day count. The elapsed-day count is zero when the current logical date falls in a month earlier than `budget_month`; it is the full day count of the month when the current logical date falls in a later month; and when the current logical date falls inside `budget_month` itself, it is the day-of-month of the current logical date, floored at zero and capped at the month's day count. The prorated allocation is `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents`, multiplied by the elapsed-day count, divided by the month's day count, and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `variance_ptd_cents` is then that prorated allocation minus `settled_cents`.

`variance_direction` is keyed to the **period-to-date** figure, never to the full-period one: it reads `UNDER` when `variance_ptd_cents` is greater than zero, `EXACT` when it is exactly zero, and `OVER` when it is negative. The choice is deliberate. `variance_full_cents` can never go negative — rung 36 forbids it — so an `OVER` direction read off the full-period figure would be an enum value no client could ever reach. The period-to-date figure, by contrast, goes negative as soon as a month's spending front-loads, which is precisely the condition the field exists to flag. All five derived figures are recomputed whenever a payment on the line is created, revised, cancelled, stopped, settled or returned, and whenever an override is granted.

### 3.3. Preparing a Disbursement: Fees, Dates, Ceilings, and Dual Control

Only a `BillPaySpecialist` prepares a disbursement. The request names the four rows the payment hangs off — `account_id` for the account the funds leave, `payee_id` for the recipient, `authorization_id` for the instrument under whose authority the practice acts, and `budget_category_id` for the line the spending consumes — plus the `amount_cents` of principal, the `channel` the money will travel on, and the mandatory `expected_account_version` concurrency token described in §2.10. Three inputs are optional: a `requested_send_date` naming the day the preparer wants the money to leave, a `memo` for the remittance, and a `duplicate_override` boolean that defaults to false. The `amount_cents` must be greater than zero, and `channel` is one of `CHECK`, `ACH` or `BILLER_DIRECT`.

**The fiduciary service fee.** Before any fiscal rung is evaluated, the practice's per-disbursement fee is selected from a three-band table keyed on the **pre-fee principal alone**. A principal below 50000 cents carries a fee of 250 cents. A principal from 50000 cents up to but not including 250000 cents carries 500 cents. A principal of 250000 cents or more carries 900 cents. The chosen figure is written to `service_fee_cents`, and `total_charge_cents` is `amount_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`. The definition looks circular — the total charge is what the account is charged, and the fee is the fee on that charge — so the evaluation order is pinned: select the band from `amount_cents`, read that band's flat fee, add the two, and **never re-select the band from `total_charge_cents`**. A principal of 249900 cents therefore carries a 500-cent fee and a 250400-cent total charge, even though the total crosses the third band's floor.

**Three commitment bases, deliberately different.** The figure a payment commits differs by which aggregate is being charged, and the three are not interchangeable. The account's `committed_disbursements_cents` accumulates `total_charge_cents` — principal plus fee, because the fee leaves the account too. The budget line's `committed_cents` accumulates `amount_cents` only, because the practice's fee is not the client's grocery money. The bond's `exposure_committed_cents` also accumulates `amount_cents` only. A payment of 49999 cents therefore commits 50249 against the account, 49999 against the budget line, and 49999 against the bond.

**The projected settlement date.** Alongside the fee, the platform computes when the money will actually land, and writes that to `projected_settlement_date`. The computation takes three inputs — the instant of evaluation, the `requested_send_date` if one was supplied, and the `channel` — and runs in three ordered stages.

*Stage one, the cut-off.* Start from the `requested_send_date` when the preparer supplied one, and from the evaluation instant's own calendar date otherwise. If that starting date is the same calendar date as the evaluation instant **and** the evaluation instant's time of day is at or after 16:00:00 UTC, advance the date by one calendar day. The cut-off boundary is inclusive on the late side: an instant of exactly 16:00:00 rolls to the next day, while 15:59:59 does not. A `requested_send_date` in the future is never touched by the cut-off, because it is not the evaluation instant's own date.

*Stage two, the weekend roll.* While the resulting date falls on a Saturday or a Sunday, advance it one calendar day at a time until it lands on a Monday through Friday. The roll only ever moves forward, never backward. No holidays are observed anywhere in this system — the only non-business days are the two weekend days. The date at the end of this stage is the effective submit date.

*Stage three, the business-day lead.* Each channel carries a lead time counted in business days: `CHECK` takes 5, `ACH` takes 2, and `BILLER_DIRECT` takes 0. The **submit date itself is not counted** — it is day zero, and the lead advances from it. Advance the requisite number of times; each single advance moves the date forward one calendar day and then, if that lands on a weekend, keeps moving forward one day at a time until it reaches a weekday. A `BILLER_DIRECT` payment therefore settles on its own submit date, while an `ACH` payment submitted on a Thursday settles on the following Monday.

A supplied `requested_send_date` may not be earlier than the current logical date; the current logical date itself is fine. An earlier one is refused with `ERR_SEND_DATE_IN_PAST` at rung 20.

**The duplicate-payment buffer.** Paying the same bill twice is the classic bill-pay error, so the platform looks for a near-collision before it accepts a new payment. A collision exists when some other payment shares this request's account, shares its payee, carries exactly the same `amount_cents`, is still in `settlement_status` `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FLIGHT`, and whose `projected_settlement_date` is within three whole calendar days of the new payment's own `projected_settlement_date`. The buffer is symmetric around the projected date — three days earlier collides just as three days later does — and it is inclusive at the boundary: a gap of exactly 3 days collides, a gap of 4 days does not. Payments that are `CANCELLED`, `STOPPED`, `SETTLED` or `RETURNED` never collide, however close their dates. When a collision is found and `duplicate_override` was not supplied as true, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYMENT_WINDOW` at rung 19. Setting `duplicate_override` to true skips this rung and only this rung. The rule is evaluated when a payment is first scheduled and never when one is revised.

**Referential integrity.** The payee and the budget line named on a payment must both belong to the same client as the account, and the budget line's `budget_month` must equal the calendar month of the payment's `projected_settlement_date` — which is why the settlement date is computed before this check runs. A mismatch on either count is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` at rung 13.

**The organization's surety bond.** A `PlatformAdmin` registers the practice's bond by naming the `carrier_name`, the carrier-issued `bond_number`, the aggregate `coverage_cents` — which must be greater than zero — and the `effective_date` and `expiry_date` bounding its force, with expiry strictly after effective. A new bond is created `IN_FORCE`, and creating it moves any bond currently `IN_FORCE` to `SUPERSEDED`; exactly one bond is `IN_FORCE` at any instant, and that is the one the bond gate at rung 6 reads. Registering a bond recomputes both exposure figures against the current payment population, so a bond registered mid-practice immediately reflects what is already outstanding. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and a superseded bond's exposure figures are no longer maintained — they retain whatever they last held.

Two exposure figures ride on the in-force bond and only one of them gates anything. `exposure_committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over **every payment belonging to any client in the system** whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FLIGHT` — money promised but not yet gone. `exposure_settled_inclusive_cents` is that committed figure plus the sum of `amount_cents` over every payment of any client whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `settled_on` falls in the current logical calendar month; it is therefore always at least the committed figure, it may exceed `coverage_cents` freely, and it refuses nothing — it exists for supervisory review. `headroom_committed_cents` is `coverage_cents` minus `exposure_committed_cents`, and it may go negative if coverage was lowered after exposure had already accrued. The committed figure is the only one the ceiling at rung 35 consults: a payment is refused with `ERR_BOND_COVERAGE_EXCEEDED` when `exposure_committed_cents` plus this payment's `amount_cents` would exceed `coverage_cents`, and landing exactly on coverage is permitted. Because that aggregate spans every client, a small payment for one client can be refused because a different client's committed payments have consumed the headroom.

**The three fiscal ceilings on a fresh payment.** In rung order: the instrument's per-payment ceiling at rung 33 tests `amount_cents` against the tier limits in §3.1; the bond ceiling at rung 35 tests the organization-wide committed exposure; the budget ceiling at rung 36 refuses the payment with `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` when the line's `committed_cents` plus this `amount_cents` would exceed `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents`, landing exactly on the allocation being permitted; and the account ceiling at rung 37 refuses with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE` when the account's `available_balance_cents` is less than this payment's `total_charge_cents`, an account holding exactly the total charge being sufficient. Note the asymmetry once more: the budget ceiling is tested against principal, the account ceiling against principal plus fee.

**Two axes, resolved and set.** A payment carries two independent status axes on the same row, and both are established by the create. On the settlement axis, `settlement_status` is set to `SCHEDULED`. On the approval axis, the machine's entry state `PREPARED` is resolved inside the very same request: a payment whose `amount_cents` is **below** 100000 cents lands in `APPROVED` under single control with `approved_by_user_id` left null, and a payment whose `amount_cents` is **at or above** 100000 cents lands in `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` under dual control. The threshold boundary is inclusive on the dual-control side — a payment of exactly 100000 cents needs a second approver. Because the resolution happens inside the request, no read of a payment ever returns `PREPARED`. The caller's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `prepared_by_user_id`, `revision_count` starts at zero, and `effective_settlement_date`, `released_at`, `settled_on`, `return_reason` and `stop_reason` are all null.

Finally the commitments land: the account's `committed_disbursements_cents` rises by `total_charge_cents` and its `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, the budget line's `committed_cents` rises by `amount_cents` and its variance figures are recomputed, and the in-force bond's `exposure_committed_cents` rises by `amount_cents`.

**Second control.** A payment sitting in `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` is adjudicated by a `FiduciaryManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` — either role may render either verdict, since role membership is the only role check — who submits a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` with an optional `note`. The decision requires the payment's `approval_status` to be `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` and its `settlement_status` to still be `SCHEDULED`; a violation of either is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It also requires that the approver's `X-User-ID` differ from the payment's `prepared_by_user_id` — the same natural person may not prepare and approve the same disbursement even while wearing a different role's hat — and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at rung 29. An `APPROVE` moves `approval_status` to `APPROVED` and writes the approver's `X-User-ID` to `approved_by_user_id`. A `REJECT` moves `approval_status` to `REJECTED`, sets `approved_by_user_id` back to null, and — this is the point of keeping the axes independent — **leaves `settlement_status` at `SCHEDULED`**, so the funds stay committed against the account, the budget line and the bond while the payment is reworked.

**Rework.** A `BillPaySpecialist` reworks a rejected payment by submitting a new `amount_cents` and an optional `memo`. The rework requires `approval_status` to be `REJECTED` and `settlement_status` to still be `SCHEDULED`. It recomputes `service_fee_cents` and `total_charge_cents` from the revised principal using the same band table, adjusts the account's `committed_disbursements_cents` by the `total_charge_cents` delta and recomputes `available_balance_cents`, adjusts the budget line's `committed_cents` by the `amount_cents` delta and recomputes the variance figures, adjusts the bond's `exposure_committed_cents` by the `amount_cents` delta, and increments `revision_count` by one. It does **not** recompute `projected_settlement_date`, which stays exactly as it was fixed at creation.

Where the rework lands depends only on the revised amount: a revised `amount_cents` at or above 100000 cents re-enters `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` for another round of dual control, and a revised `amount_cents` below 100000 cents goes straight to `APPROVED`, dropping the payment out of dual control entirely. The rejection-and-rework loop is uncapped by design, because every pass re-runs every fiscal rung. Those rungs are re-evaluated **on the delta**: the bond ceiling asks whether `exposure_committed_cents`, with this payment's current `amount_cents` backed out and the revised `amount_cents` put in, would exceed `coverage_cents`; the budget ceiling asks the same of `committed_cents` against `allocation_cents` plus `override_granted_cents`; and the account ceiling asks whether `available_balance_cents` plus this payment's current `total_charge_cents` is at least the revised `total_charge_cents`. The instrument ceiling at rung 33 and the payee-blocked check at rung 28 apply to a rework exactly as they do to a fresh payment; the duplicate-payment buffer does not.

### 3.4. The Settlement Axis: Release, Settle, Return, Cancel, Stop

The settlement axis runs on its own clock, independent of the approval axis on the same row. It starts at `SCHEDULED`, advances through `IN_FLIGHT` to `SETTLED`, and has three terminal exits — `CANCELLED`, `STOPPED`, and `RETURNED` — the last of which is reached from `SETTLED`, after the money has already moved.

**Release.** A `BillPaySpecialist` releases an approved payment to the rail, moving `settlement_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_FLIGHT`. The release carries no request body. It requires the payment to be `SCHEDULED` on the settlement axis — checked at rung 12 — and it separately requires `approval_status` to read `APPROVED`, which is checked at rung 30 and reported as `ERR_SECOND_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`. This split is normative: releasing a payment that is `SCHEDULED` but still `PENDING_SECOND_APPROVAL` returns `ERR_SECOND_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A release into a frozen account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_FROZEN` at rung 9; a release is **not** subject to the reconciliation freeze, because it moves no money onto the ledger.

Releasing writes `released_at` from the current logical instant and computes `effective_settlement_date` — the second variant of the settlement-date calculation. The effective variant runs the same three stages as the projection, but at the release instant and **with `requested_send_date` treated as absent**, so the starting date is the release instant's own calendar date and the cut-off test always applies. `projected_settlement_date` is deliberately left untouched, so the two figures can be compared afterwards: a payment released on a later day, or on the same day but at or after the 16:00:00 UTC cut-off, carries an effective date strictly later than its projection.

**Settlement events.** The rail's outcome arrives as an `event` of `SETTLE` or `RETURN`, with an optional `return_reason`.

A `SETTLE` moves the payment `IN_FLIGHT` → `SETTLED` and writes the current logical date to `settled_on`, which is the moment that starts the dispute filing window. It appends **two** ledger entries dated the current logical date, both naming this payment as their source: a `DISBURSEMENT` entry of negative `amount_cents` and a `SERVICE_FEE` entry of negative `service_fee_cents`. The account's `committed_disbursements_cents` falls by `total_charge_cents` while its `cleared_balance_cents` falls by the same total, so `available_balance_cents` is exactly unchanged by settlement — the money simply moves from promised to gone. The payee's `settled_payment_count` rises by one. The receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` rises by two. On the bond, `exposure_committed_cents` falls by `amount_cents`; because `exposure_settled_inclusive_cents` is the committed figure plus this month's settled sum, and this payment has just joined that settled sum, `exposure_settled_inclusive_cents` itself is left exactly where it was — the same cents simply moved from the committed term into the settled term. On the budget line, `committed_cents` is deliberately **left alone** — a settled payment is still committed spending — while `settled_cents` rises by `amount_cents`.

A `RETURN` moves the payment `SETTLED` → `RETURNED` and requires a `return_reason` that is present and non-empty. It appends two more entries dated the current logical date: a `RETURN_REVERSAL` of positive `total_charge_cents`, giving back principal and fee alike, and an `NSF_FEE` of negative 3500 cents. The net effect on the account is therefore `total_charge_cents` minus 3500 cents — the compensation is deliberately lossy by exactly the insufficient-funds fee. The payee's `settled_payment_count` falls by one. The budget line's `committed_cents` **and** `settled_cents` both fall by `amount_cents` and the variance figures are recomputed. The bond's `exposure_settled_inclusive_cents` falls by `amount_cents`. The receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` rises by two.

**Cancellation.** A `BillPaySpecialist` or a `FiduciaryManager` may cancel a payment that is still `SCHEDULED`, optionally supplying a `reason`, moving it `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED`. Cancellation is the exact compensating action for scheduling: the account's `committed_disbursements_cents` falls by `total_charge_cents` and `available_balance_cents` is recomputed, the budget line's `committed_cents` falls by `amount_cents` and its variance figures are recomputed, and the bond's `exposure_committed_cents` falls by `amount_cents`. Every conserved quantity returns to precisely its pre-scheduling value. Crucially, **cancellation appends no ledger entry at all** — no money ever left the account, so there is nothing to record and there is deliberately no cancellation entry type in the ledger's vocabulary. The same cancellation routine is what the authorization-revocation cascade, the payee-block sweep and the account freeze sweep invoke.

**Stop payment.** A `FiduciaryManager` alone may stop a payment, supplying a `stop_reason`. A stop is legal from either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FLIGHT` — the two branches behave identically — and it is bounded by a window: the current logical instant must be at or before the payment's `created_at` plus 48 hours, with the boundary inclusive, so a stop request landing at exactly 48 hours after creation still succeeds and one at 48 hours and one second is refused with `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at rung 21. Stopping appends a `STOP_PAYMENT_FEE` ledger entry of negative 3000 cents dated the current logical date, then releases all three commitments exactly as a cancellation does — `total_charge_cents` off the account's commitment, `amount_cents` off the budget line, `amount_cents` off the bond — and raises the receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` by one. The net effect on `available_balance_cents` is therefore a rise of `total_charge_cents` minus 3000 cents.

The 48-hour window belongs to the operator-initiated stop and to nothing else. When the account freeze sweep described in §3.5 stops an in-flight payment, the window is not consulted at all — the sweep is a system action taken on the practice's own initiative, not a request from an operator.

### 3.5. Scam and Fraud Response: Alerts, Posture, and the Freeze Sweep

Clients in daily money management are targeted constantly, so raising an alert is cheap and deliberately available to two roles: a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `BillPaySpecialist` may raise one, naming the `account_id` the alert concerns, the `payment_id` of the disbursement that triggered it, a `reason_code` of `UNRECOGNIZED_PAYEE`, `AMOUNT_ANOMALY`, `CHANNEL_ANOMALY` or `THIRD_PARTY_REPORT`, and an optional free-text `detail`. Raising an alert works even while the account's month is closed — a frozen ledger must never silence a fraud report.

A new alert lands in status `NEW` and is scored immediately. **The score is the sum of three components**, each read off exactly one payment row and its payee row.

- *Amount.* A payment of 500000 cents or more contributes 50 points. A payment of at least 100000 cents but below 500000 contributes 30 points. Anything below 100000 cents contributes 10 points. Both thresholds are inclusive at their lower edge.
- *Novelty.* A payee whose `settled_payment_count` is exactly zero — the practice has never successfully paid them — contributes 40 points. A payee with a thin history, meaning a `settled_payment_count` of 1 or 2, contributes 20 points. An established payee, meaning 3 or more settled payments, contributes nothing.
- *Channel.* A `CHECK` payment contributes 20 points, an `ACH` payment 10, and a `BILLER_DIRECT` payment none.

The three components sum to a score between 10 and 110 inclusive. That score becomes `intake_risk_score`, and `intake_risk_band` follows from it: a score of 90 or more is `HIGH`, a score of at least 50 but below 90 is `MEDIUM`, and anything below 50 is `LOW`. Both band boundaries are inclusive at their lower edge. The band then sets the acknowledgement clock: `ack_due_at` is the alert's `created_at` plus 4 hours for a `HIGH` band, plus 24 hours for `MEDIUM`, and plus 72 hours for `LOW`. `review_risk_score`, `review_risk_band`, `acknowledged_at` and `disposition_note` are all null on a fresh alert.

Adjudication belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer` alone, who supplies an `action` and an optional `disposition_note`. An `ACKNOWLEDGE` moves the alert from `NEW` or from `ESCALATED` into `ACKNOWLEDGED` — the escalated path rejoins the ordinary one and is adjudicated identically — writes the current logical instant to `acknowledged_at`, and **re-scores the alert**. The re-score uses exactly the same three components but reads the payee's `settled_payment_count` as it stands **now**, writing the result to `review_risk_score` and `review_risk_band`. If payments to that payee settled between intake and acknowledgement, the novelty component drops from 40 to 20 or from 20 to 0 and the band can fall a whole step — while `ack_due_at` keeps the deadline the intake band gave it, because the deadline was set once and is never recomputed. Nothing else changes on a re-score.

From `ACKNOWLEDGED` the alert forks. A `DISMISS` moves it to `DISMISSED` and leaves the account's `fraud_posture` completely untouched. A `CONFIRM_FRAUD` moves it to `CONFIRMED_FRAUD` and, caused by that confirmation, drives the named account's `fraud_posture` to `FROZEN`, which in turn runs the freeze sweep below. Both `CONFIRMED_FRAUD` and `DISMISSED` are terminal.

An alert that nobody acknowledges in time does not sit quietly. The periodic checkpoint of §3.9 moves every alert still in `NEW` whose `ack_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant into `ESCALATED`; the boundary is inclusive, so an alert whose deadline equals the clock exactly is escalated. Escalation changes nothing but the status — it is a supervisory flag, not a state that blocks anything.

**Account posture.** Independently of any alert, a `ComplianceOfficer` may drive an account's `fraud_posture` directly, supplying a `to_posture` and a `reason` that is recorded as `fraud_posture_reason`. The axis is cyclic and re-entrant: `NORMAL` → `WATCH`, `WATCH` → `FROZEN`, `NORMAL` → `FROZEN` directly, `WATCH` → `NORMAL`, and `FROZEN` → `NORMAL`. There is no terminal posture; an account that has been unfrozen may be frozen again without limit. `WATCH` is purely observational — it gates no disbursement whatsoever, and only `FROZEN` does. Any request whose `to_posture` is `NORMAL`, whether it comes from `WATCH` or from `FROZEN`, requires its `reason` to be at least 20 characters long; a shorter one is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 15. Unfreezing carries one further precondition beyond that: **no fraud alert on the account may still be in `NEW`, `ESCALATED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`**. Every alert must have reached `CONFIRMED_FRAUD` or `DISMISSED` before the money can move again; otherwise the transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Posture changes are permitted while a reconciliation period is frozen.

**The freeze sweep.** Both routes into `FROZEN` — the direct posture change and the confirmed-fraud cascade — run the identical ordered routine, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. Every payment on the account is visited in ascending order of its `created_at`, and for each:

- **Hop A.** A payment whose `settlement_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`. No ledger entry is appended, because nothing has left the account. The cancellation decrements the account's `committed_disbursements_cents` by that payment's `total_charge_cents`, decrements the budget line's `committed_cents` by its `amount_cents`, and decrements the bond's `exposure_committed_cents` by its `amount_cents`.
- **Hop B.** A payment whose `settlement_status` is `IN_FLIGHT` moves to `STOPPED`. It decrements the same three aggregates in the same way **and**, unlike hop A, appends one `STOP_PAYMENT_FEE` ledger entry of negative 3000 cents dated the current logical date. The 48-hour stop window is not consulted; the sweep is a system action, not an operator's stop request.
- **Hop C.** Caused by hop B's new entry: the reconciliation period that receives that entry has its `unreconciled_entry_count` raised by one. Hop C fires only for payments that went through hop B — a sweep that finds only scheduled payments produces no entries and therefore no counter movement at all.
- **Hop D.** Caused by hop C: if — and only if — that receiving period's `status` is `IN_REVIEW`, it reverts to `OPEN`, because a period under review can no longer be reviewed once a fresh unreconciled entry has landed in it. A period that is `OPEN`, `REOPENED`, `CLOSED` or `LOCKED` when the entry arrives stays exactly where it is; hop D does not fire for it.

Payments already `SETTLED`, `RETURNED`, `CANCELLED` or `STOPPED` are untouched by the sweep in all four hops. Once the account is `FROZEN`, the fraud-freeze gate at rung 9 refuses new payments, revisions, releases, settlement events, account adjustments and provisional credits against it with `ERR_ACCOUNT_FROZEN`.

### 3.6. Disputes and Provisional Credit

When a settled disbursement turns out to be wrong — unauthorised, mis-stated, never delivered, or the product of a scam — a `FiduciaryManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` files a dispute against it, naming the `payment_id`, a `reason_code` of `UNAUTHORIZED`, `WRONG_AMOUNT`, `GOODS_NOT_RECEIVED` or `SCAM_INDUCED`, and the `disputed_amount_cents`. The disputed amount must be greater than zero and no greater than the disputed payment's own `amount_cents`; a larger claim is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Filing writes the current logical date to `filed_on` and records `days_since_settlement` as the whole number of days from the payment's `settled_on` to `filed_on`. The filing window is 60 days from settlement, inclusive: a dispute filed on exactly the sixtieth day still goes through, and one filed on the sixty-first is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at rung 22. A new dispute sits in `FILED` with `provisional_credit_cents` at zero and `resolution_note` null.

From `FILED` there are three ways out, and the ledger consequences differ sharply between them.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may extend a **provisional credit** while the investigation runs, moving the dispute `FILED` → `PROVISIONAL_CREDITED`. The credit is capped: `disputed_amount_cents` must be at or below 500000 cents, with exactly 500000 still crediting and anything above refused with `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` at rung 39. On success `provisional_credit_cents` is set to `disputed_amount_cents`, a `DISPUTE_PROVISIONAL_CREDIT` ledger entry of positive `provisional_credit_cents` dated the current logical date is appended, the account's `cleared_balance_cents` and `available_balance_cents` both rise by that amount, and the receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` rises by one.

A `ComplianceOfficer` resolves a dispute by supplying an `outcome` of `UPHELD` or `DENIED` with an optional `resolution_note`, and the four combinations of source state and outcome are all reachable and all distinct:

- **Credited and upheld** (`PROVISIONAL_CREDITED` → `UPHELD`): the provisional credit stays in place permanently. **No reversal entry is appended and no balance moves.** The client keeps the money.
- **Credited and denied** (`PROVISIONAL_CREDITED` → `DENIED`): a `DISPUTE_CREDIT_REVERSAL` ledger entry of negative `provisional_credit_cents` dated the current logical date is appended, and the account's `cleared_balance_cents` and `available_balance_cents` fall back to exactly their pre-credit values. The receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` rises by one. File, credit, deny is an exact round trip on the cleared balance.
- **Uncredited and denied** (`FILED` → `DENIED`): **no ledger entry is appended at all** — there was never a credit to reverse — and no balance moves.
- **Uncredited and upheld** (`FILED` → `UPHELD`): the payout happens at resolution instead of before it. `provisional_credit_cents` is set to `disputed_amount_cents`, a `DISPUTE_PROVISIONAL_CREDIT` ledger entry of positive `disputed_amount_cents` dated the current logical date is appended, the account's `cleared_balance_cents` and `available_balance_cents` both rise, and the receiving period's `unreconciled_entry_count` rises by one. Because this branch grants a credit, the 500000-cent cap at rung 39 applies to it exactly as it applies to the provisional-credit path.

`UPHELD` and `DENIED` are terminal. Applying a provisional credit is gated by the account fraud freeze and by the reconciliation freeze; resolving a dispute is gated by the reconciliation freeze but not by the account freeze, because a resolution may need to unwind a credit on an account that has since been frozen.

### 3.7. Bookkeeping: the Ledger, Reconciliation Periods, and Statement Matching

Every movement of real money in this platform produces a `LedgerEntry`, and those entries are the system's ground truth. An entry is written by the platform, never by a caller, and it is immutable once written: nothing edits it, nothing deletes it, and a correction is always a fresh reversing entry. Each entry names the `account_id` it posts against, carries a signed `amount_cents` in which a negative value reduces the account's cleared balance, an `entry_date` fixing which month it belongs to, a human-readable `memo`, and an `entry_type` from a closed vocabulary: `DEPOSIT`, `DISBURSEMENT`, `SERVICE_FEE`, `STOP_PAYMENT_FEE`, `RETURN_REVERSAL`, `NSF_FEE`, `DISPUTE_PROVISIONAL_CREDIT`, `DISPUTE_CREDIT_REVERSAL` and `BENEFIT_AWARD`. There is deliberately **no cancellation entry type**: cancelling a scheduled payment produces no entry, because nothing had left the account. Entries produced by a payment carry that payment's identifier in `source_payment_id`; deposits and benefit awards leave `source_payment_id` null. The `retro_period_month` field is null on every entry except a retroactive benefit award, as §3.8 explains.

Which month an entry belongs to is resolved the moment it is appended. The platform looks for the reconciliation period of the same account whose `period_month` equals the `YYYY-MM` of the entry's `entry_date`. Because a period's month is unique per account, that search finds either exactly one period or none — there is no ranking and no tie-break to perform. When it finds one, that period's identifier is written to `period_id`; when it finds none, `period_id` stays null. An entry with a null `period_id` is never frozen. The `frozen` flag itself is not stored: it is derived at read time as true exactly when the entry's `period_id` names a period whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `LOCKED`, and false otherwise — so freezing and reopening a period flips the flag on its entries without any of them being rewritten.

**Opening a period.** A `BillPaySpecialist` or `FiduciaryManager` opens the books for one account-month by naming the `account_id` and the `period_month`. Only one period may exist per account per month; a repeat is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` at rung 18. Calendar months partition the timeline exactly, so an adjacent month never collides — only an exact repeat does. A new period lands in `OPEN` with `imported_line_count`, `open_exception_count` and `reopen_count` at zero, `closed_at` null and `has_retro_adjustment` false. Opening a period is retroactive in one respect: every existing ledger entry of that account whose `entry_date` falls inside `period_month` is adopted into the new period — those entries' `period_id` resolves to it, and the period's `unreconciled_entry_count` is set to the number adopted.

**Importing statement lines.** A `BillPaySpecialist` imports the institution's statement one line at a time, supplying the line's `external_ref` — the bank's own reference, which must be unique within the period — its signed `amount_cents` exactly as the institution reported it, the `line_date` it posted, and an optional `line_description` copied from the statement. A duplicate `external_ref` within the same period is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 16. Each import creates a line in `match_status` `UNMATCHED` with `matched_ledger_entry_id` and `exception_note` null, and raises the period's `imported_line_count` by one.

**Matching.** A `BillPaySpecialist` or `FiduciaryManager` disposes of a line by supplying an `action`, the mandatory `expected_period_version` concurrency token, and — depending on the action — a `ledger_entry_id` or an `exception_note`. Four dispositions exist and they form a two-level fork.

From `UNMATCHED`, a `MATCH` names the `ledger_entry_id` of the entry the line corresponds to and moves the line to `MATCHED`. The named entry must belong to the same period as the line and its `amount_cents` must equal the line's `amount_cents` exactly; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` at rung 14. On success the entry's identifier is written to `matched_ledger_entry_id` and the period's `unreconciled_entry_count` falls by one. Also from `UNMATCHED`, a `FLAG_EXCEPTION` records the supplied `exception_note` and moves the line to `EXCEPTION`, raising the period's `open_exception_count` by one; nothing on the ledger side moves.

From `EXCEPTION` the line forks again. A `RESOLVE_MATCHED` names a `ledger_entry_id` subject to the same period-and-amount test, moves the line to `RESOLVED_MATCHED`, writes `matched_ledger_entry_id`, and lowers **both** the period's `open_exception_count` and its `unreconciled_entry_count` by one. A `RESOLVE_WRITTEN_OFF` records the `exception_note`, moves the line to `RESOLVED_WRITTEN_OFF`, and lowers only `open_exception_count` — writing a line off leaves `unreconciled_entry_count` untouched, because nothing on the ledger side was ever matched. `MATCHED`, `RESOLVED_MATCHED` and `RESOLVED_WRITTEN_OFF` are all terminal. All four dispositions are available to either authorized role — a `BillPaySpecialist` and a `FiduciaryManager` may each perform any of them, since endpoint role membership is the only role check the disposition performs.

**Closing the books.** The period's own status axis is a cycle. A `BillPaySpecialist` or `FiduciaryManager` moves an `OPEN` period to `IN_REVIEW` by supplying a `to_status`. A period `IN_REVIEW` reverts automatically to `OPEN` the moment a new ledger entry lands in it, from whatever source — a settlement, a stop fee, a deposit, a dispute credit or a benefit award. That revert is the hop that closes the freeze-sweep cascade.

Closing requires the period to be `IN_REVIEW` with `open_exception_count` at zero; an outstanding exception is refused with `ERR_OPEN_EXCEPTIONS` at rung 27. A period with zero imported lines closes perfectly well — the test is on the exception count, not on non-emptiness. **Where the close lands depends on the period's history**: a period whose `reopen_count` is still zero closes into `CLOSED`, while a period that has already been reopened once — `reopen_count` of 1 — closes into `LOCKED` instead. The same request, the same `to_status` of `CLOSED`, produces two different destination states. Either way the current logical instant is written to `closed_at`, every ledger entry of that period now reads `frozen` as true, and the reconciliation-freeze gate at rung 10 begins refusing the operations it guards.

A `CLOSED` period may be reopened once, by supplying a `to_status` of `REOPENED`; the request must arrive at or before `closed_at` plus 30 days, with the boundary inclusive, and a later request is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_NOT_PERMITTED` at rung 23. Reopening raises `reopen_count` by one and every ledger entry of the period reads `frozen` as false again. From `REOPENED` the period goes back to `IN_REVIEW`, and the next close — because `reopen_count` now reads 1 — lands in `LOCKED`. `LOCKED` is terminal and can never be reopened: the state gate at rung 12 refuses it with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, which is why the reopen *count* has no rung of its own. The period status endpoint is deliberately exempt from the reconciliation freeze in every direction, since the reopen path must be able to survive the freeze it exists to lift. Every one of the period's status moves — into review, into `CLOSED` or `LOCKED`, and back out to `REOPENED` — is available to either authorized role, a `BillPaySpecialist` and a `FiduciaryManager` alike, because endpoint role membership is the only role check the status endpoint performs.

### 3.8. Benefit Applications, Program Limits, and Retroactive Awards

Keeping a client's means-tested benefits alive is a core part of the service, and the platform models the filing as its own lifecycle. A `FiduciaryManager` drafts an application by naming the `client_id` applying, the `authorization_id` under which the practice acts on their behalf, the `program` — one of `SSI`, `MEDICAID`, `SNAP` or `LIHEAP` — the `asserted_monthly_income_cents` claimed on the form, which must be zero or greater, and a `resource_items` array declaring what the client owns. Each element of that array is an object with an `item_type` of `HOME`, `VEHICLE`, `BURIAL_FUND`, `RETIREMENT`, `LIFE_INSURANCE` or `CASH`, and a `value_cents` that must be zero or greater. Drafting requires an instrument of tier `FULL_AGENCY` or `REP_PAYEE`; a `LIMITED_BILLPAY` instrument is refused at rung 8.

Two resource totals are computed at draft time and both are reported. `countable_resources_gross_cents` is the plain sum of `value_cents` over every declared item, with no exclusion of any kind — the raw declared worth of the estate. `countable_resources_net_cents` applies the programme exclusion table item by item, and this is the figure the limits are tested against:

- a `HOME` counts as nothing;
- a `VEHICLE` counts as nothing;
- a `BURIAL_FUND` counts as its `value_cents` less 150000 cents, floored at zero, so a burial fund of 150000 cents or less counts as nothing;
- a `RETIREMENT` holding counts as its `value_cents` multiplied by 0.665, rounded half up to a whole cent;
- a `LIFE_INSURANCE` policy counts as its `value_cents` multiplied by 0.500, rounded half up to a whole cent;
- `CASH` counts at full `value_cents`.

The rounding order is pinned: **round each item individually, then sum the rounded figures**. Summing first and rounding once produces a different total whenever two or more rate-bearing items are present, and the per-item order is the one this platform uses. For any application holding a home or a vehicle the two totals differ by at least that item's full declared value.

Submitting is a separate act, driven by an `action` of `SUBMIT`. It requires the application to be in `DRAFT` and it tests both programme limits at once, refusing with `ERR_RESOURCE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at rung 38 if either fails. The resource ceilings are 200000 cents for `SSI`, 300000 for `MEDICAID`, 275000 for `SNAP` and 400000 for `LIHEAP`; the monthly income ceilings are 96700 cents for `SSI`, 150000 for `MEDICAID`, 182000 for `SNAP` and 250000 for `LIHEAP`. Both boundaries are inclusive — an application landing exactly on a ceiling passes. On success `submitted_on` is written with the current logical date and the application moves `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`.

**Recording the agency's answer.** When the awarding agency responds, a `FiduciaryManager` records the `outcome` — `APPROVED` or `DENIED` — together with the `decision_date` the agency issued it, and the `account_id` of the client account that should receive any money. A denial additionally requires a `denial_reason` that is present and non-empty, and an approval additionally carries the `award_effective_date` on which the award takes effect and the `award_monthly_amount_cents` granted. Recording a decision is permitted while a reconciliation period is frozen.

A denial writes `decision_date` and `denial_reason` and lands the application in `DENIED`, and it does so identically whether the decision arrived on a submission or on an appeal. `DENIED` is deliberately **not** terminal: it is the state an appeal departs from and the state a second denial returns to, rather than a separate final state. That matters because the only thing stopping a second appeal is the appeal cap — routing a second denial into a terminal state would let the state gate at rung 12 pre-empt that cap and make `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` unreachable. An `APPEAL` action moves the application `DENIED` → `APPEALED` and raises `appeal_count` by one; it requires `appeal_count` to still be below 1, so exactly one appeal is available and the second is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` at rung 26.

**The retroactive award.** An approval — from `SUBMITTED` or from `APPEALED`, with identical consequences either way — computes and posts the arrears the agency owes. First the lookback is checked: `decision_date` minus `award_effective_date` must be at most 180 days, inclusive, and `award_effective_date` must not fall after `decision_date`; a violation of either is `ERR_RETRO_LOOKBACK_EXCEEDED` at rung 24. Then `retro_months` is computed as the whole-calendar-month distance between the two dates — the decision's year multiplied by twelve plus the decision's month, less the effective date's year multiplied by twelve plus the effective date's month — which is zero when both dates fall in the same calendar month. `retro_award_cents` is `award_monthly_amount_cents` multiplied by `retro_months`, and `decision_date`, `award_effective_date`, `award_monthly_amount_cents`, `retro_months` and `retro_award_cents` are all written to the application.

Caused by that write, a `BENEFIT_AWARD` ledger entry of positive `retro_award_cents` is appended to the named account. **The entry is appended even when `retro_award_cents` is zero**, so a non-retroactive award is just as observable on the ledger as a retroactive one. Which month the entry lands in is decided by a two-branch rule that exists precisely so an award never has to write into books that are already closed. Let the award month be the `YYYY-MM` of `award_effective_date`, and look for the receiving account's reconciliation period for that month:

- **If such a period exists and its `status` is `CLOSED` or `LOCKED`**, the entry is dated the **current logical date** instead, its `period_id` is resolved from that current date in the ordinary way, and its `retro_period_month` records the award month it relates to. The closed period itself is then marked with `has_retro_adjustment` set to true — the single field the reconciliation freeze permits writing into a frozen period, and the reason this whole operation is exempt from the freeze gate.
- **Otherwise** — the award month has no period at all, or has one that is `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `REOPENED` — the entry is dated `award_effective_date`, its `period_id` is resolved from that date, and its `retro_period_month` is left null.

Caused in turn by the entry, the account's `cleared_balance_cents` and `available_balance_cents` both rise by `retro_award_cents`, and the period that actually received the entry has its `unreconciled_entry_count` raised by one — with the usual consequence that a receiving period sitting in `IN_REVIEW` reverts to `OPEN`.

`APPROVED` is terminal, as is `WITHDRAWN`. An application is withdrawn only by the system, when the instrument governing it is revoked, and only from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`.

### 3.9. Family Reporting, Care-Team Coordination, and the Periodic Checkpoint

Families want to know their relative's bills are being paid; the client has a right to decide how much of their financial life the family sees. The platform reconciles the two with a disclosure wall that the client themselves controls.

A `FiduciaryManager` enrols a recipient by naming the `client_id` whose finances are at issue, the `authorization_id` the subscription derives from, the recipient's `full_name` and `contact_email`, and their `relationship` — `CHILD`, `SPOUSE`, `SIBLING`, `OTHER_RELATIVE` or `TRUSTED_FRIEND`. Enrolment requires an instrument of tier `FULL_AGENCY` or `REP_PAYEE`; a `LIMITED_BILLPAY` instrument is refused at rung 8. A new recipient sits in `subscription_status` `PENDING` with an **empty** `permitted_categories` array, `consent_recorded_by_role` and `consent_recorded_at` null, and `report_count` at zero. A pending recipient sees nothing at all.

Consent unlocks the subscription. Either the `Client` themselves or a `FiduciaryManager` records it, supplying a `permitted_categories` array whose elements are drawn from `BILL_PAY`, `BUDGET`, `BENEFITS`, `FRAUD`, `CARE_TEAM` and `BALANCES`. Recording consent moves a `PENDING` recipient to `ACTIVE`, and an already-`ACTIVE` recipient re-enters `ACTIVE` — a re-entrant self-transition, so restrictions can be tightened or loosened any number of times. The supplied array **replaces** `permitted_categories` wholesale; it is a set, never a delta, and an empty array withdraws every category at once. The role that recorded it is written to `consent_recorded_by_role` as either `Client` or `FiduciaryManager`, and the current logical instant to `consent_recorded_at`. Two guards apply beyond the ordinary gates: when the caller's role is `Client`, that client's `capacity_determination` must read `RETAINS_CAPACITY` or the request is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_REQUIRED` at rung 31 — a client with `DIMINISHED_CAPACITY`, `LACKS_CAPACITY` or `NOT_DETERMINED` cannot set their own disclosure rules, though their fiduciary manager can — and, also when the caller's role is `Client`, `X-User-ID` must equal that client's own `id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED` at rung 32. The governing instrument must additionally be `ACTIVE` on the current logical date, checked at rung 7.

A recipient's subscription also moves without anyone asking. Suspending the client suspends every `ACTIVE` recipient of that client; reinstating the client restores every `SUSPENDED` one to `ACTIVE`. Revoking the governing instrument terminates the recipient outright from `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` alike. `TERMINATED` is terminal.

**Generating a report.** A `FamilyRecipient` or a `FiduciaryManager` generates a monthly report by naming the `recipient_id`, the `report_month` covered, the mandatory `expected_recipient_version` concurrency token, and optionally a `requested_categories` array. Omitting `requested_categories` means all six categories were asked for. When the caller's role is `FamilyRecipient`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `recipient_id` named, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED`. Report generation is permitted while a reconciliation period is frozen, because it only reads.

The projection walks the six categories in one fixed order — `BILL_PAY`, `BUDGET`, `BENEFITS`, `FRAUD`, `CARE_TEAM`, `BALANCES` — and never in request order. All three category arrays on the stored report are emitted in that fixed order, `requested_categories` included: a caller who asks for `BALANCES` and `BUDGET` gets `requested_categories` back reading `BUDGET` then `BALANCES`, and a caller who omits the field entirely gets all six categories in the fixed order. `included_categories` holds those categories, in that fixed order, that appear in the requested set **and** in the recipient's `permitted_categories`. `withheld_categories` holds those categories, in the same fixed order, that appear in the requested set but not in the included set. Together they partition the requested set exactly: nothing requested is missing from both, and nothing appears in both.

Two figures ride on the report and each is gated by its own category. `total_disbursed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the client's payments across all of that client's accounts whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `settled_on` falls inside `report_month` — but only when `BILL_PAY` made it into `included_categories`; otherwise it reads 0. `closing_available_balance_cents` is the sum of `available_balance_cents` across all of that client's accounts as they stand at generation time — but only when `BALANCES` made it into `included_categories`; otherwise it reads 0. The instant of generation is written to `generated_at`.

`is_provisional` tells the family how much to trust the figures. It reads false only when the client has at least one account **and** every one of those accounts has a reconciliation period for `report_month` whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `LOCKED`. In every other case — a client with no accounts at all, or any account whose month is still open, in review or reopened — it reads true. Generating the report raises the recipient's `report_count` by one.

**The audit export behind the wall.** The per-client audit trail is readable by a `FiduciaryManager`, a `ComplianceOfficer`, a `PlatformAdmin` and a `FamilyRecipient`, and may be narrowed with a `subject_entity` filter and an `outcome` filter. A `FamilyRecipient` reading it sees a projection rather than the whole trail: only entries whose `disclosure_category` is a member of that recipient's own `permitted_categories` are returned, and entries categorised `ADMINISTRATION` are **never** disclosed to a recipient regardless of what they have been granted, since `ADMINISTRATION` is not one of the six grantable categories. A recipient whose `client_id` differs from the client named in the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_DENIED` at rung 32. The export remains fully readable while a period is frozen.

**Care-team coordination.** A `CareCoordinator` or a `FiduciaryManager` opens a coordination request against a client by naming the `client_id`, a `subject` describing what the care team is being asked to do, a `tier` of `URGENT`, `ROUTINE` or `INFORMATIONAL`, and the `assigned_member_user_id` of the care-team member who should act. The tier sets the acknowledgement clock: `ack_due_at` is the current logical instant plus 2 hours for `URGENT`, plus 48 hours for `ROUTINE`, and plus 120 hours for `INFORMATIONAL`. A new request sits in `OPEN` with `reroute_count` at zero and `acknowledged_at` and `disposition_note` null.

Four dispositions drive it, selected by an `action`, with an optional `disposition_note` and an optional `reassign_to_user_id`.

- **`ACKNOWLEDGE`** moves an `OPEN` request to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and writes the current logical instant to `acknowledged_at`. A `CareCoordinator` may acknowledge only the requests assigned to them: a `CareCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the request's `assigned_member_user_id` is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, and that refusal happens at the role rung, above every business rung. The check is scoped to that role alone — a `FiduciaryManager` acknowledging on the care team's behalf is not compared against `assigned_member_user_id` at all.
- **`DECLINE`** keeps the request `OPEN` but hands it to somebody else. It requires `reassign_to_user_id` to be present and to differ from the current `assigned_member_user_id`; a missing or identical value is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. On success `assigned_member_user_id` becomes `reassign_to_user_id`, `reroute_count` rises by one, and `ack_due_at` is recomputed from scratch as the current logical instant plus the tier's own SLA hours. Rerouting is capped at 2: a decline arriving when `reroute_count` already reads 2 is refused with `ERR_REROUTE_LIMIT_REACHED` at rung 25, and the request must be escalated to case review instead.
- **`COMPLETE`** moves an `ACKNOWLEDGED` request to `COMPLETED`, recording the `disposition_note`.
- **`ESCALATE`** moves a request to `ESCALATED` from either `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, recording the `disposition_note`.

`COMPLETED` and `ESCALATED` are terminal. Coordination requests may be opened and dispositioned while a reconciliation period is frozen.

**The periodic checkpoint.** Time alone changes nothing in this platform; a `PlatformAdmin` must run the checkpoint sweep to convert elapsed logical time into state. The sweep does exactly three things and reports a count for each. It moves every `ACTIVE` authorization instrument whose `expiry_date` falls **strictly before** the current logical date into `EXPIRED`, counting them as `authorizations_expired` — an instrument is still in force on its expiry date itself, so only the day after does it sweep. It moves every fraud alert still in `NEW` whose `ack_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant into `ESCALATED`, counting them as `alerts_escalated`. And it moves every coordination request still in `OPEN` whose `ack_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant into `ESCALATED`, counting them as `coordination_requests_escalated`. Both deadline boundaries are inclusive: a deadline exactly equal to the clock escalates. Setting the logical clock does none of this — the clock endpoint moves time and performs no sweep of its own, so an operator who advances the clock by a week and then reads a fraud alert still sees it in `NEW` until the checkpoint runs.

**The read surface.** Every listing in the system supports the pagination and ordering rules of §2.3 and, where noted, exact-match filters that also serve as the reverse-reachability channel for the corresponding relationship. A client's authorizations and payees may be narrowed by `status`, their budget lines by `budget_month`, their coordination requests by `status`, their accounts not at all, and their audit entries by `subject_entity` and `outcome`. An account's reconciliation periods may be narrowed by `status`. Payments may be narrowed by `account_id`, `payee_id`, `authorization_id`, `budget_category_id`, `approval_status` and `settlement_status`. Statement lines may be narrowed by `period_id`, `matched_ledger_entry_id` and `match_status`. Ledger entries may be narrowed by `account_id`, `period_id`, `source_payment_id` and `entry_type`. Fraud alerts may be narrowed by `account_id`, `payment_id` and `status`. Benefit applications may be narrowed by `client_id`, `authorization_id` and `status`. Family recipients may be narrowed by `client_id`, `authorization_id` and `subscription_status`, and generated family reports by `recipient_id` and `report_month`. Every one of these reads is available while any period is frozen, and no read of any kind mutates a single row.
