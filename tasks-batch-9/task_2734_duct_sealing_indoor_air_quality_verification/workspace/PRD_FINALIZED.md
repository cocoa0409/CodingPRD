# Duct Sealing IAQ API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Aerosol duct sealing is one of the few retrofits a homeowner cannot inspect for themselves. A crew arrives, masks every register, pressurizes the duct system, injects sealant while a leakage curve falls on a laptop screen, and leaves behind a number the homeowner has to take on faith. Utilities that pay rebates against that number, and dealers who back it with a written guarantee, both need the number to be defensible months later — which means the measurement that produced it, the technician who was certified to take it, the combustion-safety checks that proved the house was safe to pressurize, and the independent inspector who re-measured it all have to be recorded as a single connected chain of evidence rather than as loose paperwork.

This document specifies the backend service that carries that chain. The service is mounted under `/duct-sealing` and covers the full dealer workflow: registering utility rebate programs and certified technicians, opening a retrofit project for a dwelling, cataloguing its duct systems, screening them for asbestos and other retrofit exclusions, reserving and reviewing pre- and post-seal leakage measurements, running combustion spillage tests on atmospherically vented appliances, monitoring the sealing run itself sample by sample against safety cutoffs and stall thresholds, issuing the post-test verification report that stamps the guarantee outcome, filing and paying the utility rebate claim behind an independent inspector spot check, registering the homeowner warranty with its follow-up re-test window, and diagnosing comfort complaints through room-by-room airflow balancing.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **every quantity that money or a guarantee depends on is computed once, at a nominated moment, and then frozen** — the rebate arithmetic is not re-derived when a claim is corrected and resubmitted, and a report's reduction percentages are copied from the tests that backed it rather than recomputed on read. Second, **safety and eligibility are allowed to invalidate work that has already been done**: a late asbestos confirmation, a failed combustion spillage test, or an over-pressure injection sample can reach backwards through seal jobs, verification reports and rebate claims and unwind them, releasing committed program budget as it goes. Implementers should read the compensation rules in §2 and the cascades in §3 as the load-bearing parts of the specification, not as edge cases.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. When `X-User-ID` is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), and this check precedes every other check in the system. When the role carried by `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the target endpoint permits, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Exactly four roles exist, spelled as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `FieldTechnician`, `QAVerifier`, and `UtilityInspector`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the dealer's back-office administrator. They set up utility programs and technicians, open projects, manage retrofit exclusions, register homeowner warranties, and file, resubmit and pay rebate claims. A `FieldTechnician` is the certified Aeroseal technician in the house: they run pre- and post-seal leakage measurements, combustion safety tests, the masking and injection run itself, and room-by-room airflow balancing. A `QAVerifier` is the quality-assurance reviewer who accepts or rejects measurements, records asbestos screening outcomes, drafts and issues the post-test verification report, and can place a project on safety hold. A `UtilityInspector` works for the utility rather than the dealer and independently re-measures a sealed system, recording the spot check that gates rebate payment.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's technician foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a technician's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `performed_by_user_id` on a leakage test and on a combustion test and into `measured_by_user_id` on an airflow reading, and it is the value compared against a seal job's `technician_id` when a report is signed. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `QAVerifier`, or `UtilityInspector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition nominates it — a verifier's identifier lands in `reviewed_by_user_id` on a reviewed leakage test and in `issued_by_user_id` on an issued report, and an inspector's identifier lands in `inspector_user_id` on a spot-checked claim — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

Three role pairings are forbidden by policy rather than by permission, and they are described in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints — every endpoint whose `response_type` is `array` — accept two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1, and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

On every array response the four pagination fields are returned inside `meta` alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`: `meta.limit`, `meta.offset`, `meta.total_count`, and `meta.has_more`. The `data` field stays a bare array of rows — it is never wrapped in a further envelope. `total_count` is the size of the filtered population, not the size of the returned page, so applying a filter shrinks `total_count`. `has_more` is true when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows actually returned is less than `total_count`, and false otherwise.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two collections have their own natural ordering and use it instead: the leakage curve of a sealing run is returned in ascending `sample_seq` order, and the airflow readings of a comfort complaint are returned in ascending `reading_seq` order.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded when the row is first written. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

Two record types are append-only measurement rows and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all: the individual leakage-curve samples appended during an injection run, and the individual room airflow readings taken during balancing. Every other record type in this system — utility programs, technicians, projects, duct systems, leakage tests, seal jobs, combustion tests, verification reports, rebate claims and comfort complaints — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

The server keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time before it has been set. An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock to an explicit ISO 8601 UTC instant supplied as `to_iso8601`; that call mutates no entity and eagerly recomputes no derived field. Every deadline, certification expiry, warranty re-test window and `*_at` stamp in this specification reads the logical clock: issuance instants, warranty registration instants, complaint resolution instants, certification-expiry comparisons and re-test window comparisons all resolve against it.

### 2.5. Path Hierarchy and Reverse Reachability

Resources are peers linked by explicit foreign keys; no resource is addressed more than one segment below another. Where a child is created under its parent's path — a duct system under a project, a combustion test under a project, a leakage sample under a seal job, an airflow reading under a comfort complaint — the parent identifier is taken from the path and never from the body, and supplying it in the body has no effect.

Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. When a path names a project and then a nested collection, the project's existence is confirmed first: a request against a project that does not exist fails with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before anything in the body is examined. The same precedence applies to `ERR_DUCT_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEAKAGE_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAL_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VERIFICATION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBATE_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMFORT_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_UTILITY_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. After the path entity resolves, foreign keys in the payload are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that resource.

Because relationships are expressed as foreign keys rather than as deep paths, every relationship is discoverable in the reverse direction through a filtered list. Projects can be filtered by `utility_program_id` and by `status`. A project's duct systems are listed under the project and can be narrowed by `eligibility_status` and by `seal_status`. A duct system's leakage tests are listed under the duct system and can be narrowed by `phase` and by `status`. A project's combustion tests are listed under the project and can be narrowed by `phase` and by `verdict`. Seal jobs are listed at the top level and can be filtered by `duct_system_id`, `pre_test_id`, `technician_id`, `retest_of_job_id` and `status` — the last of these being how a follow-up re-test is found from the job it re-tests. Verification reports can be filtered by `seal_job_id`, `post_test_id` and `status`. Rebate claims can be filtered by `verification_report_id`, `utility_program_id` and `status`. A project's comfort complaints are listed under the project and can be narrowed by `status`, and a complaint's airflow readings can be narrowed by `room_label`.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every derived decimal quantity in this system is rounded to **2 decimal places using round-half-up**. That applies to normalized leakage figures, per-area leakage figures, reduction percentages, CFM reduction totals, seal rates, ledger reductions, airflow deviation percentages, tier bonus percentages, spot-check normalizations and variance percentages alike.

Monetary quantities are integer cents throughout; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the rebate arithmetic. Where a monetary formula involves a rate or a percentage, the intermediate product is rounded half-up to a whole number of cents at each nominated rounding point rather than once at the end.

Two rounding orders are deliberately pinned and must not be optimised away. The per-area leakage figure is computed **from the already-rounded** normalized leakage value, not from the unrounded intermediate — round, then divide, then round again. And in the rebate arithmetic the per-claim ceiling is applied to the bonused subtotal **before** the administrative fee is taken, with the fee computed as a percentage of the already-capped subtotal. Reversing either order produces a different, wrong answer whenever the values sit near a rounding boundary or the ceiling binds.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Supersede-Capable Creates

Two create operations in this system are allowed to supersede work that already exists, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the parent resource's `version`.

Scheduling a sealing run requires a non-optional `expected_duct_system_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target duct system as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the duct system's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DUCT_SYSTEM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because a newly scheduled run legitimately supersedes an existing not-yet-started run on the same system: a create that observed a stale duct-system version would silently discard whatever a previous writer scheduled, or whatever screening decision has been recorded since the client read the row.

Drafting a verification report requires a non-optional `expected_seal_job_version` field, carrying the `version` of the target seal job as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the seal job's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEAL_JOB_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Here too the guard protects a supersede: a new draft report voids any existing draft for the same run, so a create carrying a stale seal-job version would void a draft its author never saw.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two ignore any version-like key in the body.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

Every mutation endpoint evaluates its checks in a fixed ascending precedence and returns the error of the **first** rule violated. Lower-numbered rungs are checked earlier and therefore win. The ladder, in order, is:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` present and non-empty on every endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the caller's role appears among the endpoint's permitted roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Segregation of duties** — the three forbidden self-pairings of §2.9, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
4. **Existence** — every identifier in the path and every foreign key in the payload resolves to a live row, else the matching not-found error (404). The entity named by the path segment is checked before payload foreign keys, and payload foreign keys are checked in the order their fields are declared for the resource.
5. **Request validity** — required fields present; enum values drawn from the declared sets; numbers of the declared scalar type; measured, design and pressure quantities strictly greater than zero where the field declares it; percentages inside 0 to 100; cents non-negative integers; ISO 8601 UTC instants well formed; `limit` inside 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected.
6. **State** — the target resource is in a from-state that permits the requested transition, else the resource's invalid-state error (409). This rung is checked **before any business rule**, so an action fired from the wrong state reports the state error even when a safety, referential, structural or fiscal precondition is also unmet. There is exactly one carve-out, described at rung 8(d).
7. **Concurrency** — when the endpoint declares an expected-version input, it equals the named parent's current `version`, else the matching stale-version error (412).
8. **Combustion and project safety** — the four gates described in §2.12, all HTTP 422.
9. **Cross-entity coherence** — the artefacts being wired together actually belong together, all HTTP 422: `ERR_PRE_TEST_SYSTEM_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TEST_PHASE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_RETEST_TARGET_INVALID`, `ERR_DUCT_SYSTEM_EXCLUDED`, `ERR_ASBESTOS_SCREENING_REQUIRED` and `ERR_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. This rung applies to scheduling a sealing run, drafting a verification report, and submitting a rebate claim.
10. **Structural and completeness constraints** — certification standing and expiry, masking completeness, presence of at least one airflow reading, sample sequencing and reading-sequence uniqueness, all HTTP 422: `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_MASKING_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_NO_AIRFLOW_READINGS`, `ERR_SAMPLE_SEQUENCE_INVALID` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_READING_SEQ`. This rung applies to scheduling a sealing run, starting injection, appending a leakage sample, entering the balancing phase, and recording an airflow reading.
11. **Interval and window rules** — technician schedule overlap, buffered test-window overlap, and the elapsed re-test window, all HTTP 409: `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and `ERR_RETEST_WINDOW_ELAPSED`. This rung applies to scheduling a sealing run and reserving a leakage test window.
12. **Fiscal and performance gates** — the guarantee threshold, the program minimum reduction, and the remaining program budget, all HTTP 422, and evaluated among themselves in that order: `ERR_GUARANTEE_NOT_MET` first (on report issuance), then `ERR_REBATE_REDUCTION_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`, then `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (both on claim creation and on claim resubmission). This rung applies to issuing a verification report, submitting a rebate claim, and resubmitting a rebate claim.

The practical consequence of rung 6 sitting above rungs 8 through 12 is worth stating twice: a run that is in the wrong status reports its state error and nothing else, even if the project is simultaneously on safety hold and the technician's certification has simultaneously lapsed.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three pairings are forbidden, and all three compare the caller's `X-User-ID` against a stored user identifier. Because the role itself is checked one rung earlier, presenting the same `X-User-ID` under a different `X-User-Role` does **not** escape this rule.

First, reviewing a leakage test requires that `X-User-ID` differ from that test's `performed_by_user_id`, so the technician who took a measurement cannot accept or reject it. Second, issuing a verification report requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the `technician_id` of the seal job being verified, so the technician assigned to a run cannot sign off on its own outcome. Third, recording the inspector spot check on a rebate claim requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the `issued_by_user_id` of the verification report that claim was built on, so the verifier who issued a report cannot also serve as the independent inspector. Any of the three violations yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

### 2.10. The Leakage Ledger and Its Compensation Identity

Each duct system maintains a small three-field ledger that must stay internally consistent at all times. `baseline_leakage_cfm25` is the normalized 25-Pa leakage of the most recently accepted pre-phase measurement on that system — where two accepted pre-phase measurements exist, the one with the greatest `created_at` governs — and it is null until such a measurement exists. `working_leakage_cfm25` is the system's live leakage: it equals the baseline while no sealing has been recorded, and otherwise tracks the current curve value of the run that last reached a sealed state or is currently injecting or paused. `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` is the difference obtained by subtracting `working_leakage_cfm25` from `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, and it defaults to 0.00.

Whenever a run is undone — aborted by the technician, aborted by the injection-pressure cutoff, aborted by a post-phase combustion failure, aborted by a retrofit exclusion, or reopened for a follow-up re-test — the ledger is **compensated** rather than left dangling: `seal_status` returns to `SEAL_PENDING`, `working_leakage_cfm25` is restored to `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` returns to 0.00, and `active_seal_job_id` is cleared to null. Compensation restores the identity exactly; there is no residual credit for a run that did not finish.

A duct system carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `eligibility_status` tracks retrofit eligibility and moves through `PENDING_SCREENING`, `ELIGIBLE` and the terminal `EXCLUDED`. `seal_status` tracks sealing progress and moves through `SEAL_PENDING`, `SEALING`, `SEALED` and `VERIFIED`. The two advance separately: an `EXCLUDED` row can still read `seal_status` of `SEAL_PENDING`, and a row that already reads `VERIFIED` can be driven to `EXCLUDED` by a late asbestos confirmation.

### 2.11. Program Budget Ledger

Each utility program keeps a three-part money ledger in integer cents. `committed_rebate_cents` is the sum of `payable_cents` over that program's claims whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. `paid_rebate_cents` is the sum of `payable_cents` over that program's claims whose `status` is `PAID`. `remaining_budget_cents` is `annual_budget_cents` less `committed_rebate_cents` less `paid_rebate_cents`.

Money moves between the two accumulators, never out of thin air. Submitting a claim adds its `payable_cents` to `committed_rebate_cents` and therefore lowers `remaining_budget_cents` by the same amount. Paying an approved claim subtracts `payable_cents` from `committed_rebate_cents` and adds it to `paid_rebate_cents`, leaving `remaining_budget_cents` unchanged. Releasing a commitment — because an inspector spot check failed, or because a retrofit exclusion withdrew the claim — subtracts `payable_cents` from `committed_rebate_cents` and raises `remaining_budget_cents` by the same amount, and sets the claim's `committed` flag to false. Re-committing on resubmission adds it back and sets `committed` to true again.

### 2.12. Combustion Safety and the Project Safety Hold

Blowing a duct system up to test pressure can back-draft an atmospherically vented appliance, so combustion safety gates the retrofit at two points and can freeze a whole project at any time. Four gates sit on rung 8 of the ladder, all returning HTTP 422.

(a) Starting an injection run requires pre-phase combustion coverage on the project: at least as many pre-phase combustion tests with a verdict of `PASS` or `MARGINAL` as the project's `gas_appliance_count`. Missing coverage is `ERR_COMBUSTION_SAFETY_REQUIRED`; a governing verdict of `FAIL` is `ERR_COMBUSTION_SPILLAGE_FAIL`. (b) Issuing a verification report requires post-phase combustion coverage on the same terms, again reported as `ERR_COMBUSTION_SAFETY_REQUIRED` when absent and `ERR_COMBUSTION_SPILLAGE_FAIL` when a governing verdict is `FAIL`. (c) No seal job may advance, no report may be issued and no rebate may be submitted while the project's `safety_hold` reads true; that rejection is `ERR_PROJECT_SAFETY_HOLD`. (d) A leakage sample whose injection pressure exceeds the cutoff is still accepted and stored, but it trips the safety cutoff and aborts the run; a later attempt to resume a run the cutoff aborted is `ERR_INJECTION_PRESSURE_LIMIT`.

These four gates guard exactly six operations: starting an injection run, resuming a paused run, completing a run, issuing a verification report, submitting a rebate claim, and resubmitting a returned rebate claim.

Gate (d) is the single carve-out to the state rung. A resume aimed at a run the safety cutoff already aborted **skips** the state check and falls through to gate (d), because that from-state was produced by the cutoff itself and the caller is entitled to hear why. Every other action aimed at such a run — pausing it, completing it, appending a further sample to it, registering a warranty on it — still reports `ERR_SEAL_JOB_INVALID_STATE` at the state rung.

A project's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SAFETY_HOLD`, and `safety_hold` is the boolean mirror of that posture: true exactly while `status` reads `SAFETY_HOLD`. `safety_hold_reason` carries free text stamped when the hold is raised and is null while the project is `ACTIVE`. While a hold is in force, new sealing runs cannot be scheduled and new rebate claims cannot be submitted anywhere under the project.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Setup, Technician Certification and Project Intake

Before any measurement can be taken, a `ProgramAdmin` records the commercial frame. Registering a utility duct-sealing rebate program captures its `name` and the parameters that will later decide what a claim is worth: `rebate_basis` selects which candidate amount becomes the rebate base and takes one of `PER_CFM`, `PERCENT_OF_INVOICE` or `LESSER_OF_BOTH`; `rate_cents_per_cfm_reduced` is the whole number of cents the program pays for each whole-house CFM25 of leakage removed and cannot be negative; `pct_of_invoice_bps` is the basis-point share of the retrofit invoice the alternative candidate uses and must fall between 0 and 10000 inclusive; `cap_cents` is the per-claim ceiling and cannot be negative; and `annual_budget_cents` is the total the program may commit and pay across all claims, likewise non-negative. `min_reduction_pct` is a percentage between 0.00 and 100.00 carried to two decimals, and it does double duty — it is both the minimum whole-house reduction the program will fund and the guarantee threshold the program imposes on the dealer. Two booleans govern process rather than money: `requires_inspector_verification` defaults to true and, while true, prevents a claim from being paid before an inspector spot check has passed, and `combustion_safety_required` also defaults to true and, while true, makes post-phase combustion coverage a precondition of report issuance. A newly registered program opens with `committed_rebate_cents` and `paid_rebate_cents` both at 0, `remaining_budget_cents` equal to `annual_budget_cents`, and `version` at 1.

Technicians are registered next. A technician record carries `full_name` and a `license_number` that must be unique across all technician rows; because the catalogue declares no dedicated identifier for a uniqueness clash, a registration whose `license_number` equals that of an existing technician row is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Certification standing lives in `certification_status`, one of `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`; when the field is omitted at registration it defaults to `PENDING`. `certification_expires_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which an `ACTIVE` certification stops being honoured, and it is null for a technician who has never been certified. A freshly registered technician has `open_seal_job_count` at 0 and `version` at 1. That counter tracks how many of the technician's sealing runs are still live — specifically, runs whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `MASKING`, `INJECTING` or `PAUSED` — and it is maintained as runs are scheduled, superseded, completed and aborted.

Certification is not immutable. An administrator can record or change a technician's Aeroseal standing at any time, replacing both `certification_status` and `certification_expires_at` wholesale and bumping the technician's `version` by 1. This matters because certification is checked at the moment injection starts, not at the moment a run was booked: a technician whose standing was `ACTIVE` when the run was scheduled but who has since been suspended will be stopped at the door with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and a technician who is still nominally `ACTIVE` but whose `certification_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock will be stopped with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. Note the boundary: an expiry instant exactly equal to the logical clock counts as expired.

Opening a retrofit project records the dwelling. `utility_program_id` names the rebate program behind the work and may be omitted or null for a self-funded retrofit with no program at all. `address_line` is the street address. `conditioned_floor_area_sqft` is the conditioned floor area used later to normalize leakage per 100 square feet and must be at least 100. `gas_appliance_count` is the number of atmospherically vented combustion appliances in the dwelling and may be zero. `invoice_amount_cents` is the retrofit invoice total the percent-of-invoice rebate candidate reads, and may be zero. A new project opens `ACTIVE`, with `safety_hold` false, `safety_hold_reason` null, `duct_system_count` and `eligible_duct_system_count` both 0, and `version` at 1. The two counters diverge deliberately: `duct_system_count` counts every duct system under the project regardless of eligibility, while `eligible_duct_system_count` counts only those whose `eligibility_status` reads `ELIGIBLE`.

### 3.2. Cataloguing Duct Systems, Asbestos Screening and Retrofit Exclusions

Each air handler in the dwelling is catalogued as its own duct system under the project, and the project identifier comes from the path rather than from the body. Creation captures `system_label` (for example `AHU-1`), `total_register_count` — the supply and return registers that must all be masked before injection, at least 1 — `design_cfm`, the system's design airflow, which must be strictly greater than zero, `duct_material`, one of `SHEET_METAL`, `FLEX`, `DUCT_BOARD` or `ASBESTOS_WRAP`, and `condition_grade`, the field condition assessment, one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `FAIL`. A new duct system opens with `asbestos_screening_result` at `PENDING`, `lab_reference` null, `eligibility_status` at `PENDING_SCREENING`, `seal_status` at `SEAL_PENDING`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` at 0.00, `baseline_leakage_cfm25` and `working_leakage_cfm25` both null, `active_seal_job_id` null and `version` at 1. Creation raises the project's `duct_system_count` by 1 and deliberately leaves `eligible_duct_system_count` untouched — that counter does not move until screening clears the system.

`active_seal_job_id` deserves a note: it is a computed pointer, not a declared relationship, naming whichever sealing run on this system is currently `SCHEDULED`, `MASKING`, `INJECTING` or `PAUSED`, and null when none is. It is repointed when a new run supersedes an older scheduled one and cleared whenever a run is completed, aborted or compensated away. Readers who want the authoritative list of runs on a system should use the filtered seal-job listing rather than this pointer.

Recording the asbestos lab screening result is the gate that makes a system sealable. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `QAVerifier` submits `asbestos_screening_result` — one of `PENDING`, `CLEARED`, `SUSPECT` or `CONFIRMED` — together with the `lab_reference` identifying the lab report, and both are stamped on the row. A result of `CLEARED` on a system whose `condition_grade` is not `FAIL` and whose `duct_material` is not `ASBESTOS_WRAP` advances `eligibility_status` from `PENDING_SCREENING` to `ELIGIBLE` and raises the project's `eligible_duct_system_count` by 1. A result of `CLEARED` on a system that nonetheless fails one of those two structural tests does the opposite: `eligibility_status` goes straight to `EXCLUDED`, with `exclusion_reason` recorded as `STRUCTURAL_DAMAGE` when the disqualifier was a `condition_grade` of `FAIL` and as `MATERIAL_NOT_SEALABLE` when the disqualifier was a `duct_material` of `ASBESTOS_WRAP`. A result of `SUSPECT` stamps the result and the reference but leaves `eligibility_status` at `PENDING_SCREENING`, so the system stays unsealable until a re-screen either clears or confirms it. Every screening bumps the duct system's `version` by 1. Screening — or excluding — a system whose `eligibility_status` already reads `EXCLUDED` is rejected with `ERR_DUCT_SYSTEM_INVALID_STATE`, because `EXCLUDED` is terminal on that dimension.

A result of `CONFIRMED` is the destructive case, and it may be recorded from either `PENDING_SCREENING` or `ELIGIBLE`. It runs a **four-hop cascade**. Hop one rewrites the duct system itself: `asbestos_screening_result` becomes `CONFIRMED`, `eligibility_status` becomes `EXCLUDED`, `exclusion_reason` becomes `ASBESTOS_CONFIRMED`, `seal_status` is compensated back to `SEAL_PENDING`, `working_leakage_cfm25` is restored to `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` returns to 0.00, `active_seal_job_id` is cleared, and the project's `eligible_duct_system_count` drops by 1 — but only when the previous `eligibility_status` was `ELIGIBLE`. Hop two, caused by hop one, sweeps every sealing run on that system whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `MASKING`, `INJECTING`, `PAUSED`, `SEALED`, `VERIFIED` or `RETEST_REQUIRED` into `ABORTED_EXCLUSION`, stamping `abort_reason` as `asbestos confirmed`. Hop three, caused by hop two, drives every verification report belonging to one of those aborted runs whose status is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` into `VOIDED`. Hop four, caused by hop three, drives every rebate claim referencing one of those voided reports whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` into `WITHDRAWN`, sets that claim's `committed` flag to false, and releases its `payable_cents` from the program's `committed_rebate_cents`, raising `remaining_budget_cents` by the same amount.

Exclusion for a non-asbestos reason is an administrator-only action available from `PENDING_SCREENING` or `ELIGIBLE`, and it takes an `exclusion_reason` drawn from `STRUCTURAL_DAMAGE`, `MATERIAL_NOT_SEALABLE` or `INACCESSIBLE` — the fourth member of the enumeration, `ASBESTOS_CONFIRMED`, is reserved for the screening path and cannot be supplied here. The identical four-hop cascade runs, with the supplied reason substituted for `ASBESTOS_CONFIRMED`, the project's `eligible_duct_system_count` dropping by 1 when the system had been `ELIGIBLE`, and the duct system's `version` bumping by 1. `exclusion_reason` remains null for as long as `eligibility_status` is anything other than `EXCLUDED`.

Once a system is `EXCLUDED` no new sealing run may be booked against it — that attempt is `ERR_DUCT_SYSTEM_EXCLUDED` — and a system still sitting at `PENDING_SCREENING` is equally unbookable, reported as `ERR_ASBESTOS_SCREENING_REQUIRED`.

### 3.3. Reserving, Pressurizing and Reviewing a Leakage Measurement

A leakage measurement is booked before it is taken. Either a `FieldTechnician` or a `QAVerifier` reserves a measurement window on a duct system by supplying `duct_system_id`, the `phase` — `PRE` for the pre-seal baseline or `POST` for the post-seal verification measurement — the `method`, which names the rig and is one of `PRESSURE_PAN`, `DUCT_BLASTER` or `AEROSEAL_INTERNAL`, and the window bounds `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, with the end strictly after the start. The reservation is created in status `DRAFT` with `test_pressure_pa`, `fan_ring_config`, `masked_register_count`, `measured_leakage_cfm`, `ambient_temp_f`, `normalized_leakage_cfm25` and `leakage_per_100_sqft` all null and `version` at 1.

Two measurements on the same duct system may not tread on each other's stabilization time. Each reservation projects a **closed buffered interval** running from half an hour before its `window_start_at` to half an hour after its `window_end_at`. If that buffered interval intersects the buffered interval of any other non-`REJECTED` measurement on the same duct system, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the buffered interval is closed at both ends, two measurements whose buffered endpoints merely touch **do** conflict — a reservation ending at ten o'clock and another beginning at eleven o'clock share the eleven-o'clock buffer boundary and are refused.

Pressurizing the system is the setup step and belongs to the `FieldTechnician` alone: it moves the measurement from `DRAFT` to `PRESSURIZED` and stamps three values. `test_pressure_pa` is the pressurization setpoint and must lie between 10 and 100 inclusive. `fan_ring_config` names the calibrated fan ring installed for the reading and is one of `RING_1`, `RING_2` or `RING_3`. `masked_register_count` is the number of registers masked for the measurement; a rig that is not sealed to every register cannot produce a legal reading, so this count must equal the duct system's `total_register_count`, and a pressurization that leaves registers unmasked is refused. A duct system whose `eligibility_status` has reached `EXCLUDED` cannot be pressurized at all. Pressurization bumps the measurement's `version` by 1.

With the setpoint held, the technician records the raw reading, moving the measurement from `PRESSURIZED` to `RECORDED`. The submission carries `measured_leakage_cfm`, the raw fan flow at the held setpoint, which cannot be negative, and `ambient_temp_f`, the ambient temperature logged with it. Recording stamps `performed_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, bumps `version` by 1, and immediately runs the normalization in a **pinned two-step order**. Step one converts the raw reading to the 25-pascal reference by multiplying `measured_leakage_cfm` by the ratio of 25 to `test_pressure_pa` raised to the power of the flow exponent 0.60, and rounds that product half-up to two decimal places to produce `normalized_leakage_cfm25`. Step two derives `leakage_per_100_sqft` by dividing the **already-rounded** `normalized_leakage_cfm25` from step one by the project's `conditioned_floor_area_sqft` expressed in hundreds of square feet, then rounding half-up to two decimals again. Computing step two from the unrounded step-one intermediate is wrong and differs from the pinned answer for readings sitting near a half-cent boundary. The project reached for the floor area is the one that owns the duct system named by `duct_system_id`. Attempting to pressurize from anything other than `DRAFT`, to record from anything other than `PRESSURIZED`, or to review from anything other than `RECORDED` is rejected with `ERR_LEAKAGE_TEST_INVALID_STATE`.

Review closes the measurement and belongs to the `QAVerifier`. The submission carries a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` together with an optional `review_note`, and in both cases `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped with the reviewer's `X-User-ID`, the note is stored, and the measurement's `version` bumps by 1. The `decision` is a submission input only: it is not kept as a field of its own on the measurement and does not appear anywhere in the measurement's representation, so the choice the reviewer made is readable only from the resulting `status`. `ACCEPT` moves the measurement from `RECORDED` to `ACCEPTED`; `REJECT` moves it from `RECORDED` to `REJECTED`. Both are terminal, and a rejected measurement can never back a sealing run or a verification report. Accepting a `PRE`-phase measurement is the moment the duct system's ledger is established: `baseline_leakage_cfm25` is set to the measurement's `normalized_leakage_cfm25`, `working_leakage_cfm25` is set to the same value, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` is set to 0.00, and the duct system's `version` bumps by 1 alongside the measurement's. Accepting a `POST`-phase measurement changes no duct-system field whatsoever — the post value reaches the duct system only through the sealing run's ledger — and neither does a rejection of either phase. Because the reviewer must not be the measurer, an attempt to review one's own measurement is stopped by the segregation rule of §2.9.

### 3.4. Combustion Spillage Testing and the Safety-Hold Cycle

A combustion test is recorded against a project, with the project identifier taken from the path, and only a `FieldTechnician` may run one. The submission names the appliance in `appliance_label` (for example `WH-1`), classifies it in `appliance_type` as `ATMOSPHERIC_WATER_HEATER`, `FURNACE`, `BOILER` or `RANGE`, states whether this is the `PRE` test that gates injection start or the `POST` test that gates report issuance in `phase`, and carries four measurements: `spillage_seconds`, the whole seconds of combustion spillage observed at the draft diverter; `co_ppm_air_free`, the air-free carbon monoxide in the flue in parts per million; `draft_pressure_pa`, the measured draft pressure; and `worst_case_depressurization_pa`, the worst-case room depressurization. Spillage seconds and carbon monoxide readings cannot be negative. The caller's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `performed_by_user_id` and the row opens at `version` 1.

The `verdict` is computed from those four measurements the moment the test is created, and three limits are selected by `appliance_type`. For an `ATMOSPHERIC_WATER_HEATER`, a `FURNACE` or a `BOILER` the carbon-monoxide ceiling is 100 ppm, the carbon-monoxide warning level is 50 ppm, and the worst-case depressurization limit is 5.0 pascals. A `RANGE` is held to looser numbers: an 800 ppm ceiling, a 400 ppm warning level, and a depressurization limit of 999.0 pascals that in practice never binds. Spillage is limited to 60 seconds for every appliance class, and the draft is considered weak below 1.0 pascal.

The verdict is decided in three ordered branches. The test is `FAIL` if any one of the following holds: `co_ppm_air_free` is strictly above the appliance's carbon-monoxide ceiling; or `spillage_seconds` is strictly above 60; or `worst_case_depressurization_pa` is strictly above the appliance's depressurization limit **and**, at the same time, `draft_pressure_pa` is strictly below 1.0. Otherwise the test is `MARGINAL` if `co_ppm_air_free` is at or above the appliance's warning level, or `spillage_seconds` is exactly 60. Otherwise the test is `PASS`. The three inputs interact deliberately: the depressurization limit only bites when the draft is simultaneously weak, so a house with 7 pascals of worst-case depressurization but a healthy 3-pascal draft is not failed on that account. The boundaries are exact — exactly 60 seconds of spillage is `MARGINAL` and not `FAIL`, and a carbon-monoxide reading exactly equal to the ceiling is `MARGINAL` and not `FAIL`, since it is not strictly above the ceiling but is at or above the warning level. Readings are used as supplied, with no rounding.

A `FAIL` verdict has teeth. It drives the project from `ACTIVE` to `SAFETY_HOLD`, sets `safety_hold` true and stamps `safety_hold_reason` as `combustion spillage failure on ` followed by the offending `appliance_label`. When the failing test is a `POST`-phase test, every sealing run under the project whose status is `MASKING`, `INJECTING` or `PAUSED` is driven to `ABORTED_SAFETY` with `abort_reason` recorded as `combustion spillage failure`, and each affected duct system's ledger is compensated exactly as described in §2.10 — `seal_status` back to `SEAL_PENDING`, `working_leakage_cfm25` back to `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` back to 0.00 and `active_seal_job_id` cleared. When the failing test is a `PRE`-phase test the hold alone is raised and any run already `INJECTING` is driven to `PAUSED`.

An administrator or a verifier may also raise the hold by hand, supplying the `safety_hold_reason` directly. That action moves the project from `ACTIVE` to `SAFETY_HOLD`, sets `safety_hold` true, drives every run under the project that is `INJECTING` to `PAUSED` while leaving runs in `SCHEDULED` or `MASKING` where they are, and bumps the project's `version` by 1.

There are two ways out of a hold, and both leave paused runs paused. The first is measurement-driven: recording a `POST`-phase combustion test whose verdict is `PASS` returns the project from `SAFETY_HOLD` to `ACTIVE`, clearing `safety_hold` and setting `safety_hold_reason` to null — but only when **every** `POST`-phase combustion test on the project created strictly after the hold was raised has a verdict of `PASS`. One lingering non-passing retest keeps the hold in force, and the cycle can repeat as many times as the house demands. The second is administrative: a `ProgramAdmin` clears the hold directly, supplying a `release_note` that is stored with the release, clearing `safety_hold` and `safety_hold_reason` and bumping the project's `version` by 1. This path is intended for holds that were raised by hand rather than by a failed combustion test. In neither case is any paused run resumed automatically — each one still requires an explicit resume by its technician.

### 3.5. Booking a Sealing Run and Masking the Registers

Scheduling an aerosol sealing run is a `FieldTechnician` action that wires four things together. `duct_system_id` names the system to be sealed. `pre_test_id` names the measurement that establishes its baseline and must be an `ACCEPTED`, `PRE`-phase measurement on that very same duct system: a measurement belonging to a different system is rejected with `ERR_PRE_TEST_SYSTEM_MISMATCH`, while a measurement of the wrong phase or one that is not in status `ACCEPTED` is rejected with `ERR_TEST_PHASE_MISMATCH`. `technician_id` names the certified technician who owns the run. `retest_of_job_id` is supplied only when this run is the follow-up re-test of an earlier run, and is null otherwise; when present it must point at a run on the **same** duct system whose status is `VERIFIED` or `RETEST_REQUIRED`, and anything else is `ERR_RETEST_TARGET_INVALID`. The booked slot is delimited by `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly after the start, and `target_leakage_cfm` records the leakage value at or below which the run may be closed out; it must be strictly greater than zero. Finally, `expected_duct_system_version` carries the duct system's `version` as the client last read it, per §2.7.

A technician cannot be in two houses at once. Booking is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` when the named technician already owns a non-terminal run whose booked slot overlaps the requested one, where each slot is treated as **half-open** — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. Two runs that merely share an endpoint instant, one ending exactly when the next begins, therefore do **not** conflict. Certification is enforced here as well as at injection: a technician whose `certification_status` is not `ACTIVE` yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and one whose `certification_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`. A follow-up re-test carries one further deadline: if the logical clock is strictly past the parent run's `retest_due_at`, the re-test can no longer be booked and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETEST_WINDOW_ELAPSED`.

A successful booking opens the run in status `SCHEDULED` with `masked_register_labels` empty, `masked_register_count` at 0, `sample_count` at 0, `current_leakage_cfm` null, `target_reached` false, `stall_flag` false, `pause_count` at 0, `warranty_status` at `NOT_REGISTERED`, `warranty_suspended` false, and `version` at 1. The named technician's `open_seal_job_count` rises by 1, the duct system's `active_seal_job_id` is repointed at the new run, and the duct system's `version` bumps by 1. Booking is also a **supersede**: any pre-existing run on the same duct system whose status is still `SCHEDULED` is moved to `ABORTED_SUPERSEDED` with `abort_reason` recorded as `superseded by a newer scheduled job`, and that superseded run's technician has their `open_seal_job_count` reduced by 1. This is precisely why the operation is guarded by the stale-version check of §2.7 — without it a client working from a stale read would silently discard a colleague's booking.

When the booking is a follow-up re-test whose `retest_of_job_id` names a run of this system sitting in `RETEST_REQUIRED`, the duct system is reopened for work: `seal_status` returns from `VERIFIED` to `SEAL_PENDING`, `working_leakage_cfm25` is restored to `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` returns to 0.00, and `active_seal_job_id` points at the new re-test run. Booking a re-test is the **only** way a `VERIFIED` system is reopened; the inspector-failure cascade described in §3.8 deliberately stops at the run level and leaves `seal_status` reading `VERIFIED`.

Masking is the next step and it is incremental. Each masking call carries `register_labels`, the labels being masked on this call, and the not-yet-present labels are appended to `masked_register_labels` in call order while labels already present are ignored rather than duplicated; `masked_register_count` is then recomputed as the length of that list. The first masking call moves the run from `SCHEDULED` to `MASKING` and requires the duct system to be `ELIGIBLE`; every subsequent call leaves the run in `MASKING`. Each call bumps the run's `version` by 1. A masking call aimed at a run that is in neither `SCHEDULED` nor `MASKING` is rejected with `ERR_SEAL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`.

Starting the injection run takes no payload at all, but it is the most heavily gated action in the system. It moves the run from `MASKING` to `INJECTING` and simultaneously moves the duct system's `seal_status` from `SEAL_PENDING` to `SEALING`, setting `active_seal_job_id` to this run and bumping both the run's and the duct system's `version` by 1. Every one of the following must hold: the run is in `MASKING`; the duct system's `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`; `masked_register_count` equals the duct system's `total_register_count`, with any shortfall — including the case of no registers masked at all — reported as `ERR_MASKING_INCOMPLETE`; the assigned technician's `certification_status` is `ACTIVE` and their `certification_expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock; the project's `safety_hold` is false; and, when the project's `gas_appliance_count` is greater than zero, the count of `PRE`-phase combustion tests on the project whose verdict is `PASS` or `MARGINAL` is at least `gas_appliance_count`. A project with no gas appliances needs no combustion coverage to start.

### 3.6. The Injection Run: the Real-Time Leakage Curve, Stalls and Cutoffs

While sealant is injecting, the technician streams the leakage curve into the system one point at a time. Each point carries `sample_seq`, the 1-based monotone position of this point in the run's curve; `elapsed_seconds`, whole seconds since injection began, which cannot be negative; `instantaneous_leakage_cfm`, the leakage reading at this point, which cannot be negative; and `injection_pressure_pa`, the injection pressure at this point, likewise non-negative. The owning run, carried on the sample as `seal_job_id`, is taken from the path rather than from the body. Sequencing is strict: `sample_seq` must equal the run's current `sample_count` plus 1, so no out-of-order point and no duplicate point can enter the series, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_SAMPLE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. Curve points are replayed strictly in ascending `sample_seq` order whenever the curve is re-read.

Storing a point updates the run and the duct system together. The run's `sample_count` rises by 1 and `current_leakage_cfm` becomes the `instantaneous_leakage_cfm` of the point with the greatest `sample_seq` — that is, of the point just stored. If that reading is at or below the run's `target_leakage_cfm`, `target_reached` latches true and never clears again, even if a later point reads higher. The duct system's `working_leakage_cfm25` becomes the run's `current_leakage_cfm`, and `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` becomes the run's `current_leakage_cfm` subtracted from the duct system's `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Both the run's `version` and the duct system's `version` bump by 1.

Two automatic reactions are evaluated on each stored point, and the **safety cutoff is evaluated before the stall check**. Every stored point carries `cutoff_triggered`, which is false unless that point itself tripped the cutoff. The cutoff fires when `injection_pressure_pa` is strictly above 1000.0 pascals: the point's `cutoff_triggered` is set true, the run is driven from `INJECTING` to `ABORTED_SAFETY` with `abort_reason` recorded as `injection pressure cutoff`, the duct system's ledger is compensated back to baseline as described in §2.10, and — a third hop — the project is driven from `ACTIVE` to `SAFETY_HOLD` with `safety_hold` true and `safety_hold_reason` recorded as `injection pressure cutoff on system ` followed by the duct system's `system_label`. The point itself is still accepted and stored. The boundary is exact: a pressure of exactly 1000.0 does **not** cut off. The cutoff can fire on a run that is `INJECTING` or `PAUSED`.

When no cutoff fires, stall detection runs over a fixed **three-sample backward window**. For any point whose `sample_seq` is at least 3, `seal_rate_cfm_per_min` is computed by taking the `instantaneous_leakage_cfm` of the point two sequence positions earlier, subtracting the `instantaneous_leakage_cfm` of the point just stored, and dividing that drop by the elapsed time between the two points expressed in minutes — that is, by the difference of their `elapsed_seconds` values divided by 60 — then rounding half-up to two decimals. For a point whose `sample_seq` is 1 or 2, `seal_rate_cfm_per_min` is null. When the computed rate is strictly below half a CFM per minute, the sealant has stopped finding holes: the run is driven from `INJECTING` to `PAUSED`, `stall_flag` is set true and `pause_count` rises by 1. That boundary is exact too — a rate of exactly 0.50 does **not** stall.

A technician may also pause a run by hand, supplying a `pause_reason` that is recorded on the run log; the manual pause moves the run from `INJECTING` to `PAUSED`, raises `pause_count` by 1, leaves `stall_flag` as it stands, and clears `abort_reason` to null. Resuming takes no payload, moves the run from `PAUSED` back to `INJECTING`, clears `stall_flag` to false, and bumps `version` by 1; it requires the duct system to be `ELIGIBLE` and the project's `safety_hold` to be false, so a paused run under a held project stays paused with `ERR_PROJECT_SAFETY_HOLD` until the hold clears. The `INJECTING` and `PAUSED` pair therefore forms a cycle that a run may traverse many times, with `pause_count` keeping the loop count observable, and the one thing resume cannot recover from is the safety cutoff — a resume aimed at a run in `ABORTED_SAFETY` reports `ERR_INJECTION_PRESSURE_LIMIT` rather than a state error, as described in §2.12.

Closing out a run requires that the target actually have been hit. Completion is legal only from `INJECTING` and only while `target_reached` reads true; it carries `sealant_lbs_used`, the pounds of sealant consumed, which stays null until the run is sealed. Completion moves the run from `INJECTING` to `SEALED`, moves the duct system's `seal_status` from `SEALING` to `SEALED`, sets the duct system's `working_leakage_cfm25` to the run's `current_leakage_cfm`, sets `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` to the run's `current_leakage_cfm` subtracted from `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, clears `active_seal_job_id` to null, lowers the technician's `open_seal_job_count` by 1, and bumps both `version` counters by 1.

Aborting is available by hand from `SCHEDULED`, `MASKING`, `INJECTING` or `PAUSED`, to either the run's `FieldTechnician` or a `ProgramAdmin`, and carries an `abort_reason` that is stamped on the run. The run moves to `ABORTED_MANUAL`, the duct system's `seal_status` returns from `SEALING` to `SEAL_PENDING`, `working_leakage_cfm25` is restored to `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, `sealed_leakage_reduction_cfm` returns to 0.00 so the ledger identity holds exactly, `active_seal_job_id` is cleared, the technician's `open_seal_job_count` falls by 1, and both `version` counters bump by 1. Five terminal statuses exist for a run and none of them accepts any further action: `RETEST_CLOSED`, `ABORTED_MANUAL`, `ABORTED_SAFETY`, `ABORTED_EXCLUSION` and `ABORTED_SUPERSEDED`. `abort_reason` is null on any run that has not been aborted.

### 3.7. Post-Test Verification, the Two Reduction Bases and the Guarantee

Once a run is `SEALED`, a `QAVerifier` drafts the verification report that will carry the dealer's guarantee. Drafting names `seal_job_id`, the run being verified, and `post_test_id`, the measurement that proves the result — which must be an `ACCEPTED`, `POST`-phase measurement taken on the **same** duct system as the run, with a foreign system reported as `ERR_PRE_TEST_SYSTEM_MISMATCH` and a wrong phase or a status other than `ACCEPTED` reported as `ERR_TEST_PHASE_MISMATCH`. It also carries `expected_seal_job_version` per §2.7. The new report opens in status `DRAFT` with `correction_round` at 0, `issued_by_user_id` and `issued_at` null, `signature_note` null and `version` at 1, and it bumps the run's `version` by 1. Drafting is a supersede: any earlier report on the same run that is still `DRAFT` is moved to `VOIDED`, keeping its computed values but losing forever the ability to be issued.

Drafting is also the moment every number on the report is computed and frozen. `pre_leakage_cfm25` is copied from the `normalized_leakage_cfm25` of the measurement named by the run's `pre_test_id`, and `post_leakage_cfm25` is copied from the `normalized_leakage_cfm25` of the measurement named by `post_test_id`. From those two values, **`reduction_pct_system_basis`** — the single-system reduction that the homeowner guarantee is written against — is obtained by subtracting `post_leakage_cfm25` from `pre_leakage_cfm25`, dividing that difference by `pre_leakage_cfm25`, multiplying by 100, and rounding half-up to two decimals. This basis looks only at the one system that was sealed and ignores every sibling system, sealed or not.

The second basis is the one the utility pays on, and it is deliberately harsher. **`reduction_pct_whole_house_basis`** and **`cfm_reduced_whole_house`** are aggregated over a candidate set consisting of every duct system belonging to the report's project whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE` **and** whose `baseline_leakage_cfm25` is not null, taken in ascending `created_at` order. Systems whose `eligibility_status` is `EXCLUDED` are omitted from both sides of the aggregation. The pre-side total is the sum of `baseline_leakage_cfm25` across the candidates. The post-side total is the sum, across the same candidates, of the `normalized_leakage_cfm25` of that system's most recent `ACCEPTED`, `POST`-phase measurement — the one with the greatest `created_at` — or, when a candidate has no accepted post measurement at all, of that system's own `baseline_leakage_cfm25`, which is to say an unsealed sibling is charged at its full original leakage. `cfm_reduced_whole_house` is the post-side total subtracted from the pre-side total, rounded half-up to two decimals, and it is the quantity the per-CFM rebate candidate is paid on. `reduction_pct_whole_house_basis` is that same difference divided by the pre-side total, multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to two decimals. Each of the three outputs is rounded independently. The practical consequence is that a dwelling with a second eligible-but-unsealed system yields a strictly smaller whole-house percentage than its single-system percentage on identical measurements, and a dealer who seals only one of two air handlers will meet the guarantee while failing the rebate minimum.

`guarantee_threshold_pct` is copied onto the report at drafting: it is the `min_reduction_pct` of the project's utility program when the project names one, and 80.00 percent when `utility_program_id` is null, so a self-funded retrofit still carries a real threshold. `guarantee_met` is true exactly when `reduction_pct_system_basis` is greater than or equal to `guarantee_threshold_pct` — the comparison is inclusive, so a reduction exactly equal to the threshold meets the guarantee.

Issuing the report is the act that verifies the run, and it carries only a `signature_note`. Issuance is legal from `DRAFT` and from `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`; attempted from `VOIDED`, from an already-`ISSUED` report, or from any other posture, it is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`. From `DRAFT`, the underlying run must be in status `SEALED`. The verifier must not be the run's own technician, so `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `technician_id` or the segregation rule of §2.9 stops the request. `guarantee_met` must read true, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_NOT_MET`. Post-phase combustion coverage is required whenever the project's program sets `combustion_safety_required` to true, and equally whenever the project has no program at all: every `POST`-phase combustion test on the project must have a verdict of `PASS` or `MARGINAL`, and there must be at least as many such tests as the project's `gas_appliance_count` — a shortfall is `ERR_COMBUSTION_SAFETY_REQUIRED` and a governing `FAIL` is `ERR_COMBUSTION_SPILLAGE_FAIL`. Finally the project's `safety_hold` must be false, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_SAFETY_HOLD`.

A successful issuance from `DRAFT` moves the report to `ISSUED`, stamps `issued_by_user_id` with the verifier's `X-User-ID`, stamps `issued_at` with the logical clock, stores the `signature_note`, moves the run from `SEALED` to `VERIFIED`, moves the duct system's `seal_status` from `SEALED` to `VERIFIED`, and bumps the report's, the run's and the duct system's `version` by 1 each. When the run being verified is itself a follow-up re-test — that is, when its `retest_of_job_id` is not null — the parent run it re-tests is moved from `RETEST_REQUIRED` to `RETEST_CLOSED` and that parent's `warranty_suspended` is cleared to false, closing the warranty loop through the self-referential link.

Re-issuance closes the correction cycle. A report that an inspector spot check pushed to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` can be re-issued once the underlying problem is fixed: the same segregation, guarantee and safety-hold preconditions apply, `issued_by_user_id`, `issued_at` and `signature_note` are re-stamped, the report returns to `ISSUED`, and the run moves from `RETEST_REQUIRED` back to `VERIFIED` with `warranty_suspended` cleared to false. `correction_round` counts how many times the report has been pushed from `ISSUED` into `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` and is never decremented, so a report on its second correction reads 2 there regardless of how many times it has been re-issued. A report may also be driven to the terminal `VOIDED` status from `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` by a retrofit exclusion, as described in §3.2, and a voided report can never be issued or re-issued.

### 3.8. Rebate Documentation, the Frozen Payable Amount and the Inspector Spot Check

A `ProgramAdmin` files the utility rebate claim on top of an issued report. The submission names `verification_report_id`, which must reference a report whose status is `ISSUED` — a claim built on a report in any other status is refused with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE` — and `utility_program_id`, which must be the very program the report's project names in its own `utility_program_id`; a different program, or a project that has no program at all, is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. `documentation_bundle_ref` references the uploaded rebate documentation bundle. Submission is blocked while the project's `safety_hold` reads true, reported as `ERR_PROJECT_SAFETY_HOLD`.

Two performance and money gates guard the submission, and they are evaluated in this order. First, the report's `reduction_pct_whole_house_basis` must reach the program's `min_reduction_pct`; strictly below it, the claim is refused with `ERR_REBATE_REDUCTION_BELOW_PROGRAM_MINIMUM`. Only then is the money computed, and the resulting `payable_cents` must fit inside the program's `remaining_budget_cents`; if it exceeds what is left, the claim is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

The rebate arithmetic runs once, at submission, and is **frozen thereafter** — nothing in the correction-and-resubmission cycle ever recomputes it. It proceeds in six pinned steps.

Step one computes both base candidates, and both are always computed and both are always persisted on the claim regardless of which one is used. `per_cfm_candidate_cents` is the program's `rate_cents_per_cfm_reduced` multiplied by the report's `cfm_reduced_whole_house`, rounded half-up to a whole number of cents. `percent_of_invoice_candidate_cents` is the project's `invoice_amount_cents` multiplied by the program's `pct_of_invoice_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half-up to a whole number of cents.

Step two selects the base according to the program's `rebate_basis` and records the choice in `basis_applied`. Under `PER_CFM` the base is `per_cfm_candidate_cents` and `basis_applied` reads `PER_CFM`. Under `PERCENT_OF_INVOICE` the base is `percent_of_invoice_candidate_cents` and `basis_applied` reads `PERCENT_OF_INVOICE`. Under `LESSER_OF_BOTH` the base is whichever of the two candidates is smaller, and `basis_applied` reads `PER_CFM` when the per-CFM candidate is less than or equal to the invoice candidate and `PERCENT_OF_INVOICE` only when the invoice candidate is strictly smaller — **ties resolve to `PER_CFM`**. The two bases behave very differently on identical work: the per-CFM route scales with whole-house CFM25 removed and is insensitive to the invoice, while the percent-of-invoice route scales with the retrofit price and is insensitive to how much leakage was actually removed.

Step three reads a performance tier off the **whole-house** percentage, never off the system-basis percentage. `tier_bonus_pct` is 0.20 when `reduction_pct_whole_house_basis` is at least 90.00, otherwise 0.10 when it is at least 75.00, otherwise 0.05 when it is at least 60.00, and otherwise 0.00. Each band boundary is inclusive at its lower edge, so exactly 75.00 percent earns the 0.10 bonus rather than the 0.05 one.

Steps four through six are order-sensitive and the order is pinned. The bonused subtotal is the base multiplied by one plus `tier_bonus_pct`, rounded half-up to a whole number of cents. `capped_subtotal_cents` is then the smaller of that bonused subtotal and the program's `cap_cents`. `admin_fee_cents` is five percent of `capped_subtotal_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole number of cents — computed **after** the cap, never before. `payable_cents` is `admin_fee_cents` subtracted from `capped_subtotal_cents`. Taking the administrative fee first and capping afterwards yields a different, larger number whenever the cap binds, and is wrong.

A newly filed claim opens in status `SUBMITTED` with `committed` true, `inspector_outcome` at `PENDING`, `resubmission_count` at 0 and `version` at 1. Its `payable_cents` is added to the program's `committed_rebate_cents`, lowering `remaining_budget_cents` by the same amount, and the program's `version` bumps by 1.

The `UtilityInspector` then re-measures the sealed system independently. The spot check carries `spot_check_leakage_cfm`, the inspector's raw re-measured leakage, and `spot_check_pressure_pa`, the pressure at which they measured. `spot_check_cfm25` normalizes that raw reading to the 25-pascal reference exactly as a leakage measurement is normalized — the raw value multiplied by the ratio of 25 to `spot_check_pressure_pa` raised to the power of the flow exponent 0.60, rounded half-up to two decimals. `inspector_variance_pct` is then the absolute difference between `spot_check_cfm25` and the report's `post_leakage_cfm25`, divided by the report's `post_leakage_cfm25`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to two decimals. `inspector_outcome` becomes `FAIL` when `inspector_variance_pct` is strictly above 10.00 percent and `PASS` otherwise; a variance of exactly 10.00 is a pass. The spot check is legal only from `SUBMITTED` — anywhere else it is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` — requires the underlying report to be `ISSUED`, and requires the inspector to be someone other than the verifier who issued that report, per §2.9. `inspector_user_id` is stamped with the inspector's `X-User-ID` and the claim's `version` bumps by 1.

A passing spot check moves the claim from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED` and leaves the commitment exactly as it was: `committed` stays true and the program's `committed_rebate_cents` is unchanged. A failing spot check runs a three-hop cascade. Hop one stores the spot-check fields with `inspector_outcome` at `FAIL`, moves the claim from `SUBMITTED` to `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, sets `committed` false and releases `payable_cents` from the program's `committed_rebate_cents` so `remaining_budget_cents` rises by that amount. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves the linked verification report from `ISSUED` to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` and raises its `correction_round` by 1. Hop three, caused by hop two, moves that report's sealing run from `VERIFIED` to `RETEST_REQUIRED` and sets its `warranty_suspended` true, which is what makes the run's `warranty_status` read `SUSPENDED` once a warranty has been registered. On a failure the program's, the report's and the run's `version` each bump by 1 alongside the claim's. Note what the cascade deliberately does **not** do: the duct system's `seal_status` stays at `VERIFIED`, and only the booking of a follow-up re-test reopens it.

Resubmission closes the return cycle and is an administrator action. It is legal only from `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, and only once the underlying report has been re-issued — the report's status must read `ISSUED` again. Because the program may have committed money elsewhere in the meantime, the frozen `payable_cents` must still fit inside `remaining_budget_cents` or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, and the whole-house reduction gate applies here too. Resubmission recomputes **nothing**: `per_cfm_candidate_cents`, `percent_of_invoice_candidate_cents`, `basis_applied`, `tier_bonus_pct`, `capped_subtotal_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `payable_cents` all stay exactly as they were frozen at first submission. What does change: the claim returns to `SUBMITTED`, `resubmission_count` rises by 1, `documentation_bundle_ref` is replaced with the corrected reference, `inspector_outcome` resets to `PENDING`, `spot_check_leakage_cfm`, `spot_check_pressure_pa`, `spot_check_cfm25`, `inspector_variance_pct` and `inspector_user_id` are all cleared, `committed` returns to true, `payable_cents` is re-committed to the program's `committed_rebate_cents`, and the claim's and the program's `version` each bump by 1.

Payment is the last step and is legal only from `APPROVED`; attempted anywhere else it is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. When the program's `requires_inspector_verification` reads true, `inspector_outcome` must read `PASS` before payment is permitted. Payment carries `payment_reference`, which is stamped on the claim and is null on any claim that has not been paid, moves the claim to the terminal `PAID` status, sets `committed` false, subtracts `payable_cents` from the program's `committed_rebate_cents` and adds it to `paid_rebate_cents` — so `remaining_budget_cents` does not move — and bumps the claim's and the program's `version` by 1 each. The other terminal status is `WITHDRAWN`, reached only through the retrofit-exclusion cascade of §3.2, which releases the commitment on the way if it was still held.

### 3.9. Warranty Registration, the Follow-Up Re-Test and Comfort Complaint Balancing

Registering the homeowner warranty is a `ProgramAdmin` action available only on a run whose status is `VERIFIED`; anywhere else it is `ERR_SEAL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. It carries `homeowner_name` and `homeowner_email`, both null before registration, stamps `warranty_registered_at` with the logical clock, sets `retest_due_at` to one year — 365 whole days — after `warranty_registered_at`, and bumps the run's `version` by 1. `retest_due_at` is null for as long as no warranty is registered.

`warranty_status` is not stored as a decision; it is derived on every read in a strict precedence order, and the first matching rule wins. It reads `NOT_REGISTERED` whenever `warranty_registered_at` is null. Otherwise it reads `SUSPENDED` whenever `warranty_suspended` is true — the flag an inspector failure sets and a report re-issuance or a closed re-test clears. Otherwise it reads `LAPSED` when the logical clock is strictly past `retest_due_at` **and** no follow-up run pointing at this run through `retest_of_job_id` has reached a status of `VERIFIED` or `RETEST_CLOSED`. Otherwise it reads `REGISTERED`. Note the interaction: a warranty whose re-test window has passed but whose follow-up re-test was completed and verified stays `REGISTERED`, and a warranty that is suspended reads `SUSPENDED` even after its re-test window has expired, because suspension is checked first.

Comfort complaints run on a separate track from sealing, and any `ProgramAdmin` or `FieldTechnician` may log one. A complaint names the `project_id` of the dwelling it belongs to, the `room_label` the homeowner complained about, a `complaint_type` drawn from `TOO_HOT`, `TOO_COLD`, `NOISE`, `ODOR` and `DUST`, and `reported_by`, the name of the person who reported it. It opens in status `OPEN` with `diagnosis_code`, `tolerance_pct`, `damper_adjustment_note` and `resolved_at` all null, `balanced_room_count` and `rebalance_count` both 0, and `version` at 1.

A `FieldTechnician` diagnoses the complaint, moving it from `OPEN` to `DIAGNOSED` and recording two things: `diagnosis_code`, one of `UNDERSIZED_BRANCH`, `DAMPER_MISADJUSTED`, `BLOCKED_REGISTER`, `EXCESS_BYPASS` or `RESIDUAL_LEAKAGE`, and `tolerance_pct`, the absolute room deviation tolerance that will govern every subsequent reading, which must lie between 0.00 and 50.00 inclusive and is carried to two decimals. The house default the crews work to is 10.00 percent, and a diagnosis that does not name a tighter or looser figure is governed by that number. Diagnosing a complaint that is not `OPEN` is `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`, and the complaint's `version` bumps by 1.

Readings are taken per room. Each reading names the `room_label` measured, a `reading_seq` giving its 1-based monotone position within that room's series under this complaint, `measured_cfm`, the measured supply airflow at the register, which cannot be negative, `design_cfm`, the design airflow for that room, which must be strictly greater than zero, and `damper_position_pct`, the balancing damper position at the moment of measurement, between 0 and 100. The owning complaint, carried on the reading as `comfort_complaint_id`, is taken from the path rather than from the body. `reading_seq` must be unique for a given combination of `comfort_complaint_id` and `room_label`; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_READING_SEQ`. Each reading stamps `measured_by_user_id` with the technician's `X-User-ID` and bumps the complaint's `version` by 1. `deviation_pct` is computed by subtracting `design_cfm` from `measured_cfm`, dividing by `design_cfm`, multiplying by 100 and rounding half-up to two decimals — so a room delivering less than its design flow carries a negative deviation. `within_tolerance` is true exactly when the absolute value of `deviation_pct` is less than or equal to the complaint's `tolerance_pct`; the comparison is inclusive, so a deviation whose magnitude equals the tolerance is within tolerance.

Entering the balancing phase requires evidence: the transition from `DIAGNOSED` to `BALANCING` is refused with `ERR_NO_AIRFLOW_READINGS` unless at least one airflow reading already exists under the complaint. It carries a `damper_adjustment_note` describing what was adjusted, which is null before that point, and it bumps the complaint's `version` by 1. Attempting it from any status other than `DIAGNOSED` is `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`.

While a complaint is `BALANCING`, each new reading can move the complaint by itself. For every distinct `room_label` under the complaint, the **governing** reading is the one with the greatest `reading_seq` for that room — and because `reading_seq` is unique per room, no tie is possible. `balanced_room_count` is recomputed after every reading as the number of distinct rooms whose governing reading has `within_tolerance` true. If the reading just stored has `within_tolerance` false, the complaint is thrown back from `BALANCING` to `DIAGNOSED` and `rebalance_count` rises by 1, so the technician must re-diagnose and re-enter balancing; this is why the `DIAGNOSED` and `BALANCING` pair forms a cycle a stubborn room can drive several times. If instead every distinct room under the complaint now has a governing reading within tolerance, and at least one room exists, the complaint auto-resolves from `BALANCING` to the terminal `RESOLVED` status: `balanced_room_count` is set to the number of distinct rooms and `resolved_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Recording a reading against a complaint that is already `RESOLVED` is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`.
