# Gift Card Fraud & Balance Protection API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Closed-loop gift cards are the retail industry's most liquid instrument and, for exactly that reason, its most attacked one. Criminal crews photograph the barcodes of racked cards in a store aisle, reseal the tamper-evident packaging, and then poll the balance endpoint in a loop until an unsuspecting shopper loads the card at the register — at which point the value is drained within minutes through a handful of merchants or resold on a secondary marketplace. Digital issuance is attacked differently: stolen payment instruments, freshly minted email addresses and rented devices are used to mint high-value e-codes that never touch a physical store. The retailer discovers the loss last, usually when a victim walks back into the store with a receipt and an empty card.

This document specifies the backend service that the gift card risk team uses to run that entire problem end to end. It carries the activation controls at the point of sale (tamper-evident packaging attestation and rack monitoring), the draining-detection engine (balance-inquiry velocity and redemption-pattern scoring), bulk-purchase limits with government identity capture, digital-issuance fraud screening on device, email and payment signals, victim-claim intake with an evidence chase and a reissue policy, the retail-floor procedures that suspend a compromised sales floor and escalate to law enforcement, third-party reseller validation for the secondary marketplace, and the balance-liability accounting that books unauthorized-redemption reserves against each closed period.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is never written, only replayed**: every balance figure on a gift card is a projection over an append-only ledger, and a mistake is corrected by appending a compensating event, never by editing history. Second, **risk and funding are independent axes**: a card's funding lifecycle and its fraud posture evolve on separate dimensions of the same record, so a fully funded card can be frozen and a depleted card can still be under investigation. Implementers who collapse either commitment will produce a service that looks correct on the happy path and silently loses money on the compensating one.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every path in this specification is absolute and mounted at the service root — there is no additional URL prefix.

### 2.1. Identity, Roles and Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. When the header is present but the role it resolves to is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Exactly seven roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `StoreManager`, `PosAssociate`, `FraudAnalyst`, `RiskManager`, `LiabilityAccountant`, `ResellerPartner`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

`StoreManager` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RetailStore`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `RetailStore` compare the resource's store-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the effective store of the target must equal `X-User-ID`, where the effective store is the store's own `id` for a store target, `store_id` for a rack, gift card, issuance request or bulk purchase order target. When the target's effective store resolves to nothing — as it does for every `DIGITAL` gift card and every `DIGITAL` issuance request, both of which carry no store — a `StoreManager` is denied with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This scope check is evaluated after the plain role check and before request-body well-formedness.

The remaining six roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PosAssociate`, `FraudAnalyst`, `RiskManager`, `LiabilityAccountant`, `ResellerPartner` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a workflow step attributes authorship — as `created_by_user_id` on a ledger event, `triaged_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a draining alert, `intake_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a victim claim, `decided_by_user_id` on an issuance request or bulk purchase order, `referred_by_user_id` on a law-enforcement referral, `validated_by_user_id` and `reseller_partner_id` on a reseller listing — but it never compares that value against any entity's `id`.

Two of those recorded identifiers do more than document authorship: they enforce segregation of duties. The `X-User-ID` that resolves a draining alert must differ from the `triaged_by_user_id` already recorded on it, and the `X-User-ID` that decides a victim claim must differ from that claim's `intake_user_id`. Violating either produces `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The role split reinforces this at the authorization layer: a `FraudAnalyst` triages alerts and chases claim evidence but can never resolve an alert or decide a claim, and a `RiskManager` — who can do both of those — is still blocked from acting twice on the same artefact.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` block, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current window. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four endpoints declare a different primary order and are called out where they are described: the per-card balance-inquiry log and the global ledger-event list are ordered by `occurred_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break, the per-card hold list is ordered by `hold_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break, and the accounting-period list is ordered by `period_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break. The `id` of a resource is never an ordering key anywhere in this system.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource exposes a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally expose `updated_at`, formatted identically, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation — including mutations that arrive as a cascaded side effect of an operation on a different resource. Whenever this document says a version is incremented, `updated_at` is refreshed from the clock in the same step.

Three resources are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable audit records. Ledger events, balance inquiries and law-enforcement referrals carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` and no `version` at all, and no update, delete or state-transition endpoint exists for any of them. A ledger event is corrected only by appending another ledger event; a referral packet, once filed, is frozen forever.

All time in this system comes from a deterministic logical clock, never from wall-clock time. Every `created_at`, `updated_at`, `occurred_at`, `activated_at`, `closed_at`, `screened_at`, `last_audit_at`, `referred_at` and `restated_at` value is read from that clock. At process start the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, so a test that never moves it still observes a fixed, reproducible present. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc` and the number of holds the move expired as `expired_hold_count`. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and performs no expiry sweep.

### 2.5. The Risk Policy Register

A single, always-present configuration row supplies every threshold, limit and rate this system reasons about. It is seeded at process start with all of the defaults below, so reading it succeeds against an empty database, and it is updated by partial write: only the keys supplied on the update are overwritten, and omitted keys retain their current values. Updating the register increments its `version` by 1 and never retroactively recomputes anything already persisted — a screening score, an activation fee or a booked reserve stays on the constants that produced it.

The register's eighteen settings, with their defaults and permitted ranges, are:

- `inquiry_velocity_window_minutes` — the width in minutes of the trailing window over which balance inquiries are counted. Default 60. Must be at least 1 and at most 10080.
- `inquiry_velocity_threshold` — the inquiry count inside that trailing window at or above which a draining alert fires. Default 5. Must be at least 2 and at most 100.
- `redemption_window_minutes` — the width in minutes of the trailing window for the recent-redemption count consumed by the redemption-weighted drain score. Default 1440, which is one full day. Must be at least 1 and at most 43200.
- `drain_watch_score_threshold` — the drain risk score at or above which a card's fraud posture escalates to `WATCH`. Default 40. Must be at least 1 and at most 100.
- `drain_restrict_score_threshold` — the drain risk score at or above which the posture escalates to `RESTRICTED`. Default 70. Must be at least 1 and at most 100, and must be strictly greater than `drain_watch_score_threshold`.
- `digital_hold_score_threshold` — the digital issuance screening score at or above which the request is held for human review. Default 45. Must be at least 1 and at most 100.
- `digital_decline_score_threshold` — the screening score at or above which the request is automatically declined. Default 75. Must be at least 1 and at most 100, and must be strictly greater than `digital_hold_score_threshold`.
- `bulk_identity_threshold_cents` — the bulk order total at or above which government identity capture becomes mandatory. Default 50000, which is five hundred dollars. Must be at least 0.
- `bulk_quantity_limit` — the largest card quantity accepted on one bulk order. Default 25. Must be at least 1.
- `rack_shrink_threshold` — the rack shrink count at or above which a rack is classified as tampered. Default 3. Must be at least 1.
- `rack_seal_break_threshold` — the observed seal-break count at or above which a rack is classified as tampered. Default 3. Must be at least 1.
- `floor_heightened_alert_threshold` — the number of live draining alerts on a store's cards at or above which the store's floor posture becomes heightened. Default 2. Must be at least 1.
- `claim_window_days` — the number of days after purchase during which a victim claim may be filed. Default 90. Must be at least 1.
- `claim_evidence_round_limit` — the maximum number of evidence-request rounds permitted on one claim. Default 3. Must be at least 1.
- `reissue_cap_cents` — a hard ceiling on the gross recovery computed for a claim; an approval whose gross exceeds this cap is refused outright. Default 100000, one thousand dollars. Must be at least 0.
- `recovery_factor_bps` — the basis points of each unauthorized redemption line that the reissue policy restores to the victim. Default 8500, which is eighty-five percent. Must be at least 0 and at most 10000.
- `provisional_reserve_rate_bps` — the basis points applied to gross alert exposure when an accounting period is closed. Default 3500, which is thirty-five percent. Must be at least 0 and at most 10000.
- `final_reserve_rate_bps` — the basis points applied to confirmed-only alert exposure when an accounting period is restated. Default 1000, which is ten percent. Must be at least 0 and at most 10000.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points, Scores and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency fields, and no amount is ever stored with a fractional cent. Every rate is expressed in basis points, meaning hundredths of a percent: applying a rate to an amount means multiplying the amount by the basis-point figure and dividing by 10000.

Wherever a division or a rate application does not land on a whole cent, the result is rounded **half up** — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half always rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude, never to the nearest even number. A fee of 1498.5 cents becomes 1499 cents, never 1498.

Two rules about *where* rounding happens are load-bearing, and getting them wrong produces off-by-a-few-cents differences that this specification treats as outright wrong answers. First, **the number of roundings is fixed per calculation**: the activation fee on a bulk order is rounded exactly once, over the whole order, and a period reserve is rounded exactly once, over the aggregated exposure. Second, **one calculation deliberately rounds per line and then sums**: the victim-claim gross recovery applies the recovery factor to each unauthorized redemption line, rounds that line to whole cents, and only then adds the lines together. Both conventions are stated again at the point of use in §3, because a reader who applies the wrong one arrives at a plausible but incorrect number.

The one non-integer field in the system is a card's `drawdown_ratio`, which is rounded half up to exactly two decimal places and clamped into the closed range from 0.00 through 1.00. Every risk score — the two card drain scores and the digital issuance screening score — is an integer produced by rounding half up exactly once, at the very end of the composition, and then clamped into the closed range 0 through 100. Intermediate products inside a score are never rounded: a `drawdown_ratio` of 0.60 contributes exactly 18.0 to the inquiry-weighted drain score and exactly 27.0 to the redemption-weighted one.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Additive Writes

Two write operations in this system are *legitimately additive*, meaning a second, independently-formed request would also succeed on its own merits. Neither can therefore be protected by a state precondition, so both are protected by an explicit stale-version guard on the parent record.

Placing a redemption hold on a gift card requires the request to carry `expected_gift_card_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the gift card the client last observed. If that value does not equal the card's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A gift card's version is bumped by every hold, every redemption, every reversal, every posture change and every cascade that touches it, so a client that has not re-read the card since any of those events will be turned away and must re-read and resubmit. Without this guard a client could pile a second hold onto a card whose balance it has not observed — and because overlapping holds are legal under the aggregate capacity rule, nothing else in the system would object.

Restating an accounting period requires the request to carry `expected_liability_period_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the period the client last observed. If it does not equal the period's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Restatement is deliberately repeatable — a period may be restated any number of times — so no state precondition would catch a client working from a superseded view of the period's exposure basis. This guard is what stops an accountant from overwriting a final reserve computed from numbers that have since moved.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field of any kind. In particular, redemptions, alert transitions, claim decisions, listing validation and period closing carry no expected-version input and are guarded only by their state preconditions.

### 2.8. Request Validation Order

Every request is validated as an ordered ladder, and **the first violation encountered wins**: the request is rejected with that rule's error and no later rule is evaluated. This ordering is normative, because several rules can be violated simultaneously and only one error surfaces. The tiers, from first-evaluated to last, are:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` present and non-empty on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization** — the caller's role appears in the target endpoint's authorized role list. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Store scoping** — for a `StoreManager`, the target's effective store must equal `X-User-ID`, and a target with no store denies the caller. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
4. **Request well-formedness** — every field the endpoint declares as required is present and non-null; every enum-typed field carries one of its declared values; every datetime parses as ISO 8601 UTC. The only client-supplied datetime inputs in the whole system are `hold_start`, `hold_expires_at`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `purchased_at` and `to_iso8601`; the bulk-order `id_expiry_date` of §3.3 is deliberately *not* one of them and is checked only as a required non-empty string, so a bare calendar date is well-formed there. The amount fields `amount_cents`, `face_value_cents`, `face_value_cents_each`, `disputed_amount_cents` and `asking_price_cents` must each be at least 1, while `listed_balance_cents` must be at least 0; `card_quantity` must be at least 1; `expected_card_count`, `observed_card_count` and `seal_breaks_observed` must each be at least 0; `device_reputation_score` must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive; `email_age_days` must be at least 0; `limit` must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` must be at least 0; `hold_expires_at` must be strictly after `hold_start`; `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`; `card_number_masked`, when supplied, must match the masked-PAN pattern of four asterisks, a hyphen, four asterisks, a hyphen, four asterisks, a hyphen and then exactly four digits; and every element of `missing_evidence_kinds` must be either `PURCHASE_RECEIPT` or `PACKAGING_PHOTO`. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This tier applies to every endpoint that accepts a body or query parameters.
5. **Referenced-resource existence**, evaluated in this internal order: store references first (a path store id, a `store_id`, or a `merchant_store_id` must resolve, otherwise `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — this applies to reading one store, creating a rack, stocking a card, opening an issuance request, opening a bulk order, redeeming against a card, and settling a hold); then rack references (a path rack id or a `rack_id`, otherwise `ERR_RACK_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — applying to reading one rack, auditing a rack, clearing a rack's tamper flag, stocking a card, and opening an issuance request); then gift-card references (a path card id, a `card_id`, or a `reissued_from_card_id`, otherwise `ERR_CARD_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — applying to reading one card, recording and listing balance inquiries on a card, redeeming, placing and listing holds on a card, listing a card's reseller listings, overriding a card's posture, opening an issuance request, raising a draining alert, filing a victim claim and submitting a reseller listing); then the issuance request named by the path (`ERR_ISSUANCE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading, screening and deciding an issuance request); then bulk-order references by path or `bulk_order_id` (`ERR_BULK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading a bulk order, verifying its identity, deciding it, and opening an issuance request that links to it); then draining-alert references by path or `alert_id` (`ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading, triaging, resolving and reopening an alert, listing its referrals, filing a referral, and filing a victim claim that links to it); then the victim claim named by the path (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading a claim, requesting and resubmitting evidence, deciding and rescinding it); then the reseller listing named by the path (`ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — validating a listing); then the accounting period named by the path (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading a period, closing it, restating it, and reading its exposure summary); then ledger-event references by path or `reverses_event_id`, which must resolve to an existing event on the same card (`ERR_LEDGER_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — reading one event and reversing one); and finally the redemption hold named by the path (`ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, 404 — settling a hold).
6. **Stale-version guards** — `expected_gift_card_version` on hold creation (`ERR_STALE_CARD_VERSION`, 412) and `expected_liability_period_version` on period restatement (`ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION`, 412). Note carefully that both are checked *before* any state precondition below.
7. **Card funding and posture state** — the target card's funding state must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CARD_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); then its fraud posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CARD_FROZEN` (409). Both apply to exactly three operations: redeeming against a card, placing a hold on a card, and settling a hold with a capture disposition. Neither applies to reversing a ledger event — a frozen card's fraudulent redemptions must remain unwindable.
8. **Per-entity transition legality**, each rejecting with its own conflict code (409): an issuance request must be `SUBMITTED` to be screened and `SUBMITTED` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` to be decided, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ISSUANCE_STATE`; a bulk order must be `PENDING_IDENTITY` for identity verification and `PENDING_IDENTITY` or `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` to be decided, with the request-more-information decision admitted only from `IDENTITY_VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BULK_ORDER_STATE`; a draining alert must be `OPEN` to be triaged, `TRIAGED` to be resolved and `DISMISSED` to be reopened, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ALERT_STATE`; a victim claim must be `SUBMITTED` for an evidence request or a decision, `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED` for an evidence resubmission and `APPROVED` for a rescission, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`; a reseller listing must be `SUBMITTED` to be validated, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`; an accounting period must be `OPEN` to be closed and `CLOSED` or `RESTATED` to be restated, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`; a redemption hold must be `ACTIVE` to be settled, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_HOLD_STATE`; and the ledger event named for reversal must itself be of the reversible kind `REDEMPTION` and must not already be pointed at by an existing reversal, otherwise `ERR_LEDGER_EVENT_ALREADY_REVERSED`.
9. **Retail-floor safety**, in this internal order: a physical issuance request must declare its tamper-evident packaging intact, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGING_SEAL_BROKEN` (422) — checked both when the request is opened and when it is screened; then the rack backing a physical issuance must be classified `INTACT`, otherwise `ERR_RACK_TAMPERED` (422) — checked when the request is opened, when it is screened, and when a card is stocked onto the rack; then the store whose floor is involved must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED` (422) — checked when an issuance request is opened, when it is screened, when a bulk order is approved, and when a card is redeemed; and then segregation of duties, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — checked when an alert is resolved and when a claim is decided.
10. **Referential business rules** — a bulk order at or above the identity threshold must carry a captured identity number before it may be approved, otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` (422); a claim approval requires channel-appropriate evidence, otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (422); and a law-enforcement referral may only be filed against a `CONFIRMED` alert, otherwise `ERR_ALERT_NOT_CONFIRMED` (422). Because this tier follows the safety tier, a suspended store floor masks a missing identity on the same bulk approval — the caller sees `ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`.
11. **Temporal windows** — a victim claim must be filed inside its filing window, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).
12. **Quantitative business limits** — the bulk quantity limit (`ERR_BULK_QUANTITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422, on bulk order creation); the evidence-round limit (`ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422, on an evidence request); accounting-period non-overlap (`ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, 409, on period creation); and listing uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`, 409, on listing submission).
13. **Fiscal ceilings**, evaluated last of all — a redemption may not exceed the card's spendable funds (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`, 422); a hold must fit the aggregate capacity of the holds it intersects (`ERR_HOLD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422); and a claim's gross recovery may not exceed the per-claim cap (`ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422).

A worked consequence of this ladder: a point-of-sale redemption against a depleted, frozen card at a suspended store for more than the available balance surfaces `ERR_CARD_NOT_ACTIVE`, because the funding-state tier precedes the posture tier, which precedes the floor-safety tier, which precedes the fiscal tier.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascades and Deterministic Ordering

A large fraction of this system's data is derived rather than written. A store's floor posture, a rack's tamper classification, every balance and pattern figure on a gift card, both card drain scores, a bulk order's pricing breakdown, and every reserve figure on an accounting period are all computed by the server. **No endpoint accepts a derived field as input**, and an attempt to supply one is simply ignored rather than honoured. Derived values are recomputed eagerly, inside the request that disturbs their inputs — this system is immediately consistent and has no background recomputation, no lazy-on-read refresh and no eventual convergence.

Several operations mutate more than one record. When they do, **every touched record has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed**, including records the caller never named — the store whose counters moved, the cards a cascade voided, the listings a freeze blocked, the accounting period a write-off touched. Three multi-hop cascades exist and each is described in full where it originates: the three-hop rack-tamper cascade in §3.1, the three-hop alert-confirmation cascade in §3.7, and the four-hop claim-reissue cascade in §3.9.

Wherever a cascade must process a set of records, it processes them in `created_at` ascending order. A record's `id` is strictly forbidden as an input to any selector, ranking, tie-break, allocation order, cascade order or chronological replay order anywhere in this system; where a further tie-break beyond a domain key is needed, `created_at` ascending supplies it.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Reverse Lookups

No business resource in this system is created underneath another resource's URL. Every collection is addressable at the top level, and the only multi-segment paths are action endpoints hanging off a resource (recording an inquiry against a card, auditing a rack, settling a hold) and reverse-reachability reads that answer "what points at this record?".

When a path carries more than one segment, its segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error. A request naming a card that does not exist, in order to list that card's holds, returns `ERR_CARD_NOT_FOUND` — the sub-resource is never consulted. This matters most for the referral read hanging off a draining alert: a missing alert yields `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty list.

Most list collections accept a `state` query parameter that filters on the resource's lifecycle `state` field exactly, alongside whatever foreign-key filters that collection offers; an unrecognised value for such a filter is an invalid request rather than an empty result. Gift cards are the exception in form only — carrying two independent lifecycle dimensions, they are filtered by `activation_state` and `risk_posture` separately rather than by a single combined filter.

Five reverse-reachability reads are the only way to discover certain relationships. A card's balance-inquiry log, a card's competing holds, a card's secondary-marketplace listings, and an alert's law-enforcement referral packets are each reachable only through the owning resource's path. In particular, law-enforcement referrals have no listable global collection: a referral is discovered either by reading it directly by its own `id` or through the referral list on the alert it documents.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Retail Floor Monitoring: Racks, Audits and the Tamper Cascade

A retail store is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `region_code` of exactly two uppercase letters used for reporting rollups, and an `address_line` of 1 to 200 characters. It is born with a `floor_posture` of `OPEN` and every derived counter — `tampered_rack_count`, `suspect_rack_count`, `open_alert_count` and `declined_issuance_count` — sitting at 0. The first three of those counters are recomputed from live data whenever anything relevant moves; `declined_issuance_count` is different in kind, being a monotonic tally that increases by exactly 1 each time an issuance request naming this store lands in `DECLINED`, and never decreases.

Each gift-card fixture on the sales floor is registered as a rack belonging to a store, identified by a `rack_label` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique within that store, and carrying an `expected_card_count` between 0 and 500 inclusive. The expected count is the planogram slot count and is the baseline against which shrink is measured. A newly registered rack is `INTACT`, with `missing_card_count` 0, `seal_breaks_observed` 0, and both `observed_card_count` and `last_audit_at` still empty because no audit has run. Registering a rack triggers a recomputation of its store's posture — which an `INTACT` rack cannot change — and still bumps the store's `version`.

Rack monitoring happens through audits, which a `StoreManager` or a `PosAssociate` records by reporting the `observed_card_count` physically counted in the fixture and the `seal_breaks_observed` — the number of broken tamper-evident seals found. Both figures are persisted verbatim so that the classification is reproducible from the stored row, and `last_audit_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Shrink is then derived as the expected count minus the observed count, floored at zero and stored as `missing_card_count`: finding *more* cards than expected is a stocking discrepancy, not shrink, and must never produce a negative count.

The rack's `tamper_state` is then re-derived from those two signals, with the checks applied strictly in this order and the first match winning. If `missing_card_count` is at least `rack_shrink_threshold`, **or** `seal_breaks_observed` is at least `rack_seal_break_threshold`, the rack is `TAMPERED`. Otherwise, if `missing_card_count` is at least 1 **or** `seal_breaks_observed` is at least 1, the rack is `SUSPECT`. Otherwise it is `INTACT`. The rule is a disjunction and not a sum, so either signal alone reaches `TAMPERED`: with the defaults in place, three missing cards and zero broken seals is `TAMPERED`, and so is zero missing cards with three broken seals. Ten expected against seven observed with no broken seals yields shrink of exactly three and lands on `TAMPERED` at exactly the threshold; ten against eight yields shrink of two and lands on `SUSPECT`; ten against twelve yields shrink of zero and, with no broken seals, stays `INTACT`.

Classification is fully re-derived on every audit and is deliberately not sticky, with one exception. A `SUSPECT` rack audited again with clean numbers returns to `INTACT`, and a `SUSPECT` rack audited again with dirty-but-sub-threshold numbers stays `SUSPECT` while refreshing its observation fields. A `TAMPERED` rack, however, cannot be talked out of that classification by a clean audit: it stays `TAMPERED` until a `StoreManager` or a `RiskManager` remediates it. Remediation records a `remediation_note`, resets `observed_card_count` to equal `expected_card_count`, resets `seal_breaks_observed` and `missing_card_count` to 0, and returns `tamper_state` to `INTACT`. Remediation is also available on a `SUSPECT` rack. Auditing is idempotent in effect but not in bookkeeping — re-auditing with identical numbers reclassifies to the same state and still bumps the rack's `version`.

A store's `floor_posture` is entirely derived and is never writable through the API. It is recomputed to a fixed point after every rack audit, every remediation, and every draining-alert creation, triage, resolution and reopen. The derivation first counts the store's racks classified `TAMPERED` into `tampered_rack_count` and those classified `SUSPECT` into `suspect_rack_count`, and counts into `open_alert_count` every draining alert in state `OPEN` or `TRIAGED` whose card belongs to this store. Then, first match winning: if `tampered_rack_count` is at least 1 the floor is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise, if `suspect_rack_count` is at least 1 **or** `open_alert_count` is at least `floor_heightened_alert_threshold`, the floor is `HEIGHTENED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. A single tampered rack outranks any number of alerts. An alert count landing exactly on the threshold heightens the floor while one below it does not. The derivation reads only rack and alert state and never its own previous value, so it terminates in a single pass with no oscillation, and it is bidirectional over time: remediating the last tampered rack drops the floor straight back to `HEIGHTENED` or `OPEN`, and resolving alerts below the threshold does the same. A store with no racks and no alerts is `OPEN`. Note that both `CONFIRMED` and `DISMISSED` alerts stop counting toward `open_alert_count`, so confirming the last live alert can relax the floor even though the fraud turned out to be real; and because alerts are attributed to a store through the card's store, a reissued card that preserves its store keeps contributing to that store.

The three-hop rack-tamper cascade begins the moment an audit classifies a rack `TAMPERED`, whether it arrives there from `INTACT` or from `SUSPECT`. Hop one is the rack's own transition to `TAMPERED`. Hop two is the store's recomputation, which — because `tampered_rack_count` is now at least 1 — drives `floor_posture` to `SUSPENDED` from either `OPEN` or `HEIGHTENED`. Hop three follows from the store now being suspended: every gift card belonging to that store whose funding state is still `INACTIVE` transitions to `VOIDED`, has its fraud posture forced to `FROZEN`, and has its `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Those cards are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Cards that are `ACTIVE`, `DEPLETED` or `CLOSED` are left completely untouched, because their value is already funded and belongs to a customer. The rack's `version`, the store's `version` and each voided card's `version` are each incremented by 1.

Remediating the rack afterwards is explicitly **not** a compensation. `VOIDED` is the terminal funding state and voided cards are never restored; remediation only releases the store gate, letting `floor_posture` fall back to `HEIGHTENED` or `OPEN` depending on the remaining suspect racks and live alerts. Both the rack's `version` and the store's `version` increment on remediation.

### 3.2. Stocking, Activation Controls and Digital Issuance Screening

A physical gift card enters the system when a `PosAssociate` or a `StoreManager` stocks it, naming the `store_id` that stocked it and the `rack_id` it sits on. An optional `card_number_masked` may be supplied; when it is omitted the server generates one. The masked PAN takes the form of three hyphen-separated groups of four asterisks followed by a hyphen and the last four digits of the account number, and it must be unique across every card in the system. The card is created with `channel` `PHYSICAL`, a funding state of `INACTIVE`, a fraud posture of `CLEAR`, `face_value_cents` of 0 and every balance figure at 0. Stocking onto a rack that is not `INTACT` is refused with `ERR_RACK_TAMPERED` — a compromised fixture may not be restocked. Digital cards are never stocked this way; the server mints them itself when a digital issuance request is approved, and such a card carries no store and no rack at all.

An issuance request is the instrument that turns a stocked card into a funded one, and a `PosAssociate` opens it. Its `channel` is either `PHYSICAL`, for point-of-sale activation of a racked card, or `DIGITAL`, for e-commerce issuance screened on fraud-vendor signals. Both channels require `face_value_cents`, the value to be loaded, which must be at least 100 and at most 500000. A `PHYSICAL` request additionally requires `store_id`, `rack_id`, `card_id` naming an `INACTIVE` card stocked on that rack, `pos_terminal_id` identifying the register that raised the request, and `packaging_seal_intact` — the associate's attestation that the tamper-evident packaging was unbroken. A `DIGITAL` request must omit `store_id`, `rack_id` and `card_id` entirely (the server fills the card reference in itself when it mints one at approval) and must instead supply `device_reputation_score`, a fraud-vendor device reputation from 0 to 100 where higher is *more* trustworthy; `email_age_days`, the age in days of the purchaser's email address; `payment_risk_band`, the acquirer's assessment of the payment instrument as `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`; and `recipient_email_hash`, a salted hash of the digital recipient's address. Either channel may optionally carry `bulk_order_id`, linking the issuance to a bulk purchase.

Three refusals guard the opening of a request, and the packaging attestation is the first of them: a `PHYSICAL` request that declares `packaging_seal_intact` false is refused with `ERR_PACKAGING_SEAL_BROKEN`, because packaging that has already been opened means the card data may already be in a criminal's hands. A `PHYSICAL` request naming a rack that is not `INTACT` is refused with `ERR_RACK_TAMPERED`, and a request naming a store whose floor is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED`. A request that survives all three is created in state `SUBMITTED`, with `screening_score`, `screening_components` and `decline_reason` all empty and `screened_at` not yet stamped.

Screening is the branching fork of the issuance lifecycle, and it forks on entirely different grounds per channel. A `PosAssociate`, `StoreManager`, `FraudAnalyst` or `PlatformAdmin` may screen. For a `PHYSICAL` request, the fork reads the referenced store's floor posture: an `OPEN` floor drives the request straight to `APPROVED`, a `HEIGHTENED` floor drives it to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and a `SUSPENDED` floor refuses the screening entirely with `ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED`. Approval from a physical screening additionally requires the backing rack to be `INTACT` and the named card to still be `INACTIVE`. For a `DIGITAL` request, the fork reads a computed risk score. Either way `screened_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the request's `version` is incremented; `screening_score` and `screening_components` are written for `DIGITAL` requests only and stay empty forever on a `PHYSICAL` one.

The digital screening score is a multi-factor, non-linear composition. Each raw signal is first pushed through its own transform, and only then are the transformed components blended — blending the raw signals directly gives a different and wrong answer, because none of the four transforms is linear across its whole domain.

- The **device component** is a linear inversion floored at zero: one hundred minus `device_reputation_score`, and never below 0. A device reputation above 100 therefore contributes 0 rather than a negative number.
- The **email component** is a step function and is emphatically not interpolated. An `email_age_days` of at least 365 contributes 0. From 90 through 364 days inclusive it contributes 20. From 7 through 89 days inclusive it contributes 45. Below 7 days it contributes 70. The boundary at one year is a twenty-point cliff: 365 days scores 0 while 364 days scores 20, so any linear treatment is detectably wrong.
- The **payment component** is a categorical lookup: `LOW` contributes 0, `MEDIUM` contributes 25, and `HIGH` contributes 60.
- The **amount component** is a capped staircase over `face_value_cents`: take the face value divided by 5000 and discarded down to a whole number of steps, award 5 points per step, and cap the result at 30. A face value of 29999 cents yields five whole steps and 25 points; 30000 cents yields six steps and 30 points; and 500000 cents also yields 30 points because of the cap.

Those four transformed components are then blended with fixed weights and rounded, exactly once, half up to an integer: thirty-five hundredths of the device component, plus twenty-five hundredths of the email component, plus twenty-five hundredths of the payment component, plus fifteen hundredths of the amount component. Call that rounded figure the base.

A single surcharge is then applied **after** the blend and **after** the rounding. When the request carries a `bulk_order_id` and that bulk order has not yet cleared identity — meaning it is still in `PENDING_IDENTITY` — 15 points are added to the base and the result is capped at 100. Otherwise the score is the base unchanged. The order here is load-bearing: surcharging before the blend would scale those 15 points down by the blend weights and produce a different number. One consequence is worth internalising — the surcharge alone can flip an otherwise-approvable request into review without a single fraud signal changing, and once the bulk order reaches `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` the surcharge stops applying, so re-screening the same request yields a strictly lower score. The resulting figure is stored as `screening_score`, and `screening_components` preserves the composition for audit, recording the four transformed components alongside the pre-surcharge base and the surcharge actually applied under the keys `device`, `email`, `payment`, `amount`, `base` and `bulk_surcharge`.

The digital disposition then reads that score against two inclusive thresholds. A score at or above `digital_decline_score_threshold` sends the request to `DECLINED` with `decline_reason` set to `DIGITAL_RISK_SCORE`. Otherwise, a score at or above `digital_hold_score_threshold` sends it to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with no decline reason. Otherwise it goes to `APPROVED`, also with no decline reason. Both boundaries bite on the threshold itself: a score exactly equal to the hold threshold is held rather than approved, and a score exactly equal to the decline threshold is declined rather than held.

Reaching `APPROVED` is what funds a card. For a `DIGITAL` request the server first mints a brand-new card with `channel` `DIGITAL`, no store, no rack, a funding state of `INACTIVE` and a posture of `CLEAR`, and points the request's `card_id` at it. Then, on either channel, the referenced card moves from `INACTIVE` to `ACTIVE`: its `face_value_cents` is copied from the request, a credit event of kind `ACTIVATION_LOAD` for that amount is appended to the card's ledger, all of its balance figures are re-derived, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `rack_id` is cleared to nothing because the card has left the fixture. The card's `version` increments, as does the request's.

Reaching `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` produces no funding movement whatsoever — no card is minted on the digital side and no ledger event is appended on either. The physical branch does one extra thing, and it is the cleanest available proof that funding and fraud are independent axes: the referenced card's fraud posture escalates from `CLEAR` to `WATCH` while the card remains `INACTIVE` with a zero balance. A card held because its store floor was `HEIGHTENED` is therefore watched before it has ever carried a cent.

Reaching `DECLINED` from screening sets `decline_reason` to `DIGITAL_RISK_SCORE` and, when the request named a store, increments that store's `declined_issuance_count` by 1 and the store's `version` by 1.

A held request is resolved manually by a `StoreManager` or a `RiskManager`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` and an optional `review_note`. This is the join point of the fork: approval reaches exactly the same `APPROVED` terminal, with exactly the same minting, loading and activation side effects as the automatic path, except that it tolerates a `HEIGHTENED` floor — only a `SUSPENDED` floor blocks it, and a physical approval still requires an `INTACT` rack and an `INACTIVE` card. Declining sets `decline_reason` to `MANUAL_DECLINE`, leaves any referenced card untouched at `INACTIVE` with a zero balance, and increments the store's `declined_issuance_count` and `version` when the request named a store. Either decision records `decided_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and the supplied `review_note`, and increments the request's `version`. On the automatic path `decided_by_user_id` stays empty, which is how a reader distinguishes an automatic outcome from a reviewed one. `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are both terminal — no further transition leaves either.

### 3.3. Bulk Purchase Monitoring and Identity Capture

Bulk gift-card purchases are the classic laundering vector, so every bulk order is priced, size-limited and — above a value threshold — identity-bound before it can be approved. A `PosAssociate` or a `StoreManager` opens the order against a `store_id`, stating the `card_quantity` requested, the `face_value_cents_each` requested per card (at least 100 and at most 500000), the `purchaser_name` given at the counter (1 to 120 characters), and the `payment_method` being tendered, which is one of `CASH`, `DEBIT`, `CREDIT` or `CORPORATE_ACH`.

Before anything is priced, the order is size-checked: a `card_quantity` greater than `bulk_quantity_limit` is refused with `ERR_BULK_QUANTITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A quantity exactly equal to the limit is accepted; one more than the limit is not.

Pricing then runs in a pinned order, and the order is the whole difficulty of the rule. First, `face_value_subtotal_cents` is the card quantity multiplied by the per-card face value — exact, with no rounding required. Second, `fee_rate_bps` is selected **from that pre-fee subtotal**, as a whole-order average-rate band rather than a marginal ladder, so exactly one rate applies to the entire subtotal: a subtotal below 25000 cents attracts no fee at all; from 25000 up to but not including 100000 cents the rate is 150 basis points; from 100000 up to but not including 500000 cents it is 225 basis points; and at 500000 cents or above it is 300 basis points. Third, `activation_fee_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that basis-point figure and divided by 10000, rounded half up **exactly once** over the whole order. Fourth, `total_cents` is the subtotal plus the activation fee.

Two tempting orderings are explicitly wrong and are excluded. Selecting the tier from the total rather than the subtotal would let an order whose subtotal sits just under a boundary be pushed into the next band by its own fee — a subtotal of 99900 cents sits in the 150-basis-point band and pays a fee of 1499 cents (the exact half-cent at 1498.5 rounds up), for a total of 101399 cents; reading the tier off that total would wrongly place it in the 225-basis-point band. Rounding per card and then multiplying by the quantity is equally wrong, because it accumulates up to one half-cent error per card instead of one for the whole order. A subtotal of exactly 25000 cents sits in the 150-basis-point band, since the lower edge of each band is inclusive; 24999 cents pays nothing.

The order's initial state falls straight out of the total. When `total_cents` is at or above `bulk_identity_threshold_cents` the order is created in `PENDING_IDENTITY`, because government identity is mandatory before approval. When the total is strictly below the threshold the order is already `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` in the creation response, with all three identity fields left empty because none is required. The boundary is inclusive on the upper side, so a total sitting exactly on the threshold still requires identity.

Identity capture is performed by a `PosAssociate` or a `StoreManager` on an order in `PENDING_IDENTITY`, supplying `id_type`, `id_number_hash` and `id_expiry_date`. Those land on the order as `identity_id_type` — one of `DRIVERS_LICENSE`, `PASSPORT`, `STATE_ID` or `MILITARY_ID` — together with `identity_id_number_hash`, a salted hash of the document number, and `identity_id_expiry_date`, the document's expiry. `id_expiry_date` is an opaque string field rather than a datetime: it is required and non-null, but it is never parsed, validated against any date or instant format, reformatted, or length-capped — any non-empty string is accepted, a bare calendar date such as `2030-04-30` included, and whatever arrived is stored on the order and echoed back in responses exactly as supplied. The order moves to `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` and its `version` increments.

A `StoreManager` or a `RiskManager` then decides the order, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DECLINE` or `REQUEST_INFO`, plus an optional `decision_note`. The request-more-information decision is the cyclic edge of this lifecycle and is admitted only from `IDENTITY_VERIFIED`: it returns the order to `PENDING_IDENTITY`, clears `identity_id_type`, `identity_id_number_hash` and `identity_id_expiry_date` all back to empty, and increments `info_request_count` by 1. An order may loop between `PENDING_IDENTITY` and `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` any number of times, and each loop leaves the money fields alone — the subtotal, the fee rate, the activation fee and the total are frozen at creation and are never recomputed by a decision of any kind.

Approval is admitted from either `PENDING_IDENTITY` or `IDENTITY_VERIFIED` and is guarded twice. The store's floor must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED`; and, on an order whose total is at or above `bulk_identity_threshold_cents`, `identity_id_number_hash` must not be empty, otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Because the floor check sits in an earlier validation tier than the identity check, a suspended floor masks a missing identity: the caller sees the floor error. Approval from `PENDING_IDENTITY` is therefore only ever reachable on an order below the identity threshold, where no identity was required in the first place. Declining is admitted from either state with no further checks at all. Either terminal decision records `decided_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` along with the `decision_note`, and increments the order's `version`. `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are both terminal.

### 3.4. The Card Ledger: Redemption, Reversal and Balance Projection

A gift card's money is never stored as a number that anyone writes. It is a **pure projection over the card's own append-only ledger**, re-derived from scratch every time an event lands. Each ledger event names the `card_id` whose balance it moves and carries an `amount_cents` that is always a positive magnitude of at least 1, together with a `type` fixed by the operation that produced it, an `occurred_at` stamped from the logical clock at append time, a `created_by_user_id` recording the caller's `X-User-ID`, and optionally the `pos_terminal_id` of the register or channel that produced the movement and a free-text `memo`. A `merchant_store_id` names the store where a redemption was taken and is empty on every non-redemption kind. A `reverses_event_id` is present only on a compensating event and points back at the event being undone.

Seven event kinds exist, and each carries a sign that the projection reads. The credit kinds, whose `signed_amount_cents` is the positive amount, are `ACTIVATION_LOAD`, `REDEMPTION_REVERSAL`, `REISSUE_CREDIT` and `REISSUE_DEBIT_REVERSAL`. The debit kinds, whose `signed_amount_cents` is the negated amount, are `REDEMPTION`, `REISSUE_DEBIT` and `REISSUE_CREDIT_REVERSAL`.

The projection replays the card's events in `occurred_at` ascending order, breaking ties on `created_at` ascending. That pair is a total order because `created_at` is unique per append, and the card's `id` is never consulted. `current_balance_cents` is the sum of every event's signed amount. `total_loaded_cents` is the sum of the raw amounts over the credit kinds only. `total_redeemed_cents` is the sum of `REDEMPTION` amounts minus the sum of `REDEMPTION_REVERSAL` amounts — equivalently, the sum of the amounts of the *unreversed* redemptions. An event counts as reversed precisely when some other event exists whose `reverses_event_id` points at it; there is no mutable flag on the parent event, and the question is always answered by looking for the child. Because the replay sorts on `occurred_at` rather than on append order, an event whose `occurred_at` precedes an already-stored event slots into its chronological position and the projection is order-independent with respect to the sequence in which events arrived.

`drawdown_ratio` is then derived: 0.00 when `total_loaded_cents` is zero, and otherwise `total_redeemed_cents` divided by `total_loaded_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places and clamped into the closed range 0.00 through 1.00. Two redemption-pattern counters are restricted to the recent window running from the current logical instant back by `redemption_window_minutes`, with both ends of that window inclusive, and consider only unreversed redemptions: `distinct_merchant_count` counts the distinct non-empty `merchant_store_id` values among them, ignoring events with no merchant entirely rather than treating "no merchant" as its own merchant, and `recent_redemption_count` counts those events themselves.

Holds contribute the last two figures. A hold counts against a card while it is `ACTIVE` and its `hold_expires_at` is at or after the current logical instant — a hold expiring exactly *now* still counts. `held_amount_cents` is the sum of the amounts of the counted holds, and `available_balance_cents` is the current balance minus the held amount, floored at zero. Note the asymmetry: the available balance floors at zero while the current balance is not floored by holds at all, so the two figures diverge whenever holds exceed the ledger balance. A card with no ledger events at all reports every figure as 0 and a `drawdown_ratio` of 0.00 — not empty, and not an error.

A `PosAssociate` redeems value at the register by supplying `amount_cents`, the `merchant_store_id` where the sale happened, and optionally `pos_terminal_id` and `memo`. A `REDEMPTION` event is appended with `occurred_at` at the logical now, every balance and pattern figure is re-derived, and the card's `version` increments. A redemption is refused when the card is not `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CARD_NOT_ACTIVE`), when its posture is `FROZEN` (`ERR_CARD_FROZEN`), when the merchant store's floor is `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_STORE_FLOOR_SUSPENDED`), and when `amount_cents` exceeds the card's `available_balance_cents` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`) — in exactly that order of precedence. The spendable ceiling is the *available* balance, not the current one, so outstanding holds genuinely reduce what may be spent.

The two funding-state edges that the ledger drives are automatic and mutually inverse. Whenever an append or a reversal leaves an `ACTIVE` card's re-derived `current_balance_cents` at exactly zero, the card transitions `ACTIVE → DEPLETED` and its `version` increments. Whenever an append or a reversal leaves a `DEPLETED` card's re-derived balance above zero, it transitions `DEPLETED → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments. The two alternate freely as compensating events land, which is exactly the cycle the reversal path exercises.

Reversal is the only correction mechanism, and it is the exclusive province of a `RiskManager`, who names the event to unwind and supplies a `reversal_reason`. A new `REDEMPTION_REVERSAL` event is appended, carrying the original's amount as a positive movement, with `reverses_event_id` pointing at the original, the supplied reason recorded as the new event's `memo`, and `occurred_at` at the logical now. The original event is never mutated — history is append-only. All balance and pattern figures are then re-derived, so `current_balance_cents` returns exactly to its pre-redemption value and the reversed line drops out of `total_redeemed_cents`, which in turn pulls `drawdown_ratio` back down. Only `REDEMPTION` events are reversible, and an event that some existing event already reverses cannot be reversed again; either violation is `ERR_LEDGER_EVENT_ALREADY_REVERSED`. Reversal deliberately leaves `risk_posture` completely untouched — a `FROZEN` card stays `FROZEN` after its fraudulent redemptions have been unwound — and, just as deliberately, reversal is the one value-moving operation that the frozen-card guard does **not** block, because freezing a drained card must never prevent making its victim whole.

### 3.5. Redemption Holds and Aggregate Interval Capacity

A pending authorization reserves part of a card's balance as a redemption hold. A `PosAssociate` or a `RiskManager` places one by supplying the `amount_cents` to reserve, the `hold_start` at which the reservation begins, the `hold_expires_at` at which it lapses, and the `expected_gift_card_version` described in §2.7. The interval is **closed at both ends**: both `hold_start` and `hold_expires_at` are inside the reservation, and `hold_expires_at` must be strictly after `hold_start`. The hold is created `ACTIVE` with `captured_amount_cents` at 0 and no `disposition` yet. Placing a hold requires the card to be `ACTIVE` and its posture not to be `FROZEN`; it recomputes the card's `held_amount_cents` and `available_balance_cents` and increments the card's `version`, but it does **not** move `current_balance_cents` — a hold reserves value, it does not spend it.

Overlapping holds on one card are perfectly legal; what is capped is their sum. Two holds intersect when the existing hold's start is at or before the candidate's expiry **and** the existing hold's expiry is at or after the candidate's start. Because the intervals are closed, touching at a single instant counts as intersecting — a hold ending at noon and a hold starting at noon are in conflict. The capacity test is then taken over the whole intersecting set rather than pairwise: sum the amounts of every `ACTIVE` hold on the card that intersects the candidate, add the candidate's own amount, and refuse the candidate with `ERR_HOLD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that sum exceeds the card's `current_balance_cents`. The comparison is strict, so a candidate that exactly exhausts the balance is accepted and drives `available_balance_cents` to exactly 0; one cent more is refused. Because the candidate is measured against the union of everything it touches, a short hold that bridges two otherwise-disjoint holds must fit under the sum of *both* — a pairwise check would wrongly wave it through. Only `ACTIVE` holds bear capacity: released, captured and expired holds free their capacity immediately, which is why advancing the logical clock past a hold's expiry can turn a refused candidate into an accepted one with nothing else having changed.

Settlement is performed by a `PosAssociate` or a `RiskManager` on an `ACTIVE` hold, supplying a `disposition` of either `RELEASE` or `CAPTURE`, an optional `settle_note`, and an optional `merchant_store_id`. The merchant store is required in practice for a capture and ignored on a release, which is why it is declared optional. A release moves the hold to `RELEASED`, records the disposition and note, leaves `captured_amount_cents` at 0, appends no ledger event, and leaves `current_balance_cents` entirely unchanged while recomputing `held_amount_cents` and `available_balance_cents`. A capture moves the hold to `CAPTURED`, sets `captured_amount_cents` to the hold's own amount, and appends a `REDEMPTION` event for that amount at the supplied merchant store — which means the card's whole projection is re-derived, and the card may auto-transition `ACTIVE → DEPLETED` when the recomputed balance reaches zero. A capture additionally requires the card to be `ACTIVE` and not `FROZEN`; a release does not. Either disposition increments both the hold's `version` and the card's `version`. A captured hold has by then turned into a real redemption, so its value shows up in `current_balance_cents` rather than in `held_amount_cents` — there is no double counting.

The fourth hold state is reached only by the passage of time. When a `PlatformAdmin` advances the logical clock, every `ACTIVE` hold whose `hold_expires_at` falls strictly before the new clock reading is swept to `EXPIRED`, deterministically, in `hold_expires_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as tie-break. The sweep is never performed lazily on read. Each swept hold has its `version` incremented, each affected card has `held_amount_cents` and `available_balance_cents` recomputed and its `version` incremented, and the count of holds swept is reported back as `expired_hold_count`. `RELEASED`, `CAPTURED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Card-Draining Detection: Inquiry Velocity, Drain Scoring and Posture

Card draining announces itself through balance inquiries. A criminal who has captured a card's data has no way to know when a victim will load it, so they poll — and that polling is the single richest fraud signal this system has. A `PosAssociate`, `FraudAnalyst` or `ResellerPartner` records an inquiry against a card by supplying the `channel` it arrived on, one of `POS`, `IVR`, `WEB`, `MOBILE` or `RESELLER_API`, together with `requester_ip_hash`, a salted hash of the requesting IP address or terminal fingerprint, 1 to 128 characters long. Inquiries are permitted against a card in **any** funding state and **any** fraud posture — a frozen card's balance may still be probed, and probing it is precisely how the velocity evidence accumulates. The inquiry is appended with `occurred_at` at the logical now and `balance_disclosed_cents` recording the card's `current_balance_cents` at that instant. Inquiries are immutable: they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`, and the per-card inquiry log reads in `occurred_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as tie-break.

Velocity is evaluated **after** the new row has been appended, so the triggering inquiry counts itself. The window runs from the current logical instant back by `inquiry_velocity_window_minutes`, with both ends inclusive, so an inquiry sitting exactly on the window's opening edge is inside it. The number of that card's inquiries inside the window is stored on the new row as `velocity_count_at_inquiry`. The very first inquiry on a card therefore records a count of 1, never 0. A breach occurs when that count is at or above `inquiry_velocity_threshold` — at exactly the threshold, never one below it. Because advancing the logical clock ages older inquiries out of the window, the count can *decrease* between consecutive inquiries, and a later inquiry may fail to breach even though an earlier one did.

On breach the handler raises a draining alert on the card automatically, with a detection basis of `INQUIRY_VELOCITY` and an initial state of `OPEN`, copying `velocity_count_at_inquiry` into the alert's `basis_velocity_count`. Automatic alerts are deduplicated: no auto-alert is raised when the card already carries an alert in state `OPEN` or `TRIAGED` whose detection basis is `INQUIRY_VELOCITY`, so repeated breaching does not fan out a swarm of duplicates. When an alert is created this way, the store's `open_alert_count` and `floor_posture` are recomputed and the store's `version` increments; the card's `version` increments on every inquiry regardless.

Two drain risk scores live on every card, and **they are not two roundings of one number**. They consume different inputs with different weights, are stored in different fields, and are refreshed at different moments, so a card normally carries two different live scores at once.

The **inquiry-weighted** score is recomputed on every balance inquiry, written to the card's `drain_risk_score_inquiry` and copied onto the inquiry row as `drain_score_at_inquiry`. Its raw figure is 12 points per inquiry in the current velocity count, plus 30 multiplied by the card's `drawdown_ratio`, plus 5 points per entry in `distinct_merchant_count`. Velocity dominates it and `recent_redemption_count` is not an input at all.

The **redemption-weighted** score is recomputed when an alert on the card is triaged, written to the card's `drain_risk_score_redemption` and copied into the alert's `basis_drain_score`. Its raw figure is 45 multiplied by the card's `drawdown_ratio`, plus 14 points per entry in `distinct_merchant_count`, plus 6 points per event in `recent_redemption_count`. It ignores inquiry velocity entirely.

Both variants round half up to an integer exactly once, at the very end, and then clamp into the closed range 0 through 100. Intermediate products are never rounded. The clamp genuinely bites: a card with a `drawdown_ratio` of 1.00, six distinct merchants and twelve recent redemptions raws to 45 plus 84 plus 72, which is 201, and stores 100. A brand-new card with no ledger events scores 0 under both variants and stays `CLEAR`.

Whichever score was just recomputed then drives a posture escalation up a fixed four-rung ladder, on which `CLEAR` is the lowest rung, then `WATCH`, then `RESTRICTED`, and `FROZEN` is the highest. A target rung is selected as follows. If an inquiry velocity breach just occurred, the target is `RESTRICTED` — the velocity breach alone forces that rung no matter how low the score is; this clause applies only to the inquiry-weighted variant, since a breach is only ever evaluated inside the inquiry handler. Otherwise, if the score is at or above `drain_restrict_score_threshold`, the target is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if the score is at or above `drain_watch_score_threshold`, the target is `WATCH`. Otherwise the target is `CLEAR`. The card's `risk_posture` is then set to that target **only when the target sits strictly higher on the ladder than the card's current rung**. Scoring therefore never lowers a posture: a score exactly on the watch threshold escalates a `CLEAR` card to `WATCH` while one point below it does not; a card already `FROZEN` stays `FROZEN` even when the freshly computed score maps to `WATCH`. Because the two variants read disjoint signals, a card can be driven to `RESTRICTED` by the redemption-weighted variant while its inquiry-weighted score is still under the watch threshold, and the reverse is equally possible.

Only two things can *lower* a posture. The first is dismissing an alert, described in §3.7. The second is a manual override, which is the exclusive province of a `RiskManager` and the only unconstrained write to this dimension: the manager supplies the desired `risk_posture` — `CLEAR`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — together with a `reason`, and the card's posture is set to exactly that value, upward or downward, with the monotone escalation rule not applying. An override leaves the funding state and every balance figure completely untouched, which is the plainest demonstration that the two dimensions are independent. Overriding to `FROZEN` additionally triggers the listing-block cascade of §3.8. The override increments the card's `version` by 1, plus the `version` of each listing the cascade blocks.

### 3.7. Alert Investigation, the Confirmation Cascade and Law-Enforcement Referral

A draining alert is the case file for one suspected drained card. Alongside the ones the inquiry handler raises automatically, a `FraudAnalyst` or a `RiskManager` may raise one manually against a `card_id`, stating the `detection_basis` — `INQUIRY_VELOCITY`, `REDEMPTION_PATTERN`, `RESELLER_SIGNAL` or `MANUAL_REVIEW` — and optionally a `note` explaining why. A manually raised alert starts in `OPEN` with `basis_velocity_count` and `basis_drain_score` both at 0.

Every alert captures `exposure_cents` at the instant it is created, and that figure is a **frozen snapshot that is never recomputed**. It is the card's `current_balance_cents` plus its `total_redeemed_cents` — that is, the total funds the alert puts at risk: what is still redeemable plus what has already been drained away. Later redemptions, reversals, triage and resolution all leave it untouched. That deliberate staleness is exactly what makes the period reserves of §3.10 reproducible after the fact. A card whose balance is zero because it has already been fully drained still carries a positive exposure, because the redeemed total carries the loss; a brand-new card with no ledger events yields an exposure of 0; and reversing a redemption after the alert exists does not lower the snapshot.

Raising an alert recomputes the store's `open_alert_count` and `floor_posture` — which can heighten a floor once the live alert count reaches `floor_heightened_alert_threshold` — and increments the store's `version` when a store was touched. An alert on a card with no store, such as any `DIGITAL` card, is attributed to no store at all.

Triage is exclusively a `FraudAnalyst` operation and is admitted only from `OPEN`. The analyst supplies a `triage_note` and `redemption_pattern_reviewed`, an attestation that the card's redemption pattern was actually inspected. The alert moves `OPEN → TRIAGED`, `triaged_by_user_id` is recorded from the caller's `X-User-ID`, the card's redemption-weighted drain score is recomputed as described in §3.6 and copied into the alert's `basis_drain_score`, the card's posture escalates upward only under the same ladder — reaching `RESTRICTED` when the recomputed score is at or above `drain_restrict_score_threshold` and the card's current rung is lower — and the store's `open_alert_count` and `floor_posture` are recomputed. The alert's `version`, the card's `version` and the store's `version` all increment by 1. A `WATCH` card whose triage score crosses the restrict threshold therefore moves `WATCH → RESTRICTED`; a `CLEAR` card in the same position moves `CLEAR → RESTRICTED`.

Resolution is exclusively a `RiskManager` operation, is admitted only from `TRIAGED`, and requires a `resolution` of either `CONFIRM` or `DISMISS` together with a mandatory `resolution_note`. It is also the harder half of the segregation-of-duties boundary: the resolving `X-User-ID` must differ from the alert's recorded `triaged_by_user_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either resolution records `resolved_by_user_id` from the caller and the supplied note, recomputes the store's `open_alert_count` and `floor_posture`, and increments the alert's, the card's and the store's `version`.

The two resolutions have deliberately disjoint side-effect sets. **Confirming** is the head of a three-hop cascade. Hop one moves the alert `TRIAGED → CONFIRMED` and records the resolution. Hop two follows from the alert being confirmed: the card's `risk_posture` is forced to `FROZEN`, from whatever rung it was on — `CLEAR → FROZEN`, `WATCH → FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED → FROZEN`. Hop three follows from the card being frozen: every reseller listing on that card in state `VALIDATED` transitions to `BLOCKED` with a `block_reason` of `CARD_RISK_POSTURE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each such listing's `version` increments. `CONFIRMED` is the sole terminal state of this lifecycle.

**Dismissing** touches no listing whatsoever. It moves the alert `TRIAGED → DISMISSED`, and it lowers the card's `risk_posture` to `CLEAR` — from `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` alike — but only when no *other* alert on that same card remains in state `OPEN` or `TRIAGED`. When another live alert does remain, the posture is left exactly as it was. This is one of only two mechanisms in the system that can lower a posture, the other being the manual override of §3.6.

A dismissal is not final. A `RiskManager` may reopen a `DISMISSED` alert when new evidence arrives, supplying a `reopen_reason`. The alert returns `DISMISSED → OPEN`, its `resolution`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note` are all cleared back to empty, and `reopen_count` increments by 1. Crucially, `triaged_by_user_id` is **preserved** across the reopen, so segregation of duties still binds the same original triager on the next resolution attempt. The store's `open_alert_count` and `floor_posture` are recomputed, and both the alert's and the store's `version` increment. The cycle from `DISMISSED` through `OPEN` and `TRIAGED` back to `DISMISSED` may repeat indefinitely; only `CONFIRMED` closes the file for good. For the purposes of the store floor, note that a dismissed alert stops counting immediately and a reopened one starts counting again.

Once an alert is `CONFIRMED`, a `FraudAnalyst` or a `RiskManager` may package it for law enforcement, naming the `alert_id`, the receiving `agency_name` (1 to 120 characters), a `packet_summary` narrative of 1 to 2000 characters transmitted with the packet, and optionally a `case_reference` when the agency has already assigned a case number. Filing against an alert in any state other than `CONFIRMED` is refused with `ERR_ALERT_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The referral records `referred_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, stamps `referred_at` from the logical clock, and takes `exposure_snapshot_cents` as a **second, independent** copy of the alert's `exposure_cents` — copied rather than joined, so that the packet remains a standalone immutable record even if anything about the alert were later to change. Referrals carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update, delete or state-transition operation exists for them anywhere.

### 3.8. Secondary-Marketplace Listings and Card Validation

Third-party resellers move enormous volumes of gift cards, and a drained card resold at a discount transfers the loss to a second innocent party. A `ResellerPartner` submits a listing naming the `card_id` being offered, the `marketplace_name` it is listed on (1 to 80 characters), the `asking_price_cents` being sought, and the `listed_balance_cents` the reseller claims the card carries. The listing is created in state `SUBMITTED`, with `reseller_partner_id` recorded from the submitting partner's `X-User-ID` — an opaque operator identifier and explicitly not a foreign key to any entity — and with `block_reason` empty. A submission is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING` when a listing in state `SUBMITTED` or `VALIDATED` already exists for the same combination of card and marketplace name; a previously rejected or blocked listing does not block a fresh attempt. Notably, a `ResellerPartner` may submit and read listings and may probe card balances, but may **not** validate a listing — validation is the retailer's control, not the reseller's.

Validation is exclusively a `FraudAnalyst` operation, is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`, accepts an optional `validation_note`, and records `validated_by_user_id` from the caller. It applies four checks against the live card **in this fixed order, with the first match deciding**, so a listing failing several checks reports only the first reason:

1. If the card's funding state is anything other than `ACTIVE`, the listing becomes `REJECTED` with `block_reason` `CARD_NOT_ACTIVE`.
2. Otherwise, if the card's `risk_posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, the listing becomes `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `CARD_RISK_POSTURE`.
3. Otherwise, if the listing's `listed_balance_cents` is not exactly equal to the card's `current_balance_cents`, the listing becomes `REJECTED` with `block_reason` `BALANCE_MISMATCH`.
4. Otherwise, if the card's `drain_risk_score_inquiry` is at or above `drain_restrict_score_threshold`, the listing becomes `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `DRAIN_SCORE`.
5. Otherwise the listing becomes `VALIDATED` and `block_reason` stays empty.

The distinction the two verdicts draw is meaningful: `REJECTED` means the listing is wrong about the card, while `BLOCKED` means the card itself is not safe to resell. That is precisely why the ordering matters — a frozen card quoted at a stale balance reports `CARD_RISK_POSTURE` from check two, not `BALANCE_MISMATCH` from check three, and a `DEPLETED` card honestly listed at a zero balance still reports `CARD_NOT_ACTIVE` from check one. A posture of `CLEAR` or `WATCH` passes check two, so a watched card with a matching balance and a sub-threshold inquiry score validates — `WATCH` is informational, not blocking. The score comparison is inclusive: an inquiry score exactly equal to the restrict threshold blocks, one point below it validates. The balance comparison is exact with no tolerance whatsoever — a card whose balance moved by a single cent since the listing was drafted rejects. Every validation increments the listing's `version`.

`REJECTED` and `BLOCKED` are terminal. `VALIDATED` is not: it is the one state the alert-confirmation cascade of §3.7 and the manual posture override of §3.6 can later drive to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `CARD_RISK_POSTURE`, whenever the underlying card reaches `FROZEN`. A validated listing is *not* automatically re-validated when the card's balance later drifts — only a posture escalation to `FROZEN` cascades.

### 3.9. Victim Claims, the Evidence Cycle and Card Reissue

When a customer discovers that the card they bought was drained before they could spend it, they file a claim. A `PosAssociate` or a `FraudAnalyst` takes the intake, naming the drained `card_id`, the `claimant_name` (1 to 120 characters), a `claimant_contact` phone or email (1 to 160 characters), the `disputed_amount_cents` the claimant asserts was drained (at least 1), and `purchased_at`, the moment they bought the card. An `alert_id` may optionally be attached when a draining alert already surfaced the same card. Evidence references — `purchase_receipt_ref` for the till receipt and `packaging_photo_ref` for a photograph of the tamper-evident packaging — may be supplied at intake or left empty.

`purchased_at` starts the filing window: intake is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` when the elapsed time from `purchased_at` to the logical now exceeds `claim_window_days`. The boundary is inclusive, so a claim filed exactly `claim_window_days` after purchase is accepted and one filed a moment later is not. A successful intake creates the claim in state `SUBMITTED` with `evidence_round` at 0, `missing_evidence_kinds` empty, `intake_user_id` recorded from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and every money and decision field — `unauthorized_line_count`, `gross_reissue_cents`, `reissue_amount_cents`, `residual_transfer_cents`, `reissued_card_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `rescind_reason` — left empty or zero. Deliberately, **evidence completeness is not checked at intake**: a claim may be filed with no evidence at all. Completeness is checked only at approval, which is precisely what makes the evidence cycle worth having.

That cycle is a two-legged loop. On the outbound leg a `FraudAnalyst` bounces the claim back for what is missing, supplying `missing_evidence_kinds`; the claim moves `SUBMITTED → EVIDENCE_REQUESTED`, `evidence_round` increments by 1, the requested kinds are recorded, and `version` increments. On the inbound leg a `PosAssociate` or a `FraudAnalyst` returns the claim to review with fresh evidence, supplying either or both of `purchase_receipt_ref` and `packaging_photo_ref`; the claim moves `EVIDENCE_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and **only the references actually supplied are overwritten** — an omitted reference retains whatever it already held rather than being cleared. Critically, resubmission does **not** reset `evidence_round`. That counter only ever climbs, and it is what bounds the loop: a further evidence request is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` once `evidence_round` has already reached `claim_evidence_round_limit`. A claim can therefore ping-pong between the two states at most `claim_evidence_round_limit` times, and one that has exhausted its rounds is stuck in `SUBMITTED` and must be decided on the evidence in hand.

Deciding is exclusively a `RiskManager` operation, is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`, and takes a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `decision_note`. Segregation of duties binds here as it does on alert resolution: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `intake_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The person who took the customer's story cannot be the person who authorises the money.

An approval must additionally clear the evidence gate. A receipt reference is mandatory for every claim; a packaging photo reference is additionally mandatory when the referenced card's `channel` is `PHYSICAL`, because a digital card has no packaging to photograph. A missing required reference is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`. A `DIGITAL` card therefore approves on a receipt alone, while a `PHYSICAL` card with a receipt but no packaging photo does not.

The recovery arithmetic then runs, and every figure it produces is frozen onto the claim at decision time and never recomputed. First the **unauthorized line set** is identified: those ledger events on the claim's card whose `type` is `REDEMPTION`, whose `occurred_at` is at or after `purchased_at`, and which have not been reversed by any later event pointing at them through `reverses_event_id`. Reversed redemptions are excluded because the cardholder already got that money back; redemptions predating the purchase are excluded because they cannot belong to this claimant. A redemption whose `occurred_at` falls exactly on `purchased_at` **is** included. The count of that set becomes `unauthorized_line_count`.

Second, the recovery factor is applied. `recovery_factor_bps` is applied to **each line individually**, and each line's result is rounded to whole cents half-up **before** the addition; `gross_reissue_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-line figures. Rounding the sum once instead is a different number in general, and the per-line order is normative: three lines of 1010, 1030 and 1050 cents at a recovery factor of 8500 basis points give 859 plus 876 plus 893, or 2628, whereas summing first and rounding once would give 2627.

Third, the cap is tested before any other clamping: an approval whose `gross_reissue_cents` exceeds `reissue_cap_cents` is refused with `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, nothing at all is written, and the claim stays in `SUBMITTED`. The comparison is strict, so a gross landing exactly on the cap is payable while one cent above it is not.

Fourth, the claimant ceiling applies: `reissue_amount_cents` is the smaller of `gross_reissue_cents` and `disputed_amount_cents`. A claimant is never reissued more than they themselves disputed, and when the ceiling bites, `gross_reissue_cents` still retains the uncapped figure for audit. Fifth, `residual_transfer_cents` takes whatever is still live on the compromised card — its `current_balance_cents` at that moment — so that value is moved rather than duplicated. A claim with zero unauthorized lines yields a count of 0, a gross of 0 and a payout of 0, and the replacement card is still minted carrying the residual alone.

Approval is then the head of a four-hop cascade. Hop one freezes `unauthorized_line_count`, `gross_reissue_cents`, `reissue_amount_cents` and `residual_transfer_cents` onto the claim and records `decided_by_user_id`. Hop two follows from the claim being approved: a replacement gift card is minted, inheriting the original card's `channel` and `store_id`, taking no `rack_id` at all, carrying `reissued_from_card_id` pointed at the original, and created directly in `ACTIVE` — it is never stocked and never activated through the ordinary route. A ledger event of `type` `REISSUE_CREDIT` is appended on it for `reissue_amount_cents` plus `residual_transfer_cents`, and the claim's `reissued_card_id` is set. Hop three follows from the replacement now holding the transferred residual: the original card transitions to `CLOSED` — from `ACTIVE` or from `DEPLETED` alike — its `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and a `REISSUE_DEBIT` event for `residual_transfer_cents` is appended **only when that residual is above zero**, so a card already at a zero balance closes with no ledger event whatsoever and its projected balance simply stays at 0. Hop four follows from the original card having been written off: the `OPEN` liability period whose span contains the logical now has its `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` incremented by `reissue_amount_cents`, and that hop is silently skipped when no open period covers the current instant. The claim's `version`, both cards' `version` values and the period's `version` each increment by 1.

A denial takes the disjoint path. The claim moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` are recorded, every money field stays empty, no replacement card is minted, no ledger event is appended, and no liability period is touched. `DENIED` is terminal.

An approval can be undone. A `RiskManager` or a `LiabilityAccountant` may rescind an `APPROVED` claim with a mandatory `rescind_reason`, and the rescission is an exact compensation that unwinds all four hops. The replacement card transitions `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, gaining a `REISSUE_CREDIT_REVERSAL` event for `reissue_amount_cents` plus `residual_transfer_cents` whose `reverses_event_id` names the original `REISSUE_CREDIT`, having its `risk_posture` forced to `FROZEN` and its `closed_at` stamped — a voided replacement is quarantined, not merely emptied. The original card returns `CLOSED → ACTIVE`, its `closed_at` cleared back to empty, gaining a `REISSUE_DEBIT_REVERSAL` event for `residual_transfer_cents` whose `reverses_event_id` names the original `REISSUE_DEBIT`, again only when that residual was above zero. The `OPEN` period containing the logical now has `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` **decremented** by `reissue_amount_cents`. The claim moves `APPROVED → RESCINDED`, records the reason, and **retains** `reissue_amount_cents`, `residual_transfer_cents` and `reissued_card_id` for audit rather than clearing them. The claim's, both cards' and the period's `version` each increment. `RESCINDED` is terminal — a rescinded claim cannot be re-approved, and a fresh claim must be filed instead.

### 3.10. Balance-Liability Accounting and Unauthorized-Redemption Reserves

Outstanding gift-card balances are a real liability on the retailer's books, and suspected fraud against them has to be reserved against before it is proven. A `LiabilityAccountant` opens an accounting period with a `label` (1 to 40 characters, unique across all periods), a `period_start` and a `period_end` that must fall strictly after it. The period occupies the span as a **half-open interval** — the start instant belongs to the period, the end instant does not — with **exclusive occupancy**: no two periods may overlap. Two periods overlap when the candidate's start falls strictly before the existing period's end **and** the existing period's start falls strictly before the candidate's end. Both comparisons being strict makes **adjacency legal**: a period ending at one instant and another beginning at exactly that same instant do not overlap, and both may exist. A candidate that fully contains an existing period, one fully contained inside an existing period, and one overlapping by a single microsecond are all refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, and periods in **every** state participate in the check — closing or restating a period does not release its calendar span. Well-formedness is tested at a lower priority than occupancy, so a period whose end equals its start is an invalid request rather than an overlap. This is the deliberate opposite of the redemption-hold intervals of §3.5, whose closed intervals treat a shared instant as a conflict but tolerate genuine overlap under an aggregate ceiling.

A new period is created in `OPEN` with `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` at 0, `restatement_count` at 0, and `outstanding_liability_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents`, `reserve_basis_alert_count`, `provisional_reserve_cents`, `final_reserve_cents`, `closed_at`, `restated_at`, `close_note` and `restatement_note` all empty. While a period is `OPEN` it accumulates nothing except writeoffs: `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` is the running total of `reissue_amount_cents` credited by approved victim claims whose approval landed inside its span, decremented again when such a claim is rescinded, and it is the **only** field the claim cascade of §3.9 ever touches on a period. It is maintained incrementally rather than recomputed, and it is never touched on a closed or restated period.

Closing is a `LiabilityAccountant` operation admitted only from `OPEN`, taking an optional `close_note`. It stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock and books the **provisional** reserve on a prudent basis — the assumption that every alert still under investigation might turn out to be real. The basis is the set of draining alerts whose `created_at` falls inside the period's half-open span and whose state is `OPEN`, `TRIAGED` or `CONFIRMED`. `gross_exposure_cents` is the sum of `exposure_cents` over that basis, `reserve_basis_alert_count` is its cardinality, and `provisional_reserve_cents` is `provisional_reserve_rate_bps` applied to `gross_exposure_cents`, rounded half-up **once over the aggregate** rather than per alert — an aggregate exposure of 60011 at 3500 basis points gives 21004, not the sum of per-alert roundings. The in-period test is half-open in the same sense as the period itself: an alert created exactly at `period_start` belongs to this period, and one created exactly at `period_end` belongs to the next, so adjacent periods partition time with no alert counted twice.

Closing also refreshes `outstanding_liability_cents`, which is alert-independent: the sum of `current_balance_cents` over every gift card whose `activation_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DEPLETED`. Voided and closed cards are excluded because they can no longer be redeemed, and inactive cards are excluded because they were never funded. A period with no in-period alerts closes with a gross exposure of 0, a basis count of 0 and a reserve of 0 — zero, not empty, and certainly not an error.

Restating is the same role's operation, admitted from `CLOSED` and again from `RESTATED` as a self-loop, and it is the second place a stale-version guard applies: the supplied `expected_liability_period_version` must equal the period's current `version` or the restatement is refused with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION`, as described in §2.7. It requires a `restatement_note`, stamps `restated_at`, and increments `restatement_count` by 1. It books the **final** reserve on the settled basis that only proven fraud is reservable: the basis narrows to in-period alerts whose state is exactly `CONFIRMED`, and `final_reserve_cents` is `final_reserve_rate_bps` applied to the recomputed `gross_exposure_cents`, rounded half-up once over the aggregate.

The two reserve variants differ on **both** axes simultaneously — the provisional one pairs a wider alert-state filter with a higher rate, the final one pairs a narrower filter with a lower rate — so the final reserve typically lands far below the provisional one and neither can be derived from the other by any single scale factor. A restatement **overwrites** `gross_exposure_cents`, `reserve_basis_alert_count` and `outstanding_liability_cents`, but it **never** overwrites `provisional_reserve_cents`, so both bases remain readable side by side on the same period forever. A dismissed alert is excluded from both bases, so dismissing an alert between a close and a restatement shrinks the basis twice over; and restating a period whose alerts are all still `OPEN` yields a final reserve of 0 while the provisional figure stays positive. Note also that `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` participates in neither reserve. Every restatement increments `version` by 1, which means a client intending two restatements in a row must re-read the period between them. There is no terminal state in this lifecycle at all — a period may be restated indefinitely.

Finally, a computed read available to a `RiskManager`, a `LiabilityAccountant` or a `PlatformAdmin` exposes the system's conserved quantity for a chosen period. It reports `net_funded_liability_cents`, computed live as the sum of `current_balance_cents` over cards whose `activation_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DEPLETED`, minus that period's `unauthorized_writeoff_cents`; `funded_card_count`, the number of cards in that same funded population; `in_period_alert_count`, the number of alerts created inside the period's half-open span in any state; and `confirmed_alert_count`, how many of those are `CONFIRMED`. Alongside these it echoes the period's stored `unauthorized_writeoff_cents`, `outstanding_liability_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents`, `provisional_reserve_cents` and `final_reserve_cents`. The read stores nothing and increments no `version`.

`net_funded_liability_cents` is conserved in the strict sense that **every compensating pair in the system leaves it unchanged**. A redemption followed by its reversal restores the card's balance exactly, so the sum returns to its prior value while the writeoff term is untouched. A claim approval moves `residual_transfer_cents` from the original card to the replacement (net zero on the sum), adds `reissue_amount_cents` to the replacement card, and adds that same `reissue_amount_cents` to `unauthorized_writeoff_cents` — the two additions cancel in the subtraction. The matching rescission reverses both legs and restores the pre-approval value precisely. Any drift in either leg is therefore detectable as a change in one scalar. With no cards and no periods the summary reports 0; a voided replacement card contributes 0 because voided is outside the funded set; an inactive card contributes 0 despite carrying a face value, because it was never funded; and a depleted card contributes 0 to the sum yet is still counted as funded, since its balance is genuinely zero rather than excluded. This conservation is entirely independent of the reserve arithmetic above, which conserves nothing and is a pure function of alert exposure.

The table below is the complete surface of the service. Endpoints that create a new resource respond with `201 Created`; all other successful requests, including state transitions, settlements, decisions, overrides, the policy update and the clock advance, respond with `200 OK`.
