# Air Traffic Flow Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The Federal Aviation Administration's traffic flow management function exists to keep demand safely below capacity whenever the National Airspace System cannot absorb the scheduled flow of traffic. Convective weather parked over a major arrival airport, a staffing shortfall in an en route sector, an unplanned equipment outage — each erodes the number of flights a facility can accept per hour, and somebody must decide, quickly and defensibly, which flights wait on the ground, which are rerouted, and for how long. This document specifies a REST API for administering that decision process end to end: forecasting degraded acceptance rates from reported constraint events, selecting and operating traffic management initiatives (TMIs) across ground delay programs, ground stops, airspace flow programs and reroutes, assigning arrival slots to flights in original schedule order with delay distributed across operators, and recovering unused capacity when flights cancel.

The API is built around collaborative decision making. Airline operations centers and general aviation operators file and maintain their own flights, exchange estimated arrival times with the FAA under explicit data-freshness requirements, substitute slots among their own flights inside a running program, and petition for accommodation out of a reserved general aviation pool, while FAA flow managers and command center administrators propose, publish, activate, revise, cancel and complete the programs themselves under a strict segregation of duties. Every delay-affecting mutation is written to an append-only slot transaction log and reconciled into per-operator delay ledgers, so the cost of a program — measured in attributed delay minutes — is always auditable and is apportioned across operators by a deterministic fair-share rule. Compression runs reclaim the slots of cancelled flights and move surviving flights earlier; general aviation requests waitlisted for lack of quota are promoted out of the same reclaimed pool.

All time-based behavior in the system reads a logical clock rather than the wall clock; before any explicit set operation the logical clock reads exactly 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z and never falls back to wall-clock time. The API surface is mounted under the `/atfm` prefix, with a single unprefixed administrative endpoint for driving that clock. This document is the sole normative specification of the service: §2 defines the system-wide contracts, §3 the business workflows, §4 the endpoint table, and §5 the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two identity headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the role the principal acts in. When either header is missing, or when `X-User-Role` names a value outside the declared role set, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the role is declared but the endpoint does not permit that role, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Exactly four roles exist, spelled precisely as follows:

- **`SystemAdministrator`** — the FAA system operator. It registers facilities and operators, maintains facility status, and drives the logical clock. Its exclusive operations are `POST /atfm/facilities`, `PATCH /atfm/facilities/{id}`, `POST /atfm/operators`, and `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- **`FlowManager`** — an FAA traffic management specialist. It reports, confirms, extends and clears constraint events, computes and publishes capacity forecasts, proposes, activates and revises traffic management initiatives, runs compression, adjudicates general aviation accommodation requests, and assigns reroutes. Its exclusive operations are `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/constraint-events`, `POST /atfm/constraint-events/{id}/confirm`, `POST /atfm/constraint-events/{id}/clear`, `POST /atfm/constraint-events/{id}/extend`, `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/capacity-forecasts`, `POST /atfm/capacity-forecasts/{id}/publish`, `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/tmis`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/activate`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/revisions`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/compression-runs`, `POST /atfm/ga-requests/{id}/adjudicate`, and `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/reroute-assignments`. When `X-User-Role` is `FlowManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow requires (for example, as `proposed_by_user_id` on a TrafficManagementInitiative) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- **`CommandCenterAdmin`** — the ATCSCC command center. It publishes, cancels and completes traffic management initiatives. Its exclusive operations are `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/publish`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/cancel`, and `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/complete`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommandCenterAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `published_by_user_id` on publication but does not compare it to any entity's `id` other than through the segregation-of-duties rule of §3.4.
- **`Operator`** — an airline operations center or a general aviation / unscheduled operator. It files, updates and cancels its own flights, substitutes slots between two of its own flights inside a ground delay program, and requests GA accommodation. Its exclusive operations are `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/flights`, `POST /atfm/flights/{id}/updates`, `POST /atfm/flights/{id}/cancel`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/substitutions`, and `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/ga-requests`. When `X-User-Role` is `Operator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Operator; ownership checks on any resource that references an Operator compare the resource's operator foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch — a path operator id that differs from `X-User-ID`, a flight whose `operator_id` differs, or a substitution body referencing any flight owned by a different operator — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

All four roles additionally share every read endpoint: `GET /atfm/facilities/{id}`, `GET /atfm/operators/{id}`, `GET /atfm/facilities/{id}/constraint-events`, `GET /atfm/facilities/{id}/capacity-forecasts`, `GET /atfm/facilities/{id}/tmis`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/revisions`, `GET /atfm/flights`, `GET /atfm/flights/{id}`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/slots`, `GET /atfm/flights/{id}/slots`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/compression-runs`, `GET /atfm/operators/{id}/ga-requests`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/ga-requests`, `GET /atfm/reroute-assignments`, `GET /atfm/reroute-assignments/{id}`, `GET /atfm/operators/{id}/delay-ledgers`, `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/delay-ledgers`, and `GET /atfm/tmis/{id}/slot-transactions`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100; a `limit` outside that range is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` defaults to 0. Every list response carries the pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside its `meta` object. Unless a more specific ordering is stated for a particular list in §3, list endpoints return resources sorted by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every primary resource carries four system-maintained fields. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation. `updated_at` uses the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the resource. `version` is an integer that increments by exactly one on every mutation; clients read it and echo it back on the optimistic-concurrency-guarded writes described in §2.7.

Four record-type resources are exceptions to this rule: a TMIRevision, a SlotTransaction, a CompressionRun, and a RerouteAssignment each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from these four types — they are append-only records, never mutated after creation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a single logical clock that every temporal rule in this document reads: window-in-the-past checks, substitution data freshness, reroute hourly bucketing, and the workflow-stamped instants `published_at`, `activated_at`, `applied_at`, and `last_eta_reported_at` all consume it. Before any client has set it, the logical clock reads exactly 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time. A `SystemAdministrator` advances it through `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` — an administrative endpoint mounted outside the `/atfm` prefix — supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`. A successful set returns HTTP 200 with the new logical time echoed as `current_utc` in the response data.

### 2.6. Request Evaluation Order and Validation Priority

Independently of the ordered list below, a structurally malformed request body — a missing required field, an unknown enum value, or a value of the wrong type — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Otherwise, when several validations could fail on the same request, the server evaluates them in the following priority order and reports the first failure only:

1. **Authentication.** The `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` headers are present and the role is a declared role; failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The declared role is permitted on this endpoint per §2.1; failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Entity-backed ownership.** When `X-User-Role` is `Operator`, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning operator id — the path operator id, the flight's `operator_id`, or the operator of every flight referenced in the body; failure yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Applies to `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/flights`, `POST /atfm/flights/{id}/updates`, `POST /atfm/flights/{id}/cancel`, `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/ga-requests`, and `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/substitutions`.
4. **Existence.** Path-template ids resolve left to right — facility before tmi, forecast or event; operator before flight; then the GA request — and body foreign-key references (`arrival_facility_id`, `tmi_id`, `flight_id_1`, `flight_id_2`, `flight_id`, `via_sector_id`) resolve. Failure yields the specific not-found error: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TMI_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSTRAINT_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GA_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_REROUTE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` (all HTTP 404).
5. **State preconditions.** The target sits in a legal from-state for the requested transition: publishing needs a `PROPOSED` TMI, activation needs `PUBLISHED` or `REVISING`, substitutions, compression runs, GA adjudication and reroute assignment need an `ACTIVE` TMI (otherwise `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409), any action on a `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` TMI yields `ERR_TMI_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), constraint-event transitions start from a legal from-state, flight operations follow `SCHEDULED` to `AIRBORNE` to `LANDED`, and the facility must not be in `OUTAGE` for activation or reroute assignment (`ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, HTTP 409). The generic wrong-from-state failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
6. **Segregation of duties.** On publication, the publisher's `X-User-ID` must differ from the TMI's `proposed_by_user_id`; failure yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). Applies only to `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/publish`.
7. **Reroute sector capacity.** The count of existing reroute assignments for the same `via_sector_id` and hourly bucket must remain below the sector's `base_acceptance_rate`; failure yields `ERR_SECTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Applies only to `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/reroute-assignments`.
8. **Window well-formedness.** Every windowed create or extend requires start strictly before end; failure yields `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/constraint-events`, `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/capacity-forecasts`, `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/tmis`, `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/revisions`, `POST /atfm/constraint-events/{id}/extend`, and `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/ga-requests`.
9. **Program window in the future.** A TMI's `program_end` must be strictly after the current logical clock at propose time, and `new_program_end` strictly after it at revision time; failure yields `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/tmis` and `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/revisions`.
10. **GA window containment.** A GA accommodation request's window must lie entirely inside the target program window; failure yields `ERR_GA_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM` (HTTP 422). Applies only to `POST /atfm/operators/{id}/ga-requests`.
11. **Substitution data freshness.** Both substitution flights' `last_eta_reported_at` must lie within `FLIGHT_DATA_FRESHNESS_SECONDS` (3600 seconds) of the current logical clock; failure yields `ERR_STALE_FLIGHT_DATA` (HTTP 422). Applies only to `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/substitutions`.
12. **Facility ident uniqueness.** The `ident` must be unique across all facilities; failure yields `ERR_FACILITY_IDENT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Applies only to `POST /atfm/facilities`.
13. **Constraint-event overlap.** No other non-`CLEARED` event of the same `event_type` on the same facility may overlap the half-open window; adjacency — the new start equalling an existing end — is allowed. Failure yields `ERR_CONSTRAINT_EVENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Applies to `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/constraint-events` and `POST /atfm/constraint-events/{id}/extend`.
14. **GDP-family exclusivity.** No other `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` or `GROUND_STOP` TMI on the same facility with status in `PROPOSED`, `PUBLISHED`, `ACTIVE` or `REVISING` may overlap the candidate's half-open program window; `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` and `REROUTE` initiatives are exempt from this hard rule and interact through aggregate capacity instead. Failure yields `ERR_TMI_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Checked at proposal and re-checked at activation, since another program may have activated in between.
15. **Rate format.** A ground delay program's `controlled_rate` must be an integer between 6 and 60 inclusive such that 3600 is exactly divisible by it; an airspace flow program's must be an integer from 1 to 60 inclusive; a ground stop's must be 0; a reroute's is ignored and stored as 0. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_RATE` (HTTP 422). Applies to `POST /atfm/facilities/{id}/tmis` and `POST /atfm/tmis/{id}/revisions`.
16. **Referenced facility and TMI types.** A flight's `arrival_facility_id` must reference a facility of type `AIRPORT` (failure yields `ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_NOT_AIRPORT`, HTTP 422); a reroute's `via_sector_id` must reference an `EN_ROUTE_SECTOR` (failure yields `ERR_REROUTE_SECTOR_TYPE`, HTTP 422); a GA request's `tmi_id` must reference a `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` (failure yields `ERR_TMI_TYPE_NOT_GDP`, HTTP 422).
17. **Operator-type eligibility.** GA accommodation requests are accepted only from operators of type `GENERAL_AVIATION` or `UNSCHEDULED_CHARTER`; failure yields `ERR_OPERATOR_TYPE_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).
18. **Substitution shape.** Evaluated in this sub-order: first `flight_id_1` must differ from `flight_id_2` (`ERR_SUBSTITUTION_SAME_FLIGHT`, HTTP 422), then both flights must hold `ASSIGNED` slots in this TMI (`ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_IN_PROGRAM`, HTTP 422), then for each flight the absolute shift between the other flight's slot time and this flight's `scheduled_arrival` must not exceed `SUBSTITUTION_MAX_SHIFT_MINUTES` (120 minutes) (`ERR_SUBSTITUTION_SHIFT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422).
19. **Extension cap.** A constraint event's `extension_count` must be below `CONSTRAINT_EVENT_MAX_EXTENSIONS` (2) for another extension to be accepted; failure yields `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
20. **Capacity gate.** The `controlled_rate` must not exceed the maximum feasible rate for the facility and window, recomputed at gate time, for `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` and `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` initiatives; `GROUND_STOP` and `REROUTE` are exempt. Failure yields `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY` (HTTP 422). This gate binds at publish, activate, and manual revision — never at proposal, because the supporting forecast may not exist yet.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control; no other endpoint accepts a version field.

- Creating a revision requires the request to carry `expected_tmi_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the TrafficManagementInitiative. If the TMI's current `version` has advanced past the supplied value — because another writer has mutated the initiative since the client last read it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so the client re-reads the moved baseline instead of chaining a revision onto a state it never observed.
- Submitting a flight data-exchange update requires `expected_flight_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the Flight. A mismatch with the flight's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). ETA and ops updates are otherwise always legal on non-terminal flights and never change a state field that would reject a later update, so the stale-version check is the only correct rejection for a moved baseline — note that a cascaded slot mutation may itself have advanced the flight's `version` between the client's read and its write.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Acceptance rates are whole flights per hour: every rate computation floors after multiplication, and because the capacity multiplier is a product of per-event factors, multiplication is commutative and the order in which events entered the product is irrelevant. Delays are whole minutes, always computed as the floor of a second-level difference divided by 60 — provisional delay, self-inflicted delay, final delay, and the signed `delay_delta_minutes` on transactions all follow this rule. Slot times are exact to the second: the interval between consecutive slots of a ground delay program is exactly 3600 divided by the `controlled_rate` seconds, and rate validity guarantees that division leaves no remainder. Fair-share distribution works in integer minutes with a fixed rounding order — each operator's raw share is floored first, and the leftover minutes are then distributed one at a time by largest fractional remainder, never by rounding each share to the nearest integer. Sector load for reroutes is an integer count per hourly bucket. All timestamps exchanged with the API are ISO 8601 UTC; system-maintained `created_at` and `updated_at` carry three fractional digits, and datetime inputs are compared to the second.

### 2.9. Windows, Overlap, and Adjacency

Every window in the system — constraint-event degradation windows, forecast windows, program windows, GA request windows — is half-open: the start instant is included and the end instant is excluded. Two windows overlap when they share at least one instant under this convention; two windows that merely touch, where one window's start equals the other's end, are adjacent and do not overlap. Every windowed create or extend requires the start to be strictly before the end, failing with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422), and an extension that does not move an event's `window_end` forward fails the same way. Separately, a TMI's `program_end` must lie strictly after the current logical clock at proposal time — and a revision's `new_program_end` strictly after it at revision time — failing with `ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. Cascades, Consistency, and Path Resolution

The consistency model is immediate: every mutation a request triggers, including the cascades it fires into other entities, is visible in that same request's response and to every subsequent read. Each mutated entity's `version` increments by exactly one and its `updated_at` refreshes. Cascades in this domain form explicit chains — a constraint-event confirmation staleness every overlapping published forecast, which in turn can auto-downgrade an active ground delay program into `REVISING`; a program cancellation releases slots, reverts flights, reverses ledgers, and expires GA requests in one request; a flight cancellation releases its slot and reconciles its operator's ledger. §3 specifies each chain at its source.

Path identifiers on nested routes resolve left to right — the facility before the TMI, forecast, or event beneath it; the operator before the flight; the GA request after its own id — and the first unresolvable id produces the corresponding 404 error from the catalogue before any business rule is consulted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Data: Facilities and Operators

Everything in the system hangs off two pieces of standing data. A **Facility** is an airport or an en route sector, registered by a `SystemAdministrator` with its `ident` (a public identifier such as KEWR or ZDC42), its `facility_type` (exactly one of `AIRPORT` or `EN_ROUTE_SECTOR`), and its `base_acceptance_rate` — the unconstrained capacity in flights per hour, an integer from 1 to 120 inclusive. The `ident` is unique across all facilities: a duplicate registration is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_IDENT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A facility is born with `status` `OPERATIONAL`; a `SystemAdministrator` may later patch its `base_acceptance_rate` and/or `status`, moving the status freely among `OPERATIONAL`, `DEGRADED`, and `OUTAGE` in either direction — any other status value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — and each patch bumps the facility's `version`. `OUTAGE` is a hard gate downstream: it blocks TMI activation and reroute assignments into the facility with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (HTTP 409), though it forces no cascade by itself. `DEGRADED` is purely informational.

An **Operator** is an airline operations center, a general aviation operator, or an unscheduled charter, registered by a `SystemAdministrator` with a display `name` (deliberately not unique), an `operator_type` of `SCHEDULED_CARRIER`, `GENERAL_AVIATION`, or `UNSCHEDULED_CHARTER`, and an optional `cdm_member` flag (defaulting to false) recording participation in collaborative decision making data exchange. The operator-type matters downstream: only `GENERAL_AVIATION` and `UNSCHEDULED_CHARTER` operators may request GA accommodation; a request from a `SCHEDULED_CARRIER` is rejected with `ERR_OPERATOR_TYPE_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Each operator also carries `cumulative_delay_minutes`, a computed integer starting at 0 that accumulates the operator's FINAL attributed delay minutes across all `COMPLETED` programs, is adjusted by compensation reversals when a program is cancelled, and is never decremented by provisional activity while a program runs.

### 3.2. Constraint Events: From Report to Clearance

When weather, staffing, or equipment degrades a facility, a `FlowManager` reports a **ConstraintEvent** against it — the event records its subject facility as `facility_id`, a reference to that Facility's `id`, and the same `facility_id` linkage ties each CapacityForecast and TrafficManagementInitiative to the facility it covers — supplying an `event_type` of `WEATHER`, `STAFFING`, or `EQUIPMENT`, an integer `severity` from 1 to 5 inclusive where 5 is worst, and the degradation window `window_start` to `window_end` (UTC). The window must be well-formed — `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` — and it must not overlap any other non-`CLEARED` event of the same `event_type` on the same facility, where overlap is judged on half-open windows and mere adjacency is allowed; a conflicting report is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRAINT_EVENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A new event is born `REPORTED` with its `extension_count` at 0, and events on a facility can be listed with optional `status` and `event_type` filters.

Confirmation is the consequential act. Confirming a `REPORTED` event — from any other state the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — moves it to `CONFIRMED` and fires a two-link cascade. First, every `PUBLISHED` CapacityForecast on the same facility whose window overlaps the event's window turns `STALE`. Second, for each `ACTIVE` ground delay program on that facility whose window overlaps the event, the system recomputes the implied capacity: the facility's `base_acceptance_rate` multiplied by the product, over all `CONFIRMED` events overlapping the program window, of one minus that event's per-type step times its `severity` — with the multiplier never allowed below ten percent of base and the resulting rate floored to an integer with a minimum of 1. If that implied rate has collapsed below the program's `controlled_rate`, the program auto-transitions from `ACTIVE` to `REVISING` and the system writes a TMIRevision of origin `SYSTEM_AUTO_DOWNGRADE` whose `new_controlled_rate` is the largest valid GDP rate not exceeding the implied rate — or 6 when the implied rate is below 6.

An event's window can be extended while it is `REPORTED` or `CONFIRMED`; extending from any other state fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The extension supplies a new `window_end` strictly later than the current one (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`) and may optionally update `severity`. The same-type overlap rule is re-checked against the widened window (`ERR_CONSTRAINT_EVENT_OVERLAP`), and the hard cap `CONSTRAINT_EVENT_MAX_EXTENSIONS` — 2 extensions per event — is enforced through `extension_count`: a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Each accepted extension increments `extension_count` by 1. When the extended event is `CONFIRMED`, the same staleness and auto-downgrade cascades as confirmation fire again, because a confirmed event now covers a wider window.

Clearing is the terminal act, allowed from `REPORTED` or `CONFIRMED` and rejected from `CLEARED` with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Clearing moves the event to `CLEARED` and deliberately does nothing else: a forecast that went `STALE` stays `STALE`, and regaining a trustworthy capacity picture requires creating and publishing a fresh forecast.

### 3.3. Capacity Forecasting

Before publishing a capacity-limiting initiative, the FAA computes a **CapacityForecast** for the facility and window in question. A `FlowManager` requests one with a `variant` — `PLANNING` or `OPERATIONAL` — and a window `window_start` to `window_end`; the window must be well-formed. The computation runs once, at creation, and the result is immutable thereafter: the forecast stores the computed `predicted_acceptance_rate` and the `included_event_ids` — the ids of the ConstraintEvents that entered the computation — and is born `DRAFT` with `published_at` null.

The computation works as follows. The input set is the facility's constraint events whose half-open windows overlap the forecast window; `CLEARED` events are always excluded. Each included event contributes a factor of one minus the product of its per-type impact step, its `severity`, and its weight. The impact steps are `WEATHER_IMPACT_STEP` 0.15 for `WEATHER`, `STAFFING_IMPACT_STEP` 0.10 for `STAFFING`, and `EQUIPMENT_IMPACT_STEP` 0.05 for `EQUIPMENT`. The weight is 1.0 for a `CONFIRMED` event; a `REPORTED` event weighs in at `REPORTED_EVENT_WEIGHT` 0.5 — and only the `OPERATIONAL` variant admits `REPORTED` events at all. The two variants therefore differ exactly this way: `PLANNING` includes `CONFIRMED` events only, while `OPERATIONAL` additionally includes `REPORTED` events at half weight, so identical facility data yields a lower-or-equal predicted rate under `OPERATIONAL`. Multiplying all included events' factors together yields the raw multiplier; the effective multiplier is the raw multiplier but never below `MIN_CAPACITY_FLOOR_PCT` (10) percent, that is, never below 0.10. The `predicted_acceptance_rate` is then the facility's `base_acceptance_rate` times the effective multiplier, floored to a whole number of flights per hour, with a minimum of 1 — a facility never forecasts below one flight per hour.

Publishing moves a `DRAFT` forecast to `PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, and bumps its `version`; publishing from any other state fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Publication is what makes a forecast eligible as the gate for initiative publish and activate — and only the `PLANNING` variant is accepted by that gate. Forecasts for a facility can be listed with optional `status` and `variant` filters.

A published forecast does not stay trustworthy by itself. When an overlapping constraint event on the same facility is confirmed or extended, every `PUBLISHED` forecast whose window overlaps the event's window is automatically marked `STALE`, its `version` incrementing, and `STALE` is terminal — there is no path back to `PUBLISHED`. When several forecasts could serve a gate, the system selects the gate forecast deterministically: among `PUBLISHED`, non-`STALE`, `PLANNING`-variant forecasts covering the required window, the one with the latest `published_at` wins, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; when none exists the gate fails with `ERR_NO_CURRENT_FORECAST` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Traffic Management Initiatives: Proposal, Publication, Activation

A **TrafficManagementInitiative** begins when a `FlowManager` proposes one against a facility, choosing a `tmi_type` of `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM`, `GROUND_STOP`, `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM`, or `REROUTE`, a program window `program_start` to `program_end`, and — for the rate-bearing types — a `controlled_rate`. The window must be well-formed and `program_end` must lie strictly after the current logical clock (`ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_IN_PAST`). The rate's shape depends on the type: for a `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` it must be an integer from 6 to 60 inclusive and 3600 must be exactly divisible by it, so that slot spacing lands on whole seconds; for an `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` any integer from 1 to 60 inclusive; for a `GROUND_STOP` it must be exactly 0; for a `REROUTE` it is ignored and stored as 0. A rate violating its type's shape is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RATE` (HTTP 422); when omitted, `controlled_rate` defaults to 0. What proposal does not check is capacity: no forecast need exist yet, so the capacity gate deliberately binds only later, at publish and at activate. Proposal does enforce GDP-family exclusivity — no other `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` or `GROUND_STOP` on the same facility in `PROPOSED`, `PUBLISHED`, `ACTIVE`, or `REVISING` may overlap the half-open program window, else `ERR_TMI_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` and `REROUTE` candidates are exempt from this hard rule. A proposed TMI is born `PROPOSED`, records the proposer's `X-User-ID` as `proposed_by_user_id`, starts with `revision_count` 0 and `activated_at` null, and can be listed on its facility filtered by `status` and `tmi_type`.

Publication is the command center's act, and it is deliberately separated from proposal: the `CommandCenterAdmin` who publishes must not be the principal who proposed, so a `published_by_user_id` equal to `proposed_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). Publishing requires the TMI in `PROPOSED` — any other non-terminal state fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` initiative answers `ERR_TMI_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). For `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` and `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` initiatives, publication re-runs two gates; `GROUND_STOP` and `REROUTE` are exempt from both. The forecast gate requires a `PUBLISHED`, non-`STALE`, `PLANNING`-variant CapacityForecast covering the whole program window (else `ERR_NO_CURRENT_FORECAST`). The rate gate recomputes the maximum feasible rate now and requires `controlled_rate` not to exceed it (else `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`, HTTP 422).

The maximum feasible rate for a facility and window is derived as follows. If any `GROUND_STOP` initiative other than the candidate itself sits on the facility in `PUBLISHED`, `ACTIVE`, or `REVISING` with a window overlapping the candidate's, the answer is 0 — a live ground stop zeroes everything. Otherwise the gate forecast's `predicted_acceptance_rate` is taken, capped from above by the facility's `base_acceptance_rate`; from that, the aggregate load of every `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM` on the facility in `PUBLISHED`, `ACTIVE`, or `REVISING` overlapping the candidate window — summed `controlled_rate` values, excluding the candidate itself — is subtracted; the result is floored at 0. This is how overlapping airspace flow programs interact with one another and with ground delay programs: not through the exclusivity rule, but through this aggregate rate budget.

Activation is where a ground delay program becomes real. A `FlowManager` activates a `PUBLISHED` initiative; from `REVISING` the same act applies a pending revision instead (§3.6), and from any other state it fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The facility must not be in `OUTAGE` (`ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPERATIONAL`), the forecast and rate gates run again for the rate-bearing types, and GDP-family exclusivity is re-checked because another program may have activated since proposal (`ERR_TMI_OVERLAP`). For `GROUND_STOP`, `AIRSPACE_FLOW_PROGRAM`, and `REROUTE` initiatives activation is a status flip and nothing more; for a `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM` the full ration-by-schedule allocation runs.

Ration-by-schedule works positionally over generated **ArrivalSlot** rows. The slot interval is exactly 3600 divided by the `controlled_rate` seconds. The number of slots is the whole number of such intervals that fit in the program window, and slot with `slot_index` k — zero-based, ascending in `slot_time` — is placed at `program_start` plus k interval-lengths. A reserved tail is carved out for general aviation: the count of reserved slots is `GA_QUOTA_PCT` (10) percent of the slot count rounded down, but never fewer than `GA_MIN_RESERVED` (1), and the last reserved-count slots carry `is_ga_reserved` true and are skipped by the initial carrier allocation. The eligible flight set is snapshotted at this instant: flights arriving at the program's facility, in ops state `SCHEDULED` or `AIRBORNE`, still `UNCONTROLLED`, whose `scheduled_arrival` falls inside the half-open program window. Eligible flights are ranked by `scheduled_arrival` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — original schedule order — and the j-th flight in that order takes the j-th non-reserved slot ascending in `slot_time`. Each assigned flight flips from `UNCONTROLLED` to `SLOTTED`, receives the slot's `slot_time` as its `controlled_arrival`, has its `provisional_delay_minutes` recomputed as the whole-minute floor of its controlled-minus-scheduled difference (never below zero), and the slot records it in `assigned_flight_id` and — on this first assignment — in `original_flight_id` as an audit reference. Flights beyond the last non-reserved slot are overflow: each receives the program's `program_end` as a virtual `controlled_arrival` with no ArrivalSlot row, is flagged `is_overflow`, and stays `UNCONTROLLED`. An OperatorDelayLedger is created per operator with at least one eligible flight, and an `INITIAL_ASSIGNMENT` SlotTransaction is written per assigned or overflow flight — for initial assignments `from_slot_time` is null — before the initiative flips to `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, and increments its `version`.

The **SlotTransaction** is the system's append-only audit record. Each entry denormalizes its `tmi_id`, the affected `flight_id`, and the owning `operator_id` at write time; names a `transaction_type` of `INITIAL_ASSIGNMENT`, `SUBSTITUTION_SWAP`, `COMPRESSION_MOVE`, `RELEASE`, `GA_ASSIGNMENT`, `REVISION_MOVE`, or `REVERSAL`; records the controlled time before the event in `from_slot_time` (null for initial assignments) and after it in `to_slot_time` (null for releases and reversals); quantifies the signed change in the flight's attributed delay as `delay_delta_minutes`; and may carry an optional free-text `note`. The `slot_id` references the affected ArrivalSlot where one exists and is null for virtual overflow CTAs and for `REVERSAL` summaries. A program's transaction log is listed in chronological `created_at` order, filterable by `transaction_type`; a program's slots are listed in `slot_index` order, filterable by `status` — both listings are meaningful only for a `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM`, and attempting the slot listing against any other initiative type is rejected with `ERR_TMI_TYPE_NOT_GDP` (HTTP 422). A flight's own slot history — zero or one currently `ASSIGNED` slot plus the audit trail reachable through `original_flight_id` — is listed per flight.

### 3.5. Flights Under Control: Filing, Data Exchange, Substitution, Compression

An **Operator** files a **Flight** under its own operator id — the path operator must equal `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — supplying a `designator` such as UAL123, a free-text `origin`, the `arrival_facility_id` of the destination airport, and the original `scheduled_arrival`. The arrival facility must resolve and must be of type `AIRPORT`; pointing a flight at an `EN_ROUTE_SECTOR` fails with `ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_NOT_AIRPORT` (HTTP 422). `scheduled_arrival` is the OAG basis for ration-by-schedule and is immutable after filing. The operator may supply a current `estimated_arrival`; when omitted it defaults to `scheduled_arrival`. A filed flight is born with `ops_status` `SCHEDULED` and `control_status` `UNCONTROLLED` — two independent dimensions carried on the same row — with `controlled_arrival` null, `is_overflow` false, `provisional_delay_minutes` 0, `final_delay_minutes` null, and `last_eta_reported_at` stamped from the logical clock at filing. Flights are listed with optional `operator_id`, `arrival_facility_id`, `ops_status`, and `control_status` filters plus pagination.

The data-exchange update is the operator's channel for keeping the FAA's picture current, and it is optimistic-concurrency-guarded per §2.7 with `expected_flight_version`. An update may carry a `new_eta`, a `new_ops_status`, or both. Supplying `new_eta` alone is an ETA-only report: it refreshes `estimated_arrival` and `last_eta_reported_at` while leaving `ops_status` untouched, and it is legal while the flight is `SCHEDULED` or `AIRBORNE` — against a `LANDED` or `CANCELLED` flight any update fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Supplying `new_ops_status` advances the operations dimension along its single chain: `SCHEDULED` to `AIRBORNE`, and `AIRBORNE` to `LANDED`; requesting an advance from the wrong from-state fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every accepted update bumps the flight's `version`.

Cancellation is allowed only while the flight is `SLOTTED`-or-not but operationally `SCHEDULED`: cancelling an `AIRBORNE` or `LANDED` flight is rejected with `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (HTTP 409). When a cancelled flight holds an `ASSIGNED` slot, the release cascade runs inside the same request: the slot moves from `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED`, a `RELEASE` SlotTransaction is written whose `delay_delta_minutes` is the negation of the flight's `provisional_delay_minutes`, the flight's control fields revert (`controlled_arrival` to null, `control_status` to `UNCONTROLLED`, `provisional_delay_minutes` to 0, `is_overflow` to false), and the operator's ledger for the program has `attributed_delay_minutes` decremented by the same amount and `flight_count` decremented by 1. The flight's `ops_status` then becomes `CANCELLED`. The released slot is exactly the raw material compression later feeds on.

**Substitution** is the CDM mechanism by which an operator reshuffles delay among its own flights inside one `ACTIVE` ground delay program — against a `REVISING` program it fails with `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The request names `flight_id_1` and `flight_id_2` and may carry an optional `note`; the two flights must be distinct (`ERR_SUBSTITUTION_SAME_FLIGHT`), must both hold `ASSIGNED` slots in this program (`ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_IN_PROGRAM`), must each be owned by the acting operator (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), and must each have reported in recently — both `last_eta_reported_at` values within `FLIGHT_DATA_FRESHNESS_SECONDS` (3600 seconds) of the logical clock, else `ERR_STALE_FLIGHT_DATA` (HTTP 422), the data-exchange freshness requirement. The shift bound protects schedule integrity: for each flight, the absolute difference between the other flight's `slot_time` and this flight's `scheduled_arrival` must not exceed `SUBSTITUTION_MAX_SHIFT_MINUTES` (120 minutes), else `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_SHIFT_EXCEEDED`. A legal swap exchanges the two flights' controlled arrival times, writes two `SUBSTITUTION_SWAP` transactions with opposite `delay_delta_minutes` values — the created response returns the transaction recorded for `flight_id_1` — recomputes both flights' `provisional_delay_minutes`, reconciles the operator's ledger, and recomputes fair shares, all while conserving the program's assigned-slot multiset exactly.

**Compression** is the system's mechanism for recovering capacity that cancellations stranded. A `FlowManager` runs it against an `ACTIVE` ground delay program (`REVISING` again fails with `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE`), and it executes a pinned evaluation order that removes any circularity between moves and the pool's contents. First, all slots and assignments are snapshotted. Second, the released pool is formed from every slot in `RELEASED` status — those freed by holder cancellations and any left `RELEASED` from earlier activity — ordered by `slot_time` ascending. Then GA promotion happens first: `WAITLISTED` GAAccommodationRequests, ranked by `priority_class` with `HIGH_PRIORITY` before `STANDARD` and then by `created_at` ascending, consume reserved slots in the pool whose `slot_time` lies within each request's window, so long as the count of reserved slots currently `ASSIGNED` to GA-created flights stays below the program's reserved-slot count; each promotion runs the full accommodation cascade of §3.7 — a new flight created, the slot assigned, a `GA_ASSIGNMENT` transaction written. Only then do carrier moves begin: candidate flights — those holding `ASSIGNED` slots and not operationally `CANCELLED` — are walked in ration-by-schedule order (`scheduled_arrival` ascending, ties by `created_at` ascending), and each candidate takes the earliest pool slot strictly earlier than its current `slot_time`; the slot it frees is inserted back into the pool, kept in `slot_time` order, and is reusable by later candidates in the same pass — which is how a freed mid-compression slot transitions from `ASSIGNED` back to `OPEN` and re-enters the pool. Reserved-flagged pool slots are available to carrier candidates only when no `WAITLISTED` GA request exists for the program at all. Each move emits a `COMPRESSION_MOVE` transaction recording the old and new slot times and the signed change in the flight's provisional delay, and reconciles the ledgers.

Passes repeat until a pass makes zero moves or `COMPRESSION_MAX_PASSES` (3) passes have run; the recorded `passes_executed` counts the final no-move pass and is at least 1. At the end, every `RELEASED` slot still unclaimed returns to `OPEN` — the only way a released slot comes back — and the **CompressionRun** record is written: `released_slot_count` (the number of `RELEASED` slots that entered the pool), `moves_made` (total carrier flight moves across all passes), `passes_executed`, `promoted_ga_request_ids` (the waitlisted requests promoted during the run, possibly empty), and `remaining_open_slots` (slots `OPEN` after the run, counting never-assigned and unclaimed released slots alike). A program's compression runs can be listed oldest first.

### 3.6. Program Revision and the REVISING Cycle

Conditions change mid-program, and the system models every change as a two-step cycle rather than an in-place edit: the initiative moves from `ACTIVE` to `REVISING` while a **TMIRevision** record sits unapplied, and a subsequent activation applies it and returns the program to `ACTIVE`. While a program sits in `REVISING`, substitutions, compression runs, reroute assignments, and GA adjudication are all rejected with `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A manual revision starts when a `FlowManager` posts one against an `ACTIVE` program — from any other state, `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE` — carrying a free-text `reason`, the OCC field `expected_tmi_version` per §2.7, and at least one of `new_controlled_rate` or `new_program_end`; a revision supplying neither is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A supplied `new_controlled_rate` must satisfy the GDP rate shape (`ERR_INVALID_RATE`) and must not exceed the maximum feasible rate recomputed now (`ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`); a supplied `new_program_end` must be strictly after `program_start` (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`) and strictly after the current logical clock (`ERR_PROGRAM_WINDOW_IN_PAST`). Acceptance creates the TMIRevision with origin `MANUAL`, `applied` false, and `revision_index` equal to the program's `revision_count` plus one; the record captures `previous_controlled_rate` and `new_controlled_rate` — both null when only the window changed — and `previous_program_end` and `new_program_end` — both null when only the rate changed. The program's `revision_count` increments, its status becomes `REVISING`, and its `version` bumps.

A system revision needs no human hand. When a constraint event is confirmed or extended and the implied capacity of an overlapping `ACTIVE` ground delay program collapses below its `controlled_rate` — the computation is the one described in §3.2 — the cascade auto-creates a TMIRevision of origin `SYSTEM_AUTO_DOWNGRADE` with `reason` set to the literal string `AUTO_CAPACITY_DOWNGRADE`, its `new_controlled_rate` chosen as the largest valid GDP rate not exceeding the implied rate, or 6 when the implied rate falls below 6, and moves the program to `REVISING` exactly as a manual revision would. A program's revisions can be listed in `revision_index` order.

Applying the pending revision is the second half of the cycle: activation against a `REVISING` program requires exactly one unapplied TMIRevision and a facility not in `OUTAGE`. The pending record's values become the program's `controlled_rate` and/or `program_end`, the record's `applied` flips true and `applied_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Then a full re-derivation runs: GA-accommodated flights keep their slots, every other slot is torn down and rebuilt over the new rate and window, the eligible flight set is re-snapshotted, and flights whose controlled arrival changed receive `REVISION_MOVE` transactions and recomputed `provisional_delay_minutes`. Ledgers are recomputed — `attributed_delay_minutes` and `flight_count` — and fair shares redistributed, before the program returns to `ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented.

### 3.7. General Aviation Accommodation

A ground delay program reserves a tail of slots for general aviation, and operators outside the scheduled carriers petition into that pool through a **GAAccommodationRequest**. The requesting operator files under its own operator id against a target `tmi_id`, supplying a `requested_window_start` and `requested_window_end` and a `priority_class` of `HIGH_PRIORITY` or `STANDARD`. The target must be a `GROUND_DELAY_PROGRAM`, else `ERR_TMI_TYPE_NOT_GDP`; the requester must be a `GENERAL_AVIATION` or `UNSCHEDULED_CHARTER` operator, else `ERR_OPERATOR_TYPE_INELIGIBLE`; and the requested window must lie entirely inside the program window — `requested_window_start` no earlier than `program_start` and `requested_window_end` no later than `program_end`, with start strictly before end — else `ERR_GA_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM` (HTTP 422). The request is born `REQUESTED`, records the path operator as its `operator_id`, and can be listed per operator or per program with an optional `status` filter.

Adjudication belongs to the `FlowManager` and requires the program `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE`; the request itself must be `REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The `decision` is `ACCOMMODATE` or `DENY`. Denial simply moves the request to `DENIED`. Accommodation tests a quota predicate rather than erroring on scarcity: the number of reserved slots currently `ASSIGNED` to GA-created flights must be below the program's reserved-slot count — a cancelled GA flight frees its quota headroom — and an `OPEN` reserved slot must exist whose `slot_time` lies within the requested window. When either leg fails, the request moves to `WAITLISTED` instead of erroring; when both hold, accommodation grants the earliest in-window reserved `OPEN` slot and runs the full cascade: a new Flight is created for the requesting operator with a server-minted `designator` of the prefix `GA-` followed by the first eight characters of the request's `id`, `scheduled_arrival` equal to `requested_window_start`, and `arrival_facility_id` equal to the program's facility; the slot moves from `OPEN` to `ASSIGNED`; the flight becomes `SLOTTED` with its `controlled_arrival` set to the slot time; a `GA_ASSIGNMENT` transaction is written; the operator's ledger is created or updated and fair shares recomputed; and the request lands `ACCOMMODATED` with `accommodated_flight_id` and `accommodated_slot_time` recorded.

Waitlisted requests get a second chance without re-adjudication: a compression run promotes them out of the freed pool, in `priority_class` order — `HIGH_PRIORITY` before `STANDARD` — and then by `created_at` ascending, as §3.5 describes, subject to the same quota predicate and window containment. A `FlowManager` may also retry adjudication of a `WAITLISTED` request directly. Whatever their state, pending requests do not outlive the program: when the initiative is cancelled or completed, every `REQUESTED` and `WAITLISTED` request on it expires to `EXPIRED`. `ACCOMMODATED`, `DENIED`, and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

### 3.8. Reroute Initiatives and Sector Loading

A `REROUTE` initiative steers flights around a constrained area by assigning them alternative paths through en route sectors. Against an `ACTIVE` reroute initiative — any other TMI state yields `ERR_TMI_NOT_ACTIVE` — a `FlowManager` creates a **RerouteAssignment** naming a `flight_id`, a `via_sector_id`, and a free-text `assigned_route`. The flight must currently be `UNCONTROLLED`; the `via_sector_id` must resolve to a facility of type `EN_ROUTE_SECTOR`, and pointing it at an `AIRPORT` fails with `ERR_REROUTE_SECTOR_TYPE` (HTTP 422); the sector itself must not be in `OUTAGE`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. Capacity is enforced per sector per hour: the assignment's `hourly_bucket` is the flight's `estimated_arrival` truncated to the hour, and the load is the count of existing assignments sharing the same `via_sector_id` and `hourly_bucket`. The assignment is rejected with `ERR_SECTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) whenever the load has already reached the sector's `base_acceptance_rate` — the boundary is exact: a load one below the rate accepts, a load equal to the rate rejects. Assignments are listed with optional `tmi_id`, `flight_id`, and `via_sector_id` filters plus pagination, and fetched individually.

### 3.9. Termination: Cancellation, Completion, and Delay Equity

A `CommandCenterAdmin` can cancel any non-terminal initiative. From `PROPOSED` or `PUBLISHED` nothing has been allocated, so cancellation is a bare status flip to `CANCELLED`. From `ACTIVE` or `REVISING` the full compensation cascade runs inside the same request: every `ASSIGNED` ArrivalSlot moves to `RELEASED`; each release reverts its holder flight — `controlled_arrival` to null, `control_status` to `UNCONTROLLED`, `provisional_delay_minutes` to 0, `is_overflow` cleared — and writes a `REVERSAL` SlotTransaction whose `delay_delta_minutes` is the negation of the flight's previously attributed delay; and the operator's ledger has `attributed_delay_minutes` decremented and `reversed_delay_minutes` incremented by that same amount, so that after a full cancellation the ledger's attributed-minus-reversed balance reads zero. Every `REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED` GA request on the program expires. Once an initiative is `CANCELLED` — or `COMPLETED` — any further action against it is rejected with `ERR_TMI_TERMINAL`.

Completion is the clean ending, available from `ACTIVE` or `REVISING` and rejected elsewhere with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_TMI_TERMINAL`. Completion performs the FINAL delay attribution for each controlled flight: the flight's self-inflicted minutes are the whole-minute floor of its `estimated_arrival` minus `scheduled_arrival`, never below zero, counting only when the operator's last ETA report pushed the estimate beyond the schedule — otherwise zero — and its `final_delay_minutes` is then its `provisional_delay_minutes` minus those self-inflicted minutes, never below zero. Each operator's `cumulative_delay_minutes` grows by the sum of `final_delay_minutes` over that operator's flights in the program, and the ledgers are reconciled. Slots are wound down: every remaining `OPEN` or `RELEASED` slot moves to `RELEASED` as its terminal disposition, while `ASSIGNED` slots stay `ASSIGNED` for audit. Pending GA requests expire. The initiative becomes `COMPLETED`.

The **OperatorDelayLedger** is where delay equity lives. One ledger exists per operator per program, created at activation for every operator with at least one eligible flight, recording a `flight_count` — the operator's flights holding slots or virtual CTAs in the program, set at activation and decremented on cancellation removal — and a running `attributed_delay_minutes`, reconciled on every slot mutation, alongside the `reversed_delay_minutes` that compensation returns. On activation and after every delay-affecting mutation — substitution, compression, revision application, cancellation removal — the system recomputes each operator's fair share of the program's total delay. The program's total attributed delay and total flight count are summed across all its ledgers; each ledger's raw share is the total delay times its `flight_count` divided by the total flights. The rounding order is pinned: each raw share is floored first, and the leftover minutes — the total delay minus the sum of those floors — are distributed one minute per ledger in order of largest fractional remainder, with ties broken toward the operator whose earliest in-program flight has the earliest `scheduled_arrival`, and then by operator `created_at` ascending. The result is stored as `fair_share_minutes`, and `deviation_minutes` — the signed difference between `attributed_delay_minutes` and `fair_share_minutes` — exposes how far each operator sits from its proportional share. Ledgers are listed per operator and per program, giving both the operator-centric and the program-centric view of the same numbers.

The **ArrivalSlot** lifecycle, for reference, is a small machine of its own: slots are born `OPEN` at activation, become `ASSIGNED` through ration-by-schedule, a compression move, or GA accommodation; an `ASSIGNED` slot becomes `RELEASED` when its holder cancels or when a program-level cancel or complete cascade sweeps it, and becomes `OPEN` again when a compression move frees it mid-pass; a `RELEASED` slot returns to `OPEN` only when a compression run ends with it unclaimed. `RELEASED` is otherwise the terminal disposition for slots of cancelled or completed programs.
