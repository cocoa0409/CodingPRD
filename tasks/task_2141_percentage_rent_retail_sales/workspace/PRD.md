# Percentage Rent & Retail Sales Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Shopping center ownership earns money twice from the same square foot. The first stream is contractual minimum rent, which is predictable and easy to bill. The second stream — percentage rent — depends entirely on what the tenant actually rings through the register, which means the landlord must run a reporting programme: telling each tenant when sales are due, receiving and vetting what arrives, deciding which categories of revenue are legitimately excludable, billing the overage above a contractual breakpoint, auditing the numbers when they look wrong, and reconciling everything at year end. Today this programme lives in spreadsheets, and every one of its edges — a tenant that files late, an anchor department store that goes dark and triggers co-tenancy rent relief, a chain whose sales fall below a kick-out threshold, an audit that lands after the year was already reconciled — is handled by hand and handled inconsistently.

This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets. It manages shopping centers and the tenants operating inside them, the leases that define percentage-rent economics, the reporting years those leases are measured over, and the per-period sales reports tenants file against them. It computes net reported sales after applying a capped exclusion waterfall, bills percentage rent under one of two contractually selected variants, estimate-bills delinquent tenants from their own trailing history, monitors co-tenancy covenants against live center occupancy, arms and evaluates kick-out rights, and performs a year-end true-up that reconciles annual-basis arithmetic against period-basis billing. Every movement of a reporting year's running totals is written to an append-only ledger so that ownership, the lease administration desk and an independent auditor can all reconstruct how a number came to be.

The service is deliberately conservative about who may do what. Owner-side administration, day-to-day lease administration, the tenant itself and an independent sales auditor are separate actors with separate powers, and one pair of powers — activating co-tenancy rent relief and later waiving the kick-out right on the same lease — is explicitly forbidden to the same human. All resources are mounted under the `/retail` prefix, except the two logical-clock endpoints, which sit at the server root.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names something that is not one of the five roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to invoke the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `CenterAdmin`, `LeaseAdministrator`, `TenantUser`, `Auditor` and `OwnershipAnalyst`, spelled exactly that way.

A `CenterAdmin` is the owner-side administrator. Center registration, lease creation and economic amendment, lease termination, attachment of co-tenancy and kick-out clauses, activation of rent relief, waiver of a kick-out right, permanent closure of a reporting year and control of the logical clock all belong to this role. When `X-User-Role` is `CenterAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it in the fields the workflow narratives nominate — `relief_approved_by_user_id` when relief is activated and `waiver_approved_by_user_id` when a kick-out right is waived — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `LeaseAdministrator` runs the lease administration desk: opening reporting years, scheduling reporting periods, accepting filed reports, estimate-billing delinquent tenants, issuing and voiding charges, reconciling and reopening years, and working co-tenancy events through acknowledgement and cure. When `X-User-Role` is `LeaseAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

An `Auditor` is an independent sales auditor who opens audits against accepted reports, issues findings, settles them and rescinds them. When `X-User-Role` is `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `auditor_user_id` on the audit it opens but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

An `OwnershipAnalyst` reads portfolio data and is the only role permitted to pull the center sales-trend report. When `X-User-Role` is `OwnershipAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

A `TenantUser` is the retail tenant acting as itself. When `X-User-Role` is `TenantUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tenant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Tenant` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for a tenant-facing operation the server walks from the target resource to the owning lease — a sales report reaches its lease through its reporting year, a charge reaches its lease through its reporting year, and a co-tenancy event or kick-out clause reaches its lease directly — and requires `X-User-ID` to equal that lease's `tenant_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_LEASE_TENANT` (HTTP 403). This ownership test guards exactly six operations: report submission, report dispute, report amendment, applying a payment to a charge, electing termination on a co-tenancy event, and exercising a kick-out right.

One separation-of-duties rule spans two roles' worth of judgement but is enforced on a single operation. The `CenterAdmin` who waives a kick-out right on a lease must not be the same `CenterAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded in that lease's `relief_approved_by_user_id` when co-tenancy relief was activated. When they are the same identifier, the waiver is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless the endpoint's own description in Section 4 states otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any rows remain beyond the current window.

Filters offered by a list endpoint narrow the matching set before pagination. Unless stated otherwise, filters combine conjunctively, and a filter naming an id that does not exist simply yields an empty page rather than an error.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource the service persists is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`; any client-supplied value is ignored. Alongside `id`, most resources carry `created_at` and `updated_at` — both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations performed as a side effect of an operation targeting some other resource.

`ShoppingCenter`, `Tenant`, `Lease`, `LeaseYear`, `SalesReport`, `SalesExclusion`, `PercentageRentCharge`, `CoTenancyClause`, `CoTenancyEvent`, `KickOutClause` and `SalesAudit` all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. The reporting ledger entry is the single exception. A `ReportingLedgerEntry` is append-only and is never modified after it is written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and those two keys must be absent from its representation rather than present and null.

Whenever a cascade rewrites a field on a resource — a running total on a reporting year, a watchlist posture on a lease, a monitoring posture on a clause — that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is advanced. This is what makes the concurrency rules of Section 2.8 meaningful: a client that read a reporting year, then watched an unrelated acceptance move that year's cumulative totals, is holding a stale `version`.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are whole integer cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage, in request payloads or in responses. All rates and ratios are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points is one hundred percent, so a 6.00% percentage-rent rate is stored as a `percentage_rate_bps` of 600.

Two rounding modes are used, and the choice between them is never incidental. **Round half up** is applied when converting a monetary computation to whole cents: the exact value is rounded to the nearest whole cent, and a value exactly halfway between two cents is rounded away from zero — so a positive 0.5 rounds up and a negative 0.5 rounds down in magnitude terms, that is, away from zero. **Floor** is applied when computing a cap or a ratio: the exact value is truncated toward negative infinity to the next whole unit. Every place where one of these applies is named explicitly in the workflow narratives of Section 3; where a formula names round half up more than once, the roundings happen in the written order and are not collapsed into a single rounding at the end.

The general reading rule is that exclusion caps and basis-point ratios floor, while rent amounts, averages, uplifts and pro-rations round half up.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Whole-Day Arithmetic

Deadlines pervade this domain: reporting deadlines, cure periods, election windows, audit cooldowns. To keep them testable the server does not read wall-clock time for any business comparison. Instead it maintains a **logical UTC clock**. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `CenterAdmin` may move it forward or backward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and any role may read it. The clock's current value is returned as `current_utc`.

Every deadline comparison in this specification — whether a report is past due, whether a cure period has elapsed, whether an election window is still open, whether a termination right has ripened — reads the logical clock, as does every timestamp the server stamps onto a resource as a business event (`submitted_at`, `issued_at`, `observed_at`, `relief_started_at`, `relief_ended_at`, `triggered_at`). The `created_at` and `updated_at` audit timestamps of Section 2.4 are ordinary record-keeping timestamps.

Durations expressed in days are whole-day durations added in UTC. Adding a whole number of days to an instant advances that instant by exactly that many twenty-four-hour spans; it does not truncate to midnight and does not adjust for any local calendar.

### 2.7. Snapshot Pinning: Why a Reporting Year Ignores Later Amendments

Percentage-rent economics change over the life of a lease, but a reporting year must be measured against the economics that were in force when it opened. When a reporting year is created, the service copies three values off the lease and pins them onto the year: the lease's `effective_breakpoint_cents` becomes the year's `breakpoint_cents`, the lease's `percentage_rate_bps` becomes the year's `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot`, and the lease's `billing_mode` becomes the year's `billing_mode_snapshot`.

These snapshots are immutable for the life of the reporting year. A later economic amendment to the lease recomputes the lease's own derived breakpoint fields but does not rewrite any existing reporting year's snapshot values. Every percentage-rent computation described in Section 3 reads the snapshot on the reporting year, never the current value on the lease — including the choice between the two billing variants, which is decided by `billing_mode_snapshot` alone.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Reporting Years and Leases

Two write operations carry an optimistic-concurrency guard because neither can be excluded by a state precondition alone: both are legitimately repeatable, and a repeat computed from an out-of-date view of the world would silently destroy work.

`POST /retail/sales-reports/{id}/estimate-bill` requires a non-optional `expected_lease_year_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `LeaseYear` that owns the report. Re-estimating a delinquent period is legitimate once the year's history has changed, so nothing about the report's own state forbids a second estimate; what must be forbidden is posting an estimate that was computed from a lookback window the client can no longer vouch for. If `expected_lease_year_version` does not equal the reporting year's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_YEAR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful estimate billing itself advances that reporting year's `version` by 1.

`POST /retail/leases/{id}/amendments` requires a non-optional `expected_lease_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Lease`. A new amendment legitimately supersedes the prior economic terms, so a second amendment carrying an out-of-date view of `percentage_rate_bps` or `breakpoint_type` would silently undo the first. If `expected_lease_version` does not equal the lease's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful amendment advances the lease's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection neither require nor honour one.

### 2.9. Request Admission Order

When a request could fail several checks at once, the order in which those checks run decides which error the client sees, and that order is fixed. The service evaluates, in this sequence:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and naming one of the five declared roles, and `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This runs on every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's permitted role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This runs on every endpoint.
3. **Existence.** Every resource named in the URL path must exist, validated left to right; only then is every id referenced in the request body resolved. The rejection is the not-found code matching whichever resource was missing: `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_YEAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALES_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CO_TENANCY_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CO_TENANCY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KICK_OUT_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. This runs on every endpoint.
4. **Tenant ownership.** For an acting `TenantUser`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `tenant_id` of the lease reached from the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_LEASE_TENANT` (403). This runs on the six tenant-facing operations enumerated in Section 2.1: report submission, report dispute, report amendment, charge payment, co-tenancy termination election and kick-out exercise. Note that this precedes body validation, so a tenant acting on someone else's lease is told so even if the payload is also malformed.
5. **Request shape.** The body is well formed: every required field is present, every enum-typed field names a declared member, every integer sits inside its declared range, `period_end` is strictly after `period_start`, and `audit_window_end` is greater than or equal to `audit_window_start` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This runs on every endpoint.
6. **State.** The target entity is in a from-state from which the requested transition is declared, including the owning reporting year's own status gate. Report creation and tenant submission from `PENDING` require the reporting year to be `OPEN`; acceptance, estimate billing and audit actions require the reporting year to be `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`; and every mutation scoped to a reporting year is refused once that year is `CLOSED`. The rejection is the state code matching the entity that blocked: `ERR_TENANT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LEASE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LEASE_YEAR_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CHARGE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CO_TENANCY_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_KICK_OUT_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409. This runs on every state-changing endpoint.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** For the two endpoints that declare a version field, the supplied value equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_LEASE_YEAR_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (412), as described in Section 2.8. Because this sits below the state check, a stale version on an entity that is also in the wrong state surfaces as the state error, not the concurrency error.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The kick-out waiver's same-administrator rule, rejecting with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. **Open-audit interlock.** Reconciliation is refused while any audit targeting a report of that reporting year is `OPEN` or `FINDINGS_ISSUED` — that is, while the year's `open_audit_count` is greater than zero — rejecting with `ERR_OPEN_AUDIT_BLOCKS_RECONCILE` (409). This applies only to reconciliation.
10. **Cross-entity referential rules** (all HTTP 409), applying to lease creation, reporting-year creation, reconciliation and kick-out exercise: a lease's `tenant_id` must name a tenant belonging to the lease's `center_id` (`ERR_CENTER_MISMATCH`); a reporting year's `year_index` must be unique within its lease (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LEASE_YEAR_INDEX`); reconciliation requires every sales report of the year to be out of `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `DISPUTED` and `AMENDED` (`ERR_UNACCEPTED_REPORTS_BLOCK_RECONCILE`); exercising a kick-out right requires the measurement reporting year to be `RECONCILED` (`ERR_KICK_OUT_MEASUREMENT_YEAR_NOT_RECONCILED`); and exercising a kick-out right is refused while the lease is `RELIEF_ACTIVE` (`ERR_RELIEF_ACTIVE_BLOCKS_EXERCISE`).
11. **Interval and clock rules** (all HTTP 409), applying to report scheduling, audit opening, estimate billing, relief activation, kick-out exercise and termination election: `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELINQUENT`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_KICK_OUT_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` and `ERR_TERMINATION_RIGHT_NOT_RIPE`. Each is defined in the workflow that raises it.
12. **Domain shape rules** (all HTTP 422), applying to lease creation, lease amendment, report scheduling, report submission, report dispute and report amendment: `ERR_BREAKPOINT_CONFIG_INVALID`, `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_LEASE_YEAR`, `ERR_EXCLUSION_CODE_NOT_PERMITTED` and `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, each defined in the workflow that raises it.
13. **Money rules, applied last.** A payment applied to a charge must be strictly positive and must not exceed that charge's outstanding balance — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422). This applies only to charge payments.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Because ownership (step 4) outranks request shape (step 5), an ill-formed payload from the wrong tenant yields the ownership error. Because state (step 6) outranks concurrency (step 7), referential rules (step 10), interval rules (step 11), shape rules (step 12) and money rules (step 13), an operation attempted from the wrong state always reports the state error even when a later rule would also have blocked it.

### 2.10. Interval Semantics for Reporting Periods and Audit Windows

The service uses two different and deliberately incompatible interval conventions, and confusing them is the most common source of wrong answers in this domain.

A **reporting period** — both a reporting year's `period_start` to `period_end` and a sales report's `period_start` to `period_end` — is a half-open interval: the start instant is inside the period and the end instant is outside it. Two reporting periods on the same reporting year therefore collide only when one strictly begins before the other ends and that other strictly begins before the first one ends. Periods that merely touch — where a new period's `period_start` equals an existing period's `period_end` — do not collide, which is exactly what makes consecutive monthly periods expressible. Voided reports are ignored by this test; every report of the year whose status is anything other than `VOID` participates.

An **audit window** — a sales audit's `audit_window_start` to `audit_window_end` — is a closed interval: both endpoints are inside the window. Audit windows are further separated by a cooldown of 30 whole days on each side, so a landlord cannot chain back-to-back audits on the same tenant. A proposed window collides with an existing non-rescinded audit window on the same lease when the proposed start is at or before that existing window's end extended forward by the 30-day cooldown, and the existing window's start pulled back by the same 30-day cooldown is at or before the proposed end. Touching the expanded edge does collide here, which is the opposite of the reporting-period rule. Settled audits still participate in this test; only audits that have reached `RESCINDED` drop out of it.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and the Reporting Ledger

Many operations in this domain move more than one entity. The specification treats these as ordered cascades: the primary state change happens first, and each dependent effect is applied in the order the workflow narrative gives, with every touched resource's `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced. A cascade never partially applies — if the operation is accepted, every effect described for it lands in the same request.

Whenever a cascade moves a reporting year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` or `cumulative_billed_cents`, it also appends a `ReportingLedgerEntry` recording the movement. Each entry carries `lease_year_id`, an `entry_type` naming which cascade wrote it, a signed `delta_net_sales_cents`, a signed `delta_billed_cents` and a free-text `note` recording the entity name and action that produced it. The permitted `entry_type` values are `REPORT_ACCEPTED`, `REPORT_WITHDRAWN`, `ESTIMATE_ISSUED`, `ESTIMATE_VOIDED`, `PERIODIC_CHARGE_ISSUED`, `TRUE_UP_ISSUED`, `CREDIT_ISSUED`, `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT` and `AUDIT_RESCINDED`. An entry that moves only sales carries a `delta_billed_cents` of 0, and an entry that moves only billing carries a `delta_net_sales_cents` of 0. Ledger entries are never updated or deleted, and they are read back oldest first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Center and Keeping Its Occupancy Honest

Everything begins with a shopping center. A `CenterAdmin` registers one by supplying its legal `name`, a short `code` of between 1 and 16 characters that must be unique across every center in the system, the `total_gla_sqft` of gross leasable area (which must be greater than zero), and the `anchor_gla_sqft` portion attributed to anchor boxes (which must be at least zero and must not exceed `total_gla_sqft`). The centre's reporting calendar opens on `fiscal_year_start_month`, a calendar month from 1 to 12 that defaults to 1 when the caller omits it. Three further attributes are derived rather than supplied and initialise to zero on registration: `occupied_gla_sqft`, `occupancy_bps` and `open_anchor_count`.

Tenants are registered into a center by a `CenterAdmin` or a `LeaseAdministrator` with a `center_id` naming the center, a `legal_name` for the entity on the lease, a `trade_name` — the banner the public sees, and the name ownership's trend reporting speaks in — a merchandising `category` and a `gla_sqft` footprint greater than zero. The permitted categories are `ANCHOR`, `JUNIOR_ANCHOR`, `INLINE`, `FOOD` and `KIOSK`. From the category the service derives `is_anchor`, which is true exactly when the category is `ANCHOR` or `JUNIOR_ANCHOR` and false for the other three. A newly registered tenant enters the system in `PROSPECT` status. Tenants can be listed and filtered by `center_id`, by `status` and by `category`.

The tenant's operating status is what co-tenancy covenants are measured against, and it moves along a small but non-linear machine driven by a `to_status` value supplied by a `CenterAdmin` or a `LeaseAdministrator`. A `PROSPECT` becomes `OPERATING` when the store opens. An `OPERATING` tenant that shutters but has not surrendered its space goes `OPERATING → DARK`; a dark box that reopens goes `DARK → OPERATING`, which is the revert leg that makes this machine non-linear. A tenant that surrenders its space reaches the terminal `VACATED` status either as `OPERATING → VACATED` or as `DARK → VACATED`. No other pairing is legal; a request naming a `to_status` that is not reachable from the tenant's current status is refused with `ERR_TENANT_STATE_INVALID`.

Every one of those five transitions re-runs the center's occupancy monitor, and the monitor is where the interesting consequences live. It recomputes three center-level figures from scratch. `open_anchor_count` becomes the number of tenants of that center whose `is_anchor` is true **and** whose `status` is `OPERATING` — dark and vacated anchors do not count, which is precisely why an anchor going dark is a co-tenancy event. `occupied_gla_sqft` becomes the sum of `leased_sqft` over every lease of that center whose status is `ACTIVE`, `RELIEF_ACTIVE` or `TERMINATION_PENDING`; leases still in `DRAFT` and leases already `TERMINATED` contribute nothing. `occupancy_bps` becomes the occupied area multiplied by 10000 and divided by `total_gla_sqft`, floored to a whole basis point, and is defined as 0 when `total_gla_sqft` is zero.

The monitor then re-evaluates every co-tenancy clause in the center whose own lease is `ACTIVE` or `RELIEF_ACTIVE`, taking them in `created_at` order oldest first. A clause is violated when the center's recomputed `open_anchor_count` is strictly less than the clause's `required_open_anchor_count`, or when the center's recomputed `occupancy_bps` is strictly less than the clause's `required_occupancy_bps`. Both comparisons are strictly-less-than, so a center whose open anchor count exactly equals the required count, or whose occupancy in basis points exactly equals the required floor, is satisfied and not violated. A violated clause's `monitoring_status` flips to `VIOLATED`; a clause whose tests now pass flips to `SATISFIED`.

When a clause is violated and it does not already have a co-tenancy event in `OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING` or `RELIEF_ACTIVE`, the monitor opens one. The new event records `clause_id`, an `event_type` of `ANCHOR_WENT_DARK` when the anchor-count leg was the failing test and `OCCUPANCY_BELOW_FLOOR` when it was the occupancy leg, a `trigger_tenant_id` naming the tenant whose status change caused this run when that tenant is an anchor and null otherwise, an `observed_at` set to the logical clock's current instant, and a `cure_deadline_at` equal to `observed_at` advanced by the clause's `cure_period_days` whole days. The event is created in `OPEN` status with `relief_started_at` and `relief_ended_at` both null. In the same cascade the clause's lease has its `co_tenancy_status` moved from `COMPLIANT` to `VIOLATION_OPEN`.

Reading that cascade back through an anchor going dark makes the layering explicit. The tenant transition itself is the first effect. The second is the recomputation of `open_anchor_count`, which drops by one when the departing tenant was an anchor. The third, caused by the second, is the clause re-evaluation that flips `monitoring_status` and opens the co-tenancy event. The fourth, caused by the third, is the lease-side posture change: each lease that just received a new co-tenancy event has its `co_tenancy_status` moved `COMPLIANT → VIOLATION_OPEN`. `OPERATING → VACATED` runs exactly the same four-layer cascade. `DARK → VACATED` runs the monitor too, but because a dark anchor was already excluded from `open_anchor_count`, no further co-tenancy event results from it. And when a dark box reopens on `DARK → OPERATING`, clauses whose tests now pass flip `VIOLATED → SATISFIED`, but their already-open co-tenancy events are **not** auto-cured: a `LeaseAdministrator` must close them explicitly, which is the workflow described in Section 3.9.

### 3.2. Lease Economics: Breakpoints, Billing Modes and Amendment

A lease is the contract that turns a tenant's sales into rent, and it is created in `DRAFT` by a `CenterAdmin` or a `LeaseAdministrator`. The payload names the `center_id` the demised premises sit in, the `tenant_id` of record, a `suite_code` identifying the unit, the `leased_sqft` counted toward center occupancy (greater than zero), the contract `annual_base_rent_cents` in whole cents (greater than zero), and the `percentage_rate_bps` at which sales above the breakpoint are billed (from 1 to 10000 basis points inclusive). The named tenant must belong to the named center; a `tenant_id` whose tenant sits in a different center is refused with `ERR_CENTER_MISMATCH`.

The breakpoint — the annual sales level above which percentage rent begins to accrue — comes in two contractual flavours selected by `breakpoint_type`. A `NATURAL` breakpoint is derived: `natural_breakpoint_cents` is the lease's `annual_base_rent_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `percentage_rate_bps`, rounded half up to whole cents. An `ARTIFICIAL` breakpoint is stated in the contract, supplied as `artificial_breakpoint_cents` and required to be greater than zero. The service still computes `natural_breakpoint_cents` for reference in both cases, and it publishes `effective_breakpoint_cents`, which equals `artificial_breakpoint_cents` when the type is `ARTIFICIAL` and equals `natural_breakpoint_cents` when the type is `NATURAL`. A lease declaring `ARTIFICIAL` without a positive `artificial_breakpoint_cents`, or declaring `NATURAL` while supplying an `artificial_breakpoint_cents` at all, is refused with `ERR_BREAKPOINT_CONFIG_INVALID`.

Four more contractual selections complete the economics. `billing_mode` chooses which percentage-rent variant is applied when a report is accepted, and is either `CUMULATIVE_YTD` or `PERIOD_STANDALONE`. `reporting_frequency` is `MONTHLY` or `QUARTERLY` and fixes how many reporting periods a year contains — twelve for monthly reporting, four for quarterly. `reporting_deadline_days` is the whole number of days after a reporting period ends by which the tenant must file, and must lie between 0 and 60 inclusive. `exclusion_profile` is either `STANDARD` or `RESTRICTIVE` and selects the per-code cap table applied when a filing is vetted.

A lease also carries a set of derived and administrative attributes that begin at documented values and are rewritten by the workflows that own them. `co_tenancy_status` begins at `COMPLIANT` and takes the values `COMPLIANT`, `VIOLATION_OPEN` and `RELIEF_ACTIVE`. `effective_monthly_rent_cents` is the rent actually payable each month and equals `annual_base_rent_cents` divided by twelve, rounded half up, whenever `co_tenancy_status` is anything other than `RELIEF_ACTIVE`; under active relief it is whatever the relief computation of Section 3.9 writes. `relief_monthly_credit_cents` is the monthly abatement — the unreduced monthly figure, `annual_base_rent_cents` divided by twelve rounded half up, less `effective_monthly_rent_cents` — and it is 0 whenever no relief is active. `relief_approved_by_user_id` and `waiver_approved_by_user_id` are both null until relief is activated and until a kick-out waiver is recorded respectively. `delinquency_strikes` counts estimate billings issued against this lease's reports and starts at 0. `ytd_occupancy_cost_cents` and `occupancy_cost_ratio_bps` both start at 0 and are rewritten by the occupancy-cost computation of Section 3.5. `watchlist_status` starts at `NORMAL` and takes only `NORMAL` or `WATCHLIST`. Leases can be listed and filtered by `center_id`, `tenant_id`, `status` and `watchlist_status`.

Activating a lease moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and it is available to a `CenterAdmin` or a `LeaseAdministrator`. Activation requires the lease to be in `DRAFT` and the lease's tenant to be in `PROSPECT` or `OPERATING`; a tenant already `DARK` or `VACATED` blocks it with `ERR_TENANT_STATE_INVALID`, and a lease not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_LEASE_STATE_INVALID`. On activation the service sets `effective_monthly_rent_cents` to `annual_base_rent_cents` divided by twelve rounded half up, sets `relief_monthly_credit_cents` to 0, runs the center occupancy monitor of Section 3.1 — which raises `occupied_gla_sqft` and `occupancy_bps` — and advances the lease's `version` by 1.

Economic amendment is reserved to a `CenterAdmin` and is guarded by the optimistic-concurrency rule of Section 2.8: the payload must carry `expected_lease_version`, and every other field it carries is optional. An amendment may restate `percentage_rate_bps`, `annual_base_rent_cents`, `breakpoint_type`, `artificial_breakpoint_cents`, `billing_mode` or `exclusion_profile`, in any combination. After the restatement the service recomputes `natural_breakpoint_cents` and `effective_breakpoint_cents` from the new terms, applying the same `ERR_BREAKPOINT_CONFIG_INVALID` consistency rule as at creation, and advances the lease's `version` by 1. What it does **not** do is rewrite history: the `breakpoint_cents`, `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot` and `billing_mode_snapshot` pinned onto every existing reporting year are left exactly as they were, per Section 2.7.

A lease reaches `TERMINATED` only through `TERMINATION_PENDING`, and only a `CenterAdmin` may complete it. The lease must be in `TERMINATION_PENDING`, and no reporting year of that lease may still be `OPEN` or `RECONCILING` — an unfinished year blocks the termination with `ERR_LEASE_YEAR_STATE_INVALID`. Completing the termination re-runs the center occupancy monitor, and because a `TERMINATED` lease no longer counts, `occupied_gla_sqft` drops by that lease's `leased_sqft`. Note the asymmetry deliberately built into the intermediate state: a lease sitting in `TERMINATION_PENDING` still counts toward `occupied_gla_sqft` and stops mattering to `open_anchor_count` changes, so the center's occupancy does not collapse the instant a termination right is exercised.

Two system-driven legs complete the lease machine and are described where they are caused rather than here: `ACTIVE → RELIEF_ACTIVE` and its return leg `RELIEF_ACTIVE → ACTIVE` form a genuine cycle driven by the co-tenancy event machine, and both `ACTIVE → TERMINATION_PENDING` and `RELIEF_ACTIVE → TERMINATION_PENDING` are driven by a kick-out exercise or a co-tenancy termination election. Section 3.9 gives all four.

### 3.3. Opening a Reporting Year and Scheduling Its Periods

A reporting year is the measurement container for percentage rent, and only a `LeaseAdministrator` may open one. Creation names the `year_index` — a 1-based ordinal of at least 1 that must be unique within the lease, with a duplicate refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEASE_YEAR_INDEX` — together with the year's `period_start` and `period_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start. The year is created `OPEN`, its `lease_id` set from the lease it was opened against, its `true_up_delta_cents` null, and its three running counters — `cumulative_net_sales_cents`, `cumulative_billed_cents` and `open_audit_count` — all at 0. In the same operation the snapshots of Section 2.7 are pinned: `breakpoint_cents`, `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot` and `billing_mode_snapshot`.

The year's status machine has four values — `OPEN`, `RECONCILING`, `RECONCILED` and the terminal `CLOSED` — and one of its edges is a cycle. Closing the period moves `OPEN → RECONCILING` and freezes the year against new report scheduling and against submission of reports still in `PENDING`. Reopening moves `RECONCILING → OPEN` and makes both available again. Reconciliation moves `RECONCILING → RECONCILED`, and reopening a reconciled year moves `RECONCILED → RECONCILING`, which is how a late audit landing after year end is absorbed. Permanent closure moves `RECONCILED → CLOSED`. All four of the first transitions belong to a `LeaseAdministrator`; only a `CenterAdmin` may perform the final closure. Any of them attempted from the wrong status is refused with `ERR_LEASE_YEAR_STATE_INVALID`, and Section 3.10 gives the reconciliation and closure preconditions in full.

Reporting periods within the year are scheduled by a `LeaseAdministrator`, one at a time, each with its own `period_start` and `period_end` where the end is strictly after the start. The new report is created in `PENDING` with `submitted_at` null, `gross_sales_cents` null, `allowed_exclusions_cents` and `net_sales_cents` at 0, `source` at `TENANT_REPORTED`, `amendment_round` at 0, `is_delinquent` false, and its `lease_year_id` pointing at the year it was scheduled into. Its `due_at` is computed as the period's `period_end` advanced by the lease's `reporting_deadline_days` whole days in UTC — this is the single place where the contractual filing deadline becomes a concrete instant.

Two rules constrain scheduling. The proposed period must lie entirely inside the reporting year's own `period_start` to `period_end` span; a period that falls partly or wholly outside it is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_LEASE_YEAR`. And the proposed period must not overlap any non-`VOID` report already on the year, under the half-open convention of Section 2.10 — so a February period starting exactly where the January period ended is accepted, while a period that genuinely straddles an existing one is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Scheduling is also gated by the year's status: it requires the year to be `OPEN`.

Reports carry a computed `is_delinquent` flag that is true exactly when the report is still in `PENDING` and the logical clock now reads strictly later than its `due_at`, or when its `source` is `LANDLORD_ESTIMATE`. A report that was filed late but has been accepted is therefore not delinquent by this definition, while an estimate-billed period remains flagged forever. Reports can be listed and filtered by `lease_year_id`, by `status`, by `source` and by `is_delinquent`; reporting years themselves can be listed and filtered by `lease_id`, by `status` and by `year_index`.

### 3.4. Tenant Filing and the Exclusion Capping Waterfall

Filing is the tenant's own act. A `TenantUser` — and only the tenant of record on the lease, per the ownership rule of Section 2.1 — files a period by supplying `gross_sales_cents`, the gross retail sales for that period in cents, which may not be negative, together with an optional `exclusions` list. Each entry of that list claims one exclusion line with a `code` naming the category and a `claimed_cents` amount that may not be negative. Filing sets the report's `source` to `TENANT_REPORTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock's current instant.

Three different starting states lead into `SUBMITTED`, and they are not interchangeable. The ordinary case is `PENDING → SUBMITTED`, which requires the owning reporting year to be `OPEN`. A late actual replacing a landlord estimate is `ESTIMATED → SUBMITTED`, which also requires the year to be `OPEN`. A resubmission after an amendment is `AMENDED → SUBMITTED`, and this one is permitted while the year is either `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`, because a report withdrawn for amendment must be able to come back even after the year has been frozen for reconciliation. Any other origin status is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. All three rebuild the report's exclusion lines wholesale from the payload — the previous lines are discarded, the payload's lines are written fresh in `CLAIMED` status with `allowed_cents` at 0 — and then run the net-reported-sales computation described below.

The `ESTIMATED → SUBMITTED` leg carries a three-layer cascade because the estimate it replaces had already been counted and billed. First, the report leaves the counted set and takes its new tenant-reported figures. Second, caused by the first, the reporting year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` decreases by the estimate's former `net_sales_cents` and a ledger entry of type `REPORT_WITHDRAWN` is appended carrying that figure negated as its `delta_net_sales_cents` and 0 as its `delta_billed_cents`. Third, caused by the second, every percentage-rent charge whose `sales_report_id` is this report, whose `charge_type` is `ESTIMATE` and whose `billing_status` is `ISSUED` flips to `VOID`; the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` decreases by that charge's `amount_cents` and a ledger entry of type `ESTIMATE_VOIDED` is appended carrying that amount negated as its `delta_billed_cents` and 0 as its `delta_net_sales_cents`.

**How much of a claimed exclusion is actually allowed** is decided by a three-step waterfall that runs on every submission, on every audit settlement and on every audit rescission. It never runs against a landlord estimate, which carries no exclusions at all.

Step one caps each line individually, taking the report's exclusion lines in `created_at` order oldest first. Each line's category has a percentage cap expressed in basis points of the report's `gross_sales_cents`, and the cap that applies depends on the lease's `exclusion_profile`. Under the `STANDARD` profile: `RETURNS_AND_ALLOWANCES` is capped at 1200 basis points — twelve percent of gross; `SALES_TAX` at 10000 basis points, which is to say uncapped in percentage terms; `GIFT_CARD_ISSUANCE` at 500 basis points, five percent; `INTERCOMPANY_TRANSFER` at 10000 basis points; `EMPLOYEE_DISCOUNT` at 300 basis points, three percent; `ONLINE_SHIP_FROM_STORE` at 10000 basis points; and `VENDING_AND_SERVICE` at 200 basis points, two percent. Under the `RESTRICTIVE` profile the same seven codes are capped at 800, 10000, 0, 10000, 100, 0 and 200 basis points respectively — so a restrictive lease allows only eight percent for returns and one percent for employee discounts, and permits nothing at all for gift-card issuance or for ship-from-store online sales. The percentage cap for a line is the report's `gross_sales_cents` multiplied by that basis-point figure and divided by 10000, floored to whole cents. Two categories additionally carry an absolute ceiling regardless of profile or gross sales: gift-card issuance may never exceed 500000 cents — five thousand dollars — and employee discounts may never exceed 250000 cents, two thousand five hundred dollars. Where an absolute ceiling exists, the line's raw cap is the smaller of the percentage cap and that ceiling; where none exists, the raw cap is the percentage cap. The line's `allowed_cents` then becomes its `claimed_cents` limited by the raw cap, and never less than zero.

Because a `RESTRICTIVE` lease caps two categories at zero, claiming them at all is treated as a contract error rather than as a silent zeroing: an exclusion line whose code maps to a cap of 0 basis points under the lease's `exclusion_profile` and whose `claimed_cents` is greater than zero causes the whole submission to be refused with `ERR_EXCLUSION_CODE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Step two applies an aggregate ceiling across all lines together. Total exclusions on a report may not exceed 1500 basis points — fifteen percent — of `gross_sales_cents`, computed as `gross_sales_cents` multiplied by 1500 divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. If the sum of the per-line allowances from step one exceeds that aggregate cap, the excess is trimmed by a waterfall and **not** pro-rated: the lines are taken in descending order of their step-one `allowed_cents`, ties broken by `created_at` oldest first, and each line in turn surrenders the smaller of its own `allowed_cents` and the amount of overage still outstanding, until the overage reaches zero. The largest claims absorb the trim first, and a small line may survive untouched while a large one is reduced to nothing.

Step three settles the line statuses and the report totals. A line whose final `allowed_cents` equals its `claimed_cents` moves `CLAIMED → ALLOWED`; a line whose final `allowed_cents` is zero while its `claimed_cents` is greater than zero moves `CLAIMED → DISALLOWED`; a line whose final `allowed_cents` is greater than zero but less than its `claimed_cents` moves `CLAIMED → PARTIALLY_ALLOWED`. An `ALLOWED` line contributes its full claim, a `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED` line contributes only the allowed portion, and a `DISALLOWED` line contributes nothing. The report's `allowed_exclusions_cents` is the sum of every line's final `allowed_cents`, and its `net_sales_cents` is `gross_sales_cents` less `allowed_exclusions_cents`. A report with no exclusion lines at all yields `allowed_exclusions_cents` of 0 and `net_sales_cents` equal to `gross_sales_cents`.

All arithmetic in this waterfall is in whole cents, and every intermediate cap uses flooring — never round-half-up. Every exclusion line of the report participates in the computation regardless of the status it happened to be in beforehand.

The exclusion lines can travel backwards as well as forwards. When a disputed report is withdrawn for amendment, and equally when a fresh submission rebuilds the exclusion set, every line returns to `CLAIMED` with `allowed_cents` reset to 0 pending the next run of the waterfall — that is `ALLOWED → CLAIMED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED → CLAIMED` and `DISALLOWED → CLAIMED`, all three treated identically. Exclusion lines of a report are read back oldest first, and can be filtered by `code` and by `status`; each line also carries the `sales_report_id` of the report it belongs to.

### 3.5. Acceptance, Percentage-Rent Billing and the Occupancy-Cost Watchlist

Acceptance is the landlord's vetting act, performed by a `LeaseAdministrator` and permitted while the reporting year is `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`. It moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and sets off the longest cascade in the system.

The first layer is the acceptance itself: the report enters the **counted set**. Throughout this specification, the counted set means reports whose status is `ACCEPTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `DISPUTED`, `ADJUSTED` or `ESTIMATED`. Reports in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `AMENDED` or `VOID` are outside it and contribute nothing to any cumulative figure.

The second layer, caused by the first, moves the year's running sales: `cumulative_net_sales_cents` increases by the report's `net_sales_cents`, and a ledger entry of type `REPORT_ACCEPTED` is appended with that figure as its `delta_net_sales_cents` and 0 as its `delta_billed_cents`.

The third layer, caused by the second, computes percentage rent against the new cumulative and bills it. **Which variant runs is decided solely by the reporting year's `billing_mode_snapshot`**, never by the lease's current `billing_mode`, which a later amendment may have changed.

Under the `CUMULATIVE_YTD` variant the whole year is recomputed on every acceptance and only the delta is billed. The service sums `net_sales_cents` over every report of that reporting year that is in the counted set **excluding the report being processed**, adds the processed report's own `net_sales_cents` to obtain the year-to-date cumulative net sales, subtracts the year's `breakpoint_cents` and takes the result if it is positive or zero if it is not. That excess is multiplied by the year's `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to whole cents, giving the gross percentage rent due for the year to date. From that the service subtracts the sum of `amount_cents` over every charge of the year whose `billing_status` is `ISSUED` — draft and voided charges are excluded from this sum — and the difference is the amount to bill. Because the annual breakpoint is applied to the whole year at once, sales below the annual breakpoint produce zero percentage rent no matter how unevenly they are spread across periods.

Under the `PERIOD_STANDALONE` variant each period stands on its own against a pro-rated breakpoint, with no credit for prior periods. The number of months in a period is one when the lease's `reporting_frequency` is `MONTHLY` and three when it is `QUARTERLY`. The period breakpoint is the year's `breakpoint_cents` multiplied by that month count, divided by twelve, and rounded half up to whole cents. The excess is the report's own `net_sales_cents` less that period breakpoint, taken as zero if the subtraction is negative. The amount to bill is that excess multiplied by the year's `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to whole cents. Because each period is measured against only its pro-rata share of the breakpoint and no prior billing is credited back, identical annual sales produce strictly more percentage rent under this variant whenever any single period runs above its pro-rata share.

In either variant the round-half-up is applied exactly once within the branch that runs. When the computed amount is zero, **no charge row is created and no billing ledger entry is appended** — a period that produced no percentage rent leaves no trace in the billing figures. When the amount is non-zero, a percentage-rent charge is created against the reporting year with `charge_type` of `PERIODIC`, `billing_status` of `ISSUED`, `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, `collection_status` of `UNPAID`, `paid_cents` of 0, `supersedes_charge_id` null and `sales_report_id` naming the report that produced it. The year's `cumulative_billed_cents` moves by that `amount_cents`, and a ledger entry is appended carrying `amount_cents` as its `delta_billed_cents` — of type `PERIODIC_CHARGE_ISSUED` when the amount is positive and `CREDIT_ISSUED` when it is negative.

The fourth layer, caused by the third, re-runs the lease's occupancy-cost computation. Occupancy cost measures how much of a tenant's sales its total rent burden consumes, and it is evaluated against the single reporting year of that lease with the highest `year_index` whose status is not `CLOSED`. The service counts the elapsed periods of that year — the number of its reports currently in the counted set — and derives the periods per year from the lease's `reporting_frequency`, twelve for `MONTHLY` and four for `QUARTERLY`. The lease's `ytd_occupancy_cost_cents` becomes the lease's `annual_base_rent_cents` multiplied by the elapsed period count, divided by the periods per year and rounded half up to whole cents, plus the year's `cumulative_billed_cents`. The lease's `occupancy_cost_ratio_bps` becomes `ytd_occupancy_cost_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents`, floored to a whole basis point; it is defined as 0 when that cumulative net sales figure is zero.

The fifth layer, caused by the fourth, sets the lease's `watchlist_status`. A lease is put on `WATCHLIST` when its recomputed `occupancy_cost_ratio_bps` is at or above 1500 basis points — that is, when rent is consuming fifteen percent or more of net sales — or when its `delinquency_strikes` has reached three or more. Otherwise it is `NORMAL`. The flip `NORMAL → WATCHLIST` therefore has two independent triggers, and the ratio trigger is an at-or-above test: a lease sitting exactly at 1500 basis points is on the watchlist. This same occupancy-cost computation re-runs whenever a charge's `billing_status` becomes `ISSUED` or `VOID`, and whenever a delinquent period is estimate-billed.

### 3.6. Delinquency Enforcement and Estimate Billing

A tenant that simply does not file cannot be allowed to stall the year. Once the logical clock reads strictly later than a `PENDING` report's `due_at`, a `LeaseAdministrator` may estimate-bill the period, moving the report `PENDING → ESTIMATED`. Attempting this while the clock is at or before `due_at` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELINQUENT`; the report must genuinely be past due, not merely due. The operation requires the owning reporting year to be `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`, and it carries the `expected_lease_year_version` guard of Section 2.8.

The estimate is derived from the tenant's own trailing history, and the evaluation order is pinned because the dependency only looks circular. First the lookback window is built from state as it stands **before** this estimate is posted. Eligible history consists of reports of the *same* reporting year whose `source` is `TENANT_REPORTED`, whose status is either `ACCEPTED` or `ADJUSTED`, and whose `period_end` is at or before the delinquent report's `period_start`. Reports whose `source` is `LANDLORD_ESTIMATE` are excluded at every depth, so a run of consecutive estimates never compounds on itself. That history is sorted by `period_end` newest first, ties broken by `created_at` newest first, and the three most recent entries form the window.

Second, the estimated gross is derived from the window. When the window contains at least one report, the base is the sum of `gross_sales_cents` across the window divided by the number of reports in the window, rounded half up to whole cents; the estimated gross is then that base multiplied by 11000 basis points and divided by 10000 — a ten percent uplift over the tenant's own trailing average, applied as a penalty for non-filing — rounded half up to whole cents. The two roundings happen in that order and are not collapsed. When the window is empty, the tenant has no usable history, and the estimate instead falls back to the contract: the reporting year's `breakpoint_cents` divided by the number of periods in a year — twelve for a `MONTHLY` lease, four for a `QUARTERLY` one — multiplied by 8000 basis points and divided by 10000, that is eighty percent of a pro-rata share of the annual breakpoint, rounded half up to whole cents.

The resulting figure is written to the report's `gross_sales_cents`. An estimate carries no exclusions at all, so `allowed_exclusions_cents` is set to 0 and `net_sales_cents` is set equal to the estimated gross. The report's `source` becomes `LANDLORD_ESTIMATE`, which also makes its `is_delinquent` flag permanently true.

Only then, third, does percentage-rent billing run — against a cumulative that now includes the estimate. The reporting year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` increases by the estimate's `net_sales_cents` and a ledger entry of type `ESTIMATE_ISSUED` is appended with that figure as its `delta_net_sales_cents`. The percentage-rent computation of Section 3.5 then runs under whichever variant the year's `billing_mode_snapshot` names; when its amount is non-zero a charge is created with `charge_type` of `ESTIMATE`, `billing_status` of `ISSUED`, `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock and `sales_report_id` naming this report, the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` moves by that `amount_cents`, and a further ledger entry of type `ESTIMATE_ISSUED` is appended carrying `amount_cents` as its `delta_billed_cents`.

Two more effects close the operation. The lease's `delinquency_strikes` increases by 1, and the occupancy-cost computation of Section 3.5 re-runs — which matters because a lease that has accumulated three or more strikes is forced onto `WATCHLIST` regardless of how healthy its occupancy-cost ratio looks. Finally the reporting year's `version` advances by 1, which is what makes a second estimate on the same year require a freshly read version.

An estimate is never the last word. When the tenant eventually files the real numbers, the `ESTIMATED → SUBMITTED` leg of Section 3.4 withdraws the estimate from the year's cumulative sales and voids the `ESTIMATE` charge, and ordinary acceptance then bills the actual figures.

### 3.7. Audit, Findings, Dispute and Amendment

An `Auditor` opens an examination against an accepted report by naming the `sales_report_id` under examination together with the on-site `audit_window_start` and `audit_window_end`, which must satisfy end at or after start. The report must currently be `ACCEPTED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. The proposed window is tested against every non-`RESCINDED` audit already recorded on the same lease under the buffered closed-interval rule of Section 2.10, with its thirty-day cooldown on each side; a collision is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Opening the audit creates it in `OPEN` status with `proposed_adjustment_cents` and `settled_adjustment_cents` both null, resolves its `lease_id` from the target report's reporting year, and records `auditor_user_id` as the acting auditor's `X-User-ID`. The report moves `ACCEPTED → UNDER_AUDIT` and the reporting year's `open_audit_count` increases by 1. Because `UNDER_AUDIT` is inside the counted set, the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` does not move at this point. Audits can be listed and filtered by `lease_id`, by `sales_report_id` and by `status`.

Issuing findings moves the audit `OPEN → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and records the `proposed_adjustment_cents`, a gross-sales adjustment that may be negative. Findings are a statement of position only: no sales figure and no billing figure moves. The report must still be `UNDER_AUDIT` for findings to be issued.

Settlement moves the audit `FINDINGS_ISSUED → SETTLED` with a `settled_adjustment_cents` supplied at settlement — which need not equal what was proposed and may likewise be negative — and drives the report `UNDER_AUDIT → ADJUSTED` through a four-layer cascade. First, the audit settles with its `settled_adjustment_cents` recorded and the year's `open_audit_count` decreases by 1. Second, caused by the first, the report's `gross_sales_cents` increases by `settled_adjustment_cents` — a negative adjustment therefore reduces it — its `source` becomes `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, and the exclusion waterfall of Section 3.4 re-runs over the report's existing exclusion lines against the new gross, rewriting `allowed_exclusions_cents` and `net_sales_cents`. Third, caused by the second, the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` moves by the difference between the report's new `net_sales_cents` and its previous `net_sales_cents`, and a ledger entry of type `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT` is appended carrying that difference as its `delta_net_sales_cents`. Fourth, caused by the third, the percentage-rent computation re-runs on the new cumulative; when its amount is non-zero a supplemental charge is created against the reporting year with `sales_report_id` naming this report, `billing_status` of `ISSUED`, and a `charge_type` of `PERIODIC` when the amount is positive or `CREDIT` when it is negative, the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` moves by `amount_cents`, and a matching ledger entry — `PERIODIC_CHARGE_ISSUED` for a positive amount, `CREDIT_ISSUED` for a negative one — is appended with `amount_cents` as its `delta_billed_cents`.

Rescission is the auditor's undo, and it takes three different shapes depending on where the audit stands, each supplied with a `reason`. From `OPEN`, rescinding simply abandons the examination: the audit becomes `RESCINDED`, the report returns `UNDER_AUDIT → ACCEPTED`, the year's `open_audit_count` decreases by 1, and no sales or billing figure moves. From `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, the same abandonment applies with the same three effects. From `SETTLED`, rescission is a compensating reversal and additionally requires the reporting year to be `OPEN` or `RECONCILING`: the report moves `ADJUSTED → ACCEPTED`, its `gross_sales_cents` decreases by the audit's `settled_adjustment_cents`, its `source` returns to `TENANT_REPORTED`, and the exclusion waterfall re-runs. The year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` moves by the difference between the report's new and previous `net_sales_cents` and a ledger entry of type `AUDIT_RESCINDED` records the reversal. Every charge that the settlement itself created — that is, every charge whose `sales_report_id` is this report and whose `issued_at` is at or after the moment the audit was settled — flips `ISSUED → VOID`, the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` is reduced by that charge's `amount_cents`, and a matching `AUDIT_RESCINDED` ledger entry is appended carrying that amount negated as its `delta_billed_cents`.

The tenant's answer to findings is a dispute. A `TenantUser` on the lease may move the report `UNDER_AUDIT → DISPUTED` with a `reason`, but only while the report's audit stands at `FINDINGS_ISSUED` — disputing an audit that is merely `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the report's `amendment_round` is still strictly below two. Disputing moves no figures at all, because `DISPUTED` is inside the counted set.

Amendment is the withdrawal that follows. A `TenantUser` moves the report `DISPUTED → AMENDED` with a `reason`, again only while `amendment_round` is strictly below two. Amendment increases `amendment_round` by 1 and takes the report out of the counted set: the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` decreases by the report's `net_sales_cents` and a ledger entry of type `REPORT_WITHDRAWN` is appended carrying that figure negated. Every exclusion line of the report resets to `CLAIMED` with `allowed_cents` of 0. The report's audit flips `FINDINGS_ISSUED → RESCINDED` and the year's `open_audit_count` decreases by 1. The tenant then refiles through the `AMENDED → SUBMITTED` leg, closing the cycle `ACCEPTED → UNDER_AUDIT → DISPUTED → AMENDED → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`.

Two full dispute-amend cycles are the contractual limit. Once a report's `amendment_round` has reached 2, both dispute and amendment are refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and the audit trail on that period is final.

### 3.8. Charges: Two Independent Axes, Issuance, Voiding and Collection

A percentage-rent charge tracks two things that have nothing to do with each other — whether the landlord has billed it, and whether the tenant has paid it — so it carries two status fields that advance independently. `billing_status` is the billing axis and takes `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or the terminal `VOID`. `collection_status` is the collection axis and takes `UNPAID`, `PARTIAL`, and the terminal values `PAID` and `WRITTEN_OFF`. A charge is born `DRAFT` and `UNPAID`; charges created directly by acceptance, estimate billing or audit settlement are born `ISSUED` and `UNPAID`.

Every charge names the `lease_year_id` it belongs to. Its `sales_report_id` names the report that produced it, and is null for the true-up and credit charges that reconciliation emits. Its `supersedes_charge_id` names the earlier charge it replaces, set when a late actual replaces a landlord estimate and null otherwise. Its `charge_type` is `PERIODIC`, `ESTIMATE`, `TRUE_UP` or `CREDIT`, its `amount_cents` is the output of the percentage-rent or true-up computation and is negative for a `CREDIT`, its `paid_cents` accumulates payments and starts at 0, and its `issued_at` is null while `DRAFT` and stamped from the logical clock when the charge is issued. Charges can be listed and filtered by `lease_year_id`, `sales_report_id`, `supersedes_charge_id`, `charge_type`, `billing_status` and `collection_status`.

Issuing a draft charge is a `LeaseAdministrator` action moving the billing axis `DRAFT → ISSUED`. It requires the charge to be `DRAFT` and the owning reporting year to be anything other than `CLOSED`. It stamps `issued_at`, increases the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` by `amount_cents`, and appends a ledger entry carrying `amount_cents` as its `delta_billed_cents` — of type `TRUE_UP_ISSUED` when the amount is positive and `CREDIT_ISSUED` when it is negative. The collection axis is untouched by issuance: a draft charge already sits at `UNPAID`, and it stays there.

Voiding is also a `LeaseAdministrator` action, supplied with a `reason`, and it drives both axes at once. From `ISSUED` the billing axis moves `ISSUED → VOID`, which requires the collection axis to be `UNPAID` or `PARTIAL` — a charge already `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` cannot be voided — and requires the reporting year not to be `CLOSED`; the year's `cumulative_billed_cents` decreases by `amount_cents` and a ledger entry with that amount negated as its `delta_billed_cents` is appended. From `DRAFT` the billing axis moves `DRAFT → VOID` with the same year-status requirement, but with **no** ledger movement whatsoever, because a draft charge never entered `cumulative_billed_cents` in the first place. In the same request the collection axis follows: a charge that was `UNPAID` moves `UNPAID → WRITTEN_OFF`, and a charge that was `PARTIAL` moves `PARTIAL → WRITTEN_OFF` with its `paid_cents` retained on the row as a tenant credit — no refund resource is created.

Payments touch only the collection axis and may be applied by a `LeaseAdministrator` or by the lease's own `TenantUser`, supplying an `amount_cents`. The charge's `billing_status` must be `ISSUED`; paying against a draft or voided charge is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_STATE_INVALID`. The payment must be strictly greater than zero and must not exceed the outstanding balance, which is the charge's `amount_cents` less its `paid_cents`; a payment that is zero, negative, or larger than the outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. An accepted payment increases `paid_cents` by the amount paid, and the collection axis advances accordingly: a payment smaller than the whole outstanding balance moves `UNPAID → PARTIAL`, a payment exactly settling the remaining balance on a partly paid charge moves `PARTIAL → PAID`, and a payment exactly equal to the full `amount_cents` on an untouched charge moves `UNPAID → PAID` in one step. Throughout, `billing_status` is untouched — the two axes advance independently.

### 3.9. Co-Tenancy Covenants, Rent Relief and Kick-Out Rights

Co-tenancy is the promise a landlord makes to an inline tenant that the center it signed into will still be the center it signed into. A `CenterAdmin` attaches a co-tenancy clause to a lease by declaring the `required_open_anchor_count` — the minimum number of `OPERATING` anchor tenants the center must maintain, which may be zero — the `required_occupancy_bps` floor for center occupancy, between 0 and 10000 basis points inclusive, the `cure_period_days` the landlord is given to fix a violation before relief can begin, which must be at least zero, the `relief_type` of `RENT_REDUCTION` or `ALTERNATIVE_SALES_RENT`, a `relief_pct_bps` between 1 and 10000 basis points used by both relief variants, and the `termination_right_after_days` of continuous active relief after which the tenant may walk, likewise at least zero. The clause is created with `monitoring_status` of `SATISFIED`, but the center's occupancy monitor of Section 3.1 runs immediately on creation — so a clause attached into an already-violating center flips straight to `VIOLATED` and opens a co-tenancy event in the same request. Clauses can be listed and filtered by `lease_id`, by `monitoring_status` and by `relief_type`.

A co-tenancy event opens in `OPEN` and follows a branching lifecycle. A `LeaseAdministrator` acknowledges it, moving `OPEN → CURE_PENDING`; acknowledgement changes no rent and does not restart the cure clock, which has been running from the `cure_deadline_at` declared when the event was created. From `CURE_PENDING` the event branches. If the landlord fixes the underlying problem — a new anchor opens, occupancy recovers — the monitor flips the clause back to `SATISFIED` and a `LeaseAdministrator` closes the event `CURE_PENDING → CURED`, which sets the lease's `co_tenancy_status` back to `COMPLIANT` and leaves the lease's own status entirely untouched, because relief was never activated. This is the branch that skips relief altogether. If instead the cure period lapses, a `CenterAdmin` activates relief `CURE_PENDING → RELIEF_ACTIVE`, which requires the logical clock to read at or after the event's `cure_deadline_at` — an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` — requires the clause's `monitoring_status` to still be `VIOLATED`, and requires the lease to be `ACTIVE`. Activation stamps `relief_started_at` from the logical clock, runs the relief computation below, moves the lease `ACTIVE → RELIEF_ACTIVE`, sets the lease's `co_tenancy_status` to `RELIEF_ACTIVE`, and records the acting administrator's `X-User-ID` in the lease's `relief_approved_by_user_id`. Events can be listed and filtered by `clause_id`, by `trigger_tenant_id`, by `event_type` and by `status`.

**How much relief the tenant gets** depends on the clause's `relief_type`, and the two variants produce genuinely different answers from the same inputs. Both start from the unreduced monthly figure: the lease's `annual_base_rent_cents` divided by twelve, rounded half up to whole cents.

Under `RENT_REDUCTION`, relief is a fixed share of contract base rent and is completely independent of the tenant's sales. The monthly abatement is the unreduced monthly figure multiplied by the clause's `relief_pct_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents, and the lease's `effective_monthly_rent_cents` becomes the unreduced monthly figure less that abatement.

Under `ALTERNATIVE_SALES_RENT`, relief instead resets rent to a share of what the store is actually selling. The service gathers every sales report across **all** reporting years of that lease whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `ADJUSTED` — estimated and under-audit reports are excluded so that a landlord estimate can never inflate an alternative-rent floor — sorts them by `period_end` newest first with ties broken by `created_at` newest first, and takes the three most recent. The average net is the sum of `net_sales_cents` across that window divided by the number of reports in it, rounded half up to whole cents. The alternative rent is that average multiplied by the clause's `relief_pct_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. The lease's `effective_monthly_rent_cents` becomes the smaller of the unreduced monthly figure and that alternative rent — which means a tenant selling strongly enough that its alternative rent equals or exceeds contract rent receives no relief at all. Should the window be empty because the lease has no accepted or adjusted reports anywhere, the variant falls back to behaving exactly like `RENT_REDUCTION` at a default rate of 5000 basis points, fifty percent of the unreduced monthly figure.

In both variants the lease's `relief_monthly_credit_cents` is set to the unreduced monthly figure less the resulting `effective_monthly_rent_cents`, and every named rounding is applied in the order written rather than deferred to the end.

Relief ends one of two ways. When the center recovers and the clause returns to `SATISFIED`, a `LeaseAdministrator` closes the event `RELIEF_ACTIVE → CURED`: `relief_ended_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the lease moves `RELIEF_ACTIVE → ACTIVE`, its `co_tenancy_status` returns to `COMPLIANT`, its `effective_monthly_rent_cents` is restored to `annual_base_rent_cents` divided by twelve rounded half up, and its `relief_monthly_credit_cents` returns to 0. Attempting to cure while the clause is still `VIOLATED` is refused with `ERR_CO_TENANCY_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`. Alternatively, when relief has run long enough, the tenant walks: a `TenantUser` on the lease moves the event `RELIEF_ACTIVE → TERMINATION_ELECTED`, which requires the logical clock to read at or after `relief_started_at` advanced by the clause's `termination_right_after_days` whole days — an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_TERMINATION_RIGHT_NOT_RIPE`. Election stamps `relief_ended_at`, moves the lease `RELIEF_ACTIVE → TERMINATION_PENDING` and sets the lease's `co_tenancy_status` to `VIOLATION_OPEN`. `CURED` and `TERMINATION_ELECTED` are both terminal for the event.

The kick-out right is the other escape hatch, and it is armed against sales rather than against occupancy. A `CenterAdmin` attaches a kick-out clause to a lease by declaring the `measurement_year_index` — the `year_index`, at least 1, of the reporting year whose reconciled cumulative net sales will be tested — a `threshold_sales_cents` floor greater than zero, and an `election_window_days` count greater than zero during which the tenant may exercise. The clause is created `ARMED`, with `triggered_at` and `election_deadline_at` both null and `waiver_consideration_cents` at 0. Clauses can be listed and filtered by `lease_id`, by `status` and by `measurement_year_index`.

Evaluation happens exactly once, at the moment the measurement year is reconciled, and it is a strict comparison. Every kick-out clause of that lease that is still `ARMED` and whose `measurement_year_index` equals the reconciling year's `year_index` is taken in `created_at` order oldest first. If the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` is strictly less than the clause's `threshold_sales_cents`, the clause moves `ARMED → TRIGGERED`, `triggered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `election_deadline_at` is set to `triggered_at` advanced by `election_window_days` whole days. Otherwise the clause moves `ARMED → EXPIRED` with `triggered_at` and `election_deadline_at` left null and the tenant never gaining an election. Sales landing exactly on the threshold therefore expire the right rather than trigger it. Clauses for other reporting years are left untouched.

A triggered right can end three ways. The tenant exercises it — a `TenantUser` on the lease moving the clause `TRIGGERED → EXERCISED`, which drives the lease `ACTIVE → TERMINATION_PENDING`. Exercise carries four preconditions beyond the clause being `TRIGGERED`: the measurement reporting year, meaning the year of the same lease whose `year_index` equals the clause's `measurement_year_index`, must be `RECONCILED`, otherwise `ERR_KICK_OUT_MEASUREMENT_YEAR_NOT_RECONCILED`; the logical clock must read at or before `election_deadline_at`, otherwise `ERR_KICK_OUT_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and the lease must be `ACTIVE`, because a lease already sitting in `RELIEF_ACTIVE` cannot also be kicked out — that attempt is refused with `ERR_RELIEF_ACTIVE_BLOCKS_EXERCISE`.

Or the landlord buys the right out. A `CenterAdmin` moves the clause `TRIGGERED → WAIVED`, optionally recording a `waiver_consideration_cents` amount of at least zero as the cash paid for the waiver, and the acting administrator's `X-User-ID` is written to the lease's `waiver_approved_by_user_id`. This is the operation guarded by the separation-of-duties rule of Section 2.1: an administrator whose own `X-User-ID` already sits in that lease's `relief_approved_by_user_id` may not also grant the waiver, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Or the window simply closes. When the same waiver operation is invoked while the logical clock reads strictly later than `election_deadline_at`, the clause lapses instead of being bought out: it moves `TRIGGERED → EXPIRED`, and `waiver_consideration_cents` stays 0. `WAIVED`, `EXERCISED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal; a clause in any of them refuses further exercise or waiver with `ERR_KICK_OUT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.10. Year-End Reconciliation, Closure and Ownership Trend Reporting

Reconciliation is where the year's arithmetic is settled. A `LeaseAdministrator` first freezes the year `OPEN → RECONCILING`, which stops new reporting periods from being scheduled and stops reports still in `PENDING` from being submitted, then reconciles it `RECONCILING → RECONCILED`. Reconciliation refuses to run while any audit on the year's reports is still `OPEN` or `FINDINGS_ISSUED` — that is, while `open_audit_count` is greater than zero — with `ERR_OPEN_AUDIT_BLOCKS_RECONCILE`, and it refuses to run while any sales report of the year is still in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `DISPUTED` or `AMENDED`, with `ERR_UNACCEPTED_REPORTS_BLOCK_RECONCILE`.

**The true-up exists because two rounding orders disagree, and the whole point of it is to materialise exactly that disagreement.** The annual recompute sums first and rounds once: the service sums `net_sales_cents` across every report of the year in the counted set as exact integer cents, subtracts the year's `breakpoint_cents`, takes the result if positive or zero if not, multiplies that annual excess by the year's `percentage_rate_bps_snapshot`, divides by 10000, and rounds half up to whole cents exactly once — this is the annual amount due. Periodic billing, by contrast, rounded each period separately and then summed those rounded figures. The service therefore subtracts from the annual amount due the sum of `amount_cents` over every charge of the year whose `billing_status` is `ISSUED` — draft and voided charges are excluded from that sum — and writes the difference into the year's `true_up_delta_cents`.

When `true_up_delta_cents` is greater than zero, reconciliation creates a charge with `charge_type` of `TRUE_UP`, `billing_status` of `DRAFT`, `collection_status` of `UNPAID`, `amount_cents` equal to the delta and `sales_report_id` null. When it is less than zero, it creates a charge with `charge_type` of `CREDIT`, `billing_status` of `DRAFT`, `collection_status` of `UNPAID`, `amount_cents` equal to the (negative) delta and `sales_report_id` null. When it is exactly zero, no charge is created at all. Either charge is born in `DRAFT` and must be issued separately through the billing axis of Section 3.8 before it moves the year's `cumulative_billed_cents`.

The two billing modes behave very differently here, and the difference is expected rather than incidental. In a year whose `billing_mode_snapshot` is `CUMULATIVE_YTD`, the already-issued figure equals the rounded annual amount due as of the last acceptance, so `true_up_delta_cents` is zero unless a charge was voided or an audit moved sales after that last acceptance. In a year whose snapshot is `PERIOD_STANDALONE`, the already-issued figure is the sum of per-period amounts each rounded against its own pro-rated breakpoint, so the delta captures both the difference between pro-rata and annual breakpoint treatment and the accumulated per-period rounding residue.

Reconciliation also runs the kick-out evaluation of Section 3.9 over every `ARMED` kick-out clause of that lease whose `measurement_year_index` matches the year's `year_index`.

Years can be reopened. A `LeaseAdministrator` supplies a `reason` and moves the year either `RECONCILING → OPEN`, which restores report scheduling and submission, or `RECONCILED → RECONCILING`, which is how a late audit landing after year end is absorbed. Leaving `RECONCILED` sets `true_up_delta_cents` back to null. Reopening never re-arms a kick-out right: clauses already `TRIGGERED` keep their `status`, their `triggered_at` and their `election_deadline_at` untouched.

Final closure `RECONCILED → CLOSED` belongs to a `CenterAdmin` and requires every percentage-rent charge of the year to have reached a settled billing posture — that is, every charge's `billing_status` must be `ISSUED` or `VOID`, so a `DRAFT` true-up left unissued blocks the close. Once the year is `CLOSED` it is immutable: every mutating operation scoped to it is refused with `ERR_LEASE_YEAR_STATE_INVALID`. Should a report still sit in `PENDING` at the moment of closure, it is driven to the terminal `VOID` status and frozen out of every calculation input set.

The reporting year's ledger is readable in full by a `CenterAdmin`, a `LeaseAdministrator` or an `Auditor`, oldest first, and can be narrowed by `entry_type`. Read together with the year's `cumulative_net_sales_cents` and `cumulative_billed_cents`, it is the reconstruction path for any figure ownership questions.

Ownership sees the portfolio through a different lens. An `OwnershipAnalyst` — and no other role — pulls a comparable-store trend report for a center and a nominated `year_index`, which for this computation is read as year N. The comparable set consists of every lease of that center whose status is `ACTIVE`, `RELIEF_ACTIVE` or `TERMINATION_PENDING` that has **both** a reporting year at index N and a reporting year at index N minus 1, each of which must be `RECONCILED`; leases missing either year, or holding either year in any other status, are excluded. Leases in the comparable set are taken in `created_at` order oldest first.

Eight figures are returned. `tenant_count` is the number of leases in the comparable set. `comp_sqft` is the sum of their `leased_sqft`. `current_net_sales_cents` is the sum of the year-N `cumulative_net_sales_cents` across the set, and `prior_net_sales_cents` the same sum taken over year N minus 1. `percentage_rent_cents` is the sum of the year-N `cumulative_billed_cents` across the set. `comp_growth_bps` is the current net less the prior net, multiplied by 10000, divided by the prior net and rounded half up — and it is defined as 0 when the prior net is zero; it may be negative, in which case a value exactly halfway between two basis points rounds away from zero. `sales_psf_cents` is the current net divided by `comp_sqft`, floored to whole cents, and is defined as 0 when `comp_sqft` is zero. `occupancy_cost_ratio_bps` here is a center-level figure: the sum of each comparable lease's `annual_base_rent_cents` plus the `percentage_rent_cents` total, multiplied by 10000, divided by the current net and floored to whole basis points, defined as 0 when the current net is zero. A center with no comparable lease at all is not an error: the report returns HTTP 200 with `tenant_count` of 0 and every other metric at 0.
