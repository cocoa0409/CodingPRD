# Prior Learning Assessment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Universities have accepted for decades that learning happens outside the classroom — in military service schools, in industry certification programmes, on the job, and in nationally administered credit-by-examination — yet the office that must convert that learning into transcript credit typically runs on spreadsheets, email threads and hand-carried folders. The Prior Learning Assessment (PLA) office is asked to do four incompatible things at once: judge evidence fairly against published course learning outcomes, hold the institution's credit ceilings and residency requirements absolutely, bill and collect assessment fees that federal student aid is not permitted to cover, and do all of it inside a published turnaround promise. When those four duties are tracked in separate systems, students discover their credit cap was already exhausted only after paying for an assessment, faculty assessors are quietly overloaded, and a retired national credit recommendation keeps producing awards for months after the issuing body withdrew it.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. It governs the full arc of a prior-learning claim: the catalogue of degree programmes with their per-programme credit caps and residency floors, the courses and weighted learning outcomes credit is claimed against, the crosswalk that articulates national credential recommendations to local course equivalencies under segregated review and approval duties, the student's claim with its evidence channel, the portfolio of artifacts mapped to outcomes, the faculty assessor selected under a deterministic load-balancing policy, the rubric that produces a verified coverage score, the appeal that a denied student may file, the invoice that prices the work, and finally the registrar's transcript posting and the graduation audit that reads it back.

Two design commitments run through every section and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **the credit ledger is authoritative and pessimistic**: credits are reserved the moment a claim enters assessment, and a claim that cannot fit under the programme's ceiling is parked rather than silently accepted, then released back into contention in a strictly defined order when capacity reappears. Second, **every institutional decision is dated, versioned and attributable**: national recommendation terms are pinned at the moment a crosswalk is approved and re-validated at award and posting time, the student ledger is guarded by a version token the awarding coordinator must present, and no single person may both propose and approve the same articulation, or both score and adjudicate the same claim.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and revision semantics, arithmetic discipline, concurrency control, and the fixed order in which requests are validated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. A request missing either header is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The same error is returned when the acting role is backed by a persisted entity and `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing row of that entity. Once the caller is identified, the endpoint's own authorization list is consulted; a role that does not appear on the called endpoint's list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Student`, `PlaCoordinator`, `FacultyAssessor`, `AppealReviewer`, `ArticulationOfficer`, and `Registrar`.

The `Student` role is the claimant — it opens claims, builds portfolios, pays assessment fees, files appeals and reads its own graduation audit. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Student` compare the resource's student-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a claim's `student_id`, a portfolio's parent claim's `student_id`, an artifact's grandparent claim's `student_id`, an invoice's `student_id`, an appeal's parent claim's `student_id`, and the `student_id` path segment of a graduation audit must all equal `X-User-ID`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_STUDENT_SCOPE` (403).

The `FacultyAssessor` role is subject-matter faculty who accept assignments, score rubrics against course learning outcomes and complete assessments. When `X-User-Role` is `FacultyAssessor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyAssessor` row; an assignment-scoped operation is permitted only when the assignment's `assessor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ASSESSOR_SCOPE` (403).

The `AppealReviewer` role is an appeal-panel faculty member who adjudicates appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of a `FacultyAssessor` row — the two roles share one backing entity, which is precisely what makes the segregation-of-duties comparison against an appeal's `prior_scorer_assessor_ids` meaningful. An `AppealReviewer` whose backing `FacultyAssessor` row carries `appeal_panel_member` set to false is refused with `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_PANEL_MEMBER` (403).

The remaining three roles are operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `PlaCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `Registrar`, sole authority over the transcript. When `X-User-Role` is `ArticulationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it: submitting a crosswalk rule for review writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `reviewed_by_user_id`, and approving one writes it into `approved_by_user_id`. Those two recorded strings are compared to each other, never to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. A value outside those bounds is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, records are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Several collections carry a domain-specific default order instead, and where they do it is stated with the endpoint: learning outcomes sort by `outcome_code` ascending, crosswalk rules by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, students by `student_number` ascending, faculty assessors by `employee_number` ascending, prior-learning claims by `created_at` ascending and then `claim_number` ascending, assignments by `assigned_at` ascending, and appeals by `filed_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` counts the records matching the filter before pagination is applied; `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Two Revision Counters

Every resource is addressed by an `id`: a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and never supplied by the client. Alongside it, most resources carry `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only touch derived fields, and including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request against the row.

Two entity types are deliberately leaner. A `LearningOutcome` and an `Artifact` carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources. Everything else in the system — `Program`, `Course`, `CredentialRecommendation`, `ArticulationRule`, `Student`, `FacultyAssessor`, `PlaClaim`, `Portfolio`, `AssessorAssignment`, `RubricScore`, `Appeal` and `FeeInvoice` — carries the full quartet. Note that `RubricScore` carries the full quartet even though it is a child of an assignment.

A `CredentialRecommendation` is unusual in carrying **two** counters, and confusing them is the single easiest way to build this system wrong. The generic `version` is the optimistic-concurrency counter and moves on *every* write that touches the row: publication, revision, retirement, and each sibling crosswalk rule's approval, suspension or reinstatement. The separate `terms_version` counter moves only when the recommendation's substantive terms are revised — that is, only on the amendment operation that can change `recommended_credits`, `recommended_level`, `title` or `effective_to`. `terms_version` starts at 1. A crosswalk rule's `pinned_recommendation_version` is pinned to `terms_version`, never to `version`, which is exactly why approving one rule does not invalidate the pins its sibling rules already hold.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All deadline, validity-window and service-level comparisons resolve against a server-held logical clock, never against wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. The clock advances only when an operator explicitly sets it, supplying `current_utc`; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. A requested value earlier than the clock's present value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422) — the clock never moves backwards, though setting it to exactly its current value is permitted.

Advancing the clock fires no state transition by itself. Deadlines that have passed become *visible* immediately (a service-level posture recomputed on read will report the new reality), but assignments are only actually revoked when the escalation sweep is run.

### 2.6. Basis Points, Cents and Rounding

Three units appear throughout, and each has a fixed arithmetic discipline.

**Basis points.** Ratios, weights, thresholds and coverage scores are integers in basis points, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent. A course's outcome weights are basis points that must total exactly 10000. Coverage scores, mastery thresholds, pass thresholds, discount rates, surcharge rates and elapsed-time fractions are all basis points. Wherever a basis-point figure is produced by a division, the division is followed immediately by rounding half up to a whole basis point. Products of two integers are computed exactly; only the final division rounds.

**Cents.** All money is integer cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system and no decimal money fields. When an invoice is priced, **each line is rounded half up to whole cents before any summation, and the rounded lines are then added** — rounding the sum of unrounded lines is explicitly not the rule and produces different totals whenever a percentage line lands on a half cent.

**Credit hours.** Credits are whole integers. Where a credit ceiling is derived from a percentage, the product is truncated downward to the next whole credit — floored, never rounded.

"Round half up" means that a value exactly halfway between two representable results resolves to the larger magnitude in the positive direction; it is applied to the magnitude of the negative service-credit line as well, which is computed as a positive rounded amount and then negated.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency: Two Version Gates

Two operations in this system require the client to present the version of a *parent* row it last observed. Both are gates against acting on a stale view, and both are checked before any state precondition of the operation they guard.

Awarding a claim requires `expected_student_version` in the request body. It is a mandatory field, not an optional hint. Its value must equal the current `version` of the claim's `Student` row. The ledger fields the cap decision depends on — `pla_credits_reserved`, `pla_credits_awarded`, `pla_credits_posted` and `pla_upper_division_committed` — are mutated by every other award, every denial, every posting, every payment and every cascade, so a coordinator holding an older view of the student could pass a cap check that the current ledger would fail. When `expected_student_version` does not match, the award is refused with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and the coordinator must re-read the student before retrying.

Approving a crosswalk rule requires `expected_recommendation_version` in the request body, again mandatory. Its value must equal the current `version` — the generic optimistic-concurrency counter, not `terms_version` — of the parent `CredentialRecommendation`. Because publication, revision, retirement and every sibling rule's approval, suspension or reinstatement all advance that counter, a mismatch means the approver is about to pin terms they never observed and would silently articulate a superseded national credit recommendation to a local course. The mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_RECOMMENDATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, reinstating a suspended crosswalk rule carries no concurrency token at all, even though it re-pins `pinned_recommendation_version`; the only declared recommendation-level gate is on approval.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reference Checking Order

Identifiers in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, parent segment before child segment, and the first identifier that does not resolve ends the request with the 404 belonging to *that* segment. A request naming a portfolio that does not exist together with an artifact that does not exist reports `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`. The path-level 404 family is `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUBRIC_SCORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ARTICULATION_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. An artifact identifier that exists but belongs to a portfolio other than the one named in the path is also reported as `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`.

Only after every path segment resolves are identifiers carried in the request body resolved, and those are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. The body-level family adds `ERR_OUTCOME_NOT_FOUND` for a `learning_outcome_id` or `outcome_id` that does not exist, and reuses the same claim, student, course, assessor, recommendation and rule codes. A `supersedes_claim_id` that names no existing claim is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; a `reviewer_assessor_id` that names no existing assessor is `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Most endpoints in this system evaluate several independent rules, and the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract: a caller who violates two rules at once must receive the error belonging to the *lower-numbered* rung. The ladder below is exhaustive and strictly ordered. Where several rules share a rung number they are mutually exclusive in practice — they guard disjoint endpoints or disjoint fields — and may be evaluated in any order relative to one another.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present, and for `Student`, `FacultyAssessor` and `AppealReviewer` the supplied `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row of its backing entity. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears on the called endpoint's role list. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Student ownership scope.** A `Student` acts only on rows scoped to itself. Failure: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_STUDENT_SCOPE` (403). Applies to reading one claim, submitting a claim, withdrawing a claim, reading one portfolio, adding a portfolio artifact, deleting a portfolio artifact, submitting a portfolio, reading one invoice, paying an invoice, filing an appeal, and reading a graduation audit.
4. **Assessor assignment scope.** A `FacultyAssessor` acts only on an assignment whose `assessor_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Failure: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ASSESSOR_SCOPE` (403). Applies to accepting an assignment, declining an assignment, recording a rubric score, and completing an assignment.
5. **Appeal-panel membership.** The acting `AppealReviewer`'s backing `FacultyAssessor` row has `appeal_panel_member` true. Failure: `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_PANEL_MEMBER` (403). Applies to deciding an appeal.
6. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path exists, resolved left to right. Failure: the matching 404 from the family in §2.8. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every identifier referenced in the request body exists, resolved in the endpoint's declared field order. Failure: the matching 404. Applies to creating a claim, creating a crosswalk rule, creating a student, creating a course, creating a portfolio, creating an assessor assignment for a claim, recording a rubric score, filing an appeal, and assigning an appeal reviewer.
8. **Request shape.** Required input fields are present and every supplied field satisfies its declared type, enum domain and numeric range. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
9. **Claim-shape business rules.** Three sibling rules share this rung. (a) On claim creation, `credits_requested` is an integer of at least 1 and at most the target course's `credits`; failure is `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_REQUEST` (422). (b) On claim creation, `articulation_rule_id` is present for every `source_type` other than `PORTFOLIO` and absent for `PORTFOLIO`, and a supplied `supersedes_claim_id` names a claim of the same student and same course whose `assessment_status` is `DENIED_FINAL` or `WITHDRAWN`; failures are `ERR_ARTICULATION_RULE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ARTICULATION_RULE_FORBIDDEN` and `ERR_SUPERSEDED_CLAIM_NOT_TERMINAL`, all 422. (c) On portfolio creation, the target claim's `source_type` is `PORTFOLIO` and it has no portfolio yet; failures are `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_SOURCE` (422) and `ERR_PORTFOLIO_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409).
10. **Outcome weights.** The `learning_outcomes` supplied with a new course have `weight_bp` values totalling exactly 10000 and pairwise distinct `outcome_code` values. Failure: `ERR_OUTCOME_WEIGHTS_INVALID` (422). Applies to course creation.
11. **Rubric point range.** `points_awarded` is an integer from 0 through 4 inclusive. Failure: `ERR_RUBRIC_POINTS_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to recording a rubric score.
12. **Student ledger concurrency.** `expected_student_version` equals the current `Student` `version`. Failure: `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to awarding a claim.
13. **Recommendation concurrency.** `expected_recommendation_version` equals the current `CredentialRecommendation` `version`. Failure: `ERR_STALE_RECOMMENDATION_VERSION` (412). Applies to approving a crosswalk rule.
14. **Claim not terminal.** The claim's `assessment_status` is none of `AWARDED`, `DENIED_FINAL`, `WITHDRAWN`. Failure: `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to submitting a claim, withdrawing a claim, requesting a revision, denying a claim, creating an assessor assignment for a claim, and filing an appeal.
15. **From-state checks.** Three sibling rules share this rung. (a) The claim's `assessment_status` is a declared from-state of the requested assessment transition; failures are `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SCORED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DENIED` and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_AWARDED`, all 409, and this applies to submitting, requesting a revision, creating an assessor assignment, awarding, denying, filing an appeal, posting to the transcript and reversing a posting. (b) The crosswalk rule's `status`, or the recommendation's `status`, is a declared from-state; failures are `ERR_RULE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_RULE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_RULE_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_RULE_NOT_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_DRAFT` and `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_PUBLISHED`, all 409, and this applies to submitting a rule for review, approving, suspending, reinstating, publishing a recommendation, retiring a recommendation and amending a recommendation. (c) The claim's `credit_status` permits the operation — `NOT_POSTED` or `REVERSED` to post, `POSTED` to reverse; failures are `ERR_CREDIT_ALREADY_POSTED` and `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_POSTED`, both 409, applying to transcript posting and posting reversal.
16. **Portfolio state.** A portfolio in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` may take artifact additions, artifact deletions and submission; `SUBMITTED`, `LOCKED` and `VOIDED` permit none of them. Failures: `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_EDITABLE` and `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, both 409. Applies to adding an artifact, deleting an artifact and submitting a portfolio.
17. **Assignment state.** `ASSIGNED` permits accept and decline; `ACCEPTED` permits rubric scoring and completion. Failures: `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_PENDING` and `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`, both 409. Applies to accepting, declining, recording a rubric score and completing.
18. **Appeal state.** `FILED` permits reviewer assignment; `UNDER_REVIEW` permits a decision. Failures: `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FILED` and `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, both 409. Applies to assigning a reviewer and deciding.
19. **Invoice payability.** An invoice whose `fee_status` is `UNBILLED`, `WAIVED`, `REFUNDED` or `RETAINED` accepts no money movement, and an invoice already at `PAID` with `balance_due_cents` of 0 accepts no further payment. Failure: `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE` (409). Applies to paying an invoice and waiving an invoice.
20. **Segregation of duties.** The `X-User-ID` that submitted a crosswalk rule for review must not be the `X-User-ID` that approves it, and an appeal reviewer must not appear in the appeal's `prior_scorer_assessor_ids`. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to approving a crosswalk rule, assigning an appeal reviewer and deciding an appeal.
21. **Crosswalk still approved.** The claim's pinned crosswalk rule is `APPROVED` and its parent recommendation is `PUBLISHED`. Failure: `ERR_ARTICULATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to awarding a claim and posting to the transcript.
22. **Pin still current.** The pinned rule's `pinned_recommendation_version` equals the parent recommendation's current `terms_version`. Failure: `ERR_ARTICULATION_STALE_PIN` (409). Applies to awarding a claim and posting to the transcript. Because only a terms amendment moves `terms_version`, publication, retirement and sibling rule approvals never trip this rung.
23. **Outcome belongs to the course.** Every `learning_outcome_id` inside an artifact's `outcome_mappings`, and every `learning_outcome_id` on a rubric score, belongs to the claim's course. Failure: `ERR_OUTCOME_NOT_IN_COURSE` (422). Applies to adding an artifact and recording a rubric score.
24. **Source and course eligibility.** The claim's `source_type` is listed in the student's programme `allowed_source_types`, and the target course has `pla_eligible` true. Failures: `ERR_SOURCE_NOT_ALLOWED_BY_PROGRAM` and `ERR_COURSE_NOT_PLA_ELIGIBLE`, both 422. Applies to claim creation.
25. **Enrolment and programme status.** The student's `enrollment_status` is `MATRICULATED` and the programme's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Failures: `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_MATRICULATED` and `ERR_PROGRAM_INACTIVE`, both 409. Applies to claim creation, claim submission, awarding and transcript posting.
26. **Residency.** After the posting, the student's `transfer_credits_accepted` plus `pla_credits_posted` remains at most `total_credits_required` minus `residency_min_credits`. Failure: `ERR_RESIDENCY_REQUIREMENT_VIOLATION` (409). Applies to transcript posting.
27. **Attempt date inside the crosswalk window.** For `CREDIT_BY_EXAM` and `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL` claims, `attempt_date` falls inside the pinned rule's validity window, which includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`. Failure: `ERR_ATTEMPT_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (422). Applies to claim creation and claim submission.
28. **Appeal window open.** The logical clock reads strictly earlier than the claim's `appeal_deadline_at`; a clock standing exactly at the deadline finds it closed. Failure: `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to filing an appeal.
29. **Crosswalk window exclusivity.** No other `APPROVED` rule for the same recommendation-and-course pair has a validity window overlapping this one, under semantics that include `effective_from` and exclude `effective_to`; adjacency is legal. Failure: `ERR_ARTICULATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to approving and reinstating a crosswalk rule.
30. **Uniqueness and clock monotonicity.** Three sibling rules share this rung. (a) The student has no other claim for the same `course_id` in a non-terminal or `AWARDED` state, unless the new claim declares a `supersedes_claim_id` pointing at a `DENIED_FINAL` or `WITHDRAWN` claim for that course; failure is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409) on claim creation. (b) At most one rubric score exists per assignment-and-outcome pair; a second is rejected rather than overwriting, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RUBRIC_SCORE` (409). (c) A clock value supplied to the clock endpoint is greater than or equal to the current logical clock; failure is `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
31. **Assessor availability.** Two sibling rules share this rung. (a) The named assessor has never held an assignment on this claim in any status — per-claim exclusion is permanent; failure is `ERR_ASSESSOR_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409) on creating an assessor assignment. (b) An explicitly named assessor has `status` `ACTIVE`; failure is `ERR_ASSESSOR_INACTIVE` (409) on creating an assessor assignment and on assigning an appeal reviewer.
32. **Assessor eligibility and capacity.** An explicitly named assessor satisfies every remaining eligibility predicate — department match, credential-rank floor for the course level, and appeal-panel membership when a reviewer is being chosen — and has buffered-window capacity strictly below `max_concurrent_assignments`; when the server selects instead, the eligible pool is non-empty. Failures: `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422) when a non-capacity predicate fails, `ERR_ASSESSOR_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` (409) when the capacity predicate is the only one failing, and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR` (409) when server-side selection finds nobody. Applies to creating an assessor assignment and assigning an appeal reviewer.
33. **Round limits.** `revision_round` is strictly below 2 before another revision may be requested, and `appeal_count` is strictly below 1 before another appeal may be filed. Failures: `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, both 409. Applies to requesting a revision and filing an appeal respectively.
34. **Artifact floor.** A `PORTFOLIO`-source claim's portfolio has `artifact_count` of at least 1. Failure: `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NO_ARTIFACTS` (422). Applies to submitting a portfolio and submitting a claim.
35. **Provisional coverage floor.** `provisional_coverage_bp` is at least 5000 basis points at portfolio submission. Failure: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OUTCOME_COVERAGE` (422). Applies to submitting a portfolio.
36. **Rubric completeness and consistency.** Completion requires exactly one rubric score for every learning outcome of the claim's course, and the submitted `recommendation` must equal the recommendation the server computes from `verified_coverage_bp` against the effective pass threshold. Failures: `ERR_INCOMPLETE_RUBRIC` and `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_INCONSISTENT`, both 422. Applies to completing an assignment.
37. **Award bounded by the crosswalk.** `credits_awarded` is at most the pinned rule's `awarded_credits`, and the claim's course level matches the rule's recommended level. Failures: `ERR_AWARD_EXCEEDS_RECOMMENDATION` and `ERR_LEVEL_MISMATCH`, both 422. Applies to awarding a claim.
38. **Upper-division sub-cap.** For an `UPPER`-level course, `credits_awarded` is at most `pla_upper_division_max_credits` minus the student's `pla_upper_division_committed`. Failure: `ERR_UPPER_DIVISION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to awarding a claim and posting to the transcript.
39. **Programme credit cap.** At award, `credits_awarded` is at most the reserved-inclusive remaining capacity measured with this claim's own reservation released; at posting, `posted_credits` is at most the posted-only remaining capacity. Failure: `ERR_PLA_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to awarding a claim and posting to the transcript.
40. **Aid ineligibility.** A payment whose `method` is `FINANCIAL_AID` is refused, because assessment fees are institutional charges that Title IV aid may not cover and `aid_eligible` on every invoice is permanently false. Failure: `ERR_AID_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422). Applies to paying an invoice.
41. **No overpayment.** `amount_cents` is at most the invoice's `balance_due_cents`; an overpayment is refused outright and never auto-refunded. Failure: `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422). Applies to paying an invoice.
42. **Assessment fee settled.** The claim's invoice `fee_status` is `PAID` or `WAIVED` before an assessor may be assigned. Failure: `ERR_ASSESSMENT_FEE_UNPAID` (402). Applies to creating an assessor assignment.
43. **Appeal fee settled.** The appealed claim's invoice has `balance_due_cents` of 0 before a reviewer may be assigned. Failure: `ERR_APPEAL_FEE_UNPAID` (402). Applies to assigning an appeal reviewer.

The two fiscal gates sit deliberately at the bottom of the ladder: a request that is both unauthorised and unpaid reports the authorisation failure, and a request against a terminal claim that is also unpaid reports the terminal-claim conflict.

### 2.10. Cascades, Derived Counters and Hierarchical Consistency

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. A cascade is not a background job: it completes inside the originating request, and every row it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as a direct write would. Four cascades reach four hops deep and are specified in §3, but the general rules hold everywhere.

Derived counters are maintained by the server and are never client-writable. `Course.outcome_count` is the number of learning outcomes belonging to that course. `CredentialRecommendation.approved_rule_count` is the number of crosswalk rules referencing it whose `status` is `APPROVED`. `FacultyAssessor.active_assignment_count` is the number of that assessor's assignments whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED`. `Portfolio.artifact_count` is the number of artifacts belonging to that portfolio. `Student.fee_paid_net_cents` is the sum, over the student's invoices, of `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. `Student.pla_credits_reserved`, `pla_credits_awarded`, `pla_credits_posted` and `pla_upper_division_committed` are the four ledger counters described in §3.3; a registrar amendment of a student may change `enrollment_status`, `transfer_credits_accepted` and `institutional_credits_earned`, but never those four.

A claim carries three independent status dimensions on one row: `assessment_status`, `fee_status` and `credit_status`. Each advances on its own triggers. Except where a precondition says so explicitly, no transition in one dimension is a precondition of a transition in another — a claim can be `SCORED` while still `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and a claim's `credit_status` stays `NOT_POSTED` throughout the entire assessment dimension.

### 2.11. Credit Ceilings, Residency and the Two Capacity Variants

A programme's appetite for prior-learning credit is expressed by four configuration numbers, and the operative ceiling is the tightest of them. `pla_max_credits` is an absolute credit ceiling. `pla_max_pct_bp` is a percentage ceiling in basis points of `total_credits_required`; the credits it permits are `total_credits_required` multiplied by `pla_max_pct_bp`, divided by 10000, and then floored to a whole credit. The **programme ceiling** is the smaller of `pla_max_credits` and that floored percentage figure. `residency_min_credits` names the credits that must be earned in residence at this institution; neither transfer credit nor prior-learning credit ever counts toward residency, so the **residency headroom** available to non-resident credit is `total_credits_required` minus `residency_min_credits` minus the student's `transfer_credits_accepted`, and never below zero. The **effective cap** for a given student is the smaller of the programme ceiling and that student's residency headroom. `pla_upper_division_max_credits` is a separate sub-cap applying only to credit earned against courses whose `level` is `UPPER`.

The `Program` resource exposes `effective_pla_cap_credits`, computed by exactly this rule with `transfer_credits_accepted` treated as zero: the smaller of the programme ceiling and `total_credits_required` minus `residency_min_credits`. It is recomputed whenever the programme's configuration is amended. A student's graduation audit reports the student-specific effective cap under the same field name, with that student's transfer credit deducted.

Two variants of the remaining-capacity question are asked in different places, and they routinely disagree.

The **reserved-inclusive** variant is the gate for every forward-looking admission decision: claim submission, award, an overturned or remanded appeal, the cap-blocking cascade, and the unblock waterfall. It treats as already consumed the sum of `pla_credits_reserved`, `pla_credits_awarded` and `pla_credits_posted`, and reports the effective cap minus that sum. Claims in `DRAFT`, `CAP_BLOCKED`, `DENIED`, `APPEAL_PENDING`, `DENIED_FINAL` and `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing, and a reversed posting contributes nothing. When the decision under consideration concerns a claim that itself holds a live reservation — that is, a claim whose `assessment_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCORED` — that claim's own `credits_requested` is removed from the consumed sum before the headroom is measured, so a claim is never made to compete with itself.

The **posted-only** variant is the gate for transcript posting and for the graduation audit projection. It ignores reserved and awarded-but-unposted credit entirely and reports the effective cap minus `pla_credits_posted` alone. For an identical student row it therefore yields a strictly larger remaining figure whenever `pla_credits_reserved` plus `pla_credits_awarded` is positive. Both numbers are surfaced side by side on the graduation audit, as `pla_cap_remaining_posted_only` and `pla_cap_remaining_reserved_inclusive`, so the divergence is directly observable.

The upper-division remaining figure is a single number in both variants: `pla_upper_division_max_credits` minus `pla_upper_division_committed`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Programme Configuration, Courses and Weighted Learning Outcomes

Nothing else in the system can be created until a degree programme exists, because a programme is where the institution's tolerance for prior learning is written down. A `PlaCoordinator` registers one by supplying a catalogue code in `program_code` — unique across all programmes and between 2 and 16 uppercase alphanumeric characters — a human-readable `title`, and the graduation requirement `total_credits_required`, which must be at least 30. Alongside those come the four ceiling numbers described in §2.11: `residency_min_credits`, at least 0 and at most `total_credits_required`; `pla_max_credits`, at least 0; `pla_max_pct_bp`, an integer from 0 through 10000 inclusive; and `pla_upper_division_max_credits`, at least 0. The coordinator also declares `allowed_source_types`, a non-empty subset of the four eligible credit sources `CREDIT_BY_EXAM`, `PORTFOLIO`, `MILITARY_TRAINING` and `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL`. Two further fields are optional at registration: `pla_pass_threshold_bp`, which defaults to null and when supplied must be from 1 through 10000, and `status`, which defaults to `ACTIVE` and may only ever be `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`. An `INACTIVE` programme blocks both award and transcript posting for its students. The programme's `effective_pla_cap_credits` is computed at registration and recomputed on every amendment.

Amendments are the coordinator's lever when a faculty senate changes its mind. Any of `pla_max_credits`, `pla_max_pct_bp`, `pla_upper_division_max_credits`, `residency_min_credits`, `total_credits_required`, `pla_pass_threshold_bp`, `allowed_source_types` and `status` may be revised, each independently optional; the amendment recomputes `effective_pla_cap_credits` and increments the programme's `version`. Amendments are not retroactive to credit already posted — a programme whose cap is lowered below what a student has already posted will surface that fact as an audit finding rather than clawing credit back.

A course is the unit credit is claimed against. A `PlaCoordinator` creates one with a unique `course_code`, a `title`, a `department_code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters, a `credits` value from 1 through 12 inclusive, and a `level` of either `LOWER` or `UPPER`. The optional `pla_eligible` flag defaults to true; setting it false removes the course from prior-learning consideration entirely. Crucially, a course is created **together with its complete learning-outcome set** in one request: `learning_outcomes` is a list whose entries each carry an `outcome_code`, a `description`, a `weight_bp`, and optionally a `mastery_threshold_bp`. One learning outcome row is created per entry, and the course's `outcome_count` is set to the number created. Each created outcome's `course_id` is taken from the course being created and is thereafter immutable.

The weighting rules on that outcome set are strict, because every coverage score in the system divides by them. Each `outcome_code` is between 1 and 12 characters and must be unique within its course; it also serves as the deterministic sort key when outcomes are listed. Each `weight_bp` is from 1 through 10000, and **the `weight_bp` values of all outcomes belonging to one course must total exactly 10000** — a set totalling 9999 or 10001 is rejected, as is a set containing two identical outcome codes, both under `ERR_OUTCOME_WEIGHTS_INVALID`. Each `mastery_threshold_bp` is from 1 through 10000 and defaults to 7000 when omitted; it is the score ratio at or above which a rubric score marks that individual outcome as met. Learning outcomes may be read one at a time or listed with optional `course_id` and `outcome_code` filters, and a learning outcome carries only an `id` and a `created_at` — no revision counter and no modification timestamp, because outcomes are never edited after their course is created.

### 3.2. Articulating National Credential Recommendations to Local Courses

The bridge between a national body's judgement and this institution's catalogue is a two-layer structure: the **credential recommendation**, which records what an outside body recommends, and the **articulation rule**, a junction record that says which local course that recommendation buys and for how many credits. An `ArticulationOfficer` owns both layers.

A recommendation is recorded first, in `DRAFT`. Its `source_type` names which national channel it comes from and may be `CREDIT_BY_EXAM`, `MILITARY_TRAINING` or `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL` — `PORTFOLIO` is excluded here, since a student's own experiential portfolio has no national recommendation behind it. The officer supplies a `credential_code`, unique per combination of `source_type` and `credential_code`, a `title`, the `issuing_body` publishing the recommendation, a `recommended_credits` figure from 1 through 12, a `recommended_level` of `LOWER` or `UPPER`, an inclusive `effective_from` instant, and optionally an `effective_to` instant which defaults to null meaning open-ended. A recommendation's `status` cycles through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and `RETIRED`, and `approved_rule_count` starts at 0. Both revision counters described in §2.4 apply: `terms_version` starts at 1 and `version` follows the general rule.

Publication is the officer's assertion that the recommendation may be leaned on. It moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and requires that `effective_to` is either null or strictly later than `effective_from`; publication increments the recommendation's `version` but deliberately leaves `terms_version` untouched. Amending a recommendation is the only operation that moves `terms_version`: it accepts any of `recommended_credits`, `recommended_level`, `title` and `effective_to`, increments `terms_version` by one **and** increments `version` by one. It may be applied to a `DRAFT` or a `PUBLISHED` recommendation; attempting it on a `RETIRED` one is `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The consequence of an amendment is not applied eagerly: every already-approved crosswalk rule whose `pinned_recommendation_version` no longer matches the new `terms_version` becomes a stale pin, and that staleness is detected only when someone tries to award or post credit through it.

Drafting a crosswalk rule requires naming the `recommendation_id` being articulated and the `course_id` receiving the equivalency, together with `awarded_credits` — the local credit hours the equivalency grants, from 1 through 12 and never above the recommendation's `recommended_credits` — an inclusive `effective_from`, and an optional `effective_to` defaulting to null for open-ended. The rule begins in `DRAFT` with `pinned_recommendation_version`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `suspend_reason` all null.

Segregation of duties governs the rest of the rule's life, and it operates *within* the `ArticulationOfficer` role rather than between roles. Submitting a rule for review advances it `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps the acting `X-User-ID` into `reviewed_by_user_id`; it requires the parent recommendation to be `PUBLISHED`, requires `awarded_credits` to be at most `recommended_credits`, and — where the target course's `level` is `UPPER` — requires the recommendation's `recommended_level` to be `UPPER` as well. Approval then advances `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and stamps the approver's `X-User-ID` into `approved_by_user_id`, which **must differ** from `reviewed_by_user_id`; a self-approval is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval also demands the concurrency token `expected_recommendation_version` from §2.7, demands that the recommendation still be `PUBLISHED`, and runs the window-exclusivity check below. On success it pins `pinned_recommendation_version` to the recommendation's current `terms_version` — a counter this operation does not move, so a freshly approved rule is never stale — increments `approved_rule_count` by one, increments the recommendation's `version` by one, and increments the rule's own `version` by one.

**Window exclusivity** protects the catalogue from two contradictory equivalencies being live at once. Two rules conflict when they share both `recommendation_id` and `course_id`, the other rule's `status` is `APPROVED`, and their validity windows overlap under **half-open** semantics: each window includes its `effective_from` and excludes its `effective_to`, and a null `effective_to` extends to positive infinity. Formally, two windows overlap when the first window's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the second's `effective_to` and the second's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the first's `effective_to`, treating a null end as unbounded. Adjacency is therefore legal: a rule ending exactly at `2027-01-01T00:00:00Z` and a rule starting exactly at `2027-01-01T00:00:00Z` do not overlap and may both be `APPROVED`. Rules in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` never conflict with anything. A conflict is `ERR_ARTICULATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

An officer may suspend an `APPROVED` rule — perhaps because a department has raised a concern — which advances it `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`, sets `suspend_reason` to `MANUAL_REVIEW`, decrements the recommendation's `approved_rule_count` by one and increments the recommendation's `version` by one. Suspension does not stop there. **Every claim pinned to the suspended rule whose `assessment_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` is swept to `REVISION_REQUESTED` with `hold_reason` set to `ARTICULATION_SUSPENDED`.** This institutional hold is explicitly *not* charged to the student's revision budget: `revision_round` is not incremented. Each swept claim's assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` are moved to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and for each such assignment the assessor's `active_assignment_count` is decremented by one and that assessor's `version` incremented. Each swept claim's invoice, when its `fee_status` is `INVOICED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, is waived at the institution's expense: `waived_cents` absorbs the outstanding `balance_due_cents`, any net cash already paid is refunded, and `fee_status` becomes `WAIVED`. Claims already at `SCORED` are deliberately left untouched — they are stopped later, at the award decision, where the crosswalk check fires instead.

Reinstating a `SUSPENDED` rule reverses the institutional hold. It advances `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, requires the parent recommendation to be `PUBLISHED`, re-runs the window-exclusivity check against sibling `APPROVED` rules, clears `suspend_reason` to null, and **re-pins `pinned_recommendation_version` to the recommendation's current `terms_version`** — so a rule suspended before an amendment and reinstated after it is pinned to the new terms. It increments `approved_rule_count` by one and the recommendation's `version` by one. Claims that were parked with `hold_reason` `ARTICULATION_SUSPENDED` for this rule keep their `assessment_status` of `REVISION_REQUESTED` but have `hold_reason` cleared to null, which is what makes them re-submittable. Reinstatement carries no concurrency token. The cycle `APPROVED → SUSPENDED → APPROVED` may repeat indefinitely.

Retiring a recommendation is the heaviest single operation in the system, and it fires a four-hop cascade. The officer supplies a `retire_reason`, which is recorded on the row; the recommendation moves `PUBLISHED → RETIRED` and its `version` increments. **Hop one:** every crosswalk rule of this recommendation whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspend_reason` set to `RECOMMENDATION_RETIRED`, and `approved_rule_count` falls to 0. Any rule that was already `SUSPENDED` at retirement time moves to `RETIRED` instead, from which it can never return to `APPROVED`. **Hop two, caused by hop one:** every claim pinned to a just-suspended rule whose `assessment_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` moves to `REVISION_REQUESTED` with `hold_reason` `ARTICULATION_SUSPENDED`, again without incrementing `revision_round`; `SCORED` claims are again deliberately untouched. **Hop three, caused by hop two:** each such claim's assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` move to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `REVISION_REQUESTED`, decrementing each affected assessor's `active_assignment_count`, **and** each such claim's invoice whose `fee_status` is `INVOICED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID` is waived at the institution's expense — every cent of net paid cash is refunded, the whole `total_cents` is moved into `waived_cents`, `balance_due_cents` lands on 0, and `fee_status` becomes `WAIVED`. **Hop four, caused by hop three:** each affected student's `fee_paid_net_cents` is recomputed from the refunded invoices and that student's `version` is incremented.

Crosswalk rules may be read individually — the response exposes `pinned_recommendation_version`, which is a `terms_version` pin and not a generic version — or listed with optional `recommendation_id`, `course_id` and `status` filters. A recommendation read exposes its current `version` and `approved_rule_count`.

### 3.3. Students, the Four-Counter Credit Ledger, and Opening a Claim

A student record is created by a `PlaCoordinator` or a `Registrar` with a registrar-issued `student_number` unique across all students, a `full_name`, and the `program_id` of the programme whose caps and residency rule will govern every claim they make. Three further fields are optional: `enrollment_status`, which defaults to `MATRICULATED` and may be `APPLICANT`, `MATRICULATED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `GRADUATED`; `transfer_credits_accepted`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, which records credit already accepted by transfer and which consumes the very same non-residency headroom that prior-learning credit does; and `institutional_credits_earned`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, which records credit earned in residence. Only a `MATRICULATED` student may open or submit a claim.

Four system-maintained counters make up the prior-learning ledger, and all four start at 0. `pla_credits_reserved` holds credits committed by claims whose `assessment_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCORED`. `pla_credits_awarded` holds credits granted but not yet posted to the transcript. `pla_credits_posted` holds credits posted and not reversed. `pla_upper_division_committed` aggregates, over this student's claims against `UPPER`-level courses, the credits requested — or the credits awarded once an award has happened — for claims in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SCORED` or `AWARDED`, plus posted upper-division credits. A fifth derived figure, `fee_paid_net_cents`, is the cross-entity cash position: the sum over the student's invoices of `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. A registrar or coordinator amendment may revise `enrollment_status`, `transfer_credits_accepted` and `institutional_credits_earned` — each independently optional — which recomputes the effective cap view for that student because residency headroom depends on `transfer_credits_accepted`, and increments the student's `version`. The four ledger counters are never writable through that amendment. Students may be listed with optional `program_id` and `enrollment_status` filters, and a single student read exposes all four ledger counters plus the current `version` a coordinator needs for the award concurrency gate.

Opening a claim is deliberately cheap: a `Student` or a `PlaCoordinator` names the `student_id`, the `course_id`, the `source_type`, and `credits_requested`, and the claim comes into being in `assessment_status` `DRAFT`, `fee_status` `UNBILLED` and `credit_status` `NOT_POSTED` with no reservation, no invoice and no assessor. Four fields are optional at creation. `articulation_rule_id` names the crosswalk being relied on and is **required for every `source_type` other than `PORTFOLIO` and forbidden for `PORTFOLIO`** — omitting it on an exam, military or industry-credential claim is `ERR_ARTICULATION_RULE_REQUIRED`, and supplying it on a portfolio claim is `ERR_ARTICULATION_RULE_FORBIDDEN`. `attempt_date` records the date the exam was passed or the training completed and is likewise required for every source other than `PORTFOLIO`. `rush_requested` defaults to false; setting it true both adds the rush surcharge line to the invoice and halves the assessment turnaround allowance, and it sets `priority_tier` to `EXPEDITED` rather than the default `STANDARD`. `supersedes_claim_id` is the self-referential link a student uses to make a second attempt: it must point at a claim of the same student and the same course whose `assessment_status` is `DENIED_FINAL` or `WITHDRAWN`, and anything else — a claim still in flight, or a claim belonging to another student or another course — is `ERR_SUPERSEDED_CLAIM_NOT_TERMINAL`.

At creation the server assigns `claim_number` as one greater than the highest `claim_number` already used by that student, starting at 1 for a student's first claim. That number never changes and is the deterministic final tie-break wherever two of a student's claims share a `created_at`. The `credits_requested` figure must be an integer of at least 1 and at most the target course's `credits`, failing which the claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_REQUEST`. Creation also enforces that the `source_type` appears in the student's programme `allowed_source_types` and that the course's `pla_eligible` is true, that the student is `MATRICULATED` and the programme `ACTIVE`, and — for a `CREDIT_BY_EXAM` or `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL` claim — that `attempt_date` falls inside the pinned rule's validity window, which includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`.

One claim per course per student is the rule. A creation attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` when the student already holds another claim for the same `course_id` in any non-terminal state or in `AWARDED`, unless the new claim declares a `supersedes_claim_id` pointing at a `DENIED_FINAL` or `WITHDRAWN` claim for that course. For a `PORTFOLIO`-source claim no portfolio is created automatically; the student creates it explicitly as a separate act. A claim's `credits_awarded`, `provisional_coverage_bp`, `verified_coverage_bp`, `assessor_recommendation`, `hold_reason`, `denial_reason`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `submitted_at`, `posted_term` and `posted_credits` all begin null, while `revision_round`, `sla_breach_count` and `appeal_count` all begin at 0. Claims may be read individually — one read exposes all three status dimensions and both coverage figures — or listed with optional `student_id`, `course_id`, `articulation_rule_id`, `supersedes_claim_id`, `assessment_status`, `fee_status` and `credit_status` filters, sorted by `created_at` ascending then `claim_number` ascending, which is exactly the total order the unblock waterfall of §3.8 walks.

### 3.4. Portfolio Evidence, Artifacts and Self-Reported Coverage

A student pursuing the `PORTFOLIO` channel must assemble evidence that their experiential learning actually reaches the course's published outcomes. That evidence lives in a **Portfolio**, of which exactly one may exist per claim and only for a claim whose `source_type` is `PORTFOLIO`. The student creates it by naming the `claim_id` and optionally supplying a `narrative`, the prose statement of their experiential learning. A portfolio for a claim of any other source is refused with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_SOURCE`, and a second portfolio for the same claim with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_ALREADY_EXISTS`. A new portfolio begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `artifact_count` at 0, `revision_round` at 0, `submitted_at` null and both `provisional_coverage_bp` and `verified_coverage_bp` null. Its lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and terminates at either `LOCKED` or `VOIDED`.

Artifacts are the individual pieces of evidence. A student attaches one by supplying its `artifact_type` — one of `WORK_PRODUCT`, `EMPLOYER_LETTER`, `TRAINING_CERTIFICATE`, `LICENSE`, `MILITARY_RECORD` or `SUPERVISOR_EVALUATION` — a `title`, an `evidence_uri` locating the stored file, an optional `description` of what the artifact demonstrates, and the mapping payload `outcome_mappings`. The artifact's `portfolio_id` is taken from the parent portfolio rather than from the body. `outcome_mappings` is a non-empty array of between 1 and 12 objects, each carrying a `learning_outcome_id` and an `alignment` of either `PRIMARY` or `SUPPORTING`, with at most one entry per `learning_outcome_id`. Every `learning_outcome_id` must belong to the claim's course; one that does not is `ERR_OUTCOME_NOT_IN_COURSE`. Attaching an artifact increments `artifact_count` by one and recomputes the portfolio's `provisional_coverage_bp`. Deleting one decrements `artifact_count` by one and recomputes `provisional_coverage_bp` as well; a deleted artifact is gone rather than flagged, and it contributes nothing to any subsequent computation. Artifacts carry only an `id` and a `created_at` — no `updated_at` and no `version`. They may be read individually, exposing their `outcome_mappings`, or listed with optional `portfolio_id` and `artifact_type` filters.

Artifacts are mutable only while the portfolio is editable, meaning its `status` is `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Adding to or deleting from a portfolio in `SUBMITTED`, `LOCKED` or `VOIDED` is `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_EDITABLE`. Note carefully that the artifact count has two forward-looking effects and no retroactive ones: changing it changes the artifact surcharge tier and the turnaround multiplier tier that a *future* invoice recompute or a *future* assignment will pick up, but it never moves an already-priced invoice line or an already-issued deadline.

**Self-reported coverage** is the portfolio's own claim about how much of the course it reaches, expressed in basis points. It is a weight-weighted average over **every** learning outcome of the claim's course, taken in ascending `outcome_code` order, and outcomes with no evidence at all enter the sum contributing zero rather than being dropped from the denominator — the denominator is fixed at 10000 precisely because the course's weights total 10000. Each outcome earns a credit figure: 10000 basis points when any artifact of the portfolio maps that outcome with `alignment` `PRIMARY`; otherwise 5000 basis points when any artifact maps it with `alignment` `SUPPORTING`; otherwise 0. For each outcome, multiply its `weight_bp` by its credit figure — an exact integer product — sum those products across all outcomes, divide the total by 10000, and round half up to a whole basis point. That figure is `provisional_coverage_bp`. A portfolio that maps every outcome as `PRIMARY` therefore scores exactly 10000, regardless of how thin the evidence behind each mapping actually is; that generosity is the whole reason a faculty rubric exists.

Submitting a portfolio is what makes it assessable. The student may submit from `DRAFT` or from `REVISION_REQUESTED`; submitting from `SUBMITTED`, `LOCKED` or `VOIDED` is `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. Two evidence floors are enforced. First, `artifact_count` must be at least 1, or the submission fails with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NO_ARTIFACTS`. Second, the recomputed `provisional_coverage_bp` must be at least 5000 basis points — half the course's weighted outcomes reached, counting a `SUPPORTING` mapping as half credit — or the submission fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OUTCOME_COVERAGE`. On success the portfolio's `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `provisional_coverage_bp` is recomputed and frozen, and that same value is copied onto the parent claim's `provisional_coverage_bp`. The portfolio's `version` increments.

For non-portfolio sources there is no artifact evidence, so the claim's `provisional_coverage_bp` is derived arithmetically at claim submission instead: it is the pinned crosswalk rule's `awarded_credits` multiplied by 10000, divided by the course's `credits`, rounded half up, and then capped at 10000 — a rule granting 4 credits against a 3-credit course reports 10000, not 13333.

The portfolio cycles. When a coordinator sends a claim back for more evidence, the portfolio moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, its `revision_round` increments by one, and its artifacts become mutable again; the student then resubmits, which returns it to `SUBMITTED` under the same two floors and recomputes and re-copies `provisional_coverage_bp`. That `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` cycle is bounded by the claim's revision budget, not the portfolio's own. When the assessor finishes scoring, the portfolio moves `SUBMITTED → LOCKED`, receives the assignment's `verified_coverage_bp` as its own, and its artifacts become permanently immutable. When the claim is withdrawn, the portfolio moves to `VOIDED` from whichever of `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` it was in. Portfolios may be read individually, exposing `artifact_count` and both coverage figures, or listed with optional `claim_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.5. Submission, Reservation, Revision and Withdrawal

Submitting a claim is the moment the institution starts spending money and credit headroom on it, so it is the most heavily guarded transition in the assessment dimension. A `Student` or a `PlaCoordinator` submits, and the claim advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Nine conditions must hold. The student's `enrollment_status` must be `MATRICULATED` and their programme's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The target course must have `pla_eligible` true. The claim's `source_type` must be a member of the programme's `allowed_source_types`. `credits_requested` must be at least 1 and at most the course's `credits`. For any source other than `PORTFOLIO`, `articulation_rule_id` must be non-null, that rule's `status` must be `APPROVED`, the rule's `pinned_recommendation_version` must still equal the parent recommendation's `terms_version`, `credits_requested` must be at most the rule's `awarded_credits`, and `attempt_date` must fall inside the rule's validity window **and** inside the recommendation's own validity window, each window including its `effective_from` and excluding its `effective_to`. For a `PORTFOLIO` source, a portfolio must exist for this claim with `status` `SUBMITTED`, and its `artifact_count` must be at least 1. No other claim of the same student and course may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SCORED`, `CAP_BLOCKED`, `AWARDED`, `DENIED` or `APPEAL_PENDING`.

The last two conditions are the capacity gates, and they behave differently from the rest: instead of rejecting the request, they park the claim. The reserved-inclusive remaining capacity of §2.11 is measured for the student. **If `credits_requested` exceeds that remaining capacity, the claim lands in `assessment_status` `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` set to `PLA_CAP`.** If capacity is sufficient but the course's `level` is `UPPER` and `credits_requested` plus the student's `pla_upper_division_committed` would exceed the programme's `pla_upper_division_max_credits`, the claim lands in `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` set to `UPPER_DIVISION_CAP` instead. In both parked outcomes no reservation is taken, and the fee dimension is left where it stood — the invoice is created only by a submission that actually reaches `SUBMITTED`.

A submission that does reach `SUBMITTED` has five effects. `assessment_status` becomes `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, while `claim_number` remains whatever was fixed at creation. `priority_tier` is set to `EXPEDITED` when `rush_requested` is true and to `STANDARD` otherwise. `credits_requested` is added to the student's `pla_credits_reserved`, and when the course's `level` is `UPPER` the same amount is added to `pla_upper_division_committed`; the student's `version` increments by one. On a first submission the claim's invoice is created with its line set, subtotal, discount and total per §3.10, and the fee dimension advances `UNBILLED → INVOICED`. For non-portfolio sources `provisional_coverage_bp` is computed as described in §3.4. Finally the claim's own `version` increments by one.

Re-submission after a revision is the same transition read from a different starting state: `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, under all the same conditions with two adjustments. The duplicate-claim check ignores this claim itself, since it is one of the rows in a reserving state. And `hold_reason` must be null — a claim parked by an institutional articulation hold cannot be resubmitted until the crosswalk rule returns to `APPROVED`, which is what clears the hold. Re-submission does **not** re-increment `pla_credits_reserved`, because the reservation persisted unbroken through the `REVISION_REQUESTED` hold. Where a portfolio exists it must already be back in `status` `SUBMITTED`. The existing invoice is recomputed rather than recreated — the artifact tier and rush lines may well have changed since the first pricing — and `recompute_count` increments by one. If the recompute lowers the total below what has already been settled, so that net paid cash exceeds the new `total_cents`, the excess is moved into `refunded_cents` and `fee_status` remains `PAID`; if the recompute raises the total above net paid cash, `fee_status` becomes `PARTIALLY_PAID`. The claim's `version` increments.

Requesting a revision is the coordinator's instrument for demanding better evidence. It is available only from `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`; from anywhere else the request is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_ASSESSMENT`. The coordinator supplies a `revision_note`. The revision budget is two: `revision_round` must be below 2, since one more round is being consumed, and a third request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success `revision_round` increments by one and `assessment_status` becomes `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Every assignment of this claim in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and for each one the assessor's `active_assignment_count` decrements by one and that assessor's `version` increments by one. The claim's portfolio, when present, is reopened from `SUBMITTED` (or from `LOCKED`, if scoring had already locked it) to `REVISION_REQUESTED` with the portfolio's own `revision_round` incremented by one. The `credits_requested` reservation stays exactly where it is throughout, and the claim's `version` increments. Note the asymmetry with the institutional articulation hold of §3.2: that hold also lands the claim in `REVISION_REQUESTED` and also revokes assignments, but it does **not** consume a revision round and it does set `hold_reason`.

Withdrawal is available to a `Student` or a `PlaCoordinator` from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `CAP_BLOCKED`, and the caller supplies a `withdraw_reason`. In every case `assessment_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, the claim's portfolio when present moves to `VOIDED`, and the claim's `version` increments. What differs is the ledger and the money.

From `DRAFT` there is nothing to release and nothing to settle — no reservation was ever taken and no invoice exists. From `SUBMITTED`, `credits_requested` is subtracted from `pla_credits_reserved` (and from `pla_upper_division_committed` when the course is `UPPER`), the student's `version` increments, and the invoice is settled at a retention rate of zero basis points: all net cash is refunded, any remaining balance is absorbed into `waived_cents`, and `fee_status` becomes `REFUNDED` — or `WAIVED` when nothing had been paid at all. From `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` the same reservation release happens, but the retention rate is 5000 basis points: **half** the net cash paid is retained by the institution and only the other half is refunded, with `fee_status` becoming `REFUNDED`. Withdrawal from `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` additionally revokes every open assignment of the claim with `revoke_reason` `CLAIM_WITHDRAWN`, decrementing each affected assessor's `active_assignment_count`. From `REVISION_REQUESTED` the reservation — which persisted through the hold — is released, and the retention rate is zero. From `CAP_BLOCKED` there is no reservation left to release, because it was released the moment the claim was blocked, and the retention rate is again zero. Every withdrawal recomputes the student's `fee_paid_net_cents`, and every withdrawal from a state that held a reservation runs the cap unblock waterfall of §3.8 over the student's `CAP_BLOCKED` claims.

The claim's assessment dimension has exactly three terminal states — `AWARDED`, `DENIED_FINAL` and `WITHDRAWN` — and any attempt to advance a claim sitting in one of them is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_TERMINAL` before any more specific from-state check is consulted.

### 3.6. Assessor Selection, Buffered Capacity and Turnaround Service Levels

A `PlaCoordinator` moves a `SUBMITTED` claim into assessment by creating an assessor assignment for it, which advances the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_ASSESSMENT` and creates an `AssessorAssignment` row in `status` `ASSIGNED`. Two things must be true before selection even begins: the claim must be `SUBMITTED`, and the claim's invoice `fee_status` must be `PAID` or `WAIVED`. An unsettled fee blocks assessment with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_FEE_UNPAID` and HTTP 402 — the office does not spend faculty time on unpaid work.

Faculty assessors are registered by a `PlaCoordinator` with an `employee_number` unique across all assessors, a `full_name`, the `department_code` they may assess for, and a `credential_rank` from 1 through 3. Three fields are optional: `max_concurrent_assignments`, from 1 through 10, which bounds how many buffered assignment windows they may hold at once; `appeal_panel_member`, defaulting to false, which is what allows them to act under the `AppealReviewer` role; and `status`, defaulting to `ACTIVE` with the alternatives `ON_LEAVE` and `INACTIVE`. Their `active_assignment_count` starts at 0. An amendment may revise `status`, `credential_rank`, `max_concurrent_assignments`, `appeal_panel_member` and `department_code`, and increments the assessor's `version`. Assessors may be read individually, exposing the live `active_assignment_count` the selector ranks on, or listed with optional `department_code`, `status` and `appeal_panel_member` filters.

The coordinator may supply `assessor_id` to pin a specific person, or omit it and let the server choose. Either way the same **eligibility filter** applies, and every predicate must hold. The candidate's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. Their `department_code` must equal the claim's course's `department_code`. Their `credential_rank` must be at least 3 when the course's `level` is `UPPER`, and at least 2 when it is `LOWER`. Their buffered-window conflict count, computed as described below, must be strictly below their `max_concurrent_assignments`. And they must not be the `assessor_id` of **any** assignment of this claim in any status whatsoever — per-claim exclusion is permanent, so an assessor who declined, was revoked, or already completed a scoring round on this claim can never be assigned to it again.

**Buffered-window capacity** exists so that a single assessor is not handed four overlapping deadlines. The window being created is the closed interval running from the new assignment's `assigned_at` to its `due_at`. Each of the assessor's existing assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` projects a padded closed interval running from 24 hours before its `assigned_at` to 24 hours after its `due_at`. The conflict count is the number of those padded intervals that intersect the new window. Because both intervals are closed on both ends, **touching endpoints do collide**: a new window beginning exactly 24 hours after another assignment's `due_at` conflicts, while one beginning a single millisecond later does not. Comparisons are made at millisecond resolution. Assignments in `DECLINED`, `REVOKED` or `SCORING_COMPLETE` have released their windows and never collide. Capacity is satisfied when the conflict count is strictly less than `max_concurrent_assignments`.

When the coordinator names an assessor explicitly, the failure modes are distinguished. A candidate failing only the `status` predicate is `ERR_ASSESSOR_INACTIVE` (409). One failing only the buffered-window capacity predicate is `ERR_ASSESSOR_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` (409). One who has ever held an assignment on this claim is `ERR_ASSESSOR_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409). Any other failing predicate — a department mismatch, a `credential_rank` below the course-level floor, or for a reviewer a false `appeal_panel_member` — is `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).

When the coordinator omits `assessor_id`, the server ranks the surviving pool and takes the first row. The ordering is by `active_assignment_count` ascending, then `credential_rank` descending, then `created_at` ascending, then `employee_number` ascending. That is a total order because `employee_number` is unique, so the choice is fully deterministic; no random property, and specifically never the row's `id`, participates in the filter, the ranking or any tie-break. Read plainly: the least-loaded assessor wins, ties break toward the *more* credentialed assessor, then toward the longer-registered one, then alphanumerically by employee number. An empty pool on a directly requested assignment is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR` (409).

Creating the assignment records `selection_reason` as `EXPLICIT` when the coordinator named the assessor and `AUTO_SELECTED` when the server chose. `assigned_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and **the turnaround clock starts there regardless of whether the assessor ever accepts.** The chosen assessor's `active_assignment_count` increments by one and their `version` increments by one; the claim's `assessment_status` becomes `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, its `hold_reason` is cleared to null, and its `version` increments by one.

**The turnaround allowance** is computed once, at assignment creation, from the claim's `source_type` and `priority_tier` as they stand at that instant plus the artifact count of the claim's portfolio at that instant. The source base allowance is 72 hours for `CREDIT_BY_EXAM`, 240 hours for `PORTFOLIO`, 96 hours for `MILITARY_TRAINING` and 96 hours for `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL`. The artifact tier then stretches it: a portfolio holding 3 artifacts or fewer applies a multiplier of 10000 basis points (no change), one holding 4 through 8 artifacts applies 12500 basis points, and one holding 9 or more applies 15000 basis points; a claim with no portfolio at all applies 10000 basis points. **Rounding happens in two ordered steps and the order matters.** Step one: multiply the source base by the artifact multiplier, divide by 10000, and round half up to whole hours — call this the tiered allowance. Step two: when the claim's `priority_tier` is `EXPEDITED`, multiply the tiered allowance by 5000 basis points, divide by 10000, and round half up to whole hours; when it is `STANDARD`, the tiered allowance stands unchanged. The result is `sla_hours`.

`due_at` is `assigned_at` advanced by `sla_hours` hours. `at_risk_at` is `assigned_at` advanced by `sla_hours` multiplied by 8000 basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole hours — and it is derived from the **final** `sla_hours`, never from the pre-rush tiered allowance. To make the intent unmistakable: an `EXPEDITED` portfolio claim whose portfolio holds 5 artifacts has a tiered allowance of 300 hours, an `sla_hours` of 150, and an at-risk offset of 120 hours — not an `sla_hours` of 150 with an at-risk offset of 240. Later artifact edits never move an existing assignment's `due_at`; only a newly created assignment picks up a new tier.

**Service-level posture** is recomputed on every read of an assignment and on every escalation sweep, and it is never stored stale. Elapsed hours is the exact, possibly fractional, difference between the logical clock and `assigned_at`. `elapsed_bp` is that elapsed figure multiplied by 10000, divided by `sla_hours`, rounded half up. `sla_state` is `BREACHED` when `elapsed_bp` is at least 10000, `AT_RISK` when it is at least 8000 but below 10000, and `ON_TRACK` otherwise. Both comparisons are inclusive: a clock standing exactly at `at_risk_at` reports `AT_RISK`, and a clock standing exactly at `due_at` reports `BREACHED`. Only assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` are candidates for revocation by the sweep; `DECLINED`, `REVOKED` and `SCORING_COMPLETE` assignments keep their last computed `sla_state` for display but are never revoked a second time.

An assessor accepts an assignment from `ASSIGNED`, which advances it to `ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at` with the logical clock. Acceptance requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the assignment's `assessor_id` and requires the assessor's own `status` to still be `ACTIVE` — an assessor who went `ON_LEAVE` after being assigned cannot accept. Neither `sla_hours` nor `due_at` nor `at_risk_at` is recomputed on acceptance, because the allowance was never anchored to acceptance. Accepting from any status other than `ASSIGNED` is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_PENDING`, and the assignment's `version` increments on success.

Declining is the alternative, also only from `ASSIGNED` and also only by the named assessor. It sets `status` to `DECLINED`, records the free-text `decline_reason`, decrements the assessor's `active_assignment_count` by one and increments that assessor's `version`. It then fires the **replacement cascade**: the selector runs again over the eligible pool with every assessor ever assigned to this claim excluded, and the winner receives a new assignment with `selection_reason` `AUTO_REPLACEMENT` and their `active_assignment_count` incremented by one. When that pool turns out to be empty, no replacement is created, the claim's `assessment_status` stays `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, and the claim's `hold_reason` is set to `NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR` so the office can see why nothing is moving. Assignments may be read individually — a read recomputes `elapsed_bp` and `sla_state` without changing `status` — or listed with optional `claim_id`, `assessor_id`, `status` and `sla_state` filters, sorted by `assigned_at` ascending. An assignment's `status` is one of `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `SCORING_COMPLETE` or `REVOKED`, all three of the last group being terminal. Its `revoke_reason` is null until it is revoked and then carries exactly one of `SLA_BREACH`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLAIM_WITHDRAWN`, `CAP_BLOCKED` or `ASSESSOR_DECLINED`. Its `recommendation` is null until completion, when it becomes `PASS` or `FAIL`, and its `accepted_at`, `rubric_submitted_at` and `verified_coverage_bp` are likewise null until the moments that set them.

**The escalation sweep** is the coordinator's periodic enforcement pass over every assignment in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED`, evaluated in order of `due_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. It recomputes each candidate's posture and reports `evaluated_count`, `at_risk_count` and `breached_count` alongside the identifier lists `revoked_assignment_ids`, `replacement_assignment_ids` and `credited_invoice_ids`. Each `BREACHED` assignment fires a four-hop cascade. **Hop one:** the assignment moves to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `SLA_BREACH`, and the assessor's `active_assignment_count` decrements by one with their `version` incremented. **Hop two:** the parent claim's `sla_breach_count` increments by one and its `priority_tier` is escalated to `EXPEDITED`, so any replacement assignment is issued on the halved allowance. **Hop three:** the claim's invoice gains a negative service-credit line and is repriced, which can re-select a lower discount tier and therefore change `discount_bp` as well as `total_cents`; the resulting overpayment is auto-refunded so that `balance_due_cents` returns to 0 and `fee_status` remains `PAID`. **Hop four:** a replacement assignment is created through the selector, again excluding every assessor ever assigned to this claim, or the claim receives `hold_reason` `NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR` when the pool is empty. Every affected student's `fee_paid_net_cents` is recomputed as part of hop three.

### 3.7. Rubric Scoring, Verified Coverage and Completing an Assessment

An assessor who has accepted an assignment scores it one learning outcome at a time. Each rubric score names a `learning_outcome_id` and a `points_awarded` value, with an optional `narrative`. Scoring is permitted only while the assignment's `status` is `ACCEPTED`; from any other status the attempt is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Only the assessor named in the assignment's `assessor_id` may score it. `points_awarded` must be an integer from 0 through 4 inclusive; anything else is `ERR_RUBRIC_POINTS_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The named outcome must belong to the course of the assignment's claim, or the request is `ERR_OUTCOME_NOT_IN_COURSE`. The pair of `assignment_id` and `learning_outcome_id` is unique: a second score for the same outcome on the same assignment is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RUBRIC_SCORE` and is emphatically **not** an overwrite of the first.

Each stored score carries `points_possible`, which is always 4, and two derived fields. `score_ratio_bp` is `points_awarded` multiplied by 10000, divided by 4, rounded half up — so 0 points is 0, 1 point is 2500, 2 points is 5000, 3 points is 7500 and 4 points is 10000. `outcome_met` is true when `score_ratio_bp` is at least the outcome's own `mastery_threshold_bp` and false otherwise, which means the per-outcome mastery flag and the aggregate pass decision are two different judgements computed from two different thresholds. Rubric scores may be read individually or listed with optional `assignment_id` and `learning_outcome_id` filters, sorted by `created_at` ascending.

Completion is the act that turns a pile of rubric scores into an institutional recommendation. The assessor submits a `recommendation` value. Four things must hold: the assignment's `status` is `ACCEPTED`; the acting user is the assignment's assessor; exactly one rubric score exists for **every** learning outcome of the claim's course, so a course with seven outcomes needs seven scores and six is `ERR_INCOMPLETE_RUBRIC`; and the submitted `recommendation` agrees with the recommendation the server computes for itself, or the request is `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_INCONSISTENT`.

**Verified coverage** is the assessor-verified variant of the coverage calculation, and it shares its shape with the provisional variant of §3.4 while replacing the input. For each learning outcome of the claim's course, taken in `outcome_code` ascending order, the outcome's contribution is its `weight_bp` multiplied by the `score_ratio_bp` of that outcome's rubric score on **this** assignment — or by 0 when the outcome carries no score, though completion cannot succeed in that condition. Those products are summed exactly, as integers, and only then divided by 10000 and rounded half up to yield `verified_coverage_bp`. The denominator is fixed at 10000 because course weights sum to exactly 10000, and an outcome that scored zero enters the sum as a zero contribution rather than being dropped from the denominator. Scores belonging to `REVOKED` or `DECLINED` assignments of the same claim never enter this figure; only the completing assignment's own scores do. Because a rubric ratio is at most 10000 and reaches it only at a perfect 4 of 4, verified coverage is strictly below the provisional figure for identical mappings whenever any outcome scores below 4.

The **effective pass threshold** is the programme's `pla_pass_threshold_bp` when that override is set, and 7000 basis points when it is null. The computed recommendation is `PASS` when `verified_coverage_bp` is at least the effective threshold and `FAIL` otherwise; the comparison is inclusive, so coverage landing exactly on the threshold passes. Note that this pass decision uses the verified variant only — the student's self-reported provisional figure never decides pass or fail, it only decides whether the portfolio was submittable at all.

A successful completion has five effects. The assignment moves `ACCEPTED → SCORING_COMPLETE`, `rubric_submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the computed `verified_coverage_bp` is stored on the assignment. The assessor's `active_assignment_count` decrements by one and their `version` increments by one. The claim's `verified_coverage_bp` is copied from the assignment, its `assessor_recommendation` is set to the recommendation, its `assessment_status` advances `UNDER_ASSESSMENT → SCORED`, and its `version` increments. The claim's portfolio, when present, advances `SUBMITTED → LOCKED` with `verified_coverage_bp` copied over from the completing assignment, after which its artifacts are immutable. The claim now waits on a human decision; nothing about `SCORED` releases or changes the credit reservation.

### 3.8. Award, Denial and the Cap Unblock Waterfall

Awarding is the coordinator's decision to grant credit, and it is guarded by eight preconditions plus the concurrency gate of §2.7. The claim's `assessment_status` must be `SCORED` and its `assessor_recommendation` must be `PASS` — a `FAIL` claim cannot be awarded. The programme's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. For any source other than `PORTFOLIO` the pinned crosswalk rule's `status` must still be `APPROVED` and its `pinned_recommendation_version` must still equal the parent recommendation's `terms_version`; those two checks are what make the deliberately un-swept `SCORED` claim of §3.2 fail at the award desk rather than earlier, with `ERR_ARTICULATION_NOT_APPROVED` and `ERR_ARTICULATION_STALE_PIN` respectively. The invoice's `fee_status` must be `PAID` or `WAIVED`. `credits_awarded` must not exceed `credits_requested`, and for non-portfolio sources must also not exceed the pinned rule's `awarded_credits`. The reserved-inclusive remaining capacity, measured with **this claim's own reservation treated as already released**, must cover `credits_awarded`. And for an `UPPER`-level course, `credits_awarded` plus the student's `pla_upper_division_committed` reduced by this claim's own `credits_requested` must not exceed the programme's `pla_upper_division_max_credits`.

The request body carries `expected_student_version` and `credits_awarded`. Where `credits_awarded` is omitted the server treats it as `credits_requested`, which is the ordinary full award; supplying a smaller figure is a partial award and is legitimate. On success `assessment_status` becomes `AWARDED` and `credits_awarded` is recorded on the claim. The ledger moves in one step: `credits_requested` leaves `pla_credits_reserved` and `credits_awarded` enters `pla_credits_awarded`, so a partial award frees the difference outright. When the course is `UPPER`, `pla_upper_division_committed` is adjusted by the difference between `credits_awarded` and `credits_requested` — that is, it falls by the un-awarded remainder. The student's `version` increments by one and the claim's `version` increments by one. `AWARDED` is terminal in the assessment dimension: nothing further may move the claim there, though the credit dimension is only just beginning.

**The cap-blocking cascade** fires from the award, and it is the mirror image of the unblock waterfall. After the ledger update, the student's reserved-inclusive remaining capacity is measured again. If it is exactly 0, every **other** claim of this student whose `assessment_status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` or `SCORED` is moved to `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` `PLA_CAP`, and each one's reservation is released — `credits_requested` leaves `pla_credits_reserved`, and leaves `pla_upper_division_committed` too when that claim's course is `UPPER`. For each claim so blocked, every assignment in `ASSIGNED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `CAP_BLOCKED`, and each affected assessor's `active_assignment_count` decrements by one with their `version` incremented. That is a depth-three cascade from a single award: claim, then sibling claims and the student ledger, then the assessors' workload counters.

Denial is the other side of the `SCORED` fork and is far simpler. Only a `SCORED` claim may be denied — anything else is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SCORED` — and the coordinator supplies a `denial_reason`. `assessment_status` becomes `DENIED`, the reason is recorded, and `appeal_deadline_at` is set to the logical clock advanced by 336 hours. The reservation is released: `credits_requested` leaves `pla_credits_reserved`, and leaves `pla_upper_division_committed` too for an `UPPER` course, with the student's `version` incremented. The invoice is deliberately **not** refunded — the assessment was performed and the fee is earned — so `fee_status` is untouched. The freed capacity then drives the unblock waterfall, and the claim's `version` increments. Note that `DENIED` is not terminal: it is the doorway to appeal.

**The cap unblock waterfall** runs whenever capacity is freed — on a withdrawal, on a denial and on a transcript posting reversal. It begins by measuring the student's reserved-inclusive remaining capacity. It then walks that student's claims in `CAP_BLOCKED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending and then `claim_number` ascending, a total order because `claim_number` is unique within a student. For each candidate in turn, the walk asks whether the running remaining capacity covers the candidate's `credits_requested` **and**, for an `UPPER`-level course, whether the upper-division remaining headroom covers it too. When both hold, the candidate returns to `SUBMITTED` with `hold_reason` cleared to null, its `credits_requested` is added back into `pla_credits_reserved`, the running remaining figure is reduced by that amount, and the claim's `version` increments. When either fails, the candidate stays in `CAP_BLOCKED` and **the walk continues to the next candidate rather than stopping.** This skip-and-continue behaviour is the rule and it matters: a single pass, first-fit by creation order, is what the system does, and a claim skipped for being too large is not revisited later in the same pass even if nothing else consumes the headroom it was denied. Best-fit packing, or halting at the first claim that does not fit, would unblock a different subset and is not the rule.

### 3.9. Appeals

A denied student has one appeal, and one only. Filing is a `Student` act: the body names a `claim_id` and states the `grounds`. Four conditions must hold. The claim's `assessment_status` must be `DENIED`, or the filing is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DENIED`. The logical clock must read strictly earlier than the claim's `appeal_deadline_at`; a clock standing exactly at the deadline finds the window closed, with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The claim's `appeal_count` must be strictly below 1, so a claim that has already been appealed once is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. And no appeal of this claim may currently sit in `FILED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`.

Filing creates an `Appeal` in `status` `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and it snapshots `prior_scorer_assessor_ids` — the identifiers of every assessor who scored this claim. That snapshot is taken at filing time and is what the segregation-of-duties check consults later; it does not track assessors added afterwards. The appeal's `reviewer_assessor_id`, `assigned_at`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` all begin null. The claim's `appeal_count` increments by one, its `assessment_status` advances `DENIED → APPEAL_PENDING`, and its `version` increments. On the money side, an appeal filing fee line is appended to the claim's invoice and the whole invoice is repriced: the subtotal rises, the discount tier is re-selected from the **new** pre-discount subtotal, `discount_bp`, `discount_cents` and `total_cents` are recomputed, and `recompute_count` increments by one. Because a settled invoice has just grown, `fee_status` moves `PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` whenever net paid cash falls short of the new `total_cents` — the backward step that makes the fee dimension cyclic. Appeals may be read individually, exposing `prior_scorer_assessor_ids`, or listed with optional `claim_id`, `reviewer_assessor_id` and `status` filters, sorted by `filed_at` ascending.

A `PlaCoordinator` then assigns a reviewer, advancing the appeal `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW`. From any other status the request is `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FILED`. The appealed claim's invoice must be fully settled — `balance_due_cents` of 0 — before review may begin; an outstanding filing fee is `ERR_APPEAL_FEE_UNPAID` with HTTP 402. The coordinator may name a `reviewer_assessor_id` or omit it and let the selector choose. The eligibility filter is the assessor filter of §3.6 with two additions: the candidate's `appeal_panel_member` must be true, and the candidate must not appear in the appeal's `prior_scorer_assessor_ids`. Naming a reviewer who scored the claim is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; naming one who is not an appeal-panel member is `ERR_ASSESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; letting the server select from an empty pool is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR`. On success `reviewer_assessor_id` is set, `assigned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the appeal's `version` increments.

The reviewer then decides, acting under the `AppealReviewer` role. The body carries a `decision` of `UPHELD`, `OVERTURNED` or `REMANDED`, an optional `decision_note`, and `credits_awarded`, which is required when the decision is `OVERTURNED` and forbidden otherwise — supplying it on an `UPHELD` or `REMANDED` decision is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is any `decision` value outside the three. The appeal's `status` must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the request is `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. The acting user must be the appeal's `reviewer_assessor_id`, their `appeal_panel_member` must still be true — otherwise `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_PANEL_MEMBER` — and they must not appear in `prior_scorer_assessor_ids`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every decision sets `decided_at` from the logical clock and records `decision_note`.

**Upholding** the denial closes the matter. The appeal's `status` becomes `UPHELD`, the claim's `assessment_status` advances `APPEAL_PENDING → DENIED_FINAL`, which is terminal, and the fee moves `PAID → RETAINED`: no cash moves, and the institution recognises the fee as earned. This transition requires the claim's `fee_status` to be `PAID`, which the reviewer-assignment gate already guaranteed.

**Overturning** the denial grants the credit. The appeal's `status` becomes `OVERTURNED`. The programme must be `ACTIVE`, and for non-portfolio sources the pinned rule must still be `APPROVED` with its pin current. The claim holds no reservation while it sits in `DENIED` or `APPEAL_PENDING`, so the reserved-inclusive remaining capacity must cover `credits_requested` outright. When it does, the claim's `assessment_status` becomes `AWARDED` with `assessor_recommendation` set to `PASS` and `credits_awarded` recorded from the request — ordinarily the full `credits_requested`. The student's `pla_credits_awarded` rises by `credits_awarded`, `pla_upper_division_committed` rises by the same amount when the course is `UPPER`, and the student's `version` increments. The fee is refunded in full: `fee_status` moves `PAID → REFUNDED` with `refunded_cents` increased by the whole net paid figure, and the student's `fee_paid_net_cents` is recomputed as the sum over their invoices of `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. The same depth-four cap-blocking cascade the award desk fires then runs: the claim reaches `AWARDED`, the ledger updates, and if the reserved-inclusive remaining capacity is now 0 the student's sibling claims in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` or `SCORED` fall to `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` `PLA_CAP` and their reservations released, whose open assignments in turn go `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `CAP_BLOCKED` and their assessors' `active_assignment_count` decremented. When the reserved-inclusive capacity does **not** cover `credits_requested`, the claim lands in `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` `PLA_CAP` instead, and the fee refund still runs.

**Remanding** sends the claim back for a fresh assessment. The appeal's `status` becomes `REMANDED`. The reserved-inclusive remaining capacity must cover `credits_requested`, because the reservation is being re-taken. The claim's `assessment_status` returns to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` and four fields are cleared to null: `assessor_recommendation`, `verified_coverage_bp`, `denial_reason` and `appeal_deadline_at`. `credits_requested` is added back into `pla_credits_reserved` and the student's `version` increments. A replacement assignment is created through the selector, excluding every assessor ever assigned to this claim, with `selection_reason` `AUTO_REPLACEMENT`; when that pool is empty no assignment is created and the claim takes `hold_reason` `NO_ELIGIBLE_ASSESSOR`. Where the reserved-inclusive capacity does not cover `credits_requested`, the claim lands in `CAP_BLOCKED` with `hold_reason` `PLA_CAP` and no replacement assignment is created at all. On the money side the appeal filing fee line is removed from the invoice, the subtotal, discount tier and total are recomputed from the reduced line set, `recompute_count` increments, and exactly 6000 cents is refunded — `refunded_cents` rises by 6000, with no basis-point arithmetic applied to that figure. Because removing the appeal line also removes the discount that line had earned, the refund does not always settle the invoice exactly: when net paid cash then equals `total_cents` minus `waived_cents` the `fee_status` returns to `PAID`, and when it falls short the `fee_status` stays `PARTIALLY_PAID` with the residue — precisely the discount the appeal line had earned — exposed in `balance_due_cents`. That positive residue is **not** swept to zero; only a negative balance is swept, as §3.10 describes.

### 3.10. Assessment Fees: Pricing, Payment, Waiver and Settlement

Exactly one invoice exists per claim, created by the claim's first submission, and its `claim_id` is unique across all invoices. The invoice copies `student_id` from the claim at creation and carries `aid_eligible`, which is always false because prior-learning assessment fees are institutional charges that Title IV aid may not cover. The state of the money is carried on the claim as its `fee_status` dimension, and because the invoice-to-claim relationship is one-to-one, invoices may be listed by `claim_id`, `student_id` or `fee_status`, sorted by `created_at` ascending. A single invoice read exposes the whole line breakdown, the discount tier and the balance.

**Pricing runs in four steps, and it runs in full on every path that touches the line set** — first submission, re-submission after revision, appeal filing, appeal remand and the service-credit cascade. `recompute_count` counts every one of those recomputations after creation.

**Step one builds the line set, and each line is rounded to whole cents before anything is summed.** The base assessment fee is 5000 cents for a `CREDIT_BY_EXAM` claim, 15000 cents for `PORTFOLIO`, nothing at all for `MILITARY_TRAINING`, and 7500 cents for `INDUSTRY_CREDENTIAL`. The per-credit fee is 2500 cents multiplied by `credits_requested`. The artifact tier surcharge reads the `artifact_count` of the claim's portfolio and charges nothing for 3 artifacts or fewer, 4000 cents for 4 through 8, and 9000 cents for 9 or more; a claim with no portfolio is charged nothing on this line. The rush surcharge applies only when `rush_requested` is true, and equals the base fee plus the per-credit fee, multiplied by 2500 basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up — note that it is computed from those two lines only, before the artifact surcharge. The appeal filing fee is 6000 cents whenever an appeal of this claim has `status` `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED`, and nothing when there is no such appeal; a `REMANDED` appeal is excluded from that set, which is exactly how the remand refund expresses itself in the pricing. The service credit is a **negative** line, present only when the claim's `sla_breach_count` is at least 1, equal to minus one times the base, per-credit, artifact and rush lines summed, multiplied by 1500 basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up — the appeal line is deliberately outside that base.

The stored `lines` array holds objects of `line_type` and `amount_cents` in one fixed order: `BASE_ASSESSMENT_FEE`, `PER_CREDIT_FEE`, `ARTIFACT_TIER_SURCHARGE`, `RUSH_SURCHARGE`, `APPEAL_FILING_FEE`, `SLA_SERVICE_CREDIT`. Lines whose amount is zero are omitted from the array entirely, so a `MILITARY_TRAINING` claim with no portfolio, no rush and no appeal shows a single line.

**Step two sums the already-rounded lines** into `subtotal_cents`. Round-per-line-then-sum is the pinned rule. Summing first and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, and the two disagree in cents whenever the rush line or the service-credit line has a half-cent remainder.

**Step three applies the volume discount, and its tier is keyed on the pre-discount `subtotal_cents`.** A subtotal of at least 25000 cents earns `discount_bp` of 1000; a subtotal of at least 10000 but below 25000 earns 500; anything below 10000 earns 0. The circular-looking dependency is broken here deliberately and in favour of the pre-discount figure: keying the tier on `total_cents` is not the rule. `discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `discount_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents`. `balance_due_cents` is `total_cents` minus `waived_cents` minus the net cash figure of `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. Because the tier is keyed on the subtotal, a negative service-credit line can drop a claim into a lower discount band and therefore change `discount_bp` as well as the total.

**Step four is the excess sweep.** A recompute can lower `total_cents` below what the student has already settled, making `balance_due_cents` negative. The overpayment is then moved into `refunded_cents` so that `balance_due_cents` lands on exactly 0. Clamping the balance at zero with a floor and leaving the money unaccounted for is not the rule. The sweep runs **after** any explicit refund a transition has ordered — the remand's fixed 6000-cent refund, for instance — and it never overrides that ordered amount; it only absorbs whatever is still left over. And it sweeps negatives only: a positive residue stays due.

Payment is a `Student` or `PlaCoordinator` act carrying `amount_cents` and a `method` of `CARD`, `CASH`, `CHECK`, `THIRD_PARTY` or `FINANCIAL_AID`. A `FINANCIAL_AID` payment is always refused with `ERR_AID_NOT_APPLICABLE`, because `aid_eligible` is false on every assessment-fee invoice without exception. `amount_cents` may not exceed `balance_due_cents`, or the payment is `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. An invoice whose `fee_status` is `UNBILLED`, `WAIVED`, `REFUNDED` or `RETAINED` accepts no money at all, and neither does one already at `PAID` with a zero balance: those are `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`. A valid payment increases `paid_cents` by `amount_cents`, recomputes `balance_due_cents`, adds the same amount to the student's `fee_paid_net_cents` and increments the student's `version`. The `fee_status` then follows the balance: a payment that leaves net cash short of `total_cents` minus `waived_cents` yields `PARTIALLY_PAID`, whether the invoice was `INVOICED` or already `PARTIALLY_PAID`; a payment that brings net cash exactly to `total_cents` minus `waived_cents` yields `PAID`, whether reached in one full payment from `INVOICED` or as the last of several from `PARTIALLY_PAID`.

Waiving is a coordinator act carrying a `waive_reason`, available from `INVOICED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. It sets `waived_cents` to `total_cents` minus net cash paid, drives `balance_due_cents` to 0, refunds any net cash already received by increasing `refunded_cents` by that net figure, recomputes the student's `fee_paid_net_cents`, and moves `fee_status` to `WAIVED`. A waived invoice satisfies the fee gate at assessor assignment and at award exactly as a paid one does.

The institution also waives at its own expense, without being asked, when it withdraws the ground beneath a claim: the articulation hold of §3.2 waives the swept claim's invoice from `INVOICED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID`, moving the balance into `waived_cents`, refunding every cent of net cash, and recomputing the student's `fee_paid_net_cents`.

**Settlement on exit** selects a retention rate from the `assessment_status` the claim is **leaving**, never the one it lands in. Net paid cash is `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. The retention rate is 5000 basis points for a withdrawal from `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` and zero for a withdrawal from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `CAP_BLOCKED`, zero for an overturned appeal, and zero for the articulation-hold waiver; a remanded appeal bypasses rate arithmetic entirely and refunds exactly 6000 cents. Retained cash is net paid multiplied by the retention rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The refund is the exact complement — net paid minus retained — so that after the movement `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents` equals the retained figure with no drift in either direction. `refunded_cents` rises by the refund, `waived_cents` absorbs whatever balance is still outstanding when the claim ends in a terminal state, and the student's `fee_paid_net_cents` is recomputed as the sum over all their invoices of `paid_cents` minus `refunded_cents`.

The fee dimension therefore begins at `UNBILLED`, passes through `INVOICED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID` and `WAIVED`, and ends at either `RETAINED` — the fee earned on a finally denied claim — or `REFUNDED`. It is a cyclic machine: an appeal filing or a re-submission that raises the total drives a settled invoice backwards from `PAID` to `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and a remand refund can drive it forward to `PAID` again.

### 3.11. Transcript Posting, Reversal and the Graduation Audit

Posting is the `Registrar`'s exclusive authority and is the only act that touches the transcript. The registrar supplies a `posted_term`. Five conditions must hold: the claim's `assessment_status` is `AWARDED`, or the posting is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_AWARDED`; the `fee_status` is one of `PAID`, `WAIVED`, `REFUNDED` or `RETAINED`, so an unbilled or partly paid claim cannot be posted; the `credit_status` is `NOT_POSTED`, or `ERR_CREDIT_ALREADY_POSTED`; the programme's `status` is `ACTIVE`; and the residency rule holds — the student's `transfer_credits_accepted` plus `pla_credits_posted` plus this claim's `credits_awarded` must not exceed the programme's `total_credits_required` minus its `residency_min_credits`, or the posting is `ERR_RESIDENCY_REQUIREMENT_VIOLATION`. The posted-only capacity variant is the cap gate here, not the reserved-inclusive one, and a breach of it is `ERR_PLA_CAP_EXCEEDED`; for an `UPPER`-level course the upper-division sub-cap is re-checked too, with `ERR_UPPER_DIVISION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Because posting is also an award-integrity checkpoint, the pinned crosswalk rule must still be `APPROVED` with a current pin, failing as `ERR_ARTICULATION_NOT_APPROVED` or `ERR_ARTICULATION_STALE_PIN`.

A successful posting sets `credit_status` to `POSTED`, records `posted_term`, and sets `posted_credits` to `credits_awarded`. The ledger moves the credit one shelf along: `credits_awarded` leaves `pla_credits_awarded` and enters `pla_credits_posted`, so the reserved-inclusive total is unchanged by a posting while the posted-only total rises. The student's `version` increments and the claim's `version` increments.

Reversal is the compensating transition and is likewise the registrar's alone, carrying a `reversal_reason`. It requires `credit_status` to be `POSTED`, or the request is `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_POSTED`. It sets `credit_status` to `REVERSED` and `posted_credits` to 0, subtracts the reversed credits from `pla_credits_posted` and returns them to `pla_credits_awarded`, and subtracts the upper-division portion from `pla_upper_division_committed`. The student's `version` increments. Because the credit merely moves back one shelf, **the reserved-inclusive total does not move at all** — which has a consequence worth stating plainly. The reversal runs the cap unblock waterfall of §3.8 over the student's `CAP_BLOCKED` claims, but since reserved-inclusive headroom is unchanged, only claims blocked with `hold_reason` `UPPER_DIVISION_CAP` can actually come free there; claims blocked with `hold_reason` `PLA_CAP` remain blocked. Reversal is a depth-three cascade: reversal, then the student ledger, then the waterfall's re-reservations.

Re-posting closes the credit cycle. A claim in `REVERSED` whose `assessment_status` is still `AWARDED` may be posted again, with the residency and posted-only capacity checks re-run from scratch and `posted_term` overwritten by the new value. The credit dimension has no terminal state: `NOT_POSTED`, `POSTED` and `REVERSED` cycle indefinitely between the last two.

The graduation audit is a pure computed read available to the `Registrar`, the `PlaCoordinator` and the student themselves, and it changes nothing. It counts **posted credit only**: claims whose `credit_status` is `POSTED`. Claims in `REVERSED` or `NOT_POSTED` contribute zero to every figure it reports, including the upper-division tally, no matter what their `assessment_status` says.

The audit reports `student_id` and `program_id`, the `effective_pla_cap_credits` for this student — the smaller of the programme ceiling and the student's residency headroom — and the three live ledger figures `pla_credits_posted`, `pla_credits_reserved` and `pla_credits_awarded`. It reports both capacity variants side by side as `pla_cap_remaining_posted_only` and `pla_cap_remaining_reserved_inclusive`, precisely so the divergence between them is directly observable. It reports `upper_division_pla_posted`, the sum of `credits_awarded` over this student's claims whose `credit_status` is `POSTED` and whose course `level` is `UPPER`, and `upper_division_remaining`, the programme's `pla_upper_division_max_credits` less the student's `pla_upper_division_committed`. It echoes `institutional_credits_earned` and `transfer_credits_accepted`, and reports `total_credits_applied` as `institutional_credits_earned` plus `transfer_credits_accepted` plus `pla_credits_posted`.

`residency_satisfied` is a boolean requiring two things at once: the student's `institutional_credits_earned` must be at least the programme's `residency_min_credits`, **and** the non-residency total of `transfer_credits_accepted` plus `pla_credits_posted` must not exceed `total_credits_required` minus `residency_min_credits`.

`audit_status` is evaluated in exactly this order, and the order is part of the specification. When `residency_satisfied` is false the status is `RESIDENCY_DEFICIENT`, and no other condition is consulted. Otherwise, when `pla_credits_posted` exceeds the effective cap the status is `PLA_CAP_VIOLATION`. Otherwise, when `total_credits_applied` is below the programme's `total_credits_required` the status is `DEFICIENT_CREDITS`. Otherwise the status is `ELIGIBLE`. A student who is both residency-deficient and over the prior-learning cap therefore reports `RESIDENCY_DEFICIENT`, because residency is tested first.
