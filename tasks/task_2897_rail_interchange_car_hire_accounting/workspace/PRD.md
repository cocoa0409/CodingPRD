# Rail Interchange & Car Hire Accounting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a freight car leaves the rails of the road that owns it, it starts earning money for that owner and costing money to whoever is holding it. North American railroads have settled that arrangement between themselves for a century through interchange reporting, car hire rates, reclaim rules and monthly reconciliation — and the accounting departments that run it spend most of their time not on arithmetic but on disagreement: a handoff nobody reported, a timestamp entered a day late, a car that sat in a shipper's yard for three days, a wheel defect that each road insists the other caused. This document specifies the service that a car accounting department uses to record all of that and to arrive at one number per month per pair of roads.

The system is built around an append-only interchange log. Every custody change is captured as an interchange event at a junction; between two consecutive events lies a **hire segment**, and each hire segment is the atomic unit that carries money — a time component driven by the car's rate book, a mileage component driven by the odometer, a credit for hours the car was unusable, and a penalty when the empty car was routed the wrong way. Segments accumulate into monthly settlement cycles between one payer road and one payee road; the payee computes and serves a statement, the payer may contest individual lines, and once the negotiation has closed the payer remits. Repair bills between handling lines are priced and assigned to a responsible road under the same industry rules and ride into the same monthly statement.

Two forces shape almost every rule below. The first is **segregation of duties**: the road that raises a claim may never be the road that decides it, so filing, confirming, allowing, accepting and settling are deliberately split across different railroads. The second is **retroactive correction**: because the interchange log is written by clerks under time pressure, the specification must survive a corrected timestamp arriving after a cycle has already been settled — which means changes cascade forward through segment boundaries, segment money, reclaim credits, ledger entries, cycle totals and both roads' balances, and a settled cycle can be reopened and recomputed. All business resources are mounted under the `/car-hire` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participating Roads and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers. `X-User-Role` names the caller's function and `X-User-ID` identifies the acting railroad. A request with a missing or empty `X-User-Role` is unauthenticated and is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header that names something outside the five roles below is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (also 401). A recognised role that is not on the authorised list of the endpoint being called is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ClearinghouseAdmin`, `InterchangeClerk`, `CarHireAccountant`, `RepairBillingAgent` and `FleetOwner`.

`InterchangeClerk`, `CarHireAccountant`, `RepairBillingAgent` and `FleetOwner` are all entity-backed roles. When `X-User-Role` is any one of those four, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Railroad`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Railroad` compare the resource's railroad-valued field to `X-User-ID`. For these four roles, `X-User-ID` must be present and must resolve to a registered railroad — a missing header, or one naming an unknown railroad, is refused with `ERR_ACTING_RAILROAD_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 403), and this check happens before any path identifier is looked up.

When `X-User-Role` is `ClearinghouseAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The clearinghouse is not a railroad: the server does not compare its `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, the clearinghouse counts as a party to every resource, and it is never subject to the segregation-of-duties ownership checks that bind the four road-backed roles.

Beyond role membership, the acting railroad must have **standing** on the resource it touches. A road is a party to a hire segment when it is that segment's `handling_road_id` or its `owner_road_id`; to a settlement cycle when it is the cycle's `payer_road_id` or `payee_road_id`; to a reclaim claim, a dispute, a repair bill or a correction request when it is one of the two railroads named on that row; to a car when it is the car's owner or its current handling road; and to a ledger entry when it is that entry's `debit_road_id` or `credit_road_id`. A road with no standing receives `ERR_NOT_A_PARTY_TO_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403) whether or not the action it attempted would have been legal for a party.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned: it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1 and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. Both are integers; a value outside those bounds is a request-shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific collection declares its own ordering (several do, and each is stated with the workflow that owns it in §3), list endpoints return rows ordered by `created_at` ascending.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

There is no wall-clock time anywhere in this system. A single server-held logical UTC clock is the only source of "now" for every deadline, every accrual, every expiry sweep and every window boundary described in this document. Before it has ever been set, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the machine's real time.

An admin-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back. That capability is the single endpoint exempt from the authentication and role checks of §2.1 — everything else in the API requires a valid role header. The clock may be moved forward or backward; moving it backward does not undo anything already written, so a settlement, a reclaim decision or a ledger entry made at a later instant survives a rewind.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Columns and Immutable Records

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix, and every resource carries a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — refreshed on every mutation, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only touch derived money fields. A cascade that rewrites a hire segment's amounts is a mutation of that segment and therefore bumps its `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, even though no client sent a request naming that segment.

Three record kinds are immutable by design and carry only `id` and `created_at` — for these, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely:

- A car type is a rate book entry. There is no update endpoint for it, and a rate change is expressed by registering a new car type rather than editing an existing one.
- An interchange event is an append-only log line. Only its `status` is ever rewritten, and only by an applied correction.
- A hire ledger entry is an immutable double-entry line. It is never updated and never deleted; a reversal is always a new entry carrying the opposite sign.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a JSON integer, never a decimal string. Rates may be finer than the cent: a car type's mileage rate is quoted in **millicents** (thousandths of a cent) per mile precisely so that the rounding point is observable, and a percentage rate such as the clearinghouse fee or the reverse-route penalty is applied to an integer basis and rounded once.

The rounding method is **round half up**, everywhere and without exception. Where a rule names its rounding point, that is the only place rounding occurs — an implementation that carries fractional cents past that point and rounds later will produce different totals. Two consequences are normative and are restated with their formulas in §3: mileage is rounded once per hire segment and the rounded per-segment figures are then summed, never the reverse; and a reclaim's split percentage multiplies inside the same single rounding as the pro-ration, never after it.

Hour-valued quantities — a segment's `chargeable_hours`, a claim's `claimed_hours` and `allowed_hours` — are JSON numbers computed to two decimal places under the same round-half-up rule. The fleet utilization percentage is a number carrying exactly two decimal places. A reclaim's `split_pct_applied` is a number reported as either 1.0 or 0.5.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Segments and Statements

Two write endpoints require the client to prove it has read the parent row it is acting against. Both use optimistic concurrency control: the client echoes the `version` it last observed, and the server compares it to the parent's current `version`.

Filing a reclaim claim — `POST /car-hire/reclaim-claims` — requires a non-optional `expected_movement_version` field carrying the `version` the client read from the target `CarHireMovement`. Every mutation of a hire segment increments that `version`: closing it, stamping a bad-order window on it, allowing or withdrawing another reclaim against it, applying a correction that rewrites it, or charging it a reverse-route penalty. If `expected_movement_version` does not equal the segment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. On success, the segment's `version` is incremented by 1 — which means resubmitting the identical body a second time also fails with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and the client must re-read the segment between filings.

Filing a dispute — `POST /car-hire/disputes` — requires a non-optional `expected_cycle_version` field carrying the `version` the client read from the exchanged `SettlementCycle`. If the payee has recomputed the cycle since, or a cascade has reopened it, the version has moved on and the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) with no `Dispute` row created. On success the cycle's `version` is incremented by 1, so the same duplicate-submission consequence applies.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, the transition endpoints that advance a claim, a bill, a cycle or a dispute do not accept `expected_movement_version` or `expected_cycle_version`, and supplying one there has no effect.

### 2.8. Order of Evaluation and Error Precedence

Checks are not independent. They run in one fixed ascending order, and the **first** rule that fails determines the response — later violations are never reported even when several hold simultaneously. The order below is normative; every trigger condition is also described in business terms alongside the workflow it guards in §3, and the HTTP status of each error is listed in §5.

1. `X-User-Role` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The role appears on the called endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. For the four entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to a registered railroad — otherwise `ERR_ACTING_RAILROAD_UNKNOWN`.
5. Every identifier in the URL path resolves to an existing row of its entity, checked left to right, the error naming the entity that failed: `ERR_RAILROAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JUNCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAR_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERCHANGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECLAIM_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMBARGO_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_BILL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Request shape on every write: each required field present, of the declared JSON type, and inside its declared numeric or string bounds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Integers presented as strings, negative cents on a non-negative field, malformed ISO 8601 instants and unparseable bodies all land here. Fields the specification marks as system-maintained or computed are ignored when a client supplies them, never rejected.
7. Every enum-typed input is a member of its declared value set — `event_type`, `load_status`, `reason`, `defect_class`, `correction_type`, `direction`, `dispute_reason`, `variant` and every `status` filter — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.
8. Every foreign key in the request body resolves to an existing row, reported through the same not-found catalogue as step 5. Body foreign keys are checked **after** shape and enum validity, so a body carrying both an invalid enum and a dangling identifier reports `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.
9. The acting railroad has standing on the resource per §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_A_PARTY_TO_RESOURCE`.
10. On the two endpoints of §2.7, the supplied `expected_movement_version` or `expected_cycle_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. This runs before every state and business rule below, so a stale version outranks all of them: a dispute filing that is both stale and names a subject outside the cycle reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_NOT_IN_CYCLE`; a reclaim filing that is both stale and targets a still-open segment reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_CLOSED`.
11. The target row's current status is a legal from-state for the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Re-issuing a transition that already ran, or driving a terminal row, fails here. This generic gate covers every transition endpoint **except** correction apply and the two repair-bill actions accept and counter-bill, which carry their own more specific gates at steps 15 and 16.
12. Filing a reclaim requires the parent hire segment to be `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_CLOSED`. Hire cannot be reclaimed against a segment that is still accruing, and never against a `VOID` segment.
13. Filing a dispute requires the parent cycle to be `EXCHANGED` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_EXCHANGED`. A cycle that is merely `COMPUTED` has not been served on the counterparty yet, and a `SETTLED` or `REOPENED` cycle is past the dispute stage.
14. No resource touched by the write belongs to a settlement cycle in status `ARCHIVED` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_ARCHIVED_IMMUTABLE`. This guards reclaim filing, the allow, disallow and withdraw actions on a claim, correction apply, and cycle reopen.
15. Correction apply requires the request to be `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_CONFIRMED`. A `FILED`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` request can never be applied.
16. Repair-bill accept and counter-bill require the bill to be `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_REPAIR_BILL_NOT_SUBMITTED`.
17. An interchange delivery requires the car's `mechanical_status` to be neither `BAD_ORDER` nor `UNDER_REPAIR` — otherwise `ERR_CAR_BAD_ORDER_NOT_INTERCHANGEABLE`.
18. Every railroad named on a write — the acting road, the delivering road, the receiving road, the payer, the payee, the owner — has `status` `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RAILROAD_SUSPENDED`. A suspended road may read, but may neither move cars nor settle.
19. Interchange capture and the issuing of an empty-routing obligation both require the referenced junction to have `status` `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_JUNCTION_CLOSED`.
20. Drafting a repair bill requires the car to be `BAD_ORDER`; releasing a car to service requires it to be `BAD_ORDER` or `UNDER_REPAIR` — otherwise `ERR_CAR_NOT_BAD_ORDERED`.
21. On an embargo filing, `issuing_road_id` equals the acting railroad **and** is one of the junction's two roads — otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_SELF_ISSUED`.
22. On interchange capture, `delivering_road_id` and `receiving_road_id` are exactly the two distinct roads of the junction, in either order — otherwise `ERR_ROADS_NOT_CONNECTED_AT_JUNCTION`.
23. On interchange capture, `delivering_road_id` equals the car's `current_handling_road_id` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERING_ROAD_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`.
24. The disputed subject belongs to the cycle: the named `movement_id`, or the named `repair_bill_id`, already carries `settlement_cycle_id` equal to the cycle being disputed — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_NOT_IN_CYCLE`.
25. A dispute filing supplies exactly one of `movement_id` and `repair_bill_id` — supplying both, or neither, is `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_AMBIGUOUS`.
26. A reclaim filing is evidenced: `reason` `BAD_ORDER` requires the parent segment's `bad_order_from` to be non-null; `reason` `EMBARGO` requires `supporting_embargo_id`; `reason` `CUSTOMER_DETENTION` requires `detention_notified_at`. Otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_EVIDENCE_MISSING`.
27. For an embargo-grounded reclaim, the cited order actually covers the window — otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_DOES_NOT_COVER_WINDOW` (the coverage test is spelled out in §3.5).
28. A correction filing matches its type to its target — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_TARGET_NOT_VOIDABLE` (the per-type requirements are in §3.9).
29. Submitting a handling-damage repair bill requires some hire segment of the car to cover `defect_occurred_at` — otherwise `ERR_NO_CUSTODY_AT_DEFECT_TIME`.
30. No client-supplied instant lies after the logical clock. This guards interchange capture, bad-order reporting, release to service and embargo lift; the violation is `ERR_EVENT_IN_FUTURE`.
31. On interchange capture, `interchange_at` is recent enough to be captured directly — otherwise `ERR_LATE_EVENT_REQUIRES_CORRECTION`.
32. On interchange capture, `interchange_at` is strictly later than the car's latest recorded event and `odometer_miles` has not moved backwards — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`.
33. A reclaim window lies inside its parent segment — otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_MOVEMENT`.
34. A reclaim is filed inside the industry filing window measured from the segment's `end_at` — otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
35. A correction is confirmed inside its confirm window — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_CONFIRM_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
36. A cycle is computed only once its month has ended — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`.
37. A dispute is filed inside the cycle's dispute window — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
38. A cycle is reopened inside its reopen window — otherwise `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
39. A cycle is archived only strictly after its reopen window has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN`.
40. Every supplied interval is strictly ordered — `window_from` strictly before `window_to`, `effective_from` strictly before `effective_to`, `issued_at` strictly before `due_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. This guards reclaim filing, embargo filing, empty-routing obligations and correction filing.
41. A reclaim window is long enough — otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`.
42. A reclaim window does not overlap a live claim on the same segment — otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
43. A new embargo does not conflict with an existing active one — otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
44. The parent hire segment is not locked against reclaims — otherwise `ERR_MOVEMENT_RECLAIM_LOCKED`.
45. Declared uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The unique keys are a railroad's `reporting_mark`, a car type's `car_type_code`, a junction's `junction_code`, a car's `car_mark`, the triple of `payer_road_id`, `payee_road_id` and `period_month` on a settlement cycle, and at most one non-terminal dispute per cycle-and-subject pair.
46. A dispute counter is available — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.
47. A repair counter-billing is available — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_BILL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.
48. A large repair bill carries its pre-authorisation — otherwise `ERR_REPAIR_PREAUTH_REQUIRED`.
49. Every dispute on a cycle is resolved before the payer remits — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_SETTLEMENT`.
50. A correction is confirmed or rejected only by the counterparty road — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_CONFIRM_NOT_COUNTERPARTY`.
51. A reclaim is allowed or disallowed only by the owner road that would fund the credit, and withdrawn only by the road that filed it — otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_DECISION_NOT_OWNER_ROAD`.
52. A dispute move is made only by the party whose turn the dispute's state names — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_TURN_VIOLATION`.
53. A cycle computation does not push the payer past its per-diem credit limit — otherwise `ERR_PER_DIEM_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
54. `disputed_amount_cents` is inside the subject's own value at filing — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SUBJECT`.
55. `counter_amount_cents` is inside the range the current move allows — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_AMOUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
56. `counter_total_cents` on a repair counter-billing is a genuine counter-offer — otherwise `ERR_REPAIR_AMOUNT_INVALID`.

### 2.9. Cascade Depth and Derived-Field Refresh

A single write in this system routinely mutates several rows. The rule is that every consequence is applied immediately and synchronously within the request that triggered it — there is no deferred or batched reconciliation — and every row touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

Cascades run outward in a fixed layering. A change to a car's mechanical or routing dimension propagates to the hire segment that car is currently on (layer 2); a change to a segment's money propagates to any reclaim claim resting on that segment and to the ledger and settlement cycle that already hold the segment as a line (layer 3); a change to a cycle's totals propagates to the participating railroads' derived balances, namely `per_diem_exposure_cents` and `foreign_car_balance` (layer 4). The deepest chain in the system — applying a confirmed correction — runs five layers, and is described end to end in §3.9.

Two cascade behaviours are load-bearing. First, when a cascade changes a line of a cycle that has already been settled, the cycle is first taken from `SETTLED` back to `REOPENED` and then recomputed; the server performs that transition itself, without a client request naming the cycle. Second, every money movement inside a cycle is journalled: the ledger is append-only, so a reversal is a new entry carrying the opposite sign rather than an edit or a deletion, and the sum of a cycle's entries is the audit trail against which its totals can be reconstructed.

### 2.10. A Flat Resource Graph, Read Through Filters

The thirteen resource kinds in this system are peers. No collection is nested inside another's URL path — every collection hangs directly off `/car-hire` — so parent-and-child navigation happens by filtering a list rather than by walking a hierarchy. Where a path does carry an identifier, it carries exactly one, and it is resolved before any body validation runs (§2.8, step 5).

Each collection read accepts filters on its foreign keys, its enumerations and, where the resource is time-bounded, on a pair of range bounds; each declares its own ordering. The available filters and orderings are named with the workflows in §3. Two orderings are worth calling out here because a calculation depends on them: reclaim claims are listed in `window_from` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, which is exactly the order in which reclaim credits fill a segment's cap; and interchange events are listed in `interchange_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, which is the order from which segment boundaries are re-derived after a correction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Clearinghouse: Roads, Junctions and Rate Books

Nothing can be interchanged until the clearinghouse knows who the carriers are. A `ClearinghouseAdmin` admits a railroad by supplying its `reporting_mark` — the industry identifier of two to four uppercase letters, such as BNRR, which must be unique across all registered roads — together with its legal carrier `name`. Two optional fields may accompany the registration. `status` is one of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` road may still be read but may not appear on either side of a new interchange event, and cannot move cars or settle. `per_diem_credit_limit_cents` caps how much snapshot per-diem exposure the road is allowed to carry, must not be negative, and defaults to 100000000 cents — one million dollars. A road is created with `foreign_car_balance`, `per_diem_exposure_cents` and `owned_car_count` all at 0 and `version` at 1. Thereafter `foreign_car_balance` is maintained as the count of open hire segments where this road is the handling road and the car belongs to somebody else, `owned_car_count` as the count of cars this road owns, and `per_diem_exposure_cents` as the snapshot exposure figure defined in §3.9. Reading a single road returns those three live figures; the road collection can be filtered by `status` and by `reporting_mark`, and is readable only by the clearinghouse and by car hire accountants.

An interchange point connects exactly two carriers. A junction is registered with a unique `junction_code`, a human-readable `name`, and the identifiers `road_a_id` and `road_b_id` of the two connected railroads, which must be two different existing roads. Its `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED` and defaults to `OPEN`; a `CLOSED` junction refuses every interchange event, and cannot be named as the return point of an empty-car obligation. Junctions are addressed individually by `junction_id`, and the collection can be narrowed by `road_a_id`, `road_b_id`, `status` or `junction_code` so that a clerk can find every point touching a given road.

The rate book lives on the car type, and this is the one configuration in the system that is deliberately frozen: a car type has no update endpoint, no `updated_at` and no `version`, and a rate change is expressed by registering a new car type. Each is created with a unique `car_type_code` — an AAR-style mechanical designation such as XM, GS or FBC — a plain-language `description`, and a mandatory `time_component_mode` of either `PER_DIEM` or `HOURLY`, which selects the branch that prices custody time. `free_time_hours` is the number of hours of custody excluded from the chargeable base before any rate applies, must not be negative, and defaults to 0. In per-diem mode, `per_diem_rate_cents` is the rate for each chargeable day up to and including the day count in `tier2_threshold_days`, and `tier2_per_diem_rate_cents` is the escalated incentive rate charged for every chargeable day beyond that threshold; `tier2_threshold_days` must be at least 1 and defaults to 5, while both rates default to 0. In hourly mode, `hourly_rate_cents` prices each chargeable hour and defaults to 0. Independent of the time mode, `mileage_rate_millicents_per_mile` prices distance in thousandths of a cent per mile and defaults to 0, and the boolean `loaded_mileage_only` — true by default — decides whether mileage accrues only while the car is loaded. A car type is addressed by `car_type_id`; the collection can be filtered by `car_type_code` and by `time_component_mode`.

### 3.2. Fleet Registration and a Car's Three Status Dimensions

A `FleetOwner` registers a car into its own fleet, and only into its own: the acting railroad must be the `owner_road_id` named on the request. The registration supplies a `car_mark` — the reporting mark plus the car number, for example 'BNRR 100001', unique across all cars — the `car_type_id` that pins every rate this car will ever be charged at, and the `owner_road_id` that will receive car hire. An optional `initial_handling_road_id` records that the car is already in somebody else's hands at registration time; when it is omitted the owner road is assumed to be holding the car.

Registration does two further things. It increments the owner's `owned_car_count`, and it opens the car's very first hire segment — the bootstrap segment — whose `start_at` is the logical clock at the moment of registration, whose `handling_road_id` is the car's current handling road, whose `load_status` is `EMPTY`, whose `start_odometer_miles` is 0, and whose `received_event_id` is null, because no interchange event opened it. That bootstrap segment is the only segment in the system with a null `received_event_id`. Its `hire_liable` flag is true exactly when the handling road differs from the owner road.

A car carries **three independent status dimensions on one row**, and this is central to the model. `custody_status` is `ON_HOME_ROAD` or `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD` and is always derived from custody: it reads `ON_HOME_ROAD` when `current_handling_road_id` equals `owner_road_id` and `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD` otherwise. `mechanical_status` is `SERVICEABLE`, `BAD_ORDER` or `UNDER_REPAIR`, and starts at `SERVICEABLE` for every newly registered car regardless of who is holding it. `routing_status` is `NO_OBLIGATION`, `OBLIGATION_ACTIVE`, `OBLIGATION_MET` or `OBLIGATION_VIOLATED`, and starts at `NO_OBLIGATION`. No transition on one dimension reads or constrains the value of another: a car may legitimately be `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD`, `UNDER_REPAIR` and `OBLIGATION_VIOLATED` at the same time, and clearing the mechanical dimension leaves custody and routing exactly as they were.

Several nullable columns hang off those dimensions. While a return obligation is live, `prescribed_return_junction_id` names the junction where the empty car must be handed back, `obligation_issued_at` records when the obligation was issued and `obligation_due_at` the instant past which an unmet obligation is treated as violated. On the mechanical side, `bad_order_since` records when the current defect was reported or discovered at a junction and `bad_order_cleared_at` when the most recent defect was cleared. Finally, `cumulative_mileage` reports the car's travelled distance: the sum, over the car's closed hire segments that have a recorded closing odometer, of the closing odometer reading minus the opening one.

Reading one car returns all three dimensions together. The car collection can be filtered on each dimension — `custody_status`, `mechanical_status`, `routing_status` — and on `owner_road_id`, `current_handling_road_id`, `car_type_id`, `prescribed_return_junction_id` and `car_mark`, which is how a fleet owner finds, say, every serviceable car of one type sitting on foreign rails.

### 3.3. Capturing Interchange at the Junction

The interchange log is the spine of the whole system, and only an `InterchangeClerk` writes to it. A capture names the `car_id` changing hands, the `junction_id` where the handoff happened, the `event_type`, the `delivering_road_id` offering the car, the `receiving_road_id` on the other side, the `interchange_at` instant of the handoff, the `odometer_miles` reading taken at that moment, and the `load_status` — `LOADED` or `EMPTY` — of the car as handed over. The acting railroad must be either the delivering or the receiving road. Every captured event is minted at `status` `RECORDED` with `source` `CLERK_CAPTURE` and with `recorded_by_road_id` set to the acting railroad; `corrects_event_id` is null on a clerk capture and is only ever populated on a replacement event minted by a correction (§3.9).

`event_type` has two members and they behave completely differently. An `INTERCHANGE_DELIVERY` transfers custody: the car's open hire segment goes `OPEN → CLOSED` with `end_at` set to `interchange_at`, `delivered_event_id` set to the new event and `end_odometer_miles` set to `odometer_miles`; the car's `current_handling_road_id` becomes the receiving road, its `custody_status` is re-derived — `ON_HOME_ROAD → ON_FOREIGN_ROAD` when the receiving road is not the owner, `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD → ON_HOME_ROAD` when the car comes home, and the self-loop `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD → ON_FOREIGN_ROAD` when one foreign road hands the car to another — and a fresh segment opens for the receiving road with `start_at` and `start_odometer_miles` taken from the same event, `load_status` copied from the event, and `received_event_id` pointing at it. When the receiving road is not the owner the new segment's `hire_liable` is true; when the car has come home to its owner the new segment's `hire_liable` is false, and a segment with `hire_liable` false accrues no time component, no mileage component and no penalty however long it lasts. The closing segment is priced at this moment (§3.4), and if it carried an open bad-order stamp — a non-null `bad_order_from` with a null `bad_order_to` — that stamp is closed at `end_at`. Both roads' `per_diem_exposure_cents` and `foreign_car_balance` are refreshed as part of the same request.

An `INTERCHANGE_REJECTION` is the receiving road refusing the car at the junction, and it deliberately changes no custody: no segment closes and none opens. What it does is drive the car's mechanical dimension `SERVICEABLE → BAD_ORDER` with `bad_order_since` set to `interchange_at`, and stamp the delivering road's still-open segment with `bad_order_from` equal to `interchange_at` — but only when that segment's `bad_order_from` is currently null, so an existing stamp is never overwritten — leaving `bad_order_to` null. A rejection is therefore the mechanism by which a receiving road documents that the car it was offered was defective, and the delivering road's exposure to a later reclaim opens at that instant.

Four temporal rules police direct capture, and all four are stated as boundaries rather than as approximations. No supplied instant may lie after the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_IN_FUTURE`. `interchange_at` must be no more than 168 hours — seven days — older than the logical clock, and the boundary is inclusive: an event exactly 168 hours old is still accepted, while one 168 hours and one minute old is refused with `ERR_LATE_EVENT_REQUIRES_CORRECTION` and must instead enter the log through a confirmed correction of type `MISSED_EVENT_INSERT`, which is exempt from this limit. `interchange_at` must be strictly later than the `interchange_at` of the car's latest `RECORDED` event, and `odometer_miles` must be greater than or equal to that event's `odometer_miles`, both under `ERR_EVENT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE` — direct capture is append-only in time, and out-of-order insertion is a correction rather than a capture. Additionally, `odometer_miles` may not be negative, and the closing of a segment requires the event's instant to be strictly after that segment's `start_at` and its odometer to be at least the segment's `start_odometer_miles`.

Business preconditions layer on top of the temporal ones. A delivery requires the car's mechanical dimension to be neither `BAD_ORDER` nor `UNDER_REPAIR`, otherwise `ERR_CAR_BAD_ORDER_NOT_INTERCHANGEABLE`: a bad-ordered car cannot be handed to another road until it has been released to service. The junction must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_JUNCTION_CLOSED`; the delivering and receiving roads must be exactly the junction's two connected roads in either order, otherwise `ERR_ROADS_NOT_CONNECTED_AT_JUNCTION`; and the delivering road must be the road actually holding the car, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERING_ROAD_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`. Both roads must be `ACTIVE`, since a `SUSPENDED` road can read but neither move cars nor settle, and naming one is `ERR_RAILROAD_SUSPENDED`.

Events are never edited. Only an applied correction ever rewrites an event's `status`, moving it `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED` when a timestamp amendment replaces it or `RECORDED → VOID` when an erroneous rejection is erased; both are terminal, and a `SUPERSEDED` or `VOID` event is retained for audit but ignored by every calculation and by every custody derivation. An event is addressed by `interchange_event_id`; the event collection is returned in `interchange_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and can be filtered by `car_id`, `junction_id`, `delivering_road_id`, `receiving_road_id`, `recorded_by_road_id`, `corrects_event_id`, `event_type`, `status`, `source` and the range bounds `interchange_at_from` and `interchange_at_to`, which is how a correction chain is walked forwards and backwards.

### 3.4. Pricing a Hire Segment

A hire segment is created by the system, never by a client, and it is the row that carries the money. Alongside the boundary fields already introduced — `car_id`, `handling_road_id`, `start_at`, `end_at`, `received_event_id`, `delivered_event_id`, `start_odometer_miles`, `end_odometer_miles`, `load_status` and `hire_liable` — a segment denormalises the `owner_road_id` at the moment it opened, so that settlement membership never has to join through the car, and carries a nullable `settlement_cycle_id` that is stamped the first time a settlement cycle admits the segment as a line. Its `status` is `OPEN` while the handling road still holds the car, `CLOSED` once a delivery ends it, and `VOID` when an applied correction removes the segment entirely; a `VOID` segment is excluded from every calculation and from every settlement cycle. The counter `recompute_count` records how many times an applied correction has rewritten this segment's boundaries or amounts, and `reclaim_locked` — false by default — records that the segment has lost its reclaim rights.

**Time component.** When a segment closes, three steps run in a fixed order that is not commutative. First, elapsed hours are the difference between `end_at` and `start_at` expressed in hours to two decimal places. Second, `chargeable_hours` is that elapsed figure minus the car type's `free_time_hours`, floored at zero — a segment lying entirely inside free time yields `chargeable_hours` of 0 and `time_component_cents` of 0, and is still a member line of its settlement cycle. Third, the mode branch applies. Free time is deducted from **hours** before any conversion to days: a segment of exactly 76.00 elapsed hours on a car type with 4 free hours bills three days, because 72.00 chargeable hours divided by 24 and rounded up is 3, and not four days as it would be if the free hours were deducted after the day count.

In `PER_DIEM` mode, `chargeable_days` is `chargeable_hours` divided by 24 and rounded **up** to a whole number — any part of a day counts as a day. The tier is **marginal, not flat**: the number of days billed at `per_diem_rate_cents` is the lesser of `chargeable_days` and `tier2_threshold_days`, the number billed at `tier2_per_diem_rate_cents` is `chargeable_days` minus `tier2_threshold_days` floored at zero, and `time_component_cents` is the sum of those two products. The first `tier2_threshold_days` days always bill at the base rate no matter how long the segment runs. In `HOURLY` mode, `chargeable_days` plays no part in the price and the segment reports it as 0, while `time_component_cents` is `chargeable_hours` multiplied by `hourly_rate_cents` and rounded half up to a whole cent. Either way the result is an integer number of cents, and no per-segment floor or ceiling applies.

**Mileage component.** Segment miles are the closing odometer minus the opening odometer, a figure that is never negative. Billable miles are zero when the car type has `loaded_mileage_only` true and the segment's `load_status` is `EMPTY`; otherwise billable miles are the full segment miles. `mileage_component_cents` is billable miles multiplied by `mileage_rate_millicents_per_mile`, divided by 1000 to convert millicents to cents, and rounded half up — **once per segment**. Rounding per line and then summing is normative and is observably different from summing and then rounding: at a rate of 2350 millicents per mile, a 415-mile segment prices at 975.25 cents and rounds to 975, a 227-mile segment prices at 533.45 cents and rounds to 533, and a cycle containing both must report 1508 cents of mileage — not the 1509 that an implementation accumulating exact millicents across lines and rounding at the end would produce.

**Net.** `gross_hire_cents` is the sum of `time_component_cents` and `mileage_component_cents`. `net_hire_cents` is `gross_hire_cents` less `reclaim_credit_cents` plus `reverse_route_penalty_cents` — the penalty is added, not subtracted. `adjusted_net_hire_cents` equals `net_hire_cents` until a dispute citing this segment is accepted, after which it is `net_hire_cents` less that dispute's resolution amount. On closing, all of these are computed and `adjusted_net_hire_cents` is set equal to `net_hire_cents`.

Segments are addressed by `movement_id` and the collection is returned in `end_at` ascending order, with still-open segments placed last and ordered among themselves by `start_at` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. It can be filtered by `car_id`, `handling_road_id`, `owner_road_id`, `received_event_id`, `delivered_event_id`, `settlement_cycle_id`, `status`, `hire_liable`, `reclaim_locked` and the settlement-window bounds `end_at_from` and `end_at_to`. Reading a single segment is also how a car hire accountant obtains the `version` it must echo before filing a reclaim.

### 3.5. Withholding Hire: Bad Order, Embargo and Detention Reclaims

Car hire runs whether or not the car is useful, so the industry lets a handling road ask the owner to credit back the hours during which the car could not be used. That request is a **reclaim claim**, and everything in this subsection exists to establish, evidence and price one.

**Reporting a defect.** The road currently holding a car reports it mechanically defective, which drives the mechanical dimension `SERVICEABLE → BAD_ORDER`. The report is written by an `InterchangeClerk` whose railroad must be the car's `current_handling_road_id`, and it supplies the `reported_at` instant, the industry `defect_code`, and an optional `defect_note`. `reported_at` may not be after the logical clock and may not be earlier than the start of the car's currently open segment. The car's `bad_order_since` becomes `reported_at`. Because the mechanical dimension changed, the cascade reaches the open segment, which is stamped with `bad_order_from` equal to `reported_at` and `bad_order_to` null and has its `version` incremented; and because that segment now carries an open bad-order stamp, any claim already `FILED` against the same segment for reason `BAD_ORDER` is re-validated against the widened window.

**Returning a car to service.** The holding road clears the defect, taking the mechanical dimension `BAD_ORDER → SERVICEABLE` or `UNDER_REPAIR → SERVICEABLE`. The acting railroad must again be the car's current handling road, and the supplied `released_at` may not be after the logical clock and must be strictly later than `bad_order_since`; an optional `release_note` may accompany it. The car's `bad_order_since` is cleared to null and `bad_order_cleared_at` becomes `released_at`, and the segment whose `bad_order_from` is non-null while its `bad_order_to` is still null has that `bad_order_to` stamped at `released_at`. Attempting this on a car that is already `SERVICEABLE` is refused with `ERR_CAR_NOT_BAD_ORDERED`.

**Embargoes.** A road may close a junction to a class of cars for a stated period, and that order is the evidence an embargo-grounded reclaim must cite. An `InterchangeClerk` files it with the `issuing_road_id` — which must be the acting railroad and must be one of the junction's two connected roads, otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_SELF_ISSUED` — the `junction_id` being closed, a `car_type_id` scoping the order to one mechanical type or left null to embargo every type at that junction, the interval bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to`, and the operating `reason` cited. The interval must be strictly ordered, with `effective_from` before `effective_to`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Unlike a reclaim window, an embargo interval is **closed at both ends**: both `effective_from` and `effective_to` are inside it. A new order conflicts with an existing `ACTIVE` order for the same junction and car type when their closed intervals intersect, and also when the new interval starts within 24 hours of the existing order's `effective_to` — embargo orders carry a mandatory 24-hour cooling gap, so two orders that merely touch at their endpoints do conflict, even though two reclaim windows that touch do not. A conflict is `ERR_EMBARGO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. An order is created `ACTIVE`; the issuing road may lift it early by supplying a `lifted_at` instant that is at or after `effective_from` and not after the logical clock, which moves it `ACTIVE → LIFTED`. After a lift, the order supports a claim window that ends at or before `lifted_at` and supports none extending beyond it — its effective coverage shrinks to the interval from `effective_from` to whichever of `effective_to` and `lifted_at` is earlier — and reclaims that were already allowed against it are not disturbed. Orders are addressed by `embargo_order_id`, listed in `effective_from` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and filtered by `issuing_road_id`, `junction_id`, `car_type_id`, `status` and the range bounds `effective_from_from` and `effective_to_to`.

**Filing a claim.** A `CarHireAccountant` of the handling road files against a specific closed segment. The payload names the `movement_id`, the `reason`, the half-open window bounds `window_from` (inclusive) and `window_to` (exclusive), and the `expected_movement_version` of §2.7; it may also carry a `supporting_embargo_id`, a `detention_notified_at` instant, and a free-text `filing_note`. The server fills in `filed_by_road_id` as the segment's handling road and `against_road_id` as the segment's owner road — the owner road being the only road that will ever be allowed to decide the claim — sets `status` to `FILED`, computes `claimed_hours` as the length of the window in hours, and increments the segment's `version` by 1.

`reason` is one of three grounds and each demands its own evidence, all checked at the same step: `BAD_ORDER` requires the segment to carry a non-null `bad_order_from`; `EMBARGO` requires a `supporting_embargo_id`; `CUSTOMER_DETENTION` requires a `detention_notified_at`, which is the instant the consignee was notified and which must itself fall inside the claimed half-open window. A missing ground is `ERR_RECLAIM_EVIDENCE_MISSING`. An embargo-grounded claim additionally requires the cited order to genuinely cover the claim: the order's `car_type_id` must equal the car's `car_type_id`, its `junction_id` must be the junction of either the event that opened the segment or the event that closed it, and the claimed window must lie inside the order's effective coverage as described above; failure is `ERR_EMBARGO_DOES_NOT_COVER_WINDOW`.

Five further gates apply to a filing, in the order set out in §2.8. The parent segment must be `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_CLOSED`. The window must be strictly ordered, with `window_from` before `window_to`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. It must lie inside the segment: `window_from` at or after the segment's `start_at`, and `window_to` at or before its `end_at`, otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_MOVEMENT`. The claim must be filed within 60 days of the segment's `end_at`, measured against the logical clock and inclusive of the boundary, otherwise `ERR_RECLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. `claimed_hours` must be at least 24 — exactly 24 hours is claimable, one minute less is `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`. The window must not overlap the window of any existing claim on the same segment whose `status` is `FILED` or `ALLOWED`; because windows are half-open, a claim beginning exactly when another ends does not overlap and is accepted, while one minute of genuine intersection is `ERR_RECLAIM_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — and claims that ended up `DISALLOWED` or `WITHDRAWN` release their window for re-filing. Finally the segment's `reclaim_locked` must be false.

**Deciding a claim.** Only the owner road named in `against_road_id` may allow or disallow, and only the filing road may withdraw; the filer deciding its own claim is `ERR_RECLAIM_DECISION_NOT_OWNER_ROAD`. Allowing takes the claim `FILED → ALLOWED` and may optionally supply an `allowed_hours` override, which must be greater than 0 and no greater than `claimed_hours`, plus an optional `decision_note`; when no override is supplied, `allowed_hours` equals `claimed_hours`. Every decision stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock.

The credit is priced in four steps. The effective hours are `allowed_hours`. The `split_pct_applied` depends on the ground: 1.0 for `BAD_ORDER` and 1.0 for `EMBARGO`, but only 0.5 for `CUSTOMER_DETENTION`, because the two roads share the cost of a shipper holding the car. The raw credit is the segment's `time_component_cents`, multiplied by the effective hours, divided by the segment's `chargeable_hours`, multiplied by the split percentage, and rounded half up — with the split multiplying **inside** that single rounding, never applied to an already-rounded full credit. Then `credit_cents` is the lesser of the raw credit and the segment's `time_component_cents`: the credit pro-rates the **time** component only, and mileage is never reclaimable. Two illustrations: a segment with `time_component_cents` of 7800 and `chargeable_hours` of 72.00, carrying a 36.00-hour `BAD_ORDER` claim, credits 3900; the same segment with a 30.00-hour `CUSTOMER_DETENTION` claim credits 1625, which is the half-share of 3250 computed inside the rounding.

Because more than one claim may rest on one segment, the segment's own `reclaim_credit_cents` is re-derived by a waterfall whenever any claim on it enters or leaves `ALLOWED`. The allowed claims are ordered by `window_from` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `claimed_hours` descending — never by identifier and never randomly. The running total starts at zero and the remaining capacity starts at the segment's `time_component_cents`; each claim in turn takes the lesser of its own `credit_cents` and the capacity that remains, adding that to the running total and reducing the remaining capacity by it. A claim reached when nothing remains contributes nothing, and its own `credit_cents` field is left untouched — the cap lives on the segment, not on the claim. The order is by window and not by amount, so a later-window larger claim can be the one that gets clipped. The running total becomes `reclaim_credit_cents`, from which `net_hire_cents` and then `adjusted_net_hire_cents` are recomputed as in §3.4, and the segment's `version` is incremented.

The consequences of an allowance run further outward. When the segment already carries a `settlement_cycle_id`, a `RECLAIM_CREDIT` ledger entry is minted for the negative of the credit just granted, the cycle's money fields are recomputed, a cycle that had already reached `SETTLED` is first taken back to `REOPENED`, and the payer road's `per_diem_exposure_cents` is refreshed. Disallowing is the quiet branch, taking the claim `FILED → DISALLOWED`: it records the supplied `disallow_reason`, stamps `decided_at`, leaves `credit_cents` at 0, mints no ledger entry and changes no cycle total.

Withdrawal is available to the filer both as `FILED → WITHDRAWN` and as `ALLOWED → WITHDRAWN`, and requires a `withdraw_reason`. Withdrawing an allowed claim is a compensating transaction that must restore every derived figure **exactly**: `credit_cents` returns to 0, the segment's waterfall is re-run from the beginning so that `reclaim_credit_cents` and `net_hire_cents` return precisely to their previous values with no drift, the segment's `version` is incremented, and where a credit had already reached a cycle a `RECLAIM_REVERSAL` entry for exactly the amount removed is minted and the cycle recomputed, restoring `pre_fee_net_cents` and `clearinghouse_fee_cents` to their pre-allowance values. One further path leaves `ALLOWED`: when an applied correction erases the bad-order stamp that a `BAD_ORDER` claim relied upon — that is, when the voided event's instant was the segment's `bad_order_from` — the claim is moved `ALLOWED → DISALLOWED` by the system with `disallow_reason` set to the literal `SUPPORTING_EVENT_VOIDED`, its credit removed and the same unwinding performed, except that the ledger entry carries the type `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` rather than `RECLAIM_REVERSAL`.

No claim may be filed, allowed, disallowed or withdrawn when the segment behind it belongs to an `ARCHIVED` cycle. Claims are addressed by `reclaim_claim_id`, listed in `window_from` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and filtered by `movement_id`, `filed_by_road_id`, `against_road_id`, `supporting_embargo_id`, `reason` and `status`.

### 3.6. Empty-Car Return Obligations and Reverse-Route Liability

An owner that wants its empty cars back prescribes where and by when they must be returned. A `FleetOwner` whose railroad is the car's `owner_road_id` — never the road holding the car — issues the obligation by naming a `prescribed_return_junction_id`, an `issued_at` instant and a `due_at` instant. The junction must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_JUNCTION_CLOSED`, and must have the owner road as one of its two connected roads; `issued_at` must be at or before the logical clock, the logical clock must be strictly before `due_at`, and `issued_at` must be strictly before `due_at`, an unordered pair being `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Issuing takes the routing dimension `NO_OBLIGATION → OBLIGATION_ACTIVE`, or `OBLIGATION_MET → OBLIGATION_ACTIVE` when the car has already satisfied a previous obligation — a car can be re-obligated after a compliant return, and the new obligation supersedes the satisfied one. Those are the only two routing states an obligation may be issued from: a car already at `OBLIGATION_ACTIVE` or at `OBLIGATION_VIOLATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The car's `prescribed_return_junction_id`, `obligation_issued_at` and `obligation_due_at` are set accordingly. The custody and mechanical dimensions are neither read nor written by this action.

The obligation is judged at the next empty delivery, and only at an empty delivery. When a car standing at `OBLIGATION_ACTIVE` is delivered with `load_status` `EMPTY` at the prescribed junction, to its owner road, at an instant at or before `obligation_due_at`, the routing dimension moves `OBLIGATION_ACTIVE → OBLIGATION_MET` and `prescribed_return_junction_id`, `obligation_issued_at` and `obligation_due_at` are all cleared to null. When any one of those three conditions fails — the wrong junction, a receiving road other than the owner, or an instant after `obligation_due_at` — the dimension moves `OBLIGATION_ACTIVE → OBLIGATION_VIOLATED` instead.

A violation shifts liability, and the cascade is the point of the rule. The segment that this delivery has just closed — the violating handling road's own segment — is charged a `reverse_route_penalty_cents` equal to its `time_component_cents` multiplied by 25 percent and rounded half up, so a segment whose time component is 7800 carries a penalty of 1950 and, with no reclaims, nets 9750 rather than 7800. That segment's `reclaim_locked` becomes true, which permanently bars new reclaim filings against it with `ERR_MOVEMENT_RECLAIM_LOCKED` while leaving reclaims already `ALLOWED` untouched. Its `net_hire_cents` is recomputed with the penalty **added** to gross hire, and its `version` is incremented. If the segment already carries a `settlement_cycle_id` for a cycle that is `COMPUTED` or `EXCHANGED`, a `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry for the difference between the new and the old net is minted and the cycle's money fields recomputed; a cycle that had reached `SETTLED` is first taken back to `REOPENED`. Finally the payer road's `per_diem_exposure_cents` and the payee road's `foreign_car_balance` are refreshed. In short, the road that violated the reverse-route rule loses every reclaim right on the segment it just ended and pays a quarter of the time component on top of the hire it already owed.

### 3.7. Repair Billing and Responsibility Between Handling Lines

When a car is bad-ordered, somebody has to fix it and somebody has to pay for it, and industry rules make those two roads different in the ordinary case. A repair bill therefore has three distinct actors across its life: the road that did the work drafts and submits it, the road held responsible accepts or counters it, and the clearinghouse settles it.

**Drafting.** A `RepairBillingAgent` drafts the bill against a car that is currently `BAD_ORDER`; a serviceable car has nothing to repair, which is `ERR_CAR_NOT_BAD_ORDERED`. The draft carries the `car_id`, the `billing_road_id` — which must be the acting agent's own railroad, since a road may not bill in another road's name — the industry `defect_code`, the `defect_class`, the `defect_occurred_at` instant the defect is deemed to have arisen, the `labor_hours` claimed to two decimal places, and the `material_cents` of parts consumed. It may optionally cite the `discovery_event_id` of the `RECORDED` interchange event at which the defect came to light, which must reference the same car, and a `pre_authorization_reference`. Both amounts must be at least zero and they must not both be zero — a bill for nothing is not a bill. `defect_class` is one of `HANDLING_DAMAGE`, `NORMAL_WEAR` or `OWNER_APPLIANCE_DEFECT`. The bill is created at `DRAFT` with `responsible_road_id`, `responsibility_rule_applied`, `labor_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `handling_fee_cents` and `total_cents` all null, and with `counter_bill_round` at 0: responsibility is deliberately not decided at drafting time.

**Submission is where responsibility and price are decided.** The billing road submits, taking the bill `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamping `submitted_at` from the logical clock while `counter_bill_round` stays 0. Two calculations run, in order.

First, `defect_class` selects the responsibility rule, and the rule's name is recorded in `responsibility_rule_applied`. Under the `HANDLING_LINE_RULE`, used when `defect_class` is `HANDLING_DAMAGE`, custody decides: the car's segments are scanned in `start_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, the one whose half-open interval from `start_at` up to but excluding `end_at` contains `defect_occurred_at` is selected — an `OPEN` segment being treated as extending to the logical clock — and `responsible_road_id` becomes that segment's `handling_road_id`. If no segment covers the instant, the handling-line rule has no road to name and the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_CUSTODY_AT_DEFECT_TIME`. Under the `OWNER_RESPONSIBILITY_RULE`, used when `defect_class` is `NORMAL_WEAR` or `OWNER_APPLIANCE_DEFECT`, custody is irrelevant and `responsible_road_id` is simply the car's `owner_road_id`, because ordinary wear and owner-supplied appliances stay with the owner however far the car has travelled. The two rules read the same segment history and the same `defect_occurred_at` and reach different roads: holding every other field constant and changing `defect_class` from `HANDLING_DAMAGE` to `NORMAL_WEAR` moves `responsible_road_id` from the handling road to the owner road, and therefore also changes `total_cents`.

Second, the bill is priced from its own `labor_hours` and `material_cents` in four steps, with independent rounding at the first and third. `labor_cents` is `labor_hours` multiplied by 9500 cents per hour, rounded half up. `subtotal_cents` is `labor_cents` plus `material_cents`. `handling_fee_cents` is 7.5 percent of `subtotal_cents`, rounded half up, but only when `responsible_road_id` differs from `billing_road_id`; a road repairing its own car bills no handling fee and the field is 0. `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents`. For 3.50 labor hours and 12000 material cents billed to another road: labor 33250, subtotal 45250, handling fee 3394 from 3393.75 rounded half up, total 48644 — and the identical bill where the responsible road is the billing road has a handling fee of 0 and a total of 45250.

A final gate closes over the priced result: when the computed `total_cents` exceeds 150000 cents and `pre_authorization_reference` is null or empty, the submission is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_PREAUTH_REQUIRED` and nothing is persisted. The gate reads the fee-inclusive total, so a bill can cross the threshold on the handling fee alone. Submission also cascades to the car, moving the mechanical dimension `BAD_ORDER → UNDER_REPAIR`.

**Negotiation.** From `SUBMITTED`, only the road named in `responsible_road_id` may act — never the biller — and it has three moves, all reached through the same counter-billing and acceptance endpoints. It may counter the amount, which is the cyclic self-loop `SUBMITTED → SUBMITTED`: supplying a `counter_total_cents`, optionally with a `counter_note`, sets `total_cents` to the countered figure, increments `counter_bill_round` by 1 and stamps `last_counter_at`, leaving the status at `SUBMITTED`. The counter must satisfy `0 < counter_total_cents < total_cents` as the bill currently stands — an absent, zero, negative, or at-or-above-standing-total figure is not a counter-offer at all and is `ERR_REPAIR_AMOUNT_INVALID` — and `counter_bill_round` must be strictly less than 2, so the second counter is the last one accepted and a third attempt is `ERR_COUNTER_BILL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. It may instead end the negotiation by omitting `counter_total_cents` and sending `reject` as true, which takes the bill `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps `rejected_at`, mints no ledger entry and leaves the car `UNDER_REPAIR`. Or it may accept at the current total, taking the bill `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, stamping `accepted_at` and minting a `REPAIR_CHARGE` ledger entry for `total_cents` that debits `responsible_road_id` and credits `billing_road_id`. Any of these attempted against a bill that is not `SUBMITTED` is `ERR_REPAIR_BILL_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and that dedicated gate is evaluated in place of the generic state gate of §2.8.

**Settlement.** Only a `ClearinghouseAdmin` settles, taking the bill `ACCEPTED → SETTLED` and stamping `settled_at`. Settlement is also how a repaired car re-enters service: the mechanical dimension moves `UNDER_REPAIR → SERVICEABLE`, `bad_order_since` is cleared to null, `bad_order_cleared_at` is stamped at the logical clock, and the open bad-order stamp on the car's current segment is closed. `SETTLED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal.

Bills are addressed by `repair_bill_id`, listed in `created_at` ascending order, and filtered by `car_id`, `discovery_event_id`, `billing_road_id`, `responsible_road_id`, `settlement_cycle_id`, `status` and `defect_class`. Reading one bill is how a road learns the `responsible_road_id` and `responsibility_rule_applied` that submission decided. A bill's own `settlement_cycle_id` is stamped by the settlement machinery of §3.8, not by anything in this subsection, and `defect_occurred_at` is the month key that decides which cycle the bill lands in.

### 3.8. Monthly Reconciliation, Disputes and Settlement

Roads do not pay each other segment by segment; they net a month's worth of traffic into a single statement. A settlement cycle is that statement, and its life is a loop rather than a line: it can be computed, served, paid, reopened and computed again, and only archiving is final.

**Opening a cycle.** A `CarHireAccountant` of either party opens one by naming the `payer_road_id` — the handling road being billed — the `payee_road_id` — the car-owning road being paid, which must differ from the payer — and the accounting month `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form. From that month the server derives `period_start_at`, the first instant of the month in UTC and inclusive, and `period_end_at`, the first instant of the following month in UTC and exclusive. The triple of `payer_road_id`, `payee_road_id` and `period_month` is unique, so a month between one pair of roads can be opened only once and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The cycle is created at `OPEN` with every money field 0 and `version` 1, and is addressed thereafter by `settlement_cycle_id`.

**Computing the statement.** Only the payee road computes, and only once the month is over: the logical clock must be at or after `period_end_at`, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. Compute is legal from `OPEN` and again from `REOPENED`, and it is the edge that closes the loop. The membership sets are precise. A segment is a member when its `status` is `CLOSED`, its `handling_road_id` is the cycle's payer, its `owner_road_id` is the cycle's payee, its `hire_liable` is true, and its `end_at` falls in the half-open period from `period_start_at` up to but excluding `period_end_at`; `VOID` segments and still-`OPEN` segments are excluded however much hire they have accrued. A repair bill is a member when its `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `SETTLED`, its `billing_road_id` is the cycle's payee, its `responsible_road_id` is the cycle's payer, and its `accepted_at` falls in the same half-open period. Every qualifying member that carries no `settlement_cycle_id` yet is stamped with this cycle's identifier and mints its ledger entry — `HIRE_CHARGE` for a segment's `net_hire_cents`, `REPAIR_CHARGE` for a bill's `total_cents` — while members already stamped with this cycle are re-read at their current figures rather than re-stamped and re-minted.

The statement itself is assembled in eight steps, and which figure each member segment contributes depends on the variant. Under `AS_REPORTED` — the default, and the only variant in play while no dispute on the cycle has been accepted — a segment contributes its `net_hire_cents`, the figure as originally reported by the handling road, ignoring every dispute outcome. Under `RECONCILED` — used once at least one dispute on the cycle is `ACCEPTED` — it contributes its `adjusted_net_hire_cents` instead. The steps are: `gross_hire_cents` is the sum of the members' `gross_hire_cents`; `reclaim_credit_cents` the sum of their `reclaim_credit_cents`; `penalty_cents` the sum of their `reverse_route_penalty_cents`; `repair_charge_cents` the sum of the member bills' `total_cents`, and `dispute_adjustment_cents` the sum over member segments of `adjusted_net_hire_cents` minus `net_hire_cents` under `RECONCILED` and 0 otherwise; `pre_fee_net_cents` is the sum of each member segment's variant figure plus `repair_charge_cents`, which is identically `gross_hire_cents` minus `reclaim_credit_cents` plus `penalty_cents` plus `repair_charge_cents` plus `dispute_adjustment_cents`; `clearinghouse_fee_cents` is the fee described next; `net_settlement_cents` is `pre_fee_net_cents` minus `clearinghouse_fee_cents`, which is what the payer remits; and `movement_line_count` is the count of member segments, while `variant_applied` records the variant used and `as_reported_net_cents` and `reconciled_net_cents` each retain the most recent `net_settlement_cents` produced by their own variant, so a reconciled evaluation never destroys the as-reported figure. Segments are visited in `end_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending so the statement is reproducible; because every per-line figure is already an integer, that order affects only the printed line sequence and never the totals. Everything is integer cents and the only rounding anywhere in the statement is inside the fee.

**The clearinghouse fee is a flat selected rate, not a marginal ladder.** Its basis is the absolute value of `pre_fee_net_cents` — the net *before* the fee, never `net_settlement_cents`, since feeding the post-fee figure back would be circular. The rate is 0.5 percent when the basis is at or below 500000 cents and 0.35 percent when it is above, and the selected rate applies to the whole basis with no blending, so the fee curve steps *down* as the basis crosses the break: a basis of 499999 yields 2500, a basis of exactly 500000 also yields 2500 because the break is still tier one, and a basis of 500001 yields 1750 — one cent more basis, 750 cents less fee. Ordinary tier-one cases round half up in the usual way: 18708 yields 94, 14008 yields 70, 8035 yields 40 from 40.175, and 3906 yields 20 from 19.53.

Compute also enforces the payer's credit: after computation the payer road's `per_diem_exposure_cents` must not exceed its `per_diem_credit_limit_cents`, and a compute that would breach the limit is refused with `ERR_PER_DIEM_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, leaving the cycle at `OPEN` or `REOPENED`. A successful compute takes the cycle `OPEN → COMPUTED` on the first pass and `REOPENED → COMPUTED` on any later one, stamps `computed_at` and increments `version`. The `variant` may be named explicitly on the request.

**Serving, paying, reopening, archiving.** The payee road exchanges the computed statement, moving `COMPUTED → EXCHANGED`, stamping `exchanged_at` and setting `dispute_deadline_at` to 30 days after it; exchange is what makes the cycle a legal dispute container. The payer road — and only the payer road, never the payee — then settles, moving `EXCHANGED → SETTLED`, but only if no dispute on the cycle is still `OPEN` or `COUNTERED`; otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_SETTLEMENT`. Settling stamps `settled_at`, sets `reopen_deadline_at` to 30 days after it, mints a `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT` ledger entry for the negative of `net_settlement_cents`, and reduces the payer road's `per_diem_exposure_cents` by `net_settlement_cents`. A `ClearinghouseAdmin` may reopen a settled cycle at or before `reopen_deadline_at`, taking it `SETTLED → REOPENED` with a supplied `reopen_reason`, stamping `reopened_at`, incrementing `reopen_count`, reversing the `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT` with a `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` entry of the opposite amount and restoring the payer's exposure; after the deadline it is `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The same `SETTLED → REOPENED` edge is also taken automatically as a cascade whenever an allowance, a withdrawal or an applied correction changes a member already stamped into a settled cycle. From `REOPENED` the payee computes again and the loop continues. Finally the admin archives a settled cycle, `SETTLED → ARCHIVED`, once the reopen window has *fully* elapsed — the logical clock must be strictly after `reopen_deadline_at`, and archiving while the cycle can still be reopened is `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN`. Archiving stamps `archived_at` and freezes everything: every later write touching a member segment, reclaim, repair bill or ledger entry is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_ARCHIVED_IMMUTABLE`. `ARCHIVED` is the only terminal state.

Cycles are listed in `period_month` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and filtered by `payer_road_id`, `payee_road_id`, `period_month` and `status`. Reading one cycle also yields the `version` that a dispute filing must echo. Separately, a computed read returns the statement without mutating anything, so the two variants can be compared side by side: it accepts a `variant` selecting `AS_REPORTED` by default or `RECONCILED`, and returns `settlement_cycle_id`, `variant_applied`, `payer_road_id`, `payee_road_id`, `period_month`, `movement_line_count`, `gross_hire_cents`, `reclaim_credit_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `repair_charge_cents`, `dispute_adjustment_cents`, `pre_fee_net_cents`, `clearinghouse_fee_cents`, `net_settlement_cents`, and the membership lists `included_movement_ids` and `included_repair_bill_ids`.

**Filing a dispute.** Only the payer road disputes, and only one line at a time. The filing names the `settlement_cycle_id`, the `dispute_reason` — one of `HIRE_RATE_MISAPPLIED`, `MILEAGE_DISCREPANCY`, `RECLAIM_DENIED_IMPROPERLY`, `REPAIR_RESPONSIBILITY` or `DUPLICATE_CHARGE` — the `disputed_amount_cents` the filer asserts the line should be, the `expected_cycle_version` of §2.7, and exactly one of `movement_id` and `repair_bill_id`, optionally with a `filing_note`. Supplying both, or neither, leaves the subject ambiguous and is `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_AMBIGUOUS`. The named subject must belong to the named cycle — its own `settlement_cycle_id` must be that cycle — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_SUBJECT_NOT_IN_CYCLE`. The cycle must be `EXCHANGED`: a merely `COMPUTED` cycle has not been served on the counterparty and a `SETTLED` or `REOPENED` one is past the dispute stage, both `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_EXCHANGED`. The filing must arrive at or before `dispute_deadline_at`, inclusive, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. At most one non-terminal dispute may exist per cycle and subject, a second being `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The server snapshots `subject_net_at_filing_cents` from the subject — the segment's `net_hire_cents` or the bill's `total_cents` — and `disputed_amount_cents` must be greater than 0 and no greater than that snapshot, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SUBJECT`. The dispute is created `OPEN` with `round_number` 0, `filed_by_road_id` set to the cycle's payer and `respondent_road_id` to the cycle's payee, and the cycle's `version` is incremented by 1.

**The negotiation alternates by state.** The dispute's status *is* whose turn it is: while `OPEN` the respondent moves, and while `COUNTERED` the filer moves. Countering flips the state — `OPEN → COUNTERED` when the respondent counters and `COUNTERED → OPEN` when the filer counters back — incrementing `round_number`, replacing `counter_amount_cents` with the supplied value, stamping `last_action_at` and incrementing `version`. A `counter_note` may accompany the move. `round_number` must be strictly below 3, so the third counter succeeds and the fourth is `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The permitted range depends on the direction of the move: from `OPEN`, `counter_amount_cents` must be at least 0 and at most `disputed_amount_cents`; from `COUNTERED`, it must be at least the standing `counter_amount_cents` and at most `disputed_amount_cents`, so the filer may only walk the offer up, never back down. A figure outside the range for the direction of the move is `ERR_COUNTER_AMOUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A party acting out of turn — the respondent countering twice in a row, for instance — is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_TURN_VIOLATION` even though it is a party to the cycle.

Acceptance is available to whichever party the state names: the respondent accepts `OPEN → ACCEPTED`, the filer accepts `COUNTERED → ACCEPTED`. Either way `resolution_amount_cents` becomes the standing `counter_amount_cents` when a counter stands and `disputed_amount_cents` when none does, `resolved_at` is stamped, and the money moves: the subject segment's `adjusted_net_hire_cents` becomes its `net_hire_cents` minus `resolution_amount_cents`, a `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry for the negative of the resolution is minted, and the cycle is recomputed under `RECONCILED`. Withdrawal belongs to the filer alone, as either `OPEN → WITHDRAWN` or `COUNTERED → WITHDRAWN`, requires a `withdraw_reason`, sets `resolution_amount_cents` to 0, stamps `resolved_at`, mints no ledger entry and leaves the statement standing `AS_REPORTED`. `ACCEPTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

Because the fee tier is re-evaluated on the new basis, an accepted dispute does not move the remittance by its own amount. Three member segments netting 8775, 5233 and 4700 with no repair bills give an `AS_REPORTED` `pre_fee_net_cents` of 18708, a fee of 94 and a `net_settlement_cents` of 18614; after a dispute on the 4700 line is accepted at its full 4700, `dispute_adjustment_cents` is -4700, `pre_fee_net_cents` is 14008, the fee is 70 and the remittance is 13938 — a movement of 4676, not 4700.

Disputes are addressed by `dispute_id`, listed in `created_at` ascending order, and filtered by `settlement_cycle_id`, `movement_id`, `repair_bill_id`, `filed_by_road_id`, `respondent_road_id`, `status` and `dispute_reason`. Reading one dispute is also how a road learns whose turn it is.

### 3.9. Data-Quality Corrections, Exposure Management and the Hire Ledger

The interchange log is append-only in time, so a handoff nobody recorded, a timestamp typed wrong, or a rejection that never happened cannot simply be edited. Each is repaired through a correction request, which is filed by one road, agreed by the other, and only then applied.

**Filing.** An `InterchangeClerk` files against a car's event history, naming the `car_id`, the `junction_id`, the `counterparty_road_id` — the other road to the corrected handoff, and the only road that may confirm or reject — and the `correction_type`, which is one of `MISSED_EVENT_INSERT`, `TIMESTAMP_AMEND` or `ERRONEOUS_EVENT_VOID`. The acting railroad becomes `filed_by_road_id`. The remaining inputs are conditional on the type. A `MISSED_EVENT_INSERT` reconstructs a handoff from scratch and therefore requires the `junction_id`, a `direction`, a `proposed_interchange_at`, a `proposed_odometer_miles` and a `proposed_load_status`, and requires `target_event_id` to be absent — there is no event to point at. `direction` is `FILER_DELIVERS`, meaning the reconstructed event has `delivering_road_id` equal to `filed_by_road_id` and `receiving_road_id` equal to `counterparty_road_id`, or `FILER_RECEIVES`, its mirror; `proposed_load_status` is `LOADED` or `EMPTY`. A `TIMESTAMP_AMEND` moves a recorded instant and requires a `target_event_id` naming a `RECORDED` event plus a `proposed_interchange_at`. An `ERRONEOUS_EVENT_VOID` erases a rejection and requires a `target_event_id` naming a `RECORDED` event whose `event_type` is `INTERCHANGE_REJECTION` — no other event type is voidable this way. A type-target mismatch in any direction is `ERR_CORRECTION_TARGET_NOT_VOIDABLE`. A `filing_note` may explain the request. The request is created `FILED` with `confirm_deadline_at` set to 72 hours after `created_at`.

**Agreement.** Only the `counterparty_road_id` may respond; the road that filed a correction can never confirm or reject its own filing, which is `ERR_CORRECTION_CONFIRM_NOT_COUNTERPARTY`. Confirming takes the request `FILED → CONFIRMED`, stamps `confirmed_at` and may carry a `confirm_note`; it must arrive at or before `confirm_deadline_at`, and a confirmation at exactly that instant is still accepted while one instant later is `ERR_CORRECTION_CONFIRM_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Rejecting takes it `FILED → REJECTED` with the supplied `rejection_reason` and `rejected_at` stamped, and the log stands as captured. A `ClearinghouseAdmin` may also run a sweep that expires every `FILED` request whose window has elapsed, processing them in `created_at` ascending order, taking each `FILED → EXPIRED` and stamping `expired_at`; requests already `CONFIRMED`, `APPLIED`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` are untouched, so a second call with nothing newly due expires 0. The sweep returns `expired_count`, the list `expired_correction_request_ids` and the `evaluated_at` instant it used. An expired request can never be confirmed or applied. `APPLIED`, `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

**Application is the deepest cascade in the system**, and it runs in five layers in a fixed order, taking the request `CONFIRMED → APPLIED`. It is available to the filing road — the acting railroad must be `filed_by_road_id` — or to a `ClearinghouseAdmin`, which is unrestricted; the request must be `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and that dedicated gate replaces the generic state gate of §2.8. No segment the correction touches may belong to an `ARCHIVED` cycle.

Layer one rewrites the event log and stamps `applied_at`. A `MISSED_EVENT_INSERT` mints a new interchange event whose `source` is `CORRECTION_APPLY`; a `TIMESTAMP_AMEND` takes the target event `RECORDED → SUPERSEDED` and mints a replacement; an `ERRONEOUS_EVENT_VOID` takes the target event `RECORDED → VOID`. Where an event is minted, its identifier is recorded on the request as `resulting_event_id`. Layer two rewrites segment boundaries: `start_at` and `end_at` are re-derived from the surviving `RECORDED` events in `interchange_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, a still-open segment left with zero length — one whose `start_at` equals the reconstructed `proposed_interchange_at` — transitions `OPEN → VOID`, a closed segment whose opening event has been erased transitions `CLOSED → VOID` provided it belongs to no `ARCHIVED` cycle, with any `HIRE_CHARGE` entry already minted for it reversed by a `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` entry of the opposite amount, and voiding an `INTERCHANGE_REJECTION` clears the `bad_order_from` and `bad_order_to` stamps it had placed on the segment. A `VOID` segment is thereafter excluded from every calculation and from every settlement cycle. Layer three recomputes money on every affected segment — `chargeable_hours`, `time_component_cents`, `mileage_component_cents`, `gross_hire_cents` and `net_hire_cents` — and increments each `version`. Layer four re-examines evidence: any `ALLOWED` reclaim claim whose supporting bad-order stamp has disappeared moves `ALLOWED → DISALLOWED` with `disallow_reason` set to `SUPPORTING_EVENT_VOIDED` and its credit removed from the segment, exactly as described in §3.5. Layer five reaches the ledger and the statements: for every affected segment already stamped into a cycle a `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` entry is minted for the new net minus the old net, each affected cycle recomputes its money fields — a `SETTLED` cycle being taken back to `REOPENED` first — and both railroads' `per_diem_exposure_cents` and `foreign_car_balance` are refreshed. Requests are addressed by `correction_request_id`, listed in `created_at` ascending order, and filtered by `car_id`, `junction_id`, `filed_by_road_id`, `counterparty_road_id`, `target_event_id`, `resulting_event_id`, `correction_type` and `status`; reading one is how the filer learns its `resulting_event_id`.

**Per-diem exposure.** A road's exposure is what it currently owes for foreign cars it is holding, and it governs whether a statement can be computed against it. The input set is every segment whose `handling_road_id` is that railroad, whose `hire_liable` is true, and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, less the amounts already remitted. Under the `SNAPSHOT` variant — the default, and the variant that maintains the stored `per_diem_exposure_cents` — a `CLOSED` segment contributes its `net_hire_cents` as it stands, while an `OPEN` segment is accrued as if it closed at the logical clock, running the time component of §3.4 with `end_at` set to now and adding mileage of 0, since no delivery odometer has been reported yet. From that sum is subtracted the `net_settlement_cents` of every `SETTLED` cycle in which the railroad is the payer. The `PROJECTED` variant is identical except that each `OPEN` segment accrues to 72 hours beyond the logical clock; `CLOSED` segments and the settled subtraction are unchanged, so the two variants differ by exactly the extra accrual on the open set. Each open segment is rounded by the time-component calculation before summing, and everything is integer cents. One open segment starting at `2026-08-30T00:00:00Z` on an `XM`-rated car with the clock at `2026-09-02T00:00:00Z` gives a snapshot of 7800 — 72.00 elapsed, 68.00 chargeable, three days — and a projection of 17100, accruing to `2026-09-05T00:00:00Z` for 144.00 elapsed, 140.00 chargeable and six days priced as two days at 2350 plus four at 3100; the projection is more than double the snapshot because the escalated second-tier rate dominates the extra three days. `credit_headroom_cents` is `per_diem_credit_limit_cents` minus the exposure, and a statement compute that would drive headroom negative is refused with `ERR_PER_DIEM_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Exposure is refreshed on every segment close, reclaim decision, correction application and cycle settlement.

**Fleet utilization** is evaluated on demand over every car whose `owner_road_id` is the railroad. `owned_car_count` is the number of owned cars; `on_foreign_count` those whose `custody_status` is `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD`; `bad_order_count` those whose `mechanical_status` is `BAD_ORDER` or `UNDER_REPAIR`; and `serviceable_foreign_count` those that are both `ON_FOREIGN_ROAD` and `SERVICEABLE`. `utilization_pct` is 100 times `serviceable_foreign_count` divided by `owned_car_count`, rounded half up to exactly two decimal places — 7 owned cars of which 3 are serviceable on a foreign road gives 42.86 — and is 0.00 when the fleet is empty, never an error and never null.

Both blocks are returned by one computed read on a railroad, which accepts a `variant` selecting `SNAPSHOT` by default or `PROJECTED` and returns `railroad_id`, `variant_applied`, `exposure_cents`, `open_movement_count`, `closed_unsettled_cents`, `settled_paid_cents`, `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_headroom_cents`, `owned_car_count`, `on_foreign_count`, `bad_order_count`, `serviceable_foreign_count`, `utilization_pct` and `foreign_car_balance`.

**The hire ledger.** Every movement of money leaves an immutable double-entry line. Ledger entries are never updated and never deleted, and every reversal is a new entry with the opposite sign; there is no endpoint that creates one, because rows are minted only by the settlement, reclaim, dispute and correction cascades described above. Each entry carries the `settlement_cycle_id` of the cycle whose running total it belongs to, and whichever subject reference applies: `movement_id` on `HIRE_CHARGE` and `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT` entries, `repair_bill_id` on `REPAIR_CHARGE` entries, `reclaim_claim_id` on `RECLAIM_CREDIT` and `RECLAIM_REVERSAL` entries, and `dispute_id` on `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT` entries. Its `entry_type` names the cascade that minted it and is one of `HIRE_CHARGE`, `REPAIR_CHARGE`, `RECLAIM_CREDIT`, `RECLAIM_REVERSAL`, `CORRECTION_ADJUSTMENT`, `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT`, `RECOMPUTE_REVERSAL` or `SETTLEMENT_PAYMENT`. Its `amount_cents` is signed from the payer road's perspective — positive increases what the payer owes the payee, negative decreases it — with `debit_road_id` being the road charged, which is the cycle's payer, and `credit_road_id` the road credited, which is the cycle's payee. `effective_at` is the logical clock at the mutation that minted it, and `memo` is a machine-generated audit note naming the triggering endpoint. Entries are read one at a time by `hire_ledger_entry_id` and listed in `effective_at` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, filtered by `settlement_cycle_id`, `movement_id`, `repair_bill_id`, `reclaim_claim_id`, `dispute_id`, `debit_road_id`, `credit_road_id`, `entry_type` and the range bounds `effective_at_from` and `effective_at_to`. This is the audit surface on which money conservation is checked, and it is visible only to the clearinghouse and to car hire accountants.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/car-hire` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control described in §2.4. Creation endpoints return HTTP 201 with the created resource; every other successful call returns HTTP 200 with the resource as it stands after the call, except the two sweeps and computed reads whose bespoke payloads are set out in §3.8 and §3.9. In the authorization column, "All roles" means all five roles named in §2.1 — `ClearinghouseAdmin`, `InterchangeClerk`, `CarHireAccountant`, `RepairBillingAgent` and `FleetOwner` — and a role's presence in that column is only the first gate: the party-standing and actor rules of §2.1 and §3 still decide whether the acting railroad may perform the action on that particular resource.
