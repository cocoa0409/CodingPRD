# Consumer Repair Parts & Self-Service Repair Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Right-to-repair legislation has turned what used to be an internal spare-parts desk into a regulated, jurisdiction-aware public service. A consumer electronics manufacturer must now prove — per model, per region, for years after the last unit leaves the line — that spare components are still orderable, that disassembly guides and safety sheets are still published and current, and that the price it charges for documentation stays inside a statutory cap. Set against that, the manufacturer carries genuine safety obligations: a lithium cell or a high-voltage board handed to an untrained consumer is not the same transaction as the same part handed to a trained, audited independent shop.

This document specifies the API that reconciles those two pressures. It governs a parts catalog with repairability tiers and safety-restricted component policies; a pricing structure spanning components, tools and rental kits with refundable core deposits; revision-controlled repair documentation whose publication cascades into regional compliance posture; a self-repair eligibility engine that issues a warranty-impact disclosure and a liability warning the buyer must counter-sign; parts authentication and post-replacement calibration; an authorization program for independent repair providers gated on training certifications and a computed compliance score; defective-part returns with failure analysis and graded credit; and a long-tail demand forecast used to decide how much stock a decade-old model still warrants.

Two properties dominate the design and should be read as constraints rather than nice-to-haves. First, almost nothing in this system is local: restricting one part's safety class revokes eligibility rulings and freezes orders; publishing one manual revision can flip a model's documentation posture, breach a regional obligation, and block ordering for that model entirely. Second, money and credits are conserved, never merely computed — the core-deposit ledger on a part and the training-credit ledger on a provider must balance after every single write. Every API surface described below is mounted under the `/repair-program` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere: identity, envelopes, pagination, time, rounding, stale-version protection, cascade discipline, rejection ordering, and the two conservation invariants.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting individual, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. The programme recognises exactly seven roles: `ProgramAdmin`, `SafetyReviewer`, `DocumentationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SelfRepairCustomer`, `RepairProviderTech`, and `FailureAnalysisEngineer`. A request whose role header is absent, empty, or not one of those seven strings is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (401); where `X-User-Role` is absent altogether, an `X-Role` header is consulted as a fallback and carries exactly the same meaning. A request whose role is one of the seven but is not among the roles the target endpoint authorises is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). A write that records an actor identity — an eligibility assessment, an order, a line, a disclosure acknowledgment, a review decision, a publication, a failure analysis, a credit decision, an attestation, a certification, a certification void, or an authorization decision — sent without a `X-User-ID` header, or with an empty one, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (400). That user-id requirement covers every write endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint and the compliance-sweep endpoint, both of which are operator utilities that record no actor.

Six of the seven identities are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `SafetyReviewer`, `DocumentationEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, or `FailureAnalysisEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `authored_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `ManualRevision`, as `last_attested_by_user_id` on a `ComplianceObligation`, as `analyzed_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a `DefectiveReturnCase` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SelfRepairCustomer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque consumer identifier that no entity row represents; it is recorded as `customer_user_id` on a `PartsOrder`, as `requester_user_id` on an `EligibilityAssessment`, and as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on a `DisclosureAcknowledgment`, and ownership checks compare those stored user-id fields to the caller's `X-User-ID` — never to an entity `id`. A `SelfRepairCustomer` acting on an order whose recorded `customer_user_id` differs from the caller is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED` (403); the ownership comparison is applied to the order only, so an assessment's `requester_user_id` is never compared to the caller and a customer may open an order against another requester's ruling. On the acknowledgment endpoint alone the comparison against `customer_user_id` is applied whatever the buyer role, so a technician acting on an order recorded under a different user id is likewise rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`.

`RepairProviderTech` is the one entity-backed identity. When `X-User-Role` is `RepairProviderTech`, `X-User-ID` is resolved to the acting `RepairProvider` by looking for a `TrainingCertification` row whose `technician_user_id` equals that value; the certification's `provider_id` names the acting provider. The search takes the earliest such row by `created_at` and insertion sequence and reads it whatever its `status` and whatever its validity window, so a `VOIDED` or lapsed certification still resolves a technician. Ownership checks on any provider-scoped resource compare that resolved provider against the resource's `provider_id`; where the resource carries a null `provider_id` — a consumer-class order or ruling opened by a technician, for instance — resolution to some provider is still required but no comparison is made. A technician whose user id resolves to a different provider, or resolves to no provider at all, is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). That check guards every write a technician may perform against a provider-scoped resource — the eligibility ruling, the order, the line append, the disclosure request, the acknowledgment, the confirmation, the cancellation, the authenticate and calibrate actions, the certification filing and the defective-return filing — and, among the reads, the provider compliance-score read. The plain single-resource and collection reads apply no provider scoping at all, so a technician may read them whatever their user id resolves to.

Authority is deliberately partitioned. A `ProgramAdmin` alone may register product models and drive both lifecycle actions, create and publish catalog items, register compatibility rows, deliver an order, book in a core return, register providers and start their training, void a certification, and book in a defective unit. A `SafetyReviewer` alone may restrict a published part — it is the only role that can raise a part's safety class and fire the four-hop safety cascade — and alone may render a review decision on a manual revision or publish one. A `DocumentationEngineer` authors and submits revisions but can neither approve nor publish them, and cannot restrict a part. A `ComplianceOfficer` alone may attest an obligation, and shares profile creation, obligation creation, compliance sweeps, authorization decisions and clock movement with the `ProgramAdmin`. `SelfRepairCustomer` and `RepairProviderTech` share the whole buyer flow and defective-return filing. A `FailureAnalysisEngineer` alone may record a root cause on a returned unit, and shares the credit decision with the `ProgramAdmin`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time, so it repeats until the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every collection read is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips rows and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside that band, or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`; a value of either that is not parseable as an integer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, collections are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, and because the logical clock does not advance on its own, rows sharing a `created_at` are ordered by insertion sequence. Every list response repeats `limit` and `offset` in the envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count`, which counts the whole filtered set before the page is cut, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when the `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still less than `total_count` — so an `offset` beyond the end yields an empty page and `has_more` false.

Collections also accept domain filters, all optional and all combinable with pagination. A filter supplied empty is ignored exactly as an absent one is; a string filter matches by exact equality; a boolean filter accepts `true`, `1`, `yes`, `false`, `0`, and `no` and refuses anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and an `as_of_date` filter must parse as a timestamp, an unparseable value being refused the same way, and selects the compatibility rows whose half-open window contains the instant given. Product-model listings filter on `product_family`, `support_state`, `repairability_tier`, `compliance_posture`, and `documentation_posture`. Regional profile listings filter on `region_code` and `self_service_mandate_tier`. Obligation listings filter on `model_id`, `region_code`, `status`, and `deadline_basis`. Catalog listings filter on `part_class`, `safety_class`, `status`, and `supersedes_part_id` — that last filter is the flat reverse lookup across the self-referential supersession edge, and a dedicated successors read returns the parts that name a given part as their predecessor. Compatibility listings filter on `part_id`, `model_id`, `required_tool_part_id`, `self_service_eligible`, and `as_of_date`; the model-scoped and part-scoped compatibility reads accept the same `as_of_date` filter to ask which fitment row was in force at a chosen instant. Manual-revision listings filter on `model_id`, `doc_type`, and `status`. Assessment listings filter on `model_id`, `part_id`, `provider_id`, `pinned_manual_revision_id`, `decision`, and `status`. Order listings filter on `model_id`, `assessment_id`, `provider_id`, `pinned_manual_revision_id`, `fulfillment_status`, and `core_return_status`. Order-line listings filter on `order_id`, `part_id`, `calibration_status`, and `authentication_status`. Acknowledgment listings filter on `order_id` and `acknowledged_scope`. Provider listings filter on `region_code`, `authorization_status`, and `parts_access_tier`. Certification listings filter on `provider_id`, `technician_user_id`, `course_code`, and `status`. Defective-return listings filter on `order_line_id`, `part_id`, `status`, `root_cause_code`, and `reported_failure_code`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only change a computed field. Assigning a field the value it already holds is not a mutation: a row a request only ever re-assigns identical values to keeps its `version` and its `updated_at`, unless the endpoint that reached it is described here as stamping that row unconditionally. There is one deliberate exception: a `DisclosureAcknowledgment` is an append-only legal record. It carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no endpoint updates it after creation. Where `updated_at` and `version` are carried, they move at most once per request: a request that writes several fields of the same row, or that reaches that row again through a later cascade hop, still advances its `version` by exactly one, and a row the request itself created stays at `version` 1 however many derivations the same request then applies to it. A write's own response body reports the post-write values — the row it returns already carries its incremented `version`, its refreshed `updated_at`, and every derived or recomputed field the request wrote onto that row. A request that ends in a refusal changes nothing at all: rows it had already altered are restored and rows it had already inserted are discarded, so no `version` moves and no partial write survives.

All temporal rules read a single **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An admin-only test-driving capability moves it, taking a `to_iso8601` instant in the body and answering with the new `current_utc` value. Movement must be strictly forward: an instant equal to or earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONICITY` (422). Moving the clock is inert by itself — it re-evaluates no obligation, expires no assessment, and closes no window. Derived compliance state changes only when a compliance sweep is run or when a cascade forces a re-evaluation.

### 2.5. Money, Counts, Ratios and Rounding

All money in this system is integer cents. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response body. Where rounding is required, the method is always **round half up**, meaning half-away-from-zero to whole cents. Rounding points are pinned per calculation and are never applied twice: for order pricing the single rounding per line happens when the line total is formed, and the displayed unit price is rounded separately and never re-used as an input. Ratios and multipliers are carried as exact rational values through every intermediate step and are rounded only where the owning calculation says so. Scores are integers in the range 0 to 100, rounded half up at the end. Unit counts in the demand forecast are made integral only by the two explicit round-up steps described in §3.9; the projected-unit figure is reported rounded half up to two decimal places, but the unrounded value is what feeds the safety-stock tier and the recommended-stock arithmetic. Both conservation ledgers of §2.10 permit no rounding at all — every movement is an exact integer transfer.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Stale Parent Versions

Exactly two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and both guard against a client writing a child row against a parent snapshot it has not re-read.

`POST /repair-program/manual-revisions` requires a non-optional `expected_model_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductModel`. If that value does not equal the model's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MODEL_VERSION` (412). This guard is the whole point of the field: the product deliberately permits more than one `DRAFT` revision for the same model and document type, so a second create against unchanged state would legitimately succeed. What the guard forbids is an author whose view of the model predates a publication, an end-of-production stamp, or a documentation-posture cascade adding another revision without re-reading the model first. The canonical way to obtain the current value is the single-model read. On success, the model's `version` also increments.

`POST /repair-program/parts-orders/{order_id}/lines` requires a non-optional `expected_order_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PartsOrder`. If it does not equal the order's current `version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (412). Multiple lines per order are entirely legitimate and no state precondition blocks a second append; the guard exists so that a buyer cannot append against a pricing snapshot that an earlier append or a disclosure request has already moved. Because a successful append increments the order's `version`, the order must be re-read before each further append.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

Many writes in this programme are not confined to the row named in the path. Where a write mutates a second or third row, the effect is specified explicitly in §3 and is part of the contract: each touched row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, even when the client never named it. Two cascades run four hops deep. Restricting a part flags its compatibility rows, revokes matching eligibility rulings, and conditionally freezes the orders behind those rulings. Publishing a manual revision supersedes the previous revision of the same document family, re-derives the model's documentation posture, and re-evaluates every compliance obligation of that model, whose new statuses in turn re-derive the model's compliance posture. Both are described hop by hop in §3.3 and §3.4 respectively.

Three derived rollups are recomputed rather than stored by clients: a model's `documentation_posture` together with its two published-revision numbers, a model's `compliance_posture` together with its `open_obligation_count`, and an obligation's `status`. A rollup always reads current state and overwrites the derived field; it never merges with what was there before. One consequence is worth stating plainly: an `ATTESTED` obligation status is written only by the attestation action and is not preserved by re-evaluation, so the next sweep or cascade re-derives one of the other five values.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Nesting and Ownership

Where a URL path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first unresolvable one determines the error. Each identifier has its own not-found code: `model_id` yields `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `part_id` yields `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `compatibility_id` yields `ERR_COMPATIBILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `revision_id` yields `ERR_MANUAL_REVISION_NOT_FOUND`, `assessment_id` yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `order_id` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `line_id` yields `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `provider_id` yields `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `certification_id` yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `case_id` yields `ERR_RETURN_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `obligation_id` yields `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, and `acknowledgment_id` yields `ERR_ACKNOWLEDGMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve are 404 responses. The same codes apply when the identifier arrives in a request body rather than in the path — an unknown `supersedes_part_id` or `required_tool_part_id` is an `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `order_line_id` on a defective-return filing is an `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `provider_id` in a body is an `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Nesting is also an ownership assertion. A line identifier that resolves to a row belonging to a different order than the one named in the path is treated as not found and yields `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, on both the authenticate and the calibrate actions. Region codes are resolved as a natural key rather than by identifier: any `region_code` supplied when creating an obligation, an eligibility assessment, an order, or a provider must match an existing regional profile, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_REGION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` (404).

### 2.9. The Global Rejection Ladder

Checks are evaluated in one global order, and the first check whose condition holds returns immediately — no later check is evaluated, so the earlier code always masks the later one. The ordering, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. **Role admissibility** — unknown or missing role (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then missing user id on an identity-recording write (`ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`), then a known role that the endpoint does not authorise (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`).
2. **Structural validity** — malformed body, missing required field, wrong JSON type, out-of-set enum value, or an unknown field (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`); then numeric range (`ERR_INVALID_RANGE`); then inverted intervals (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`); then natural-key uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`).
3. **Referential resolution** — the twelve not-found codes of §2.8, then `ERR_REGION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, then nested-ownership not-found, then supersession-graph refusals (`ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE`, then `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CLASS_MISMATCH`).
4. **Stale parent version** — `ERR_STALE_MODEL_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`.
5. **Caller scope** — `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`, then `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, then the two segregation-of-duties refusals `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` and `ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN`.
6. **Safety interlocks** — `ERR_ORDER_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PART`, then `ERR_AUTHENTICATION_NOT_APPLICABLE`, then `ERR_AUTHENTICATION_REQUIRED_FIRST`, then `ERR_CALIBRATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, then `ERR_CALIBRATION_INCOMPLETE`.
7. **State admissibility** — the generic undefined-trigger refusal `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, then in order `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_CERTS`, `ERR_TRAINING_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VALID`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BLOCKED`, `ERR_REVISION_COVERAGE_GAP`, `ERR_PART_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_CALIBRATION_PROCEDURE`, `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_ROOT_CAUSE`, `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_REACHED`, `ERR_SAFETY_WARNINGS_REQUIRED`.
8. **Compliance interlocks** — `ERR_MODEL_ORDER_RESTRICTED`, then `ERR_PARTS_ACCESS_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_DISCLOSURE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_LIABILITY_WARNING_STALE`, then `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, then `ERR_ATTESTATION_UNSUPPORTED`.
9. **Temporal windows** — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_PARTS_WINDOW_ELAPSED`, then `ERR_RENTAL_WINDOW_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_CORE_RECEIPT_BEFORE_CONFIRM`, then `ERR_CERTIFICATION_ALREADY_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONICITY`.
10. **Capacity and quota** — `ERR_TRAINING_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_RENTAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_CORE_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_RETURN_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_LINE`, then the two window-overlap refusals `ERR_COMPATIBILITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` and `ERR_CERTIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
11. **Conservation refusals, evaluated last of all** — `ERR_CORE_LEDGER_INVARIANT`, then `ERR_CREDIT_RESTORE_FORBIDDEN`.

Two consequences of that ordering are easy to get wrong and are therefore called out. On order confirmation, the restricted-part interlock in band 6 is evaluated **before** the expired-assessment check in band 9: an order that both holds a line on a restricted part and carries an assessment past its validity window is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PART`, not with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_EXPIRED`. On certification filing, the provider-state check in band 7 is evaluated **before** the training-credit balance check in band 10: a certification filed against a provider that is not accepting certifications and that also has no credits left is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_CERTS`, not with `ERR_TRAINING_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`.

The generic undefined-trigger refusal in band 7 covers any action that the entity's current state does not define, including repeating an action that has already been consumed and any action attempted out of a terminal state. It has exactly one exemption: a second core-return booking against an order whose core dimension has already settled is a *declared but guarded* action, so it is not reported as an undefined trigger — it falls all the way through to the conservation refusal in band 11.

### 2.10. The Two Conservation Invariants

**Core money on a catalog part.** For every `PartCatalogItem`, the sum of `core_liability_outstanding_cents`, `core_credit_settled_cents`, and `core_credit_forfeited_cents` must equal `core_face_value_committed_cents` at all times. Confirming an order raises both the committed total and the outstanding total by that order's core face value for that part. Cancelling an order lowers *both* of those totals by the same amount — the compensation moves both sides, which is why a cancellation is not a settlement. Booking in a core return moves face value out of the outstanding total and into the settled total for the credited portion and into the forfeited total for the uncredited remainder. The face value in play for a part on an order is that part's `core_credit_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`. No rounding is permitted anywhere in this ledger. Any operation that would drive the outstanding total below zero is refused with `ERR_CORE_LEDGER_INVARIANT` (409).

**Training credits on a provider.** For every `RepairProvider`, the sum of `training_credit_balance`, the `credits_consumed` totals of that provider's certifications whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, and `training_credit_forfeited_total` must equal `training_credit_granted_total` at all times. Filing a certification lowers the balance by two units and sets that certification's consumption to two. Voiding a certification returns the balance by two units and sets the certification's consumption to zero — a `VOIDED` row contributes nothing. Revoking a provider sweeps the entire remaining balance into the forfeited total and leaves the balance at zero. Because that sweep is irreversible, a void that would restore credit to a revoked provider is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_RESTORE_FORBIDDEN` (409). Credit units are always whole numbers.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this programme may never be taken by the person who prepared them. On documentation, the individual whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as a revision's `authored_by_user_id` may never supply that revision's review decision; the attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` (403). This holds even when the author holds the `SafetyReviewer` role — authoring and reviewing are separated by identity, not by role. On defective returns, the individual whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as a case's `analyzed_by_user_id` may never supply that case's credit decision, whether the decision issues credit or rejects it; the attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN` (403). A single engineer can therefore never both diagnose a failure and pay for it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Product Models and the Support Lifecycle

Everything in the programme hangs off a **product model**. A `ProgramAdmin` registers one by supplying a `model_code` — the manufacturer's code, globally unique and between 3 and 32 characters — a `product_family` naming the marketing family, a `market_release_date` giving first retail availability, and a `repairability_tier`, which is one of `TIER_A_SELF_SERVICE`, `TIER_B_GUIDED`, `TIER_C_AUTHORIZED_ONLY`, or `TIER_D_NON_SERVICEABLE`. Two dates are optional at registration: `end_of_production_date`, the day the last unit left the line, which stays null while the model is still in production, and `last_hardware_change_date`, the date of the last board or enclosure revision, which the documentation-staleness rule later reads. A `model_code` outside the 3-to-32-character band is a length violation rather than a range one and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Re-using an existing `model_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`; an `end_of_production_date` earlier than the `market_release_date` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, and that date comparison is evaluated before the duplicate-code check.

A freshly registered model starts with `support_state` at `ACTIVE_PRODUCTION` — or directly at `LEGACY_SUPPORT` when an `end_of_production_date` was supplied in the same request — with `support_end_date` null, `documentation_posture` at `MISSING`, `compliance_posture` at `OPEN`, `open_obligation_count` at zero, and both `published_disassembly_revision_number` and `published_safety_sheet_revision_number` null.

Two lifecycle dimensions live on the same row and evolve independently. The first is `support_state`, driven by the two admin lifecycle actions below and ending in the terminal `SUPPORT_ENDED`. The second is `compliance_posture`, which takes the values `OPEN`, `WATCH`, or `ORDER_RESTRICTED` and is re-derived every time one of the model's compliance obligations changes status; it never moves in response to a lifecycle action of its own, and `ORDER_RESTRICTED` is what gates ordering against the model.

Stamping end of production advances the model from `ACTIVE_PRODUCTION` to `LEGACY_SUPPORT`. The action requires the model to actually be in `ACTIVE_PRODUCTION` — any other `support_state` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and requires the body's `end_of_production_date` to fall on or after the model's `market_release_date`, an earlier date being refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, which sits in band 2 and is therefore evaluated before the state check. Beyond storing that date, the action re-derives the mandated support window on every compliance obligation of the model, flipping each obligation's `deadline_basis` from `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` to `END_OF_PRODUCTION` and filling in the two deadline dates that were null until now; it then re-evaluates each of those obligations' `status`; and it then recomputes the model's `compliance_posture` from the resulting statuses. The model's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every obligation of the model, whether or not the re-evaluation moved that obligation's `status`.

Ending support advances the model from `LEGACY_SUPPORT` to the terminal `SUPPORT_ENDED`. It requires the model to be in `LEGACY_SUPPORT`, requires the body's `support_end_date` to fall on or after the `end_of_production_date` — an earlier date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, checked before the state check and skipped altogether where the model carries no `end_of_production_date` — and requires that every compliance obligation of the model already sits in either `CLOSED` or `ATTESTED` — an obligation in any other status blocks the action with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Ending support stores `support_end_date`, closes every obligation still in `OPEN`, `AT_RISK`, or `ATTESTED` whose mandated parts deadline already lies in the past, expires every eligibility assessment of the model that was still `VALID`, and blocks any further manual-revision approval for that model from then on.

### 3.2. Regional Mandates, Compliance Obligations and the Sweep

A **regional compliance profile** encodes one right-to-repair jurisdiction. It is created with a `region_code` — a unique key of 2 to 8 uppercase characters, which is how child rows join to it rather than by identifier — a `min_parts_availability_years` between 0 and 15 giving the years after end of production during which parts must remain orderable, a `min_documentation_years` between 0 and 15 giving the years during which manuals must remain published, a `requires_public_manual` flag which when true means documentation must be free of charge, a `max_documentation_fee_cents` giving the statutory cap on the per-order documentation fee, an `allows_pairing_restriction` flag which when false means the jurisdiction outlaws parts pairing, and a `self_service_mandate_tier` of `MANDATE_NONE`, `MANDATE_BASIC`, or `MANDATE_FULL` expressing the strength of the local self-service mandate. Only the length of `region_code` is policed, not its case, and a code shorter than 2 or longer than 8 characters is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; either year figure outside 0 to 15, or a negative `max_documentation_fee_cents`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. Both flags are optional in the payload — `requires_public_manual` defaults to false and `allows_pairing_restriction` defaults to true — while `max_documentation_fee_cents` is mandatory. Re-using a `region_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`.

Pairing one model with one region produces a **compliance obligation**, created by a `ComplianceOfficer` or `ProgramAdmin` from a `model_id` and a `region_code`. A second obligation for the same model and region is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`. On creation the obligation's mandated window is computed, its initial `status` is derived, the parent model's posture is rolled up, `breach_count` starts at zero, `documentation_verified` starts false, and `last_attested_at` and `last_attested_by_user_id` start null. That initial derivation counts like any other, so an obligation whose creation-time status lands on `BREACHED_PARTS` or `BREACHED_DOCUMENTATION` is created with `breach_count` already at one.

The **mandated window** is computed as follows. If the model has no `end_of_production_date`, the obligation's `deadline_basis` is `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` and both `parts_available_until` and `documentation_available_until` are null — the clock has not started. Otherwise `deadline_basis` is `END_OF_PRODUCTION`; `parts_available_until` is the end of production advanced by the region's `min_parts_availability_years` whole calendar years, taken at the very end of that calendar day, namely 23:59:59.999Z; and `documentation_available_until` is the later of `parts_available_until` and the end of production advanced by the region's `min_documentation_years` whole calendar years, again at 23:59:59.999Z of that day. Calendar arithmetic adds whole years, and 29 February advanced by a number of years clamps to 28 February when the target year is not a leap year. Both deadlines are inclusive of that whole final day: a logical clock reading exactly `parts_available_until` still permits ordering, and the next millisecond does not.

An obligation's `status` is never set by a client. It is re-derived by evaluating the following branches in order and taking the first that matches. First: when `deadline_basis` is `END_OF_PRODUCTION` and the logical clock is strictly after both `parts_available_until` and `documentation_available_until`, the status is `CLOSED`. Second: when no catalog part in `PUBLISHED` status holds a compatibility row for this model whose window contains the logical clock, the status is `BREACHED_PARTS`. Third: when the model's `documentation_posture` is `MISSING`, the status is `BREACHED_DOCUMENTATION`. Fourth: when `deadline_basis` is `END_OF_PRODUCTION` and the logical clock has reached or passed the point ninety days before `parts_available_until`, or when the model's `documentation_posture` is `STALE`, the status is `AT_RISK`. Fifth and otherwise: the status is `OPEN`. Every evaluation that lands on either breached value increments `breach_count` by one, and it does so again on each repeated sweep — the counter is cumulative and is never decremented, not even when the obligation recovers to `OPEN`, because the provider compliance score keeps reading it.

Each of those outcomes bubbles up into the parent model. When any obligation of the model sits in `BREACHED_PARTS` or `BREACHED_DOCUMENTATION`, the model's `compliance_posture` becomes `ORDER_RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, when any obligation sits in `AT_RISK`, the posture becomes `WATCH`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Alongside the posture, `open_obligation_count` is set to the number of the model's obligations whose status is `OPEN`, `AT_RISK`, or `ATTESTED`. Every obligation of the model participates regardless of region; `CLOSED` obligations contribute to neither branch, and a model with no obligations at all keeps its posture at `OPEN`. An `AT_RISK` obligation raises the posture to `WATCH` only when no sibling obligation already forces `ORDER_RESTRICTED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may **attest** an obligation, moving it from `OPEN`, `AT_RISK`, `BREACHED_DOCUMENTATION`, or `BREACHED_PARTS` to `ATTESTED`. The body carries an optional `attestation_note` and a `documentation_verified` flag that defaults to false when omitted. Attestation is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_UNSUPPORTED` when `documentation_verified` is anything other than true — an attestation must assert verification — and equally when the parent model's `documentation_posture` is not `CURRENT`, so both `STALE` and `MISSING` block it. A `CLOSED` obligation, and one already sitting in `ATTESTED`, cannot be attested at all: neither is an admissible source state and both are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, a band-7 refusal that therefore masks `ERR_ATTESTATION_UNSUPPORTED`. A successful attestation stores `last_attested_at` from the logical clock, `last_attested_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and the `documentation_verified` flag, then rolls the model's posture up again; an `ATTESTED` row counts as open, never as breached, so the posture drops back to `OPEN` or `WATCH` when no sibling obligation is breached.

The **compliance sweep** is the batch counterpart, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or `ProgramAdmin`, optionally narrowed to a single `model_id` — one that names no model is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` — and otherwise covering every obligation in the system. It re-evaluates each obligation in scope, incrementing `breach_count` on each breached outcome; it moves every obligation whose `deadline_basis` is `END_OF_PRODUCTION` and whose logical clock has passed both deadlines to the terminal `CLOSED`, after which that obligation contributes nothing to the model's posture; it materialises expiry by moving every eligibility assessment still in `VALID` whose `expires_at` lies before the logical clock to `EXPIRED` — that pass is global and is not narrowed by `model_id`; and it re-rolls the posture of every model it touched. Unlike a cascade re-evaluation, the sweep advances the `version` of every obligation in scope — including one whose `status` and `breach_count` it leaves exactly as they were — and of every model behind those obligations. The sweep answers with `swept_obligation_count`, which counts the obligations in scope, a `breached_obligation_ids` list, an `expired_assessment_ids` list, and a `restricted_model_ids` list naming those of the models it touched that now sit at `ORDER_RESTRICTED`. Recovery runs through the same evaluation: an obligation in `AT_RISK`, `BREACHED_DOCUMENTATION`, or `BREACHED_PARTS` returns to `OPEN` when at least one `PUBLISHED` part holds an in-window compatibility row for the model, the model's `documentation_posture` is `CURRENT`, and either the `deadline_basis` is still `PRODUCTION_ACTIVE` or the logical clock is strictly earlier than ninety days before `parts_available_until`.

### 3.3. The Parts Catalog, Supersession and the Safety Restriction Cascade

A `ProgramAdmin` builds the catalog. A **catalog item** is registered with a `sku` — globally unique, 3 to 32 characters — a `part_class` of `COMPONENT`, `TOOL`, `RENTAL_KIT`, or `CONSUMABLE`, a `safety_class` of `UNRESTRICTED`, `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY`, `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_HIGH_VOLTAGE`, or `PAIRED_SECURITY`, a non-negative `list_price_cents` giving the undiscounted unit list price, and a `core_return_required` flag which when true means ordering the part places a refundable core deposit and opens a core-return obligation. Five inputs are optional. `core_credit_cents` is the face value of the core return credit per unit and defaults to zero. `rental_deposit_cents` is the per-unit deposit charged on rental-kit lines and defaults to zero. `concurrent_rental_capacity` is the number of kits in the rental pool and defaults to null, which is what non-rental classes carry. `requires_authentication`, defaulting to false, means a delivered line mints a pairing token and must be authenticated. `requires_calibration`, defaulting to false, means a delivered line must pass calibration after replacement. Finally `supersedes_part_id` names the predecessor SKU this part replaces and is null for original parts. A `sku` outside the 3-to-32-character band is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a negative `list_price_cents`, `core_credit_cents`, `rental_deposit_cents`, or `concurrent_rental_capacity` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE` — at registration a capacity of zero is admissible, and only publication demands at least 1. A duplicate `sku` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`, and the graph checks below run only after that duplicate check and the predecessor lookup.

A new catalog item is born in `DRAFT` with `restriction_reason` null and all four core-ledger aggregates — `core_face_value_committed_cents`, `core_liability_outstanding_cents`, `core_credit_settled_cents`, and `core_credit_forfeited_cents` — at zero. The lifecycle runs `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED`, and out of `PUBLISHED` to either the terminal `RESTRICTED` or the terminal `SUPERSEDED`.

Publication makes a part orderable and brings it inside documentation coverage checks. It requires the part to be in `DRAFT`, any other status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; because both of the following checks read stored columns rather than the request body, that `DRAFT` precondition is evaluated first and masks them. A rental kit additionally requires a `concurrent_rental_capacity` that is not null and is at least 1, otherwise the publication is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`; a part whose `core_return_required` is true additionally requires a `core_credit_cents` of at least 1, likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. Publication then reaches outward: for every product model that holds an in-window compatibility row for this part, the model's `documentation_posture` is re-derived, and a model whose published disassembly guide does not list this part's `sku` flips from `CURRENT` to `STALE`. Every one of those models then has its compliance obligations re-evaluated and its `compliance_posture` recomputed, whether or not its documentation posture actually moved. Where the newly published part declares a `supersedes_part_id`, that predecessor moves from `PUBLISHED` to `SUPERSEDED` in the same request; a predecessor sitting in any other status is left exactly as it stands. Every row touched has its `version` incremented: the part itself, and every model reached through an in-window compatibility row, unconditionally; an obligation only when the re-evaluation actually changed one of its fields.

Supersession can also be re-pointed after the fact, by naming a mandatory `supersedes_part_id` together with an optional `supersession_note` that is recorded nowhere; the newly named predecessor moves to `SUPERSEDED` if and only if it was `PUBLISHED`. Re-pointing carries no state precondition on the part being re-pointed, whatever its own status. Both at creation and at re-pointing, the supersession graph is policed: a chain that would close a cycle — where the referenced part transitively supersedes the part being created or re-pointed, or is that same part — is refused with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CYCLE`, and a successor whose `part_class` differs from its predecessor's is refused with `ERR_SUPERSESSION_CLASS_MISMATCH`. A part that has moved to `SUPERSEDED` stays orderable only through orders that were already confirmed; its compatibility rows keep their windows, but any new eligibility assessment against it resolves to a blocked, part-unavailable ruling.

**Restriction is the sharpest tool in the programme and belongs to the `SafetyReviewer` alone.** It moves a part from `PUBLISHED` to the terminal `RESTRICTED` — any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — taking a replacement `safety_class`, which must be something other than `UNRESTRICTED` or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the part is even resolved, and an optional `restriction_reason` that is stored as null when omitted. The effect propagates four hops. The first hop sets the part's `status` to `RESTRICTED`, overwrites its `safety_class` from the body, and stores the reason. The second hop, caused by the first, finds every compatibility row for this part whose `self_service_eligible` was true, forces that flag to false, and raises `superseded_by_safety_restriction` to true on those rows. The third hop, caused by the second, finds every eligibility assessment still in `VALID` whose part-and-model pair matches one of the rows just changed and whose `decision` is either `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_TOOL_RENTAL`, and moves it to `REVOKED_BY_SAFETY`. The fourth hop is conditional: among the orders standing behind the assessments just revoked, only those whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` **and** whose `disclosure_acknowledged_at` is still null move to `BLOCKED_SAFETY_HOLD` with `blocked_reason` set to the string `SAFETY_RESTRICTION_ON_PART`. Orders that already carry an acknowledgment are deliberately left in `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`; they are instead stopped later, at confirmation time, by the restricted-part interlock that answers `ERR_ORDER_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PART`. The restricted part and every row touched across hops two, three and four each have their `version` incremented.

**Fitment** is recorded separately, as a compatibility row joining one part to one model. It carries a `part_id`, a `model_id`, an `effective_from` giving the inclusive start of the compatibility window, an optional `effective_until` giving the exclusive end — null means open-ended — an optional `self_service_eligible` flag saying whether an unassisted consumer may install this part on this model, defaulting to false when omitted, a mandatory `install_minutes` figure between 1 and 600 giving documented bench time for the replacement — outside that band it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, checked before any identifier is resolved — and an optional `required_tool_part_id` naming the tool or rental kit needed to install it, null when no tool is needed. `superseded_by_safety_restriction` starts false. An `effective_until` that is at or before the `effective_from` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, since the window is half-open and must be non-empty. Two windows for the same part-and-model pair may not overlap: an overlap is refused with `ERR_COMPATIBILITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, while abutting windows — where one ends exactly when the next begins — are accepted. Registering a compatibility row re-derives the model's `documentation_posture`, because the set of parts the documentation must cover may have just grown.

### 3.4. Repair Documentation Under Revision Control

Repair documentation is versioned per model and per document family. A `DocumentationEngineer` or a `SafetyReviewer` opens a **manual revision** with a `model_id`, a `doc_type` of `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE`, `SAFETY_SHEET`, `CALIBRATION_PROCEDURE`, or `PARTS_LIST`, a mandatory `covered_part_skus` array listing the SKU strings the revision documents — which may legitimately be empty at this point, emptiness being policed only at submission and publication — a non-negative `safety_warning_count` giving the number of liability warnings the revision contains, a negative value being refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, and the `expected_model_version` guard described in §2.6. The server assigns `revision_number` itself, as one more than the highest existing revision number for the same model and document type, starting at 1. The new row begins in `DRAFT` with `authored_by_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `rejection_cycle_count` at zero, `effective_from` and `published_at` null, and `coverage_gap_sku_count` computed against the model's current required-SKU set.

The revision-control cycle is `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`, then either `IN_REVIEW` back to `DRAFT` on rejection or `IN_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`, then `APPROVED` to `PUBLISHED`, and finally `PUBLISHED` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`. The rejection loop is genuinely cyclic and repeats until the third rejection.

Submitting a revision for review advances `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`. It requires the revision to be in `DRAFT` and requires `covered_part_skus` to be a non-empty array — an empty list is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_WARNINGS_REQUIRED`. Submission recomputes `coverage_gap_sku_count` against the model's parts that are `PUBLISHED` and hold an in-window compatibility row. A revision whose `rejection_cycle_count` has already reached three cannot be resubmitted; the fourth submission is refused with `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_REACHED`.

A `SafetyReviewer` renders the **review decision**, supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with an optional `review_note` that is not itself persisted. Before either branch is considered, and before the `IN_REVIEW` state check, the segregation-of-duties guard of §2.11 applies to *both* decisions alike: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`, or the decision — approval or rejection — is refused with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`. A rejection returns the revision from `IN_REVIEW` to `DRAFT`, increments `rejection_cycle_count` by one, and stores `reviewed_by_user_id`; it too requires `rejection_cycle_count` to be below three beforehand, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_CYCLE_CAP_REACHED`. An approval moves `IN_REVIEW` to `APPROVED` and stores `reviewed_by_user_id`, and carries two further guards of its own: the parent model's `support_state` must not be `SUPPORT_ENDED`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and for a `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` the `coverage_gap_sku_count` must be exactly zero, or the approval is refused with `ERR_REVISION_COVERAGE_GAP`, because a guide that omits a published, in-window part of the model is not a complete guide.

**Publication** is likewise a `SafetyReviewer` action and moves `APPROVED` to `PUBLISHED`, any other source status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The body may supply an `effective_from`; when it is omitted the publication effective instant defaults to the logical clock, and `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock either way. The supplied or defaulted `effective_from` must fall at or after the `effective_from` of the revision currently published for the same model and document type; an earlier instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and because that refusal shares the earliest slot in band 7 it is evaluated before the warnings check that follows. A `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` or `SAFETY_SHEET` may not be published with a `safety_warning_count` of zero or with an empty `covered_part_skus` list; either is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_WARNINGS_REQUIRED`.

Publication then cascades four hops. The first hop sets the revision's `status` to `PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` and `effective_from`. The second hop, caused by the first, moves the previously published revision of the same model and document type — if there was one — to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`; that row is then frozen, and orders that pinned it keep their pinned revision number unchanged. The third hop, caused by the change of which revision is current, refreshes the parent model's `published_disassembly_revision_number` and `published_safety_sheet_revision_number` and re-derives its `documentation_posture`. The fourth hop, caused by the third, re-evaluates every compliance obligation of that model and then recomputes the model's `compliance_posture` from the new obligation statuses. The `version` increments on this revision, on the superseded revision, on the model unconditionally, and on an obligation only where the re-evaluation actually changed one of its fields — an obligation the fourth hop leaves on the status it already held keeps its `version`.

**Documentation posture** itself is derived, and it is worth stating precisely because three separate events recompute it: publishing a revision, publishing a catalog part, and registering a compatibility row. Take the model's revision whose `doc_type` is `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` — there is at most one. If there is none, the model's `documentation_posture` is `MISSING`, its `published_disassembly_revision_number` is set back to null, its `published_safety_sheet_revision_number` is still refreshed from whatever `SAFETY_SHEET` is published, and no coverage gap is computed at all. Otherwise, build the required-SKU set: the `sku` of every catalog part whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and for which a compatibility row exists on this model whose half-open window from `effective_from` up to but not including `effective_until` contains the logical clock. Parts in `RESTRICTED`, `DRAFT`, or `SUPERSEDED` status are excluded from that set. The gap is the required SKUs that do not appear in the published guide's `covered_part_skus`, and the size of that gap is written to the guide's `coverage_gap_sku_count`. When the gap is non-empty the posture is `STALE`. When the gap is empty but the model has a `last_hardware_change_date` that is later than the published guide's `effective_from`, the posture is also `STALE` — the hardware moved on after the guide was written. Otherwise the posture is `CURRENT`. The same pass refreshes `published_disassembly_revision_number` from that guide's `revision_number`, and `published_safety_sheet_revision_number` from the revision number of the model's published `SAFETY_SHEET`, using null where no such revision exists. Submitting a revision for review runs only the `coverage_gap_sku_count` part of this pass.

### 3.5. Self-Repair Eligibility, Warranty Impact and Risk

Before anyone can buy a part, they ask for a ruling. An **eligibility assessment** is opened by a `SelfRepairCustomer`, a `RepairProviderTech`, or a `ProgramAdmin`, supplying the `model_id` of the model being repaired, the `part_id` of the part the requester wants to install, the `region_code` of the jurisdiction, a `requester_class` of either `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` or `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER`, and — only when the requester class is `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` — a `provider_id`. An `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` request that omits `provider_id` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked before any identifier is resolved; a `provider_id` supplied alongside a `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` request is not rejected but is resolved and stored like any other. The caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `requester_user_id`. The new assessment begins with `status` at `VALID` and an `expires_at` set to its `created_at` plus sixty days. An assessment is always created successfully, with a 201 response: a blocked ruling is a successful assessment, not an error.

The **decision** is computed by taking the first matching branch of a pinned sequence. The compatibility row it reads is the row for this part and model whose half-open window from `effective_from` up to but not including `effective_until` contains the logical clock, treating a null `effective_until` as open-ended; where more than one such row exists, the row with the latest `effective_from` wins, and a tie on `effective_from` is broken in favour of the earlier `created_at`. Branch one: if there is no such compatibility row, the `decision` is `BLOCKED_NO_COMPATIBILITY`. Branch two: if the part's `status` is anything other than `PUBLISHED`, the decision is `BLOCKED_PART_UNAVAILABLE`. Branch three: if the model's `repairability_tier` is `TIER_D_NON_SERVICEABLE`, the decision is `BLOCKED_TIER`. Branch four is not a decision but a substitution — the *effective* safety class is `UNRESTRICTED` when the part's `safety_class` is `PAIRED_SECURITY` and the region's `allows_pairing_restriction` is false, and is otherwise the part's own `safety_class`; a jurisdiction that outlaws parts pairing sees a paired-security part as if it were unrestricted, and every later branch reads the effective value. Branch five: a `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` whose effective safety class is `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_HIGH_VOLTAGE` or `PAIRED_SECURITY` gets `BLOCKED_SAFETY`. Branch six: a `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` whose effective safety class is `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY` gets `BLOCKED_NO_MANUAL` when the model's `published_safety_sheet_revision_number` is null. Branch seven: any requester gets `BLOCKED_NO_MANUAL` when the model's `published_disassembly_revision_number` is null. Branch eight: a `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` gets `BLOCKED_TIER` when the compatibility row's `self_service_eligible` is false. Branch nine: the decision is `ELIGIBLE_WITH_TOOL_RENTAL` when the compatibility row names a `required_tool_part_id`, and also when the requester is a `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` whose effective safety class is `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY`. Branch ten, otherwise: the decision is `ELIGIBLE`.

Three side outputs are written on every assessment, whatever the decision. `pinned_manual_revision_id` captures the identifier of the model's published `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` revision as observed at assessment time, and is null when none is published. `liability_warning_version` is the warning-text version string the later acknowledgment must echo back verbatim; it is formed as the literal `LW-`, then the model's `model_code`, then the literal `-R`, then the model's `published_disassembly_revision_number`, and it is null whenever `pinned_manual_revision_id` is null. `tool_rental_part_id` is a copy of the in-window compatibility row's `required_tool_part_id`, and is null when no tool is required, when no compatibility row was found, and — because it is emitted only by the tool-rental branch of the decision sequence — on every blocked decision and on a plain `ELIGIBLE` decision, even where the compatibility row does name a tool.

The same request also classifies **warranty impact**, again by first matching branch, and this classification reads the part's stored `safety_class` rather than the pairing-override effective value. Branch one: an `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` request whose named provider sits in `AUTHORIZED` authorization status yields `WARRANTY_PRESERVED`. Branch two: a part whose `safety_class` is `UNRESTRICTED` on a model whose `repairability_tier` is `TIER_A_SELF_SERVICE` yields `WARRANTY_PRESERVED_WITH_LOG`. Branch three: a part whose `safety_class` is `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY` yields `WARRANTY_LIMITED_COMPONENT_EXCLUSION`. Branch four, otherwise: `WARRANTY_VOID_AFFECTED_SUBSYSTEM`. The resulting `warranty_impact_class` is the value the buyer's disclosure acknowledgment must echo.

Finally the assessment records a `self_repair_risk_index`, an integer from 0 to 100 that is written even when the decision is a blocked one. Three weights combine. The tier weight is 0.10 for `TIER_A_SELF_SERVICE`, 0.40 for `TIER_B_GUIDED`, 0.80 for `TIER_C_AUTHORIZED_ONLY`, and 1.00 for `TIER_D_NON_SERVICEABLE`. The safety weight is 0.10 for `UNRESTRICTED`, 0.60 for `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY`, 0.90 for `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_HIGH_VOLTAGE`, and 0.70 for `PAIRED_SECURITY`, and like the warranty classification it reads the part's stored `safety_class` rather than the pairing-override effective value, so a region that outlaws pairing lowers the decision but never the index. The install weight is the `install_minutes` of that same in-window compatibility row the decision selected, divided by 120 — two hours of bench time being the normalising figure — capped at 1, and taken as 0 when no compatibility row was found. The index is then 100 multiplied by the smaller of 1 and the sum of 0.40 times the tier weight plus 0.35 times the safety weight plus 0.25 times the install weight, rounded half up to a whole number.

An assessment's `status` runs `VALID`, and out of it to `REVOKED_BY_SAFETY` when a safety restriction or a provider suspension invalidates it, to `EXPIRED` when a sweep or an end-of-support action finds it past its sixty-day validity, or to `CONSUMED` the moment an order is created against it.

### 3.6. Assembling, Pricing and Confirming a Parts Order

A **parts order** is opened by a `SelfRepairCustomer` or a `RepairProviderTech` against a ruling. The creation payload carries the `assessment_id` of the eligibility ruling the order is placed against, the `region_code` whose profile supplies the pricing and documentation-fee parameters, a `buyer_class` of either `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` or `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER`, a `provider_id` which is required when the buyer class is `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` and null otherwise, and — when a rental kit is going to be ordered — a `rental_window_start` and `rental_window_end` bounding the requested kit occupation inclusively at both ends. An `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` order that omits `provider_id` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a `provider_id` supplied on a consumer order is resolved and stored rather than rejected. A `rental_window_end` earlier than the `rental_window_start` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, a comparison made only when both bounds are present. The referenced assessment must still be in `VALID` status: an assessment that is `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED_BY_SAFETY`, or already `CONSUMED` by another order is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VALID`, and an assessment whose `decision` is any of the blocked values is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_BLOCKED`. Creation copies `model_id`, `pinned_manual_revision_id`, and `warranty_impact_class` from the assessment and derives `pinned_manual_revision_number` by reading the `revision_number` of the pinned revision row, leaving it null when the assessment pinned none; the order's own `region_code` is validated against the profile register but is never required to equal the assessment's. Creation moves the assessment from `VALID` to `CONSUMED`, records the caller's `X-User-ID` as `customer_user_id`, and starts the order with `fulfillment_status` at `DRAFT`, `core_return_status` at `NOT_REQUIRED`, every money field at zero, `core_credit_final_cents`, `core_days_late`, `core_return_due_at`, `disclosure_acknowledged_at`, `confirmed_at`, `delivered_at`, `blocked_reason`, and `cancellation_reason` all null.

An order carries two independent state dimensions on the same row. The fulfilment dimension runs `DRAFT` to `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` to `CONFIRMED` to `DELIVERED` to the terminal `CLOSED`, with a side exit from `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` to `BLOCKED_SAFETY_HOLD` and a cancellation path from any non-terminal state to the terminal `CANCELLED`. The core dimension is `core_return_status` and holds `NOT_REQUIRED` until confirmation, from which point it runs `PENDING_CORE` to `CORE_RECEIVED` and then to one of `CORE_CREDIT_ISSUED`, `CORE_FORFEITED`, or — on cancellation — `CORE_RELEASED`. The two dimensions advance on their own triggers and never gate each other except at the closing rollup.

**Appending a line** to an order names a `part_id`, a `quantity` between 1 and 500, and the `expected_order_version` guard of §2.6. Beyond the stale-version check, an append faces a dense set of interlocks. The order itself must still be in `DRAFT`: an append to an order that has already reached `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` or any later or terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The referenced part must be in `PUBLISHED` status and must hold a compatibility row for this order's model whose half-open window contains the logical clock; a part in `DRAFT`, `RESTRICTED`, or `SUPERSEDED` status, or one with no in-window fitment, is refused with `ERR_PART_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The order's model must not sit at `ORDER_RESTRICTED` compliance posture, otherwise the append is refused with `ERR_MODEL_ORDER_RESTRICTED` — a refusal that guards both the line append and the disclosure request. When the buyer class is `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER`, the part's `safety_class` must not demand a stricter parts-access tier than the provider holds: a `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_HIGH_VOLTAGE` part requires the provider's `parts_access_tier` to be `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED`, while `SAFETY_RESTRICTED_BATTERY` and `PAIRED_SECURITY` parts require either `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED` or `TIER_2_SAFETY`; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_PARTS_ACCESS_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. A `quantity` below 1 or above 500 is an `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. A successful append captures the part's authentication and calibration requirements onto the line — `authentication_status` and `calibration_status` both start at `NOT_REQUIRED` and stay there for a part that needs neither step — and re-runs the whole order's pricing.

Rental kits bring two extra rules to the append. First, a `RENTAL_KIT` line may only be appended to an order that actually declares a rental window: a null `rental_window_start` or `rental_window_end`, or a `rental_window_start` that falls strictly before the logical clock's calendar date, is refused with `ERR_RENTAL_WINDOW_REQUIRED`. Second, the pool is finite. The requested occupation is the closed interval from `rental_window_start` to `rental_window_end`. Every line on the same part belonging to a *different* order whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`, `CONFIRMED`, or `DELIVERED` counts as an occupant; occupations on orders in `DRAFT`, `BLOCKED_SAFETY_HOLD`, `CLOSED`, or `CANCELLED` are released and never counted. Each occupant's interval runs from its order's `rental_window_start` to its order's `rental_window_end` extended by a two-day turnaround buffer for cleaning and inspection. Because both intervals are closed, touching endpoints do collide: a requested start equal to an occupied end is a conflict. The occupied unit count is the sum of the `quantity` of every colliding occupant, and when that sum plus the new line's `quantity` exceeds the part's `concurrent_rental_capacity` — a null capacity counting as zero — the append is refused with `ERR_RENTAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Pricing** is re-run in full over every line of the order — there is no status filter on lines, because a line has no lifecycle of its own before delivery — both on each append while the order is still in `DRAFT` and again when disclosure is requested. Region parameters come from the regional profile whose `region_code` equals the order's, and model parameters from the order's product model. Lines are processed in ascending `created_at` order and each line is priced as follows. The base is the part's `list_price_cents`, unrounded. The line's `buyer_multiplier` is 1.00 when the order's `buyer_class` is `SELF_REPAIR_CONSUMER` and 0.82 when it is `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER` — the eighteen percent trade discount that makes the provider channel worth joining. The line's `volume_multiplier` is 0.85 when `quantity` is at least 25, otherwise 0.92 when `quantity` is at least 10, otherwise 1.00; both thresholds are inclusive. The line's `tier_surcharge_multiplier` is 1.00 for a `TIER_A_SELF_SERVICE` model, 1.05 for `TIER_B_GUIDED`, and 1.12 for `TIER_C_AUTHORIZED_ONLY`; a `TIER_D_NON_SERVICEABLE` model never reaches pricing because eligibility blocks it upstream. The effective unit value is the base multiplied by the buyer multiplier, then by the volume multiplier, then by the tier surcharge multiplier, and it stays unrounded. `line_total_cents` is then the effective unit value multiplied by the line's `quantity`, rounded half up to whole cents — and that is the one and only rounding for the line. `unit_price_cents` is separately the effective unit value rounded half up, and it exists purely for display: it is never multiplied by quantity and never summed into any total. Rounding the unit price first and then multiplying, and summing unrounded line values and rounding the order total once, are both wrong here.

Five order-level figures follow, in this order. `parts_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `line_total_cents` across lines whose part's `part_class` is `COMPONENT` or `CONSUMABLE`. `tool_purchase_cents` is the sum of `line_total_cents` across lines whose part's `part_class` is `TOOL`. The order's `rental_deposit_cents` is the sum, across lines whose part's `part_class` is `RENTAL_KIT`, of that part's `rental_deposit_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`. `core_deposit_cents` is the sum, across lines whose part has `core_return_required` true, of that part's `core_credit_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`. Then `documentation_fee_cents`: when the region's `requires_public_manual` is true the fee is zero, because the statutory free-documentation override wins before anything else is considered; otherwise the fee is the smaller of the region's `max_documentation_fee_cents` and a computed figure of five dollars — 500 cents — plus one dollar twenty — 120 cents — for each liability warning counted in the model's published `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` revision's `safety_warning_count`. Where no disassembly guide is published yet, the warning count contributes nothing and the fee is simply the smaller of the cap and the 500-cent base. Finally `total_due_cents` is the sum of `parts_subtotal_cents`, `tool_purchase_cents`, `rental_deposit_cents`, `documentation_fee_cents`, and `core_deposit_cents`.

One consumer protection sits on top of the core deposit. A consumer order whose recomputed `core_deposit_cents` would exceed five hundred dollars — 50000 cents — is refused with `ERR_CORE_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and that ceiling is checked both when a line is appended and when disclosure is requested. Provider orders are not capped.

**Requesting disclosure** advances the order from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` and freezes its money. It requires the order to be in `DRAFT`, requires at least one line to exist — an empty order is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES` — and requires the referenced assessment to be the one this order consumed, never since moved to `REVOKED_BY_SAFETY` or `EXPIRED`, with a `decision` of either `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_TOOL_RENTAL`; a failure on any of those points is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VALID`. The model's `compliance_posture` must not be `ORDER_RESTRICTED`, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_ORDER_RESTRICTED`. The action re-runs the full pricing pass described above, writing every per-line and order-level money field, and copies `warranty_impact_class` across from the assessment.

**Acknowledging the disclosure** is what turns a quotation into a signed transaction. The buyer submits a `warranty_impact_class_ack` that must equal the order's computed `warranty_impact_class`, a `liability_warning_version` that must equal the value pinned on the assessment, and an `acknowledged_scope` of `FULL_SELF_REPAIR`, `TOOL_RENTAL_ONLY`, or `PROVIDER_MANAGED`. The order must be sitting in `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`; an acknowledgment filed against an order still in `DRAFT`, or already `CONFIRMED` or beyond, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A warranty class that does not match, or a `TOOL_RENTAL_ONLY` scope on an order that holds a line whose part is specifically of `part_class` `COMPONENT` — a `CONSUMABLE` line does not trip this particular check — is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_MISMATCH`. A `liability_warning_version` other than the pinned one is refused with `ERR_LIABILITY_WARNING_STALE` — the model's published documentation has moved on since the ruling was issued, and the buyer would be signing the wrong warning text. The stored record captures `acknowledged_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, which must equal the order's `customer_user_id`; `manual_revision_number`, copied from the order's pinned revision number at signature time; and `acknowledged_at`, the logical-clock instant of signature. The record is append-only and is never updated. The first acknowledgment for an order also stamps the order's `disclosure_acknowledged_at` with the logical clock, and that stamp is precisely what makes the order immune to the fourth hop of the safety-restriction cascade.

**Confirmation** advances `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` to `CONFIRMED` and carries the densest precondition set in the programme. The order must be in `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`. Its `disclosure_acknowledged_at` must not be null, and there must exist an acknowledgment whose `acknowledged_scope` actually covers the order's contents — `PROVIDER_MANAGED`, and nothing else, for a provider order; `FULL_SELF_REPAIR` alone for a consumer order holding any line whose part is of `part_class` `COMPONENT` *or* `CONSUMABLE`, the consumable counting here even though it does not count at acknowledgment time; and either `FULL_SELF_REPAIR` or `TOOL_RENTAL_ONLY` for a consumer order limited to rentals and tools; a failure here is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. A published `DISASSEMBLY_GUIDE` revision must exist for the order's model, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. No line may reference a part in `RESTRICTED` status, whatever the buyer class, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_CONTAINS_RESTRICTED_PART`. Every line's part must still hold an in-window compatibility row for the model at the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The pinned assessment must not have expired: a confirmation attempted when the assessment's `expires_at` falls strictly before the logical clock is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_EXPIRED`. Where a compliance obligation exists for the order's model and region, the logical clock must not have passed that obligation's `parts_available_until`; because that deadline is an inclusive end-of-day instant, a clock reading exactly equal to it still passes and one millisecond later is refused with `ERR_PARTS_WINDOW_ELAPSED`. The model's `compliance_posture` must not be `ORDER_RESTRICTED`. And when the `buyer_class` is `AUTHORIZED_PROVIDER`, the named provider's `authorization_status` must be exactly `AUTHORIZED` — `APPLIED`, `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED`, and `REVOKED` are all refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

Confirmation stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock. It deliberately leaves `pinned_manual_revision_id` and `pinned_manual_revision_number` exactly as the assessment pinned them: a publication that happened between the ruling and the confirmation never moves those fields, and the acknowledgment check on the liability warning version is evaluated against the pinned values rather than against the model's currently published revision. The assessment is left `CONSUMED`, as it already was. On the core dimension, when any line's part has `core_return_required` true, `core_return_status` moves from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING_CORE`, `core_return_due_at` is set to `confirmed_at` plus thirty days, and `core_credit_provisional_cents` is stamped with the full core face value of the order — the sum, across core-required lines, of the part's `core_credit_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity` — ungraded, with no condition grading and no lateness decay. For each such part, both `core_face_value_committed_cents` and `core_liability_outstanding_cents` rise by that part's face value on this order.

**Cancellation** is available to a `SelfRepairCustomer`, a `RepairProviderTech`, or a `ProgramAdmin` and takes an optional `cancellation_reason`, stored as supplied and left null when omitted. It is permitted out of `DRAFT`, `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`, `BLOCKED_SAFETY_HOLD`, and `CONFIRMED`, and moves the order to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Out of `CONFIRMED` it additionally requires the core dimension to be in either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `PENDING_CORE`; a cancellation attempted once the core has been received or settled is refused with `ERR_CORE_LEDGER_INVARIANT`. Cancellation stores the reason and compensates fully: where `core_return_status` was `PENDING_CORE` it moves to `CORE_RELEASED`, `core_credit_provisional_cents` is reset to zero and `core_return_due_at` to null, and for every core-required line both that part's `core_liability_outstanding_cents` and its `core_face_value_committed_cents` decrease by the same face value, which is what keeps the conservation identity of §2.10 balanced. The pinned assessment stays `CONSUMED` — a cancellation never resurrects eligibility.

### 3.7. Delivery, Authentication, Calibration and the Core Return

Delivery is a `ProgramAdmin` action that advances the order from `CONFIRMED` to `DELIVERED`; an order in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It accepts an optional `delivered_at` and otherwise stamps the logical clock; a supplied instant is stored exactly as given and is never compared against the clock or against `confirmed_at`. Delivery is also what activates the two per-line service dimensions. Every line whose part has `requires_authentication` true moves its `authentication_status` from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING_AUTHENTICATION` and has a `pairing_token` minted onto it — the token is readable through the order's line listing. Every line whose part has `requires_calibration` true moves its `calibration_status` from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED`. Where no line needs either step and the core dimension is `NOT_REQUIRED`, the order continues straight through to `CLOSED`.

A line carries two independent dimensions of its own. The authentication dimension runs from `PENDING_AUTHENTICATION` to either the terminal `AUTHENTIC` or the terminal `COUNTERFEIT_SUSPECT`. The calibration dimension runs from `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED` through `CALIBRATION_IN_PROGRESS` to `CALIBRATION_PASSED`, `CALIBRATION_FAILED`, or `CALIBRATION_ESCALATED`, and it is genuinely cyclic: a failed attempt returns to `CALIBRATION_IN_PROGRESS` on retry until the attempt cap bites.

**Authenticating a part** is open to a `ProgramAdmin`, a `RepairProviderTech`, or a `SelfRepairCustomer` and takes a `pairing_token` in the body. It requires the line's `authentication_status` to be `PENDING_AUTHENTICATION` and the parent order's `fulfillment_status` to be `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When the submitted token equals the token minted on the line at delivery, `authentication_status` becomes `AUTHENTIC`; when the part also requires calibration, the calibration dimension stays at `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED` and a successful authentication is simply what unblocks it. Any other token value moves the line to `COUNTERFEIT_SUSPECT`, which is terminal for the authentication dimension and, when calibration was required, forces the calibration dimension straight to `CALIBRATION_ESCALATED`. A `COUNTERFEIT_SUSPECT` line also permanently zeroes the final credit of any defective-return case later opened against it, whatever the root cause. Attempting to authenticate a line whose part has `requires_authentication` false is refused with `ERR_AUTHENTICATION_NOT_APPLICABLE`. Either outcome may enable the parent order's rollup to `CLOSED`.

**Calibrating a part** is open to a `SelfRepairCustomer` or a `RepairProviderTech` — and to a `ProgramAdmin` — and takes a `measured_offset_ppm` reading, which must lie between negative one hundred thousand and positive one hundred thousand or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. Calibration requires the parent order to be `DELIVERED`. It requires the line's `authentication_status` to be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `AUTHENTIC`: a line whose part requires authentication but whose authentication is not yet `AUTHENTIC` is refused with `ERR_AUTHENTICATION_REQUIRED_FIRST`. It also requires the model to have a published `CALIBRATION_PROCEDURE` revision, otherwise `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_CALIBRATION_PROCEDURE`. A first attempt moves `calibration_status` from `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED` to `CALIBRATION_IN_PROGRESS`; a retry moves it from `CALIBRATION_FAILED` back to `CALIBRATION_IN_PROGRESS`; those two are the only admissible source values, so a call against a line whose dimension is `NOT_REQUIRED` — the part never needed calibrating — or already `CALIBRATION_PASSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Either way `calibration_attempt_count` increments by one and `last_measured_offset_ppm` is stored, and the tolerance branch is then evaluated inside the same request. When the absolute value of the measured offset is at most 150 parts per million — 150 exactly passes — the dimension continues to `CALIBRATION_PASSED`. When it exceeds 150 and `calibration_attempt_count` is still below three, the dimension continues to `CALIBRATION_FAILED` and the line remains retryable. When it exceeds 150 on the attempt that brings `calibration_attempt_count` to three, the dimension continues to the terminal `CALIBRATION_ESCALATED`, and the parent order can never reach `CLOSED` through that line. Any further calibrate call — a fourth attempt, or any attempt on an already escalated line — is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`.

An order rolls up from `DELIVERED` to the terminal `CLOSED` on its own, with no client request, as soon as two conditions hold together: every line has `authentication_status` in `NOT_REQUIRED` or `AUTHENTIC` and `calibration_status` in `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CALIBRATION_PASSED`, and the core dimension sits in `NOT_REQUIRED`, `CORE_CREDIT_ISSUED`, or `CORE_FORFEITED`. A closed order is frozen, though defective returns remain openable against its lines.

**Booking in a core return** is a `ProgramAdmin` action taking a `core_condition` and a mandatory `received_at`. It requires `core_return_status` to be `PENDING_CORE` and the order's `fulfillment_status` to already be `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; a core dimension of `NOT_REQUIRED` is refused that way too, and the `DELIVERED` requirement is asserted only for an order actually in `PENDING_CORE`. The `received_at` must fall on or after the order's `confirmed_at` and must not fall after the logical clock; a receipt dated before confirmation or in the future is refused with `ERR_CORE_RECEIPT_BEFORE_CONFIRM`, and that temporal check sits in band 9 ahead of the conservation refusal below, so a re-booking that also carries a bad date reports the date. The settlement then grades the same core face value that was provisionally held at confirmation. First, `core_days_late` is the number of whole days from the order's `core_return_due_at` to the recorded `received_at`, floored at zero. A condition multiplier is read from the submitted `core_condition`: 1.00 for `INTACT`, 0.70 for `DAMAGED_SHELL`, 0.40 for `INCOMPLETE`, and 0.00 for `NON_RETURNABLE`. A lateness decay is one minus five hundredths — that is, 0.05 — for each whole day late, floored at zero — so twenty days late erases the credit entirely on its own. When `core_days_late` has reached twenty, or the condition is `NON_RETURNABLE`, `core_credit_final_cents` is zero and the core dimension continues from `CORE_RECEIVED` to `CORE_FORFEITED`. Otherwise `core_credit_final_cents` is the face value multiplied by the condition multiplier and then by the lateness decay, rounded half up to whole cents, and the dimension continues from `CORE_RECEIVED` to `CORE_CREDIT_ISSUED` — unless that graded figure itself comes out at zero cents, in which case the outcome is `CORE_FORFEITED`, the terminal state being chosen purely on whether the final credit is zero. `core_days_late` is recorded on both outcomes. Because the provisional figure was the ungraded face value, identical inputs always leave the provisional figure at or above the final one, strictly above it whenever the core arrives late or imperfect.

The ledger movements are exact. On a forfeiture, each core-required part's `core_liability_outstanding_cents` decreases by its face value on this order while its `core_credit_forfeited_cents` increases by the same amount. On a credit issue, each part's `core_liability_outstanding_cents` decreases by its face value while `core_credit_settled_cents` increases by the credited share and `core_credit_forfeited_cents` increases by the unpaid remainder. Where the order commits core face value on more than one part, the single graded credit figure is divided among them pro rata by face value using largest-remainder apportionment: each part takes the floor of its exact share, and the leftover cents are handed out one at a time in descending order of the discarded fractional remainder, ties broken by ascending part identifier — which keeps the sum of the shares exactly equal to `core_credit_final_cents` and every part's ledger identity intact. Either outcome is terminal for the core dimension and enables the rollup to `CLOSED`. A second core-return booking against an order whose core dimension already sits in `CORE_RECEIVED`, `CORE_CREDIT_ISSUED`, `CORE_FORFEITED`, or `CORE_RELEASED` is a declared but guarded call: the face value has already left the outstanding total, so re-settling would drive it below zero, and the call is refused with `ERR_CORE_LEDGER_INVARIANT` rather than reported as an undefined trigger.

### 3.8. The Independent Repair Provider Programme

An independent shop enters the programme as a **repair provider**, registered by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `legal_name` giving the registered name of the business, a `region_code`, a `requested_access_tier` of `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED`, `TIER_2_SAFETY`, or `TIER_3_STANDARD` naming the parts access tier the applicant asked for, and a `technician_count` between 1 and 500, a head count outside that band being refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE` before the region profile is resolved. Registration derives `required_cert_count` as the technician head count divided by four and rounded up — one certified technician per four on staff — and starts `active_cert_count` at zero, `authorization_status` at `APPLIED`, `parts_access_tier` at null, `attested_compliance_score` at null, `suspension_reason` at null, and all three training-credit aggregates (`training_credit_granted_total`, `training_credit_balance`, and `training_credit_forfeited_total`) at zero.

The authorization lifecycle runs `APPLIED` to `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS` to `AUTHORIZED`, with a cycle through `SUSPENDED` and back to `AUTHORIZED` that may repeat any number of times, and a terminal `REVOKED` reachable from any of the four earlier states. Beginning training is a `ProgramAdmin` action out of `APPLIED` that takes a `training_credit_grant` of at least two; both `training_credit_granted_total` and `training_credit_balance` rise by the granted amount, which preserves the conservation identity by construction. Any `training_credit_grant` below two — negative, zero, or one — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, evaluated before the provider is resolved, and there is no upper bound on the grant. A provider in any state other than `APPLIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

**Training certifications** are filed by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `RepairProviderTech` against a provider. Each carries the `provider_id` of the employing provider, a `technician_user_id` — an opaque technician identifier — a `course_code` of `CRS_BATTERY_SAFETY`, `CRS_HIGH_VOLTAGE`, `CRS_PAIRING_CALIBRATION`, or `CRS_GENERAL_REPAIR`, a `valid_from` and `valid_until` bounding the validity interval inclusively at both ends, and an `exam_score` between 0 and 100. A `valid_until` earlier than `valid_from` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`; an `exam_score` outside the 0-to-100 range is an `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. The provider must be accepting certifications, meaning its `authorization_status` is `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS` or `AUTHORIZED`; a filing against an `APPLIED`, `SUSPENDED`, or `REVOKED` provider is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACCEPTING_CERTS`. The certification must be usable: a `valid_until` that is not strictly after the logical clock, or an `exam_score` below the passing mark of 70, is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_ALREADY_EXPIRED`. Validity windows are unique per technician and course: a new closed window that overlaps an `ACTIVE` certification for the same `technician_user_id` and `course_code` is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, and because both bounds are inclusive, windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide. Finally the provider must be able to pay: a filing when `training_credit_balance` is below two is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_CREDIT_INSUFFICIENT`. A filed certification begins with `status` at `ACTIVE`, `credits_consumed` at two, and `void_reason` null; the provider's `training_credit_balance` drops by two and its `active_cert_count` rises by one, while `required_cert_count` is left untouched.

Voiding a certification is a `ProgramAdmin` action that takes an optional `void_reason` — stored as supplied and left null when omitted — and moves `ACTIVE` to the terminal `VOIDED`, any other source status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` before the revoked-provider guard below is even reached. It compensates: `credits_consumed` returns to zero, the provider's `training_credit_balance` rises by two, and `active_cert_count` drops by one. A void that would restore credit to a provider whose `authorization_status` is `REVOKED` — whose balance was already swept into `training_credit_forfeited_total` — is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_RESTORE_FORBIDDEN`. When a void pushes `active_cert_count` below `required_cert_count` for a provider that was `AUTHORIZED`, the provider is additionally suspended in the same request, moving `AUTHORIZED` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` recorded as `CERT_COVERAGE_LOST`, which in turn revokes that provider's still-`VALID` eligibility assessments. A certification's `status` may also read `EXPIRED`, whose meaning is that its window has passed; an `EXPIRED` row still holds its two consumed credits, and only a `VOIDED` row releases them. No action in the programme writes that value, though: nothing ages an `ACTIVE` certification, so a row stays `ACTIVE` after its `valid_until` has gone by, and `active_cert_count` — which is derived on every read from the count of the provider's `ACTIVE` rows rather than stored — therefore counts a lapsed certification too. It is only the rolling-attested compliance score below that applies the validity window.

**Authorization decisions** are taken by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` — either role may render any of the four decisions, the endpoint applying no further per-decision narrowing — supplying a `decision` of `AUTHORIZE`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE`, or `REVOKE`, an optional `decision_reason`, and optionally a `granted_access_tier`. The source state is checked first of all the decision's own checks, so a decision attempted from a state it does not serve is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` before any coverage or ledger consideration. Authorizing moves `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS` to `AUTHORIZED` and reinstating moves `SUSPENDED` back to `AUTHORIZED`; both require `active_cert_count` to be at least `required_cert_count`, otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Both stamp `attested_compliance_score` from the rolling attested variant of the compliance score described below, and set `parts_access_tier` to the *stricter* of the requested tier — the body's `granted_access_tier` where supplied, otherwise the provider's `requested_access_tier` — and the tier band implied by that score, where `TIER_3_STANDARD` is stricter than `TIER_2_SAFETY`, which is stricter than `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED`. A reinstatement additionally clears `suspension_reason`. Suspending is a decision out of `AUTHORIZED` that stores the body's `decision_reason` as the provider's `suspension_reason` — leaving it null where no reason was supplied — stops that provider's orders from being confirmed, and revokes every still-`VALID` assessment of the provider. Revoking is available from any of `APPLIED`, `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS`, `AUTHORIZED`, and `SUSPENDED`, that is from any state other than `REVOKED`; it leaves `suspension_reason` untouched, it is terminal, it sets `parts_access_tier` to null, and it sweeps the whole of `training_credit_balance` into `training_credit_forfeited_total`, leaving the balance at zero.

The **provider compliance score** is a computed read, never a stored figure except for the snapshot stamped at an authorization decision. It reports the `variant` used, a `cert_coverage_ratio`, a `breach_count`, a `calibration_failure_ratio`, the integer `score`, the resulting `access_tier_band`, and the `provider_id` it was computed for. The required certification count is the `technician_count` divided by four, rounded up. The `cert_coverage_ratio` is the smaller of 1 and the counted certification count divided by the required certification count. The `breach_count` is the number of defective-return cases whose `root_cause_code` is `PROVIDER_INSTALL_ERROR` and whose source order carries this provider's `provider_id`, restricted to the case statuses the variant counts. The attempted-line population is every order line on this provider's orders whose `calibration_attempt_count` is at least 1, and the failed-line population is those among them whose `calibration_status` is `CALIBRATION_FAILED` or `CALIBRATION_ESCALATED`; the `calibration_failure_ratio` is failed lines divided by attempted lines, and is zero when no line was ever attempted. The `score` is then 100 multiplied by the sum of three weighted terms — 0.45 times the certification coverage ratio, plus 0.35 times one minus the smaller of 1 and the breach count divided by a breach cap of five, plus 0.20 times one minus the calibration failure ratio — rounded half up to a whole number. Ratios stay exact until that final rounding. The `access_tier_band` is `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED` when the score is at least 85, otherwise `TIER_2_SAFETY` when the score is at least 65, otherwise `TIER_3_STANDARD`; the comparisons are inclusive, so a score of exactly 85 lands in `TIER_1_UNRESTRICTED` and exactly 65 lands in `TIER_2_SAFETY`.

Two variants of that score exist and are selected by the `variant` query parameter; an absent or empty parameter takes the default and any other value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `ROLLING_ATTESTED` is the default and is the figure the programme contractually stands behind: it counts only certifications whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose inclusive validity interval from `valid_from` to `valid_until` contains the logical clock, and it counts only closed defective-return cases, those in `CREDIT_ISSUED` or `REJECTED`. `LIVE_RECOMPUTE` is the forward-looking figure, requested explicitly: it counts every certification whose `status` is `ACTIVE` regardless of whether the logical clock sits inside its validity window, so future-dated certifications count, and it counts cases in `REQUESTED`, `RECEIVED`, `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE`, `CREDIT_ISSUED`, and `REJECTED`, so an install-error case still under analysis lowers this score while the attested variant ignores it. Nothing is persisted by the read, which is exactly what allows the two variants to be compared against the same data.

### 3.9. Defective Returns, Failure Analysis and Long-Tail Forecasting

A buyer who receives a bad part opens a **defective return case**. A `SelfRepairCustomer` or a `RepairProviderTech` files it with the `order_line_id` of the delivered line the unit came from, a `reported_failure_code` of `NO_POWER`, `INTERMITTENT`, `PHYSICAL_DAMAGE`, `CALIBRATION_DRIFT`, or `AUTHENTICATION_FAILURE` describing the symptom, and a `quantity` of at least one — a `quantity` below one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, and there is no upper bound beyond the line-quantity conservation check below — that may not exceed the line's own quantity. The filing asserts nothing about the parent order's `fulfillment_status`: the line is expected to have been delivered, but a case may in fact be filed against a line on an order in any state, including one still in `DRAFT` or already `CANCELLED`. The requested return quantity plus the quantities of all previously filed cases against that line that are not in `REJECTED` status may not exceed the line's `quantity`; a filing that would exceed it is refused with `ERR_RETURN_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_LINE`. On creation the case's `part_id` is denormalised from the line, `status` starts at `REQUESTED`, and `root_cause_code`, `defect_confirmed`, `analyzed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `received_at` start null while `provisional_credit_cents` starts at zero and `final_credit_cents` starts null.

The case workflow runs `REQUESTED` to `RECEIVED` to `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE`, and then forks to either the terminal `CREDIT_ISSUED` or the terminal `REJECTED` with deliberately divergent effects. Booking in the returned unit is a `ProgramAdmin` action out of `REQUESTED`; it accepts an optional `received_at` — stored exactly as given, never compared against the logical clock or against the order — and otherwise stamps the logical clock, and it stamps `provisional_credit_cents` from the goodwill variant of the credit calculation. Recording the analysis is the exclusive province of a `FailureAnalysisEngineer`, moving `RECEIVED` to `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE` with a mandatory `root_cause_code` of `MANUFACTURING_DEFECT`, `PROVIDER_INSTALL_ERROR`, `CUSTOMER_MISUSE`, `PART_AUTHENTICATION_FAILURE`, or `UNDETERMINED`, a mandatory `defect_confirmed` boolean saying whether bench analysis reproduced the reported failure, and an optional `analysis_note` that is accepted but not persisted; the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `analyzed_by_user_id`. Where the root cause is `PROVIDER_INSTALL_ERROR` and the source order carries a `provider_id`, that provider's live-recompute compliance score drops immediately through its breach-count input, while the attested variant only registers the case once it closes.

The **credit decision** is taken by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `FailureAnalysisEngineer` with a `decision` of either `ISSUE_CREDIT` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_note` that is accepted but not persisted, and the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `decided_by_user_id`. Both branches require the case to be in `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE` and require the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `analyzed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN`. Issuing credit carries two further guards. A case whose `root_cause_code` is `CUSTOMER_MISUSE` may never be credited and is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_ROOT_CAUSE`. And the source line must have finished its mandated post-replacement service steps: a line whose `calibration_status` is any of `CALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, `CALIBRATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `CALIBRATION_FAILED`, or `CALIBRATION_ESCALATED`, or whose `authentication_status` is `PENDING_AUTHENTICATION`, is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_INCOMPLETE`. A `COUNTERFEIT_SUSPECT` line is deliberately absent from that list: it is decided rather than incomplete, so it clears the guard and the final calculation zeroes its credit instead. Issuing credit stamps `final_credit_cents` from the analysis-final variant of the calculation. Rejecting sets `final_credit_cents` to zero without evaluating any root-cause factor at all, and leaves `provisional_credit_cents` untouched as the audit record of the goodwill offer that was made.

The **credit calculation** reads exactly one order line — the one named by `order_line_id` — and its already-computed `line_total_cents`; the parent order's status is irrelevant here. The pro-rata basis is the line's `line_total_cents` multiplied by the case's `quantity` and divided by the line's `quantity`, rounded half up to whole cents. That is the first of two sequential, pinned roundings. The goodwill provisional figure is then the pro-rata basis multiplied by one half, rounded half up. The analysis-final figure is zero outright when the source line's `authentication_status` is `COUNTERFEIT_SUSPECT`. Otherwise a root-cause factor is read from the recorded `root_cause_code`: 1.00 for `MANUFACTURING_DEFECT`, 1.00 for `PART_AUTHENTICATION_FAILURE`, 0.50 for `UNDETERMINED`, 0.00 for `PROVIDER_INSTALL_ERROR`, and 0.00 for `CUSTOMER_MISUSE`. When `defect_confirmed` is false, that factor is reduced to the smaller of itself and one quarter. The final figure is the pro-rata basis multiplied by the resulting factor, rounded half up.

The last capability in the programme looks forward rather than back. A `ProgramAdmin` or `ComplianceOfficer` may ask for a **long-tail demand forecast** for one part on one model, supplying, as query parameters on that read, the `model_id`, a `horizon_months` between 1 and 60, and an optional `variant`; a horizon outside that range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, while a missing `model_id`, a missing or non-integer `horizon_months`, and a `variant` that is neither `TRAILING_ONLY` nor `PIPELINE_WEIGHTED` are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an omitted `variant` defaults to `TRAILING_ONLY`. Nothing is persisted. The response reports the `variant` used, `trailing_units`, `pipeline_units`, `base_monthly_units`, `obsolescence_band`, `band_multiplier`, `projected_units`, `safety_stock_pct`, `mandated_buffer_units`, and `recommended_stock_units`, and echoes back the `part_id`, `model_id`, and `horizon_months` the computation was run for. The evaluation order below is pinned and runs exactly once — the safety-stock tier looks circular, because stock depends on projected units, but the tier is never re-applied to the padded figure and no fixed-point iteration is performed.

First, `trailing_units` is the sum of `quantity` over every line for this part whose order is on the given model, whose order's `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED` or `CLOSED`, and whose order's `confirmed_at` falls within the half-open interval that starts 180 days before the logical clock and ends at, but excludes, the logical clock itself. Second, `pipeline_units` is the sum of `quantity` over the same join where the order's `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` or `CONFIRMED`, with no date filter at all. Orders in `DRAFT`, `BLOCKED_SAFETY_HOLD`, or `CANCELLED` contribute to neither figure. Third, `base_monthly_units` is `trailing_units` divided by six, the trailing window being six months long, and is left unrounded. Fourth, the age of the *model* — never of the units — sets the obsolescence band: count the whole months between the model's `end_of_production_date` and the logical clock, taking zero when that date is null; the `obsolescence_band` is `CURRENT` when that count is below 12, `LEGACY` when it is at least 12 and at most 60, and `LONG_TAIL` when it exceeds 60; the corresponding `band_multiplier` is 1.00, 1.35, and 1.80. Fifth, the monthly demand figure depends on the variant: under `TRAILING_ONLY` it is simply `base_monthly_units`, so open orders never lift the forecast, while under `PIPELINE_WEIGHTED` it is six tenths of `base_monthly_units` plus four tenths of `pipeline_units` divided by `horizon_months`. Sixth, `projected_units` is that monthly demand figure multiplied by `horizon_months` and then by the `band_multiplier`, unrounded. Seventh, `safety_stock_pct` is read from that step-six value: 0.30 when `projected_units` is below 50, otherwise 0.20 when it is below 500, otherwise 0.12. Eighth, `mandated_buffer_units` is the monthly demand figure multiplied by the number of mandated support months remaining and then by a mandate buffer ratio of one quarter, rounded up to a whole unit; the mandated months remaining are the whole months from the logical clock to the latest `parts_available_until` across this model's compliance obligations whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, taken as zero when there is no such obligation or when that deadline already lies in the past. Ninth, `recommended_stock_units` is `projected_units` multiplied by one plus `safety_stock_pct`, rounded up to a whole unit, plus `mandated_buffer_units`. Every intermediate is an exact rational; only steps eight and nine round, and both round up. `projected_units` is reported rounded half up to two decimal places, but the unrounded value is what feeds steps seven and nine — which is why the two variants can produce not only a different projected figure but a different safety-stock tier and a different recommendation from the same data set.
