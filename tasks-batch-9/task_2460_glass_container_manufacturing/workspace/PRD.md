# Glass Container Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A glass container plant is a continuous process wrapped in a discrete order book. The furnace never stops: it melts sand, soda ash and cullet twenty-four hours a day for years at a time, and every ton it melts is either packed into saleable containers, lost to a colour transition, rejected on the inspection line, or simply unaccounted for. Around that continuous core sit discrete commitments — a customer's bottle design that must be approved and tooled, a mold set that must be mounted on a forming machine, a pack lot that must age in the warehouse before it can be palletized, a filling line that will call the plant the moment bottles start breaking on its conveyor. The plant's scheduling problem is therefore an allocation problem against two finite and non-renewable pools: the melt tons a campaign can pull inside its window, and the refractory life the furnace lining has left before a cold repair is unavoidable.

This document specifies the Glass Container Manufacturing API, the system of record for that allocation. It governs furnace campaigns from planning through cold-repair scheduling, batch-house recipes and colour-change planning, forming-machine job windows and mold changeover downtime, inspection-line rejection by defect class, pack-to-melt yield reconciliation, warehouse aging and palletized shipment, customer-owned tooling and new-design approval with promised lead times, lightweighting programs that trade material savings against breakage exposure, and complaint investigation with full lot traceability back to the job that formed the containers.

The API is mounted under `/glass-plant`, with a single administrative exception documented in §2.4. Every rule in this document is normative: thresholds are exact, boundary inclusivity is deliberate, and the order in which checks fire is part of the contract because a plant operator debugging a rejected schedule needs the *first* reason it failed, not an arbitrary one.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Plant Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting person, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. The system recognises exactly seven roles: `PlantManager`, `PlantScheduler`, `BatchHouseTech`, `MoldEngineer`, `QualityEngineer`, `WarehouseOperator`, and `CustomerRep`. If `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of those seven exact strings, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the role is not among those authorised for the operation, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Six of the seven roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantManager`, `PlantScheduler`, `BatchHouseTech`, `MoldEngineer`, `QualityEngineer`, or `WarehouseOperator`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any resource's `id`. It is, however, recorded wherever a rule demands accountability or a two-person control: a quality engineer's identifier is stored as `risk_signed_by_user_id` on a bottle design at risk sign-off, as `approved_by_user_id` at design approval, as `recorded_by_user_id` on each inspection reading, and as `investigated_by_user_id` on a complaint under investigation.

`CustomerRep` is different. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; it is the only scope source available to that role. Ownership checks compare the resource's customer reference to `X-User-ID`: for a lightweighting decision, the baseline design's `customer_id`; for a complaint settlement, the complaint's `customer_id`; and when filing a complaint, the `customer_id` supplied in the request body. A `CustomerRep` acting on a program or complaint belonging to any other customer is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The same scoping governs the reads a `CustomerRep` may perform — customers, designs, programs, pack lots, inspection readings and complaints, all scoped to the represented customer.

Authority is segregated along plant function. A `PlantManager` alone registers furnaces, plans and drives campaigns, registers forming machines, registers customers, changes a customer's pallet standard, and adopts a lightweighting program into standard production. A `PlantScheduler` alone schedules, starts, closes and cancels forming jobs. A `BatchHouseTech` alone registers and approves batch recipes and plans, completes or aborts colour changes. A `MoldEngineer` alone drafts and submits bottle designs and owns mold-set inventory movements. A `QualityEngineer` alone requests design changes, signs off breakage risk, approves designs, opens and assesses lightweighting programs, suspends them, records inspection readings, disposes and rework-releases lots, and investigates complaints. A `WarehouseOperator` alone palletizes and ships lots. A `CustomerRep` alone records the customer's pilot-or-reject decision, files complaints, and settles them. Read access is broader and is enumerated per endpoint in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filters

Every collection read accepts two optional pagination parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the start of the ordered collection. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending in every list endpoint, so a stable page boundary is guaranteed for a collection that is not being written to. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). A `limit` or `offset` outside its permitted range is a field-level violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

List endpoints additionally accept filters drawn from the entity's own attributes, and filters combine conjunctively. Furnaces may be filtered by `posture`; campaigns on a furnace by `status`; recipes on a furnace by `color` and by `approved`; colour changes on a furnace by `status`; forming machines on a furnace by `status`; bottle designs by `customer_id`, by `supersedes_design_id` and by `status`; mold sets by `bottle_design_id`, `forming_machine_id`, `status` and `owner`; lightweighting programs by `baseline_design_id` and `status`; production jobs by `campaign_id`, `furnace_id`, `forming_machine_id`, `batch_recipe_id`, `bottle_design_id`, `mold_set_id` and `status`; pack lots by `production_job_id`, `campaign_id`, `bottle_design_id`, `customer_id`, `quality_status` and `logistics_status`; inspection readings on a lot by `defect_class`; and complaints by `customer_id`, `pack_lot_id`, `status` and `attribution`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and the Logical Plant Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Most resources also carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation the server applies — including mutations applied as a cascade from another entity's operation, not only those requested directly. The single exception is the inspection reading: an `InspectionResult` carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because a reading taken on the inspection line is an immutable observation that is never amended.

Time is not read from the host. The server maintains a **logical clock** which every window rule, aging rule and elapsed-time rule consults. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `PlantManager` may advance or rewind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to an administrative capability mounted outside the `/glass-plant` tree; that capability returns HTTP 200 and no resource payload. Every subsequent comparison — has a colour-change window elapsed, has a lot finished aging, which scheduled jobs are still in the future at wind-down, what instant does a shipment record — uses the logical clock's value at the moment the request is processed.

### 2.5. Melt Ledger Arithmetic: Precision and Rounding

All monetary and tonnage quantities are carried to 2 decimal places and rounded **half up** — a value exactly on the half-cent or half-hundredth boundary rounds away from zero. The same rule governs percentages: refractory remaining, both pack-to-melt yields, and the customer's weighted breakage figure are all 2-decimal values rounded half up. Three quantities depart from the two-decimal default and the departure is deliberate: parts-per-million figures on a pack lot and on a complaint are rounded half up to 1 decimal place; a recipe's cullet stress factor is rounded half up to 4 decimal places; a lightweighting reserve rate is rounded half up to 5 decimal places; and a complaint's severity share is rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Changeover downtime and design lead time are whole integers — changeover minutes are produced by rounding half up to zero decimals, lead time is computed entirely in whole days. Pallet counts and affected-pallet counts are always rounded **up** to the next whole pallet, never half up, because a partial pallet still occupies a pallet.

Where a formula composes several steps, rounding happens only where this document says it happens. In particular, the refractory stress multiplier is never rounded before it is applied, the breakage risk intermediates are never rounded before they are summed, and the complaint credit rounds the per-pallet amount to cents *before* multiplying by the pallet count. Summing unrounded per-pallet amounts and rounding once at the end produces a different figure and is incorrect.

### 2.6. The Refractory Budget and Furnace Posture

A furnace is registered with a nameplate `design_pull_tpd` (tons per day, greater than 0 and no more than 600, 2 decimals) and a `refractory_life_total_units` budget (greater than 0, 2 decimals) representing the wear units its lining can absorb before the current campaign must end. Wear accumulates in `refractory_consumed_units`, which starts at 0 and only ever grows while a campaign is live, and the derived headroom is published as `refractory_remaining_pct`: one hundred times the quantity one minus consumed units divided by total life units, rounded half up to 2 decimals and then clamped into the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00. A freshly registered furnace publishes `refractory_remaining_pct` of 100.00.

From that percentage plus the state of the furnace's campaigns the server derives `posture`, evaluated as an ordered cascade with the first matching rule winning. If any campaign on the furnace is in `REBUILD_SCHEDULED`, posture is `COLD`. Otherwise, if `refractory_remaining_pct` is 5.00 or lower, posture is `REBUILD_DUE`. Otherwise, if `refractory_remaining_pct` is 15.00 or lower, posture is `RESTRICTED`. In every other case posture is `OPERATING`. Both comparisons are inclusive on a two-decimal percentage. Posture is re-derived at exactly four moments: campaign activation, rebuild scheduling, campaign closure, and production-job closure.

Posture gates work. A campaign may not be activated on a furnace whose posture is `COLD`, and a job may not be started unless posture is `OPERATING` or `RESTRICTED` — a `REBUILD_DUE` or `COLD` furnace refuses new production. Both refusals carry `ERR_FURNACE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. `RESTRICTED` posture additionally forbids any over-pull at all, as §3.5 describes.

### 2.7. Melt Capacity Reservation and Stale-Version Protection

Melt capacity is a finite pool held on the furnace and consumed by scheduled work. `reserved_pull_tons` on the furnace is the sum of `reserved_melt_tons` across every production job on that furnace currently in `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`. Once a job closes — whether `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` — its tons leave the reservation and move permanently into the campaign's `melt_tons_charged`, so they are counted in exactly one place at a time and never twice. A job that is `CANCELLED` releases its reservation and contributes to neither figure. The furnace also publishes `campaign_count`, the number of campaigns ever created against it, starting at 0.

Because the reservation pool is shared, both writes that consume a shared parent are protected by optimistic concurrency, and in both cases the version field is a required, non-optional part of the request body.

Scheduling a production job requires `expected_furnace_version` carrying the `version` of the target furnace as the client last observed it. Scheduling a job reserves melt tons out of the furnace's finite pull pool and advances the furnace's `version`; a client holding an earlier version computed its capacity headroom from a pool that no longer reflects all committed reservations. If `expected_furnace_version` does not equal the furnace's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FURNACE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than silently over-subscribing the furnace.

Opening a lightweighting program requires `expected_design_version` carrying the `version` of the baseline bottle design as the client last observed it. A new program legitimately supersedes any non-terminal program on the same baseline design, so a client whose view of the design is stale would supersede a program it never saw. If `expected_design_version` does not equal the design's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. Reads, transitions and all other creates are accepted without one.

### 2.8. Window Semantics and Nested Path Resolution

Three kinds of interval appear in this specification and their boundary rules differ. A **forming-job window**, delimited by `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, is half-open: the start instant is inside the window, the end instant is outside it. Two jobs on the same forming machine may therefore abut exactly — one ending at the instant the next begins — except that the machine's mandatory `changeover_buffer_minutes` is appended to the earlier job's end before the comparison is made, so true abutment is only legal on a machine whose buffer is zero. A **colour-change window** is a closed interval: both `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` are inside it, and two closed windows that share only an endpoint do overlap. A **campaign window** is closed for the purpose of containment: a job window is contained in its campaign window when the job starts at or after the campaign start and ends at or before the campaign end. In all three cases `window_end_at` must be strictly after `window_start_at`; equality is not permitted.

Nested resources are addressed hierarchically and path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, with the first unresolvable identifier deciding the error. The path variables the system exposes are `furnace_id`, `campaign_id`, `batch_recipe_id`, `color_change_id`, `forming_machine_id`, `customer_id`, `bottle_design_id`, `mold_set_id`, `program_id`, `production_job_id`, `pack_lot_id` and `complaint_id`; the furnace sub-collections and the lot's inspection-reading sub-collection address their parent as `id`. Reading the inspection readings of a lot whose identifier does not resolve yields `ERR_PACK_LOT_NOT_FOUND` and never an empty list, and the campaigns of a non-existent furnace yield `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_RECIPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORMING_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOTTLE_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MOLD_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACK_LOT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. The same catalogue serves foreign keys carried in a request body, which are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that operation; a `supersedes_design_id` that names no existing design yields `ERR_BOTTLE_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND` exactly as a bad `bottle_design_id` would.

### 2.9. Two-Person Controls

Three decisions in the plant are two-person controls, and in all three the second actor must be a different `X-User-ID` from the first. A design's approver must differ from the engineer who recorded its risk sign-off. A complaint's settler must differ from the engineer who investigated it. A quarantined lot's rework releaser must differ from *every* engineer who recorded any inspection reading on that lot — one shared identifier anywhere in the lot's reading history is enough to refuse. Any of the three violations is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.10. Uniqueness, Field Validation and the Order of Checks

Human-readable identifiers are unique within their own collection: a furnace `code`, a campaign `code`, a recipe `code`, a machine `code`, a design `code`, a mold-set `code`, a job `code`, a program `code`, a complaint `code`, a customer `account_code`, and a pack lot's `lot_code` may each be used only once. A collision is `ERR_CODE_NOT_UNIQUE` (HTTP 409). Codes are 1 to 32 characters, except `lot_code`, which is 1 to 40 characters, and a customer `name`, which is 1 to 120 characters and is not required to be unique.

Field-level validation inspects each submitted field in isolation: required fields present, types correct, enum values among the declared members, numbers inside their declared ranges and decimal precision, timestamps well formed. Failures here are `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). A `produced_units` value below 1 on a close whose `outcome` is `COMPLETED` is also an `ERR_VALIDATION`. Cross-field comparisons, containment, uniqueness, cross-entity comparisons and state checks are *not* part of field-level validation; they belong to the business rules below and surface with their own codes and their own status codes.

Checks fire in a fixed precedence, and the first failing check determines the response. The sequence is:

1. `X-User-Role` present and one of the seven declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`.
3. The caller's role is authorised for the operation, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. Field-level validation of body and query parameters, else `ERR_VALIDATION`. This applies to every write and every list read.
5. Every path identifier resolves, left to right, else the corresponding not-found code.
6. Every foreign key in the body resolves, in declared field order, else the corresponding not-found code.
7. A `CustomerRep`'s ownership scope, else `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This guards the pilot-or-reject decision, complaint creation and complaint settlement.
8. Campaign state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID` — on campaign activation, wind-down, rebuild scheduling and closure.
9. A job's campaign is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE` — on both job creation and job start.
10. Job state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID` — on job start, close and cancel.
11. Mold-set state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_MOLD_SET_STATE_INVALID` — on job start and on mold-set unmount, send-to-repair and return-from-repair.
12. Design state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_DESIGN_STATE_INVALID` — on design submit, request-changes, risk sign-off and approval.
13. On job start only, the design is `APPROVED` and then the recipe's `approved` flag is true, else `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED` or `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_APPROVED` respectively.
14. Program state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` — on program assess-risk, customer decision, adoption and suspension.
15. The lot's `quality_status` permits the requested quality transition and its `logistics_status` permits the requested logistics transition, else `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` — on lot release, rework-release, palletize and ship, and on complaint creation, where the lot must be `SHIPPED`.
16. Complaint state permits the requested transition, else `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` — on complaint investigation and settlement.
17. A colour change is `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_STATE_INVALID` — on colour-change completion and abort.
18. Furnace posture permits the operation, else `ERR_FURNACE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` — on campaign activation and job start.
19. The furnace has no other campaign in `ACTIVE` or `WIND_DOWN`, else `ERR_FURNACE_CAMPAIGN_CONFLICT` — on campaign activation.
20. No `RUNNING` job blocks the operation, else `ERR_RUNNING_JOB_PRESENT` — on campaign wind-down, colour-change completion and mold-set unmount.
21. No job on the campaign is still `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, else `ERR_OPEN_JOBS_PRESENT` — on rebuild scheduling.
22. No lot on the campaign is still `PENDING_INSPECTION`, else `ERR_OPEN_LOTS_PRESENT` — on campaign closure.
23. The submitted parent version matches the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_FURNACE_VERSION` on job creation or `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION` on program creation.
24. A recipe's `cullet_ratio_pct` respects the colour-purity ceiling, else `ERR_CULLET_RATIO_UNSAFE` — on recipe creation and recipe approval.
25. The job's recipe colour and design colour both equal the furnace's `current_color`, else `ERR_COLOR_MISMATCH` — on job creation.
26. The job's `planned_pull_tpd` respects the over-pull limits, else `ERR_OVER_PULL_LIMIT` — on job creation.
27. The design's `breakage_risk_index` is within the sign-off cap, else `ERR_RISK_INDEX_ABOVE_CAP` — on risk sign-off.
28. The program's `breakage_risk_delta` is within the pilot cap, else `ERR_RISK_DELTA_ABOVE_CAP` — on the customer decision.
29. The job's machine, recipe and campaign all belong to the job's furnace, else `ERR_FURNACE_MISMATCH` — on job creation and on colour-change creation.
30. The job's mold set belongs to the job's design, else `ERR_MOLD_SET_DESIGN_MISMATCH` — on job creation.
31. A program's `target_weight_g` is strictly lighter than the baseline, else `ERR_TARGET_WEIGHT_NOT_LIGHTER` — on program creation.
32. The complaint's lot belongs to the complaint's customer, else `ERR_LOT_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH` — on complaint creation.
33. Window endpoints are correctly ordered, else `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID` — on campaign, colour-change and job creation, and on rebuild scheduling.
34. The job window sits inside the campaign window, else `ERR_JOB_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN` — on job creation.
35. The job window does not collide on its machine, else `ERR_MACHINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — on job creation.
36. Colour-change windows do not collide, else `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — on colour-change creation and on job creation.
37. The furnace's rolling colour-change allowance is not exhausted, else `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_RATE_EXCEEDED` — on colour-change creation.
38. The relevant instant has been reached on the logical clock, else `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` — on colour-change completion and lot palletization.
39. `code`, `account_code` and `lot_code` are unique, else `ERR_CODE_NOT_UNIQUE` — on every create carrying one.
40. Mounting does not exceed the machine's cavity capacity, else `ERR_CAVITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — on job start.
41. At least one inspection reading exists on the lot, else `ERR_NO_INSPECTION_RECORDS` — on lot release.
42. Cumulative rejects do not exceed the lot's packed units, else `ERR_REJECTS_EXCEED_PACKED` — on recording an inspection reading.
43. A complaint's `affected_units` does not exceed the lot's `net_packed_units`, else `ERR_AFFECTED_UNITS_EXCEED_LOT` — on complaint creation.
44. The second actor of a two-person control differs from the first, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — on design approval, complaint settlement and lot rework-release.
45. The lot's pallet standard still matches the customer's, else `ERR_PALLET_STANDARD_MISMATCH` — on shipment.
46. A campaign's `target_pull_tpd` does not exceed the furnace's `design_pull_tpd`, else `ERR_TARGET_PULL_EXCEEDS_DESIGN` — on campaign creation.
47. The job's `reserved_melt_tons` fits the campaign's remaining pull pool, else `ERR_PULL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — on job creation.

The consequence worth internalising is that authentication precedes authorisation, authorisation precedes shape, shape precedes existence, existence precedes ownership, ownership precedes state, state precedes concurrency, and concurrency precedes every business rule. A job creation that is simultaneously over-pull and over-capacity reports the over-pull; a job creation carrying a stale furnace version reports the stale version even though the pull pool is also exhausted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning the Plant: Furnaces, Machines and Customers

Before any glass is melted, a `PlantManager` registers the physical plant. A furnace is registered with a `code` unique across the plant and 1 to 32 characters long, a nameplate `design_pull_tpd`, and the `refractory_life_total_units` budget for the campaign about to be run on it. The server initialises the rest: `current_color` is `FLINT`, `posture` is `OPERATING`, `refractory_consumed_units` is 0, `refractory_remaining_pct` is 100.00, `reserved_pull_tons` is 0, and `campaign_count` is 0. The three glass colours the plant melts are `FLINT`, `AMBER` and `GREEN`, and `current_color` only ever advances through a completed colour transition — it is never set directly.

A forming machine belongs to exactly one furnace, named through the `furnace_id` in its registration payload, and carries its own unique `code`. Its physical shape is described by `section_count`, the number of IS-machine sections, which must be at least 4 and no more than 20, and by `max_cavities`, the total cavity slots available across all sections, which must be at least 4 and no more than 80. The `primary_mold_family` the machine is tooled for is one of `NNPB`, `BLOW_BLOW` or `PRESS_BLOW`; running a foreign family on it is legal but costs extra swap time. The optional `changeover_buffer_minutes` is the mandatory idle buffer appended after every job window on that machine; when the payload omits it the machine takes 45 minutes, and any supplied value must be at least 0 and no more than 240. A newly registered machine has `status` `IDLE` and `mounted_cavities` of 0.

Customers are the fillers and brand owners the plant ships to. Registration supplies `name`, a unique `account_code`, the `palletization_standard` the customer's filling lines accept — either `EURO_1200x800` or `GMA_1200x1000` — and `complaint_ppm_threshold`, the contractual breakage rate in parts per million that the customer's quality agreement tolerates, which must be greater than 0 and no more than 20000 with 2 decimals. A new customer starts in `quality_standing` `GOOD` with `weighted_breakage_ppm` of 0 and `credit_issued_usd` of 0. The two pallet patterns are not interchangeable: a `EURO_1200x800` pallet carries 1800 containers and a `GMA_1200x1000` pallet carries 2100, and those two counts drive both palletization and complaint credit.

A customer may renegotiate its pallet pattern, and a `PlantManager` records the change by supplying the new `palletization_standard`. The change is forward-looking only. Lots already packed keep the pattern that was snapshotted onto them at pack time, which means a lot packed to the old standard can never be shipped again: at shipment the lot's own `palletization_standard` must still equal the customer's current one, and a lot left stranded by a renegotiation is refused with `ERR_PALLET_STANDARD_MISMATCH`. The plant's operational answer is to scrap or re-pack such lots, not to ship them.

### 3.2. The Furnace Campaign Lifecycle

A **campaign** is the productive life of a furnace lining between two cold repairs. Planning one requires the `furnace_id` it runs on, a unique `code`, the `target_pull_tpd` the plant intends to average, and the closed `window_start_at` / `window_end_at` pair that bounds it. The campaign's target pull may not exceed the furnace's nameplate `design_pull_tpd`; a plan that asks the furnace for more than it was built for is refused with `ERR_TARGET_PULL_EXCEEDS_DESIGN`. The window must end strictly after it starts, else `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`. A newly planned campaign has `status` `PLANNED`, all five melt-ledger figures — `melt_tons_charged`, `packed_tons`, `transition_loss_tons`, `reject_loss_tons` and `unaccounted_loss_tons` — at 0, `projected_refractory_units_nominal` at 0, and both `rebuild_window_start_at` and `rebuild_window_end_at` null. Creating a campaign is also a mutation of the furnace: `campaign_count` increases by 1 and the furnace's `version` increases by 1.

Activation moves the campaign `PLANNED → ACTIVE` and is the moment the plant commits. Activation requires the campaign to be in `PLANNED`, requires the furnace's posture to be anything other than `COLD`, and requires that no other campaign on the same furnace is currently in `ACTIVE` or `WIND_DOWN` — a furnace melts one campaign at a time, and a second concurrent campaign is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_CAMPAIGN_CONFLICT`. On success the server records `projected_refractory_units_nominal` for the campaign window using the nominal refractory projection described below, re-derives the furnace's `posture`, and increments the campaign's `version` by 1.

Refractory wear is projected and charged by a single rule with two variants. The window length in days is the elapsed time between the window's start and end instants divided by 86400 seconds, and fractional days are kept — a 36-hour window is 1.5 days. A colour wear factor multiplies that: 1.00 for `FLINT`, 1.15 for `AMBER` and 1.30 for `GREEN`, because darker glass is harder on the lining. Base wear units are the pull rate in tons per day multiplied by the window length in days multiplied by the colour factor. The **nominal** variant is simply the base wear units rounded half up to 2 decimals, ignoring over-pull and cullet stress entirely; it is what a campaign records at activation, using `target_pull_tpd`, the campaign window, and the colour factor of the furnace's `current_color` at that moment. The **stress-adjusted** variant multiplies base wear units by a stress multiplier before rounding half up to 2 decimals. The stress multiplier is one, plus 2.0 times the fractional over-pull, plus 0.8 times the recipe's cullet stress factor, where fractional over-pull is the amount by which the job's `planned_pull_tpd` exceeds the furnace's `design_pull_tpd` divided by that same `design_pull_tpd`, floored at zero so that pulling at or below design contributes nothing. The stress multiplier is never rounded before it is applied. On a job, both variants use the job's `planned_pull_tpd`, the job's own window, and the colour factor of the job's batch recipe colour.

Winding a campaign down moves it `ACTIVE → WIND_DOWN` and requires that no production job on the campaign is in `RUNNING` — a wind-down attempted over live production is refused with `ERR_RUNNING_JOB_PRESENT`. Wind-down cascades: every job on the campaign still in `SCHEDULED` whose `window_start_at` is on or after the logical clock at the wind-down instant is moved to `CANCELLED`, and each cancelled job's `reserved_melt_tons` is subtracted from the furnace's `reserved_pull_tons`. A `SCHEDULED` job whose window has *already opened* is deliberately left untouched, for the plant to close or cancel by hand; that surviving job is exactly why a rebuild cannot yet be scheduled, and it will refuse the rebuild with `ERR_OPEN_JOBS_PRESENT` until it is dealt with. The campaign's `version` increases by 1. Wind-down is not always a human decision — closing a job that drives the furnace's `refractory_remaining_pct` to 5.00 or below triggers the same `ACTIVE → WIND_DOWN` transition automatically, with the same cascade.

Once wound down, the cold repair is booked, moving the campaign `WIND_DOWN → REBUILD_SCHEDULED`. This requires the campaign to be in `WIND_DOWN` and every one of its jobs to have reached `COMPLETED`, `ABORTED` or `CANCELLED`. The payload supplies `rebuild_window_start_at` and `rebuild_window_end_at`; the rebuild must start strictly after the campaign's own `window_end_at`, and a rebuild booked to begin at or before the campaign ends is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`. Scheduling the rebuild sets both rebuild instants on the campaign, forces the furnace's `posture` to `COLD`, and cascades `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` onto every colour change on that furnace whose `window_start_at` falls on or after `rebuild_window_start_at` — a transition planned into a cold furnace is meaningless. The campaign's `version` increases by 1.

Closure, `REBUILD_SCHEDULED → CLOSED`, is terminal and is the plant's accounting moment. It requires that every pack lot on the campaign has left `PENDING_INSPECTION` — that is, each is in `RELEASED`, `QUARANTINED` or `SCRAPPED` — and a campaign with a lot still awaiting disposition is refused with `ERR_OPEN_LOTS_PRESENT`. On closure the server recomputes `unaccounted_loss_tons` from the campaign melt ledger, resets the furnace's `refractory_consumed_units` to 0 and `refractory_remaining_pct` to 100.00 to reflect the new lining, sets the furnace's `posture` to `OPERATING`, and increments the campaign's `version` by 1.

The melt ledger itself deserves a precise reading. `melt_tons_charged` accumulates the `reserved_melt_tons` of every job on the campaign that has closed — whether `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` — plus the `transition_loss_tons` of every colour change completed on the furnace while this campaign was `ACTIVE` or `WIND_DOWN`. `packed_tons` accumulates net packed tonnage from released lots, `transition_loss_tons` accumulates the transition tonnage attributed to the campaign's lots, and `reject_loss_tons` accumulates tonnage lost to inspection rejects. `unaccounted_loss_tons` is the residual: melt tons charged, less packed tons, less transition loss tons, less reject loss tons, carried to 2 decimals and permitted to be zero. Reading a campaign returns the whole ledger together with the reconciled residual.

Finally, the campaign's pull pool. The pool is the campaign's `target_pull_tpd` multiplied by the campaign window length in days — the elapsed seconds between the campaign's window endpoints divided by 86400 — rounded half up to 2 decimals. Available tons are that pool less the campaign's `melt_tons_charged` less the furnace's `reserved_pull_tons`, again rounded half up to 2 decimals. A candidate job's own demand is its `planned_pull_tpd` multiplied by its window length in days, rounded half up to 2 decimals, and that figure is stored as the job's `reserved_melt_tons`. The job may only be scheduled when its `reserved_melt_tons` is less than or equal to the available tons; anything larger exhausts the pool and is refused with `ERR_PULL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.3. The Batch House: Recipes, Cullet Ratios and Colour Transitions

A `BatchHouseTech` owns what goes into the furnace. A batch recipe is registered against a `furnace_id` with a unique `code`, the `color` it melts, the `cullet_ratio_pct` giving the combined external and internal cullet share of the batch, `soda_ash_kg_per_ton` charged per melted ton, and `colorant_kg_per_ton` charged per melted ton. Soda ash must be greater than 0 with 2 decimals; colorant may be 0 or more with 2 decimals; the cullet ratio must be at least 0.00 and no more than 95.00 with 2 decimals as a matter of type range. On top of that type range sits a colour-purity limit: a `FLINT` recipe may not exceed 90.00 percent cullet, because flint glass shows contamination that amber and green hide. A recipe breaching its colour's ceiling is refused with `ERR_CULLET_RATIO_UNSAFE`, and the same check runs again at approval time so a recipe cannot be waved through by a later approval.

Cullet is also a wear driver, and the recipe carries that exposure as `cullet_stress_factor`: the amount by which `cullet_ratio_pct` exceeds 45.00, floored at zero, divided by 100, rounded half up to 4 decimals. A recipe at 45.00 percent cullet or below therefore has a stress factor of exactly 0.0000 and adds nothing to refractory wear, while a recipe at 70.00 percent carries 0.2500. New recipes are created with `approved` false; approval by a `BatchHouseTech` sets `approved` to true and is the gate that lets production use the recipe at all.

Colour transitions are planned, not improvised. A transition is scheduled against a `furnace_id` with the `to_color` the furnace will melt afterwards, the closed `window_start_at` / `window_end_at` pair during which the glass will be off-spec, and the `transition_loss_tons` the batch house expects to lose, which must be greater than 0 with 2 decimals. The server snapshots `from_color` from the furnace's `current_color` at scheduling time, so the record remains readable even after the furnace has moved on, and sets `status` to `SCHEDULED`. The `to_color` must differ from the colour the furnace is already melting; naming the current colour is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The window must end strictly after it starts, else `ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`, and the change must belong to the furnace named in `furnace_id`, with a mismatched reference refused as `ERR_FURNACE_MISMATCH`.

Three scheduling constraints then apply, all evaluated on the furnace. First, colour-change windows are closed intervals and may not overlap another colour change on the same furnace that is in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; because the intervals are closed, sharing a single endpoint counts as an overlap. Second, no job window in `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` may overlap a `SCHEDULED` colour-change window — the furnace cannot form containers while it is being transitioned. Both violations report `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the second half of that rule is re-checked from the other side whenever a job is scheduled. Third, transitions are rate-limited: counting the change being scheduled together with every colour change already on the furnace in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` whose `window_start_at` falls within the 30 days ending at the new change's `window_start_at` — that is, on or after the instant exactly 30 days earlier and on or before the new start itself — the total may not exceed four. A fifth transition inside that rolling window is refused with `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_RATE_EXCEEDED`, because each transition costs the campaign real melt tons.

Completing a transition moves it `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and requires three things: the change is still `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_STATE_INVALID`; the logical clock has reached or passed `window_end_at`, else `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`; and no job in `RUNNING` overlaps the transition window, else `ERR_RUNNING_JOB_PRESENT`. Completion is the only way the furnace's `current_color` advances: it is set to the change's `to_color`, and the furnace's `version` increases by 1. Completion also charges the plant: the change's `transition_loss_tons` is added to `melt_tons_charged` on whichever campaign of that furnace is in `ACTIVE` or `WIND_DOWN` — if there is one — and that campaign's `unaccounted_loss_tons` is immediately recomputed from the new ledger. The colour change's own `version` increases by 1.

Abandoning a planned transition moves it `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` and accepts an optional free-text `reason`. An abort leaves the furnace's `current_color` untouched, charges nothing to any campaign, and increments the colour change's `version` by 1. The same terminal state is reached without a human request when a rebuild is scheduled, as §3.2 describes.

### 3.4. New Bottle Designs, Breakage Risk, and the Mold Sets That Realise Them

A `MoldEngineer` drafts a bottle design on behalf of a customer, naming the `customer_id` it belongs to and, when the design replaces an earlier one, the `supersedes_design_id` of its predecessor; that field is null for an original design. The design carries a unique `code`, the `color` of glass it requires, `capacity_ml` between 50 and 3000 with 2 decimals, `nominal_weight_g` greater than 0 and no more than 1500 with 2 decimals, `min_wall_thickness_mm` between 0.80 and 6.00 with 2 decimals, `cavity_count` between 1 and 20 describing how many cavities its mold set needs, `target_line_speed_bpm` greater than 0 and no more than 900 describing how fast the customer's filling line will run it, `container_price_usd` greater than 0 and no more than 5 with 4 decimals, and `mold_owner`, which is `CUSTOMER` when the filler owns the tooling and `PLANT` when the glassmaker does. A drafted design begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, `breakage_risk_index` 0, `lead_time_days` 0, and both `risk_signed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null.

Submitting a design moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and computes two derived promises. The **breakage risk index** is a weighted score on a 0.00 to 100.00 scale. Its thinness term is the amount by which 0.36 exceeds the design's grams of glass per millilitre of capacity — that is, `nominal_weight_g` divided by `capacity_ml` — floored at zero and then multiplied by 100. Its speed term is `target_line_speed_bpm` divided by 250. Its history term is 0 when the owning customer's `quality_standing` is `GOOD`, 1 when it is `WATCH` and 2 when it is `CRITICAL`. A wall penalty of 8 applies when `min_wall_thickness_mm` is strictly below 1.60, and 0 otherwise. The raw score is 8 times the thinness term, plus 12 times the speed term, plus 6 times the history term, plus the wall penalty; none of those intermediates is rounded. The published `breakage_risk_index` is the raw score rounded half up to 2 decimals, capped at 100.00.

The promised `lead_time_days` is built from the same submission. A base of 45 days is increased by 5 days for every started group of 4 cavities — the cavity count divided by 4 and rounded *up* to a whole number of groups, then multiplied by 5 — and by 7 days for each recorded revision. A risk surcharge is then added: nothing when `breakage_risk_index` is 30.00 or lower, 10 days when it is above 30.00 and at most 45.00, and 25 days when it is above 45.00. A design that supersedes an existing one earns a credit of 10 days, since the tooling lineage is already understood. The result is the base plus cavity term plus revision term plus risk surcharge minus the supersede credit, and it is never allowed below a floor of 30 days regardless of how the terms combine. Lead time is always a whole number of days.

Review is a cycle. A `QualityEngineer` may send a submitted design back with an optional `note`, moving it `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Resubmission moves it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` again, and this path differs from a first submission in one important way: `revision_count` increases by 1 first, and both the risk index and the lead time are then recomputed with the new revision count, so each round trip through review lengthens the promise by another 7 days. Every one of these transitions increments the design's `version` by 1, and a transition attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_STATE_INVALID`.

Approval is a two-person control split across two transitions. Risk sign-off moves the design `SUBMITTED → RISK_SIGNED` and is permitted only when `breakage_risk_index` is 55.00 or lower; a design above that cap cannot be signed at all and is refused with `ERR_RISK_INDEX_ABOVE_CAP`, which in practice sends the engineer back to add glass or thicken the wall. Sign-off records the acting `X-User-ID` as `risk_signed_by_user_id`. Approval then moves the design `RISK_SIGNED → APPROVED` and requires an `X-User-ID` different from `risk_signed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; it records the approver as `approved_by_user_id`. Approval has one cascade: if this design names a `supersedes_design_id` and that predecessor is itself in `APPROVED`, the predecessor is moved `APPROVED → OBSOLETE` and its own `version` increases by 1. `OBSOLETE` is terminal — a superseded design is never resurrected — and the only route into it is the approval of a successor.

Tooling is tracked separately from the design it realises. A mold set is registered against a `bottle_design_id` with a unique `code`, its own `cavity_count` between 1 and 20, a `mold_family` of `NNPB`, `BLOW_BLOW` or `PRESS_BLOW`, an `owner` of `CUSTOMER` or `PLANT`, and a `condition_score` from 0 to 100 where 100 is new. It begins with `status` `AVAILABLE`, `mount_count` 0, and `forming_machine_id` null, since it is not mounted anywhere. Mold sets follow a four-state inventory life. `AVAILABLE → MOUNTED` happens only as part of starting a job, described in §3.5. `MOUNTED → AVAILABLE` is the engineer's unmount: it requires the set to be `MOUNTED` and requires that no job referencing this set is in `RUNNING`, else `ERR_RUNNING_JOB_PRESENT`; it clears `forming_machine_id`, subtracts the set's `cavity_count` from the machine's `mounted_cavities`, increments the machine's `version` by 1, and increments the set's own `version` by 1. `AVAILABLE → IN_REPAIR` sends an idle set to the mold shop and increments its `version` by 1.

Return from the mold shop is a branching transition on the re-graded `condition_score` submitted with the request. The set must be `IN_REPAIR`, else `ERR_MOLD_SET_STATE_INVALID`. When the submitted `condition_score` is 40 or above, the set is written back with that score and moves `IN_REPAIR → AVAILABLE`, ready to run again. When the submitted `condition_score` is below 40, the set is likewise written back with that score but moves `IN_REPAIR → SCRAPPED`, which is terminal — the mold shop has judged the set beyond economic repair. Both branches increment the set's `version` by 1, and both record the submitted score rather than preserving the old one.

### 3.5. Scheduling, Starting and Closing Forming Jobs

The production job is where every other entity in the plant meets. Scheduling one is the `PlantScheduler`'s work, and the payload names five references — the `campaign_id` the job is charged to, the `furnace_id` that melts for it, the `forming_machine_id` that forms it, the `batch_recipe_id` that feeds it and the `bottle_design_id` it produces — plus a unique `code`, the half-open `window_start_at` / `window_end_at` pair, the `planned_pull_tpd` requested for the job, the `expected_furnace_version` demanded by §2.7, and optionally the `mold_set_id` of the tooling that will run it. The mold set may be left unnamed at scheduling time and attached later by rescheduling, but a job with no mold set can never be started.

Scheduling is gated by a dense set of rules. The campaign must be in `ACTIVE`; scheduling against a campaign that is merely `PLANNED`, or already winding down, is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The job's forming machine and batch recipe must both belong to the furnace named in `furnace_id`, and that furnace must be the campaign's furnace; any of those three mismatches reports `ERR_FURNACE_MISMATCH`. When a `mold_set_id` is supplied, the set must belong to the job's bottle design, else `ERR_MOLD_SET_DESIGN_MISMATCH`. Both the batch recipe's `color` and the bottle design's `color` must equal the furnace's `current_color` — the furnace melts one colour at a time and cannot serve a design that needs another — and a discrepancy on either side reports `ERR_COLOR_MISMATCH`.

Pull rate is limited twice over. A job may never request more than 1.15 times the furnace's `design_pull_tpd`, which is the fifteen-percent over-pull the lining can tolerate at all; and while the furnace's posture is `RESTRICTED`, the job may not exceed `design_pull_tpd` at all, because a furnace with 15.00 percent or less of its refractory life left is no longer permitted to be pushed. Either breach is `ERR_OVER_PULL_LIMIT`. Temporally, the job window must end strictly after it starts (`ERR_WINDOW_ORDER_INVALID`); it must sit entirely inside the campaign window, starting at or after the campaign's `window_start_at` and ending at or before the campaign's `window_end_at`, else `ERR_JOB_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN`; it must not collide with another job window on the same forming machine, where collision is evaluated on half-open intervals after the machine's `changeover_buffer_minutes` has been appended to the earlier job's end and only jobs in `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` are considered, else `ERR_MACHINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; and it must not overlap any `SCHEDULED` colour-change window on the furnace, else `ERR_COLOR_CHANGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Finally the reservation must fit the pull pool computed in §3.2, else `ERR_PULL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successfully scheduled job is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `changeover_minutes` 0, `refractory_wear_units` 0, `produced_units` 0 and `outcome_note` null. The server computes `reserved_melt_tons` from the planned pull and window length, adds that figure to the furnace's `reserved_pull_tons`, increments the furnace's `version` by 1, and records both refractory projections at once: `projected_refractory_units_nominal` from the nominal variant and `projected_refractory_units_stress_adjusted` from the stress-adjusted variant, both over the job's own window using the job's recipe colour. A dedicated computed read returns those two projections side by side for a single job, so a scheduler can see what the job is planned to cost the lining and what it will cost if the requested over-pull and cullet stress actually materialise; that read recomputes the nominal figure on demand and mutates nothing.

Starting a job moves it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING` and is the plant's most heavily preconditioned transition. The job must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`. The campaign must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The batch recipe's `approved` flag must be true and the bottle design must be in `APPROVED`; the design is checked first and reports `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`, the recipe second and reports `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_APPROVED`. The job must name a mold set and that set must be in `AVAILABLE`; a job with a null `mold_set_id`, or one whose set is `MOUNTED`, `IN_REPAIR` or `SCRAPPED`, is refused with `ERR_MOLD_SET_STATE_INVALID`. The furnace's posture must be `OPERATING` or `RESTRICTED`, else `ERR_FURNACE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. The forming machine must be `IDLE`. And mounting must fit: the set's `cavity_count` added to the machine's current `mounted_cavities` may not exceed the machine's `max_cavities`, else `ERR_CAVITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A start that survives all of that produces four coordinated mutations. The mold set moves `AVAILABLE → MOUNTED`, takes the job's `forming_machine_id` as its own, has its `mount_count` increased by 1 and its `version` increased by 1. The forming machine moves `IDLE → RUNNING`, gains the set's `cavity_count` in its `mounted_cavities`, and has its `version` increased by 1. The job's `version` increases by 1. And the job's `changeover_minutes` is computed and stored as the downtime the changeover cost.

Changeover downtime is a weighted penalty on a base of 60 minutes. That base is multiplied by 1.5 when the mold set's `mold_family` differs from the machine's `primary_mold_family`, and left as it is when they match — a foreign family means the machine has to be re-tooled, not merely re-molded. Two further penalties are then computed and only the larger of the two is charged. The cavity penalty depends on the absolute difference between the mold set's `cavity_count` and the design's `cavity_count`: 0 minutes when the two are equal, 15 minutes when the difference is 1 or 2, 35 minutes when it is 3, 4 or 5, and 60 minutes when it is greater than 5. The condition penalty depends on the set's `condition_score`: 0 minutes at 85 or above, 10 minutes from 71 through 84, and 25 minutes at 70 or below. The swap minutes plus the greater of those two penalties is rounded half up to a whole number of minutes and then capped at 240, so no single changeover is ever charged more than four hours.

Closing a running job is a branching operation driven by the `outcome` field, which is either `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED`, and accepts an optional `outcome_note` of up to 400 characters as a free-text close note. Both branches require the job to be in `RUNNING`, else `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`; both compute `refractory_wear_units` from the stress-adjusted refractory variant and charge it to the furnace, adding it to `refractory_consumed_units`, recomputing `refractory_remaining_pct` and re-deriving `posture`; both move the job's `reserved_melt_tons` out of the furnace's `reserved_pull_tons` and into the campaign's `melt_tons_charged`; both move the forming machine `RUNNING → IDLE` with its `version` increased by 1; and both increment the job's `version` by 1.

Where the branches diverge is production. A `COMPLETED` close, moving the job `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, requires `produced_units` of at least 1 — the containers formed and packed — and a close claiming completion with fewer than one unit is rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION`. It creates exactly one pack lot, tying together the job that formed it, the campaign that paid for the melt, the design it realises and the customer that ordered it, so that a complaint arriving months later can be traced back to the furnace hour that produced the glass. An `ABORTED` close, moving the job `RUNNING → ABORTED`, creates no pack lot at all: the wear and the melt tons are still charged, but nothing saleable came out, which is precisely how an aborted job shows up as unaccounted loss in the campaign ledger. Both closes may trigger one further cascade: if the recharged `refractory_remaining_pct` has fallen to 5.00 or below, the furnace's posture becomes `REBUILD_DUE` and the campaign is automatically wound down with the full `ACTIVE → WIND_DOWN` cascade of §3.2.

Cancelling a job moves it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and is the scheduler's way of handing capacity back. It requires the job to be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`. The job's `reserved_melt_tons` is subtracted from the furnace's `reserved_pull_tons`, the furnace's `version` increases by 1, and the job's `version` increases by 1. Nothing is charged to the campaign ledger — a cancelled job never melted anything. `COMPLETED`, `ABORTED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. The Inspection Line, Lot Disposition and Pack-to-Melt Yield

Every pack lot created at a completed close carries the traceability chain as its own references — the originating job, the campaign, the bottle design and the customer — and a `lot_code` printed on the pallet label, derived as the job's `code` followed by the suffix `-L1` and unique across all lots. Its `packed_units` is copied from the job's `produced_units`; its `unit_weight_g` is a snapshot of the bottle design's `nominal_weight_g` at close, which is what makes tonnage conversion stable even if the design is later superseded; and its `palletization_standard` is a snapshot of the customer's standard at pack time. A new lot has `reject_units` 0, `net_packed_units` equal to `packed_units`, `major_ppm` and `minor_ppm` 0, `final_yield_pct` 0, `pallet_count` 0 and `shipped_at` null. It also has two states at once, because quality and logistics progress independently: `quality_status` begins at `PENDING_INSPECTION` and `logistics_status` begins at `IN_AGING`.

The lot's `provisional_yield_pct` is computed at that same moment, before any inspection reading exists. Gross packed tonnage is `packed_units` multiplied by `unit_weight_g` divided by 1,000,000 grams per ton, rounded half up to 2 decimals. The provisional pack-to-melt yield is one hundred times that gross tonnage divided by the job's `reserved_melt_tons`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. It is an optimistic figure by construction: it credits every container packed and charges only the melt the job itself reserved.

A `QualityEngineer` records inspection-line readings against the lot one at a time. A reading carries a `defect_class` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`; a `defect_code` naming the specific defect, drawn from `CHECK_CRACK`, `BIRDSWING`, `THIN_WALL`, `STONE`, `BLISTER`, `OVERPRESS` and `SPLIT_FINISH`; the `rejected_units` counted under that class and code, which must be at least 1; and the `inspected_at` instant the reading was taken. The lot it belongs to is identified by `pack_lot_id`, and the server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, which is what later makes the rework two-person control enforceable. Cumulative rejects may not exceed the lot's `packed_units`: a reading that would push the running total of `rejected_units` past what was packed is refused with `ERR_REJECTS_EXCEED_PACKED`. Each accepted reading recomputes the lot's `reject_units` as the sum of `rejected_units` over all its readings regardless of class, recomputes `net_packed_units` as `packed_units` less `reject_units`, recomputes both parts-per-million figures, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. `major_ppm` is one million times the summed `rejected_units` of readings whose class is `MAJOR`, divided by the lot's gross `packed_units`, rounded half up to 1 decimal; `minor_ppm` is the same computation over readings whose class is `MINOR`. Both denominators are the gross packed units, never the net. If the lot has already been palletized, the new `net_packed_units` also forces `pallet_count` to be recomputed, since fewer good containers may mean fewer pallets.

Disposition is a single operation with three outcomes. It requires `quality_status` to be `PENDING_INSPECTION`, else `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, and requires at least one inspection reading to exist on the lot, else `ERR_NO_INSPECTION_RECORDS` — a lot is never disposed on no evidence. The outcome is then selected by an ordered cascade over the recorded readings. If any reading on the lot has `defect_class` `CRITICAL`, the lot moves `PENDING_INSPECTION → SCRAPPED`, no matter how few units the reading covers: a critical defect condemns the lot. Otherwise, if `major_ppm` is 2500.0 or higher, the lot moves `PENDING_INSPECTION → QUARANTINED`. Otherwise, if `major_ppm` and `minor_ppm` together reach 9000.0 or higher, the lot again moves `PENDING_INSPECTION → QUARANTINED`. In every remaining case the lot moves `PENDING_INSPECTION → RELEASED`. Both parts-per-million comparisons are inclusive: a lot at exactly 2500.0 major is quarantined, not released.

Each of the three outcomes settles the campaign ledger differently, and each increments the lot's `version` by 1. A `RELEASED` lot computes `final_yield_pct`, adds its net packed tonnage to the campaign's `packed_tons`, adds its reject tonnage to the campaign's `reject_loss_tons`, and charges its attributed colour-transition tonnage to the campaign's `transition_loss_tons`. A `QUARANTINED` lot also computes `final_yield_pct` and adds its reject tonnage to `reject_loss_tons`, but adds nothing to `packed_tons`, because nothing has been passed for sale yet. A `SCRAPPED` lot computes no final yield and adds the entire lot tonnage — gross packed units converted to tons — to `reject_loss_tons`. Net packed tonnage is `net_packed_units` multiplied by `unit_weight_g` divided by 1,000,000, rounded half up to 2 decimals; reject tonnage is the same conversion applied to `reject_units`. In all three outcomes the campaign's `unaccounted_loss_tons` is re-reconciled from the updated ledger, so the residual always reflects the newest disposition.

The **final** pack-to-melt yield is the honest figure and is always less than or equal to the provisional one for the same lot, because it tightens both halves of the ratio. Its numerator is one hundred times the net packed tonnage rather than the gross, and its denominator is the job's `reserved_melt_tons` *plus* the colour-transition tonnage attributed to that job; the quotient is rounded half up to 2 decimals. Transition attribution works as follows. Among the colour changes on the job's furnace that are in `COMPLETED`, whose `window_end_at` falls at or before the job's `window_start_at`, and where the gap from that `window_end_at` to the job's `window_start_at` is 7 days or less, the candidate is the one with the greatest `window_end_at`, breaking ties by earliest `created_at`. That candidate's `transition_loss_tons` is attributed to this job only if this job is the earliest production job on the campaign — ordered by `window_start_at`, ties broken by earliest `created_at` — whose `window_start_at` falls at or after the candidate's `window_end_at`. In other words, the first job to run after a transition carries the cost of that transition, and every later job on the campaign is attributed 0 tons. When no candidate qualifies, the attributed transition tonnage is 0 and the denominator is the job's reserved melt tons alone.

A quarantined lot is not lost. After rework, a `QualityEngineer` may move it `QUARANTINED → RELEASED`, provided the acting `X-User-ID` differs from every `recorded_by_user_id` on that lot's inspection readings; a rework release signed by one of the inspectors is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The rework release adds the lot's net packed tonnage to the campaign's `packed_tons`, recomputes `final_yield_pct` on the same final-variant rule, re-reconciles the campaign's `unaccounted_loss_tons`, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. `SCRAPPED` is terminal on the quality dimension.

### 3.7. Warehouse Aging, Palletization and Shipment to Filling Lines

Fresh glass needs to sit. Every lot is stamped at creation with an `aged_until_at` instant before which it may not be palletized, computed as the lot's `created_at` plus an aging allowance in whole days. The allowance starts at 3 days. It gains 2 more days when the lot's bottle design has a `min_wall_thickness_mm` strictly below 1.60, because thin-walled containers are more prone to delayed stress failure. It gains a further 3 days when any lightweighting program on that design is in `ADOPTED`, which is the warehouse's standing hedge against a lighter container's unknown field behaviour. A thin-walled design under an adopted lightweighting program therefore ages 8 days, while an ordinary design ages 3.

Palletizing moves the lot `IN_AGING → PALLETIZED` on the logistics dimension and is a `WarehouseOperator`'s work. It requires `logistics_status` to be `IN_AGING`, else `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `aged_until_at`, else `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. It sets `pallet_count` to the lot's `net_packed_units` divided by the units the lot's own snapshot `palletization_standard` holds — 1800 for `EURO_1200x800`, 2100 for `GMA_1200x1000` — rounded *up* to the next whole pallet, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. Note that the logistics dimension advances independently of the quality dimension: a lot may be palletized while its `quality_status` is still `PENDING_INSPECTION`, and indeed usually is, which is exactly why a later inspection reading has to recompute `pallet_count`.

Shipping moves the lot `PALLETIZED → SHIPPED` and is where the two dimensions finally meet. It requires `logistics_status` to be `PALLETIZED`, and it requires `quality_status` to be `RELEASED` — a quarantined, scrapped or still-pending lot cannot leave the plant, and the refusal is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. It also requires the lot's snapshot `palletization_standard` to still equal the customer's current one, else `ERR_PALLET_STANDARD_MISMATCH` as §3.1 explains. A shipped lot has `shipped_at` set to the logical clock's current value and its `version` increased by 1. Only a `SHIPPED` lot can be complained about, and a lot may be pulled back to `RETURNED` by a complaint investigation, as §3.9 describes.

### 3.8. Lightweighting Programs: Material Savings Against Breakage Exposure

Taking glass out of a container saves money on every unit shipped and adds risk to every unit filled. A lightweighting program formalises that trade. A `QualityEngineer` opens one against a `baseline_design_id`, which must be an approved design, with a unique `code`, the proposed `target_weight_g`, the `annual_volume_units` the savings are forecast against — at least 1000 and no more than 500,000,000 — and the `resin_free_glass_price_per_kg` used as the glass cost basis, which must be greater than 0 and no more than 10 with 4 decimals. The target weight must be strictly lighter than the baseline design's `nominal_weight_g`; a target that is equal or heavier is not a lightweighting proposal at all and is refused with `ERR_TARGET_WEIGHT_NOT_LIGHTER`. The create also carries `expected_design_version` per §2.7, and it increments the baseline design's `version` by 1.

Opening a program supersedes the field. Any existing program on the same `baseline_design_id` that is not already terminal — that is, one in `DRAFT`, `RISK_ASSESSED`, `PILOT`, `ADOPTED` or `SUSPENDED` — is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, with its own `version` incremented by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and this is precisely the cascade the stale-version guard protects: without it, a client working from an out-of-date view of the design would quietly terminate a pilot it had never seen. A new program begins in `DRAFT` with `weight_saved_g`, `gross_savings_usd`, `breakage_risk_delta`, `risk_reserve_usd`, `net_savings_usd` and `customer_rebate_usd` all 0, `savings_tier` `TIER_A`, and `pilot_decided_by_user_id` null.

Assessment moves the program `DRAFT → RISK_ASSESSED` and requires the baseline design to be in `APPROVED`. It computes the full economics chain in a fixed order. First, `weight_saved_g` is the baseline design's `nominal_weight_g` less the program's `target_weight_g`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Second, the annual mass saved in kilograms is `weight_saved_g` multiplied by `annual_volume_units` divided by 1000, since a thousand grams make a kilogram. Third, `gross_savings_usd` is that saved mass multiplied by `resin_free_glass_price_per_kg`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Fourth, `savings_tier` is keyed on the *gross* figure and never on the net: `TIER_A` when `gross_savings_usd` is below 100000.00, `TIER_B` when it is at least 100000.00 and below 500000.00, and `TIER_C` when it is 500000.00 or above.

Fifth, `breakage_risk_delta` is the risk the lighter container adds. The breakage risk index of §3.4 is re-evaluated with the program's `target_weight_g` substituted for the design's `nominal_weight_g`, holding the baseline design's `capacity_ml`, `min_wall_thickness_mm` and `target_line_speed_bpm` fixed and using the owning customer's current `quality_standing` for the history term. The delta is that re-evaluated index less the baseline design's stored `breakage_risk_index`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Sixth, a reserve rate is derived: the tier's base rate — 0.05 for `TIER_A`, 0.09 for `TIER_B`, 0.14 for `TIER_C` — plus 0.0009 for each point of `breakage_risk_delta`, with the sum rounded half up to 5 decimals. Seventh, `risk_reserve_usd` is `gross_savings_usd` multiplied by that reserve rate, rounded half up to 2 decimals, and `net_savings_usd` is `gross_savings_usd` less `risk_reserve_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Eighth, `customer_rebate_usd` is `net_savings_usd` multiplied by the tier's rebate rate — 0.20 for `TIER_A`, 0.30 for `TIER_B`, 0.40 for `TIER_C` — rounded half up to 2 decimals. Each assessment increments the program's `version` by 1.

The customer then decides. A `CustomerRep` records a `decision` of either `PILOT` or `REJECT` on an assessed program, and the decision is scoped: the rep's `X-User-ID` must equal the baseline design's `customer_id`, else `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A `PILOT` decision moves the program `RISK_ASSESSED → PILOT`, and it is capped: a program whose `breakage_risk_delta` exceeds 30.00 cannot be piloted and is refused with `ERR_RISK_DELTA_ABOVE_CAP`, and a program whose customer is in `quality_standing` `CRITICAL` cannot be piloted either — a customer already breaking bottles is not given a thinner one. A pilot records `pilot_decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. A `REJECT` decision moves the program `RISK_ASSESSED → REJECTED`, which is terminal; the reject branch carries no risk-delta cap and no standing requirement, because declining a proposal is always permitted. Both branches increment the program's `version` by 1, and a decision recorded against a program that is not in `RISK_ASSESSED` is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

Adoption is the `PlantManager`'s call and moves the program `PILOT → ADOPTED`. It requires the customer's `quality_standing` to be `GOOD` — not merely non-critical, but genuinely good — and it extends warehouse aging for future lots of the baseline design by 3 days, as §3.7 already accounts for. The program's `version` increases by 1.

Suspension is the safety valve. A `QualityEngineer` may suspend a program with an optional `reason`, moving it either `PILOT → SUSPENDED` or `ADOPTED → SUSPENDED`; both source states are treated identically and both increment the program's `version` by 1. A suspended program is not dead: assessing it again moves it `SUSPENDED → RISK_ASSESSED` and recomputes the entire economics chain from the beginning, using the baseline design's *current* `breakage_risk_index`, which by then may reflect a customer whose standing has deteriorated and therefore a larger reserve and a smaller rebate. That cycle — assess, pilot, suspend, reassess — may repeat any number of times. Suspension also arrives without a human request: when a complaint settlement drives the customer's standing to `CRITICAL`, every program in `PILOT` or `ADOPTED` on that customer's designs is suspended automatically.

### 3.9. Complaint Investigation, Credit and Customer Standing

When bottles break on a filling line, the `CustomerRep` files a complaint against the lot that supplied them. The filing names the `customer_id` on whose behalf the rep acts, the `pack_lot_id` believed responsible, a unique `code`, the `reported_at` instant the filling line reported the breakage, the `affected_units` the customer reports as broken or unusable — at least 1 — and the `filling_line_speed_bpm` observed during the event, which must be greater than 0 and no more than 900 with 2 decimals. The rep's `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_id` in the body, else `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The lot must be in `logistics_status` `SHIPPED`, since a lot still in the plant cannot have broken at a customer, else `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`. The lot must belong to the complaining customer, and its bottle design must belong to that customer too, else `ERR_LOT_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH`. And `affected_units` may not exceed the lot's `net_packed_units`, else `ERR_AFFECTED_UNITS_EXCEED_LOT`.

A filed complaint begins in `status` `OPEN` with `attribution` `UNDETERMINED`, `credit_usd` 0, `affected_pallets` 0 and `investigated_by_user_id` null. The server computes `breakage_ppm` immediately as one million times `affected_units` divided by the lot's `net_packed_units`, rounded half up to 1 decimal — the customer's breakage rate expressed against the good glass actually delivered.

Investigation is the `QualityEngineer`'s work and uses the lot traceability chain to reach a root cause. It moves the complaint `OPEN → INVESTIGATING`, requires the complaint to be `OPEN` (else `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`) and the lot to be `SHIPPED`, and takes the concluded `attribution` — one of `UNDETERMINED`, `PLANT`, `CUSTOMER` or `SHARED` — plus optional free-text `findings`. The submitted attribution is written onto the complaint, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `investigated_by_user_id`, and the complaint's `version` increases by 1. When the attribution is `PLANT` or `SHARED`, the investigation reaches back into the warehouse: the lot moves `SHIPPED → RETURNED` on its logistics dimension, and if its `quality_status` is `RELEASED` it is pushed back to `QUARANTINED`, with the lot's `version` incremented by 1. That is the traceability cascade in action — one filling-line breakage can pull an entire lot back out of the released population for re-inspection.

Settlement is the second half of a two-person control and belongs to the `CustomerRep`, who supplies a `settlement` of either `CREDIT` or `REJECT`. Both branches require the complaint to be in `INVESTIGATING`, both require the rep's `X-User-ID` to equal the complaint's `customer_id`, and both increment the complaint's `version` by 1. A `CREDIT` settlement moves the complaint `INVESTIGATING → CREDIT_ISSUED` and carries two extra conditions: the `attribution` must be `PLANT` or `SHARED`, so a complaint still `UNDETERMINED` or attributed to `CUSTOMER` cannot be credited and is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`; and the settling `X-User-ID` must differ from `investigated_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `REJECT` settlement moves the complaint `INVESTIGATING → REJECTED` with no attribution requirement, leaves the customer's `credit_issued_usd` and `quality_standing` untouched, and computes no credit. Both `CREDIT_ISSUED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

The credit itself is computed per pallet, not per bottle, because a filling line discards damaged glass by the pallet. The units per pallet come from the lot's snapshot `palletization_standard`: 1800 for `EURO_1200x800`, 2100 for `GMA_1200x1000`. `affected_pallets` is the complaint's `affected_units` divided by that pallet size, rounded *up* to the next whole pallet. A severity share is then taken as the complaint's `breakage_ppm` divided by the customer's `complaint_ppm_threshold`, rounded half up to 4 decimals and capped at 1.0000, so breaching the contractual threshold pays in full and nothing more. The per-pallet amount is the units per pallet multiplied by the design's `container_price_usd`, multiplied by the severity share, multiplied by a credit factor of 0.5 — the plant pays half the glass value, the customer carrying the other half as its own handling exposure — and that per-pallet amount is rounded half up to cents **first**. `credit_usd` is then that rounded per-pallet amount multiplied by `affected_pallets`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Summing unrounded per-pallet amounts and rounding once at the end gives a different answer and is wrong.

A credited settlement then updates the customer. `credit_issued_usd` grows by the new `credit_usd`, and the customer's `version` increases by 1. `weighted_breakage_ppm` is recomputed from scratch over the customer's complaints in `CREDIT_ISSUED` only — complaints in `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATING` or `REJECTED` are excluded entirely — processed in `created_at` ascending order: it is the sum over those complaints of `breakage_ppm` multiplied by `affected_units`, divided by the sum of their `affected_units`, rounded half up to 2 decimals, and it is 0 when no credited complaint exists. `quality_standing` is then derived with strict comparisons: `CRITICAL` when `weighted_breakage_ppm` is strictly greater than the customer's `complaint_ppm_threshold`, `WATCH` when it is strictly greater than half that threshold, and `GOOD` otherwise. A customer sitting exactly on its threshold is `WATCH`, not `CRITICAL`.

Standing has teeth beyond reporting. A customer in `WATCH` adds a point to the history term of every breakage risk index computed for its designs, and a customer in `CRITICAL` adds two, which pushes new designs toward the sign-off cap and pushes lightweighting deltas toward the pilot cap. And when a settlement drives the standing to `CRITICAL`, every lightweighting program in `PILOT` or `ADOPTED` on any of that customer's designs is moved to `SUSPENDED`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. The plant stops thinning glass for a customer whose glass is already breaking.
