# Advanced Placement Exam Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The Advanced Placement Exam Program is the administrative backbone of a national college-level examination service. Schools that wish to label a course with the program name must first submit a syllabus for audit and win approval; only an approved audit unlocks the school's program-label authorization and lets it open exam orders for the coming administration year. Every downstream activity — registering students, assessing late fees, shipping secure exam material, provisioning testing accommodations, scheduling alternate administrations, scoring, releasing results and dispatching score sends to colleges — is gated by that authorization chain and by a calendar of program windows.

This document specifies the HTTP API that program staff, school coordinators, reviewers, scoring specialists and investigators use to drive that lifecycle, mounted under the `/ap-program` path prefix. The surface is deliberately narrow and heavily rule-bound: money is tracked to the cent, exam material moves under a chain of custody, scores are only releasable inside a release window and never while an integrity investigation is open, and the same person may not both submit a record and decide it. Every window, deadline and expiry is evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that program behavior is reproducible in test and in operation.

The system is organized around a small set of aggregates — schools, course audits, students, exam orders, exam registrations, accommodation requests, alternate-exam requests, exam scores, readers, material shipments, score sends and irregularity cases — connected by cascading transitions. A single approval can open orders, confirm registrations, recompute fee counters and flip a school's authorization flag; a single seal break can revert a shipment chain and spawn investigations. Implementers should read the workflow narratives in §3 as the authoritative account of these cascades, and §2 as the authoritative account of the envelope, identity, concurrency and validation contracts that apply everywhere.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two identity headers: `X-User-Role` names the acting role and `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal. Both must be present and non-empty on every call, including reads; a request missing either header is rejected before anything else is examined. The declared roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `SchoolCoordinator`, `CourseAuditReviewer`, `AccommodationReviewer`, `ScoringSpecialist`, `IntegrityInvestigator`, `Reader` and `SchoolStudent`. A request naming a role outside that set is rejected the same way as a request with no headers at all.

Six roles act on opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `CourseAuditReviewer`, `AccommodationReviewer`, `ScoringSpecialist` or `IntegrityInvestigator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as the `submitted_by_user_id` carried when a syllabus or accommodation request is filed, or as the `reporter_user_id` recorded when an irregularity case is opened — but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

Two roles are entity-backed and their identity is resolved against stored rows. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `School` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and a `SchoolCoordinator` request whose `X-User-ID` does not match an existing `School` is rejected as unauthenticated. When `X-User-Role` is `Reader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reader`; when it is `SchoolStudent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student`, and the student may only read registrations and students records and cancel sends that belong to it.

Beyond authentication, each endpoint publishes the set of roles allowed to call it; a role that is authenticated but not listed for that endpoint receives a permission failure rather than a not-found or state error. Certain capabilities are exclusive to a single role: only `PlatformAdmin` may register a school, drive the school test-center status dimension or move the logical clock; only `SchoolCoordinator` may file a syllabus, enroll a student, create an exam order or a registration, file accommodation or alternate-exam paperwork, or designate a score send; only `CourseAuditReviewer` may move an audit into review or issue an audit decision; only `AccommodationReviewer` may move an accommodation into documentation review or issue an accommodation decision; only `ScoringSpecialist` may recruit a reader, drive a reader's calibration dimension or create a score row; and only `IntegrityInvestigator` may open an irregularity case or drive its investigation states.

A segregation-of-duties rule overlays the role model: the acting `X-User-ID` must not be the user recorded as the submitter or reporter of the record under review. This rule applies to exactly three endpoints — the course-audit status transitions, the accommodation-request status transitions, and the irregularity-case status transitions — and it is enforced as an authentication-adjacent gate, evaluated before the state machine is consulted. Its failure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403. The rule is what prevents a coordinator who filed a syllabus from also approving it, and what prevents the investigator who opened a case from issuing its final decision.

Two additional identity-scoped capabilities deserve emphasis because they are narrower than the general role listing. A `Reader` may drive the reader status transition into `CALIBRATING` only when the acting reader is the reader itself. A `SchoolStudent` may cancel a score send only when the acting student owns the score the send belongs to. In both cases the role is otherwise confined to read operations.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts the query parameters `limit` and `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10, must be at least 1 and may not exceed 100; a value outside that range is a request-shape failure. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and counts rows skipped from the front of the result set. List responses place `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`; `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination and `has_more` is true when further rows remain past the current page.

Unless an endpoint description says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Reverse-reachability reads — the nested lists reached through a parent resource, such as the course audits of a school, the registrations of an order, the scores of a registration, the sends of a score, or the cases of a registration — accept the same `limit` and `offset` parameters and obey the same ordering rule. Filterable list endpoints additionally accept the filter parameters named in their §4 description; filters are applied before pagination, so `total_count` reflects the filtered population.

### 2.4. The Program Clock and the Ordering Calendar

The server maintains a single logical clock. At startup it is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time: every window classification, deadline check, expiry rule and release gate in this document reads the logical clock. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock through the test-driving capability that accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the new `current_utc` value. Moving the clock is not a passive operation — it can trigger time-driven transitions, most notably the expiry of an approved course audit whose term has ended.

Four ordering-window bands are derived from the clock. Before `2026-11-01T00:00:00Z` the window is `NOT_OPEN`. From `2026-11-01T00:00:00Z` through `2027-01-31T23:59:59Z` the window is `REGULAR`. From `2027-02-01T00:00:00Z` through `2027-03-15T23:59:59Z` the window is `LATE`. After that instant the window is `CLOSED`. Boundary precision is pinned: the opening edge is compared with a strict less-than, so `2026-11-01T00:00:00Z` is already `REGULAR`, while the closing edge of each band is inclusive, so `2027-01-31T23:59:59Z` is still `REGULAR` and `2027-02-01T00:00:00Z` is `LATE`. Because the default clock is `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, any order classified without an explicit clock set lands in `NOT_OPEN`.

Other program instants are pinned as follows. An approved course audit's term ends at `2027-06-30T23:59:59Z`; once the clock passes that instant the audit expires. The regular score release instant is `2027-07-05T00:00:00Z`, and score release is permitted at or after it — equality releases. The cancellation and withholding deadline is `2027-07-31T23:59:59Z`; a cancellation or withholding request arriving after that instant is refused. The score release window closes at `2027-09-15T23:59:59Z`. An irregularity appeal must be filed before the later of thirty days after the case was confirmed and `2027-08-15T23:59:59Z`. A score send may be cancelled while the clock is at or before the later of seven days after the send was created and `2027-07-31T23:59:59Z`.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Versioning and Stale-Write Rejection

Every resource in this API carries the common properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix. The `created_at` and `updated_at` values are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. The `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and is incremented by 1 on every mutation of the row; nested reads expose it alongside the other fields. All twelve persisted entities in this surface carry the full common set, so `updated_at` and `version` are present on every one of them. Timestamps written by a transition are stamped from the logical clock, not from wall-clock time.

Four status-transition endpoints enforce optimistic concurrency control. Each of them requires a non-optional version field in the request payload carrying the client's last observed `version` of the entity being transitioned, and each rejects the request with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412 when that value does not equal the entity's current `version`.

- The course-audit status transitions require `expected_course_audit_version` matching the target audit's current `version`. A caller acting on a stale view of `review_round` or `syllabus_revision_count` is rejected rather than allowed to overwrite the newer review state.
- The exam-order status transitions require `expected_exam_order_version` matching the target order's current `version`. An order whose version was advanced by a cascade — a course-audit approval opening it, or a seal-break revert pushing it back — rejects a caller whose view of `status` and `order_fee_cents` predates that cascade.
- The exam-score status transitions require `expected_exam_score_version` matching the target score's current `version`. A caller whose view of `release_blocked` predates a release-block write is rejected.
- The alternate-exam-request status transitions require `expected_alternate_exam_request_version` matching the target request's current `version`. A caller whose view of the approved `alternate_date_utc` is stale must re-file rather than overwrite the approved date.

Two further endpoints accept an optional version field that is not part of the OCC contract: the accommodation-request status transitions accept `expected_accommodation_request_version`, the school test-center status transitions accept `expected_school_version`, and the student enrollment transitions accept `expected_student_version`. These are optional payload members; they do not alter the four mandatory stale-version rejections above, and no other endpoint takes a version field at all.

### 2.6. The Ordered Validation Ladder

Validation is not a bag of independent checks. Every request is evaluated against an ordered ladder of twenty-nine ranks, and the first failing rank wins: a request that violates two rules simultaneously returns the error belonging to the lower-numbered rank, never the other one. Implementers must preserve this ordering, because a caller that trips two rules at once must see the higher-priority failure.

| Rank | Condition evaluated at this rank | Error raised | HTTP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty | `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` | 401 |
| 2 | `X-User-Role` is a declared role, and for an entity-backed role `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of an existing instance of the backing entity | `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` | 401 |
| 3 | The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized role list | `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` | 403 |
| 4 | Segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` is not the recorded submitter or reporter of the record under review (only the three review endpoints named in §2.1) | `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` | 403 |
| 5 | The path resource exists | `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` | 404 |
| 6 | Every foreign key carried in the payload resolves to an existing row | `ERR_FK_NOT_FOUND` | 404 |
| 7 | The entity's current status admits the requested `to_status` | `ERR_INVALID_STATE` | 409 |
| 8 | The entity is not sitting in a terminal state from which the machine declares no outgoing edge | `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` | 409 |
| 9 | A course audit entering `APPROVED` has `review_round` at least 1 and a non-empty `decision_notes` | `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_REVIEWED` | 409 |
| 10 | An exam order entering `OPEN` has an `APPROVED` course audit and a school whose `program_label_authorized` is true | `ERR_COURSE_AUDIT_NOT_APPROVED` | 422 |
| 11 | The ordering-window classification at the logical clock is `REGULAR` or `LATE`; `CLOSED` and `NOT_OPEN` are refused | `ERR_ORDER_WINDOW_CLOSED` | 422 |
| 12 | A student holds at most nine registrations per administration year | `ERR_MAX_EXAMS_EXCEEDED` | 422 |
| 13 | An exam order entering `SUBMITTED` has a `registration_count` of at least 1 | `ERR_ORDER_MIN_SIZE` | 422 |
| 14 | An order never holds more than 500 registrations | `ERR_ORDER_CAPACITY` | 422 |
| 15 | A shipment is only `RECEIVED` with an intact seal where `seal_required` is true | `ERR_SEAL_VERIFICATION_FAILED` | 409 |
| 16 | The custody chain is gap-free — the sequence 1..n present exactly once — before `RECEIVED` or `RETURNED` | `ERR_CUSTODY_CHAIN_GAP` | 409 |
| 17 | A reader whose reliability index is below 0.75 may not aggregate a score | `ERR_READER_RELIABILITY_BELOW_FLOOR` | 409 |
| 18 | Reader selection yields at least one eligible reader; an empty pool refuses the aggregation | `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_READER` | 409 |
| 19 | Score release requires `release_blocked` to be false; an open or investigating case blocks it | `ERR_RELEASE_BLOCKED` | 409 |
| 20 | A score is only `RELEASED` at or after its release-open instant | `ERR_SCORE_RELEASE_TOO_EARLY` | 422 |
| 21 | A cancellation or withholding request is refused once the clock passes the cancellation deadline | `ERR_CANCELLATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` | 422 |
| 22 | An alternate-exam request is only approved when its buffered interval does not collide and the same-day cap of two is respected | `ERR_ALT_SEAT_CONFLICT` | 409 |
| 23 | An accommodation is only `APPROVED` with `documentation_completeness` of at least 3 | `ERR_DOCUMENTS_INCOMPLETE` | 422 |
| 24 | A registration only reaches `CONFIRMED` when `fees_paid_cents` is at least `late_fee_cents` | `ERR_LATE_FEE_UNPAID` | 402 |
| 25 | A score send is only dispatched when its `fee_cents` has been settled | `ERR_SEND_FEE_UNPAID` | 402 |
| 26 | A new college score send is refused once the school's `designation_slots_used` has reached the budget of 8 | `ERR_DESIGNATION_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` | 422 |
| 27 | A status transition carrying a stale version field is rejected | `ERR_STALE_VERSION` | 412 |
| 28 | `school_code`, `center_code`, `student_code` within a school and `carrier_tracking_id` are unique | `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` | 409 |
| 29 | Required fields are present and enum-valued fields carry a declared member | `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` | 400 |

The ladder is evaluated in ascending rank order on every request that reaches a status-transition endpoint; reads skip the state and business ranks but still evaluate ranks 1 through 6. A request that is both unauthorized and stale therefore returns `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and a request that is both out of window and over the student exam cap returns `ERR_ORDER_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Ranks 7 and 8 are read together, and a terminal-state label does not make a state absorbing. Each machine declares a terminal-state list, but several of those states also carry a documented working operation back out of them — the return of compromised material from `SEAL_BROKEN` to `RETURNED`, the reinstatement of a `CANCELLED` registration, and the reversals of the withholding and cancellation brakes on a score — and those moves are admitted: the test applied at rank 7 is whether the machine declares the requested edge, and a declared edge out of a terminal state passes rank 7 and raises nothing at rank 8. Rank 8's veto is confined to a terminal state from which the machine declares no edge at all, such as the audit's `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED`, the order's `DELIVERED` and `VOIDED`, the reader's `INACTIVE` or the case's `DISMISSED`; because such a state admits no edge to any target, a move out of it is already refused by rank 7 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and rank 8 is consulted only after rank 7 has passed. `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` therefore remains in the ladder at rank 8 but is not the error a caller observes for any entity in this system, and a workflow sentence that calls a further transition on a terminal state "refused as terminal" means this rank-7 refusal.

### 2.7. Money and Precision Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this API — `accrued_fee_cents`, `order_fee_cents`, `provisional_order_fee_cents`, `unused_exam_charge_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `refundable_cents`, `fees_paid_cents`, `fee_cents` and `credit_back_cents` — is an integer number of cents. Fee arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding, with one pinned exception: wherever a calculation rounds a product before adding it to an accumulator, the product is rounded half-up to the nearest cent before the addition, so slice products are never summed at full precision and then rounded once.

Two calculations carry a four-decimal-place precision rule. The weighted calibration index and the accommodation review-quality weight sum are each rounded half-up to four decimal places at the final step only; intermediate terms are carried at full precision. Provisioned accommodation minutes are rounded half-up to a whole number of minutes. Score band selection performs no intermediate rounding at all: the raw composite is compared against the band thresholds at full floating-point precision, and the band chosen is the first one whose range contains the unrounded value. Instant comparisons — window classification, release-open, deadlines — are evaluated at second precision in UTC. Seat-occupancy interval arithmetic is evaluated at minute precision in UTC.

### 2.8. Chain of Custody and Material Safety

Exam material moves under a recorded chain of custody. Each shipment carries a tamper-evident `seal_number` applied at dispatch, a `seal_verified` flag reflecting whether the receiving center verified the seal, and an ordered `custody_event_types` log whose elements are objects carrying an event type, a seal-intact observation, the instant the event occurred, and a custody sequence number. The shipment also exposes `storage_checkpoint_count`, the number of storage checkpoints recorded against it, and `custody_sequence_count`, the length of the custody log. The chain must be gap-free before a shipment may be `RECEIVED` or `RETURNED`: the sequence 1..n must be present exactly once, and a missing or duplicated sequence number is a custody-chain failure.

Seal integrity is a safety matter, not a fiscal one, and it is enforced ahead of every monetary rule. A school declares whether seals are mandatory through its `seal_required` flag, which defaults to true. When a shipment is received at a center whose school requires a seal, the reported `seal_intact` observation must be true; an intact-seal violation is refused as a safety failure. Where the school does not require a seal, the received seal verification is recorded as NOT_REQUIRED rather than as verified. A shipment that arrives with a broken seal is reported as such, and that report reverts the owning order and opens investigations as described in §3.5.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and Authorization Gates

Transitions in this API are rarely local. A single status change can propagate through three hops of related rows, and implementers must apply every hop in the order the workflow narrative gives. The propagation doctrine is uniform: the primary row's `status` changes and its `version` is incremented by 1; each affected child row's `status` changes and its own `version` is incremented by 1; and the owning aggregate's counters — `registration_count`, `designated_course_count`, `accrued_fee_cents`, `designation_slots_used`, `assigned_score_count`, `storage_checkpoint_count`, `custody_sequence_count`, `review_round`, `syllabus_revision_count`, `investigation_round` — are recomputed from the rows as they stand after the transition, not adjusted incrementally. A parent row's `version` is incremented once per affected parent, never once per affected child, even when the cascade touches many children. The recomputation is not confined to creations: whenever a send is cancelled — directly, or by the cascade that follows a withholding, a score cancellation or a `SCORE_INVALIDATION_REQUIRED` invalidation — the owning school's `designation_slots_used` is recomputed from the sends as they then stand, so a send returned to `CANCELLED` stops consuming the designation budget.

Four gates govern the whole surface and are worth stating as cross-cutting rules. First, a school's `program_label_authorized` flag is derived, never set directly: it is true exactly while the school has at least one course audit in `APPROVED` status, and it gates every course-label and exam-order operation, including the dispatch of material shipments. Second, a school's `test_center_status` dimension — `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` — independently gates material dispatch and alternate scheduling, and a school whose center is not `ACTIVE` cannot open orders. Third, `release_blocked` on an exam score is true while any irregularity case for the same registration sits in `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING`, and it gates both score release and score-send creation. Fourth, a student's `accommodation_eligible` flag is derived from the existence of an approved accommodation request, and a registration whose `accommodation_flag` is true may not advance to material assignment — nor its score to release — until the approved extended-time provision has been recorded.

Counter recomputation is visible to the client: after any transition that changes registrations, the order's `registration_count` reflects the count of its registrations in `REGISTERED`, `FEE_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, `SCORED` or `SCORE_RELEASED`; after any audit decision, the school's `designated_course_count` reflects the count of that school's audits in `APPROVED`; after any fee-affecting change, the school's `accrued_fee_cents` is the sum over every one of its exam orders of `order_fee_cents` plus `unused_exam_charge_cents`.

### 2.10. Declared Field Domains and Enumerations

Fields that accept a closed set of values carry exactly the following members. A school's `test_center_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `RETIRED`. A course audit's `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`. An exam order's `ordering_window` is one of `NOT_OPEN`, `REGULAR`, `LATE`, `CLOSED`, and its `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, `MATERIALS_SHIPPED`, `MATERIALS_RECEIVED`, `DELIVERED`, `VOIDED`. A registration's `registration_tier` is one of `REGULAR`, `LATE`, `EMERGENCY`, and its `status` is one of `REGISTERED`, `FEE_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, `SCORED`, `SCORE_RELEASED`, `CANCELLED`, `INVALIDATED`. An accommodation request's `accommodation_type` is one of `EXTENDED_TIME`, `ALTERNATE_FORMAT`, `SMALL_GROUP`, `BREAK_AS_NEEDED`, and its `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_DOC_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `REVOKED`.

An alternate-exam request's `reason_code` is one of `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ILLNESS`, `RELIGIOUS_OBSERVANCE`, `EMERGENCY`, `OTHER`, and its `status` is one of `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `SCHEDULED`, `DENIED`, `WITHDRAWN`. A score's `status` is one of `NOT_SCORED`, `RUBRIC_PENDING`, `AGGREGATED`, `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED`, `WITHHELD`, `INVALIDATED`. A reader's `calibration_status` is one of `RECRUITED`, `CALIBRATING`, `CALIBRATED`, `DEMOTED`, `INACTIVE`, and its derived `load_status` is one of `AVAILABLE`, `AT_CAPACITY`, `DEMOTED`, `INACTIVE`. A shipment's `status` is one of `CREATED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `RECEIVED`, `SEAL_BROKEN`, `RETURNED`. A score send's `recipient_type` is one of `COLLEGE`, `STUDENT`, `SCHOOL`, and its `status` is one of `PENDING`, `SENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`. An irregularity case's `irregularity_type` is one of `UNAUTHORIZED_DEVICE`, `IMPROPER_ADMINISTRATION`, `IDENTITY_DISCREPANCY`, `MATERIAL_COMPROMISE`, `ADMINISTRATOR_MISCONDUCT`, and its `status` is one of `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATING`, `DECISION_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `DISMISSED`, `APPEALED`.

Numeric domains are equally explicit. `seat_capacity` is at least 1. `documentation_completeness` lies between 0 and 3 inclusive. `raw_composite` lies between 0.0 and 120.0 inclusive. `calibration_sample_count` lies between 0 and 40. `calibration_agreement_rate` lies between 0.0000 and 1.0000. `reliability_index` lies between 0.00 and 1.00, and `reader_reliability_index` on a score copies it. `extended_time_multiplier` starts at 1.0. `school_code` is a six-character code and `center_code` is a four-digit code; each is unique across schools, and `carrier_tracking_id` is unique across shipments. A `student_code` is unique within its school, not globally. A value outside a declared domain, or an undeclared enum member, is a request-shape failure.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Course Audits and the Program Label

A school earns the right to label a course with the program name by submitting a syllabus and surviving review. The `SchoolCoordinator` files the submission with the owning `school_id`, the `course_title`, the `syllabus_hash` of the syllabus document and the `submitted_by_user_id` of the person filing it; the audit opens in `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` at 0, `syllabus_revision_count` at 0, `approval_term_end_utc` empty and `designated_course_count` at 0. The `syllabus_hash` is the audit's identity for revision purposes, and the `decision_notes` field stays empty until a reviewer records a decision.

Review is a two-step movement. A `CourseAuditReviewer` first takes the audit from `SUBMITTED` to `IN_REVIEW`, which requires that the acting reviewer is not the user recorded as the submitter — the segregation-of-duties gate — and that the `syllabus_hash` is non-empty; the transition increments `review_round` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. From `IN_REVIEW` the reviewer may request a revision, moving the audit to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, but only when `decision_notes` is non-empty and `review_round` is at least 1. A revision request deliberately leaves the school's `program_label_authorized` untouched, because the audit was never approved and the school has no designation authority to lose.

The revision loop is the cyclic edge of this machine. A `SchoolCoordinator` returns a `REVISION_REQUESTED` audit to `SUBMITTED`, supplying a fresh `syllabus_hash`; the move is refused when the new hash is identical to the hash recorded on the previous `SUBMITTED` state, which raises `ERR_SYLLABUS_UNCHANGED`, and it is refused once `syllabus_revision_count` has reached five revisions. A successful revision increments `syllabus_revision_count` by 1.

Approval is the pivot of the entire program. From `IN_REVIEW`, a `CourseAuditReviewer` may move the audit to `APPROVED` only when the actor is not the submitter, `decision_notes` is non-empty, `review_round` is at least 1, and the owning school's test-center dimension is `ACTIVE`. Approval sets `approval_term_end_utc` to `2027-06-30T23:59:59Z`, recomputes the school's `designated_course_count` as the number of that school's audits now in `APPROVED`, and flips `program_label_authorized` to true, since a designated course now exists. It then cascades: every exam order whose `course_audit_id` points at this audit and whose status is `DRAFT` transitions to `OPEN`, which reclassifies that order's `ordering_window` from the logical clock; and for each newly opened order, every registration in `REGISTERED` whose student is not accommodation-eligible transitions to `CONFIRMED`, while a registration whose student holds an accommodation request for that exam that has not been revoked stays `REGISTERED`. The school's `version` is incremented exactly once for the whole cascade, not once per affected order or registration.

A reviewer may instead reject from `IN_REVIEW`, moving the audit to `REJECTED` when `review_round` is at least 1, `syllabus_revision_count` is at least 2, and `decision_notes` is non-empty; rejection recomputes `designated_course_count` and re-derives `program_label_authorized` from it, so a school whose last approved audit is rejected loses its label authority. A decision attempted before any review pass — `review_round` still 0, or with empty `decision_notes` — is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_REVIEWED`.

`APPROVED` is not permanent. The system watches the clock against `approval_term_end_utc`; when the clock passes `2027-06-30T23:59:59Z` the audit transitions to `EXPIRED`, recomputes the school's `designated_course_count`, re-derives `program_label_authorized`, and voids every `DRAFT` exam order that still points at that audit. `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are the audit's terminal states, and any further transition attempt on such an audit is refused as terminal.

Every audit transition must carry `expected_course_audit_version` equal to the audit's current `version`; a mismatch is rejected as stale before the state machine is consulted, so a reviewer who read the audit before another reviewer advanced `review_round` or `syllabus_revision_count` cannot act on that stale view. The `to_status` member selects the target state, and `decision_notes` and `syllabus_hash` are supplied on the branches that need them. Reviewers and coordinators read audits through the general audit listing, which filters on `school_id` and `status` and paginates with `limit` and `offset`, and through the reverse-reachability read that lists a single school's audits.

### 3.2. Ordering Windows and the Exam Order Lifecycle

An exam order is a school's claim on a set of exam seats for one administration year. A `SchoolCoordinator` creates it with the owning `school_id`, the `course_audit_id` whose designation authority the order spends, and the `administration_year`; the order opens in `DRAFT` with `registration_count` at 0, `materials_sealed` false, and `ordering_window` at `NOT_OPEN`. Creating an order recomputes the school's `accrued_fee_cents` and increments the school's `version` by 1.

An order becomes `OPEN` either when a coordinator explicitly opens it or as the second hop of a course-audit approval. Both paths require the referenced audit to be `APPROVED`, the school's `program_label_authorized` to be true, and the school's `test_center_status` to be `ACTIVE`; an order that tries to open against an unapproved audit or an unauthorized school is refused with `ERR_COURSE_AUDIT_NOT_APPROVED`. Opening classifies the order's `ordering_window` from the logical clock at that instant — `NOT_OPEN` before `2026-11-01T00:00:00Z`, `REGULAR` through `2027-01-31T23:59:59Z`, `LATE` through `2027-03-15T23:59:59Z`, and `CLOSED` after that. When the opening is driven by the audit-approval cascade, the school's `version` is incremented by 1; when a coordinator drives it directly, it is not.

Submitting an order to the program moves it from `OPEN` to `SUBMITTED` and requires that the order holds at least one registration, that its `ordering_window` is `REGULAR` or `LATE`, and that every one of its registrations is in `REGISTERED`, `FEE_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED`. Submission prices the order: `order_fee_cents` is set to the provisional variant of the dual-variant order fee, described below. A coordinator may void a `DRAFT` or an `OPEN` order, and a `PlatformAdmin` may void a `SUBMITTED` order; all three paths require that no registration of the order has reached `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, `SCORED` or `SCORE_RELEASED`. Voiding an `OPEN` order cancels its `REGISTERED` registrations with `refundable_cents` set to 0 and bumps the school's `version`; voiding a `SUBMITTED` order cancels every registration with `refundable_cents` set to the registration fee actually paid, zeroes `order_fee_cents`, and bumps the school's version.

Material movement drives the middle of the order's life. A `PlatformAdmin` moves `SUBMITTED` to `MATERIALS_SHIPPED` only when every registration of the order is `CONFIRMED`, at least one material shipment exists for the order, and every such shipment is in `CREATED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_TRANSIT`; the transition sets `materials_sealed` to true. `MATERIALS_SHIPPED` advances to `MATERIALS_RECEIVED` only when every shipment of the order is `RECEIVED` with `seal_verified` true and the order's `registration_count` does not exceed 500; every `CONFIRMED` registration of the order then transitions to `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`. An order sitting in `MATERIALS_SHIPPED` can also fall back to `SUBMITTED` when one of its shipments is reported with a broken seal — that revert is described with the custody workflow in §3.5.

Delivery closes the order's fiscal life. `MATERIALS_RECEIVED` advances to `DELIVERED` only when the order still holds at least one registration and none of its shipments is in `SEAL_BROKEN`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `DISPATCHED`. Delivery recomputes `order_fee_cents` into its final, consolidated form, computes `unused_exam_charge_cents` after the fee per the pinned evaluation order, and cancels every remaining `CONFIRMED` registration with `refundable_cents` set to 0, because those exams are charged as unused rather than refunded. `DELIVERED` and `VOIDED` are the order's terminal states.

The order fee is dual-variant, and both variants are readable on the same row: `provisional_order_fee_cents` always holds the provisional figure, while `order_fee_cents` holds the provisional figure while the order is `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, `MATERIALS_SHIPPED` or `MATERIALS_RECEIVED` and the consolidated figure once the order is `DELIVERED`. The provisional variant charges every registered exam at the base fee of 18000 cents and adds a late surcharge of 4000 cents for each registration whose `registration_tier` is `LATE` and an emergency surcharge of 9000 cents for each registration whose tier is `EMERGENCY`; it ignores consolidation entirely. The consolidated variant first counts the order's delivered exams — registrations in `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, `SCORED` or `SCORE_RELEASED` — and then applies marginal consolidation rates over four slices of that count: the first slice covers counts from 0 up to but excluding 2 at a rate of 1.00, the second covers 2 up to but excluding 5 at 0.97, the third covers 5 up to but excluding 10 at 0.94, and the fourth covers 10 and above at 0.90. For each slice the slice count is the delivered count clipped to that slice's bounds and floored at zero; each slice's contribution is that count multiplied by 18000 and by the slice rate, rounded half-up to a whole cent before being added to the accumulator, and the accumulator starts at zero. A single-rate base is also derived, using the rate of the slice that contains the delivered count — with a delivered count of exactly 10 using the fourth slice's rate — by multiplying the delivered count by 18000 and that rate and rounding half-up. The final fee is the single-rate base plus the late surcharge plus the emergency surcharge. The evaluation order is pinned: the delivered count is computed from current registration statuses first, the rebate tier is read off that count second, and the fee is never re-derived from the fee itself. As a worked reference, an order with eight delivered exams yields a marginal accumulator of 139140 cents against a single-rate base of 135360 cents, a difference of 3780 cents.

Every order transition carries `expected_exam_order_version`, and a caller whose view of the order's `status` or `order_fee_cents` predates a cascade — an audit approval that opened the order, or a seal-break revert that pushed it back — is rejected as stale. Order reads expose `order_fee_cents` and `unused_exam_charge_cents`; the order listing filters on `school_id`, `course_audit_id` and `status`, and the nested reads for an order's registrations and material shipments paginate with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. Student Registration, Fees and the Unused-Exam Charge

Students belong to a school of record. A `SchoolCoordinator` enrolls one with a `full_name`, the owning `school_id`, a `student_code` unique within that school and a `grade_level`; the row starts with `enrolled` true and `accommodation_eligible` false. After creation the enrollment dimension is changed only through the student status transition, whose `to_status` of `ACTIVE` sets `enrolled` to true and whose `to_status` of `INACTIVE` sets it to false; that endpoint accepts an optional `expected_student_version`. Student listings filter on `school_id`, `grade_level` and `student_code`, and the student read exposes `accommodation_eligible` and `enrolled`.

A registration is one student's seat in one exam subject inside one order. The `SchoolCoordinator` supplies the `exam_order_id`, the `student_id`, the `exam_subject` and optionally a `registration_tier`; the row opens in `REGISTERED` with `fees_paid_cents` at 0, `fee_waived` false, `accommodation_flag` false and zeroed fee fields. Acceptance is gated three ways: the ordering-window classification at the logical clock must be `REGULAR` or `LATE`, with `CLOSED` and `NOT_OPEN` refused as `ERR_ORDER_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the student must not already hold nine registrations for the administration year, or the request is refused as `ERR_MAX_EXAMS_EXCEEDED`; and the order must not exceed 500 registrations, or it is refused as `ERR_ORDER_CAPACITY`. Creating a registration recomputes the order's `registration_count` and increments the order's `version` by 1.

The late fee is assessed when a registration moves from `REGISTERED` to `FEE_PENDING`, a move that also requires the owning order to be `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` and the student to be currently enrolled. The per-registration fee schedule reads the window classification computed at submission time: a `REGULAR` tier pays nothing; a `LATE` tier pays 4000 cents when the window is `LATE` and 4000 plus the 5500-cent closed-window addition, that is 9500 cents, when the window is `CLOSED`; an `EMERGENCY` tier pays 9000 cents. The assessed amount lands in `late_fee_cents`. The same transition sets `refundable_cents` to the 18000-cent base exam fee plus the assessed late fee when the tier is `LATE`, and to the 18000-cent base alone otherwise; recomputes the order's `registration_count`; and increments the order's `version`. A second, order-level view of the same schedule exists for reversal arithmetic: the chargeable late count is the number of the order's late registrations capped at 25, and it is multiplied by 4000 cents, so an order with thirty late registrations pays 25 times 4000, that is 100000 cents, rather than 120000.

A `PlatformAdmin` may reverse `FEE_PENDING` back to `REGISTERED` when the assessed late fee is waived, which requires `fee_waived` to be true in the payload and zeroes `late_fee_cents`. The forward move to `CONFIRMED` requires `fees_paid_cents` to be at least `late_fee_cents`, and a registration whose late fee is still unpaid is refused with `ERR_LATE_FEE_UNPAID`; the order must also be in `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, `MATERIALS_SHIPPED` or `MATERIALS_RECEIVED`. Confirming re-derives the student's `accommodation_eligible` flag from the existence of an approved accommodation request and copies it onto the registration as `accommodation_flag`; when the flag flips from false to true the registration's material assignment is deferred. A `REGISTERED` registration may also be confirmed directly, either by the course-audit approval cascade — which only touches registrations whose student is not accommodation-eligible — or by an explicit confirmation, which is permitted only when `late_fee_cents` is zero.

Fee settlement happens through a dedicated fee capability that a `SchoolCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin` invokes with `fees_paid_cents` and an optional `fee_waived`. It raises the settled amount, applies the money to the assessed `late_fee_cents` first and to the base exam fee after that, recomputes `refundable_cents`, and clears the unpaid-late-fee gate that would otherwise block confirmation. The registration read exposes `late_fee_cents`, `refundable_cents`, `fees_paid_cents` and `accommodation_flag`, and the per-student nested read lists every registration the student holds across all orders.

Cancellation and reinstatement bracket the registration's terminal state. Cancelling a registration in `REGISTERED`, `FEE_PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` sets `refundable_cents` to the 18000-cent base exam fee — the late fee is never refundable — recomputes the order's `registration_count`, and increments both the order's and the school's `version`. Reinstatement is a `PlatformAdmin` reversal available only while the owning order is `OPEN` and the current window is `REGULAR`; it returns the registration to `REGISTERED` with `refundable_cents` set to the base fee plus the late fee. `CANCELLED` and `INVALIDATED` are the registration's terminal states. A registration advances to `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED` only when the order receives its materials, and only if any accommodation provision has been recorded; a registration whose `accommodation_flag` is true and whose approved extended-time multiplier has not been provisioned is refused with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_PROVISION_INCOMPLETE`. From there the registration follows its score: it moves to `SCORED` when the score row reaches `AGGREGATED`, and to `SCORE_RELEASED` when that score is released. A confirmed score-invalidation finding drives the registration from `SCORE_RELEASED` to `INVALIDATED` as described in §3.9.

The unused-exam charge is the program's remedy for registered exams that were never delivered. It is computed at the moment an order reaches `DELIVERED`, after `order_fee_cents` has been finalized, and it counts the order's registrations still in `CONFIRMED` at that instant. A count of one or zero is free; beyond that the chargeable count is the confirmed count minus the grace count of one, multiplied by 27000 cents, so two unused exams cost 27000 cents and three cost 54000. The charge is never an input to the rebate tier selection, and the count is taken from the registration statuses as they stand at the transition, not from any earlier snapshot. Registration transitions accept `to_status` and the optional `fee_waived` member.

### 3.4. Accommodations and Documentation Review

Testing accommodations are granted through a documentation review, not on request. A `SchoolCoordinator` files an accommodation request with the `student_id`, the `exam_subject` the accommodation applies to, an `accommodation_type`, a `documentation_completeness` count of distinct required documentation items supplied, and the `submitted_by_user_id`. The request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` at 0 and `extended_time_multiplier` at 1.0. The `documentation_completeness` value lies between 0 and 3 inclusive, and the completeness threshold for approval is 3.

An `AccommodationReviewer` takes the request into `UNDER_DOC_REVIEW` only when the acting reviewer is not the filing user and the recorded completeness is at least 1; the move increments `review_round` by 1. From documentation review the reviewer may request more information, moving to `INFO_REQUESTED`, whenever the completeness is below the threshold of 3. The coordinator answers by returning the request to `SUBMITTED` with a higher `documentation_completeness` than the value recorded on the previous `SUBMITTED` state, and only while `review_round` is below the maximum of 3 documentation review cycles; this is the cyclic edge of the accommodation machine.

Approval happens from `UNDER_DOC_REVIEW` and requires the completeness to be at least 3 and the actor to be someone other than the filing user; a request below the threshold is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTS_INCOMPLETE`. Approval sets `extended_time_multiplier` to 1.5 when the accommodation type is `EXTENDED_TIME` and to 1.0 for every other type, sets the student's `accommodation_eligible` flag to true and increments the student's `version`, and then cascades: every registration of that student for the same `exam_subject` that is still `REGISTERED` transitions to `CONFIRMED`, and the second hop records that a later aggregation of that registration's score requires the provisioned extended-time multiplier to have been recorded. Denial is available from `UNDER_DOC_REVIEW` once `review_round` is at least 1; it re-derives `accommodation_eligible` from whether another approved request still exists for the student, resets `extended_time_multiplier` to 1.0, and increments the student's `version`. Revocation of an approved request requires the actor to be someone other than the filing user; it re-derives `accommodation_eligible` the same way, and clears `accommodation_flag` on every registration of that student that has not yet reached `SCORED` — registrations that already reached `SCORED` keep the provisioned multiplier and are not reverted. `REVOKED` is the accommodation's terminal state.

Two provisioning numbers are computed for every accommodation decision and both are surfaced to the client. The review-quality weight sum combines a documentation score, a type weight and a review penalty: the documentation score is the count of approved documentation items capped at 3; the type weight is 1.0 for `EXTENDED_TIME`, 0.8 for `ALTERNATE_FORMAT`, 0.6 for `SMALL_GROUP` and 0.5 for `BREAK_AS_NEEDED`; the penalty is 0.05 multiplied by the request's `review_round`. The weight sum is the documentation score multiplied by 0.6, plus the type weight multiplied by 0.4, minus the penalty, rounded half-up to four decimal places. A request with full documentation, zero review rounds and the extended-time type therefore scores 2.2000. The operational quantity is provisioned minutes: the 195-minute base exam duration is multiplied by one plus a quantity parameter that is 0.5 for `EXTENDED_TIME` and 0.0 for every other type, and the product is rounded half-up to a whole minute, so an extended-time approval provisions 293 minutes rather than 292.

Accommodation transitions accept `to_status`, an optional `documentation_completeness` carrying the updated count on a revision, and an optional `expected_accommodation_request_version`. The accommodation read exposes the computed `extended_time_multiplier` together with the provisioning numbers, and the nested read lists all accommodation requests filed for one student.

### 3.5. Secure Material Custody and Seal Verification

Exam material reaches a school under a recorded chain of custody. A `PlatformAdmin` creates a shipment against a `school_id` and an `exam_order_id`, supplying a `carrier_tracking_id` unique across the program and the `seal_number` applied at dispatch; the shipment opens in `CREATED` with `seal_verified` false, an empty `custody_event_types` log, `storage_checkpoint_count` at 0 and `custody_sequence_count` at 0. Every transition on the custody machine appends the matching event to that log and recomputes the sequence count.

Dispatch is the first custody event. Moving `CREATED` to `DISPATCHED` requires a non-empty `seal_number` and requires the destination school's `program_label_authorized` flag to be true; the transition appends an event of type DISPATCH with the seal recorded as intact. The shipment then moves `DISPATCHED` to `IN_TRANSIT` only when the latest custody event is that dispatch, appending a transit scan whose custody sequence is the current count plus one.

Receipt is where the seal is verified. A `SchoolCoordinator` moves `IN_TRANSIT` to `RECEIVED` by reporting the physical observation through the `seal_intact` payload member. Where the school's `seal_required` flag is true, a reported seal that is not intact is refused as a safety failure with `ERR_SEAL_VERIFICATION_FAILED`, and the shipment's custody sequence chain must be gap-free — the sequence 1..n present exactly once — or the receipt is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_CHAIN_GAP`. A successful receipt sets `seal_verified` to true and appends a seal-verification event; where the school does not require a seal, the verification is recorded as NOT_REQUIRED rather than as verified.

A broken seal takes a different path. From `IN_TRANSIT`, a `SchoolCoordinator` reports `seal_intact` as false and the shipment moves to `SEAL_BROKEN`, appending a seal-break report. That report cascades twice. First, the owning exam order, if it is in `MATERIALS_SHIPPED`, reverts to `SUBMITTED` and its `materials_sealed` flag is set back to false — the cyclic edge of the order machine, and the reason a caller holding a pre-revert view of the order is rejected as stale when it next tries to transition it. Second, for every registration of that order that is in `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, an irregularity case of type `MATERIAL_COMPROMISE` opens in `OPEN` status, so the compromised material is investigated against each affected student rather than only against the shipment.

Return closes the custody chain in both directions. A shipment in `RECEIVED` may be returned by a `SchoolCoordinator` once every registration of the owning order that reached `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED` has been consumed or cancelled, and a shipment in `SEAL_BROKEN` may be returned by a `PlatformAdmin` once every irregularity case created from that seal break has reached `CONFIRMED` or `DISMISSED`. Both paths append a return event. The machine declares `RECEIVED`, `RETURNED` and `SEAL_BROKEN` as its terminal states, and defines one further edge out of `SEAL_BROKEN` — to `RETURNED` — for sending compromised material back to the program.

Alongside the state transitions, custody events may be appended directly through a dedicated capability that takes an `event_type` and a `seal_intact` observation and answers with the new `custody_sequence`, the `event_type`, the `seal_intact` observation and the `occurred_at_utc` instant stamped from the logical clock. Appending an event of type STORAGE_CHECKPOINT increments the shipment's `storage_checkpoint_count`. The shipment read exposes `seal_verified` and `storage_checkpoint_count`, and the order's nested read lists every shipment covering that order.

### 3.6. Alternate Administrations and Seat Occupancy

When a student cannot sit an exam on the scheduled date, the school files an alternate-exam request against one registration. A `SchoolCoordinator` supplies the `exam_registration_id`, the requested `alternate_date_utc`, a `reason_code` — one of `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ILLNESS`, `RELIGIOUS_OBSERVANCE`, `EMERGENCY` or `OTHER` — and a `form_code` identifying the separate alternate form the student will sit. The request opens in `FILED` with `seat_block_conflict` false.

A filed request moves to `UNDER_REVIEW` only when the `form_code` is non-empty and the linked registration is `CONFIRMED`. Approval from `UNDER_REVIEW` is decided by seat arithmetic, not by the reviewer's judgment. The program gathers every alternate request belonging to the same school whose linked registration is held by one of that school's students and whose status is `APPROVED` or `SCHEDULED`. Each such request occupies a buffered interval that begins 30 minutes before its `alternate_date_utc` and ends 195 minutes after it plus a further 30 minutes, so each block spans 255 minutes. Blocks whose gap is zero — that is, where one block's end equals the next block's start — merge into a single block, because the buffer must remain unoccupied; a gap of one minute or more keeps them separate. The merged blocks are summed into a total occupied minutes figure, and the center's capacity is its `seat_capacity` multiplied by the 30-minute buffer-before allowance. If the requesting request's own interval overlaps any existing block, or the merged total exceeds the available seat-minutes, the request overflows and is denied with `ERR_ALT_SEAT_CONFLICT`. A separate, stricter rule applies independently of the buffer arithmetic: no more than two alternate administrations for the same school may share one calendar day. A request denied for either reason ends in `DENIED`.

Once approved, a request is scheduled by a `SchoolCoordinator` when an alternate form is available for its `form_code` — a `form_code` that does not resolve to an available alternate form for the subject is refused with `ERR_NO_ALTERNATE_FORM` — and the linked registration is still `CONFIRMED`. Scheduling records that the registration's `exam_subject` will be scored against the alternate form, and any score rows created for that administration resolve their reader through the same selection rule used for the regular form. An approved request whose alternate date must change is not edited in place: it returns to `FILED` when the `alternate_date_utc` supplied in the payload differs from the approved date, which is the cyclic edge of this machine and the reason a caller holding a stale view of the approved date is rejected as stale rather than allowed to overwrite it. A request under review is denied when its `reason_code` is `OTHER` or when the seat-occupancy arithmetic returned an overflow, and a filed request may be withdrawn by the school. `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the request's terminal states.

Alternate-exam transitions carry `to_status`, the mandatory `expected_alternate_exam_request_version`, and the optional `alternate_date_utc` used by the refile branch. The nested read for a registration lists the alternate requests filed against it.

### 3.7. Reader Recruitment, Calibration and Score Aggregation

Scores are produced by calibrated readers, and the reader pool is managed as a lifecycle of its own. A `ScoringSpecialist` recruits a reader with a `full_name` and a `subject_expertise`; the row opens in `RECRUITED` with `reliability_index` 0.0, `calibration_index` 0.0, `calibration_round_count` 0, `assigned_score_count` 0 and a derived `load_status` of `AVAILABLE`.

Calibration proceeds in two steps. A `RECRUITED` reader is moved to `CALIBRATING`, and from there a `ScoringSpecialist` may certify it as `CALIBRATED` only when the latest pass recorded at least 10 calibration papers and an agreement rate of at least 0.75 against the master rubric. Certification increments `calibration_round_count` by 1, copies the pass's `calibration_agreement_rate` into `reliability_index`, computes `calibration_index` from the weighted formula below, and sets `load_status` to `AT_CAPACITY` when the reader's `assigned_score_count` has reached 120, or `AVAILABLE` otherwise. A calibration pass that does not clear the bar returns the reader from `CALIBRATING` to `RECRUITED` whenever the computed `calibration_index` is below 0.85; that reversal leaves `reliability_index` unchanged and is the cyclic edge of the calibration machine.

The weighted calibration index is computed from the pass's sample count and agreement rate. The sample factor is the sample count divided by 20, capped at 1.0. The precision penalty is 0.08 multiplied by one minus the sample factor, rounded half-up to four decimal places. The index is the agreement rate multiplied by 0.90, plus the sample factor multiplied by 0.10, minus the precision penalty, rounded half-up to four decimal places, with the evaluation order pinned: agreement term first, sample term second, penalty subtracted last. The threshold for passing is 0.85, and the small-sample penalty is what makes it bite: a pass with 10 papers at an agreement rate of 0.9000 yields 0.9000 times 0.90 plus 0.5000 times 0.10 minus 0.0400, that is 0.8200, which fails; the same 0.9000 agreement over a full 20-paper sample yields 0.9100, which passes.

Demotion and retirement complete the reader machine. A `CALIBRATED` reader is demoted when the payload records a `calibration_agreement_rate` below 0.75; demotion copies that rate into `reliability_index`, sets `load_status` to `DEMOTED`, and reassigns the reader's `RUBRIC_PENDING` scores through the reader-selection rule whenever at least one other eligible reader exists — when the eligible pool is empty, the score keeps its current `reader_id` and parks in `RUBRIC_PENDING`. A demoted reader returns to `CALIBRATING` when the payload records a new calibration pass for its `subject_expertise`. A `CALIBRATED` reader with no assigned scores may be retired to `INACTIVE` by a `PlatformAdmin`, which sets `load_status` to `INACTIVE`; `INACTIVE` is the reader's terminal state. Reader transitions take `to_status` plus the optional `calibration_sample_count`, `calibration_agreement_rate` and `calibration_index` members carrying the latest pass.

Reader selection decides who scores what, and it is deterministic. The eligible pool is every reader whose `subject_expertise` equals the registration's `exam_subject`, whose `calibration_status` is `CALIBRATED`, whose `load_status` is `AVAILABLE` and whose `assigned_score_count` is below the load cap of 120. Candidates are ranked by ascending `assigned_score_count`; ties are broken first by ascending `created_at`, then by the higher `reliability_index`, and finally by the lexicographically smaller `full_name`. Row identifiers are never used as a tie-break. On a reassignment caused by demotion, any reader already demoted for that particular score is excluded from the pool, which is why the next-ranked reader can win even when it carries a heavier load. When the pool is empty the score parks in `RUBRIC_PENDING` with no reader and the caller is told `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_READER` rather than being silently deferred.

A score row is created for a registration that has reached `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, with a `raw_composite` between 0.0 and 120.0 inclusive; a `ScoringSpecialist` may supply the `reader_id` explicitly, and when it is omitted the reader is resolved by the selection rule, with the selected reader's `assigned_score_count` incremented. The row opens in `NOT_SCORED` with `ap_score` 0, `reader_reliability_index` copied from the assigned reader and refreshed whenever that reader recalibrates, and `release_blocked` false. Moving a score into `RUBRIC_PENDING` requires the owning registration to be `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED`, the assigned reader to be `CALIBRATED`, and that reader's `calibration_index` to be at least 0.85; the transition increments the reader's `assigned_score_count` and flips its `load_status` to `AT_CAPACITY` the moment the count reaches 120.

Aggregation turns a rubric pass into a final score. Moving from `RUBRIC_PENDING` to `AGGREGATED` requires `raw_composite` to lie between 0.0 and 120.0 inclusive and the assigned reader's `reliability_index` to be at least 0.75 — a reader below that floor may not aggregate, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_READER_RELIABILITY_BELOW_FLOOR`. Aggregation writes `ap_score` using the final variant of the score-mapping calculation and fires the owning registration's move from `MATERIAL_ASSIGNED` to `SCORED`.

The score mapping is dual-variant. The final variant, used on the forward aggregation path, maps the unrounded `raw_composite` directly through four cross-total thresholds, evaluated top-down so that the first matching band wins: a composite of 96.0 or more is a 5; from 76.0 up to but excluding 96.0 is a 4; from 52.0 up to but excluding 76.0 is a 3; from 30.0 up to but excluding 52.0 is a 2; and anything below 30.0 is a 1. The marginal variant, used when an already aggregated score is recomputed after a cancellation is withdrawn, instead maps each of the five exam sections to its own band and sums the five band marks, then maps the integer sum through a second table: a sum of 21 or more is a 5; 17 through 20 is a 4; 13 through 16 is a 3; 9 through 12 is a 2; and 8 or less is a 1. The two variants diverge whenever the section bands straddle a cross-total edge — a raw composite of 95.5 yields a final score of 4 but a marginal score of 5. Band selection is performed at full floating-point precision with no intermediate rounding.

The reader read exposes `calibration_index`, `reliability_index`, `load_status` and `assigned_score_count`; the calibration summary read returns the same numbers side by side together with `calibration_round_count` and a `pass_threshold_met` boolean; and the reader's nested read lists every score row assigned to that reader.

### 3.8. Score Release, Sends and the Designation Budget

An aggregated score becomes visible to students and colleges only when its release gate opens. That instant is the later of the regular release date `2027-07-05T00:00:00Z` and the latest `alternate_date_utc` among the registration's alternate-exam requests that reached `SCHEDULED`; release is permitted when the logical clock is at or after that instant, so equality releases. A registration with no scheduled alternate therefore releases exactly at the regular date, while a registration whose alternate administration is scheduled for `2027-07-18T00:00:00Z` does not become releasable until that later instant. A release attempted before the gate opens is refused with `ERR_SCORE_RELEASE_TOO_EARLY`. A release is also refused while the score's `release_blocked` flag is true, which it is whenever an irregularity case for the same registration sits in `OPEN` or `INVESTIGATING`; that refusal carries `ERR_RELEASE_BLOCKED`. A registration whose `accommodation_flag` is true additionally requires that the approved extended-time provision has been recorded before its score may be released, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_PROVISION_INCOMPLETE` otherwise.

Release propagates outward. A released score drives its registration from `SCORED` to `SCORE_RELEASED`, and once every registration of an order has a released score whose release is complete, the order itself auto-transitions to `DELIVERED` and settles its fee and unused-exam charge as described in §3.2. The score's own terminal states are `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED`, `WITHHELD` and `INVALIDATED`.

Withholding and cancellation are the student-facing brakes on release. A withholding request recorded against a score lets a `ScoringSpecialist` move it to `WITHHELD` from `RELEASED`, provided the logical clock is still before the cancellation and withholding deadline of `2027-07-31T23:59:59Z`; the move cancels every score send of that score that is still `PENDING` or `SENT`, restoring each one's `fee_cents` into `credit_back_cents`. The same destination is reachable from `AGGREGATED` when a withholding request has been recorded, in which case the registration stays `SCORED` and no send exists yet to cancel. A cancellation request recorded against an aggregated score allows a `ScoringSpecialist` to move it to `CANCELLED` while the clock is before the same deadline; a request arriving after the deadline is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Both brakes are reversible: a withheld score returns to `RELEASED` once the withholding request is withdrawn, and a cancelled score returns to `AGGREGATED` under a `PlatformAdmin` once the cancellation request is withdrawn and while the clock is still before the deadline, with `ap_score` recomputed through the marginal variant of the score mapping. A released score may also be cancelled when a cancellation request has been recorded or when an irregularity decision released the block, and the transition flags `request_withholding` and `request_cancellation` record the student request that the move depends on. Every score transition carries `expected_exam_score_version`; a caller whose view of `release_blocked` predates a release-block write is rejected as stale.

Score sends designate recipients. A `ScoringSpecialist` or `SchoolCoordinator` creates a send against a released score, naming a `recipient_type` of `COLLEGE`, `STUDENT` or `SCHOOL`, a `recipient_code` — a college code, a school code or a student code — and the `designation_year` whose budget the send consumes. The send opens in `PENDING` with its fee computed by the dual-variant send-fee calculation; creating it recomputes the school's `designation_slots_used` counter, which counts every send of that school's students whose recipient type is `COLLEGE` and whose status is `PENDING`, `SENT` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and increments the school's `version`. A new college send is refused once that counter has reached the designation budget of 8 for the administration year, with `ERR_DESIGNATION_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`. Send creation is also blocked while the score's `release_blocked` flag is true.

The send fee is dual-variant and the two variants disagree on the same row because they count different populations. Both variants count only sends in `PENDING`, `SENT` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` status; cancelled and failed sends are excluded from every count. The all-inclusive variant counts every send of that score whose recipient type is `COLLEGE` plus every send whose recipient type is `SCHOOL`, subtracts the free allowance of 2, floors the result at zero and multiplies by the per-send fee of 2500 cents. The college-only variant instead excludes school sends from both the count and the allowance: it takes the college send count, subtracts one to account for the student's own free report, subtracts the same allowance of 2, floors at zero and multiplies by 2500. A student with two college sends and one school send therefore reports a 2500-cent fee for the school send under the all-inclusive view, while the per-student college-designation view reports 2500 cents for the third college send and nothing for the school send.

Dispatch follows settlement. A `PENDING` send moves to `SENT` only when the underlying score is `RELEASED` and, for a `COLLEGE` recipient, when the `designation_year` matches the administration year of the exam; the move requires the send's `fee_cents` to have been settled, and an unsettled send is refused with `ERR_SEND_FEE_UNPAID`. A send whose `recipient_code` does not resolve to a known recipient fails from `PENDING`. A sent send is acknowledged by the system once it is in `SENT`. Cancellation is available while the send is `PENDING` to a `SchoolCoordinator` — and to a `SchoolStudent` when the acting student owns the score — provided the logical clock is at or before the later of seven days after the send was created and `2027-07-31T23:59:59Z`; the cancelled send's `credit_back_cents` is set to its `fee_cents`. A sent send is cancelled by the system whenever its score becomes `WITHHELD` or `INVALIDATED`, restoring the same credit and releasing the designation budget the original dispatch consumed. `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED` are the send's terminal states.

Send transitions accept `to_status` and an optional `fees_paid_cents` settlement amount. The score read exposes `ap_score`, `release_blocked`, `reader_reliability_index` and `raw_composite`; the send read exposes `fee_cents` and `credit_back_cents`; and the send listing filters on `exam_score_id`, `recipient_type` and `status`, which is also the view from which the include-set of the send-fee calculation is read.

### 3.9. School Standing, Testing Irregularities and Invalidation

Schools enter the program through a single administrative act. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one with its legal `name`, a six-character `school_code` unique across the program, an ISO `country_code` that ordering and pricing rules branch on, a four-digit `center_code` unique across the program, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1 and an optional `seal_required` flag that defaults to true. The new school starts with `program_label_authorized` false, `accrued_fee_cents` 0, `designation_slots_used` 0 and a `test_center_status` of `ACTIVE`. Its test-center dimension is a second state machine carried on the same row, driven by a `PlatformAdmin` through the school status transition with a `to_status` and an `expected_school_version`. A school is suspended from `ACTIVE` only when a confirmed irregularity case of type `ADMINISTRATOR_MISCONDUCT` exists for a registration hosted at its center; it returns from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` only when every irregularity case hosted by that school has been dismissed, and it may be retired from `SUSPENDED` only when every alternate-exam request belonging to one of its students has reached `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`. `RETIRED` is the school's terminal state, and a school that is not `ACTIVE` cannot open orders or dispatch material. The school read exposes its name, codes, capacity, seal requirement, authorization flag and counters, and the nested read lists every irregularity case hosted at that center.

Irregularity cases are the program's integrity instrument. An `IntegrityInvestigator` or `PlatformAdmin` opens one against an `exam_registration_id` and the `school_id` of the hosting center, naming an `irregularity_type` — one of `UNAUTHORIZED_DEVICE`, `IMPROPER_ADMINISTRATION`, `IDENTITY_DISCREPANCY`, `MATERIAL_COMPROMISE` or `ADMINISTRATOR_MISCONDUCT`. The case opens in `OPEN` with `investigation_round` at 0 and an empty `finding_code`, and opening it immediately sets `release_blocked` to true on every score of that registration that is `AGGREGATED` or `RELEASED`, so an investigation stops a release before it can happen rather than unwinding it afterwards.

The investigation machine moves in three steps and then branches. A case in `OPEN` is taken to `INVESTIGATING` by an `IntegrityInvestigator` when its `irregularity_type` is non-empty; the move increments `investigation_round` by 1 and re-asserts `release_blocked` on every aggregated or released score of the registration. From `INVESTIGATING`, the investigator records a decision by moving the case to `DECISION_PENDING`, which requires at least one investigation round and a non-empty `finding_code` in the payload. The payload's `reporter_user_id` records who reported the underlying issue, and it is that value the segregation-of-duties gate compares against when the case is decided.

The decision branch has two outcomes and they are asymmetric. A case in `DECISION_PENDING` is confirmed only when the recorded `finding_code` is `SCORE_INVALIDATION_REQUIRED` or `WARNING_ONLY` and only when the acting user is not the user recorded as the case reporter; the gate failure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Confirming with `SCORE_INVALIDATION_REQUIRED` runs the deepest cascade in the program: the linked registration transitions to `INVALIDATED`, its score transitions to `INVALIDATED`, and every score send of that score transitions to `CANCELLED` with `credit_back_cents` set to its `fee_cents`, restoring the designation budget those sends had consumed. Confirming with `WARNING_ONLY` instead clears `release_blocked` on every score of the registration and changes no score state at all. A case is dismissed from `DECISION_PENDING` only when its `finding_code` is `NO_FINDING` or is empty, and dismissal clears `release_blocked` on every score of the linked registration. `DISMISSED` is the case's terminal state.

An appeal reopens the question once. A `SchoolCoordinator` may move a confirmed case to `APPEALED` while the logical clock is before the later of thirty days after the case was confirmed and `2027-08-15T23:59:59Z`; an appeal filed after that instant is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. From `APPEALED`, an `IntegrityInvestigator` returns the case to `INVESTIGATING`, incrementing `investigation_round` again and re-blocking release on every score of the registration that had been released — the cyclic edge that allows one more investigation round. Case transitions take `to_status` and the optional `finding_code` and `reporter_user_id` members, and the case read exposes `finding_code`, `investigation_round` and `test_center_id`. Case listings filter on `exam_registration_id` and `status`, and the nested reads reach cases from a registration and from a school.
