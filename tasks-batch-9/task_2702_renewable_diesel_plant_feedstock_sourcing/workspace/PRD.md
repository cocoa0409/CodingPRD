# Renewable Diesel Feedstock Sourcing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A renewable diesel plant lives or dies on the provenance of what it puts through the reactor. Every tonne of used cooking oil, animal tallow, distillers corn oil or virgin vegetable oil arrives with a commercial price, an attested carbon intensity, a laboratory profile that decides whether it can be charged at all, and a paper trail that a regulator may later audit line by line. The same tonne then becomes hydrogen demand, catalyst wear, renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel, and finally a quantity of compliance credits whose value depends on arithmetic performed months earlier at the plant gate. Today that chain is stitched together from spreadsheets held by four different desks — trading, the laboratory, the plant floor and compliance — and the seams are exactly where credits are lost, where uncertified aviation fuel gets loaded onto a barge, and where a supplier suspension fails to reach the feedstock already reserved against next week's campaign.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets with a single authoritative record. The service is deliberately narrow in scope and uncompromising about consistency: it owns supplier due diligence and traceability evidence, feedstock lots and their quality dispositions, pretreatment, the hydrotreating units with their catalyst charges and turnaround windows, hydrogen supply contracts and the daily nomination book, processing campaigns and the feedstock allocated to them, finished product batches with their certification and distribution status, pathway registrations under the renewable fuel and low carbon fuel programs, the credit blocks minted against shipped product, and the hedge book that manages feedstock and credit price exposure. Nothing is eventually consistent. When a supplier is suspended, the lots, allocations, campaigns and hydrogen nominations that depend on that supplier are corrected inside the same request that records the suspension.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the compliance officer who registered a pathway may never be the officer who internally signs off a credit block claimed under it, quality dispositions belong exclusively to the laboratory, and certification and verification decisions belong exclusively to a third-party verifier. The second is **arithmetic determinism**: every fee, blend carbon intensity, hydrogen requirement, yield, settlement cost, margin and credit quantity is produced by a fixed formula with a pinned evaluation order and a pinned rounding step, because the difference between rounding each settlement line and rounding the sum of the lines is a real number of dollars that a regulator will reconcile against.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section states an explicit exception. The service is mounted at the server root: each collection is a flat top-level path segment, and resources are linked to one another by identifier fields carried in request bodies rather than by nesting in the URL.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds anything other than one of the six role names defined below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 — before any path segment is resolved and before the body is inspected. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not listed as an authorized role for the endpoint being called, the refusal is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` with HTTP 403.

The six roles are `PlantOperator`, `FeedstockTrader`, `QualityAnalyst`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `IndependentVerifier` and `Supplier`.

A `PlantOperator` runs the plant floor: hydrotreating units, catalyst charges, turnaround windows, hydrogen nominations and the whole campaign lifecycle from creation through securement, start, completion and abort. A `PlantOperator` may read units, campaigns, lots, batches, contracts and nominations, but may not create suppliers or feedstock lots, may not record quality dispositions, may not allocate feedstock to a campaign, may not reconcile a campaign, and may not touch pathways, credit blocks or hedge positions.

A `FeedstockTrader` is the buying desk: it onboards suppliers, uploads traceability evidence on their behalf, books delivered lots, allocates and releases feedstock against campaigns, buys hydrogen supply contracts, and manages the hedge book. It never adjudicates quality and never signs compliance paperwork — due-diligence verification and suspension belong to compliance, and every quality endpoint is closed to it.

A `QualityAnalyst` is the laboratory: it draws samples, records free fatty acid, moisture, insolubles and phosphorus figures, orders pretreatment, and accepts lots for charging. It holds the concurrency-guarded write on a feedstock lot. It may read lots, quality tests and suppliers, but may not create lots or suppliers, may not allocate feedstock, and may not touch certification, pathways, credits or hedging.

A `ComplianceOfficer` is the compliance and accounting desk: it verifies and suspends suppliers, reconciles completed campaigns, files certification requests, moves finished product to offtakers, registers pathways, mints credit blocks, signs them off internally, retires credits and reverses retirements. It may read every entity. It may not make certification decisions, may not start or decide a pathway review, and may not make credit verification decisions — all four belong to the verifier.

An `IndependentVerifier` is the third-party verification body: it starts and decides pathway reviews, certifies or rejects product batches against ASTM specification, and confirms, adjusts or rejects credit blocks. It creates no business objects of its own and is read-only everywhere else; in particular it may not create credit blocks, sign them off internally, retire credits, create or submit pathway registrations, or touch suppliers, lots, campaigns or hedge positions.

A `Supplier` is an external feedstock supplier authenticated as its own supplier record. It may upload its own traceability documents and read its own record and its own documents, and nothing else.

Identity semantics differ between the two kinds of role. When `X-User-Role` is `Supplier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting supplier record; ownership checks on any resource that references a supplier compare the resource's supplier identifier to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Supplier` caller uploading or listing traceability documents, or reading a supplier record, must have an `X-User-ID` equal to the supplier id named in the path; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403, and that refusal is evaluated ahead of existence checks on nested resources. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantOperator`, `FeedstockTrader`, `QualityAnalyst`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `IndependentVerifier`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record it in two places where the business rules demand an audit anchor — as `registered_by_user_id` on a pathway registration when a `ComplianceOfficer` creates it, and as `signed_off_by_user_id` on a credit block when a `ComplianceOfficer` internally signs it off — and the segregation-of-duties rule compares those two recorded values to each other, but neither is resolved against a user table.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 — including a `limit` of 0 — is a request-shape failure and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending in every case. No list endpoint offers an alternative sort. Where a subsection below describes an ordering used *internally* by a cascade or a settlement calculation, that ordering is also `created_at` ascending, so the sequence a client observes in a listing is the sequence the server processes.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values in force for the page, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching row remains beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and the Logical Plant Clock

Every resource the service creates is addressed by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — the identifier of a feedstock lot is indistinguishable in shape from the identifier of a credit block, and clients must never parse it. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry three audit properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is the same format and records the most recent mutation; on creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request against the row. Whenever this document says that a rule "bumps the version" of an entity, it also means `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock at that instant.

Two resources are deliberately immutable append-only evidence and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: a traceability document and a quality test. Neither of them has an `updated_at` and neither of them has a `version`. A traceability document, once uploaded, is never edited or withdrawn — it expires by its own `valid_until` instant. A quality test, once appended, is never edited — a later sample supersedes it. Note that the *owning* supplier and the *owning* lot are ordinary versioned resources, so appending evidence bumps the parent's `version` even though the evidence row itself has none.

All temporal reasoning in the service — document expiry, due-diligence expiry, turnaround windows, campaign windows, hydrogen nomination coverage, hedge maturity, and the `created_at` and `updated_at` stamps themselves — reads a single **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. On a fresh process that clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, so a scenario that never touches it still observes that instant. A test-driving capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and sets the clock to it, returning the new value as `current_utc`; time may be moved forward or backward, and moving it changes no business state by itself. Every role except `Supplier` may drive the clock.

### 2.5. Metrology: Precision, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All rounding in this service is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero. There is no banker's rounding anywhere. What differs between calculations is the number of decimal places and, far more consequentially, *where in the arithmetic the rounding happens*. The following precisions are normative:

- Pretreatment fees, all hedge money figures (`gross_notional_usd`, `base_margin_usd`, `volatility_adder_usd`, `total_margin_usd`, `realized_pnl_usd`), settlement line costs and campaign feedstock cost: 2 decimal places. Carbon intensities, whether attested, blended, proposed, certified, provisional or verified: 2 decimal places.
- Feedstock and product masses in metric tonnes, yield percentages, yield variance, and credit quantities in metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent: 3 decimal places.
- Catalyst activity index: 4 decimal places, and never below its floor.
- Hydrogen kilograms per day and product volumes in gallons: 1 decimal place.
- Renewable-fuel credit counts are whole numbers produced by discarding the fractional part — truncation toward zero, never rounding.
- Sub-tier indices contributed by the four laboratory measurements are small integers.

Three calculations have a rounding *order* that is part of the specification and not an implementation detail, and each is spelled out again where it is used. The hydrogen requirement rounds the daily feed rate to 3 decimal places **before** multiplying it by the weighted hydrogen intensity; rounding only at the end produces a different kilogram figure. Feedstock settlement rounds **every allocation line** to cents and then sums the rounded lines; summing unrounded line products and rounding once at the end is the forbidden alternative. The blend carbon intensity does the opposite: the mass-weighted sum is accumulated unrounded and rounded exactly once at the end.

Where a calculation is defined by a sequence of numbered steps, that sequence is the evaluation order and may not be rearranged even when the algebra appears to permit it. The clearest case is hedge margin: the volatility tier is selected from the base margin, never from the total margin, so a position whose total margin crosses a tier floor while its base margin does not stays in the lower tier.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Lots and Campaigns

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against their parent's `version`, because in both cases the write is legitimate to repeat and therefore cannot be resolved by a state precondition alone.

Appending a quality test to a feedstock lot requires the non-optional field `expected_lot_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the lot as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the lot's current `version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Retesting a lot is a normal, repeatable operation — a later sample supersedes the earlier governing test and can rewrite the lot's pretreatment tier, its pretreatment fee and its lifecycle status — so the stale-version check is the only thing standing between a client and the silent overwriting of a disposition it never saw.

Creating a feedstock allocation against a campaign requires the non-optional field `expected_run_version` in the request body, carrying the campaign's `version` as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the campaign's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Adding further allocations to a campaign that is still in planning is likewise legitimate and repeatable, and each allocation rewrites the campaign's reserved mass, its blend carbon intensity and its hydrogen requirement; a client working from a stale blend picture would silently move the carbon intensity another writer had just established.

No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind. In particular, releasing an allocation, securing hydrogen, starting, completing, reconciling or aborting a campaign, accepting or pretreating a lot, and every certification, pathway and credit operation carry no expected-version input.

### 2.7. The Request Screening Ladder

Checks are evaluated in a strict ascending order and **the first failing check decides the response**. When a request violates two rules at once, the client always sees the error belonging to the earlier rung. The ladder is:

1. Identity headers present and the role recognized — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. The acting role authorized for this endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403.
3. Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated left to right — one of `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURNAROUND_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATHWAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, all 404. Left-to-right means the leftmost unresolvable segment decides: a request naming a supplier that does not exist and a document that does not exist reports the supplier.
4. Every identifier supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `supplier_id`, `lot_id`, `unit_id`, `catalyst_charge_id`, `run_id`, `batch_id` and `pathway_id` are screened here, reporting `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALYST_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PATHWAY_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, all 404. This rung guards lot creation, campaign creation, allocation creation, hydrogen nomination creation, pathway registration creation and credit-block creation.
5. Supplier self-scope — when the acting role is `Supplier`, `X-User-ID` must equal the supplier id in the path, otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403. This rung guards traceability-document upload, reading a supplier record and listing a supplier's documents.
6. Request-body shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members drawn from the declared sets, numeric ranges and decimal-place limits honoured, and a list `limit` within 1 through 100 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422. One domain check sits inside this same rung, after the schema and enum checks have passed: a delivered lot's `feedstock_type` must appear in the cited supplier's `feedstock_types`, and if it does not the failure is `ERR_FEEDSTOCK_TYPE_NOT_SUPPLIED`, also 422.
7. Optimistic concurrency, as described in §2.6 — `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION`, 412.
8. The target resource named in the path is in a state that permits the requested transition — one of `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_NOMINATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_CERT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PATHWAY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_BLOCK_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_HEDGE_STATE`, all 409.

Below rung 8 sit the domain rules, and their relative order also matters. In ascending order of evaluation they are: supplier due diligence incomplete (`ERR_SUPPLIER_DUE_DILIGENCE_INCOMPLETE`), supplier due diligence expired (`ERR_SUPPLIER_DUE_DILIGENCE_EXPIRED`), lot awaiting pretreatment (`ERR_PRETREATMENT_REQUIRED`), lot not accepted (`ERR_LOT_NOT_ACCEPTED`), catalyst not active (`ERR_CATALYST_NOT_ACTIVE`), SAF certification specification (`ERR_SAF_SPEC_OUT_OF_RANGE`), SAF shipping certification (`ERR_SAF_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`), pathway scope match (`ERR_PATHWAY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`), batch shipped (`ERR_BATCH_NOT_SHIPPED`), a verified supplier exists for the pathway's feedstock (`ERR_NO_VERIFIED_SUPPLIER`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), revision limit (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), duplicate document (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCUMENT`), due-diligence document sufficiency (`ERR_DUE_DILIGENCE_DOCS_INSUFFICIENT`), turnaround adjacency buffer (`ERR_TURNAROUND_BUFFER_VIOLATION`), unit window exclusivity (`ERR_UNIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), catalyst change inside an approved window (`ERR_CATALYST_CHANGE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`), nomination inside the contract window (`ERR_NOMINATION_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`), campaign window shape (`ERR_INVALID_RUN_WINDOW`), campaign window elapsed (`ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`), hedge maturity in the future (`ERR_MATURITY_IN_PAST`), at least one reserved allocation (`ERR_NO_FEEDSTOCK_ALLOCATED`), pretreatment output mass (`ERR_PRETREATMENT_MASS_EXCEEDED`), allocation mass fits the lot (`ERR_LOT_MASS_EXCEEDED`), minimum charge met (`ERR_MIN_CHARGE_NOT_MET`), daily nameplate capacity (`ERR_UNIT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), SAF share limit (`ERR_SAF_SHARE_EXCEEDED`), hydrogen coverage (`ERR_HYDROGEN_COVERAGE_SHORTFALL`), contract aggregate hydrogen capacity (`ERR_HYDROGEN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), mass balance (`ERR_MASS_BALANCE_VIOLATION`), retirement size (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS`) and, last of all, retirement reversal requires a retired balance (`ERR_NOTHING_TO_REVERSE`). The condition behind each of these is stated in §3 beside the workflow it guards; the sequence above is what decides which one a client actually sees.

### 2.8. Interval Conventions

The service mixes half-open and closed intervals deliberately, and confusing the two produces off-by-one-day errors that are invisible until a regulator audits them.

**Half-open, written as a start that is included and an end that is excluded.** Turnaround windows use `start_at` inclusive and `end_at` exclusive; a catalyst installation instant falling exactly on a window's `end_at` is therefore *outside* that window. Campaign windows use `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive; two campaigns on the same unit where one ends at the exact instant the other begins do **not** intersect and are both allowed, because there is no buffer between campaigns.

**Closed, both endpoints included.** Hydrogen supply contract availability runs from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive, and a hydrogen nomination's `start_at` through `end_at` are likewise both inclusive. A nomination whose `end_at` is `2026-09-10T00:00:00Z` still covers the calendar day 2026-09-10 and therefore collides on that day with a nomination starting at the same instant.

**Calendar days.** Hydrogen coverage and hydrogen capacity are both reasoned about one UTC calendar date at a time. A nomination covers a date when its closed interval touches any part of that date — that is, when its `start_at` is not after the last instant of the date and its `end_at` is not before the first instant of the date. Campaign windows are additionally required to be whole midnight-aligned days, which makes "every day of the campaign window" an unambiguous set of dates.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Three operations in this service reach beyond the resource named in the path and correct dependent state in the same request. Each is described in full where it belongs in §3, but three rules govern all of them.

First, cascades run to completion inside the request that triggers them. There is no deferred sweep and no eventual convergence; if the response is 200, every dependent row has already been corrected.

Second, every row a cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. A client holding a campaign's `version` from before a supplier suspension will therefore find its `expected_run_version` stale — that is the intended serialization point between compliance action and trading action.

Third, wherever a cascade processes a set of sibling rows, it processes them in `created_at` ascending order. This matters when the effect on a shared parent depends on accumulation: quarantining a supplier's lots, releasing the allocations under those lots, recomputing the campaigns that lost mass, and blocking the sibling credit blocks on a suspended pathway are all ordered by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Two ledgers in this system must balance exactly, and several endpoints exist purely as the compensating half of a conservation rule.

**Feedstock mass.** For any lot, the mass committed to campaigns plus the mass consumed by pretreatment plus the mass still free equals the lot's delivered mass. Concretely, `allocated_mass_mt` is the sum of the mass on that lot's allocations whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`; `pretreatment_consumed_mt` is the lot's own mass once it has been fed to pretreatment and zero otherwise; and `remaining_mass_mt` is the lot's `mass_mt` less `allocated_mass_mt` less `pretreatment_consumed_mt`, rounded to 3 decimal places. Releasing an allocation restores mass to the lot exactly; it is the compensating inverse of reserving it. Pretreatment conserves mass across the parent-child boundary too: a pretreated child lot's own `mass_mt` plus its `pretreatment_loss_mt` equals the mass the source lot recorded as consumed.

**Credits.** For any pathway registration, `credits_outstanding_mtco2e` is the sum, over the credit blocks citing that pathway whose status is `VERIFIED`, `ADJUSTED` or `RETIRED`, of each block's `verified_credits_mtco2e` less its `retired_credits_mtco2e`, rounded to 3 decimal places. Blocks in `DRAFT`, `INTERNAL_SIGNED_OFF`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFICATION_REJECTED` or `BLOCKED` contribute nothing at all. Reversing a retirement is the compensating inverse of retiring: after a reversal the pathway's outstanding balance returns to exactly its pre-retirement value.

**Hydrogen capacity.** A contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is derived, never accumulated: it is recomputed from the surviving confirmed nominations every time the nomination book changes. That is what makes cancelling a nomination restore booked capacity exactly, whether the cancellation was requested directly or produced by a cascade.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Supplier and Establishing Due Diligence

A `FeedstockTrader` opens the relationship by registering the counterparty. The registration payload carries the supplier's `legal_name`, which must be a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters; a `country_code` naming the ISO-3166 alpha-2 country of the collection or origin point, which must be exactly two uppercase letters; a non-empty `feedstock_types` array declaring which classes the supplier is approved to deliver, each member drawn from `UCO`, `TALLOW`, `DCO`, `SOYBEAN_OIL` and `CANOLA_OIL`; and optionally `is_high_risk_origin`, a boolean that defaults to `false` when omitted and that raises the traceability bar when the origin is flagged for fraud or indirect land-use risk. The new record starts with `dd_status` of `PENDING`, `dd_document_type_count` of 0, and `dd_verified_at`, `dd_expires_at` and `suspension_reason` all null.

Evidence arrives next. Traceability documents may be uploaded either by the `FeedstockTrader` on the supplier's behalf or by the supplier itself acting under the `Supplier` role, and each upload names the owning supplier through the path — the stored row records that supplier as its `supplier_id`. A document declares a `doc_type` drawn from `ORIGIN_ATTESTATION`, `CHAIN_OF_CUSTODY`, `MASS_BALANCE_STATEMENT` and `THIRD_PARTY_AUDIT`; an `issued_at` instant as printed on the artifact; a `valid_until` expiry instant that must be strictly after `issued_at`; and a `doc_hash` of 8 to 128 characters giving a tamper-evident content hash of the uploaded file. Uploads are append-only and immutable. Two uniqueness conditions apply together and both surface as `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCUMENT`: no other document already on this supplier may share the same `doc_type`, and no document anywhere in the system may already carry the same `doc_hash`. A supplier that needs to replace an expiring origin attestation therefore cannot simply upload a second one — the document class is single-slot per supplier.

Each upload immediately recomputes the supplier's `dd_document_type_count` and bumps the supplier's `version`. That count is the number of **distinct** `doc_type` values among the supplier's documents whose `valid_until` is strictly after the current logical clock. Expired evidence contributes nothing, and because expiry is measured against the logical clock, moving the clock forward past a `valid_until` silently reduces the count without any request touching the supplier. Documents may be listed for a supplier and optionally narrowed by `doc_type`.

Due-diligence verification is a compliance decision. A `ComplianceOfficer` verifies a supplier that is still `PENDING`, supplying `valid_months` — the number of months the verification should stand. Verification moves `dd_status` from `PENDING` to `VERIFIED`, sets `dd_verified_at` to the current logical clock, sets `dd_expires_at` to the current logical clock advanced by `valid_months` multiplied by 30 days (months are treated as exactly 30 days each, never as calendar months), clears `suspension_reason`, and bumps the supplier's `version`. The document bar is asymmetric and is the check behind `ERR_DUE_DILIGENCE_DOCS_INSUFFICIENT`: an ordinary-origin supplier needs `dd_document_type_count` of at least 2, while a supplier whose `is_high_risk_origin` is `true` needs at least 3 **and** must additionally hold at least one `THIRD_PARTY_AUDIT` document whose `valid_until` is strictly after the current logical clock. A high-risk supplier holding three valid document types, none of which is the third-party audit, fails this check. Attempting to verify a supplier that is not `PENDING` — one already `VERIFIED`, or one `SUSPENDED` — is a state failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`.

Suspension is the compliance desk's emergency brake and it is deliberately violent. A `ComplianceOfficer` suspends a supplier whose `dd_status` is either `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` — a never-verified supplier can be suspended exactly like a verified one — supplying a free-text `reason`. `SUSPENDED` is terminal: there is no path back, and a second suspension attempt on an already-suspended supplier is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`. The suspension fires a five-level cascade, which is the largest single blast radius in the system:

- **Level 1.** The supplier's `dd_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, `suspension_reason` records the supplied reason, and the supplier's `version` is bumped.
- **Level 2**, caused by level 1. Every feedstock lot belonging to that supplier whose status is `RECEIVED`, `TESTED`, `PRETREAT_REQUIRED`, `PRETREATED` or `ACCEPTED` transitions to `QUARANTINED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with each lot's `version` bumped. Lots already `CHARGED`, `REJECTED` or `QUARANTINED` are skipped — the cascade is conditional, not blanket, because material already in the reactor cannot be recalled and terminal dispositions do not need revisiting.
- **Level 3**, caused by level 2. For each lot quarantined at level 2, every feedstock allocation drawn from that lot whose status is `RESERVED` transitions to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`, and the lot's `allocated_mass_mt` and `remaining_mass_mt` are recomputed. Allocations already `CONSUMED` are untouched.
- **Level 4**, caused by level 3. For each campaign that lost reserved mass at level 3, processed in `created_at` ascending order, the campaign's `reserved_mass_mt`, `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `required_h2_kg_per_day` are recomputed. If that campaign was in `HYDROGEN_SECURED` and its recomputed `reserved_mass_mt` has fallen below the 100.0 metric tonne minimum charge, the campaign is downgraded `HYDROGEN_SECURED → PLANNED` and its `provisional_yield_pct`, `planned_total_product_mt`, `planned_rd_mt` and `planned_saf_mt` are all reset to 0.000.
- **Level 5**, caused by level 4. For each campaign downgraded at level 4, every hydrogen nomination booked against that campaign whose status is `CONFIRMED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, and each affected hydrogen supply contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is recomputed over the nominations that survive.

Every row touched at any level has its `version` incremented and `updated_at` refreshed. A trading desk that had reserved feedstock against next week's campaign discovers the correction by finding its cached campaign version stale on the next allocation attempt.

### 3.2. Receiving Feedstock, Testing It and Pretreating It

A delivered lot is booked by the `FeedstockTrader` at the plant gate. The payload cites the delivering supplier through `supplier_id`, declares the `feedstock_type`, and records `mass_mt` — the delivered mass in metric tonnes, which must be greater than 0, no greater than 20000, and expressed to at most 3 decimal places. It also records `attested_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, the supplier-attested carbon intensity in grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule, at least 0 and at most 120 with at most 2 decimal places; `purchase_price_usd_per_mt`, the contracted price per metric tonne, greater than 0 and no greater than 5000 with at most 2 decimal places; and `received_at`, the logical-clock instant the material crossed the gate. The declared `feedstock_type` must appear in the cited supplier's `feedstock_types`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_FEEDSTOCK_TYPE_NOT_SUPPLIED`. A newly booked lot starts at status `RECEIVED` with `pretreatment_tier` of `NONE`, `pretreatment_fee_usd` of 0.00, `pretreatment_loss_mt` and `pretreatment_consumed_mt` of 0.000, `allocated_mass_mt` of 0.000, `remaining_mass_mt` equal to its `mass_mt`, and `source_lot_id` null. Lots may be listed and filtered by `supplier_id`, by `source_lot_id`, by `feedstock_type` and by `status`; filtering on `source_lot_id` is how a caller finds the pretreated child lots derived from a given raw lot.

The laboratory then samples the material. A `QualityAnalyst` appends a quality test to a lot whose status is `RECEIVED`, `TESTED` or `PRETREAT_REQUIRED`, and the append must carry `expected_lot_version` as described in §2.6. The test itself records `ffa_pct` — free fatty acid content as a weight percent, at least 0, at most 100, to 2 decimal places; `moisture_pct` — water content as a weight percent, at least 0, at most 20, to 2 decimal places; `insolubles_ppm` — total insoluble impurities as a whole number of parts per million, at least 0 and at most 50000; `phosphorus_ppm` — phosphorus as a gum and phospholipid proxy, a whole number of parts per million, at least 0 and at most 2000; and `sampled_at`, the logical-clock instant the sample was drawn. The stored row also carries the identifier of the lot the sample came from as `lot_id`. Appending a test to a lot in any other status — an `ACCEPTED`, `PRETREATED`, `REJECTED`, `CHARGED` or `QUARANTINED` lot — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`.

**How a test becomes a tier and a fee.** The pretreatment assessment proceeds in five ordered steps, and the boundaries are all *at least* comparisons rather than strict ones.

1. Four independent sub-tiers are computed and stored on the test row. `ffa_tier_index` is 2 when `ffa_pct` is at least 5.00, otherwise 1 when it is at least 2.00, otherwise 0. `moisture_tier_index` is 2 when `moisture_pct` is at least 1.00, otherwise 1 when it is at least 0.50, otherwise 0. `insolubles_tier_index` is 2 when `insolubles_ppm` is at least 1500, otherwise 1 when it is at least 500, otherwise 0. `phosphorus_tier_index` is 2 when `phosphorus_ppm` is at least 20, otherwise 1 when it is at least 10, otherwise 0.
2. Outright rejection dominates everything that follows. If `ffa_pct` is greater than 15.00, or `moisture_pct` is greater than 3.00, or `insolubles_ppm` is greater than 5000, or `phosphorus_ppm` is greater than 500, the test's `disposition` is `REJECT`, its `tier` is recorded as `HEAVY`, the quoted fee is 0.00, and the remaining steps are not evaluated. Note that these rejection thresholds are strict: an `ffa_pct` of exactly 15.00 does not reject.
3. Otherwise the worst of the four sub-tiers governs — the tier index is the largest of `ffa_tier_index`, `moisture_tier_index`, `insolubles_tier_index` and `phosphorus_tier_index`.
4. A waste-feedstock escalation branch then applies. When the lot's `feedstock_type` is one of the three waste classes — `UCO`, `TALLOW` or `DCO` — and the test's `phosphorus_ppm` is at least 10, the governing tier index is raised by one, but never above 2. Virgin vegetable oils (`SOYBEAN_OIL` and `CANOLA_OIL`) are exempt from this escalation entirely. The resulting index of 0, 1 or 2 maps to a `tier` of `NONE`, `LIGHT` or `HEAVY` respectively, and the `disposition` is `PASS` when the index is 0 and `PRETREAT` otherwise. The escalation is what makes a nominally clean waste lot with 12 ppm phosphorus arrive at `LIGHT` rather than `NONE`.
5. The fee is then quoted. A tier index of 0 quotes 0.00. Otherwise the linear term is the per-tonne base rate for the governing tier — 18.50 US dollars per metric tonne for `LIGHT` and 42.75 for `HEAVY` — multiplied by the lot's `mass_mt`, multiplied by a feedstock-type multiplier of 1.20 for `UCO`, 1.35 for `TALLOW`, 1.10 for `DCO`, and 1.00 for both `SOYBEAN_OIL` and `CANOLA_OIL`. That linear term is then raised to a floor of 250.00 US dollars if it falls below it, capped at 45000.00 US dollars if it exceeds that, and finally rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The floor is applied before the cap, and both bound the linear term rather than the rounded output.

The assessment reads only the single test being appended plus the owning lot's `feedstock_type` and `mass_mt`. Which test governs the lot at any later moment is a separate question, answered by the **governing test** rule: among all tests on the lot, the governing test is the one with the greatest `sampled_at`; if two or more tie on `sampled_at`, the one with the greatest `created_at` wins. A lot with no tests at all has no governing test and its `pretreatment_tier` stays `NONE`. An earlier `sampled_at` never governs merely because it was appended later — a laboratory back-filling yesterday's sample does not overturn today's disposition.

The appended test's disposition drives the lot's status and its derived pretreatment fields, and this is where retesting becomes visible. A `PASS` moves the lot to `TESTED`, sets `pretreatment_tier` to `NONE` and `pretreatment_fee_usd` to 0.00. A `PRETREAT` moves the lot to `PRETREAT_REQUIRED`, sets `pretreatment_tier` to the test's `LIGHT` or `HEAVY`, and sets `pretreatment_fee_usd` to the quoted fee. A `REJECT` moves the lot to `REJECTED`, sets `pretreatment_tier` to `HEAVY`, sets `pretreatment_fee_usd` to 0.00, and zeroes `remaining_mass_mt`; `REJECTED` is terminal and such a lot can never be allocated, pretreated or accepted. Because a retest supersedes the earlier governing test, the same endpoint drives `TESTED → PRETREAT_REQUIRED` when a later sample dispositions `PRETREAT`, and `PRETREAT_REQUIRED → TESTED` when a later sample dispositions `PASS` — in the latter case `pretreatment_tier` returns to `NONE` and the fee returns to 0.00. Every one of these appends bumps the lot's `version`. Tests may be listed for a lot and narrowed by `disposition`.

Acceptance is the gate to the reactor. A `QualityAnalyst` accepts a lot whose status is exactly `TESTED` **and** whose `pretreatment_tier` is `NONE`, moving it `TESTED → ACCEPTED` and bumping its `version`. Only an `ACCEPTED` lot may be allocated to a campaign. Accepting a lot in any other status — a `RECEIVED` lot that has never been tested, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`.

Pretreatment is how a lot that failed on quality is rescued. A `QualityAnalyst` pretreats a lot whose status is `PRETREAT_REQUIRED` and whose `pretreatment_tier` is `LIGHT` or `HEAVY`, supplying `output_mass_mt` — the mass that survived the process. That figure must be greater than 0 and no greater than the source lot's `mass_mt`; a zero or over-large value is refused with `ERR_PRETREATMENT_MASS_EXCEEDED`. Pretreating a lot in any other status, such as a `TESTED` lot, is an `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE` failure. The operation does two things at once. The source lot moves `PRETREAT_REQUIRED → PRETREATED`, its `pretreatment_consumed_mt` is set to its own `mass_mt`, its `pretreatment_loss_mt` is set to its `mass_mt` less `output_mass_mt` rounded to 3 decimal places, its `remaining_mass_mt` becomes 0.000, and its `version` is bumped. And a **child lot** is created and returned, carrying `source_lot_id` pointing at the source lot, the same `supplier_id`, the same `feedstock_type`, the same `received_at`, a `mass_mt` and `remaining_mass_mt` equal to `output_mass_mt`, a status of `ACCEPTED` (a pretreated lot is already fit to charge and needs no separate acceptance), a `pretreatment_tier` of `NONE` and a `pretreatment_fee_usd` of 0.00.

Two of the child's numbers are worth reading twice. Its `attested_ci_gco2e_per_mj` is the source lot's attested carbon intensity plus a pretreatment carbon adder determined by the *source* lot's tier — 1.50 grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule for a `LIGHT` source and 4.00 for a `HEAVY` source, and 0.00 for a source at `NONE`. Because the child's own tier is `NONE`, the blend calculation described in §3.5 adds no second adder for it: the pretreatment penalty is baked into the child's attested figure exactly once. And its `purchase_price_usd_per_mt` rolls the pretreatment fee into the cost basis — it is the source lot's `mass_mt` multiplied by the source lot's `purchase_price_usd_per_mt`, plus the source lot's `pretreatment_fee_usd`, all divided by `output_mass_mt`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Losing mass to pretreatment therefore raises the unit cost of what survives, which is the economically correct outcome. Mass is conserved across the boundary: the child's `mass_mt` plus its `pretreatment_loss_mt` equals the source lot's `pretreatment_consumed_mt`.

### 3.3. The Hydrotreater: Units, Catalyst Charges and Turnaround Planning

A `PlantOperator` registers each hydrotreating unit with a `name` — the unit tag such as "HDT-1", 1 to 40 characters and unique across the plant — a `nameplate_capacity_mt_per_day` giving the maximum feed rate in metric tonnes per day, greater than 0 and no greater than 6000 with at most 3 decimal places, and a `max_saf_share_pct` giving the maximum share of product mass this unit may route to sustainable aviation fuel, at least 0 and at most 60 with at most 2 decimal places. A fresh unit starts at status `IDLE` with `active_catalyst_charge_id` null and `requires_turnaround` `false`. Units may be listed and filtered by `status`, by `active_catalyst_charge_id` — which resolves a catalyst charge back to the unit currently running it — and by `requires_turnaround`.

Turnaround windows are how the plant books downtime. A window declares its `scope`, drawn from `CATALYST_CHANGE`, `INSPECTION` and `MAJOR`; a `start_at` that is the inclusive first instant of the outage; and an `end_at` that is the exclusive end and must be strictly after `start_at`. The window records the unit it takes down as its `unit_id` and is created at status `PLANNED`. Before it is accepted, the proposed interval is tested for adjacency against every other window on the same unit that is neither `CANCELLED` nor `COMPLETED`: there must be at least 72 hours — three full days — of clear time on **both** sides of the proposed interval. A proposal whose `start_at` falls exactly on an existing window's `end_at` has zero hours of clearance and is refused with `ERR_TURNAROUND_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, as is anything else landing inside the buffer on either side. Cancelled and completed windows are invisible to this test. Windows may be listed per unit and filtered by `status` and `scope`.

Approval, start and completion walk the window through its life. A `PlantOperator` approves a `PLANNED` window, moving it `PLANNED → APPROVED` and bumping its `version`; approval is refused when any campaign on that unit whose status is `PLANNED`, `HYDROGEN_SECURED` or `RUNNING` has a campaign interval that intersects the window's interval, both compared as half-open intervals. Starting an `APPROVED` window requires that the current logical clock be at or after the window's `start_at` and strictly before its `end_at`, and that the unit be `IDLE`; the window moves `APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS` while the unit moves `IDLE → DOWN_TURNAROUND`, and both versions are bumped. A unit with a running campaign cannot be taken down — that attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. Completing an `IN_PROGRESS` window moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and returns the unit `DOWN_TURNAROUND → IDLE`, bumping both versions; additionally, `requires_turnaround` is cleared to `false` if and only if the unit's `active_catalyst_charge_id` now points at a charge whose status is `ACTIVE`, and is otherwise left as it stands — finishing an inspection does not pretend a spent catalyst is fresh. A window that is still `PLANNED` or already `APPROVED` may be cancelled with a `reason`, moving it to `CANCELLED` and bumping its `version`; a `CANCELLED` window stops participating in the 72-hour buffer test, stops blocking campaign windows, and no longer authorizes a catalyst change. Starting a `PLANNED` window, or cancelling a `COMPLETED` one, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`.

Catalyst charges are installed into a unit by a `PlantOperator`, who supplies `design_throughput_mt` — the end-of-run design cumulative feed throughput for the charge in metric tonnes, greater than 0 and no greater than 2000000 with at most 3 decimal places — and `installed_at`, the logical-clock instant the charge went in. The stored charge records the receiving unit as its `unit_id`. The `installed_at` instant must fall inside a turnaround window on that unit whose `scope` is `CATALYST_CHANGE` and whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, using the half-open interval convention — an `installed_at` exactly at such a window's `end_at` is outside it. A failure here is `ERR_CATALYST_CHANGE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. A new charge starts with status `ACTIVE`, `cumulative_throughput_mt` of 0.000 and `activity_index` of 1.0000, becomes the unit's `active_catalyst_charge_id`, clears the unit's `requires_turnaround` to `false`, and bumps the unit's `version`. Charges may be listed per unit and filtered by `status`.

When the unit already carries a charge in `ACTIVE`, `DERATED` or `SPENT`, installing a successor is a swap and the unit must be `DOWN_TURNAROUND` at that moment; the outgoing charge — which must be the unit's current `active_catalyst_charge_id` — moves to `REMOVED` and has its `version` bumped. All three outgoing states behave identically: `SPENT → REMOVED` at the end of a normal run life, `DERATED → REMOVED` when a variance-derated charge is swapped out, and `ACTIVE → REMOVED` when a healthy charge is changed early are the same operation with the same side effects. `REMOVED` is terminal. The very first charge on a fresh unit that carries no charge at all needs only the approved catalyst-change window and does not require the unit to be down.

**Catalyst activity decay.** A charge's `activity_index` is a multiplier on yield and it is derived, never stored incrementally. Its `cumulative_throughput_mt` is the sum of `consumed_mass_mt` over every campaign on that charge whose status is `COMPLETED` or `RECONCILED`; campaigns still `PLANNED`, `HYDROGEN_SECURED` or `RUNNING`, and campaigns that were `ABORTED`, contribute nothing. The `activity_index` is then 1.0000 reduced by 0.0125 for every thousand metric tonnes of cumulative throughput — that is, 1.0000 less the product of 0.0125 and the cumulative throughput divided by 1000 — with the result held at a floor of 0.6000 if the arithmetic would take it lower, and rounded half up to 4 decimal places. A charge that has processed 40,000 metric tonnes therefore sits at 0.5000 by raw arithmetic and is reported as the floor value 0.6000. Both `cumulative_throughput_mt` and `activity_index` are refreshed whenever a campaign on the charge completes, as described in §3.7.

### 3.4. Hydrogen Supply Contracts and the Daily Nomination Book

Hydrotreating consumes hydrogen, and hydrogen is bought ahead. A `FeedstockTrader` books a supply contract naming the `vendor_name` — the industrial-gas vendor's legal name, 1 to 120 characters — a `daily_capacity_kg` giving the maximum kilograms the vendor will deliver on any single calendar day, greater than 0 and no greater than 500000 with at most 1 decimal place, a `price_usd_per_kg` greater than 0 and no greater than 50 with at most 2 decimal places, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the period the contract may be nominated against, and `hydrogen_ci_gco2e_per_mj` recording the carbon intensity of the delivered hydrogen, at least 0 and at most 200 with at most 2 decimal places. Both window bounds are **inclusive** calendar-day bounds, unlike the half-open windows used for turnarounds and campaigns. A new contract starts with `peak_committed_kg_per_day` of 0.0.

A `PlantOperator` books volume against a contract by filing a nomination. The nomination names the campaign it supplies through `run_id`, gives the inclusive delivery interval as `start_at` and `end_at` — `end_at` must be at or after `start_at` — and states `kg_per_day`, the kilograms per calendar day nominated across that whole interval, greater than 0 and no greater than 500000 with at most 1 decimal place. The stored row records the contract it draws on as `contract_id` and starts at status `CONFIRMED`. The nomination's closed interval must lie entirely inside the contract's closed window; a `start_at` one day before the contract's `window_start`, or an `end_at` earlier than `start_at`, is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`. Nominations may be listed either per contract or per campaign, in both cases filterable by `status`.

**Committed capacity, day by day.** A contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is the largest single-day commitment anywhere in its window. For a given UTC calendar date, the committed figure is the sum of `kg_per_day` over that contract's `CONFIRMED` nominations whose closed interval touches that date — a nomination touches a date when its `start_at` is not after the last instant of the date and its `end_at` is not before the first instant of the date. The `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is the maximum of that daily figure taken over every calendar date from the contract's `window_start` through its `window_end` inclusive, rounded half up to 1 decimal place, and it is 0.0 when the contract holds no `CONFIRMED` nomination at all. `CANCELLED` nominations are excluded from the sum, which is precisely what makes cancellation restore booked capacity exactly.

Admission of a proposed nomination uses the same daily arithmetic. For **every** calendar date the proposal touches, the already-committed figure for that date plus the proposal's own `kg_per_day` must not exceed the contract's `daily_capacity_kg`. A single overloaded shared day is enough to refuse the whole nomination with `ERR_HYDROGEN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because both nomination endpoints are inclusive, a nomination ending at midnight on the tenth still competes for capacity on the tenth against one starting at that same instant. When a nomination is admitted, the contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is recomputed and the contract's `version` is bumped.

Cancelling a nomination is the compensating half of booking one. A `PlantOperator` cancels a nomination whose status is `CONFIRMED` and whose supplied campaign is still `PLANNED` or `HYDROGEN_SECURED`; the nomination moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, the parent contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is recomputed over the surviving `CONFIRMED` nominations, and both the nomination's and the contract's `version` are bumped. Cancelling the same nomination twice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOMINATION_STATE`. Cancellation can also demote the campaign it supplied: if that campaign was in `HYDROGEN_SECURED` and the nominations that remain no longer cover every calendar day of the campaign window at the campaign's `required_h2_kg_per_day`, the campaign is downgraded `HYDROGEN_SECURED → PLANNED` and its `provisional_yield_pct`, `planned_total_product_mt`, `planned_rd_mt` and `planned_saf_mt` are reset to 0.000. Note the asymmetry with the supplier-suspension and allocation-release paths: those two cancel the campaign's remaining nominations as a further cascade level, whereas a directly requested nomination cancellation does not go on to cancel its siblings.

Nominations are also cancelled by the system, without any request naming them, as level 5 of the supplier-suspension cascade described in §3.1 and as part of aborting a campaign as described in §3.7. In both cases the effect on the contract is identical: `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is recomputed and both the nomination's and the contract's `version` are bumped.

### 3.5. Planning a Campaign: Window, Feedstock Allocation and Hydrogen Securement

A processing campaign is the unit of production accounting. A `PlantOperator` creates one by naming the unit it will run on through `unit_id` and the catalyst charge it will run on through `catalyst_charge_id`, declaring the campaign interval through `window_start` and `window_end`, and setting `target_saf_share_pct` — the planned share of product mass to be routed to sustainable aviation fuel, at least 0 and at most 60 with at most 2 decimal places.

Three conditions gate creation. The campaign interval must be well shaped: both `window_start` and `window_end` must fall exactly on midnight UTC, `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, and the gap between them must be a whole number of days. A `window_start` at 06:00:00Z, or a `window_end` equal to `window_start`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_WINDOW`. The interval must not collide on that unit: treated as a half-open interval, it must not intersect the interval of any other campaign on the same unit whose status is `PLANNED`, `HYDROGEN_SECURED` or `RUNNING`, nor any turnaround window on that unit whose status is `PLANNED`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Touching endpoints do not intersect — a campaign that ends at the exact instant another begins is fine, because unlike turnaround windows there is no buffer between campaigns — but a genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_UNIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the cited catalyst charge must be `ACTIVE`; citing a `REMOVED`, `SPENT` or `DERATED` charge is refused with `ERR_CATALYST_NOT_ACTIVE`. Separately, `target_saf_share_pct` may not exceed the unit's `max_saf_share_pct`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_SAF_SHARE_EXCEEDED`.

A newly created campaign is `PLANNED`. Its `run_days` is the whole number of days between `window_start` and `window_end`. Its `reserved_mass_mt` and `consumed_mass_mt` are 0.000; `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `required_h2_kg_per_day` are null, because a campaign with no counted allocation has no blend and is feeding nothing — these two fields are null rather than zero whenever no allocation counts toward them. `provisional_yield_pct`, `planned_total_product_mt`, `planned_rd_mt`, `planned_saf_mt`, `final_yield_pct`, `yield_variance_pct` and `feedstock_cost_usd` are unstamped at creation and become populated only at the securement, completion and reconciliation steps described below; `yield_variance_flag` is `false`, `measured_rd_mt` and `measured_saf_mt` are 0.000 until the operator reports them, and `abort_reason` is null. Campaigns may be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, by `catalyst_charge_id` and by `status`.

Allocation is the trading desk's job, not the plant floor's. A `FeedstockTrader` commits material to a campaign by citing the lot through `lot_id`, the mass through `mass_mt` — greater than 0, no greater than 20000, at most 3 decimal places — and the campaign's concurrency token through `expected_run_version`. The new allocation records the campaign it serves as its `run_id`, starts at status `RESERVED`, and carries `line_cost_usd` unset and `release_reason` null. Its effect is immediate and four-sided: the mass is added to the lot's `allocated_mass_mt` and subtracted from the lot's `remaining_mass_mt` with the lot's `version` bumped; it is added to the campaign's `reserved_mass_mt`; the campaign's `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `required_h2_kg_per_day` are recomputed; and the campaign's `version` is bumped. Allocations may be listed either per campaign or per lot, filterable by `status` in both cases.

Allocation carries the densest stack of checks in the system, and their order decides which error a client sees. After the global rungs of §2.7 — role, path existence, the existence of the cited `lot_id`, body shape, then the `expected_run_version` concurrency check, then the campaign's own state — the domain conditions are evaluated in exactly this sequence:

1. The supplier behind the cited lot must have `dd_status` of `VERIFIED`. A lot from a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` supplier may not feed the reactor: `ERR_SUPPLIER_DUE_DILIGENCE_INCOMPLETE`.
2. That supplier's `dd_expires_at` must be strictly after the current logical clock. A verification that has lapsed — typically because the clock was advanced past it — yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_DUE_DILIGENCE_EXPIRED`. This is checked *after* the status check, so a suspended supplier whose verification also expired reports the incomplete-diligence error.
3. The cited lot must not be awaiting pretreatment. A lot at `PRETREAT_REQUIRED` yields `ERR_PRETREATMENT_REQUIRED`, which names the tier still owed.
4. The cited lot's status must be exactly `ACCEPTED`. A `RECEIVED`, `TESTED`, `PRETREATED`, `REJECTED`, `CHARGED` or `QUARANTINED` lot yields `ERR_LOT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Because the pretreatment check sits one rung higher, a `PRETREAT_REQUIRED` lot never reports this error.
5. The requested `mass_mt` must be no greater than the lot's `remaining_mass_mt`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_MASS_EXCEEDED`. This includes the second allocation that overdraws what the first one left behind.

Allocations that are still `RESERVED` may be released by a `FeedstockTrader` while the campaign is `PLANNED` or `HYDROGEN_SECURED`. Release sets `release_reason` to `MANUAL`, restores the mass to the lot (lowering `allocated_mass_mt`, raising `remaining_mass_mt`), lowers the campaign's `reserved_mass_mt`, recomputes the campaign's `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `required_h2_kg_per_day` over the allocations that still count — both returning to null when none remain — and bumps the allocation's, the lot's and the campaign's `version`. Releasing an allocation that is already `CONSUMED` because the campaign started, or one that is already `RELEASED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`. Release can also demote the campaign: if the campaign was `HYDROGEN_SECURED` and its recomputed `reserved_mass_mt` has fallen below the 100.0 metric tonne minimum charge, it is downgraded `HYDROGEN_SECURED → PLANNED`, its `provisional_yield_pct`, `planned_total_product_mt`, `planned_rd_mt` and `planned_saf_mt` are reset to 0.000, and every `CONFIRMED` hydrogen nomination on that campaign transitions to `CANCELLED` with each affected contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` recomputed. Allocations also reach `RELEASED` by two system paths — with `release_reason` of `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` at level 3 of the supplier-suspension cascade, and with `release_reason` of `RUN_ABORTED` when the campaign is aborted — and in both cases the lot and campaign mass figures are recomputed the same way.

**The blend carbon intensity.** A campaign's `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` is the mass-weighted average carbon intensity of what is committed to it. The counted lines are the campaign's allocations whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`; `RELEASED` lines are excluded, which is exactly why a release moves the blend. When no line counts, the blend is null rather than 0.00. Otherwise, for each counted line, multiply the line's `mass_mt` by the sum of its lot's `attested_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and a pretreatment carbon adder chosen by that lot's own `pretreatment_tier` — 0.00 for `NONE`, 1.50 for `LIGHT`, 4.00 for `HEAVY`. Add those products together without rounding anything along the way, divide by the total counted `mass_mt`, and round the single final quotient half up to 2 decimal places. Recall from §3.2 that a pretreated child lot sits at tier `NONE` and already carries its adder inside its attested figure, so the adder is never applied twice to the same material.

**The hydrogen requirement.** A campaign's `required_h2_kg_per_day` is derived from the same counted lines — allocations at `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED` — and is null when there are none, because nothing is being fed. Otherwise three quantities are formed. First, the total counted mass. Second, a mass-weighted hydrogen intensity: for each counted line multiply its `mass_mt` by the hydrogen intensity of its lot's `feedstock_type` — 32.0 kilograms of hydrogen per metric tonne for `UCO`, 30.0 for `TALLOW`, 34.0 for `DCO`, 38.0 for `SOYBEAN_OIL` and 36.0 for `CANOLA_OIL` — sum those products, and divide by the total counted mass, keeping this quotient unrounded. Third, the daily feed rate: the total counted mass divided by the campaign's `run_days`, and this one **is rounded half up to 3 decimal places before it is used**. The requirement is then the rounded daily feed rate multiplied by the unrounded weighted hydrogen intensity, multiplied by one plus twelve hundredths of the campaign's `target_saf_share_pct` expressed as a fraction — that is, aviation-fuel severity adds 0.12 of the SAF share, so a campaign targeting 50 percent SAF carries a 6 percent hydrogen uplift — with the final figure rounded half up to 1 decimal place. Rounding the daily feed rate only at the end produces a different kilogram figure and is wrong.

**Securing hydrogen** is the gate between planning and production. A `PlantOperator` secures a campaign that is `PLANNED`; the request carries no body. The checks, in ascending order of evaluation, are: the campaign must be `PLANNED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`); the cited catalyst charge must still be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CATALYST_NOT_ACTIVE`); the campaign must hold at least one allocation at `RESERVED`, a zero-cardinality guard checked ahead of any mass threshold and reported as `ERR_NO_FEEDSTOCK_ALLOCATED`; the campaign's `reserved_mass_mt` must be at least the minimum charge of 100.0 metric tonnes, so a single allocation of 99.999 metric tonnes fails with `ERR_MIN_CHARGE_NOT_MET`; the `reserved_mass_mt` divided by `run_days` and rounded half up to 3 decimal places must be no greater than the unit's `nameplate_capacity_mt_per_day`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the campaign's `target_saf_share_pct` must be no greater than the unit's `max_saf_share_pct`; and for **every** calendar day in the campaign's half-open window, the sum of `kg_per_day` over the `CONFIRMED` nominations booked against that campaign whose closed interval covers that day must be at least the campaign's `required_h2_kg_per_day`. A nomination covering only part of the window, or covering every day at too low a rate, is refused with `ERR_HYDROGEN_COVERAGE_SHORTFALL`.

Securement moves the campaign `PLANNED → HYDROGEN_SECURED`, recomputes `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `required_h2_kg_per_day`, bumps the campaign's `version`, and stamps the forward-looking production plan. That plan is the **provisional** variant of the yield split. It reads the allocations at `RESERVED` and the catalyst's `activity_index` as of this very instant. First a blend yield is formed, unrounded: for each counted line multiply its `mass_mt` by the mass yield percentage of its lot's `feedstock_type` — 82.0 for `UCO`, 80.0 for `TALLOW`, 83.0 for `DCO`, 85.0 for `SOYBEAN_OIL`, 84.0 for `CANOLA_OIL` — sum the products and divide by the total counted mass. Then `provisional_yield_pct` is that blend yield multiplied by the charge's `activity_index`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. `planned_total_product_mt` is the campaign's `reserved_mass_mt` multiplied by `provisional_yield_pct` as a percentage — that is, divided by 100 — rounded to 3 decimal places. `planned_saf_mt` is `planned_total_product_mt` multiplied by `target_saf_share_pct` as a fraction of 100, multiplied by an aviation-fuel conversion penalty of 0.94, rounded to 3 decimal places. `planned_rd_mt` is `planned_total_product_mt` less `planned_saf_mt`, rounded to 3 decimal places. The provisional variant never reads a measured mass.

A secured campaign is not irreversibly secured. Three separate paths downgrade it back to `PLANNED` — level 4 of the supplier-suspension cascade, a manual allocation release, and a hydrogen nomination cancellation — whenever, after the recompute, either its `reserved_mass_mt` has fallen below the 100.0 metric tonne minimum charge or the surviving `CONFIRMED` nominations no longer cover every day of the window at `required_h2_kg_per_day`. In all three cases the four planned figures reset to 0.000 and the campaign's `version` is bumped. The suspension-cascade and allocation-release paths additionally cancel every remaining `CONFIRMED` nomination on the campaign and recompute each affected contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day`; the nomination-cancellation path does not, since the client is already managing the nomination book directly. This back edge is what makes the campaign lifecycle cyclic: `PLANNED → HYDROGEN_SECURED → PLANNED`.

### 3.6. Running the Campaign: Start, Completion, Reconciliation and Abort

Starting a campaign advances it `HYDROGEN_SECURED → RUNNING` and is the moment material physically leaves the tank farm. A `PlantOperator` may start a campaign only when it is `HYDROGEN_SECURED`, the unit is `IDLE`, the cited catalyst charge is still `ACTIVE`, the current logical clock is at or after the campaign's `window_start`, and no turnaround window on that unit is `IN_PROGRESS`. A unit that is `RUNNING` or `DOWN_TURNAROUND` yields `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`; a charge that has decayed to `SPENT` since securement yields `ERR_CATALYST_NOT_ACTIVE`; a campaign still `PLANNED` yields `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`.

The start has four visible effects. Every allocation on the campaign at `RESERVED` transitions to `CONSUMED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with each allocation's `version` bumped. The campaign's `reserved_mass_mt` becomes 0.000 and its `consumed_mass_mt` is recomputed from the newly consumed lines; note that a lot's `allocated_mass_mt` and `remaining_mass_mt` are **unchanged** by this, because both `RESERVED` and `CONSUMED` count as allocated. Each affected lot is re-examined: a lot whose `remaining_mass_mt` is now 0.000 moves `ACCEPTED → CHARGED`, which is terminal, while a lot with mass still free stays `ACCEPTED` and remains available to other campaigns; either way the lot's `version` is bumped. And the unit moves `IDLE → RUNNING`, with the unit's and the campaign's `version` both bumped.

Completion is where the plan meets the meter. A `PlantOperator` completes a `RUNNING` campaign by reporting `measured_rd_mt` and `measured_saf_mt`, the measured renewable-diesel and sustainable-aviation-fuel product masses in metric tonnes. Three conditions are evaluated in this order after the campaign's state has been confirmed: the current logical clock must be at or after the campaign's `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`; when the two measured masses sum to more than zero, the measured SAF share — `measured_saf_mt` divided by the sum of the two measured masses, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to 2 decimal places — must be no greater than the unit's `max_saf_share_pct`, otherwise `ERR_SAF_SHARE_EXCEEDED`; and the sum of the two measured masses, rounded to 3 decimal places, must be no greater than the campaign's `consumed_mass_mt`, otherwise `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_VIOLATION`. You cannot make more product than you fed. When all three hold, the campaign moves `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, storing the two measured masses.

Completion stamps the **final** variant of the yield split, which is a deliberately different calculation from the provisional one. Where the provisional variant looked forward over `RESERVED` mass and multiplied by catalyst activity, the final variant ignores the `activity_index` and the aviation-fuel conversion penalty entirely and simply divides measured reality by consumed reality: `final_yield_pct` is the sum of `measured_rd_mt` and `measured_saf_mt`, divided by `consumed_mass_mt`, expressed as a percentage, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. Allocations released before the start are absent from `consumed_mass_mt` and therefore absent from the denominator. `yield_variance_pct` is `final_yield_pct` less `provisional_yield_pct`, rounded to 3 decimal places, and `yield_variance_flag` is set when the absolute value of that variance is strictly greater than the 2.000 percentage-point tolerance. For identical feedstock the two variants disagree whenever the catalyst's assumed activity differs from measured performance, and that disagreement is exactly what the variance measures.

The completion then reaches into the catalyst charge. Its `cumulative_throughput_mt` is recomputed and its `activity_index` re-derived as described in §3.3. If the recomputed `cumulative_throughput_mt` has reached the charge's `design_throughput_mt` — at or above it — the charge transitions to `SPENT` and the owning unit's `requires_turnaround` is set to `true`, signalling that a catalyst-change outage must be scheduled; this applies identically whether the charge was `ACTIVE → SPENT` or `DERATED → SPENT`, and in both cases the charge's `version` is bumped. Separately, when `yield_variance_flag` is `true` **and** the charge is still `ACTIVE` after the throughput effect has been applied, the charge is derated: `ACTIVE → DERATED`, the charge's `version` is bumped, and as a further consequence the owning unit's `requires_turnaround` is set to `true` with the unit's `version` bumped. The ordering matters and the interaction is one-way: a charge that the throughput effect has already moved to `SPENT` is **not** pulled back down to `DERATED`, because `SPENT` is the more advanced condition.

Product is created in the same request. One product batch with `product_type` of `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` is created when `measured_rd_mt` is greater than zero, and one with `product_type` of `SAF` is created when `measured_saf_mt` is greater than zero; a campaign that produced only diesel emits exactly one batch. Each batch records the campaign that produced it as its `run_id` and carries the corresponding measured mass as its own `mass_mt`. Its `volume_gal` is that `mass_mt` multiplied by a per-product conversion factor — 308.0 gallons per metric tonne for `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` and 322.0 for `SAF` — rounded half up to 1 decimal place. Both dimensions of a new batch start at their initial values: `cert_status` is `UNCERTIFIED` and `distribution_status` is `IN_TANK`, with `astm_annex`, `freeze_point_c`, `cert_reject_reason` and `offtaker_name` all null. Finally the unit is released `RUNNING → IDLE` and the unit's and the campaign's `version` are bumped. Batches may be listed and filtered by `run_id`, `product_type`, `cert_status` and `distribution_status`.

Reconciliation belongs to accounting, not the plant floor. A `ComplianceOfficer` reconciles a `COMPLETED` campaign, moving it `COMPLETED → RECONCILED` — the terminal accounting state that freezes the campaign's yield and cost figures so credits can be claimed against them. Reconciling a campaign that is still `RUNNING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Reconciliation settles the feedstock bill, and **the rounding order here is normative**: for each of the campaign's allocations whose status is `CONSUMED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, that line's `line_cost_usd` is its `mass_mt` multiplied by its lot's `purchase_price_usd_per_mt`, multiplied by one plus a freight loading of three and a half percent — that is, by 1.035 — and rounded half up to 2 decimal places **on that line**. The campaign's `feedstock_cost_usd` is then the sum of those already-rounded line figures, rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. Summing the unrounded line products and rounding once at the end is the forbidden alternative and produces a different cent figure. Released lines never settle and keep no `line_cost_usd`. Each settled allocation has its `version` bumped, as does the campaign.

Aborting is the escape hatch and it is available from `PLANNED`, `HYDROGEN_SECURED` or `RUNNING`. A `PlantOperator` supplies a `reason`, which is stored as `abort_reason`, and the campaign moves to `ABORTED`, a terminal state. Every allocation still `RESERVED` transitions to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` of `RUN_ABORTED`, and the owning lots' `allocated_mass_mt` and `remaining_mass_mt` are restored. Every `CONFIRMED` hydrogen nomination booked against the campaign transitions to `CANCELLED` and each affected contract's `peak_committed_kg_per_day` is recomputed. The campaign's `version` is bumped. Aborting from `RUNNING` does one extra thing and pointedly omits another: the unit is returned `RUNNING → IDLE` with its `version` bumped, but allocations already `CONSUMED` are **not** released, because that material is already in the reactor and cannot be returned to the tank farm. Aborting from `PLANNED` and from `HYDROGEN_SECURED` are otherwise identical to each other.

### 3.7. Finished Product: Certification and Distribution

A product batch carries two independent status dimensions, and keeping them independent is a deliberate design choice rather than an oversight. `cert_status` tracks the ASTM certification story through `UNCERTIFIED`, `CERT_PENDING`, `CERTIFIED` and `CERT_REJECTED`. `distribution_status` tracks where the physical product is through `IN_TANK`, `RESERVED` and `SHIPPED`. Neither dimension has terminal states in common with the other; `SHIPPED` is terminal on the distribution dimension, while the certification dimension has no terminal state at all because a rejection can always be re-filed. Operations on one dimension leave the other untouched.

Certification begins with a request filed by a `ComplianceOfficer` against a batch whose `cert_status` is `UNCERTIFIED` or `CERT_REJECTED`, and only once the producing campaign has reached `COMPLETED` or `RECONCILED`. The request supplies `astm_annex`, the ASTM D7566 annex being claimed, which must be either `A2_HEFA` or `A5_HC_HEFA`, and `freeze_point_c`, the measured freeze point in degrees Celsius, at least -80 and at most 0 with at most 1 decimal place. Both values are stored on the batch, `cert_reject_reason` is cleared to null, `cert_status` moves to `CERT_PENDING`, and the batch's `version` is bumped. `distribution_status` is not consulted and not changed. Filing a request against a batch that is already `CERTIFIED` or already `CERT_PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_CERT_STATE`.

The decision belongs to the `IndependentVerifier`, never to the officer who filed the request. The decision payload carries `decision`, which must be either `CERTIFY` or `REJECT`, and optionally a `note`. A `CERTIFY` on a batch at `CERT_PENDING` moves it `CERT_PENDING → CERTIFIED` and bumps its `version` — but for a batch whose `product_type` is `SAF` two additional conditions apply: the recorded `freeze_point_c` must be at or below -40.0 degrees Celsius, and the recorded `astm_annex` must be present. A SAF batch recorded at -39.9 degrees is warmer than the limit and is refused with `ERR_SAF_SPEC_OUT_OF_RANGE`; the same code covers a missing annex. Renewable diesel is not subject to either condition. A `REJECT` on a batch at `CERT_PENDING` moves it `CERT_PENDING → CERT_REJECTED` and stores the supplied `note` as `cert_reject_reason`, bumping the batch's `version`. Deciding on a batch that is not `CERT_PENDING` — an `UNCERTIFIED` batch, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_CERT_STATE`. Because a rejected batch may be re-filed with corrected properties, which overwrites `astm_annex` and `freeze_point_c` and clears `cert_reject_reason`, the certification dimension is cyclic: `CERT_PENDING → CERT_REJECTED → CERT_PENDING`.

Distribution runs on its own track and is driven by the `ComplianceOfficer`. Reserving a batch that is `IN_TANK` records the counterparty as `offtaker_name` and moves `IN_TANK → RESERVED`, bumping the batch's `version`; this is permitted at **any** `cert_status`, so reserving uncertified aviation fuel is perfectly legal. Releasing a reservation moves `RESERVED → IN_TANK` and clears `offtaker_name` back to null, bumping the `version`. Shipping moves `RESERVED → SHIPPED` and bumps the `version`. Reserving a `SHIPPED` batch, or shipping one still `IN_TANK`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`.

Exactly one edge crosses between the two dimensions, and only for aviation fuel: a batch whose `product_type` is `SAF` may not ship unless its `cert_status` is `CERTIFIED`. A `RESERVED` SAF batch that is `UNCERTIFIED` or `CERT_REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_SAF_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Renewable diesel may ship uncertified. Shipping is the precondition for minting credits, which is the subject of the next subsection.

### 3.8. Pathway Registration and the Credit Ledger

Credits are only worth what the regulator will accept, so the plant registers a **pathway** for each combination of production unit, program and feedstock class before it claims anything. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates a registration naming the production unit through `unit_id`, the `program` — either `RFS` for the renewable fuel standard or `LCFS` for the low carbon fuel standard — the `feedstock_type` the pathway covers, and `proposed_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, the applicant-proposed carbon intensity, at least 0 and at most 120 with at most 2 decimal places. The new registration starts at `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `variance_strikes` 0, `credits_outstanding_mtco2e` 0.000, and `certified_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and `info_request` both null. Critically, the acting officer's `X-User-ID` is captured as `registered_by_user_id`; that stored value is the anchor of the segregation-of-duties rule below. Registrations may be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `program`, `feedstock_type` and `status`.

Submission moves a registration into the regulator's queue. A `ComplianceOfficer` may submit from `DRAFT` or from `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, and both paths require that at least one supplier exists whose `dd_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `dd_expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock, and whose `feedstock_types` contains the pathway's `feedstock_type`. A pathway whose only candidate suppliers are `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or expired is refused with `ERR_NO_VERIFIED_SUPPLIER` — the plant may not claim a pathway it has no lawful way to feed. Submitting from `DRAFT` moves the registration `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, clears `info_request` to null and bumps the `version`. Submitting from `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` moves it `RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED` and additionally increments `revision_round` and requires that `revision_round` be strictly below the limit of 3 before the increment; the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` while the registration remains at `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`. Submitting a `DENIED` registration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PATHWAY_STATE`.

Review belongs to the `IndependentVerifier`. Starting a review moves a `SUBMITTED` registration `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and bumps the `version`; starting a review on a `DRAFT` registration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PATHWAY_STATE`. The review decision carries `decision` plus, depending on the decision, `certified_ci` or `info_request`. A `REGISTER` decision requires `certified_ci` to be present and at least 0; that value is stored as the registration's `certified_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and the registration moves `UNDER_REVIEW → REGISTERED`. A `RETURN` decision requires a present, non-blank `info_request`, which is stored on the registration, and moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, re-entering the submission cycle. A `DENY` decision moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, which is terminal. All three bump the `version`. The cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED` may therefore run three times before the revision bound closes it.

**Minting a credit block.** Once product has shipped, a `ComplianceOfficer` mints a credit block citing the finished-fuel batch through `batch_id` and the registered pathway through `pathway_id`. Two referential conditions gate the mint, evaluated in this order. First, the cited pathway must be scoped to the production that made the batch: the pathway's `unit_id` must equal the producing campaign's `unit_id`, **and** the pathway's `feedstock_type` must equal the campaign's dominant feedstock type — the `feedstock_type` accounting for the greatest `CONSUMED` mass on that campaign, with ties broken in favour of the allocation with the earliest `created_at`. A mismatch on either half is `ERR_PATHWAY_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Second, the cited batch's `distribution_status` must be `SHIPPED`; credits are minted on transfer, not in tank, so a batch still `IN_TANK` or merely `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_SHIPPED`. The pathway must also be `REGISTERED`.

A new block starts at `DRAFT` with its `program` copied from the cited pathway, `retired_credits_mtco2e` of 0.000, `verification_outcome` of `NONE`, and `verified_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, `verification_findings` and `signed_off_by_user_id` all null. Its `provisional_ci_gco2e_per_mj` is frozen at creation as the producing campaign's `blend_ci_gco2e_per_mj` plus a process carbon adder of 8.75 grams of CO2 equivalent per megajoule, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Its provisional quantities are then stamped according to the program, and each program leaves the other program's fields at zero: an `RFS` block keeps `provisional_credits_mtco2e` and `verified_credits_mtco2e` at 0.000, while an `LCFS` block keeps `provisional_rin_credits` and `verified_rin_credits` at 0. Blocks may be listed and filtered by `batch_id`, `pathway_id`, `program` and `status`.

For an `RFS` block, `provisional_rin_credits` is the batch's `volume_gal` multiplied by a renewable-identification-number equivalence factor of 1.7 for `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` or 1.6 for `SAF`, with the fractional part discarded to give a whole number. Credit counts are always truncated downward, never rounded.

For an `LCFS` block, `provisional_credits_mtco2e` is formed by subtracting the block's `provisional_ci_gco2e_per_mj` from the program benchmark carbon intensity for the batch's product class — 88.50 for `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` and 89.37 for `SAF` — multiplying that difference by the energy economy ratio for the product class, which is 1.00 for both `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` and `SAF`, multiplying by the energy density in megajoules per gallon — 129.65 for `RENEWABLE_DIESEL` and 126.60 for `SAF` — multiplying by the batch's `volume_gal`, dividing by one million to express the result in metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent, and rounding half up to 3 decimal places. If that arithmetic yields a negative number — a pathway dirtier than the benchmark earns nothing — the result is held at the floor of 0.000.

**Internal sign-off** is where segregation of duties bites. A `ComplianceOfficer` signs off a `DRAFT` block, which moves `DRAFT → INTERNAL_SIGNED_OFF` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as the block's `signed_off_by_user_id`, bumping the `version`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cited pathway's `registered_by_user_id`; when the same officer who registered the pathway attempts to sign off a block claimed under it, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. The cited pathway must additionally still be `REGISTERED` — a `SUSPENDED` pathway yields `ERR_INVALID_PATHWAY_STATE`.

Submitting a block for verification moves `INTERNAL_SIGNED_OFF → SUBMITTED_FOR_VERIFICATION` and bumps the `version`; it requires that the producing campaign has reached `RECONCILED`, since verification claims against frozen cost and yield figures, and a campaign still short of that reports `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Submitting a block still in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_BLOCK_STATE`.

**The verification decision** is the `IndependentVerifier`'s, and its most important property is that the landing state is *derived from arithmetic rather than requested*. The payload carries `decision`, which must be `VERIFY` or `REJECT`, plus `verified_ci` on a verify and `findings` on a reject. A `REJECT` requires a present, non-blank `findings`, stores it as `verification_findings`, sets `verification_outcome` to `REJECTED`, leaves `verified_credits_mtco2e` at 0.000 and `verified_rin_credits` at 0, and moves the block to `VERIFICATION_REJECTED`, which is terminal. A `VERIFY` requires `verified_ci` to be present and at least 0; it is stored as `verified_ci_gco2e_per_mj` and the verified quantities are always recomputed at that carbon intensity.

For an `LCFS` block, `verified_credits_mtco2e` repeats the provisional arithmetic with two substitutions: the verifier's carbon intensity replaces the block's `provisional_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, and the whole quantity is additionally multiplied by a verification discount of 0.98 before the half-up rounding to 3 decimal places and the floor at 0.000. Because of that discount the two variants differ even when the verified carbon intensity equals the provisional one.

For an `RFS` block, `verified_rin_credits` applies a proportional invalidation. First a carbon-intensity drift is formed: the verifier's carbon intensity less the block's `provisional_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, divided by the block's `provisional_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. An invalidation rate is then taken as that drift held at a floor of 0.0000 — a *better* verified carbon intensity never creates extra credits — and capped at 0.1000, so at most a tenth of the block can be invalidated however bad the drift. `verified_rin_credits` is then the batch's `volume_gal` multiplied by the equivalence factor for its product class, multiplied by one less the invalidation rate, with the fractional part discarded. A verified carbon intensity worse than the attested one therefore destroys renewable-identification numbers even though their quantity is nominally volumetric.

The landing state follows from comparing the verified quantity with the provisional one. The relevant quantity is `verified_credits_mtco2e` against `provisional_credits_mtco2e` for an `LCFS` block, and `verified_rin_credits` against `provisional_rin_credits` for an `RFS` block. Take the absolute difference between verified and provisional, divide it by the provisional quantity, and compare against a tolerance of 0.05. When the relative divergence is within tolerance — that is, not greater than five hundredths — the block lands at `VERIFIED` with `verification_outcome` set to `VERIFIED`. When it exceeds the tolerance the block lands at `ADJUSTED` with `verification_outcome` set to `ADJUSTED`. The same request body therefore yields either state depending purely on arithmetic; `ADJUSTED` is never asked for. In both cases the block's `version` is bumped, the pathway's `credits_outstanding_mtco2e` is recomputed as described in §2.10, and the pathway's `version` is bumped.

An `ADJUSTED` landing fires a four-level cascade. Level 1 is the stamping of the verified quantities and the `ADJUSTED` outcome just described. Level 2, caused by level 1, increments the cited pathway's `variance_strikes` and bumps the pathway's `version`. Level 3, caused by level 2, checks the incremented count: when `variance_strikes` has reached 2, the pathway transitions `REGISTERED → SUSPENDED` — a terminal state — and its `version` is bumped again. Level 4, caused by level 3, transitions **every other** credit block citing that pathway whose status is `DRAFT` or `INTERNAL_SIGNED_OFF` to `BLOCKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with each blocked block's `version` bumped. `BLOCKED` is terminal: two adjustments on one pathway shut down the entire unclaimed inventory behind it. Blocks already in `SUBMITTED_FOR_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED`, `ADJUSTED`, `RETIRED` or `VERIFICATION_REJECTED` are left alone.

**Retirement and its reversal.** A `ComplianceOfficer` retires credits out of a block whose status is `VERIFIED` or `ADJUSTED`, supplying `credits` — the quantity to retire. That figure must be greater than 0 and no greater than the block's `verified_credits_mtco2e` less its `retired_credits_mtco2e`; a request for zero, or for more than the remaining balance, is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS`. Retirement adds the supplied `credits` to `retired_credits_mtco2e`, recomputes the pathway's `credits_outstanding_mtco2e`, and bumps both the block's and the pathway's `version`. The block moves to `RETIRED` only when `retired_credits_mtco2e` has become equal to `verified_credits_mtco2e`; a partial retirement leaves the block where it was so that the remainder can be retired later. Retiring against a `BLOCKED` block is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_BLOCK_STATE`.

Reversal is the compensating inverse and it is all-or-nothing. A `ComplianceOfficer` reverses the whole retired balance on a block whose status is `VERIFIED`, `ADJUSTED` or `RETIRED`, supplying a `reason` that is recorded as `verification_findings`. The block's `retired_credits_mtco2e` is set to 0.000, the pathway's `credits_outstanding_mtco2e` is recomputed — returning to exactly its pre-retirement value — and both versions are bumped. The block's status is restored from its own `verification_outcome`: a block whose outcome was `VERIFIED` returns `RETIRED → VERIFIED`, and one whose outcome was `ADJUSTED` returns `RETIRED → ADJUSTED`. This is why `verification_outcome` is retained separately from `status`. A block with no retired balance at all has nothing to reverse and is refused with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_REVERSE`.

### 3.9. Managing Feedstock and Credit Price Volatility

Feedstock and credit prices move faster than the plant's production cycle, so the trading desk carries a hedge book. A `FeedstockTrader` opens a position by declaring the `instrument` — `FEEDSTOCK_SWAP`, `RIN_FORWARD` or `LCFS_FORWARD` — a `notional_units` figure whose unit depends on the instrument (metric tonnes for a feedstock swap, renewable identification numbers for a RIN forward, metric tonnes of CO2 equivalent for an LCFS forward), greater than 0 and no greater than 1000000 with at most 3 decimal places; a `strike_price_usd` per notional unit, greater than 0 and no greater than 10000 with at most 2 decimal places; and a `maturity_at` instant that must be strictly after the current logical clock — a maturity equal to the current clock is refused with `ERR_MATURITY_IN_PAST`. A new position starts at status `OPEN` with `settlement_price_usd` null and `realized_pnl_usd` 0.00. Positions may be listed and filtered by `instrument`, by `volatility_tier` and by `status`.

The margin requirement is computed at creation in a **pinned five-step order** that may not be rearranged. Step one: `gross_notional_usd` is `notional_units` multiplied by `strike_price_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Step two: `base_margin_usd` is `gross_notional_usd` multiplied by the instrument's base margin rate — 8 percent for a `FEEDSTOCK_SWAP`, 12 percent for a `RIN_FORWARD`, 15 percent for an `LCFS_FORWARD` — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Step three: `volatility_tier` is selected **from `base_margin_usd` and never from `total_margin_usd`** — it is `T3` when the base margin is at least 250000.00 US dollars, otherwise `T2` when the base margin is at least 50000.00, otherwise `T1`. Both boundaries are inclusive, so a base margin of exactly 50000.00 is `T2` and one of exactly 250000.00 is `T3`. Step four: `volatility_adder_usd` is `base_margin_usd` multiplied by the tier's adder rate — 1.0 percent for `T1`, 2.5 percent for `T2`, 4.0 percent for `T3` — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Step five: `total_margin_usd` is `base_margin_usd` plus `volatility_adder_usd`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The creation payload may optionally carry `volatility_tier` as an override. When it is omitted the tier is derived from `base_margin_usd` against the two floors just described; when it is supplied it replaces that derivation outright and step four's adder follows the supplied tier rather than the one the arithmetic would have chosen. An instrument outside the declared three — `CRUDE_SWAP`, for example — is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unwinding closes the position. A `FeedstockTrader` unwinds an `OPEN` position by supplying `settlement_price_usd`, the settlement price per unit; unwinding an already-`UNWOUND` position is refused with `ERR_INVALID_HEDGE_STATE`. The position moves `OPEN → UNWOUND`, stores the settlement price, and stamps `realized_pnl_usd` as the settlement price less the `strike_price_usd`, multiplied by `notional_units`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a figure that is negative when the market moved against the position. Margin is returned on unwind: `base_margin_usd`, `volatility_adder_usd` and `total_margin_usd` are all reset to 0.00 and `volatility_tier` returns to `T1`. The position's `version` is bumped.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 and return that resource: registering a supplier, uploading a traceability document, booking a feedstock lot, appending a quality test, pretreating a lot (which returns the newly created child lot), registering a hydrotreating unit, installing a catalyst charge, planning a turnaround window, booking a hydrogen supply contract, filing a hydrogen nomination, creating a processing campaign, creating a feedstock allocation, creating a pathway registration, minting a credit block and opening a hedge position. Every other operation — every state transition on an existing resource, every read, and the clock capability — responds with HTTP 200 and returns the affected resource in its post-transition form. The request and response bodies are not restated here; §2 and §3 are the normative description of what each operation reads and writes.
