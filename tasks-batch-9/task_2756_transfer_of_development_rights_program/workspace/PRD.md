# Transfer Of Development Rights Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Transfer of Development Rights (TDR) program lets a municipality move growth from land it wants preserved to land it wants intensified. Farmland, wetland margins and view corridors are grouped into **sending areas**; downtown blocks and transit corridors are grouped into **receiving areas**. A landowner inside a sending area may sever the development rights attached to a parcel, accept a permanent conservation easement over the corresponding acreage, and receive a tradable certificate for the severed rights. A developer inside a receiving area may buy those certificates and redeem them for a density bonus above the as-of-right yield of a site. Nothing is created and nothing disappears: every right that leaves a preserved parcel must be traceable, at every moment, either to a certificate someone still holds, to a permit that consumed it, or to a deliberate retirement.

This document specifies the municipal system of record for that program. It is an accounting system before it is a workflow engine. Severed rights are conserved in whole units from severance through issuance, splitting, sale, redemption and retirement; settlement money is conserved in whole cents across a sale and its compensating reversal. Around that core the system carries the administrative apparatus the program actually needs: two competing statutory allocation formulas whose choice is fixed by the designating ordinance and pinned onto every decision it produces, a per-neighborhood appraisal schedule that prices both private deals and the municipal bank's purchase and resale channels, an equity surcharge that varies by the neighborhood the rights came from, a design review gate on the receiving side, and a stewardship regime that inspects preserved parcels and freezes the rights of any parcel found in violation until the violation is cured.

Three pressures shape the design. First, **segregation of duties**: the reviewer who approves a severance may not be the operator who certifies it, and the monitor who records a violation may not be the operator who verifies its cure. Second, **determinism**: pricing, cure deadlines, reversal windows and evaluation windows all read a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall time, so that identical inputs always produce identical registry state. Third, **capacity discipline**: a sending area may not release more rights than its ordinance cap allows, and a receiving area may not absorb more bonus units than its absorption cap allows — and in both cases reaching the cap exactly is the event that closes the area, not merely a warning.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The whole API is mounted under the `/tdr` prefix. Every rule in this section applies to every endpoint in §4 unless the endpoint's own narrative in §3 narrows it.

### 2.1. Program Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The six roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `SeveranceReviewer`, `DesignReviewBoard`, `EasementMonitor`, `Landowner`, and `Developer`.

A `ProgramAdministrator` is municipal TDR program staff. Administrators designate and activate areas, publish appraisal schedules, enroll parcels, certify approved severances, operate the municipal bank's purchase and resale channels, settle and reverse transactions, run program evaluations, drive the logical clock, and run the compliance sweep. A `SeveranceReviewer` is a planning-department reviewer who screens severance applications and records the approve, revise, or deny decision — and who may never certify, which is precisely what gives the segregation-of-duties rule its force. A `DesignReviewBoard` member rules on receiving-site design before a density bonus can be finalized. An `EasementMonitor` is a field monitor who inspects preserved parcels and verifies cures on noticed violations. A `Landowner` owns a sending parcel, applies to sever rights from it, and trades the resulting certificates. A `Developer` buys certificates and files receiving-site applications for a density bonus. Every role may read every resource through the listing and detail endpoints, with two exceptions noted in the endpoint table: the party listing and the party detail read are not open to all six roles in the same way.

`ProgramAdministrator`, `SeveranceReviewer`, `DesignReviewBoard`, and `EasementMonitor` all carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is any of those four, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It does, however, record it wherever a transition demands an audit trail: as `screened_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a `SeveranceApplication` when a `SeveranceReviewer` screens or decides, as `certified_by_user_id` on the same application when a `ProgramAdministrator` certifies, as `design_reviewed_by_user_id` on a `ReceivingSiteApplication` when a `DesignReviewBoard` member records a design decision, and as `inspector_user_id` and `cure_verified_by_user_id` on a `MonitoringInspection` when an `EasementMonitor` files an inspection or verifies a cure.

`Landowner` and `Developer` are **entity-backed** on `Party`. When `X-User-Role` is `Landowner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Party`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `Party` compare the resource's party foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Party`. The concrete comparisons are: `owner_party_id` on a `SendingParcel`, `applicant_party_id` on a `SeveranceApplication`, `developer_party_id` on a `ReceivingSiteApplication`, `seller_party_id` on a `MarketTransaction` being cancelled, and `holder_party_id` on a `Certificate` being split or retired. Allocating a redemption carries one additional ownership requirement: the named certificate's `holder_party_id` must equal the acting developer's identity, because a developer may only redeem rights it actually holds. A failed ownership comparison is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `ProgramAdministrator` acting on a certificate split, a certificate retirement, a redemption release, or a transaction cancellation is exempt from the ownership comparison — administrative authority stands in for ownership there.

Two role rules go beyond a simple endpoint allow-list. Segregation of duties, rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), requires that the `X-User-ID` certifying a severance application differ from the `decided_by_user_id` that recorded its approval, and that the `X-User-ID` verifying a cure differ from the `inspector_user_id` that recorded the inspection. Channel-role agreement, rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), requires that a `PRIVATE` market transaction be proposed by the selling side — a `Landowner` or a `Developer` — and that a `BANK_PURCHASE` or `BANK_RESALE` transaction be proposed only by a `ProgramAdministrator`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position in the result set: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific listing says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four pagination fields ride in the `meta` object of a list response alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`: `limit` and `offset` echo the values in force for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Detail endpoints, creation endpoints and action endpoints return a single object and carry none of the four pagination fields in `meta`.

Filters on a listing narrow the matched set before pagination and before `total_count` is computed. The available filters are named in §4's descriptions and in the workflow narratives of §3; each filter matches on exact equality against the field of the same name.

### 2.4. Registry Record Properties

Every resource the API returns carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` is one of the unknown fields the request-shape rule silently ignores.

Most resources also carry three further common properties. `created_at` is the instant the row was first persisted, `updated_at` is the instant of its most recent mutation, and both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits (for example, `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row, including mutations reached through a cascade rather than through a direct request. Whenever a rule in this document says that an entity's `version` is incremented, it also means that the same entity's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock.

`CertificateTransfer` is the one deliberate exception. A chain-of-title row is append-only: once written it is never modified, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `CertificateTransfer` altogether. Every other entity in the program — `Party`, `SendingArea`, `ReceivingArea`, `AppraisalSchedule`, `SendingParcel`, `SeveranceApplication`, `ConservationEasement`, `Certificate`, `MarketTransaction`, `ReceivingSiteApplication`, `CertificateRedemption`, `MonitoringInspection`, and `ProgramEvaluation` — carries all four.

One `version` counter behaves unusually, and it does so on purpose. A `Party`'s `version` is incremented **only** by an administrative account action. Cash movement driven by a settlement or a reversal, and recomputation of `units_held` or `certificates_held_count` driven by any cascade, both leave `Party.version` untouched, so an operator's observed party version remains valid across all of them.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this program reads wall-clock time. Every timestamp the server writes, every cure-deadline comparison, every appraisal-window selection and every reversal-window check reads a single server-controlled logical clock. Before any administrator sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — a fixed startup default that is never replaced by real time. An administrator-only clock capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; every subsequent read of "the current logical clock" anywhere in this document means the value most recently set that way, or the startup default if it was never set.

Timestamp fields written from the clock therefore inherit its exact value. These include `recorded_at` and `issued_at` on registry rows, `settled_at` and `reversed_at` on transactions, `allocated_at`, `confirmed_at` and `released_at` on redemptions, `finalized_at` on receiving-site applications, `recorded_at`, `last_inspection_at` and `cure_deadline` on easements, and `cure_verified_at` and `escalated_at` on inspections.

### 2.6. Units, Cents, Basis Points and Rounding

Development rights are counted in whole units. Money is carried in integer cents throughout — never in a decimal currency amount — in `cash_balance_cents`, `fee_revenue_cents`, `per_unit_value_cents`, `agreed_price_cents`, `applied_per_unit_value_cents`, `appraised_reference_cents`, `gross_price_cents`, `program_fee_cents`, `equity_surcharge_cents`, `net_to_seller_cents`, `price_cents`, `amount_cents`, and `total_cash_cents`. Rates that would otherwise need fractions of a percent are carried in basis points, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth: `equity_adjustment_bps` and `price_deviation_bps` are signed, the rest are not.

Two rounding disciplines coexist and must not be confused. Where a figure is *rounded*, the method is always round half up — a value exactly halfway between two candidates goes to the larger magnitude candidate — applied to whole cents for money, to whole basis points for deviations, and to two decimal places for acreage, percentages and severity scores. Where a figure is *floored*, the fractional part is discarded outright and never rounded up, no matter how close to the next integer it sits. Development-right allocation and density-bonus sizing both floor; pricing, fees, surcharges, severity scores and evaluation percentages all round half up.

Acreage inputs — `gross_acres`, `constrained_acres`, `site_acres`, `affected_acres` — carry two decimal places, as do the computed `restricted_acres`, `preserved_acres`, `absorption_pct`, `equity_index`, and `severity_score`. The per-acre and per-unit configuration numbers `base_rate_units_per_acre`, `gross_discount_factor`, `base_density_units_per_acre`, and `max_bonus_pct` likewise carry two decimal places.

### 2.7. Cascading Consistency Across the Register

A single request in this program routinely rewrites several entities. When it does, the whole cascade is part of that request: it is applied before the response is returned, in the order the workflow narrative gives, and every entity it touches has its `version` incremented by one and its `updated_at` refreshed. There is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual convergence — a read taken immediately after a write observes the fully settled state.

Two entities carry **two independent state dimensions** rather than one. A `ConservationEasement` has a register dimension, `recording_status`, which moves among `PENDING_RECORDING`, `RECORDED` and `AMENDED`, and a stewardship dimension, `compliance_status`, which moves among `COMPLIANT`, `VIOLATION_NOTICED`, `UNDER_CURE`, `CURED` and `ESCALATED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's value: an `AMENDED` easement can be `ESCALATED`, and a `RECORDED` easement can be `CURED`. A `Certificate` has a rights dimension, `lifecycle_status`, which moves among `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, `REDEEMED` and `RETIRED`, and a tradability dimension, `market_status`, which moves among `HELD`, `UNDER_CONTRACT` and `FROZEN`. Proposing, settling or cancelling a deal touches only `market_status`; redeeming or retiring units touches only `lifecycle_status`; and a split touches neither, because a split is a lineage mutation rather than a lifecycle advance.

Derived totals are reconciled, not accumulated blindly. `severed_units_total` on a sending area, `severed_units` and `remaining_units` and `restricted_acres` on a parcel, `units_issued_total` and `units_redeemed_confirmed` and `units_retired` and `units_outstanding` on an easement, `units_remaining` and `units_split_out` and `units_encumbered` and `units_redeemed` and `units_retired` on a certificate, `absorbed_units` and `reserved_units` on a receiving area, `backed_units` and `pending_backed_units` on a receiving-site application, and `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` on a party are all recomputed from their defining rows whenever a cascade reaches them, and each must agree with its definition after every request.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Rejection on Registry Writes

Three write endpoints require the client to declare the parent row version it last observed, and reject the write if the registry has moved on. In all three cases the version field is a required, non-optional input, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). No other endpoint takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

Allocating certificate units to back a receiving-site application requires `expected_receiving_site_application_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `ReceivingSiteApplication`. Several redemptions may legitimately be stacked on one application, so a second allocation that carries the version the client read before the first allocation trips no state precondition at all; without this check it would succeed while sizing its units against a backing total that no longer exists. If the application's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the allocation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Splitting a certificate requires `expected_certificate_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Certificate`. A certificate may legitimately be split repeatedly, so a split carrying a superseded version passes every state precondition while reasoning about a `units_remaining` figure the registry has already changed. If the certificate's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the split is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

An administrative account action against a party requires `expected_party_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Party`. Repeated credits, debits, suspensions and reinstatements against one party are entirely legitimate, so without this check a debit carrying a superseded version would apply on top of a balance the operator never saw. If the party's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the account action is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because `Party.version` advances only through this endpoint, as §2.4 states, the operator's observed version survives intervening settlements and unit recomputations.

### 2.9. Path Resolution

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost identifier that fails to resolve decides the error. Each entity has its own not-found code, all at HTTP 404: `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SENDING_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECEIVING_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SENDING_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEVERANCE_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSERVATION_EASEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKET_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECEIVING_SITE_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_REDEMPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MONITORING_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_PROGRAM_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved the same way and produce the same codes, but only after the request's shape has been validated. Body foreign keys are checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and the first unresolved reference decides the error. This applies to parcel enrollment, severance application filing, market transaction proposal, receiving-site application filing, and redemption allocation.

### 2.10. Order of Evaluation

When several rules could reject the same request, the outcome is not left to implementation order. The checks below form one global ladder, evaluated strictly from the top; the first check that fails decides the response, and no later check is consulted. Authentication precedes authorization, existence precedes ownership, safety precedes constraint, and fiscal checks always come last.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty and `X-User-Role` names a declared role; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right; otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
4. **Request shape** — every required input field is present; types, enum membership, string lengths, decimal places and numeric ranges are honoured; `period_start` is not after `period_end`; `effective_from` is strictly before `effective_to`; any `units` figure is greater than zero; every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a non-negative integer; unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. A failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint with a request body or query parameters.
5. **Body foreign keys** — every foreign key in the body resolves, in the order the endpoint declares its inputs; otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to parcel enrollment, severance filing, transaction proposal, receiving-site filing, and redemption allocation.
6. **Ownership** — for an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` matches the resource's owning party as §2.1 describes; otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to severance filing, severance resubmission, receiving-site filing, design resubmission, finalization, redemption allocation, redemption release, certificate split, certificate retirement, and transaction cancellation.
7. **Party kind** — each party reference holds the `kind` its slot requires; otherwise `ERR_PARTY_KIND_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to parcel enrollment, severance filing, receiving-site filing, and transaction proposal.
8. **Segregation of duties** — as described in §2.1; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to severance certification and cure verification.
9. **Channel and role agreement** — as described in §2.1; otherwise `ERR_CHANNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403). Applies to transaction proposal.
10. **Party suspension** — no party involved in the request, whether the actor's own party, the seller, the buyer, the parcel owner or the developer, is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise `ERR_PARTY_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to severance filing, transaction proposal, settlement, receiving-site filing, redemption allocation, and certificate split.
11. The severance application is `SUBMITTED` before screening; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SCREENABLE` (409).
12. The severance application is `UNDER_REVIEW` before a review decision; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_IN_REVIEW` (409).
13. The severance application is `REVISION_REQUESTED` before resubmission; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_REVISABLE` (409).
14. The severance application is `APPROVED` before certification; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
15. The receiving-site application is `DESIGN_REVIEW` before a design-review decision, and `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` before a design resubmission; otherwise `ERR_DESIGN_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID` (409).
16. The receiving-site application is `DESIGN_APPROVED` before finalization — a `BACKING_DEFICIENT` or `DESIGN_REVIEW` application cannot finalize — and is in a non-terminal state before a redemption is allocated to it; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DESIGN_APPROVED` (409).
17. The redemption is `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` and its application is not `FINALIZED`; otherwise `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_RELEASABLE` (409). Applies to redemption release.
18. The market transaction is `PROPOSED` before settlement or cancellation; otherwise `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_PROPOSED` (409).
19. The market transaction is `SETTLED` before reversal; otherwise `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_SETTLED` (409).
20. The certificate's `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and its `market_status` is not `UNDER_CONTRACT`; otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_TRANSFERABLE` (409). Applies to transaction proposal, certificate split, certificate retirement, and redemption allocation. A `FROZEN` certificate is deliberately **not** caught here: it passes this rung and is stopped by the compliance check at rung 24, so the safety error is the one the caller sees.
21. The inspection is `CURE_PENDING` before a cure verification; otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_CURABLE` (409).
22. The sending parcel's `parcel_status` is not `FULLY_SEVERED`; otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_FULLY_SEVERED` (409). Applies to severance filing.
23. **Stale parent version** — the supplied expected version matches the parent's current `version` as §2.8 describes; otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to redemption allocation, certificate split, and party account actions.
24. **Certificate compliance** — the certificate's source `ConservationEasement` has `compliance_status` of either `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`; a `VIOLATION_NOTICED`, `UNDER_CURE` or `ESCALATED` easement freezes its rights and the request is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_FROZEN` (409). Applies to transaction proposal, certificate split, and redemption allocation.
25. **Application compliance** — every source `ConservationEasement` behind one of the application's redemptions in status `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` has `compliance_status` of either `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`; otherwise `ERR_EASEMENT_NONCOMPLIANT` (409). Applies to finalization, ahead of every constraint check on that endpoint.
26. The `SendingArea` named by the parcel or application has `status` of `ACTIVE`; a `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` area accepts nothing, and the request is rejected with `ERR_SENDING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to parcel enrollment, severance filing, and severance certification.
27. The `ReceivingArea` has `status` of `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_RECEIVING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to receiving-site filing, redemption allocation, and finalization.
28. When the receiving area's `eligible_sending_area_codes` list is non-empty, the certificate's origin sending area `code` appears in it; an empty list means every sending area is eligible. A violation is `ERR_SENDING_RECEIVING_PAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to redemption allocation.
29. A new appraisal schedule's window does not overlap an existing `ACTIVE` window for the same `neighborhood_code`; otherwise `ERR_APPRAISAL_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
30. The new `inspection_date` is far enough after the easement's `last_inspection_at`; otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
31. A new evaluation period does not partially overlap an existing `COMPUTED` evaluation period; otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
32. The reversal falls inside the reversal window measured from `settled_at`; otherwise `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409).
33. `apn` is unique across all sending parcels; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARCEL_APN` (409).
34. The area `code` is unique across sending and receiving areas combined; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_CODE` (409). Applies to both designation endpoints.
35. The sending parcel has no severance application in a non-terminal status; otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_HAS_OPEN_APPLICATION` (409).
36. The applicable revision counter is below its two-round limit; otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to severance review decisions, severance resubmission, design-review decisions, and design resubmission.
37. `requested_bonus_units` does not exceed the recomputed `max_bonus_units`; otherwise `ERR_BONUS_EXCEEDS_MAX` (422). Applies to design resubmission.
38. A split leaves at least one unencumbered unit on the parent; otherwise `ERR_SPLIT_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE_UNITS` (422).
39. The requested units are available on the certificate; otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CERTIFICATE_UNITS` (422). Applies to transaction proposal, redemption allocation, and certificate retirement.
40. Backing is sufficient at finalization; otherwise `ERR_BACKING_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
41. Confirming the redemptions does not push the receiving area past its absorption cap; otherwise `ERR_ABSORPTION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409).
42. The settled transaction is still cleanly reversible; otherwise `ERR_REVERSAL_NOT_PERMITTED` (409).
43. An `ACTIVE` appraisal schedule covers the certificate's origin neighborhood at the current logical clock; otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPRAISAL_SCHEDULE` (422). Applies to transaction proposal.
44. The proposed price sits inside the deviation band; otherwise `ERR_PRICE_DEVIATION_EXCEEDS_BAND` (422). Applies to transaction proposal.
45. **Funds** — at settlement the buyer's `cash_balance_cents` is at least `gross_price_cents`; at reversal the seller's `cash_balance_cents` is at least `net_to_seller_cents` and the municipal bank's `fee_revenue_cents` is at least `program_fee_cents` plus `equity_surcharge_cents`; and a debit account action may not drive a balance below zero. A failure is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` (402). Applies to settlement, reversal, and party account actions.
46. The severance grant would allocate at least one unit while the area still has capacity; a grant of zero units with capacity remaining is `ERR_NO_ALLOCABLE_UNITS` (422). Applies to severance review decisions.
47. The sending area still has unsevered capacity at all; an area whose cap has been fully consumed is `ERR_SENDING_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to severance review decisions. Exhausted capacity is reported with this code rather than as a zero allocation.
48. The area being activated is still `DRAFT`; activating an area that is already `ACTIVE`, or that is `CLOSED` or `SUSPENDED`, is rejected with `ERR_AREA_NOT_DRAFT` (409) rather than treated as a no-op. Applies to both activation endpoints. This rung is last only because those two endpoints are the only ones it guards and no rule above competes on them, so its position can change no observable outcome.

### 2.11. The Two Conservation Invariants

Everything else in this specification serves two identities that must hold exactly after every request.

**Rights are conserved.** For any single certificate, `units_remaining` equals `units_face` less `units_split_out` less `units_redeemed` less `units_retired`, and `units_encumbered` never exceeds `units_remaining`. For any single easement, `units_issued_total` equals `units_redeemed_confirmed` plus `units_retired` plus `units_outstanding` — equivalently, `units_outstanding` is the total face value of the root issuances backed by that easement, less everything redeemed and everything retired across the easement's entire certificate lineage including every split child. For any single party, `units_held` is the sum of `units_remaining` across the certificates it holds whose `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, and `certificates_held_count` is the count of those same certificates. Rows in status `RELEASED` contribute nothing to any of these totals, and rows in status `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` contribute to `units_encumbered` but never to `units_redeemed_confirmed`. Splitting a certificate, allocating a redemption and releasing a redemption must each leave both `units_issued_total` and `units_outstanding` completely unchanged; only confirmation of a redemption and retirement of units may move `units_outstanding`, and each moves it downward by exactly the units involved.

**Cash is conserved.** The program has no external source or sink of money. Summed across every `Party`, `cash_balance_cents` plus `fee_revenue_cents` is a constant. A settlement moves `gross_price_cents` out of the buyer's balance, `net_to_seller_cents` into the seller's balance, and `program_fee_cents` plus `equity_surcharge_cents` into the municipal bank's `fee_revenue_cents`; because the gross equals the net plus the fee plus the surcharge, the three movements sum to zero and the program-wide total is unchanged. A reversal applies the exact negation of all three movements, so the program-wide total after a settlement followed by its reversal is identical to the total before the settlement, with no rounding residue left behind anywhere.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Parties, the Municipal Bank, and Account Standing

Nothing in the program can happen until its participants exist in the register. A `ProgramAdministrator` registers each participant as a **Party**, supplying its `kind`, its `legal_name` (between 1 and 120 characters), its `contact_email` (which must contain an at-sign), and optionally its `neighborhood_code` (between 1 and 24 characters when supplied, and null when omitted, where it is used only in equity reporting). The `kind` is one of `LANDOWNER`, `DEVELOPER`, or `MUNICIPAL_BANK`, and at most one party of kind `MUNICIPAL_BANK` may exist in the program at any time — it is the single counterparty behind the bank purchase and bank resale channels and the single accumulator of program revenue. A newly registered party opens with `cash_balance_cents` at 0, `fee_revenue_cents` at 0, `units_held` at 0, `certificates_held_count` at 0, and `status` of `ACTIVE`.

Of those figures, only the two cash fields are ever written directly. `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` are reconciled read models, recomputed from the registry whenever a certification, settlement, reversal or retirement changes what the party holds, exactly as §2.11 defines them. `fee_revenue_cents` accrues program fees and equity surcharges and is non-zero only on the `MUNICIPAL_BANK` party; it must never fall below zero, and neither must `cash_balance_cents` — a movement that would drive either negative is rejected rather than clamped.

Administrators adjust a party's account through a single administrative action endpoint that takes an `action`, an optional `amount_cents`, an optional free-text `note`, and the required `expected_party_version` described in §2.8. The `action` is one of `CREDIT`, `DEBIT`, `SUSPEND`, or `REINSTATE`. A `CREDIT` adds `amount_cents` to `cash_balance_cents`; a `DEBIT` subtracts it, and is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS` when the resulting balance would be negative. `amount_cents` is required for `CREDIT` and for `DEBIT`, and must be omitted for `SUSPEND` and `REINSTATE`; supplying it in the wrong combination is a malformed request. A `SUSPEND` sets `status` to `SUSPENDED`; a `REINSTATE` restores `status` to `ACTIVE`. Every one of the four actions increments `Party.version` by one, and — per §2.4 — nothing else in the program does.

Suspension is a hard gate rather than a flag. While any party involved in a request carries `status` of `SUSPENDED`, that request is rejected with `ERR_PARTY_SUSPENDED`, and "involved" is read broadly: the acting party, the seller, the buyer, the parcel owner and the developer all count. The gate covers severance filing, transaction proposal, settlement, receiving-site filing, redemption allocation, and certificate splitting. A suspended party may still be read, and its existing rows are left exactly as they were — suspension prevents new activity, it does not unwind old activity.

Parties can be listed with an optional `kind` filter and an optional `status` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to see the reconciled holdings.

### 3.2. Designating Sending and Receiving Areas

A **SendingArea** is the ordinance-level unit on the preservation side. An administrator designates one by supplying its `code`, its `name`, its `neighborhood_code`, the `allocation_formula_version` the designating ordinance adopted, the `base_rate_units_per_acre` the ordinance grants, an optional `gross_discount_factor`, the `equity_tier` classification of the neighborhood, and the `sending_cap_units` ceiling on how many rights may ever be severed from the area. The `code` is 3 to 16 characters and must be unique across sending areas and receiving areas *together* — the two designation namespaces are shared, and a collision on either side is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AREA_CODE`. `neighborhood_code` is 1 to 24 characters and is what joins the area to the appraisal schedule that prices its rights. `base_rate_units_per_acre` is greater than zero and no more than 4, carried to two decimal places. `gross_discount_factor` is greater than zero and no more than 1, also to two decimal places, and defaults to 0.80 when the request omits it; it is consulted only under one of the two formula versions. `equity_tier` is one of `HIGH_VALUE`, `MODERATE`, or `UNDERSERVED`, and it is the origin area's tier — not the buyer's, not the receiving area's — that sets the equity surcharge on every later sale of rights that came from here. `sending_cap_units` is greater than zero. `allocation_formula_version` is either `V1_NET_ACRE` or `V2_GROSS_DISCOUNTED`.

A newly designated sending area opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `severed_units_total` and `issued_certificate_count` both at 0. Activation moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and increments its `version`; an area that is not in `DRAFT` cannot be activated and yields `ERR_AREA_NOT_DRAFT`. Only an `ACTIVE` sending area accepts parcel enrollment, severance filing, or certification.

Closing a sending area moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, accepts an optional `reason`, increments the area's `version`, and runs a suspension cascade over the applications filed against its parcels: every `SeveranceApplication` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `denial_reason` set to `SENDING_AREA_CLOSED`, and each suspended application's `version` is incremented by one as well. An application already in `APPROVED` is deliberately left in `APPROVED`: it is not suspended, it simply becomes uncertifiable, because certification requires the area to be `ACTIVE`. `CLOSED` is terminal — a closed sending area is never reopened. The same cascade, with the same target states, the same `denial_reason` value, and the same version increments, also runs when the system closes the area automatically on cap exhaustion, as §3.5 describes; the administrator-driven close and the system-driven close are identical in their effects.

A **ReceivingArea** is the ordinance-level unit on the growth side. An administrator designates one by supplying its `code`, `name`, and `neighborhood_code` under the same length and uniqueness rules as a sending area, plus the `base_density_units_per_acre` that expresses the as-of-right yield (greater than zero, no more than 40, two decimal places), the `max_bonus_pct` ceiling on bonus as a fraction of that as-of-right yield (greater than zero, no more than 1, two decimal places), the `absorption_cap_units` total the area may absorb across all its sites (greater than zero), the `design_review_required` flag (defaulting to true), and an optional `eligible_sending_area_codes` whitelist. That whitelist defaults to the empty list, and an empty list means every sending area is eligible; a non-empty list means only certificates whose origin sending area `code` appears in it may be redeemed here, with anything else rejected as `ERR_SENDING_RECEIVING_PAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. A new receiving area opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `absorbed_units` and `reserved_units` at 0, and activation moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE` with a `version` increment, again rejecting a non-`DRAFT` area with `ERR_AREA_NOT_DRAFT`.

Receiving areas leave `ACTIVE` only one way, and never by administrator request: when a finalization pushes the recomputed `absorbed_units` to at least `absorption_cap_units`, the area takes an `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition, incrementing its `version`. The comparison is inclusive — landing exactly on the cap suspends the area. `SUSPENDED` is terminal for a receiving area. After suspension, a new receiving-site filing naming the area is rejected with `ERR_RECEIVING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE`, while applications already in flight keep whatever `status` they held.

Both designation registers can be listed. Sending areas accept `status`, `neighborhood_code`, `equity_tier`, and `allocation_formula_version` filters; receiving areas accept `status`, `neighborhood_code`, and `design_review_required`. Both accept `limit` and `offset`, and both expose their running counters — `severed_units_total` and `issued_certificate_count` on the sending side, `absorbed_units` and `reserved_units` on the receiving side — on the detail read.

### 3.3. Enrolling Sending Parcels and the Baseline Right Allocation

A **SendingParcel** is a single assessor parcel inside an `ACTIVE` sending area. An administrator enrolls one by supplying `owner_party_id`, `sending_area_id`, the `apn` assessor parcel number (4 to 24 characters, unique across all parcels, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARCEL_APN`), `gross_acres` (greater than zero, two decimal places), `constrained_acres` (at least zero, no greater than `gross_acres`, two decimal places, defaulting to zero — the wetland, floodway, or otherwise undevelopable acreage), `soil_class`, and `existing_dwelling_units` (at least zero, defaulting to zero). The `soil_class` is one of `PRIME`, `STATEWIDE`, `LOCAL`, or `NONE`. The referenced party must be of kind `LANDOWNER`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PARTY_KIND_MISMATCH`; the referenced sending area must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SENDING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Enrollment is where the parcel's development-right endowment is fixed, and it is fixed once. `baseline_allocation_units` is computed at enrollment and is **never recomputed afterwards**; every later severance reads it rather than restating it, so a subsequent ordinance amendment to the area's rates or formula cannot retroactively change what an already-enrolled parcel is worth. The computation proceeds in four steps. First, a soil multiplier is selected from the parcel's `soil_class`: `PRIME` yields a multiplier of 1.25, `STATEWIDE` yields 1.10, `LOCAL` yields 1.00, and `NONE` yields 0.75. Second, the eligible acreage is derived, and this is where the two ordinance formulas diverge. Under `V1_NET_ACRE`, eligible acreage is `gross_acres` minus `constrained_acres`, and `gross_discount_factor` is ignored entirely. Under `V2_GROSS_DISCOUNTED`, eligible acreage is `gross_acres` multiplied by the area's `gross_discount_factor`, and `constrained_acres` is ignored entirely. A consequence worth stating plainly: a parcel whose constrained fraction is larger than one minus the discount factor receives *more* rights under `V2_GROSS_DISCOUNTED` than under `V1_NET_ACRE`, on identical inputs. Third, a raw figure is formed as the eligible acreage multiplied by the area's `base_rate_units_per_acre` multiplied by the soil multiplier. Fourth, that raw figure is floored to a whole number — truncated downward, never rounded — the parcel's `existing_dwelling_units` are subtracted from the floored value because each already-built dwelling consumes one allocated right, and the result is taken as zero if the subtraction would go negative. The outcome is a non-negative integer stored as `baseline_allocation_units`.

On enrollment the derived ledger fields open at their starting values: `remaining_units` equals `baseline_allocation_units`, `severed_units` is 0, `restricted_acres` is 0, `easement_status` is `NONE`, and `parcel_status` is `UNSEVERED`. `easement_status` takes only the values `NONE` and `RECORDED`; `parcel_status` takes only `UNSEVERED`, `PARTIALLY_SEVERED`, and `FULLY_SEVERED`. `remaining_units` is always `baseline_allocation_units` less `severed_units`, and `severed_units` is always the sum of `allocated_units` over the parcel's severance applications that reached `CERTIFIED`. `restricted_acres` on the parcel mirrors the acreage currently encumbered by the parcel's conservation easement and stays at 0 until the first certification.

Parcels can be listed with `owner_party_id`, `sending_area_id`, `parcel_status`, and `easement_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually to see the baseline, severed and remaining counts together.

### 3.4. Severance Review and the Allocation Grant

A `Landowner` opens the preservation workflow by filing a **SeveranceApplication** against one of its parcels, supplying `sending_parcel_id`, `applicant_party_id`, the `requested_units` it asks to sever (greater than zero), and an optional `note`. The `applicant_party_id` must be the same party as the parcel's `owner_party_id` and must be of kind `LANDOWNER`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal that party, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Three further gates guard intake: the parcel's sending area must be `ACTIVE`, the parcel's `parcel_status` must not be `FULLY_SEVERED` (a fully severed parcel has nothing left to give, and the filing is rejected with `ERR_PARCEL_FULLY_SEVERED`), and the parcel must not already carry an application in a non-terminal status — meaning `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED` — which is rejected with `ERR_PARCEL_HAS_OPEN_APPLICATION`. One parcel therefore has at most one live application at a time, and severances on a parcel are strictly sequential.

An accepted filing opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0 and both `allocated_units` and `residual_units_retained` at 0. `applied_formula_version`, `screened_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `decision_note`, and `denial_reason` all begin unset. The application's `status` moves only among `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `CERTIFIED`, `DENIED`, and `SUSPENDED`, of which `CERTIFIED`, `DENIED`, and `SUSPENDED` are terminal.

A `SeveranceReviewer` takes a filed application into review, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, optionally recording a `screening_note`, writing the acting `X-User-ID` into `screened_by_user_id`, and incrementing the application's `version`. Screening requires that the application actually be in `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SCREENABLE` — that the parcel's sending area still be `ACTIVE`, and that the parcel's `parcel_status` still not be `FULLY_SEVERED`; the area and parcel are re-checked here because either may have moved since intake.

The same reviewer then records a decision, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUIRE_REVISION`, or `DENY`, plus an optional `decision_note` and an optional `denial_reason`. All three branches require the application to be `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, and all three write the acting `X-User-ID` into `decided_by_user_id` and increment the application's `version`.

- `REQUIRE_REVISION` moves the application `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and stores the `decision_note`. It is permitted only while `revision_round` is below the two-round limit; a third revision request is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
- `DENY` moves the application `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` and records a `denial_reason`, taken from the supplied `decision_note` when the request carries one and set to the literal `DENIED_ON_REVIEW` when it does not.
- `APPROVE` moves the application `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, but only when the parcel's sending area is still `ACTIVE` and only when the grant computation below yields at least one unit. It runs that computation, writes `allocated_units` and `residual_units_retained`, and pins `applied_formula_version` by copying the area's `allocation_formula_version` onto the application, so that a later ordinance change cannot restate a grant that has already been decided.

The grant computation is a two-bucket waterfall in whole units, with no rounding anywhere. The parcel bucket is the parcel's `remaining_units`. The area bucket is the area's `sending_cap_units` less its `severed_units_total`. If the area bucket is zero or negative, the area has no unsevered capacity at all and the decision is rejected with `ERR_SENDING_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. Otherwise `allocated_units` is the smallest of three quantities: the `requested_units` on the application, the parcel bucket, and the area bucket. If that smallest quantity is not at least one, the approval is rejected with `ERR_NO_ALLOCABLE_UNITS`. The parcel bucket is consumed before the area bucket, so a request that the area could accommodate but the parcel cannot is trimmed down by the parcel. Finally, `residual_units_retained` is the parcel bucket less `allocated_units` — the rights the owner keeps and may sever in a future application. The parcel must not be `FULLY_SEVERED` and the area must be `ACTIVE` for the computation to run at all.

A landowner whose application came back in `REVISION_REQUESTED` resubmits it with a restated `requested_units` and an optional `note`. Resubmission requires the application to be in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_REVISABLE`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `applicant_party_id`, and requires that incrementing `revision_round` not carry it past the two-round limit — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The effect moves the application `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_round` by one, replaces `requested_units` with the resubmitted value, clears both `screened_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` back to unset so that the next screening and decision are recorded afresh, and increments the application's `version`. This closes the review cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and it may run at most twice before the limit bites.

Applications can be listed with `sending_parcel_id`, `applicant_party_id`, and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.5. Certification: Recording the Easement and Issuing the Certificate

Certification is the pivot of the whole program: it is where a decided grant becomes a recorded restriction on land and a tradable instrument in the registry, in one indivisible request. Only a `ProgramAdministrator` may certify, they may attach an optional `recording_note`, and the response is the newly issued certificate rather than the application.

Four preconditions guard it. The application must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_APPROVED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the application's `decided_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the operator who certifies may never be the reviewer who approved. The parcel's sending area must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SENDING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE`, which is what makes an `APPROVED` application in a closed area permanently uncertifiable. And `allocated_units` must be at least one.

The request then moves the application `APPROVED → CERTIFIED`, writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `certified_by_user_id`, increments the application's `version`, and runs a four-link cascade in order.

**Link one — the register.** If the parcel has no **ConservationEasement** yet, one is created against it, carrying `sending_parcel_id` and the `severance_application_id` of this certifying application. That easement is born in `recording_status` `PENDING_RECORDING` and immediately advanced `PENDING_RECORDING → RECORDED` within the same request: `recorded_at` is set to the current logical clock and `recording_reference` is minted deterministically as `EAS-` followed by the parcel's `apn`, a hyphen, and the number 1. If the parcel already has an easement — because rights were severed from it before — that existing easement instead takes a `RECORDED → AMENDED` transition, its `amendment_count` is incremented by one, and its `recording_reference` is re-minted as `EAS-`, the parcel's `apn`, a hyphen, and `amendment_count` plus one evaluated after the increment. A third or later certification on the same parcel repeats that step as an `AMENDED → AMENDED` transition with identical effects. `recorded_at` is written once at the original recording and is never rewritten by an amendment, and `severance_application_id` likewise continues to name the application that first created the easement. `recording_reference` is unique across all easements. At most one easement ever exists per parcel.

**Link two — the encumbrance, caused by link one.** The easement's `restricted_acres` is recomputed as the parcel's `gross_acres` multiplied by the cumulative severed unit count and divided by the parcel's `baseline_allocation_units`, rounded half up to two decimal places, where the cumulative severed count is the parcel's `severed_units` before this certification plus the `allocated_units` now being certified. A certification that severs the parcel's whole baseline therefore encumbers its whole acreage, and a partial severance encumbers a proportional share. The easement's `permitted_residual_units` — the buildable envelope left on the preserved parcel — is recomputed as the parcel's `existing_dwelling_units` plus the parcel's `baseline_allocation_units` less that same cumulative severed count. The easement's `units_issued_total` grows by `allocated_units` and its `units_outstanding` is recomputed accordingly. On the parcel itself, `easement_status` becomes `RECORDED`, `restricted_acres` is set to match the easement's figure, `severed_units` grows by `allocated_units`, `remaining_units` is reduced by the same amount, and `parcel_status` becomes `PARTIALLY_SEVERED` when `remaining_units` is still above zero or `FULLY_SEVERED` when it has reached zero.

**Link three — the area, caused by link two.** The sending area's `severed_units_total` is recomputed as the sum of `allocated_units` over every `CERTIFIED` application on parcels in the area, and its `issued_certificate_count` is incremented by one.

**Link four — cap exhaustion, caused by link three.** When the recomputed `severed_units_total` is at least the area's `sending_cap_units`, the area takes its `ACTIVE → CLOSED` transition. The comparison is inclusive: reaching the cap exactly closes the area. Closing runs the same suspension cascade described in §3.2 — every `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED` application on the area's parcels moves to `SUSPENDED` with `denial_reason` of `SENDING_AREA_CLOSED`, and the area's `version` plus the `version` of every suspended application are each incremented by one. Any `APPROVED` application in the area is again left `APPROVED` and becomes uncertifiable.

Alongside the cascade, the same request mints exactly one root **Certificate**. Its `units_face` and `units_remaining` both equal `allocated_units`; `holder_party_id` is the application's `applicant_party_id`; `conservation_easement_id` is the easement from link one; `origin_sending_area_id` is the parcel's `sending_area_id`; `origin_neighborhood_code` is a copy of that area's `neighborhood_code`, taken at issuance so the certificate keeps pointing at the right appraisal schedule even if the area is later re-coded; `parent_certificate_id` is unset because this is a root; `generation` is 1; `split_count` is 0; `units_split_out`, `units_encumbered`, `units_redeemed`, and `units_retired` are all 0; `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED`; `market_status` is `HELD`; and `issued_at` is the current logical clock. `serial_number` is minted deterministically as `TDR-`, the area's `code`, a hyphen, and the area's `issued_certificate_count` after its increment rendered as five digits with leading zeros — so the first certificate out of an area coded `SND-01` is `TDR-SND-01-00001`. Serial numbers are unique across all certificates.

The registry's chain of title opens at the same moment: a **CertificateTransfer** row is appended for the new certificate with `transfer_type` of `ISSUANCE`, `seq_no` of 1, `from_party_id` unset, `to_party_id` set to the applicant, `units` equal to `allocated_units`, `price_cents` unset, `market_transaction_id` unset, and `recorded_at` at the current logical clock. Finally, the applicant party's `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` are recomputed.

A freshly recorded easement opens with `units_redeemed_confirmed` at 0, `units_retired` at 0, `amendment_count` at 0, `compliance_status` of `COMPLIANT`, `last_inspection_at` unset, `prior_violation_count` at 0, and `cure_deadline` unset. Easements can be listed with `sending_parcel_id`, `severance_application_id`, `recording_status`, and `compliance_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and the detail read exposes the full unit ledger together with the compliance posture.

### 3.6. The Registry: Chain of Title, Splits, and Retirement

A certificate is the program's negotiable instrument, and the registry's job is to make its history auditable. Every event in a certificate's life appends one immutable **CertificateTransfer** row: an issuance at certification, a split-out when units leave for a child, a sale under one of the three channels, a redemption when units back a permit, a retirement when units are extinguished, and a reversal when a settled sale is undone. Rows carry `seq_no`, a sequence number scoped to a single certificate that starts at 1 and increases by exactly one for each row appended to that certificate, so the pair of `certificate_id` and `seq_no` is unique. `transfer_type` is one of `ISSUANCE`, `PRIVATE_SALE`, `BANK_PURCHASE`, `BANK_RESALE`, `SPLIT_OUT`, `REDEMPTION`, `RETIREMENT`, or `REVERSAL`. `units` is always greater than zero. `from_party_id` names the transferor and is unset on `ISSUANCE` and `SPLIT_OUT` rows; `to_party_id` names the transferee and is unset on `REDEMPTION` and `RETIREMENT` rows. `market_transaction_id` is set on `PRIVATE_SALE`, `BANK_PURCHASE`, `BANK_RESALE`, and `REVERSAL` rows and unset on the rest. `price_cents` carries the transaction's gross price on market events and is unset on `ISSUANCE`, `SPLIT_OUT`, `REDEMPTION`, and `RETIREMENT` rows. `recorded_at` is the logical-clock instant the row was appended. As §2.4 notes, these rows have no `updated_at` and no `version`. The chain-of-title log can be listed with `certificate_id`, `from_party_id`, `to_party_id`, `market_transaction_id`, and `transfer_type` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and any single row can be read on its own.

A certificate's unit ledger is what the rest of the program reasons about. `units_face` is what the certificate was minted with. `units_split_out` is the total moved to child certificates. `units_redeemed` is the total confirmed against receiving-site permits. `units_retired` is the total permanently extinguished. `units_remaining` is `units_face` less those three. `units_encumbered` is the subset of `units_remaining` currently tied up by redemptions in status `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE`; the difference between `units_remaining` and `units_encumbered` is the *available* quantity, and it is that available quantity — never `units_remaining` on its own — that a new split, sale or allocation may consume.

**Splitting.** A holder — a `Landowner` or a `Developer` whose identity matches `holder_party_id` — or a `ProgramAdministrator` acting on their behalf may split units off a certificate into a new child. The request carries `units` and the `expected_certificate_version` of §2.8. Splitting requires the parent's `lifecycle_status` to be `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and its `market_status` to be `HELD`; a certificate that is `UNDER_CONTRACT`, `REDEEMED` or `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_TRANSFERABLE`, while one that is `FROZEN` is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_FROZEN` because its source easement is out of compliance. No party involved may be `SUSPENDED`. And the split must leave at least one unencumbered unit on the parent: `units` must be strictly less than the parent's available quantity, so a split that would take everything is rejected with `ERR_SPLIT_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE_UNITS`.

The child certificate is minted with `parent_certificate_id` pointing at the parent, with `conservation_easement_id`, `origin_sending_area_id` and `origin_neighborhood_code` copied down the lineage unchanged, with `holder_party_id` equal to the parent's holder, with `units_face` and `units_remaining` both equal to the requested `units`, with `generation` one higher than the parent's `generation`, with `split_count` at 0, with `lifecycle_status` of `ISSUED` and `market_status` of `HELD`, and with `issued_at` at the current logical clock. The parent's `split_count` is incremented by one, and the child's `serial_number` is the parent's `serial_number` followed by `-S` and the parent's `split_count` after that increment — so the second child of `TDR-SND-01-00001` is serialized `TDR-SND-01-00001-S2`. On the parent, `units_split_out` grows by `units` and `units_remaining` is recomputed, but `lifecycle_status` and `market_status` are both left exactly where they were: a split is a lineage mutation, not a lifecycle advance, which is why the parent's rights dimension can stay `ISSUED` across an arbitrary number of splits. Two chain-of-title rows are appended: a `SPLIT_OUT` row on the parent with no transferor and no transferee, and an `ISSUANCE` row on the child at `seq_no` 1 naming the holder as transferee, both for `units` units and both with no price. The source easement's `units_issued_total` and `units_outstanding` are completely unchanged by a split — the rights have moved, not multiplied. The parent's `version` is incremented by one.

**Retirement.** The same set of actors may permanently extinguish units from a certificate, supplying `units` and an optional `reason`. Retirement requires `lifecycle_status` of `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED`, `market_status` of `HELD`, and `units` no greater than the certificate's available quantity — a request beyond that is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CERTIFICATE_UNITS`. Retiring the whole remaining balance of an already `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` certificate additionally requires that `units_encumbered` be zero, since encumbered units are promised elsewhere. `units_retired` grows by `units` and `units_remaining` is recomputed; when `units_remaining` reaches zero the `lifecycle_status` becomes `RETIRED`, and it does so even if redemption also contributed to emptying the certificate — retirement outranks redemption when both reduced the same certificate to zero. A retirement that leaves units behind changes no state at all: `lifecycle_status` stays where it was and only the ledger moves. A `RETIREMENT` chain-of-title row is appended with no transferee and no price, the backing easement's `units_retired` and `units_outstanding` are recomputed (leaving `units_issued_total` untouched), the holder's `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` are recomputed, and the certificate's `version` is incremented.

The registry can be listed with `holder_party_id`, `conservation_easement_id`, `parent_certificate_id`, `origin_sending_area_id`, `lifecycle_status`, and `market_status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, which is how a lineage is walked: filtering on `parent_certificate_id` yields one generation of children, and filtering on `conservation_easement_id` yields the whole family descended from one severance.

### 3.7. Valuation and the Market

**Publishing an appraisal schedule.** Prices in this program are administered, not discovered. An administrator publishes an **AppraisalSchedule** for a neighborhood, supplying `neighborhood_code`, the `per_unit_value_cents` appraised value of one development right there (greater than zero), the signed `equity_adjustment_bps` correction for historic under-appraisal (defaulting to 0 and constrained to lie between negative 5000 and positive 5000 inclusive), the inclusive `effective_from` start of its window, and the exclusive `effective_to` end, where an unset `effective_to` means the window is open-ended. A schedule opens in `status` `ACTIVE`; the only other value is `SUPERSEDED`, which is set on an existing row when a new schedule for the same `neighborhood_code` declares an `effective_from` equal to that row's `effective_from`. Publication is rejected with `ERR_APPRAISAL_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when the new window overlaps the window of any other `ACTIVE` schedule for the same neighborhood; because the interval includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, two windows that merely touch — one window's `effective_to` equal to the next window's `effective_from` — do not overlap and are both accepted. Schedules can be listed with `neighborhood_code` and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and read individually.

**Proposing a deal.** A transaction is proposed with `certificate_id`, `seller_party_id`, `buyer_party_id`, `channel`, `units`, `conveyance_mode`, and — for one channel only — `agreed_price_cents`. `channel` is one of `PRIVATE`, a landowner-to-developer market deal at a negotiated price; `BANK_PURCHASE`, the municipal bank buying rights at the administered discount; or `BANK_RESALE`, the bank selling rights at the administered markup. `agreed_price_cents` is required and must be greater than zero for `PRIVATE`, and must be omitted for both bank channels, which are priced from the appraisal schedule instead. `seller_party_id` must be the certificate's current `holder_party_id` at proposal time, and on a `BANK_PURCHASE` or `BANK_RESALE` the counterparty must be the single `MUNICIPAL_BANK` party — otherwise `ERR_PARTY_KIND_MISMATCH`. A `PRIVATE` proposal may be filed only by the selling side, a `Landowner` or a `Developer`; the two bank channels may be filed only by a `ProgramAdministrator`; mismatches are rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH`. The certificate must be tradable — `lifecycle_status` of `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and `market_status` not already `UNDER_CONTRACT`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_TRANSFERABLE` — its backing easement must be `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_FROZEN`, no involved party may be `SUSPENDED`, and `units` must not exceed the certificate's available quantity, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CERTIFICATE_UNITS`. The `conveyance_mode` supplied on the proposal is not the authoritative value: the stored `conveyance_mode` remains unset for as long as the transaction is `PROPOSED`, and is pinned by the system at settlement from a comparison of the traded units against the certificate's remaining units. Its two possible values are `WHOLE` and `PARTIAL`.

An accepted proposal opens in `status` `PROPOSED`, moves the certificate `HELD → UNDER_CONTRACT`, grows the certificate's `units_encumbered` by the traded `units`, and increments the certificate's `version`. `program_fee_cents`, `equity_surcharge_cents`, and `net_to_seller_cents` all stay at 0 until settlement, and `conveyed_certificate_id`, `settled_at`, `reversed_at`, and `reversal_reason` all stay unset. Everything else about the deal's economics is resolved at proposal time, in three steps.

*Step one — the appraised reference.* The applicable schedules are those whose `neighborhood_code` equals the certificate's `origin_neighborhood_code` — the neighborhood the **rights** came from, never the buyer's neighborhood and never the receiving area's — whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose window covers the current logical clock, meaning the clock is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`. `SUPERSEDED` rows are excluded even when their window still covers the clock. If no schedule qualifies, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_APPRAISAL_SCHEDULE`. If several qualify, they are ordered by `effective_from` descending and then by `created_at` ascending, and the first is taken. `applied_neighborhood_code` records the certificate's `origin_neighborhood_code`. `applied_per_unit_value_cents` is the selected schedule's `per_unit_value_cents` multiplied by one plus its `equity_adjustment_bps` divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole number of cents — the rounding happens on the per-unit figure, before any multiplication by units. `appraised_reference_cents` is then `applied_per_unit_value_cents` multiplied by `units`.

*Step two — the gross price.* For `PRIVATE`, `gross_price_cents` is the `agreed_price_cents` from the request, taken exactly as supplied. For `BANK_RESALE`, a per-unit price is formed by marking the equity-adjusted per-unit value up by 500 basis points — that is, multiplying it by one plus five hundred divided by ten thousand — and rounding half up to whole cents; `gross_price_cents` is that per-unit price multiplied by `units`. For `BANK_PURCHASE`, a per-unit price is formed by discounting the equity-adjusted per-unit value by 300 basis points — multiplying it by one minus three hundred divided by ten thousand — and rounding half up to whole cents; `gross_price_cents` is again that per-unit price multiplied by `units`. The order matters and is not negotiable: round to whole cents **per unit first**, then multiply by the unit count. Multiplying first and rounding the total once produces a different figure and is wrong.

*Step three — the deviation band.* `price_deviation_bps` is the difference between `gross_price_cents` and `appraised_reference_cents`, divided by `appraised_reference_cents`, multiplied by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points; it is signed, so a below-appraisal deal reports a negative figure. If the absolute value of `price_deviation_bps` is strictly greater than 3000 basis points — thirty percent away from the appraised reference in either direction — the proposal is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_DEVIATION_EXCEEDS_BAND`. The comparison is strictly greater-than, so a deal landing exactly on 3000 basis points is accepted. Because both bank channels derive their price from the same reference by a fixed markup or discount, only a `PRIVATE` proposal can ever trip this band.

**Settling a deal.** Settlement is an administrator action, optionally annotated with a `settlement_note`; authority rests with `ProgramAdministrator` rather than with either counterparty. It requires the transaction to be `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_PROPOSED`; both the buyer and the seller party to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PARTY_SUSPENDED`; the certificate's `market_status` to be `UNDER_CONTRACT`; the backing easement's `compliance_status` to be `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`; the traded units to still be available on the certificate; and the buyer's `cash_balance_cents` to be at least `gross_price_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`.

The money split runs in four steps and reads every figure off the gross price. First, a fee rate is selected by tier from `gross_price_cents` itself, never from any post-surcharge figure: a gross of at most 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — attracts 350 basis points; a gross above that but at most 25,000,000 cents — two hundred fifty thousand dollars — attracts 275 basis points; anything above that attracts 200 basis points. Tier boundaries are inclusive on their upper bound, so a gross of exactly 5,000,000 cents sits in the first tier at 350 basis points while 5,000,001 cents sits in the second at 275. Second, `program_fee_cents` is `gross_price_cents` multiplied by that rate divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. Third, `equity_surcharge_cents` is `gross_price_cents` multiplied by a surcharge rate divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents, where the surcharge rate comes from the `equity_tier` of the sending area named by the certificate's `origin_sending_area_id`: 150 basis points for `HIGH_VALUE`, 75 basis points for `MODERATE`, and 0 basis points for `UNDERSERVED`, so preserving land in an underserved neighborhood carries no surcharge at all. Fourth, `net_to_seller_cents` is `gross_price_cents` less `program_fee_cents` less `equity_surcharge_cents`. Each cents figure is rounded independently.

Cash then moves in three legs that sum to zero, as §2.11 requires: the buyer's `cash_balance_cents` is reduced by `gross_price_cents`, the seller's `cash_balance_cents` is increased by `net_to_seller_cents`, and the `MUNICIPAL_BANK` party's `fee_revenue_cents` is increased by `program_fee_cents` plus `equity_surcharge_cents`.

Title moves next, and how it moves depends on the conveyance the system now pins. When the traded `units` equal the certificate's `units_remaining`, `conveyance_mode` is pinned to `WHOLE`: the certificate itself changes hands, its `holder_party_id` becomes `buyer_party_id`, and `conveyed_certificate_id` is set to the traded `certificate_id`. When the traded `units` are fewer than `units_remaining`, `conveyance_mode` is pinned to `PARTIAL`: the traded certificate keeps its holder, a child certificate is minted for the buyer with `parent_certificate_id` pointing at the traded certificate, `units_face` equal to `units`, `generation` one higher than the parent's, `serial_number` formed from the parent's serial plus `-S` and the parent's `split_count` after its increment, `holder_party_id` equal to the buyer, `market_status` of `HELD` and `lifecycle_status` of `ISSUED`; the parent's `units_split_out` grows by `units`, a `SPLIT_OUT` chain-of-title row with no transferor and no transferee is appended to the parent, and `conveyed_certificate_id` is set to the child's id. Either way the traded certificate's `units_encumbered` is reduced by `units` and its `market_status` returns `UNDER_CONTRACT → HELD`, and a chain-of-title row is appended to whichever certificate the buyer ends up holding — `transfer_type` of `PRIVATE_SALE`, `BANK_PURCHASE`, or `BANK_RESALE` according to `channel`, with the seller as `from_party_id`, the buyer as `to_party_id`, `units` units, and `price_cents` equal to `gross_price_cents`. Both parties' `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` are recomputed, `settled_at` is set to the current logical clock, the transaction moves `PROPOSED → SETTLED`, and its `version` is incremented.

**Cancelling a deal.** A proposal can be withdrawn before settlement by the seller — the entity-backed actor whose identity matches `seller_party_id` — or by a `ProgramAdministrator`, with an optional `reason` recorded into `reversal_reason`. The transaction must be `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_PROPOSED`. Cancellation moves it `PROPOSED → CANCELLED`, reduces the certificate's `units_encumbered` by the traded `units`, returns the certificate's `market_status` to `HELD` — or leaves it `FROZEN` when the cancellation was itself produced by a compliance freeze rather than requested — moves no cash whatsoever, and increments the transaction's `version`.

**Reversing a settled deal.** A `ProgramAdministrator` may unwind a settlement inside a bounded window, supplying a required `reversal_reason`. Four preconditions apply. The transaction must be `SETTLED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_SETTLED`. Its pinned `conveyance_mode` must be `WHOLE`; a `PARTIAL` conveyance minted a child certificate that cannot be cleanly un-split, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_NOT_PERMITTED`. The conveyed certificate must have `units_redeemed`, `units_encumbered`, and `units_retired` all at zero — if any of the conveyed rights have been committed or consumed since settlement, the reversal is again rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_NOT_PERMITTED`. And the current logical clock must be no later than 30 days after `settled_at`: exactly 30 days after settlement is still reversible, 31 days is not, and a late attempt is rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The compensating cash movement must also be affordable — the seller's `cash_balance_cents` must be at least `net_to_seller_cents` and the bank's `fee_revenue_cents` at least `program_fee_cents` plus `equity_surcharge_cents` — or the reversal is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`.

A permitted reversal applies the exact inverse of all three settlement legs: the buyer's `cash_balance_cents` is increased by `gross_price_cents`, the seller's `cash_balance_cents` is reduced by `net_to_seller_cents`, and the bank's `fee_revenue_cents` is reduced by `program_fee_cents` plus `equity_surcharge_cents`. The conveyed certificate's `holder_party_id` returns to `seller_party_id` and its `market_status` returns to `HELD`. A `REVERSAL` chain-of-title row is appended naming the buyer as `from_party_id` and the seller as `to_party_id`, for `units` units, with `price_cents` equal to the negation of `gross_price_cents`. Both parties' `units_held` and `certificates_held_count` are recomputed, `reversed_at` is set to the current logical clock, `reversal_reason` is recorded from the request, the transaction moves `SETTLED → REVERSED`, and its `version` is incremented. `REVERSED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. Because the three legs are exact negations, the program-wide money total after a settlement and its reversal equals the total before the settlement, to the cent.

One further path into `CANCELLED` exists that no one requests: when a compliance violation freezes a certificate that is `UNDER_CONTRACT`, the open `PROPOSED` transaction on it moves `PROPOSED → CANCELLED` with `reversal_reason` set to the literal `EASEMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`, and the certificate goes to `FROZEN` rather than back to `HELD`. §3.9 describes that cascade in full.

Transactions can be listed with `certificate_id`, `seller_party_id`, `buyer_party_id`, `conveyed_certificate_id`, `channel`, and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`, and the detail read exposes the whole pricing breakdown — `applied_neighborhood_code`, `applied_per_unit_value_cents`, `appraised_reference_cents`, `gross_price_cents`, `price_deviation_bps`, `program_fee_cents`, `equity_surcharge_cents`, and `net_to_seller_cents` — in one place.

### 3.8. Receiving-Site Entitlement: Design Review, Backing, and Absorption

The receiving side of the program is where purchased rights turn into buildable density. A `Developer` files a receiving-site application naming `developer_party_id`, `receiving_area_id`, `site_acres`, and `requested_bonus_units`. The named area must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RECEIVING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the filing party must be `ACTIVE` and must be the acting developer.

**Sizing the bonus.** Intake immediately sizes the bonus in three steps, and the order of the flooring matters. First a site tier factor is chosen from `site_acres`: a site of at least 10.00 acres takes 1.00, a site of at least 3.00 but under 10.00 acres takes 0.90, and anything smaller takes 0.75. The boundaries are inclusive on their lower edge, so exactly 10.00 acres takes the full factor and exactly 3.00 acres takes 0.90. Second, `base_units` is `site_acres` multiplied by the area's `base_density_units_per_acre`, floored to a whole number. Third, `max_bonus_units` is the already-floored `base_units` multiplied by the area's `max_bonus_pct` and then by the tier factor, floored again. The two floors are applied at exactly those two points; collapsing them into a single floor over the whole product yields a different and incorrect number. `required_backing_units` is then the lesser of `requested_bonus_units` and `max_bonus_units` — a developer who asks for more density than the area can grant is not rejected at intake, but is only ever required to back, and can only ever receive, the area's maximum.

The same sizing routine runs in two variants that deliberately produce different figures for the same application. The provisional variant, used at intake and again on every design resubmission, writes `provisional_bonus_units` as the lesser of `requested_bonus_units` and half of `max_bonus_units` floored — a release fraction of one half. It ignores backing entirely, so even a fully backed application shows a provisional figure well below what it will eventually be granted. The final variant, used only at finalization, drops the half-release cap and writes `allocated_bonus_units` as the smallest of `requested_bonus_units`, `max_bonus_units`, and `backed_units`.

**The intake branch.** A single filing lands in one of two different states depending on a configuration flag on the area. When the area has `design_review_required` set to true, the application starts `SUBMITTED → DESIGN_REVIEW` and must clear a board before it can finalize. When the flag is false, it starts `SUBMITTED → DESIGN_APPROVED` and skips the board entirely, with `design_review_round` left at 0 and `design_reviewed_by_user_id` left unset. Either way the provisional sizing has already run, and `backed_units`, `pending_backed_units`, and `allocated_bonus_units` all start at 0.

**Design review.** A `DesignReviewBoard` actor rules on an application in `DESIGN_REVIEW` with a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUIRE_REVISIONS`, or `DENY` plus an optional `review_note`; a decision against an application in any other state is rejected with `ERR_DESIGN_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. `APPROVE` moves it `DESIGN_REVIEW → DESIGN_APPROVED`. `REQUIRE_REVISIONS` moves it `DESIGN_REVIEW → REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, and is only available while `design_review_round` is below 2; a third round of revisions is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. `DENY` moves it `DESIGN_REVIEW → DENIED`, records `denial_reason` from `review_note` or the literal `DESIGN_REVIEW_DENIED` when the note is omitted, and releases every redemption already allocated to the application — each row in `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` moves to `RELEASED`, restoring its units to its certificate's `units_encumbered` and to the area's `reserved_units`, so a denial never strands rights. Every decision sets `design_reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting user and increments the application's `version`.

The developer answers `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` by resubmitting a design with a restated `requested_bonus_units` and an optional `revision_note`. Only the developer named by `developer_party_id` may resubmit, only from `REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, and only while the incremented round would remain within the two-round limit. Unlike intake, resubmission enforces the ceiling directly: a restated `requested_bonus_units` above `max_bonus_units` is rejected with `ERR_BONUS_EXCEEDS_MAX`. A valid resubmission increments `design_review_round`, replaces `requested_bonus_units`, re-runs the provisional sizing so `required_backing_units` and `provisional_bonus_units` are both restated, moves the application `REVISIONS_REQUIRED → DESIGN_REVIEW`, and increments `version`. This closes the design cycle `DESIGN_REVIEW → REVISIONS_REQUIRED → DESIGN_REVIEW`.

**Allocating backing.** The developer backs the request by allocating certificate units to the application, supplying `certificate_id`, `units`, and `expected_receiving_site_application_version`. The application must be in a non-terminal state, otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DESIGN_APPROVED`; backing may therefore be assembled while design review is still running. The supplied version must match the application's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION`. The certificate must be transferable — `lifecycle_status` of `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` and `market_status` not `UNDER_CONTRACT` — and its source easement must be `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_FROZEN`. The area must still be `ACTIVE`. Pairing is checked next: when the area's `eligible_sending_area_codes` list is non-empty, the certificate's origin sending area code must appear in it, otherwise `ERR_SENDING_RECEIVING_PAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; an empty list means every sending area is eligible. Finally the requested `units` must not exceed `units_remaining` less `units_encumbered` on the certificate, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CERTIFICATE_UNITS`.

A successful allocation creates a redemption in `ALLOCATED` with `allocated_at` at the current logical clock and `source_easement_id` copied from the certificate's `conservation_easement_id` — the link is copied at allocation time so that later compliance checks can reach the easement directly. The certificate's `units_encumbered` grows by `units` and the area's `reserved_units` grows by `units`; nothing is consumed yet, only committed. The application's `pending_backed_units` is recomputed, its `version` increments, and a `BACKING_DEFICIENT` application whose backing has been restored to at least `required_backing_units` moves `BACKING_DEFICIENT → DESIGN_APPROVED`.

**Releasing backing.** A redemption in `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` may be released, with an optional `reason`, by the application's own developer or by a `ProgramAdministrator`. The redemption must be in one of those two states and its application must not be `FINALIZED`, otherwise `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Release sets `released_at`, reduces the certificate's `units_encumbered` by `units` and the area's `reserved_units` by `units`, recomputes `pending_backed_units`, and increments the redemption's `version`. The source easement's `units_outstanding` is deliberately unchanged: allocation and release move rights between commitment states without ever creating or destroying them, so an allocate-then-release pair returns every counter to its starting value. A `DESIGN_APPROVED` application whose backing has fallen below `required_backing_units` moves `DESIGN_APPROVED → BACKING_DEFICIENT`.

**Finalization.** Only the application's own developer finalizes, and only from `DESIGN_APPROVED` — a `DESIGN_REVIEW`, `REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, or `BACKING_DEFICIENT` application is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DESIGN_APPROVED`. An optional `finalization_note` may accompany the request. Three gates then apply in a fixed order. Compliance is checked first, before any capacity arithmetic: every source easement behind a redemption in `ALLOCATED` or `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` must be `COMPLIANT` or `CURED`, otherwise `ERR_EASEMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`. Sufficiency is checked second: `backed_units` plus `pending_backed_units` must be at least `required_backing_units`, otherwise `ERR_BACKING_INSUFFICIENT`. Capacity is checked last: the area's `absorbed_units` plus the units about to be confirmed must not exceed `absorption_cap_units`, otherwise `ERR_ABSORPTION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That boundary is inclusive — landing exactly on the cap succeeds, one unit beyond it fails.

Finalization then confirms backing in a defined order: every redemption still in `ALLOCATED` moves `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` in ascending `allocated_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, each taking `confirmed_at` from the current logical clock. Each confirmation moves its units out of the certificate's `units_encumbered` and into `units_redeemed`, moves the same units out of the area's `reserved_units` and into `absorbed_units`, increases the source easement's `units_redeemed_confirmed` and decreases its `units_outstanding` by the same amount, appends a `REDEMPTION` chain-of-title row, and advances the certificate's `lifecycle_status` — `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_REDEEMED` when part of the certificate survives, or straight to `REDEEMED` when the confirmation consumes everything that remained. Rows held in `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` are not confirmed by finalization; only `ALLOCATED` rows are. The final sizing variant then writes `allocated_bonus_units`, `finalized_at` is set from the clock, the application moves `DESIGN_APPROVED → FINALIZED`, and its `version` increments. When the recomputed `absorbed_units` has reached `absorption_cap_units`, the area itself takes `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` in the same request, after which further filings against it are refused with `ERR_RECEIVING_AREA_NOT_ACTIVE` while applications already in flight keep their status.

Note that the backing figure the final variant consumes counts redemptions in `ALLOCATED` and `CONFIRMED` only; rows in `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` and `RELEASED` are excluded from it. An application can therefore satisfy the sufficiency gate — which counts held rows through `pending_backed_units` — and still be granted fewer bonus units than it requested, because the held rows contribute nothing to `backed_units`.

`FINALIZED` and `DENIED` are both terminal. Applications can be listed with `developer_party_id`, `receiving_area_id`, and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`; redemptions can be listed with `receiving_site_application_id`, `certificate_id`, `source_easement_id`, and `status` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Easement Stewardship, the Compliance Cascade, and Program Evaluation

A recorded easement is a permanent obligation, and the program monitors it for the life of the restriction. Stewardship is the second, independent dimension of an easement's state: `recording_status` tracks the register while `compliance_status` tracks conduct on the ground, and the two never constrain each other.

**Recording an inspection.** An `EasementMonitor` records an inspection against an easement, supplying `inspection_date`, `finding`, `affected_acres`, and an optional `note`; the acting user is stored as `inspector_user_id`. The easement must be on the register — `recording_status` of `RECORDED` or `AMENDED` — and the inspection must respect a minimum interval: `inspection_date` must be at least 90 days after the easement's `last_inspection_at`, with a gap of exactly 90 days allowed and 89 days rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Every accepted inspection updates `last_inspection_at` to its `inspection_date`.

**Scoring a violation.** A finding of `COMPLIANT` records `severity_score` as 0.00, leaves `cure_deadline` unset, and opens the inspection directly in `CLOSED`. A finding of `MINOR_VIOLATION` or `MAJOR_VIOLATION` opens the inspection in `CURE_PENDING` and is scored from three weighted components. The finding component contributes 0 for `COMPLIANT`, 40 for `MINOR_VIOLATION`, and 90 for `MAJOR_VIOLATION`, at a weight of one half. The extent component is an acre ratio — `affected_acres` divided by the easement's `restricted_acres`, capped at 1 so that damage beyond the restricted area cannot push the ratio past whole — expressed as a percentage and taken at a weight of three tenths. The history component is the easement's `prior_violation_count` multiplied by 25 and capped at 100, taken at a weight of two tenths; the count is read as it stands before this inspection increments it, so a landowner's first violation carries no history term. `severity_score` is the weighted sum rounded half up to two decimal places, with the rounding applied once to the sum and never to individual terms.

The rounded score selects a cure band, and the bands are inclusive on their lower edge: a score of at least 70.00 gives 30 days, a score of at least 45.00 but below 70.00 gives 60 days, and anything lower gives 90 days. A score landing exactly on 70.00 therefore takes the 30-day band, while 69.70 takes the 60-day band. `cure_deadline` is `inspection_date` advanced by the band's day count, and the same deadline is copied onto the easement.

**The freeze cascade.** A violation finding moves the easement `COMPLIANT → VIOLATION_NOTICED`, or `CURED → VIOLATION_NOTICED` for a repeat offender, and sets its `cure_deadline`. That single move propagates through four linked levels in one request, because rights derived from land that is out of compliance must not continue to trade or to entitle density. First the easement's `compliance_status` changes and its `prior_violation_count` increments. Second — caused by the first — every certificate whose `conservation_easement_id` names this easement and whose `market_status` is `HELD` or `UNDER_CONTRACT` moves to `FROZEN`, and any market transaction on such a certificate that is still `PROPOSED` moves `PROPOSED → CANCELLED` with `reversal_reason` set to the literal `EASEMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`. Third — caused by the second — every redemption in `ALLOCATED` on a just-frozen certificate moves `ALLOCATED → HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE`; its units stay inside the certificate's `units_encumbered` and the area's `reserved_units`, but they leave the application's `pending_backed_units`, because held backing no longer counts as backing. Fourth — caused by the third — every receiving-site application in `DESIGN_APPROVED` whose recomputed `backed_units` plus `pending_backed_units` has fallen below `required_backing_units` moves `DESIGN_APPROVED → BACKING_DEFICIENT`. The `version` of the easement and of every certificate, transaction, redemption, and application touched by the cascade is incremented. An application whose backing survives the recompute stays `DESIGN_APPROVED`, so link four fires selectively.

**Verifying a cure.** An `EasementMonitor` or `ProgramAdministrator` verifies a cure against the inspection, supplying an `outcome` of `CURED` or `CURE_IN_PROGRESS` and an optional `verification_note`. The inspection must be in `CURE_PENDING` for either outcome, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_CURABLE`, and segregation of duties applies: the verifying user must differ from the `inspector_user_id` who recorded the finding, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. No one signs off on their own inspection.

An outcome of `CURE_IN_PROGRESS` acknowledges partial remediation without clearing it. The inspection stays in `CURE_PENDING` and keeps its `cure_deadline`, the easement moves `VIOLATION_NOTICED → UNDER_CURE`, and the deadline is explicitly not extended — a landowner cannot buy time by reporting progress. Certificates stay `FROZEN` and redemptions stay `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE`.

An outcome of `CURED` clears the violation and runs the exact compensating cascade, unwinding the freeze level by level. The easement moves to `CURED` from whichever of `VIOLATION_NOTICED`, `UNDER_CURE`, or `ESCALATED` it currently occupies, and its `cure_deadline` is cleared. Every certificate on that easement whose `market_status` is `FROZEN` returns to `HELD`. Every redemption in `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` on those certificates returns to `ALLOCATED`, restoring its units to the application's `pending_backed_units`. Every receiving-site application in `BACKING_DEFICIENT` whose recomputed backing again reaches `required_backing_units` returns `BACKING_DEFICIENT → DESIGN_APPROVED`. The inspection records `cure_verified_at` from the current logical clock and `cure_verified_by_user_id` from the acting user, and moves to `CURED`; the `version` of every entity touched is incremented. Note that the compensating cascade does not restore the cancelled market transaction — a cancellation is terminal, and the parties must propose a fresh deal.

**Escalation.** Cure deadlines are enforced by an administrator-triggered compliance sweep that evaluates every open violation against the current logical clock. Every inspection in `CURE_PENDING` whose `cure_deadline` is strictly earlier than the clock moves `CURE_PENDING → ESCALATED` with `escalated_at` set from the clock, and drives its easement to `ESCALATED` from `VIOLATION_NOTICED` or `UNDER_CURE`, incrementing `prior_violation_count` again. The comparison is strict: at exactly the `cure_deadline` nothing escalates, and only once the clock has moved past it does the sweep bite. Certificates remain `FROZEN` and redemptions remain `HELD_PENDING_COMPLIANCE` throughout — escalation tightens the record without changing the freeze. An escalated inspection is still curable, and a `CURED` verification against it runs the same compensating cascade and returns the easement to `CURED`.

**Closing the loop.** An easement in `CURED` is not yet back to clean standing. A later inspection with a finding of `COMPLIANT` moves it `CURED → COMPLIANT`, with the new inspection created directly in `CLOSED`, and that closes the stewardship cycle from `COMPLIANT` through `VIOLATION_NOTICED` and optionally `UNDER_CURE` to `CURED` and back to `COMPLIANT`. Inspections can be listed per easement with `status` and `finding` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

**Program evaluation.** A `ProgramAdministrator` computes a program evaluation over a period by supplying `period_start`, `period_end`, and `equity_weighting`. The window is inclusive at both ends and compared at date granularity. `period_start` must not be later than `period_end`. Overlap with existing evaluations is constrained in a specific way: no other `COMPUTED` evaluation may partially overlap the requested window, and a partial overlap is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` — but an exact-match period is allowed, and computing it moves the earlier evaluation to `SUPERSEDED`, which is how the program restates a period after corrections.

The evaluation writes eight metrics. `preserved_acres` sums `restricted_acres` over easements whose `recording_status` is `RECORDED` or `AMENDED` and whose `recorded_at` date falls in the window; easements still `PENDING_RECORDING` are excluded entirely. `easement_count` counts those same easements. `issued_units` sums `units_face` over certificates whose `issued_at` date falls in the window, and `retired_units` sums `units_retired` over that same certificate set — note that both are scoped by issuance date, so a retirement is attributed to the period in which the certificate was issued, not the period in which it was retired. `redeemed_units` sums `units` over redemptions that are `CONFIRMED` and whose `confirmed_at` date falls in the window. `outstanding_units` is `issued_units` less `redeemed_units` less `retired_units`. `absorption_pct` is the sum of `absorbed_units` divided by the sum of `absorption_cap_units` across every receiving area whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, as a percentage rounded half up to two decimal places, and reports 0.00 rather than erroring when the denominator is zero; `DRAFT` areas never enter that denominator, and the figure is a program-wide snapshot rather than a window-scoped one. `total_cash_cents` is the sum of `cash_balance_cents` plus the sum of `fee_revenue_cents` across all parties, which by §2.11 is a constant the evaluation merely reports.

`equity_index` measures how much of the period's preservation came from underserved neighborhoods, and it is computed in one of two variants over the same easement set, where an easement counts as underserved when its parcel's sending area has an `equity_tier` of `UNDERSERVED`. Under `ACREAGE_WEIGHTED` the index is the underserved share of `preserved_acres`; under `PARCEL_COUNT_WEIGHTED` it is the underserved share of `easement_count`. Both are percentages rounded half up to two decimal places, and both report 0.00 when their denominator is zero. The two variants deliberately disagree on identical data: one large underserved easement dominates an acreage-weighted index, while a small underserved easement carries exactly the same weight as a large one under count weighting.

Evaluations start in `COMPUTED` and can be listed with `status` and `equity_weighting` filters plus `limit` and `offset`.
