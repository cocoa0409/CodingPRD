# Residential Earthquake Coverage & Mitigation Incentive Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

California-style residual earthquake insurance sits in an awkward place between a private market that will not voluntarily carry the peril and a public program that must nonetheless price it, pay for it, and reduce it. This document specifies the API for such a program: a residual insurer that distributes residential earthquake coverage exclusively through admitted participating insurers, each of which carries a statutory duty to offer the product to its residential book; a hazard-based rating engine that prices a dwelling from its county's seismic tier, its site soil, its construction type, its vintage and its retrofit posture; a grant program that pays for the retrofits that reduce the hazard in the first place; and a catastrophe machinery of retentions, reinsurance towers and post-event assessments that funds the losses when the ground finally moves.

The program has three constituencies whose interests only partly align, and most of the rules in this document exist to hold that tension. Policyholders want the deductible options and coverage sublimits explained honestly and the mitigation discount paid promptly. Participating insurers want to write and renew business without friction, but the program measures whether they actually discharge their offer duty and sanctions the ones that do not. The program itself must conserve two independent ledgers to the cent — a grant funding pool that may never over-commit, and a per-event loss aggregate whose retained, ceded and uncovered slices must always reconcile — while also refusing to let one operator both approve public money and commit it, or both propose and sign off a catastrophe settlement.

Everything in this API is addressed as a flat set of top-level collections mounted under `/eqp`. There are no nested resource URLs: relationships are declared by identifier fields in request bodies and are rediscovered through filtered list reads. Time is not wall-clock time; every deadline in the domain is evaluated against a server-held logical clock that test harnesses drive explicitly. Money is integer cents throughout, and the take-up rate that governs insurer compliance is an integer count of basis points. The sections that follow define the global contract first, then the business narrative, then the endpoint surface and the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting individual or organisation, and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which they are acting. When `X-User-Role` is absent, or carries a string that is not one of the seven roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE` (HTTP 401). When the named role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorised for the target operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Five operations additionally require `X-User-ID` to be present and non-empty, because they are the operations on which the program enforces separation of duties: recording a grant review decision, reserving cycle funds against an approved grant, recording an inspection result, posting a provisional claim adjustment, and finalizing a claim adjustment. On those five, a missing `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `ParticipatingInsurer`, `Policyholder`, `GrantReviewer`, `FieldInspector`, `ClaimAdjuster` and `CertifiedContractor`. Three of them are **entity-backed**, meaning that `X-User-ID` is the primary key of a persisted row and is compared against resource ownership; four are **opaque operator identities**, meaning that `X-User-ID` is an operator handle that the server may record on a row and later compare against *another* operator handle, but never against any entity's `id`.

- `ProgramAdmin` is residual-insurer program staff: it enrolls insurers, runs funding cycles, certifies contractors, manages catastrophe events and the reinsurance tower, levies assessments, records claim payments and drives the logical clock. Its identity is opaque: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `reserved_by_user_id` on a `RetrofitGrant` when an administrator commits cycle money) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `ParticipatingInsurer` is an admitted residential insurer that must offer the earthquake product, quotes and binds it, and is exposed to post-event assessment. When `X-User-Role` is `ParticipatingInsurer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ParticipatingInsurer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ParticipatingInsurer` compare the resource's `insurer_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- `Policyholder` is a residential owner-occupant who receives the mandatory offer, buys the coverage, applies for a retrofit grant and files claims. When `X-User-Role` is `Policyholder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Policyholder` row; ownership checks compare the resource's `policyholder_id` — resolved through the dwelling where the row does not carry the field directly — to `X-User-ID`.
- `GrantReviewer` is a mitigation-program reviewer who adjudicates retrofit grant applications and sets the award amount, but may never commit cycle money to an award it approved. Its identity is opaque: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `approved_by_user_id` on the grant it approves and later compares that recorded handle to the handle presented by whoever reserves the funds.
- `FieldInspector` is a program inspector who schedules verification visits on the contractor calendar and records the outcome, driving the retrofit verification cascade. Its identity is opaque: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded as `inspector_user_id` on the `Inspection` it resolves and compared only against other operator handles.
- `ClaimAdjuster` is a catastrophe adjuster who posts the provisional and the final adjustment; the final adjustment must be signed by a different adjuster identity than the provisional one. Its identity is opaque: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded as `provisional_adjuster_user_id` and `final_adjuster_user_id` on the `Claim` and compared only against each other.
- `CertifiedContractor` is a certified retrofit contractor who performs the funded work and declares it ready for inspection. When `X-User-Role` is `CertifiedContractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor` row; ownership checks on any `RetrofitProject` or `Inspection` compare the row's `contractor_id` to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership is enforced uniformly for the three entity-backed roles and produces `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) when it fails. A caller acting as `Policyholder` may read or act only on `Dwelling`, `Policy`, `MandatoryOffer`, `RetrofitGrant` and `Claim` rows that resolve to its own `policyholder_id`. A caller acting as `ParticipatingInsurer` may read or act only on rows that resolve to its own `insurer_id`. A caller acting as `CertifiedContractor` may read or act only on `RetrofitProject` and `Inspection` rows whose `contractor_id` is its own. Where the row in question *is* the acting role's own entity row — a `ParticipatingInsurer` reading a `ParticipatingInsurer`, a `Policyholder` reading a `Policyholder`, a `CertifiedContractor` reading a `Contractor` — the comparison is against that row's own `id`, there being no `*_id` field on it to compare. Where a row carries no identifier of the acting role's kind at all — a `ParticipatingInsurer` reading a `Policyholder` or a `Dwelling` — no ownership narrowing applies and the role list of section 4 alone governs the read. The four opaque roles are not scoped by ownership at all; they are scoped by the role lists in section 4 and, on the five duty-segregated operations, by the conflict checks described in section 2.10.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one value in the whole API that is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical program clock of section 2.5, because it describes the transport rather than the domain, and no domain timestamp is ever derived from it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Transport-level failures use this same envelope rather than any framework default. A request whose path matches no declared route answers with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 404; a request whose method the matched path does not declare answers with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 405; and any unanticipated server-side failure answers with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. In the first two cases the status is the one the transport condition carries and is deliberately not the 400 that section 5 lists against `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when it is not supplied, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts rows to skip from the beginning of the result set. Both bounds are enforced by silent correction rather than by rejection: a `limit` below 1 is raised to 1, a `limit` above 100 is lowered to 100, a negative `offset` is raised to 0, and a parameter supplied empty or in a form that is not parseable as an integer falls back to that parameter's own default. No pagination input ever produces an error, and an `offset` that lands past the end of the result set simply yields an empty `data` array alongside the true `total_count`. Collections are always returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, ties broken by the order in which the rows were created — a tie-break that does real work here, because the logical clock of section 2.5 moves only when a caller drives it, so every row created inside one unmoved instant shares a `created_at` down to the millisecond — so that a stable offset walk sees each row exactly once; where a domain section describes a different presentation order (the reinsurance tower in layer order, assessment allocations in levy-rank order), that order is a property of the data the caller reads back, not a change to the default sort. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads and write operations omit those four fields.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are all optional and are combined conjunctively: supplying both `insurer_id` and `status` on the offer list returns only that insurer's offers in that status. A filter matches by comparing the supplied text to the stored field's own string form exactly, with a boolean field matched against the lower-case strings `true` and `false`; a filter parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent, and a row whose filtered field is null matches nothing. Filter values are not validated against their enum domains: a value outside the domain is matched by no row and yields an empty page, never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the One Ledger Row That Has Neither

Every resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply or predict it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits — always three, never fewer and never more, and always with a literal trailing `Z`. That format is universal on the way out: every timestamp-valued field this API emits is rendered the same way, including the instants a client itself supplied, which are parsed, converted to UTC and re-rendered to exactly three fractional-second digits rather than being echoed back verbatim. On the way in, an instant may carry a trailing `Z`, an explicit UTC offset, or no zone designator at all, in which case it is read as UTC. Thirteen of the fourteen resource kinds — `Policyholder`, `ParticipatingInsurer`, `Dwelling`, `MandatoryOffer`, `Policy`, `GrantCycle`, `RetrofitGrant`, `Contractor`, `RetrofitProject`, `Inspection`, `CatastropheEvent`, `ReinsuranceLayer` and `Claim` — additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row. Any operation that changes a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, including the cascaded mutations that a single request performs on rows other than its direct target.

`AssessmentAllocation` is the exception. An allocation is an immutable ledger line struck at the moment an assessment is levied and never touched again, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its representation.

Path identifiers are validated left to right. Because the topology is flat, most paths carry a single identifier, and a request naming a row that does not exist is rejected with the 404 belonging to that resource kind — `ERR_POLICYHOLDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DWELLING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP status 404. The same catalogue governs identifiers supplied in a request body: `insurer_id`, `policyholder_id`, `dwelling_id`, `policy_id`, `accepted_offer_id`, `grant_cycle_id`, `grant_id`, `contractor_id`, `project_id` and `event_id` must each resolve to an existing row of their kind, and the failure carries that kind's not-found code. Body identifiers are resolved in exactly the order just listed, so a request carrying two unresolvable identifiers reports the one that appears earlier in that sequence. A body identifier that is absent or null is not a resolution failure at all — its absence is left to the required-field check — whereas a value that is present but of the wrong JSON type is treated exactly like an identifier naming nothing, and draws that kind's not-found code rather than a shape error.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Nothing in this domain reads wall-clock time. The server holds a single logical clock that begins, before any request has moved it, at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. The single exception anywhere in the API is the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` of section 2.2, which is transport metadata read from real wall-clock time and takes no part in the domain. A `ProgramAdmin`-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the call writes no entity, and every temporal validation issued afterwards reads the new instant. The supplied instant is accepted unconditionally once it parses: no monotonicity is enforced, so the clock may be wound backwards, set to the epoch itself, or set to an instant earlier than the epoch, and the operation answers HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object because there is no resource to return. Quote expiry, offer expiry, the renewal window, the post-event binding moratorium, inspection appointment windows, grant-cycle application windows and contractor certification expiry are all evaluated against this clock, and every system-written timestamp — `offered_at`, `responded_at`, `effective_at`, `expires_at`, `quote_expires_at`, `started_at`, `verified_at`, `recorded_at` — is the clock reading at the moment the write happened.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integer counts of cents; there are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response. Rating factors, cession shares, match percentages and loading rates are decimal multipliers, and every calculation evaluates its chain of multipliers in exact decimal arithmetic, in the order the calculation states, and rounds **exactly once, half up**, at the point the calculation names. Rounding half up means that a fractional remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero, to the next whole cent. Where a calculation performs two roundings — the final claim adjustment, or the mitigation discount and its prorated credit — the second rounding consumes the already-rounded first result and never the unrounded product. Where a total must be split across several rows, one designated row absorbs the residual so that the parts sum exactly to the whole rather than each part being rounded independently.

The single non-monetary computed quantity is the insurer take-up rate, which is expressed in whole basis points (hundredths of a percent) as an integer, rounded half up once.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Two Shared Ledgers

Two write operations mutate a ledger that other callers legitimately share, and both require the client to pin the ledger's version it last observed.

The fund-reservation operation on an approved retrofit grant requires a non-optional `expected_grant_cycle_version` in its body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `GrantCycle` the grant applies against. If that number does not equal the funding cycle's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_CYCLE_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Two different `APPROVED` grants may each legitimately draw from the same `OPEN` cycle while the pool still has room, so no state precondition excludes the second reviewer; the stale pinned version is the only signal that a reviewer is acting on a cycle balance that has already moved beneath them.

First notice of loss requires a non-optional `expected_event_version` in its body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CatastropheEvent` the loss is claimed against. A mismatch with the event's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Many claims may legitimately be filed against the same declared event, so a second filing is never blocked by a state precondition; the stale pinned version is what marks a filer whose picture of the event — including whether its capacity has already been exhausted by another adjuster's loss aggregation — predates the change.

No other operation takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no `expected_*_version` input and must not be rejected for concurrency reasons. On both of these two the pin is a required body field first and a concurrency pin second: omitting it, or sending it as null, is caught by the request-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), whereas a value that is present but is not a JSON integer — a numeric string, a float, a boolean — counts as a pin that fails to equal the current `version` and draws the 412 concurrency error rather than a shape error.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder and Its Priority Order

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Every check in the system therefore occupies a fixed rung on one ordered ladder, and **the rung with the lowest number wins**: the server evaluates the rungs in ascending numeric order and returns the first violation it finds, without evaluating any later rung. The rungs are grouped into bands whose order reads as authentication, then existence, then resource-scoped authorization, then request shape, then optimistic concurrency, then state preconditions, then safety and compliance, then referential integrity, then uniqueness and count limits, then temporal conflicts, then capacity, then fiscal conservation.

**Authentication band (rungs 1–3).** Rung 1: `X-User-Role` must be present and must name one of the seven declared roles, otherwise `ERR_MISSING_ROLE` (401); this applies to every endpoint. Rung 2: the named role must appear in the target endpoint's authorised role list, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403); this too applies to every endpoint. Rung 3: `X-User-ID` must be present on the five duty-segregated operations — recording a grant review decision, reserving cycle funds, recording an inspection result, posting the provisional adjustment and finalizing the adjustment — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (401); a header that is present but empty, or that holds nothing but whitespace, counts as absent, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed off the value before it is ever recorded on a row or compared with another handle.

**Existence band (rungs 4–5).** Rung 4: every identifier appearing in the path must resolve to an existing row, validated left to right, otherwise that resource kind's not-found code (404); this applies to every endpoint that carries a path identifier. Rung 5: every foreign-key identifier in the request body must resolve to an existing row — `insurer_id`, `policyholder_id`, `dwelling_id`, `policy_id`, `accepted_offer_id`, `grant_cycle_id`, `grant_id`, `contractor_id`, `project_id` and `event_id` — otherwise that kind's not-found code (404); this applies to every creating and linking operation. Both rungs sit above authorization deliberately: a caller cannot probe for the existence of a row it does not own, because it will be told the row is missing before it is told it is not the owner only when the row genuinely is missing.

**Resource-scoped authorization band (rungs 6–9).** Rung 6 is the ownership rule of section 2.1, producing `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) on every read and write performed by one of the three entity-backed roles. Because `X-User-ID` is mandatory only on the five duty-segregated operations, an entity-backed caller that omits the header entirely simply fails the ownership comparison and is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, never `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`. Rungs 7 through 9 are the three segregation-of-duties conflicts, each guarding exactly one operation: rung 7, the `X-User-ID` reserving cycle funds must differ from the grant's `approved_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SOD_GRANT_REVIEWER_CONFLICT` (403); rung 8, the `X-User-ID` recording an inspection result must differ from the `approved_by_user_id` of the grant funding the inspected project, otherwise `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_CONFLICT` (403) — this one conflict alone is evaluated only after the recorded `result` has passed its own presence and enum checks, so a conflicted inspector who also sends a missing, null or out-of-domain `result` is told about the request shape first; rung 9, the `X-User-ID` finalizing an adjustment must differ from the claim's `provisional_adjuster_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SOD_ADJUSTER_CONFLICT` (403). Each of the three compares the acting handle against a handle recorded earlier on a row, so where that recorded handle is null — nothing was ever written there — there is no conflict to find and the rung passes.

**Request-shape band (rungs 10–13).** Rung 10: every required body field must be present and not null, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint. Several further shape failures share this rung and this code, because the catalogue names nothing more specific for them: a request body that parses as JSON but is not an object; a required string field supplied empty or as whitespace only, but only where the field declares a non-blank value — a contractor's `business_name` and `license_no`, a catastrophe event's `event_code` and `epicenter_county_code`, and a claim denial's `denial_reason` — since every other required string field is policed for JSON string type alone, so that an empty string is an accepted `name`, `naic_code`, `full_name`, `email`, `county_code` or `street_address`; a timestamp-valued field whose value cannot be parsed as an ISO 8601 instant; an optional field supplied with the wrong JSON type, such as a non-string `reason` or a non-boolean `decline_documented`; and a collision on any of the four declared uniqueness constraints — an insurer's `naic_code`, a policyholder's `email`, a contractor's `license_no` and a catastrophe event's `event_code`. The uniqueness comparison is the last check a creating request performs at this rung, evaluated only once that request's enum, numeric and timestamp fields have each passed their own rungs, so a request that both collides and carries an out-of-domain enum member or an out-of-range amount reports that other failure instead of the collision. A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON at all is treated as an empty object, so it fails on the first required field it is missing. The body is inspected only by operations that declare body fields; an operation that declares none — binding a quote, re-extending an offer, administratively expiring one — never looks at the body, so whatever is sent alongside it is ignored rather than rejected, and on every operation a supplied field the operation does not declare is likewise ignored. A plain optional `reason` — accepted on the insurer compliance action, on cancellation and on discount revocation — is type-checked at this rung and then discarded: no row stores a `reason`, and it never appears in a response. On the four further operations where section 3 mentions an optional `reason` that the operation does not itself declare — withdrawing a grant, abandoning a project, changing a contractor's certification and reopening a claim — the field is not even type-checked, so a non-string `reason` is simply ignored there; those four do still read the body far enough to reject one that parses as JSON but is not an object. An optional field supplied explicitly as null is treated exactly as an omitted one and takes that field's declared default, so a null `campaign_code` becomes `NONE` and a null `decline_documented` becomes false rather than failing any check. Rung 11: every enum-typed field must carry a value from its declared domain, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422). The domains are `county_hazard_tier` in `TIER_1`, `TIER_2`, `TIER_3`; `soil_class` in `ROCK`, `STIFF`, `SOFT`, `LIQUEFACTION`; `structure_type` in `WOOD_FRAME_SLAB`, `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL`, `MASONRY_URM`, `MOBILE_HOME`; `deductible_pct` in 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25; `income_tier` in `TIER_LOW`, `TIER_MID`, `TIER_ANY`; every retrofit scope code — `requested_scope`, `declared_scope`, `scope_code` and the members of `certified_scopes` — in `CRIPPLE_WALL_BRACING`, `EARTHQUAKE_BRACING_SYSTEM`, `ANCHOR_BOLT_ONLY`; the grant review `decision` in `APPROVE`, `RETURN`, `DENY`; the inspection `result` in `PASS`, `PARTIAL`, `FAIL`; the policy lifecycle `action` in `LAPSE`, `EXPIRE_QUOTE`; the insurer compliance `action` in `SANCTION`, `REINSTATE`, `WITHDRAW`; the contractor `certification_status` supplied by an administrator in `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED`; the offer `response` in `ACCEPT`, `DECLINE`; and the reinsurance layer `status` in `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`. Two type rules ride on this rung. `deductible_pct` is an integer enum, so its value must be a JSON integer drawn from the domain and the string `"10"` is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` rather than an accepted 10; every other domain is a string enum, and a non-string value in one of them is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` as well. For the one list-valued enum, `certified_scopes`, a value that is not a list, or is an empty list, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 10, while a well-formed list carrying a member outside the domain is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` here. Rung 12: numeric domains must hold, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` (422) — every field whose name ends in `_cents` must be zero or greater, `dwelling_limit_cents` must be at least 5,000,000 and at most 300,000,000 cents, `total_pool_cents` must be at least 100,000 cents, `project_cost_estimate_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, a payment's `amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, `market_share_pct` must be between 0 and 100 inclusive with at most two decimal places, `magnitude` must be between 4.0 and 9.5 inclusive with one decimal place, `ceded_pct` must be strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 with at most two decimal places, a layer's `limit_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, `layer_index` must be at least 1, `treaty_year` must be zero or greater, and `year_built` must be between 1850 and 2026 inclusive; this rung guards dwelling creation, insurer enrollment, funding-cycle creation, catastrophe-event creation, reinsurance-layer creation, grant submission, first notice of loss and payment recording. The two numeric kinds are typed differently, and this rung polices the type as well as the range. An integer-valued field — every `_cents` amount, `year_built`, `layer_index` and `treaty_year` — must arrive as a JSON integer: a float such as `5000000.0`, a numeric string such as `"5000000"`, and a boolean are each `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` rather than a shape error. A decimal-valued field — `market_share_pct`, `magnitude` and `ceded_pct` — may arrive either as a JSON number or as a decimal string, is compared in exact decimal arithmetic, and yields `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` when it is unparseable, non-finite, outside its range, or carries more decimal places than the field declares. Rung 13: every declared interval must be non-empty — a funding cycle's `opens_at` must be strictly before its `closes_at`, a take-up evaluation's `window_start` strictly before its `window_end`, and an inspection's `window_start` strictly before its `window_end` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422).

**Optimistic-concurrency band (rungs 14–15).** Rung 14: `expected_grant_cycle_version` must equal the funding cycle's current `version` on fund reservation, otherwise `ERR_STALE_GRANT_CYCLE_VERSION` (412). Rung 15: `expected_event_version` must equal the event's current `version` on first notice of loss, otherwise `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (412). Both sit above every state precondition, so a client holding a stale pin learns that fact before it learns anything about the target's state.

**State-precondition band (rungs 16–34).** Rung 16: a `MandatoryOffer` must be `PENDING` to be responded to or administratively expired, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_ALREADY_RESPONDED` (409). Rung 17: an offer must be `DECLINED` or `ACCEPTED` to be re-extended — a `PENDING` or `EXPIRED` offer cannot be — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_REOFFERABLE` (409). Rung 18: a policy's `lifecycle_status` must be `QUOTED` to be bound or to have its quote administratively expired, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_QUOTED` (409). Rung 19: a policy's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE` to be renewed, cancelled, lapsed, to have a mitigation discount revoked, to have a claim filed against it, or — where a retrofit grant names a `policy_id` at all — for that grant's review to be approved, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Rung 20: revoking a mitigation discount requires `mitigation_status` to be `DISCOUNT_APPLIED` and `mitigation_discount_cents` to be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_MITIGATION_DISCOUNT` (409). Rung 21: a `GrantCycle` must be `OPEN` to accept a new grant application, to have a review opened on one of its grants, to have a review decision approved against it, to have funds reserved from it, and to be closed at all — a second close of an already `CLOSED` cycle draws the same code — otherwise `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_CLOSED` (409). Rung 22: a `RetrofitGrant` must be in the status its action requires — `SUBMITTED` to begin review, `UNDER_REVIEW` to record a review decision, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` to resubmit, `APPROVED` to reserve funds, and any of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, `APPROVED` or `FUNDS_RESERVED` to withdraw — otherwise `ERR_GRANT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Rung 23: a `RetrofitProject` must be in the status its action requires — `SCHEDULED` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` to start work, `IN_PROGRESS` to request inspection, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` both for an inspection to be booked against it and for that inspection's outcome to be recorded, and any of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` to abandon — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Rung 24: an `Inspection` must still have `result` equal to `PENDING` for an outcome to be recorded on it, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409). Rung 25: an event must be `MONITORING` to be declared, and must be `MONITORING`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION` or `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` to be closed, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Rung 26: an event must not be `CLOSED` for any operation that touches its loss aggregate — first notice of loss, finalizing an adjustment, reopening a claim, levying the assessment and closing the event itself — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_CLOSED` (409). Rung 27: an event must not be `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` for any operation that would move the loss aggregate after the allocations were published — first notice of loss, finalizing an adjustment, reopening a claim and levying again — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_ASSESSMENT_LOCKED` (409). Rung 28: an event must be `DECLARED`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION` or `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` both for a claim to be filed against it and for that claim's provisional adjustment to be posted, since an event still `MONITORING` accepts no claims, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_DECLARED` (409); the provisional adjustment tests only this rung and not the two above it, so an event that has since reached `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` or `CLOSED` fails the provisional pass with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_DECLARED` rather than with either of those two codes. Rung 29: an event must be `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` for an assessment to be levied, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_NOT_EXHAUSTED` (409). Rung 30: every claim on an event must have reached a terminal `adjustment_status`, meaning `CLOSED` or `DENIED`, before the event may be closed, otherwise `ERR_EVENT_HAS_OPEN_CLAIMS` (409). Rung 31: a `Claim` must be in the status its action requires — `FNOL_RECEIVED` to post the provisional adjustment or to deny, `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED` to finalize, `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED` or `FINAL_ADJUSTED` to record a payment, and `FINAL_ADJUSTED` either to reopen or to close — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` (409). Rung 32: a claim's `payment_status` must be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` to reopen it and equally to record a further payment against it, because a claim that is already `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` can be neither reopened nor paid again, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409). Rung 33: a claim's `reopen_count` must be strictly less than two to reopen it, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_REOPEN_LIMIT` (409). Rung 34: a claim's `payment_status` must be `PAID` for the claim to be closed, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FULLY_PAID` (409). Two operations deviate from strict ascending order across this band, and the deviation is part of the contract. On beginning a grant review and on recording a review decision, the grant's own status at rung 22 is tested before the funding cycle's `OPEN` requirement at rung 21, before the policy's `ACTIVE` requirement at rung 19 on an approval, and before the optional `denial_reason`'s type check at rung 10, so a grant in the wrong status reports `ERR_GRANT_INVALID_STATE` even where the cycle is closed, the policy inactive or the reason malformed. And on posting the provisional adjustment, the claim's own status at rung 31 is tested before the event's intake-status requirement at rung 28. Reserving cycle funds, submitting and resubmitting an application, withdrawing one, finalizing an adjustment, reopening, paying and closing a claim all follow the ascending order exactly.

**Safety and compliance band (rungs 35–44).** Rung 35: the insurer's `compliance_status` must not be `SANCTIONED` in order to record a mandatory offer through it, to quote, to bind or to renew, otherwise `ERR_INSURER_SANCTIONED` (409). Rung 36: the insurer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` to write *new* business through it — recording an offer, quoting and binding — while a `WITHDRAWN` insurer may still renew its in-force book and remains assessable, otherwise `ERR_INSURER_WITHDRAWN` (409). Rung 37: applying the `REINSTATE` compliance action requires `compliance_status` to be `SANCTIONED`, and applying `SANCTION` requires it to be `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_INSURER_NOT_SANCTIONED` (409). Rung 38: applying the `WITHDRAW` compliance action requires `status` to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_INSURER_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN` (409). Rung 39: the contractor's `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED` both when a project is created for it and again when an inspection is booked for that project, since a contractor on `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` can neither take on new retrofit work nor be inspected for it, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409). Rung 40: the contractor's `certification_expires_at` must be strictly later than the current logical clock, both when a project is created and when an inspection is booked, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (409). Rung 41: an administratively requested `certification_status` must be reachable from the current one — `SUSPENDED` only from `CERTIFIED`, and `CERTIFIED` only from `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, with `PROBATION` never settable by this operation because only the failed-inspection cascade sets it — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_TRANSITION` (409). Rung 42: recording a passing result requires the inspected project's `scope_code` to be one of the members of the inspecting contractor's `certified_scopes`, because work outside a contractor's certified scopes cannot be verified, otherwise `ERR_RETROFIT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409). Rung 43: an event's `magnitude` must be at least 5.0 to be declared, otherwise `ERR_MAGNITUDE_BELOW_DECLARATION_FLOOR` (422). Rung 44: the dwelling's `structure_type` must be one that the retrofit program funds at all, otherwise `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_GRANT_ELIGIBLE` (422) on the review decision.

**Referential-integrity band (rungs 45–51).** Rung 45: `accepted_offer_id` must name a `MandatoryOffer` whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `insurer_id` and `dwelling_id` both match the policy's, checked at quote time, again at bind and again at every renewal, otherwise `ERR_MANDATORY_OFFER_NOT_ACCEPTED` (409). Rung 46: an offer's `policyholder_id` must equal the `policyholder_id` of the dwelling it covers, and a grant's `policy_id`, when supplied, must name a policy whose `dwelling_id` equals the grant's own `dwelling_id`, otherwise `ERR_DWELLING_OWNER_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 47: a project's `grant_id` must name a grant whose status is `FUNDS_RESERVED`, because work cannot start on money that is merely approved, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_NOT_RESERVED` (409). Rung 48: an inspection may be booked only against a project in `AWAITING_INSPECTION`, and its `contractor_id` is copied from that project, so a caller-supplied `contractor_id` that disagrees with the project's is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 49: the event's `treaty_year` must have at least one `ReinsuranceLayer` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` before the event may be declared and before any claim may be filed against it, otherwise `ERR_NO_REINSURANCE_PROGRAM` (409). Rung 50: a layer's `layer_index` must be unique within its `treaty_year`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LAYER_INDEX` (409). Rung 51: a layer whose `layer_index` is greater than 1 must attach at or above the sum of the previous index's `attachment_point_cents` and `limit_cents` within the same `treaty_year`, so that towers never overlap downward, otherwise `ERR_LAYER_ATTACHMENT_GAP` (422).

**Uniqueness and count band (rungs 52–57).** Rung 52: at most one policy per `dwelling_id` may be live, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_POLICY` (409), and the statuses that count as live differ between the two points at which the rung fires: at quote time a sibling policy on the same dwelling in `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE` *or* `QUOTED` blocks the new quote, whereas at bind only a sibling already `ACTIVE` blocks it, so a second quote left standing on the same dwelling never prevents the first from being bound. Rung 53: at most one offer per insurer-and-dwelling pair may be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PENDING_OFFER` (409), enforced both when an offer is recorded and when one is re-extended. Rung 54: at most one grant per `dwelling_id` may sit in a non-terminal status, meaning any of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, `APPROVED` or `FUNDS_RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_GRANT` (409). Rung 55: at most one claim per policy-and-event pair may have an `adjustment_status` other than `DENIED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (409). Rung 56: a grant's `review_round` must be strictly less than three for the reviewer to return it for more information again, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_REVIEW_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Rung 57: a contractor may hold at most three projects in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` at any time, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_PROJECT_CAP` (409).

**Temporal-conflict band (rungs 58–69).** Rung 58: an inspection window's length must be at least 30 minutes and at most 240 minutes, both bounds inclusive, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_DURATION_INVALID` (422). Rung 59: an inspection's `window_start` must be at or after the current logical clock, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_IN_PAST` (422). Rung 60: the contractor calendar must be free, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_SLOT_OVERLAP` (409). Rung 61: the logical clock must have reached the inspection's `window_start` before a result may be recorded, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED` (409). Rung 62: a new funding cycle's application window must not collide with any other cycle on the same `program_track`, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Rung 63: the logical clock must not have passed `quote_expires_at` when the quote is bound, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409). Rung 64: the logical clock must be strictly after `quote_expires_at` for the quote to be expired administratively, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_STILL_VALID` (409). Rung 65: the logical clock must not have passed the offer's `expires_at` when the policyholder responds, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409). Rung 66: the logical clock must be strictly after the offer's `expires_at` for the offer to be expired administratively, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_STILL_VALID` (409). Rung 67: the logical clock must lie inside the renewal window, otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409). Rung 68: the binding moratorium must not be in force for the policyholder's county, otherwise `ERR_BINDING_MORATORIUM` (409). Rung 69: the event must have struck inside the policy term, meaning its `occurred_at` is at or after the policy's `effective_at` and strictly before the policy's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE_AT_EVENT` (409). The precise boundary semantics of rungs 60, 62, 67 and 68 are given with the workflows they guard.

**Capacity band (rungs 71–72).** Rung 71: at least one participating insurer must be assessable, meaning its `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and its `market_share_pct` is strictly greater than zero, otherwise the levy is rejected with `ERR_NO_ASSESSABLE_INSURERS` (409). Rung 72: the grant's own income-tier bucket, or the shared fallback bucket, must have room for the award, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_TIER_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (409) on fund reservation.

**Fiscal band (rungs 73–75).** Rung 73: the funding cycle's `reserved_cents` plus its `disbursed_cents` plus the grant's `approved_amount_cents` must not exceed `total_pool_cents`, and this ceiling is evaluated *after* a tier bucket has been resolved, so a bucket with room can still hit the pool ceiling; the failure is `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (409). Rung 74: `contents_sublimit_cents` and `loss_of_use_sublimit_cents` must each be no greater than the dwelling's `dwelling_limit_cents` at quote time, otherwise `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` (422). Rung 75: a payment's `amount_cents` must be no greater than the claim's `payable_cents` less its `paid_cents`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYABLE` (422); the strictly-positive half of that rule is a numeric-domain check at rung 12 instead, so a zero or negative amount is `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` and only a positive amount ever reaches this rung.

**One further rung (76).** A dwelling's `retrofit_status` must not already be `VERIFIED` for a policyholder to self-attest a retrofit on it, because an inspection-verified retrofit cannot be overwritten by an attestation; the rejection is `ERR_RETROFIT_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (409). This is the only domain rule that can fire on the self-attestation operation, so its position at the bottom of the ladder carries no priority-conflict meaning.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline Across Aggregates

Several operations in this program are deliberately multi-hop: one request mutates its direct target and then propagates through two, three or four further aggregates in a fixed order. Two properties hold for every such cascade. First, each row the cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented — a request that moves a project, a dwelling, a grant, a funding cycle and a policy increments five `version` counters, not one. A row counts as touched when the request actually writes a field on it, and the write counts even where the value written is identical to the value already stored — with one deliberate exception, in the loss waterfall of section 3.7: a participating `ReinsuranceLayer` whose recomputed `ceded_cents` equals the figure already stored is not rewritten at all, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move, and re-running the waterfall over an unchanged gross bumps no layer's version. Second, cascades are conditional at each hop rather than unconditional: a hop whose own precondition does not hold simply does not fire, and the hops after it that depended on it do not fire either, while the hops that did fire stand. The verification cascade behind a passing inspection and the compensation cascade behind reopening a claim are the two deepest examples, and each is described in full in its own workflow subsection.

Where a cascade reverses an earlier one, the reversal is a **compensating** movement rather than a recomputation from scratch: releasing a grant reservation subtracts exactly the amount that was added, and reopening a claim subtracts exactly the finalized amount that was previously accumulated into the event, so that the ledger returns to the value it would have had if the original action had never happened.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Public money and catastrophe settlements each require two distinct human hands, and the program enforces this by recording operator identities on rows and comparing them later. The `GrantReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `approved_by_user_id` when an award was approved may not be the identity that later reserves that award's funds; the reserving identity is recorded as `reserved_by_user_id`, and the two must differ. The same approving identity is also barred from recording the verifying inspection result on the project that the award funds, so an approver can neither release nor certify its own money. And the `ClaimAdjuster` recorded as `provisional_adjuster_user_id` may not be the identity recorded as `final_adjuster_user_id`. These three conflicts sit at rungs 7, 8 and 9 of the ladder respectively, above every state check — and, on fund reservation and on finalization, above the shape checks too, while the inspector conflict alone is evaluated after the inspection `result`'s own shape and enum checks — so a conflicted operator is told about the conflict rather than about the target's state.

### 2.11. Flat Addressing and Filtered Discovery

Every entity in this program is addressed at its own top-level collection; no resource is nested inside another resource's URL. Parent-child linkage is expressed only through identifier fields in request bodies, and the reverse direction — all of a cycle's grants, all of an event's claims, all of a contractor's inspections, all of an insurer's allocations — is discovered by filtering the child's own list collection. Section 4 lists every filter each collection accepts. Ownership does not produce an error on a list read: for an entity-backed caller the result set is first silently narrowed to the rows whose corresponding identifier equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and only then are the query filters applied, so `total_count` counts only rows inside the caller's own scope and a row outside it is simply absent rather than refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — a `Policyholder` filtering the dwelling list by another party's `policyholder_id` gets an empty page, not a 403. Where the collection carries no identifier of the caller's kind — an insurer reading the dwelling list — the read is not narrowed at all. Two collections are read-only in practice: assessment allocations exist solely as the output of a levy and can never be created, modified or deleted directly, and they are the only record of how a shortfall was spread.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Admitting Insurers and Policing the Mandatory Offer Duty

A `ProgramAdmin` admits a residential carrier into the residual-market program by enrolling it with its legal `name`, its `naic_code` — nominally the carrier's five-digit NAIC number, which must be unique across all participating insurers, though the server checks only that it is a JSON string and never its length or its digit composition — and its `market_share_pct`, the residential market share in percent that is the *sole* basis on which post-event assessments are later allocated. `market_share_pct` may arrive as a JSON number or as a decimal string, and it is stored and echoed back in exactly the form it arrived in rather than normalised — a decimal string is returned as a string — and is read into exact decimal arithmetic only where the assessment machinery of section 3.8 needs it. Enrollment is also the one place where the numeric rung is reached before the shape rung: an out-of-range, over-precise or wrongly typed `market_share_pct` is reported as `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` even when the same request simultaneously carries a non-string `name` or `naic_code`, or a `naic_code` that collides with an existing carrier's, both of which would otherwise be `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A freshly enrolled insurer starts with `status` set to `ACTIVE` and `compliance_status` set to `COMPLIANT`, and with all five of its running counters at zero: `offers_recorded_count`, `undocumented_decline_count`, `last_take_up_rate_bp`, `assessment_balance_cents` and `policies_bound_count`. The `status` dimension records participation and takes the values `ACTIVE` and `WITHDRAWN`; the `compliance_status` dimension records the carrier's posture on the offer duty and takes the values `COMPLIANT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` and `SANCTIONED`. The two dimensions advance on entirely different triggers and are never conflated.

Registering the household side is deliberately simple. A policyholder record carries the owner-occupant's `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across the registry and is the address the program's public-education outreach targets, the `county_code` of the mailing address — nominally five characters, but accepted as any JSON string with no length check — and an optional `outreach_campaign_code` that defaults to the string `NONE` and exists so that the program can report take-up by campaign. Registry `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and may also be `INACTIVE`.

The statutory core of the program is the **mandatory offer**: an admitted insurer must extend the earthquake product to its residential customers, and the program records each such offer as its own row. Recording an offer requires the payload to name the discharging insurer through `insurer_id`, the recipient through `policyholder_id` and the covered structure through `dwelling_id`, and it may carry a `campaign_code` attributing the offer to a public-education campaign, which defaults to `NONE`. The recorded offer opens in `PENDING` with `offer_round` set to 1, `offered_at` set to the current logical clock and `expires_at` set to sixty days after `offered_at`; `decline_documented` opens false, `responded_at` stays null while the offer is unanswered, and the insurer's `offers_recorded_count` rises by one — a write on the insurer row, so the insurer's own `updated_at` and `version` move as well as the offer's being minted. An offer may not be recorded through an insurer whose `compliance_status` is `SANCTIONED`, nor through one whose `status` is `WITHDRAWN`, and the named policyholder must be the same party as the dwelling's own `policyholder_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_DWELLING_OWNER_MISMATCH`. Only one offer per insurer-and-dwelling pair may be `PENDING` at a time; a second attempt collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PENDING_OFFER`.

The policyholder answers the offer, moving it `PENDING → ACCEPTED` or `PENDING → DECLINED`, by supplying a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`; only the owner named on the offer may answer it. The answer stamps `responded_at` with the clock reading, and the answer must arrive while the clock is still at or before `expires_at` — one instant later and the response is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. A decline additionally carries `decline_documented`, a boolean that defaults to false when the field is omitted. The flag's type is policed on every response, accept or decline alike, so a non-boolean value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even on an `ACCEPT`; but it is *recorded* only on a decline, and a flag sent alongside an `ACCEPT` is discarded, leaving the accepted offer's `decline_documented` false. This default is the enforcement hook of the whole compliance regime: **an undocumented decline is one where the program has no written record that the household actually refused**, and every such decline raises the insurer's `undocumented_decline_count` by one. A documented decline raises nothing.

An unanswered offer can be retired administratively, moving `PENDING → EXPIRED`, but only once the clock has passed `expires_at`; attempting it earlier is refused with `ERR_OFFER_STILL_VALID`, and attempting it on an offer that has already been answered is refused with `ERR_OFFER_ALREADY_RESPONDED`. Insurers, meanwhile, carry an annual duty to repeat the offer, and re-extending an offer is the mechanism: an offer in `DECLINED` or `ACCEPTED` returns to `PENDING`, its `offer_round` rises by one, `offered_at` is reset to the current clock, `expires_at` is recomputed as sixty days after that new instant, `responded_at` is cleared back to null and `decline_documented` is reset to false. Where the offer being re-extended was a `DECLINED` one whose `decline_documented` was false, the insurer's `undocumented_decline_count` is *decremented* by one, because the new round supersedes the old refusal; that decrement is unconditional and is not floored at zero, so a counter a take-up evaluation has just rewritten downwards can be driven negative. What re-extension does not do is raise `offers_recorded_count` again: the new round reuses the same offer row rather than recording a fresh offer, so only the decline counter can move. Re-extension is available to program staff, and among insurers only to the one that owns the offer, and only from `DECLINED` or `ACCEPTED`: a `PENDING` or `EXPIRED` offer produces `ERR_OFFER_NOT_REOFFERABLE`. There is a sting in the tail — re-extending an `ACCEPTED` offer un-accepts it, and any policy that named that offer as the basis of its coverage can no longer roll into its next term until the household accepts the new round. That is enforced as a precondition of the renewal, not as a mutation of the policy.

Take-up is measured on demand, over a half-open window supplied as `window_start` and `window_end`. The evaluation considers exactly those of the insurer's offers whose `offered_at` is at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, and whose status is one of `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` — offers still `PENDING` count in neither the numerator nor the denominator. Within that set, the numerator is the count in `ACCEPTED` and the denominator is the count of all considered offers; the take-up rate in basis points is ten thousand multiplied by the accepted count divided by the considered count, rounded half up to a whole basis point, and is reported as zero when nothing was considered at all. The evaluation writes the result to `last_take_up_rate_bp`, writes the considered count to `offers_recorded_count`, and rewrites `undocumented_decline_count` as the count of considered offers that are `DECLINED` with `decline_documented` false. Note the interaction with re-extension: because a re-extended offer is back in `PENDING` and its `offered_at` has been moved forward, re-extending a declined offer removes it from the denominator entirely and can raise the measured rate — and can also move a row out of an earlier window altogether.

Two independent consequences are then evaluated, and both are evaluated on every run. If the recomputed `undocumented_decline_count` is at least three, the insurer's `compliance_status` becomes `SANCTIONED`; this path may jump straight from `COMPLIANT` without passing through `UNDER_REVIEW`. Otherwise, if at least four offers were considered *and* the computed rate is strictly below 2500 basis points — that is, below a twenty-five percent take-up — *and* the insurer is currently `COMPLIANT`, then `compliance_status` moves `COMPLIANT → UNDER_REVIEW`. Sanction wins whenever both conditions fire. Fewer than four considered offers is treated as statistically meaningless: no downgrade occurs at any measured rate, including the case where nothing was considered and the rate is reported as zero.

Compliance can also be moved by hand. A regulator action carries an `action` of `SANCTION`, `REINSTATE` or `WITHDRAW` together with an optional `reason`. `SANCTION` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → SANCTIONED` and is refused with `ERR_INSURER_NOT_SANCTIONED` when the carrier is not currently under review; a sanctioned insurer can neither bind nor renew policies and is excluded from the assessable pool. `REINSTATE` moves `SANCTIONED → COMPLIANT` and is likewise refused with `ERR_INSURER_NOT_SANCTIONED` when the carrier is not sanctioned; reinstatement deliberately does **not** reset `undocumented_decline_count`, because that counter is the audit trail of the original failure. `WITHDRAW` moves the participation dimension `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN` and is refused with `ERR_INSURER_ALREADY_WITHDRAWN` if the carrier has already withdrawn; a withdrawn insurer writes no new business but its existing in-force policies are untouched, it may still renew them, and — importantly for the assessment machinery — it remains assessable for events that occurred before its withdrawal.

### 3.2. Dwellings, Hazard Characteristics and the Self-Attested Retrofit

Underwriting in this program is entirely a function of the structure, so a dwelling record is a hazard description first and an address second. Creating one requires the owner through `policyholder_id`, the `street_address` of the insured structure, the `county_hazard_tier` of its county — `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, in ascending seismicity — the site's `soil_class` from the geotechnical screen, which is `ROCK`, `STIFF`, `SOFT` or `LIQUEFACTION`, the `structure_type`, which is `WOOD_FRAME_SLAB`, `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL`, `MASONRY_URM` or `MOBILE_HOME`, the `year_built` of original construction, and `dwelling_limit_cents`, the Coverage A dwelling limit that is the exposure base for both rating and the deductible. The vintage matters because construction from 1979 or earlier is pre-code and rates accordingly. A new dwelling opens with `retrofit_status` at `NONE`, `verified_retrofit_scope` at `NONE` and `grant_lifetime_count` at zero. Where the creating caller is a `Policyholder` rather than program staff, the ownership rule is applied to the `policyholder_id` the body names, so an owner may record a dwelling only for itself and naming another party draws `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; that comparison happens at its own rung, ahead of the required-field, enum and numeric checks, and is skipped altogether when `policyholder_id` is absent or null so that the missing identifier is what gets reported.

Mitigation posture is tracked in two coupled fields. `retrofit_status` takes the values `NONE`, `SELF_DECLARED` and `VERIFIED`, and `verified_retrofit_scope` names which retrofit is being claimed: `NONE`, `CRIPPLE_WALL_BRACING`, `EARTHQUAKE_BRACING_SYSTEM` or `ANCHOR_BOLT_ONLY`. There are exactly two ways to leave `NONE`. The first is the owner's own attestation: a `Policyholder` self-declares a completed retrofit by supplying a `declared_scope`, which moves `retrofit_status` to `SELF_DECLARED` and writes `declared_scope` into `verified_retrofit_scope`. Self-attestation is refused with `ERR_RETROFIT_ALREADY_VERIFIED` once `retrofit_status` has reached `VERIFIED`, because a physical inspection outranks a homeowner's word and can never be overwritten by one. From `NONE` or from `SELF_DECLARED` it always succeeds: a repeat attestation is not a duplicate to be refused but an amendment, rewriting `verified_retrofit_scope` with the newly declared scope and bumping the dwelling's `version` again. The attestation writes the dwelling row and nothing else — no policy is re-rated, no `base_premium_cents`, `annual_premium_cents` or `mitigation_status` moves, and a term already in force keeps the rating it was written with; the attestation is felt only by the next quote. The second way is the verification cascade behind a passing inspection, described in section 3.6, which is the only route to `VERIFIED`.

The distinction between the two routes has a price attached, and it is the single most consequential asymmetry in the rating engine: an attestation earns a discount on the term you buy it in and loses that discount the moment the policy rolls over. Section 3.3 gives the arithmetic; the summary is that the quote honours a `SELF_DECLARED` retrofit and the renewal treats it as though the retrofit did not exist.

`grant_lifetime_count` is a lifetime tally of how many retrofit grants on this dwelling ever reached `FUNDS_RESERVED` or beyond. It rises by one at the moment cycle money is actually committed to an award for the dwelling, not when an application is filed, and it is never decremented — a withdrawn reservation releases the money but not the historical fact that the dwelling drew on the pool.

### 3.3. Quoting the Coverage: Deductibles, Sublimits and the Hazard Rate

A quote is produced by a participating insurer (or by program staff acting for one) and is the first appearance of a `Policy` row. The payload names the rated structure through `dwelling_id`, the quoting carrier through `insurer_id`, and the accepted offer that authorises the sale through `accepted_offer_id`; it selects the household's `deductible_pct`, which must be one of 5, 10, 15, 20 or 25 percent of the dwelling limit; and it sets the two coverage sublimits, `contents_sublimit_cents` for Coverage C and `loss_of_use_sublimit_cents` for Coverage D. Each sublimit must be strictly greater than zero and no larger than the named dwelling's `dwelling_limit_cents`; a sublimit above the dwelling limit is refused with `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. The two halves of that rule fail differently and at different rungs: a sublimit of zero or below, or one that is not a JSON integer at all, is a numeric-domain violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT` at rung 12, and only a strictly positive sublimit that exceeds the dwelling limit survives to rung 74 and `ERR_SUBLIMIT_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. The quote copies the dwelling's owner into the policy's own `policyholder_id` so that ownership checks on the policy need no join, and it pins `rated_dwelling_limit_cents` from the dwelling's live `dwelling_limit_cents`. That pin is the claim-side snapshot: every deductible computed for a loss on this term uses the snapshot and never the live dwelling field, so that raising a dwelling's limit mid-term cannot retroactively enlarge the deductible on a loss already incurred.

The quote opens with `lifecycle_status` at `QUOTED`, `mitigation_status` at `UNMITIGATED`, `term_number` at 1, `mitigation_discount_cents` and `prorated_credit_cents` at zero, and both `effective_at` and `expires_at` null, since nothing is in force yet. `quote_expires_at` is set to thirty days after the row's `created_at`. A quote cannot be produced through a `SANCTIONED` or `WITHDRAWN` insurer, the offer named in `accepted_offer_id` must be in `ACCEPTED` with its own `insurer_id` and `dwelling_id` matching the policy's, and at most one policy per dwelling may sit in `ACTIVE` or `QUOTED` — a second live quote on the same structure collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_POLICY`.

**The hazard rate.** Rating runs at quote time and again at each renewal, and it reads exactly one dwelling — the one named by the policy — together with the policy's own `deductible_pct`. It reads no other policy, claim or grant. The dwelling's fields are read live at both moments. The result is written to `base_premium_cents`, and `rating_variant` records which of the two variants produced it, taking the value `QUOTE_TIME` or `RENEWAL_TIME`.

The computation proceeds as follows. First, the exposure is expressed in units of one thousand dollars of Coverage A limit: divide `dwelling_limit_cents` by one hundred thousand, which is the number of cents in one thousand dollars. Second, take the county's base rate per thousand dollars of limit, which is 180 cents for `TIER_1`, 320 cents for `TIER_2` and 520 cents for `TIER_3`. Third, collect six multiplicative factors:

- the **soil factor** — 0.85 for `ROCK`, 1.00 for `STIFF`, 1.25 for `SOFT` and 1.55 for `LIQUEFACTION`;
- the **structure factor** — 1.00 for `WOOD_FRAME_SLAB`, 1.30 for `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL`, 1.85 for `MASONRY_URM` and 1.45 for `MOBILE_HOME`;
- the **age factor** — 1.20 when `year_built` is 1979 or earlier, and 1.00 for anything built in 1980 or later;
- the **retrofit factor**, described below;
- the **deductible factor** — 1.35 at a 5 percent deductible, 1.00 at 10 percent, 0.86 at 15 percent, 0.75 at 20 percent and 0.66 at 25 percent;
- the **high-limit load** — 1.15 when `dwelling_limit_cents` is at least 75,000,000 cents, which is a Coverage A limit of seven hundred and fifty thousand dollars or more, and 1.00 below that.

The retrofit factor is where the two variants diverge. Both variants begin from the dwelling's stored `retrofit_status`, but the renewal variant first downgrades a `SELF_DECLARED` posture to `NONE`, so that only an inspection-verified retrofit survives into a renewal rating; the quote variant takes `retrofit_status` exactly as stored. From that effective posture, the factor is 0.75 when the posture is `VERIFIED` *and* the `structure_type` is `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL` *and* the `verified_retrofit_scope` is `CRIPPLE_WALL_BRACING` — the deepest discount in the program, reserved for the one retrofit that addresses the one failure mode; 0.90 when the posture is `VERIFIED` for any other combination of structure and scope; 0.95 when the effective posture is `SELF_DECLARED`, which can only ever happen under the quote variant; and 1.00 when the effective posture is `NONE`. The practical consequence is that an unverified attestation which earned a five percent credit on the first term costs five percent more at renewal on byte-identical dwelling data.

Multiply the base rate per thousand by the exposure units and then by all six factors, in that order, keeping exact decimal arithmetic throughout. Clamp the unrounded product upward to a floor of 18,000 cents and downward to a ceiling of 4,000,000 cents — that is, no annual base premium is ever less than one hundred and eighty dollars or more than forty thousand dollars. Only after the clamp is the value rounded, half up, exactly once, to whole cents, and that integer becomes `base_premium_cents`. Finally, `annual_premium_cents` — the amount actually billed — is `base_premium_cents` less whatever `mitigation_discount_cents` currently stands at, which at quote time is zero.

### 3.4. Binding, Renewing and Ending a Term

Binding is the exclusive privilege of the participating insurer and moves the coverage dimension `QUOTED → ACTIVE`. It requires the policy to still be `QUOTED`, and it requires the logical clock to be at or before `quote_expires_at`; a stale quote is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The offer behind the policy must still be in `ACCEPTED` — the check is repeated here and not merely trusted from quote time — the insurer must be `ACTIVE` with `compliance_status` of either `COMPLIANT` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, and no other policy on the same dwelling may already be `ACTIVE`. On success, `effective_at` is set to the current clock, `expires_at` to three hundred and sixty-five days later, `rated_dwelling_limit_cents` is re-snapshotted from the dwelling's live limit, and the insurer's `policies_bound_count` rises by one — a write on the insurer row, so its `version` moves alongside the policy's. `quote_expires_at` is left standing at the value the quote wrote; binding neither clears nor rewrites it. Binding does **not** re-rate: `base_premium_cents` and `annual_premium_cents` are the numbers the quote produced under the `QUOTE_TIME` variant, and `rating_variant` stays `QUOTE_TIME`.

One further gate applies only at bind, and it is a catastrophe-response rule rather than an underwriting rule. **The binding moratorium** forbids new coverage from being bound into a county that has just been shaken. If any catastrophe event whose `status` is `DECLARED`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION`, `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` or `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` has an `epicenter_county_code` equal to the `county_code` of the policy's policyholder, and the logical clock lies at or after that event's `occurred_at` and strictly before sixty days after `occurred_at`, the bind is refused with `ERR_BINDING_MORATORIUM`. The window is half-open at the far end: exactly sixty days after the event, binding in that county resumes.

Renewal is the cycle in the coverage dimension — `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` — and it may be exercised only inside the renewal window, which opens forty-five days before `expires_at` and closes at `expires_at` itself: the clock must be at or after forty-five days before expiry and strictly before expiry, or the request is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The insurer must not be `SANCTIONED` (a merely `WITHDRAWN` carrier may still renew its in-force book), and the offer named by `accepted_offer_id` must be in `ACCEPTED`, which is what makes a re-extended offer block the roll-over until the household re-accepts. A renewal does a great deal at once: `term_number` rises by one; the old `expires_at` becomes the new `effective_at` and the new `expires_at` is three hundred and sixty-five days after that, so terms abut without gap or overlap; `rated_dwelling_limit_cents` is re-snapshotted from the dwelling's current limit; `base_premium_cents` is recomputed under the `RENEWAL_TIME` variant and `rating_variant` is set to `RENEWAL_TIME`; both `mitigation_discount_cents` and `prorated_credit_cents` are reset to zero, and `annual_premium_cents` becomes equal to the freshly computed `base_premium_cents`. If `mitigation_status` was `DISCOUNT_APPLIED`, it steps back to `RETROFIT_VERIFIED` as part of the same renewal, and any other value it held is left exactly as it stands. What a renewal does *not* touch is the insurer: `policies_bound_count` counts first bindings only and is never raised again by a roll-over, so a renewal writes the policy row alone and moves no insurer's `version`. Nor is the binding moratorium re-tested at renewal, and `quote_expires_at` is left as it stands. The reason for that reset is worth stating plainly: the mid-term discount channel closes at renewal, and a verified retrofit is thereafter reflected through the renewal rating's retrofit factor instead, so that the two channels can never stack into a double credit.

Ending a term happens four ways. **Cancellation** moves `ACTIVE → CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and may be requested by the household that owns the policy, by the insurer that wrote it, or by program staff; where the caller is one of the two entity-backed roles, its `X-User-ID` must match the policy's `policyholder_id` or `insurer_id` respectively, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. An optional `reason` may accompany the request. **Lapse** moves `ACTIVE → LAPSED`, also terminal, and is an administrative action for non-payment, requested by supplying an `action` of `LAPSE`. Both cancellation and lapse carry the same conditional grant cascade: every retrofit grant on the policy whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` or `APPROVED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN`, because the coverage that made the dwelling grant-eligible is gone, while grants already in `FUNDS_RESERVED` or `VERIFIED_COMPLETE` are left exactly as they are, because their money is already committed and unwinding it is a separate, deliberate act. Neither cancellation nor lapse changes `mitigation_status`. **Administrative quote expiry** moves `QUOTED → QUOTE_EXPIRED`, terminal, and is requested by supplying an `action` of `EXPIRE_QUOTE`; it requires the clock to be strictly past `quote_expires_at`, and a quote that is still inside its window is protected by `ERR_QUOTE_STILL_VALID`. The fourth way is simply that the term runs out; that is a data condition rather than an operation, and the program models the deliberate endings only.

### 3.5. The Retrofit Grant Programme: Funding Cycles, Adjudication and Commitment of Money

Public retrofit money is appropriated in **funding cycles**, each belonging to exactly one `program_track` — nominally `BRACE_AND_BOLT`, `MOBILE_HOME` or `SOFT_STORY`, though `program_track` is not one of the enum domains of section 2.8 and the server checks only that it is a JSON string, so any string whatever names a track of its own for the window-collision rule and the list filter alike — and each carrying a human `label` such as "2026 Fall", itself policed for JSON string type alone and accepted empty, an application window bounded by `opens_at` and `closes_at`, and a `total_pool_cents` of at least 100,000 cents. Windows may not collide within a track: the closed interval running from `opens_at` through `closes_at` inclusive must not intersect the closed interval of any other cycle on the same `program_track`, in *any* status, since a closed cycle still reserves its historical window. There is no buffer and no tolerance — two cycles that merely touch at an endpoint do collide, and the second is refused with `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A new cycle opens with `status` `OPEN`, `reserved_cents` and `disbursed_cents` at zero, `uncommitted_cents` equal to `total_pool_cents`, and `version` at 1. Closing a cycle moves `status` `OPEN → CLOSED`, which refuses both new applications and new reservations while leaving money already reserved exactly where it is. Closing is available from `OPEN` alone: a cycle that is already `CLOSED` refuses a second close with `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_CLOSED`.

Three figures keep the pool honest, and the third is the residual of the first two: `reserved_cents` is the sum of the awards on this cycle's grants currently in `FUNDS_RESERVED`, `disbursed_cents` is the sum of the awards on its grants in `VERIFIED_COMPLETE`, and `uncommitted_cents` is `total_pool_cents` less `reserved_cents` less `disbursed_cents`. Every movement of money in the sections below is expressed as a paired adjustment to these figures so that the residual never has to be reconstructed from scratch.

A household applies for a grant by naming the structure through `dwelling_id`, the cycle through `grant_cycle_id`, the verified household `income_tier` — `TIER_LOW`, `TIER_MID` or `TIER_ANY`, which selects both the match percentage and the sub-pool the award draws from — the contractor's `project_cost_estimate_cents` for the eligible scope, and the `requested_scope` the applicant intends to perform. The application may also name `policy_id`, the in-force policy that makes the dwelling grant-eligible; when it is supplied, that policy's `dwelling_id` must equal the grant's own `dwelling_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_DWELLING_OWNER_MISMATCH`. A new application opens in `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` at zero, `approved_amount_cents` at zero, `funded_from_bucket` at `NONE`, `denial_reason` an empty string and both operator handles — `approved_by_user_id` and `reserved_by_user_id` — null. Where the applying caller is a `Policyholder` rather than program staff, the ownership rule is applied to the owner of the named `dwelling_id`, so a household may apply only for its own structure and naming another party's draws `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; that comparison happens at its own rung, ahead of the required-field, enum and numeric checks, and is skipped altogether when `dwelling_id` is absent or null so that the missing identifier is what gets reported. The cycle must be `OPEN`, and only one grant per dwelling may sit in a non-terminal status, so a second concurrent application on the same structure is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_GRANT`.

Adjudication is a bounded revision loop. A `GrantReviewer` (or program staff) takes the application up, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and raising `review_round` by one; that increment happens each time the application *enters* review, and the cycle must still be `OPEN` for it to happen at all. The reviewer then records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `RETURN` or `DENY`, optionally with a `denial_reason`, which is the only optional field the operation declares — anything else sent alongside, an `info_request` describing what is missing included, is an undeclared field and is ignored rather than stored. An omitted or null `denial_reason` becomes the empty string, so a return or a denial that supplies none overwrites whatever reason an earlier round had recorded. `RETURN` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO` and writes the supplied `denial_reason` onto the grant; it is available only while `review_round` is strictly less than three, and the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_GRANT_REVIEW_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. From `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` the applicant — who must be the owner of the dwelling — resubmits, moving `RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED`. Both `project_cost_estimate_cents` and `requested_scope` are optional on that resubmission, and each overwrites its stored value only where it is supplied: an omitted or null one leaves the figure the application already carried, and the `denial_reason` the return recorded is left standing untouched. `review_round` is not reset either, so the loop `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED` can turn at most three times. `DENY` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, which is terminal, and likewise records `denial_reason`.

`APPROVE` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, and computes the award into `approved_amount_cents`; it leaves `denial_reason` exactly as it stands, so a `denial_reason` sent with an approval is type-checked and then discarded. Approval additionally requires, in this order, that the policy behind the grant be `ACTIVE` — a requirement that applies only where the application named a `policy_id` at all, since a grant with no policy behind it skips the check entirely — that the cycle still be `OPEN` with the logical clock inside the inclusive interval from `opens_at` through `closes_at`, and that the dwelling's `structure_type` be one the programme funds at all.

**The award.** Each structure type carries a scope cost cap: 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand dollars — for `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL`, and 600,000 cents — six thousand dollars — for `MOBILE_HOME`. `WOOD_FRAME_SLAB` and `MASONRY_URM` carry a cap of zero, which is the programme's way of saying they are outside the retrofit scheme entirely; an approval attempt on either is refused with `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_GRANT_ELIGIBLE`. The eligible cost is the smaller of the applicant's `project_cost_estimate_cents` and that cap. Multiply the eligible cost by the match percentage for the applicant's `income_tier` — the whole eligible cost for `TIER_LOW`, three quarters of it for `TIER_MID`, and one half of it for `TIER_ANY` — round the product half up to whole cents, and then clamp the result down to a programme maximum of 300,000 cents, three thousand dollars. Prior grants on the same dwelling do not reduce the award; they are refused earlier by the one-open-grant-per-dwelling rule instead. The calculation reads one grant and its dwelling and nothing else.

**Committing the money.** An approved award becomes a real charge against the pool only when program staff reserve it, moving `APPROVED → FUNDS_RESERVED`. This is the operation guarded by the funding-cycle version pin of section 2.7: the request must carry `expected_grant_cycle_version` equal to the cycle's current `version`, or it is refused with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_CYCLE_VERSION`. It is also the operation guarded by segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the grant's `approved_by_user_id`, and the reserving identity is recorded as `reserved_by_user_id`. The cycle must be `OPEN`.

The pool itself is carved into three sub-pools whose caps deliberately over-subscribe it. The `TIER_LOW` bucket's cap is sixty percent of `total_pool_cents`, the `TIER_MID` bucket's cap is forty percent, and the shared `TIER_ANY` bucket's cap is twenty-five percent — one hundred and twenty-five percent of the pool in total — each cap computed by multiplying `total_pool_cents` by its percentage and rounding half up to whole cents. A bucket's usage is the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over this cycle's grants whose `funded_from_bucket` names that bucket and whose status is `FUNDS_RESERVED` or `VERIFIED_COMPLETE`; grants that were withdrawn or denied release their bucket usage, and grants that never got past approval have never consumed any. Reservation then resolves a bucket in two steps. Step one: if the applicant's own income tier's usage plus this award is no greater than that tier's cap, the award draws from the applicant's own tier and `funded_from_bucket` records it. Step two: otherwise, provided the applicant's tier is not already `TIER_ANY`, if the `TIER_ANY` bucket's usage plus this award is no greater than the `TIER_ANY` cap, the award draws from `TIER_ANY` — that shared bucket is the single fallback, and there is no further cascade. If neither step succeeds the reservation is refused with `ERR_GRANT_TIER_CAP_EXHAUSTED`.

Step three is the hard ceiling, and it is checked **after** a bucket has been resolved, which is exactly why a bucket with room can still fail: if `reserved_cents` plus `disbursed_cents` plus this award exceeds `total_pool_cents`, the reservation is refused with `ERR_GRANT_CYCLE_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. Because the bucket caps sum to more than the pool, this ceiling genuinely bites in practice rather than being a formality. On success the ledger moves as a pair — `reserved_cents` rises by the award and `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed as `total_pool_cents` less `reserved_cents` less `disbursed_cents` — the cycle's `version` rises by one, and the dwelling's `grant_lifetime_count` rises by one. The reservation also reaches across into the coverage side: the policy named by the grant, provided it is `ACTIVE` and its `mitigation_status` is still `UNMITIGATED`, advances `UNMITIGATED → RETROFIT_PENDING`, and the policy's own `version` rises accordingly.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to the dwelling's owner or to program staff with an optional `reason`, and it accepts a grant in any of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, `APPROVED` or `FUNDS_RESERVED`, moving it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. What it does depends entirely on whether money was ever committed. Withdrawing from any of the four pre-reservation statuses touches no ledger at all, because nothing was reserved. Withdrawing from `FUNDS_RESERVED` reverses the reservation exactly: `reserved_cents` falls by the award, `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed, the cycle's `version` rises by one, `funded_from_bucket` is set back to `NONE` — which is what releases the bucket usage for the next applicant — every retrofit project on the grant that is in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` is moved to the terminal `ABANDONED` — each such abandonment also lowering that project's contractor's `open_project_count` by one and moving that contractor's own `version` — and the policy's `mitigation_status` steps back `RETROFIT_PENDING → UNMITIGATED`, that last hop turning on `mitigation_status` alone whatever the policy's `lifecycle_status` has since become. What the withdrawal deliberately leaves alone is the award itself: `approved_amount_cents` keeps the figure the reviewer computed, and the dwelling's `grant_lifetime_count` is not decremented.

The remaining exit from `FUNDS_RESERVED` is success rather than retreat: when the funded work passes inspection, the grant advances `FUNDS_RESERVED → VERIFIED_COMPLETE`, which is terminal, and the cycle's ledger performs the *transfer* rather than a release — `reserved_cents` falls by the award and `disbursed_cents` rises by the same award, leaving `uncommitted_cents` unchanged, with the cycle's `version` rising by one. Section 3.6 describes the cascade that drives it.

### 3.6. Contractors, Retrofit Work and the Verification Cascade That Reaches the Premium

Retrofit work may only be performed by contractors the programme has certified. A contractor record carries the licensed `business_name`, the state `license_no`, which must be unique across all contractors, `certified_scopes` — a non-empty list drawn from `CRIPPLE_WALL_BRACING`, `EARTHQUAKE_BRACING_SYSTEM` and `ANCHOR_BOLT_ONLY` — and a `certification_expires_at` instant. Certification opens at `CERTIFIED`, with `open_project_count` and `failed_inspection_count` both at zero. `certification_status` takes three values: `CERTIFIED`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`. `open_project_count` tracks how many of the contractor's projects are in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`; `failed_inspection_count` is a cumulative audit tally of every inspection on the contractor's work that came back failed.

Program staff may move certification by supplying a target `certification_status`, optionally with a `reason` and optionally with a replacement `certification_expires_at` that overwrites the stored expiry when present. Only two targets may be requested. Suspension is available from `CERTIFIED` alone, moving `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`, and it leaves every one of the contractor's projects in whatever status it already holds — what suspension blocks is being assigned new work and having an inspection booked, not finishing work already in hand; a contractor already on `PROBATION` cannot be suspended by this route. Restoration is available from either of the other two, moving `PROBATION → CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`. Anything else — most notably any attempt to set `PROBATION` directly — is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_TRANSITION`, because `PROBATION` is reached only through the failed-inspection cascade below. Restoring a contractor to `CERTIFIED` deliberately does not reset `failed_inspection_count`: the tally is cumulative history, not a strike counter that clears on reinstatement.

Funded work is assigned by creating a **retrofit project** naming the reserved grant through `grant_id` and the performer through `contractor_id`. Where the creating caller is a `CertifiedContractor` rather than program staff, the `contractor_id` it names must be its own or the request draws `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, a comparison made at its own rung ahead of the required-field check and skipped when `contractor_id` is absent or null. The grant must be in `FUNDS_RESERVED` — work cannot start on money that is only approved, and an attempt against a merely `APPROVED` grant is refused with `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_NOT_RESERVED`. The contractor must be `CERTIFIED` at that moment, so a contractor on `PROBATION` cannot pick up new work, and its `certification_expires_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock. A contractor may hold at most three open projects at once; a fourth is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_PROJECT_CAP`. The new project opens in `SCHEDULED`, copies `scope_code` from the grant's `requested_scope`, starts `inspection_attempts` at zero, leaves `started_at` and `verified_at` null, and raises the contractor's `open_project_count` by one. Project status takes the values `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION`, `REWORK_REQUIRED`, `VERIFIED` and `ABANDONED`, of which the last two are terminal.

The contractor drives the work forward. Starting work moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at` with the clock reading; restarting after a failed inspection moves `REWORK_REQUIRED → IN_PROGRESS` and leaves `started_at` at its original value, since the work began once. Neither start re-examines the contractor's certification: certification is checked when the project is created and again when an inspection is booked, and nowhere else, so a contractor whose certification moves to `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` after assignment may still carry on with the work it already holds — what it loses is the ability to be assigned anything new and the ability to have a visit booked against it. Only the contractor named on the project may drive it. Declaring the work ready moves `IN_PROGRESS → AWAITING_INSPECTION` and is the contractor's exclusive act. Program staff may abandon a project from any of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_INSPECTION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`, with an optional `reason`; abandonment lowers the contractor's `open_project_count` by one, leaves any still-pending inspection rows in place while removing their hold on the contractor's calendar, and pointedly does **not** release the grant's reserved money — the grant must be withdrawn separately for that.

**Booking a verification visit.** A `FieldInspector` (or program staff) books an appointment against a project by supplying `project_id`, `window_start` and `window_end`. The project must be in `AWAITING_INSPECTION`, and the inspection's `contractor_id` is copied from the project rather than taken from the caller; a caller-supplied `contractor_id` that disagrees with the project's is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. The window is half-open — it includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end` — must be at least thirty and at most two hundred and forty minutes long, both bounds inclusive, and must begin at or after the current logical clock; a visit booked into the past is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_IN_PAST`. The contractor must be `CERTIFIED` with an unexpired certification at booking time as well as at project creation. The calendar rule is the sharpest of the temporal constraints: widen the requested window by sixty minutes on **both** sides, and if the widened interval intersects the half-open window of any existing inspection that is still `PENDING`, belongs to the same `contractor_id`, and sits on a project that is not `ABANDONED`, the booking is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_SLOT_OVERLAP`. Only `PENDING` inspections occupy the calendar; once an outcome is recorded, that window releases. The new inspection opens with `result` at `PENDING`, `inspector_user_id` and `recorded_at` null, and `deficiency_note` an empty string.

**Recording the outcome.** The inspector supplies a `result` of `PASS`, `PARTIAL` or `FAIL`, optionally with a `deficiency_note` that documents what was wrong on a failing or partial visit; the note is nonetheless written on every outcome, a `PASS` included, and an omitted or null note writes the empty string. The inspection must still be `PENDING`, the project must be in `AWAITING_INSPECTION`, and the logical clock must have reached `window_start` — reporting on a visit that has not started is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_NOT_REACHED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `approved_by_user_id` of the grant funding the project, so the reviewer who released public money cannot also certify that it bought something. A passing result additionally requires the project's `scope_code` to appear among the inspecting contractor's `certified_scopes`; work outside a contractor's certified scopes cannot be verified, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RETROFIT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Every recorded outcome writes `inspector_user_id` from the acting header and `recorded_at` from the clock, and raises the project's `inspection_attempts` by one.

A **passing** result is the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs in four hops in this order. Hop one, on the inspection and the project: `result` becomes `PASS`, and the project advances `AWAITING_INSPECTION → VERIFIED` with `verified_at` stamped from the clock. Hop two, caused by the project reaching `VERIFIED`: the dwelling's `retrofit_status` becomes `VERIFIED` and its `verified_retrofit_scope` is overwritten with the project's `scope_code`, and the funding grant advances `FUNDS_RESERVED → VERIFIED_COMPLETE`. Hop three, caused by the grant reaching `VERIFIED_COMPLETE`: the funding cycle transfers the award from reserved to disbursed, lowering `reserved_cents` and raising `disbursed_cents` by the award while `uncommitted_cents` stays where it was, and the cycle's `version` rises. Hop three-b, caused by the dwelling's `retrofit_status` reaching `VERIFIED`: the policy's `mitigation_status` advances `RETROFIT_PENDING → RETROFIT_VERIFIED`. Hop four, caused by the policy reaching `RETROFIT_VERIFIED` and firing **only** when that policy's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`: the mitigation discount is computed and applied, and `mitigation_status` advances `RETROFIT_VERIFIED → DISCOUNT_APPLIED`. A policy that has lapsed or been cancelled stops at `RETROFIT_VERIFIED` and receives no discount — the physical verification stands even though the pricing incentive has nowhere to land. Hops three-b and four are two separate writes on the same policy row, so a cascade that runs all the way to the discount moves that policy's `version` by two while every other row it touches — the inspection, the project, the dwelling, the grant and the funding cycle — moves by one.

A **failing** result is deliberately shallow. It sets `result` to `FAIL`, sends the project back `AWAITING_INSPECTION → REWORK_REQUIRED`, and raises the contractor's `failed_inspection_count` by one; when that incremented tally reaches three and the contractor is currently `CERTIFIED`, `certification_status` moves `CERTIFIED → PROBATION` in the same request. No dwelling, grant, funding-cycle or policy field is touched by a failure — the rework loop `AWAITING_INSPECTION → REWORK_REQUIRED → IN_PROGRESS → AWAITING_INSPECTION` can turn as many times as the contractor's certification survives. A **partial** result is the strange middle case: it records `result` as `PARTIAL` and raises `inspection_attempts`, but leaves the project exactly where it was, still `AWAITING_INSPECTION`. A partial verification neither verifies the retrofit nor sends it back for rework, so a further inspection must be booked before the project can move at all.

**The mitigation discount.** When hop four fires, the discount is computed from the retrofit that was actually verified, and reads the single grant that the verifying project points at, that grant's dwelling, and the policy the grant names. Start from a scope percentage: twenty-five percent when the `verified_retrofit_scope` is `CRIPPLE_WALL_BRACING` and the `structure_type` is `WOOD_FRAME_CRIPPLE_WALL`; twenty percent when the scope is `EARTHQUAKE_BRACING_SYSTEM` and the structure is a `MOBILE_HOME`; and ten percent in every other combination. Then subtract the grant offset: because public money has already paid for part of the work, a grant whose `approved_amount_cents` is strictly greater than zero costs the household five percentage points of discount, while a verified retrofit with no award behind it loses nothing. The effective percentage is the larger of that difference and a floor of five percent, so the incentive never disappears entirely. `mitigation_discount_cents` is `base_premium_cents` multiplied by the effective percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, and `annual_premium_cents` becomes `base_premium_cents` less that discount.

Because the discount lands mid-term on premium the household has already been billed, the unearned portion is credited back. Count the whole days remaining in the term: take the difference between the policy's `expires_at` and the current logical clock and round it *down* to a whole number of days, treating an already-expired term as zero days. `prorated_credit_cents` is the already-rounded `mitigation_discount_cents` multiplied by the smaller of that day count and three hundred and sixty-five, divided by three hundred and sixty-five, rounded half up to whole cents. The second rounding always consumes the first rounding's integer result and never the unrounded product.

**Revoking a discount.** Where a verified retrofit is later found deficient, program staff may withdraw the pricing incentive, moving `mitigation_status` `DISCOUNT_APPLIED → UNMITIGATED` with an optional `reason`. The policy must be `ACTIVE`, its `mitigation_status` must currently be `DISCOUNT_APPLIED` and its `mitigation_discount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_MITIGATION_DISCOUNT`. Revocation is an exact restoration: `mitigation_discount_cents` and `prorated_credit_cents` both return to zero and `annual_premium_cents` returns to exactly `base_premium_cents` — the premium ledger conserves, so that a discount granted and revoked leaves the billed premium byte-identical to what it was before. What revocation does **not** do is unwind the physical record: the dwelling's `retrofit_status` stays `VERIFIED` and its `verified_retrofit_scope` is untouched, because the inspection happened. Only the money is withdrawn.

### 3.7. Catastrophe Declaration, the Reinsurance Tower and Capacity

An earthquake enters the system as a monitoring record long before anyone knows whether it will cost anything. Program staff open one by supplying the programme's `event_code`, which must be unique across all events, the `magnitude` as a moment magnitude between 4.0 and 9.5 inclusive with one decimal place, which may arrive as a JSON number or as a decimal string and — like an insurer's `market_share_pct` — is stored and echoed back in exactly the form it arrived in rather than normalised, read into exact decimal arithmetic only where the declaration floor is tested, the `epicenter_county_code` that determines the scope of the binding moratorium, which unlike a policyholder's `county_code` really must be exactly five characters long and is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at any other length, the `occurred_at` instant, which may not be later than the current logical clock and is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it is, and `retention_cents`, the programme's own retention beneath the first reinsurance attachment point, which must be zero or greater. The record derives `treaty_year` as the calendar year of `occurred_at`, and that derived year is what selects the reinsurance tower the loss will run through. The event opens at `MONITORING` with every aggregate at zero — `aggregate_incurred_cents`, `retained_cents`, `ceded_total_cents`, `uncovered_cents`, `assessment_shortfall_cents`, `admin_loading_cents`, `assessment_total_cents` and `claim_count` — and with `version` at 1. Event status runs through `MONITORING`, `DECLARED`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION`, `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` and the terminal `CLOSED`.

Declaring the event moves `MONITORING → DECLARED` and is what opens claim intake. Two conditions guard it. The `magnitude` must be at least 5.0, and a smaller tremor is refused with `ERR_MAGNITUDE_BELOW_DECLARATION_FLOOR`. And the event's `treaty_year` must have at least one reinsurance layer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or the declaration is refused with `ERR_NO_REINSURANCE_PROGRAM` — the programme will not take losses onto a year for which it bought no cover. The same reinsurance requirement is re-checked when each claim is filed. Declaration is also the moment the sixty-day binding moratorium in the epicenter county becomes effective, as described in section 3.4.

**The tower.** Each layer of a treaty year's reinsurance tower is its own row, carrying the `treaty_year` it belongs to, its `layer_index` — the waterfall position, ascending from 1 and unique within the year — its `attachment_point_cents`, the gross incurred loss at which the layer begins to respond, its `limit_cents`, the width of the layer, which must be strictly greater than zero, and its `ceded_pct`, the fraction of the layer's exposure ceded to reinsurers, which must be strictly greater than zero and no more than one and is, like an event's `magnitude`, stored and echoed back in exactly the form it arrived in. A new layer opens `ACTIVE` with `ceded_cents` at zero. Two structural rules keep the tower well-formed: `layer_index` must not repeat within a `treaty_year`, or the row is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LAYER_INDEX`; and any layer whose `layer_index` is greater than one must attach at or above the sum of the preceding index's `attachment_point_cents` and `limit_cents` in the same year, so that towers stack upward without overlapping downward, or the row is refused with `ERR_LAYER_ATTACHMENT_GAP`; the comparison is made against the layer holding the immediately preceding index alone, whatever that layer's own `status`, so where no layer holds that index in the year there is nothing to compare against and the check does not fire at all. Program staff may lapse or reinstate a layer by supplying a `status` of `LAPSED` or `ACTIVE`; the change takes effect on the next run of the loss waterfall, which excludes or re-includes that layer accordingly. No state precondition guards that move — either value may be set from either, and setting the value the layer already holds is accepted and still moves the layer's `version`.

**The loss waterfall.** Whenever an event's gross incurred loss changes, the waterfall is recomputed from scratch over the new gross. The gross is the event's `aggregate_incurred_cents`, which is the sum of `final_payable_cents` over exactly those of its claims whose `adjustment_status` is `FINAL_ADJUSTED` or `CLOSED`; claims still at `FNOL_RECEIVED` or `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED`, and claims that were `DENIED`, contribute nothing. First, `retained_cents` becomes the smaller of the gross and the event's `retention_cents`. Next, take every layer whose `treaty_year` equals the event's and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, ordered by `layer_index` ascending and, where two layers share an index position in time, by `created_at` ascending. For each such layer, the exposure is the gross capped at the top of the layer — that is, the smaller of the gross and the sum of `attachment_point_cents` and `limit_cents` — less the layer's `attachment_point_cents`, with any negative result treated as zero; the layer's `ceded_cents` is that exposure multiplied by its `ceded_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, one rounding per layer. `ceded_total_cents` is the sum of the `ceded_cents` of the participating layers, and `uncovered_cents` is the gross less `retained_cents` less `ceded_total_cents`, floored at zero — it is a residual and is never independently rounded.

A lapsed layer is not skipped over in the sense of shifting the tower down. Attachment points are absolute, so a lapsed lower layer leaves a permanent hole: the layers above it still attach exactly where they always did, and the loss that would have fallen into the lapsed layer lands in `uncovered_cents` instead. That is the mechanism by which a thin reinsurance programme produces an assessment.

Finally, if the recomputed `uncovered_cents` is strictly greater than zero and the event is currently `LOSS_AGGREGATION`, the event advances `LOSS_AGGREGATION → CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`. Strictly greater is meant literally: a loss that is exactly covered to the cent stays in `LOSS_AGGREGATION`.

Closing an event is available from `MONITORING`, from `LOSS_AGGREGATION` and from `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED`, and moves the event to the terminal `CLOSED`. Any other starting status — `DECLARED`, or `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` — is refused with `ERR_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`, while an event that is already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_CLOSED`, and both of those are decided before the open-claim sweep below. Every claim on the event must already be terminal — `CLOSED` or `DENIED` — and an event with any claim still at `FNOL_RECEIVED` or `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED` is refused with `ERR_EVENT_HAS_OPEN_CLAIMS`. Closing from `MONITORING` is the path for a monitored tremor that never met the declaration floor and never cost anything. Closing from `LOSS_AGGREGATION` is the no-shortfall path, where reinsurance and retention absorbed the whole loss. An event sitting in `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` cannot be closed directly: its shortfall must be levied first, which is what moves it to `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` and makes closure available.

### 3.8. Claims: Adjustment in Two Passes, Payment, and the Post-Event Assessment

First notice of loss names the coverage through `policy_id` and the earthquake through `event_id`, and reports four damage figures in cents that are fixed at notice and never rewritten by either adjustment: `structural_damage_cents`, the adjuster-verified structural damage to the dwelling; `cosmetic_damage_cents`, damage that is cosmetic only; `contents_damage_cents`, the Coverage C loss before its sublimit; and `loss_of_use_cents`, the Coverage D expense before its sublimit. Every one of the four must be zero or greater. The filing must also carry `expected_event_version`, the version pin described in section 2.7. The policy must be `ACTIVE`, the event must be `DECLARED`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION` or `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` — an event still `MONITORING` accepts nothing — the event must be neither `CLOSED` nor `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED`, and the event's `treaty_year` must still have an `ACTIVE` reinsurance layer. Coverage must also have been in force when the ground moved: the event's `occurred_at` must be at or after the policy's `effective_at` and strictly before its `expires_at`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE_AT_EVENT`. At most one claim per policy-and-event pair may exist in any `adjustment_status` other than `DENIED`, so a second notice on the same pairing collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`. The new claim opens with `adjustment_status` at `FNOL_RECEIVED`, `payment_status` at `UNPAID`, `reopen_count` at zero, `denial_reason` an empty string, both adjuster handles — `provisional_adjuster_user_id` and `final_adjuster_user_id` — null, and every settlement figure — `deductible_applied_cents`, `dwelling_part_cents`, `contents_part_cents`, `loss_of_use_part_cents`, `provisional_payable_cents`, `final_payable_cents`, `payable_cents` and `paid_cents` — at zero. The event's `claim_count` rises by one and its `version` rises by one.

The claim carries two independent state dimensions. `adjustment_status` runs `FNOL_RECEIVED`, `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED`, `FINAL_ADJUSTED`, and terminally `CLOSED` or `DENIED`. `payment_status` runs `UNPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and terminally `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. They advance on disjoint triggers, and only two rules couple them at all: a claim cannot be closed until it is fully paid, and denial writes off the payment dimension.

**The deductible.** Both adjustment passes begin from the same deductible, and it is computed from the term snapshot rather than the live dwelling: `deductible_applied_cents` is the policy's `rated_dwelling_limit_cents` multiplied by its `deductible_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents. The settlement reads one claim's four reported figures plus its policy's `deductible_pct`, `contents_sublimit_cents`, `loss_of_use_sublimit_cents` and `rated_dwelling_limit_cents`. No other claim on the same event or the same policy participates.

**The provisional pass**, posted by a `ClaimAdjuster`, moves the claim `FNOL_RECEIVED → PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED` and requires the event to be `DECLARED`, `LOSS_AGGREGATION` or `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`. Cosmetic damage is excluded from the basis entirely at this stage. The Coverage A basis is the smaller of `structural_damage_cents` and the snapshot limit; `dwelling_part_cents` is that basis less the deductible, floored at zero, because the deductible is charged against Coverage A alone in this pass. `contents_part_cents` is the smaller of `contents_damage_cents` and `contents_sublimit_cents`, and `loss_of_use_part_cents` is the smaller of `loss_of_use_cents` and `loss_of_use_sublimit_cents` — both pay in full up to their sublimits, undiminished. `provisional_payable_cents` is the sum of the three parts, and `payable_cents` — the figure payments are measured against — is set equal to it. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `provisional_adjuster_user_id`, and `payment_status` is not touched, remaining `UNPAID`.

**The final pass** moves the claim `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED → FINAL_ADJUSTED` and must be signed by a different adjuster identity than the provisional one; the event must not be `CLOSED`, and it must not be `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED`. Two things change. Cosmetic damage is now admitted, but only up to a tenth of the structural figure: the allowed cosmetic amount is the smaller of `cosmetic_damage_cents` and ten percent of `structural_damage_cents` rounded half up to whole cents. The Coverage A basis becomes the smaller of the snapshot limit and the sum of `structural_damage_cents` and that allowed cosmetic amount. And the deductible is now charged against the **combined** Coverage A and Coverage C basis rather than against Coverage A alone: take the Coverage C basis as the smaller of `contents_damage_cents` and `contents_sublimit_cents`, add it to the Coverage A basis to form the combined basis, and subtract the deductible from that combined figure, flooring at zero.

Splitting the surviving amount between the two coverages has a **pinned order** that must be followed exactly. Compute `contents_part_cents` first, as the after-deductible amount multiplied by the Coverage C basis and divided by the combined basis, rounded half up to whole cents — and as zero when the combined basis is zero. Then take `dwelling_part_cents` as the residual: the after-deductible amount less the already-rounded `contents_part_cents`. Rounding both shares independently and adding them is wrong and can drift by a cent; Coverage A absorbs the residual by construction. `loss_of_use_part_cents` is again the smaller of `loss_of_use_cents` and `loss_of_use_sublimit_cents`, and is never reduced by the deductible. `final_payable_cents` is the sum of the three parts, `payable_cents` is set equal to it — the final figure supersedes the provisional one — and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `final_adjuster_user_id`. On the same reported figures the final number can land above the provisional one, when the cosmetic allowance dominates, or below it, when the deductible now also eats into Coverage C; both outcomes are correct. `payment_status` is not changed by finalization.

Finalization then cascades into the catastrophe machinery, in four hops. Hop one is the settlement rewrite just described. Hop two, caused by the claim reaching `FINAL_ADJUSTED`: the event's `aggregate_incurred_cents` rises by the claim's `final_payable_cents` and the event's `version` rises by one, and if the event was `DECLARED` it advances `DECLARED → LOSS_AGGREGATION`. Hop three, caused by the gross changing: the loss waterfall of section 3.7 re-runs, rewriting the event's `retained_cents`, every participating layer's `ceded_cents`, the event's `ceded_total_cents` and its `uncovered_cents`. Hop four, caused by `uncovered_cents` becoming strictly greater than zero: the event advances `LOSS_AGGREGATION → CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`.

**Reopening** a finalized claim is the compensating action, available to a `ClaimAdjuster` with an optional `reason`, and it moves the claim `FINAL_ADJUSTED → PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED`. It requires `reopen_count` to be strictly less than two — a claim may be reopened twice and no more — the event to be neither `ASSESSMENT_LEVIED` nor `CLOSED`, since the published allocations may not be disturbed, and the claim's `payment_status` to be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, because a fully settled claim is refused outright with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_SETTLED`. The compensation runs in three hops that mirror finalization. First, the event's `aggregate_incurred_cents` falls by exactly the claim's `final_payable_cents` and the event's `version` rises. Second, the loss waterfall re-runs over the reduced gross, rewriting `retained_cents`, every layer's `ceded_cents`, `ceded_total_cents` and `uncovered_cents`. Third, if `uncovered_cents` has returned to zero and the event is currently `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, the event steps back `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED → LOSS_AGGREGATION` — the one cycle in the event machine. On the claim itself, `final_payable_cents` returns to zero, `payable_cents` returns to `provisional_payable_cents`, `final_adjuster_user_id` is cleared to null, and `reopen_count` rises by one. Neither `payment_status` nor `paid_cents` is touched: a partially paid claim keeps every cent it has been paid, even where restoring `payable_cents` to the smaller provisional figure leaves `paid_cents` above it — nothing is clawed back, and no further payment can be recorded on such a claim because the ceiling of rung 75 is already breached.

**Payment** is recorded by program staff as an `amount_cents` against a claim whose `adjustment_status` is `PROVISIONAL_ADJUSTED` or `FINAL_ADJUSTED` and whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. The amount must be strictly greater than zero, and `paid_cents` plus the amount must not exceed `payable_cents`; one cent beyond is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYABLE`. On success `paid_cents` rises by the amount, and the payment dimension moves according to the arithmetic: `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` when the running total is still short of `payable_cents`, `UNPAID → PAID` when a single payment settles the whole payable exactly, and `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` when a later payment brings the total to exactly `payable_cents`. Equality settles; anything beyond is refused. `adjustment_status` is never changed by a payment.

**Denial** is available only at notice: a `ClaimAdjuster` supplies a mandatory `denial_reason` and the claim moves `FNOL_RECEIVED → DENIED`, which is terminal. Denial zeroes both `payable_cents` and `provisional_payable_cents`, records the reason, and cascades across dimensions to move `payment_status` `UNPAID → WRITTEN_OFF`. The event's `claim_count` is deliberately not decremented — the notice was received, whatever its outcome. **Closing** a claim moves `FINAL_ADJUSTED → CLOSED`, is performed by program staff, and requires `payment_status` to be exactly `PAID`; a claim that is still short is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FULLY_PAID`.

**The post-event assessment.** When the tower and the retention together were not enough, the shortfall is levied across the participating insurers, moving the event `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED → ASSESSMENT_LEVIED`. The event must be in `CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, or the levy is refused with `ERR_EVENT_CAPACITY_NOT_EXHAUSTED`. The shortfall is the event's current `uncovered_cents`, pinned into `assessment_shortfall_cents` at levy time. An administrative loading is added on top, and the loading rate is keyed on the **bare shortfall before any loading is added** — never on the grossed-up total, which would be circular: two percent when the shortfall is below 10,000,000,000 cents (one hundred million dollars), three and a half percent when it is below 50,000,000,000 cents (five hundred million dollars), and five percent at or above that. `admin_loading_cents` is the shortfall multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents, and `assessment_total_cents` is the shortfall plus the loading.

Who pays is a filter, not a formula. An insurer is assessable when its `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and its `market_share_pct` is strictly greater than zero. An insurer whose participation `status` is `WITHDRAWN` remains assessable — withdrawing from new business does not release a carrier from an assessment for a loss that has already happened — while a `SANCTIONED` insurer is excluded, because the programme cannot lawfully collect from it. If the assessable set is empty the levy is refused with `ERR_NO_ASSESSABLE_INSURERS`. The denominator is the sum of `market_share_pct` over the **assessable** insurers only, which is emphatically not one hundred: excluding a sanctioned carrier re-spreads its share across the remainder rather than leaving the assessment short.

The assessable insurers are then ordered by `market_share_pct` descending and, where two shares tie, by `created_at` ascending, and each is given an `allocation_rank` starting at 1. One allocation row is created per assessable insurer, recording `event_id`, `insurer_id`, the insurer's `market_share_pct` at levy time as `market_share_pct_snapshot`, its `allocation_rank`, its `assessed_cents` and the boolean `absorbs_remainder`. For every rank except the last, `assessed_cents` is `assessment_total_cents` multiplied by that insurer's `market_share_pct` and divided by the denominator, rounded half up to whole cents, with `absorbs_remainder` false. For the last rank, `assessed_cents` is `assessment_total_cents` less the sum of all the earlier ranks' `assessed_cents`, and `absorbs_remainder` is true — so the allocations sum to `assessment_total_cents` exactly, to the cent, with the rounding drift of every earlier rank absorbed by the last. Each assessed insurer's `assessment_balance_cents` rises by its own `assessed_cents`, and the event's `version` rises by one. Allocation rows are immutable once struck, and reading them back by event or by insurer is the only way to see how a shortfall was spread.

List endpoints answer with HTTP 200, a `data` array and the pagination block of section 2.3. Authorization is evaluated as described in section 2.1: the roles named in the final column are the only ones admitted, and where a role is entity-backed the additional ownership check applies on top of the role check.
