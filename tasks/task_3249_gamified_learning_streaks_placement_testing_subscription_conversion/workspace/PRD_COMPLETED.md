# Duolingo Gamified Learning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

This document specifies the product requirements for the Duolingo Gamified Learning API, the backend that powers a language-learning product built around streaks, skill decay, placement testing and subscription conversion. Learners move through a course tree of units and lesson nodes, earning crowns as they practise, watching previously mastered material decay when they stop reviewing it, and protecting a consecutive-day streak that is anchored to their own local calendar. Around that core loop the platform layers engagement machinery: conversion offers timed to milestones, leaderboard leagues with promotion and demotion, certification test products with proctoring, and institutional score reporting.

The design problem this document addresses is that learner motivation is a resource that must be both maximised and rationed. Free learners are gated by a heart and energy budget that a paid plan removes; streak loss is recoverable but only at a gem price that rises with abuse; league standings must be credible, which means cheating has to be detected and neutralised without punishing legitimate top performers. Every one of these concerns is expressed as an explicit, testable rule rather than a heuristic, because the product's behaviour must be reproducible from a fixed logical clock.

Two structural properties shape the rest of the document. First, the lesson tree is a graph: units unlock against a completion count, nodes chain to self-referential prerequisites, and a single node's decay can ripple outward to re-lock its descendants and pull a completed unit back into progress. Second, nearly every value a client reads — hearts, energy, crowns, leaves, weekly experience, repair price, discount percentage — is derived rather than stored, recomputed on the events that can move it. Implementers should expect cascades, not isolated writes.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply uniformly across every resource and endpoint described later in the document. Where a business workflow in §3 needs one of these rules, it refers back here rather than restating it.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it acts. Learner registration is the sole exception: it is reachable without either header, and when either header is nonetheless supplied it is ignored. A request that omits `X-User-ID` or supplies it as an empty string is refused with an `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` error at HTTP 401.

The system recognises four roles, spelled exactly as follows: `Learner`, `CertificationProctor`, `PlatformAdmin`, and `Anonymous`. The header value is read case-insensitively and with separators between its words ignored, so a role may be supplied in any casing or hyphenation; the recognised role is then normalised to the canonical spelling above. The acting role must be one of those listed for the operation; a request whose role is unrecognised, or is not permitted for the operation, or in which one learner acts on another learner's resource, is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403.

The two roles differ in how `X-User-ID` is interpreted. When `X-User-Role` is `Learner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Learner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Learner` — a streak row's `learner_id`, a standing's `learner_id`, an offer's `learner_id`, a placement session's `learner_id`, an exam's `learner_id` — compare that foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CertificationProctor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it, for example as `proctored_by_user_id` on `CertificationExam` or as `proctor_user_id` when a proctoring operator is named on a placement session, but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. The same opaque treatment applies to `PlatformAdmin`, the operator role that seeds the course tree, runs sweeps, sets the logical clock and injects support data, and to `Anonymous`, the unauthenticated caller who may only register a new learner.

Role-permitted operations are also scoped by exclusivity. `Learner` exclusively owns registration and profile updates, practice and review submissions, streak repairs, placement session creation and the item, score and calibration actions on a placement session, subscription creation, the cancellation and offer-redemption forms of a subscription write, exam purchase, and the submission action on an exam. `CertificationProctor` exclusively owns the proctor and finalize actions on an exam and the generation and retrieval of institutional score reports. `PlatformAdmin` exclusively owns course unit and lesson node authoring, league settlement and re-opening, offer issuance, logical clock control, system sweep firing, and the payment-outcome and gem support injections. Anonymous exclusively owns learner registration. Read operations over learners, course units, lesson nodes, practice attempts, streak rows, placement sessions, subscriptions, family seats, leagues, standings, certification exams, score reports and engagement offers are shared between the roles named against each endpoint in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is read from the server's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.8.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is absent it defaults to 10, and any supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` skips that many items and defaults to 0; a negative offset is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unless an endpoint's description states a different ordering, list responses are sorted by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order, compared as the timestamp's textual form.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` reports the size of the full result set rather than the size of the returned page, and `has_more` is true exactly when further items remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Timestamp Semantics

Resources that participate in the audited lifecycle carry a standard block of system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix embedded in the string. `created_at` records the moment of first creation as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. `updated_at` records the moment of last modification in the same format and is refreshed by any change to the resource's properties, including changes made by server-side cascades rather than by the client's own request. `version` is an integer that begins at its initial value and increments by one on every mutation of the resource.

The resources that carry all four properties are `Learner`, `Subscription`, `FamilyMembership`, `LessonNode`, `StreakState`, `PlacementTest`, `League`, `LeagueStanding`, `CertificationExam`, `EngagementOffer`, and `ScoreReport`. Two resources are deliberately narrower: `CourseUnit` and `PracticeAttempt` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a `CourseUnit` or on a `PracticeAttempt`, and no operation may be gated on a version of either.

### 2.5. Nested Resource Retrieval and Left-to-Right Existence Checks

Several endpoints address a resource through a parent. When a path carries more than one identifier, the server resolves them strictly left to right and stops at the first identifier that does not correspond to an existing resource, returning that identifier's own 404 error rather than a generic one. A practice or review submission that names a learner and a lesson node therefore reports `ERR_LEARNER_NOT_FOUND` when the learner is unknown and `ERR_LESSON_NODE_NOT_FOUND` only once the learner has been confirmed. A family-seat listing scoped to a subscription reports `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`; a lesson-node listing scoped to a course unit, and a placement-session listing narrowed by `course_id`, report `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`.

The same ordering governs creation. A request that both targets a parent and references a second entity validates the direct parent first, so that a placement session opened against a course reports the missing course as `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` before any lesson-node check runs.

### 2.6. Curriculum Consistency and Crown Propagation

The course tree is kept internally consistent by propagation rather than by client instruction. Because a `CourseUnit`'s `crown_count` is derived from the crown states of its `LessonNode` children, and a `Learner`'s `leaf_count` is derived from the crown states of the nodes that learner holds, any event that changes a node's `state` — a successful practice, a successful review, a decay, a demotion — recomputes both counters. A unit closes `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` when every lesson node it holds has reached `BRONZE` or better, so a completed unit can legitimately return to in-progress — and later complete again — once one of its children is demoted by decay, or once a further node is appended beneath it so that its children no longer all hold a crown. These cascades are applied within the same logical operation as the trigger, and every resource they touch has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented where that resource carries a version.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control and Version Discipline

Writes that mutate a versioned resource's state require the client to state the version it last observed, so that a client acting on a stale view cannot silently overwrite a newer one. The guard field is named after the parent entity: `expected_learner_version` for a learner-anchored create, `expected_node_version` for a lesson-node review, `expected_streak_version` for a streak repair, and `expected_subscription_version` for a subscription write. These fields are required inputs, not optional hints.

A guard fails when the value supplied does not equal the parent entity's current `version` — that is, when some other writer advanced the version after the client's read. The request is then refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and no part of the write is applied.

Two creates are guarded this way and are called out explicitly. Opening a `PlacementTest` requires `expected_learner_version` matching the target learner's current `version`; if the learner's version has moved on since the client last read it, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Purchasing a `CertificationExam` is guarded identically: a purchase whose `expected_learner_version` no longer matches the learner's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. In both cases the product permits a later session or purchase to supersede an earlier one, so a second attempt made against a freshly read version is legitimate and succeeds; it is only the stale observation that is rejected. A subscription write whose `expected_subscription_version` does not match the subscription's current `version` is refused with the same `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Note that the two version-free resources are outside this mechanism: no operation takes a version guard against a `CourseUnit` or a `PracticeAttempt`.

### 2.8. Local Calendar Timekeeping and the Logical Clock

Time in this system is driven by a server-side logical clock, not by wall time. On startup the clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that instant until an operator sets it; it never falls back to real wall-clock time before the first such call. An operator-only capability sets the clock to an instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, and every time-derived calculation in the system is re-evaluated against the new value.

Learner-facing calendars are local. A `Learner` carries an IANA `timezone_id` and a streak row carries a `local_date_offset_days`, and together these anchor the learner's day boundary, the `focus_date` currently under evaluation, and the derived `streak_anchor_date` on the learner profile. Day-boundary arithmetic is exact to the second, so a learner's day closes at a well-defined instant rather than at an approximate one.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All counters in this system are integers and are never stored fractionally. Where a rule produces a fractional intermediate, the document states the rounding explicitly and it must be honoured: amounts described as rounded half up are rounded to the nearer whole value with an exact half always moving upward, and divisions described as truncated drop the fractional part toward zero. Threshold comparisons expressed as "at least" or "at or below" include the boundary value itself. Engines that carry a float internally must not let binary floating-point error change a boundary decision.

### 2.10. Validation Ordering and Error Precedence

Validation is not a set of independent checks; it is an ordered cascade, and when more than one check could fire, the earliest one in the order below determines the response. The order is normative.

Authentication comes first: the presence of a non-empty `X-User-ID` (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401) and then the permission of the acting role (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403). The `X-User-ID` requirement applies to every endpoint except learner registration.

Existence checks follow, in the left-to-right order of §2.5: the learner named by `X-User-ID` or by a `learner_id` in the body or query must exist (`ERR_LEARNER_NOT_FOUND`, 404); then the lesson node named in a path (`ERR_LESSON_NODE_NOT_FOUND`, 404); then the subscription named in a path (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, 404); then the course unit named in a path or referenced by a `course_id` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, 404).

State-entry checks come next: the concurrency guard on a placement or exam create (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, 412); the lesson node's `state` being one of `UNLOCKED`, `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `LEGENDARY` for a practice attempt (`ERR_NODE_LOCKED`, 409); the lesson node's `state` being `DECAYED` for a review attempt (`ERR_NODE_NOT_DECAYED`, 409); a streak row's `status` being `BROKEN` for a repair (`ERR_STREAK_NOT_BROKEN`, 409); a subscription's `status` being `ACTIVE` when a family seat is added (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_INACTIVE`, 409); a placement session's `status` being one of `CREATED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SCORED` or `CALIBRATED` for a placement operation (`ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409); a certification exam's `status` being `PROCTORING` for a score submission or a void (`ERR_EXAM_NOT_PROCTORING`, 409); and a league's `status` being `ACTIVE` for a week-close settlement sweep (`ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_SETTLING`, 409).

The business-safety and temporal checks follow: the cheat-pattern screen on a practice attempt (`ERR_CHEAT_PATTERN_DETECTED`, 422); the repair window on a streak repair (`ERR_REPAIR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, 422); the proctoring window on an exam submission (`ERR_PROCTOR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, 422); and the placement item range check (`ERR_PLACEMENT_UNIT_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422) — which, on a placement create, is applied to `total_items` ahead of that create's own version guard, as §3.6 describes.

Referential and business-constraint checks close the cascade: a node's prerequisite being satisfied (`ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`, 409); a decayed node's `decay_level` sitting below the retention floor (`ERR_DECAY_RETENTION_FLOOR`, 409); a non-placement attempt having at least one heart available (`ERR_HEARTS_EXHAUSTED`, 409); a non-placement attempt having at least one energy unit (`ERR_ENERGY_EXHAUSTED`, 409); a streak row's `repair_purchases_this_month` being below the monthly repair cap (`ERR_REPAIR_MONTHLY_CAP`, 409); a subscription's `seats_used` being below `seats_total` when a seat is added (`ERR_SEAT_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, 409); an unused free placement probe, or a paid tier that is exempt from the probe limit (`ERR_PLACEMENT_PROBE_USED`, 402); a gem balance sufficient for the repair debit (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_GEMS`, 402); an engagement offer that is still `ISSUED` and not past its expiry when a discounted subscription write redeems it (`ERR_OFFER_NOT_REDEEMABLE`, 409); and finally, the presence of every required input field and the membership of every enum field in its declared set (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400).

Because the ordering is normative, a request that would violate a later rule but trips an earlier one reports the earlier error. Implementers should not reorder these checks even where the underlying storage would allow a different sequence.

### 2.11. Logical Clock, Support Injections and System Sweeps

A small set of operator-only capabilities keeps the system testable and drives the calendar. Chief among them is logical clock control: an operator sets the clock to an instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, and every time-derived calculation across the platform — day boundaries, energy accrual, decay meters, review due dates, league weeks, offer and exam expiries — is re-evaluated against the new value. The clock's startup reading and its behaviour before the first call are specified in §2.8. A related capability lets an operator record the renewal payment outcome for a subscription sitting in grace by supplying the `subscription_id` and an `outcome`; when the outcome is `SUCCEEDED` the subscription returns from `IN_GRACE` to `ACTIVE`. A support capability grants gems to a learner by supplying a `learner_id` and a `gems` quantity, adding to `gems_balance` and incrementing the learner's `version` by one; this is the only route by which a learner's gem balance grows outside of rewards.

The remaining operator capability fires the system's scheduled sweeps. A single sweep endpoint takes an `event_type`, matched case-insensitively after surrounding whitespace is stripped, and each event drives one family of automated transitions. `STREAK_DAY_CLOSE` runs the streak freeze sweep once for every learner whose local date has advanced past their `focus_date`, advancing `focus_date` by one day and applying the freeze or break outcome of §3.3 when no accepted attempt exists on the day being closed. `SKILL_DECAY` runs the decay schedule of §3.2 and demotes every node whose decay meter has reached the five-point ceiling. `SUBSCRIPTION_RENEWAL` drives the billing lifecycle of §3.5 — moving subscriptions past their period end into `IN_GRACE` and those past their grace window into `EXPIRED`. `LEAGUE_WEEK_CLOSE` drives a league from `ACTIVE` through `SETTLING` to `CLOSED`, flagging and ranking its standings as §3.7 describes, and `LEAGUE_WEEK_OPEN` drives a `CLOSED` league back to `ACTIVE` and restores its demoted standings. `OFFER_EXPIRY` moves `ISSUED` offers past their expiry to `EXPIRED`, and `EXAM_EXPIRY` moves `PURCHASED` exams past their expiry to `EXPIRED`. An operator — or the owning learner — also equips freeze tokens on a learner's streak row by supplying a `freeze_tokens` quantity (also accepted as `count`, and defaulting to one when neither is given) that is added to the row's existing pool, which is how the defensive capacity consumed by the freeze sweep comes to exist.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The workflows below are organised by the learner's journey through the product rather than by entity, so a reader following the narrative encounters each rule at the moment it becomes relevant. Where a rule is owned by a state machine or a calculation, it appears once in full at its most natural home and is referred to elsewhere.

### 3.1. Course Progression and the Crown Ladder

The syllabus is a two-level tree. An operator seeds it by authoring a **CourseUnit** with a monotonic `unit_index` (index 1 is the first unit), a display `title`, a `cefr_band` drawn from `A1`, `A2`, `B1`, `B2` or `C1`, an `unlock_index` expressing how many completed units are required before it opens, and a declared `total_nodes`. A newly authored unit begins with `crown_count` at zero. Individual **LessonNode** records are then appended underneath a unit, each carrying a `node_index` that positions it inside the unit — node 1 is the unit root — and an optional `prerequisite_node_id` that chains it to an earlier node in the same tree. Lesson nodes are also readable directly, either as a unit-scoped listing or through a cross-unit listing that can be narrowed by the owning `unit_id` or by the `prerequisite_node_id` being chained.

A freshly appended node starts in `LOCKED`. The only way it opens is for the system to confirm that its owning unit is in either the `AVAILABLE` or `IN_PROGRESS` posture and that either it has no prerequisite at all or the prerequisite node it names has already reached `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `LEGENDARY`. When both hold, the node transitions `LOCKED → UNLOCKED`. A practice attempt submitted against a node that has not opened, or whose prerequisite is still unmet, is refused — the first with `ERR_NODE_LOCKED` and the second with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`, both at HTTP 409.

Unit posture is derived, not driven. A unit begins in `LOCKED_UNIT`, and it advances to `AVAILABLE` when the number of units whose state is `COMPLETED` and whose `unit_index` is strictly below this unit's `unit_index` has reached this unit's `unlock_index`. That unlock is not contained to one unit: as it lands, every lesson node in the newly opened unit whose `prerequisite_node_id` is null transitions `LOCKED → UNLOCKED`, and `leaf_count` is recomputed for every learner holding a node in the unit. A unit then moves `AVAILABLE → IN_PROGRESS` the first time any node inside it is practised successfully, and it closes with `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` when every one of its lesson nodes has reached `BRONZE` or better. Completion cascades: further units whose `unlock_index` requirement is now met open in turn, `crown_count` is recomputed for every unit in the course, and `leaf_count` is recomputed again for the affected learners. The machine is genuinely cyclic, because a review that restores a decayed node pulls its completed unit back to `IN_PROGRESS` until the node is restored — the details of that move belong to §3.2.

Practice is the heartbeat of progression. A learner submits an attempt carrying `learner_id`, a client-supplied `recorded_at`, the elapsed `response_ms` confined to the range from 0 to 600000 milliseconds inclusive, a `correct` boolean, and an optional `is_placement_attempt` flag that defaults to false. Each accepted, correct attempt lifts the node one rung of the crown ladder: `UNLOCKED → BRONZE`, then `BRONZE → SILVER`, then `SILVER → GOLD`. The final rung is reserved: a node is promoted `GOLD → LEGENDARY` only when the learner's `program_tier` is `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE`, so a free learner's ladder tops out at gold. Every promotion refreshes `last_reviewed_at` to the attempt's `recorded_at`, rebases `review_due_at` by that state's review interval, resets `decay_meter` to zero, and causes `learner.leaf_count` to be recomputed. Experience is credited by a tiered scoring rule described in §3.4.

An attempt is recorded with an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED_LIMIT` or `REJECTED_DECAY_CAP`, together with the `xp_awarded` it earned. Practice attempts are individually readable and listable, ordered by `recorded_at` ascending and narrowable by `learner_id` or by `lesson_node_id`.

One safety screen runs before an attempt is booked. A learner who already holds eight attempts on the same lesson node inside a single local focus day with `response_ms` below 800 milliseconds is treated as harvesting the timer rather than practising, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CHEAT_PATTERN_DETECTED` at HTTP 422. The threshold is deliberately tight — sub-second answers repeated that many times on one node within a day are machine-generated, not human.

### 3.2. Skill Decay, Spaced Review and the Retention Floor

Mastery in this product is perishable. A **LessonNode** tracks how far it has drifted through `decay_level`, an integer bounded between 0 and 4, and `decay_meter`, a point counter that accrues against the node between reviews. A separate daily sweep evaluates every node whose `state` is `UNLOCKED`, `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `LEGENDARY`; nodes already in `DECAYED` or `LOCKED` are out of scope and are skipped.

For a node in scope the sweep measures the whole days elapsed between the logical clock and the node's `last_reviewed_at`, counting a day as 86400 seconds. Decay begins only after a grace period: the node accrues one decay point for every two complete days since that review, and the meter is capped at five points. Below that ceiling the accrued value is retained and carried forward rather than zeroed, and it is cleared only by a demotion or a spaced review, both of which reset it to zero. Separately, the node's next due date is derived from its crown state — an untouched or bronze node is due after one day, a silver node after two, a gold node after three, and a legendary node after five — and that interval is never allowed to exceed ten days. When a node's `decay_meter` has reached the five-point ceiling and the clock is at or past its `review_due_at`, the node is demoted to `DECAYED`: `decay_level` increments by one, `decay_meter` resets to zero, and `leaf_count` is recomputed for the owning learner.

A lapsed subscriber decays differently. When the learner's `Subscription.status` is `EXPIRED`, the meter is deliberately held one point below the ceiling, at four. Such a node never crosses the threshold no matter how long it is left alone — even forty days without review leaves it stalled rather than demoted. This is the product's retention grace for learners who have let their plan lapse, and it overrides the accrual above: the holder of an `EXPIRED` subscription is credited four points outright on every evaluation, without regard to how many whole days have actually elapsed since the node's last review.

Decay is contagious down the chain. When a node that had reached `BRONZE` or better is demoted, every lesson node that names it as `prerequisite_node_id` and that is currently `UNLOCKED` with no other satisfied prerequisite is pushed back to `LOCKED`, and `crown_count` is recomputed for the affected unit alongside the learner's `leaf_count`. Demotion of a node carrying a crown also threatens the learner's streak in the narrow case described in §3.3: if the decayed node was the learner's only qualified node on the streak day under evaluation, the streak breaks.

A node's decay depth is bounded by a retention floor of four — the same ceiling that `decay_level` may reach — and that floor governs recovery. Restoring a decayed node is a spaced-review action rather than an ordinary practice. A review is attempted against a node whose `state` is `DECAYED` — any other target is refused with `ERR_NODE_NOT_DECAYED` at HTTP 409 — and it carries `learner_id` together with `expected_node_version` as its concurrency guard. The outcome depends on how deep the decay has gone. A node whose `decay_level` is still below the retention floor is restored `DECAYED → UNLOCKED`, its `decay_meter` resets to zero, and any dependent node that is `LOCKED` but whose other prerequisites are satisfied is re-opened in turn; the review's attempt is recorded as `ACCEPTED`, carrying the node's decayed crown state as its base and rebasing the node's review schedule from the restoration instant. A node whose `decay_level` has reached the retention floor of four cannot be revived — it is demoted `DECAYED → LOCKED`, its `decay_meter` resets to zero, `leaf_count` is recomputed, and the review's attempt is recorded as `REJECTED_DECAY_CAP`. Attempting a review against a node already at the floor is refused outright with `ERR_DECAY_RETENTION_FLOOR` at HTTP 409 and applies no state change, and the refused review still counts as a mutation of the node: its `version` increments by one. Demotion by a league settlement also feeds this system: when a standing is demoted, every crowned node of that learner sitting in a completed unit has its `decay_meter` increased by one.

### 3.3. Streaks, Freezes and Repair Economics

A learner's streak is measured in consecutive local days, not in elapsed hours. The **StreakState** row maintains `current_length`, a `focus_date` naming the local calendar day currently under evaluation, a pool of `freeze_tokens`, a `status` drawn from the machine's working states `EVALUATING`, `QUALIFIED`, `FROZEN`, `BROKEN` and `REPAIRED`, the `last_qualified_local_date` of the most recent day that met the daily goal, the learner's `local_date_offset_days`, the `repair_cost_gems` charged for the most recent repair, and `repair_purchases_this_month`. Registration mints this row in `EVALUATING` with `focus_date` set to the learner's local calendar date, `current_length` at 0, and `repair_cost_gems` at 0. The row is owned by its learner, so any read or write is checked against the `learner_id` it carries. A streak row also exposes `learner_id` explicitly, and the declared constraint on its `status` narrows to the ordinary cycle `ALIVE`, `FROZEN`, `BROKEN` and `REPAIRED`; the evaluative pair `EVALUATING` and `QUALIFIED` are the states the day-cycle machine actually moves through and are equally valid observations.

The day boundary itself is computed, not configured. The server takes the logical clock in whole seconds, shifts it by the learner's fixed `local_date_offset_days` multiplied by 86400, and truncates to the start of the local day. The seconds already elapsed inside that local day are subtracted from a full 86400-second day to give the time remaining, and when that remainder falls to 3600 seconds or fewer the day is considered ready to close. The truncated local midnight is the `focus_date`. Because learner-facing calendars can also be anchored by an IANA `timezone_id`, a day that contains a daylight-saving transition is genuinely shorter or longer — 82800 or 90000 seconds rather than 86400 — and the remaining time on such a transition day can differ from the fixed-offset answer by as much as an hour. Both readings are supported.

A day qualifies when the learner books an accepted attempt whose `recorded_at` falls on the `focus_date` under evaluation. The first such attempt moves the streak from `EVALUATING` to `QUALIFIED`, increments `current_length` by one, and sets `last_qualified_local_date` to the `focus_date`; the learner's `xp_total` is recomputed at the same moment. Further accepted attempts on the same already-qualified day change nothing but the row's `updated_at` — only the first accepted attempt of a local day extends the streak. Whenever `current_length` lands exactly on 7 or on 30 through that extension, a conversion offer is minted for the learner with `milestone_code` `STREAK_7` or `STREAK_30` respectively, priced and possibly suppressed as §3.6 and §3.9 describe.

Day close is an operator-fired sweep that advances each learner whose local date has moved past their `focus_date`. If the learner's day was already `QUALIFIED`, the sweep simply advances `focus_date` by one local day and returns the row to `EVALUATING` to be judged afresh; the same resume applies to a row sitting in `REPAIRED` or `FROZEN`. If the closed day saw no accepted attempt, the streak is defended or lost. A learner holding at least one `freeze_token` spends one: `freeze_tokens` decreases by one, `current_length` is preserved untouched, the row is marked `FROZEN`, and `focus_date` advances by one local day — the missed day is absorbed. A learner with no tokens left has their streak broken: `current_length` drops to zero while the length that was active immediately before the break is retained as the basis for the next repair price, the next repair price is computed for the learner, `focus_date` advances by one local day, and if that learner's `hearts_available` is zero a conversion offer with `milestone_code` `HEART_DEPLETION` is issued to soften the loss. A row already `BROKEN` is left where it is by a further day-close sweep — its `focus_date` is not advanced — so the repair window of §3.3 is measured from the `focus_date` the break was recorded against.

Recovery from a broken streak takes one of two shapes. Practising on a fresh day — an accepted attempt whose `recorded_at` falls on the current `focus_date` — restarts the streak at `current_length` 1, re-qualifies the day, and rewrites `last_qualified_local_date` to that day; the owed repair price is cleared to zero because the learner has chosen to start over rather than buy back. Practising on a fresh day — an accepted attempt whose `recorded_at` falls on the current `focus_date` — restarts the streak at `current_length` 1, re-qualifies the day, and rewrites `last_qualified_local_date` to that day; the owed repair price is cleared to zero because the learner has chosen to start over rather than buy back. Buying the streak back is the other path. A repair is only purchasable while the row is `BROKEN`, and an attempt against any other posture is refused with `ERR_STREAK_NOT_BROKEN` at HTTP 409. The streak row must exist — a repair naming a streak id that is not found is refused with `ERR_LEARNER_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404 — and it is guarded by `expected_streak_version`, which is checked before the `BROKEN` state test and before the temporal and budget checks, so a repair that carries a stale guard is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 even when the row is not broken. It must be made before the clock has moved past the `focus_date` plus the repair window of `REPAIR_WINDOW_DAYS`; a late purchase is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at HTTP 422. This temporal check is evaluated ahead of the affordability and budget constraints below, so a purchase that is both late and over the monthly budget, or late and unaffordable, reports the expired window. It must have room under the monthly budget: a learner who has already reached `MONTHLY_REPAIR_CAP` — three — purchases this calendar month is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_MONTHLY_CAP` at HTTP 409. It must be affordable: a `gems_balance` below the computed debit is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_GEMS` at HTTP 402. A permitted repair debits `gems_balance` by the computed debit and records that same debited amount in `repair_cost_gems`, restores `current_length` to the length that was active immediately before the break, increments `repair_purchases_this_month` by one, transitions the row `BROKEN → REPAIRED`, and recomputes the row's post-repair displayed price for any subsequent purchase. The repair write is guarded by `expected_streak_version`, and a stale guard is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Repair is priced on a tiered schedule that rises both with the streak being restored and with how often the learner has already bought back this month. The ladder position is derived from the length at break: divide it by thirty, truncate toward zero, and cap the index at four. The five rates indexed that way are 1, 2, 4, 6 and 8 gems. A bonus then accrues on the remainder within the band — the length at break taken modulo thirty, multiplied by a per-day rate of 0.05 gems and rounded half up. The tier rate and the bonus together form the undiscounted price, and the displayed `repair_cost_gems` is that sum capped at a monthly ceiling of twenty gems. The amount actually debited is inflated by abuse: the learner's `repair_purchases_this_month` count multiplied by half, added to one, scales the undiscounted sum, and the product is rounded half up once and then capped at the same twenty-gem ceiling. A learner's first repair in a month therefore pays the plain price, while a second pays half again as much, and so on, until the ceiling bites. Lifetime streak days never enter the price — only the length at the moment of the break.

Two rounding shapes exist. The ordinary flow sums the tier rate and the bonus and applies the inflation multiplier to that sum before rounding, so a second repair of a seventeen-day streak debits three gems. The family-plan repair flow instead rounds each term of the inflation separately and then sums, so the identical inputs debit four gems even though the displayed price stays at two. Implementers should follow the flow that produced the write.

### 3.4. Hearts, Energy and the Practice Gate

Free practice is rationed by two independent budgets, and a paid plan lifts both. Every learner profile carries `program_tier`, one of `FREE`, `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE`, along with the derived counters `hearts_available`, `energy_units`, and `energy_anchor_at`.

Hearts are a daily failure allowance. For a learner on a gated tier, the server counts the attempts inside the current local day window — from the `focus_date` to 86400 seconds later — that are incorrect, are not placement items, and belong to that learner. Attempts flagged `is_placement_attempt` are deliberately excluded from the count, so a placement session never burns hearts. The available allowance is the daily grant of five hearts minus that count, floored at zero. The day window used here is the learner's local focus day derived in §3.3, so hearts refresh at local midnight rather than at UTC midnight.

Energy is a regenerating pool. A learner starts with ten units, the pool is capped at thirty, and it refills in discrete steps: for every complete interval of 900 seconds — a quarter of an hour — that has elapsed since `energy_anchor_at`, five units are credited, up to the cap. A learner who is 899 seconds past the anchor holds exactly the same energy as at anchor time; the accrual is quantised, not continuous. One unit is spent only when an attempt is actually accepted, and spending resets `energy_anchor_at` to the current clock, which restarts the accrual interval. The learner's `energy_units` value returned by the API is the regenerated figure, computed against the clock rather than stored stale.

An attempt is admitted only when both budgets have at least one unit remaining. A non-placement attempt with no hearts left is refused with `ERR_HEARTS_EXHAUSTED` at HTTP 409, and one with no energy left is refused with `ERR_ENERGY_EXHAUSTED` at HTTP 409. Because hearts are checked before energy in the cascade of §2.10, a learner who is out of both sees the hearts error.

The gate disappears entirely for a paying learner. When `program_tier` is `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` and the learner's `Subscription.limits_removed` flag is true, the gate is reported as unlimited and both `hearts_available` and `energy_units` are returned as null rather than as a number. `limits_removed` is itself derived — it is true only when the subscription's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `plan_code` is not `FREE` — so a learner in a grace period keeps the unlocked gate while a lapsed one does not. The two budgets are recomputed on every attempt that passes the gate, on every subscription state change, whenever a learner's profile is edited, and on every read of the learner profile, which returns the freshly regenerated figures rather than the stored ones.

One variant is worth stating plainly because it is observable. A learner holding a subscription whose `plan_code` is `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` and whose `status` is `IN_GRACE` accrues energy once per local day boundary rather than once per 900 seconds; such a learner practising shortly after local midnight therefore holds five fewer units than the continuous accrual would give at the same instant. The quantised day interval applies only to that `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` subscription sitting in `IN_GRACE`; a learner on any other tier, or whose subscription sits in any other state, accrues on the ordinary 900-second step.

Experience is credited only to accepted attempts by a tiered scoring rule. The base award depends on the node's crown state at the moment of practice — ten points for a node still in `UNLOCKED`, eight for `BRONZE`, six for `SILVER`, four for `GOLD`, two for `LEGENDARY`, two for a `DECAYED` node, and zero for a `LOCKED` one. A speed bonus is added on top: three points when the attempt came in under 3000 milliseconds, one point when it came in under 8000 milliseconds, and nothing beyond that. The sum is then scaled by the learner's streak multiplier, read from `current_length` as it stood before this attempt was applied: 1.50 when the streak is at least 100 days, 1.25 when it is at least 10, 1.10 when it is at least 2, and 1.00 below that. The product is rounded half up to a whole number of experience points. An incorrect attempt earns nothing at all, regardless of speed or streak. Free learners are capped at the neutral 1.00 multiplier no matter how long their streak, and the cap keys on `program_tier` alone — a learner whose tier is `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` reads the tiered ladder whether or not a subscription is currently active, while a `FREE`-tier learner stays neutral — so a node lifted out of `UNLOCKED` on a 2-day streak with a 2500-millisecond answer awards 14 experience points to a paying learner but 13 to a free one. The learner's `xp_total` is recomputed from these awards, and the award is recorded on the attempt as `xp_awarded`.

### 3.5. Subscriptions, Plan Tiers and Family Seats

Registration creates the billing scaffold as a side effect. When a learner is registered with a `display_name`, a `timezone_id` and a `local_date_offset_days` — and optionally a `program_tier` — the server also mints that learner's `StreakState` row in `EVALUATING` and a `Subscription` row in `FREE` status with `plan_code` `FREE`, `seats_total` 1 and `seats_used` 0. The learner's `gems_balance` starts at zero and can only be raised by the support injection described in §3.9. Profile fields are editable afterwards by the owning learner or by a platform operator, and a profile edit is guarded by `expected_learner_version`: the request carries `display_name`, `timezone_id`, `local_date_offset_days` and `program_tier` as optional replacements, and on success the learner's `version` increments by one, the heart and energy budgets are recomputed, `streak_anchor_date` is recomputed, and — importantly — if either `timezone_id` or `local_date_offset_days` actually changed, every engagement offer for that learner that is still `ISSUED` is expired so the learner can be re-engaged under the new calendar.

A subscription record carries `learner_id`, a `plan_code` drawn from `FREE`, `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE`, `seats_total` (a family plan's seat quota; free and single-seat plans carry 1), the derived `seats_used` count, a `status` drawn from `FREE`, `ACTIVE`, `IN_GRACE` or `EXPIRED`, `current_period_end`, an `auto_renew` flag, and the derived `limits_removed` boolean.

Subscribe to a paid plan by supplying `learner_id`, a `plan_code`, `seats_total` and `auto_renew`. The request is admitted only when the requested `plan_code` is `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` and no other subscription for that same learner is currently `ACTIVE`, and it drives the record `FREE → ACTIVE` — or revives a lapsed `EXPIRED` record in place, so the same record keeps its original `created_at`. On success `current_period_end` is set to the logical clock plus the plan period of `PERIOD_DAYS`, the owning learner's `program_tier` is set to the purchased `plan_code`, the `limits_removed` flag, the learner's `hearts_available` and the learner's `energy_units` are all recomputed, and every family membership under this subscription that is still `INVITED` is promoted to `ACTIVE` so that seats reserved before purchase come into force immediately. The reference implementation exposes this as a creation returning `201 Created`.

Subsequent changes go through a single subscription-modification action carrying `expected_subscription_version` as its concurrency guard, along with the optional instructions `plan_code`, `auto_renew`, `offer_id`, `add_seat_learner_id`, `remove_seat_learner_id` and `cancel`. Its effects compose: supplying `offer_id` re-prices the plan under the conversion discount of §3.9 and moves that offer `ISSUED → REDEEMED`; supplying `add_seat_learner_id` mints a family seat in `ACTIVE` status and increments `seats_used`; supplying `remove_seat_learner_id` moves the matching seat `INVITED → REMOVED` or `ACTIVE → REMOVED` and decrements `seats_used`; and setting `cancel` true clears `auto_renew`, moves every `ACTIVE` family seat under the subscription to `REMOVED`, and resets `seats_used` to zero. Every form of the write recomputes `limits_removed`, the learner's own `hearts_available` and `energy_units`, and the same two counters for each member whose seat changed. The write increments the subscription's `version` by one on success. A guard that no longer matches the subscription's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

The billing lifecycle itself is cyclic and operator-driven. A paid subscription normally lapses rather than being cancelled outright: when `auto_renew` is false and the clock has reached `current_period_end`, the record transitions `ACTIVE → FREE`, the owning learner's `program_tier` is set back to `FREE`, `limits_removed` is recomputed, and every `ACTIVE` family seat is moved to `REMOVED` with the affected learners' `hearts_available` recomputed. An `auto_renew` subscription that reaches the same instant is instead moved `ACTIVE → IN_GRACE` by the renewal sweep, and the gate stays unlocked for the duration of the grace period. A repeated subscription modification that sets `auto_renew` true while the record is `IN_GRACE` returns it to `ACTIVE` and extends `current_period_end` by `PERIOD_DAYS`; recording a successful renewal payment outcome through the operator-only payment injection has the same effect of moving an `IN_GRACE` subscription back to `ACTIVE`. Grace is finite: once the clock has passed `current_period_end` by `GRACE_DAYS`, the renewal sweep moves the record `IN_GRACE → EXPIRED`. An expiry is terminal and its cascade is total — `limits_removed` becomes false, the owning learner's `program_tier` is set back to `FREE`, `hearts_available` is recomputed for every learner holding a seat under the subscription, every `ACTIVE` seat under it is moved to `REMOVED`, and every engagement offer belonging to a learner under that subscription that is still `ISSUED` is moved to `EXPIRED`. A lapsed early-stage subscription can of course be subscribed again, so `FREE → ACTIVE → FREE → ACTIVE` rounds are legitimate.

Family sharing is tracked seat by seat. A `FamilyMembership` carries the owning `subscription_id`, the `member_learner_id` occupying the seat, a `seat_role` of `OWNER` or `MEMBER`, a `status` of `INVITED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMOVED`, and an `invited_at` timestamp marking when the invitation was minted. A seat begins `INVITED`. It is promoted `INVITED → ACTIVE` by a seat-adding subscription write, and only when the subscription is `ACTIVE`, its `seats_used` is still below its `seats_total`, and no other membership already holds that same member under that subscription in `INVITED` or `ACTIVE` status — so a second such write for a member who already holds a live seat leaves the count unchanged rather than minting a duplicate; on promotion `seats_used` increments, the member's `hearts_available` and `energy_units` are recomputed, and every `ISSUED` offer belonging to the member has its `scored_tier` recomputed because the member's pricing has changed. Adding a seat to a subscription that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_INACTIVE` at HTTP 409, and adding one when `seats_used` has already reached `seats_total` is refused with `ERR_SEAT_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409. At any point an `ACTIVE` seat can be revoked — by the subscription lapsing into `FREE` or `EXPIRED` or by an operator revoking it — which moves it `ACTIVE → REMOVED`, decrements `seats_used`, recomputes the member's budgets, and re-prices the member's outstanding offers. A seat that is `INVITED` or `ACTIVE` can also be withdrawn directly by a seat-removing subscription write, which the product permits when the subscription is in `FREE` or `EXPIRED` or when the acting learner owns the subscription; the withdrawn seat moves to `REMOVED` and the member's outstanding offers are re-priced. A seat-removing write that names no live seat under the subscription while the subscription is outside `FREE` and `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403. Seat listings are available both for a learner across all their subscriptions and for a subscription across all its seats.

### 3.6. Placement Testing, Calibration and Course Unlocking

A returning learner who already knows the material should not be forced through the early units. A **PlacementTest** session opens against a course anchor, carrying `learner_id`, a `course_id`, a client-supplied `administered_at`, a `total_items` count bounded from 5 to 50 inclusive, and the `expected_learner_version` guard described in §2.7. It also carries the derived `is_institution_graded` flag and the `proctor_user_id` naming the supervising operator; the flag is true exactly when `proctor_user_id` holds a non-empty operator identifier, and it selects which boundary anchor the calibration applies. A new session lands in `CREATED`, `graded_at` is left unset until the session is scored, and the learner's `version` increments by one as a result of the create. The create first resolves the direct parent course — an unknown `course_id` is reported as `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` — and then validates its fields in a fixed order: the `total_items` count, which must lie from 5 to 50 inclusive, is checked before the `expected_learner_version` guard, so a create that is both undersized and stale reports `ERR_PLACEMENT_UNIT_OUT_OF_RANGE` at HTTP 422 rather than `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Sessions are listable, ordered by creation timestamp ascending and narrowable by `learner_id` or by `course_id`, and individually readable with their sub-scores and calibration state. Sessions are owned by their learner.

Presenting an item is the learner's act of working through the test. The placement write takes an `action` selector — `PRESENT_ITEM`, the scoring action (`SUBMIT_SCORES`, also accepted as `SCORE`), `CALIBRATE`, or the abandonment action (`ABANDON`, also accepted as `CANCEL`) — plus, for `PRESENT_ITEM`, the item payload `lesson_node_id`, the attempt's `recorded_at`, its `response_ms` and the `correct` flag. The first presented item moves the session `CREATED → IN_PROGRESS`, recording a practice attempt flagged with `is_placement_attempt` true; that placement attempt is not charged against the learner's heart or energy budgets, and its `response_ms` must lie from 0 to 600000 milliseconds inclusive. Opening the test at all requires that the learner either still holds their one free probe or is on a paid tier: a free learner whose session already carries `free_probe_used` true is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_PROBE_USED` at HTTP 402, and this probe gate is applied when the session is opened rather than when an item is presented. A submitted item whose node sits in a unit beyond the permitted probe span — the larger of the session's current `placed_unit_index`, once one has been assigned, and the probe span of `PLACEMENT_PROBE_SPAN` — is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_UNIT_OUT_OF_RANGE` at HTTP 422, and once the session already holds `total_items` recorded placement attempts a further item is refused with that same error. Abandoning a session while it is still `CREATED` returns the free probe, setting `free_probe_used` back to false, and lands the session in the terminal `ABANDONED` state; abandoning a session in any later state is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` at HTTP 409.

Scoring is a separate step. The sub-score submission carries `comprehension_score` and `accuracy_score`, and it is admitted only once the number of recorded placement attempts on nodes belonging to units up to the session's `placed_unit_index` has reached the session's `total_items`; it drives `IN_PROGRESS → SCORED`, or `CALIBRATED → SCORED` on a resubmission, and sets `graded_at` to the logical clock. The learner's `xp_total` is recomputed at this point. A placement operation aimed at a session whose status is not one of `CREATED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SCORED` or `CALIBRATED` is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` at HTTP 409.

Calibration converts the raw sub-scores into a course position, and it is here that the two boundary anchors diverge. The provisional unit count is built from two credits. The first divides the combined sub-score — `comprehension_score` plus `accuracy_score` — by 40, truncated toward zero in the ordinary self-serve flow but rounded upward when the session is institution-graded, so the two flows can differ by a unit on a remainder. The second divides `total_items` by 5, truncated toward zero. The two credits are summed and capped at a maximum placement of unit 12, giving the provisional index.

The band that goes with it is read off the combined sub-score in inclusive steps: `C1` at 180 or above, `B2` at 140 or above, `B1` at 100 or above, `A2` at 60 or above, and `A1` below that. The candidate position is then clamped against how far the learner's own course has actually advanced. For a self-serve session the ceiling is the largest `unit_index` among units the learner has already `COMPLETED`; for an institution-graded session the ceiling is the largest `unit_index` among units in `COMPLETED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. When the ceiling is at least the provisional index the calibrated band stands as computed and `placed_unit_index` takes the clamped value; when the ceiling falls short of the provisional index the learner is placed at the ceiling and the calibrated band becomes that ceiling unit's own `cefr_band` instead. The ceiling unit whose band is taken is the anchor that fixed the ceiling — the highest-indexed eligible unit — which for an institution-graded session may be an `IN_PROGRESS` unit rather than a completed one. The effect is that a self-serve placement is pinned to work the learner has already demonstrated, while a proctored one may credit units they are still working through — identical sub-scores of 200 with 20 items place a self-serve learner at unit 1 but an institution-graded learner at unit 2, with the band following the unit.

Calibration has two visible consequences beyond the session itself. Every lesson node in units below the newly assigned `placed_unit_index` whose prerequisites are satisfied — and whose owning unit is itself in an open posture — is opened from `LOCKED` to `UNLOCKED`, so the learner can begin where they were placed, and `leaf_count` is recomputed. The `CALIBRATE` action may also carry a `proctor_user_id`, which overwrites the session's supervising operator and so re-derives `is_institution_graded` and the boundary-anchor choice before the band is computed. And when the resulting `placed_unit_index` is at least 5, a conversion offer with `milestone_code` `PLACEMENT_SKIP` is issued for the learner, priced per §3.9. The placement action also stamps `graded_at` and records the supplied `proctor_user_id`.

Calibration is not one-way. A calibrated session can be reopened for rescoring, which clears `calibrated_cefr_band` and re-evaluates the band from the stored sub-scores, and calibration can then be applied again; a rescoring that reopens a calibrated session leaves the session in `SCORED` with its already-assigned `placed_unit_index` retained, so the monotonic guarantee survives the loop. Across those loops the learner's `placed_unit_index` is monotonic: a recalibration takes the larger of the index already assigned and the newly computed one, so a learner never loses ground they have already been credited. Finally, a session in `SCORED` or `CALIBRATED` state whose calibrated band is `B2` or `C1`, sitting under a non-empty `proctor_user_id`, for a learner on the `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` tier, is the trigger that a certification exam purchase consumes — the session moves into the terminal `CERTIFIED` state when its assigned `placed_unit_index` is at least 5, and a new exam record is created as §3.8 describes.

### 3.7. Leagues, Weekly Ranking and Anti-Cheat Integrity

Competition is organised weekly. A **League** names a `tier` drawn from `BRONZE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD`, `SAPPHIRE` or `RUBY`, a `week_start` fixed to a Monday at 00:00:00Z, a `status`, the derived `member_count`, a `promotion_cutoff_rank` and a `demotion_cutoff_rank`, and the `settled_at` timestamp of the last completed settlement. A round is opened by an operator or a learner supplying a `tier`, a `week_start` and optionally a `learner_id`; it lands in `FORMING` with the two cutoffs pinned from the league-size constants — the promotion cutoff at rank 3, and the demotion cutoff derived from a demotion span of 3 within a league sized for 10. When a `learner_id` is supplied at open time, a **LeagueStanding** row is created for that learner in `GOOD_STANDING` and the member count becomes 1.

Joining a round is gated. Adding a standing moves a league `FORMING → ACTIVE` only when the learner either has `limits_removed` true on their subscription or holds a family membership in `ACTIVE` status, and the standing is minted in `GOOD_STANDING`; the member count is then recomputed. A standing carries the competing `learner_id`, the derived `weekly_xp`, its `standing`, the derived `rank`, the derived `flag_reason`, and the derived `settled_outcome`. Standings are owned by their learner, readable one at a time, and listable with narrowing by `league_id`, by `learner_id` or by the standing's own `id`.

A standing's weekly experience is not the learner's lifetime total. It is the sum, over the practice attempts recorded from `week_start` up to but not including 7 days later, of each attempt's experience already rounded to a whole number, where an attempt's exact experience is its crown-state base plus its speed bonus, multiplied by the learner's streak multiplier. The per-attempt rounding is pinned before the sum and the sum is never re-rounded, so ten attempts of exactly 7.5 points each total 80 rather than 75. An attempt count is tallied alongside the weekly total to break ties.

Ranks are assigned by a competition expression: a standing's rank is one greater than the number of other eligible standings that beat it. A standing beats another when its weekly experience is higher, or when the two are equal on experience and the other has the higher attempt count, or when the two are equal on both and the other standing was created earlier. Only standings in `GOOD_STANDING` are eligible; flagged, promoted and demoted rows are excluded from the candidate set entirely.

The cutoffs are computed against the live membership rather than pinned blindly. The promotion cutoff rank is the smaller of 3 and the member count, and the demotion cutoff rank is the member count minus the demotion span of 3, plus one, floored at 1.

Settlement is a two-stage operator-driven sweep. The week-close event moves an `ACTIVE` league whose clock has reached `week_start` plus 7 days into `SETTLING`, and as it does so the anti-cheat pass runs first: standings that the flag rule condemns move out of `GOOD_STANDING` and into `FLAGGED`, recording a `flag_reason` of `HIGH_ASYMMETRY_FAST_REFERENCES` and dropping out of the ranking candidate set, with the member count recomputed. The closing sweep then runs the ranking, stamps `settled_at` with the logical clock, moves each remaining `GOOD_STANDING` standing to `PROMOTED` when its rank is at or below the promotion cutoff, moves each to `DEMOTED` when its rank is at or above the demotion cutoff, and lands the league in `CLOSED`. Because the demotion sweep runs last, a standing that satisfies both cutoffs settles as `DEMOTED`. A standing that cleared neither cutoff keeps the `settled_outcome` value `HELD`, and a standing not yet reached by any sweep carries `settled_outcome` `NONE`. Promotion records `settled_outcome` `PROMOTED`, recomputes the learner's `xp_total`, and issues a conversion offer with `milestone_code` `LEAGUE_PROMOTION`. Demotion records `settled_outcome` `DEMOTED`, recomputes the learner's `leaf_count`, and — the sting of relegation — adds one decay point to every crowned node of that learner sitting in a unit that is `COMPLETED`, feeding the decay machinery of §3.2. A settled round's `member_count` keeps counting the standings that settled out of `GOOD_STANDING`: `PROMOTED` and `DEMOTED` rows remain members of the round they competed in, so the count does not fall as a settlement resolves the field. Firing a settlement sweep against a league that is not `ACTIVE` at the week close, or not `SETTLING` on the closing sweep, is refused with `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_SETTLING` at HTTP 409.

The same operations are reachable through a single league-modification action that takes an `action` selector and an optional `learner_id`. Its `JOIN` form mints a `GOOD_STANDING` standing for the named learner and increments the member count; its `SETTLE` form runs the anti-cheat pass, then the ranking, then the promotion and demotion transitions, issues the promotion offers, and stamps `settled_at`; its `REOPEN` form advances `week_start` to the current clock and returns every `DEMOTED` standing to `GOOD_STANDING`. Every form recomputes the member count. A `JOIN` for a learner who is not join-eligible is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403; the `SETTLE` form is admitted only while the league is `ACTIVE` or `SETTLING` and the `REOPEN` form only while it is `CLOSED`, each otherwise refused with `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_SETTLING` at HTTP 409; and the `REOPEN` form also resets each restored standing's `settled_outcome` to `NONE`.

Integrity is enforced by a weighted anti-cheat screen that runs before any demotion. It first freezes the eligible candidate set and computes the preliminary weekly experience for each standing, then identifies the top learner by the highest preliminary experience, breaking ties by the higher attempt count and then by the earlier standing creation. From that frozen set it derives a median weekly experience — for an even-sized candidate set the mean of the two middle values, left fractional — and computes an aggression score as one thousand times the top learner's weekly experience divided by one more than the median, rounded half up to a whole number. It picks out the top learner's benchmark node — the node with the lowest `node_index` among that learner's attempts inside the week, whether or not it still carries a crown — and counts how many of the top learner's attempts inside the week landed on that benchmark node with a `response_ms` under 800 milliseconds. It also computes an accuracy break as the top learner's correct attempts divided by their attempt count, left unrounded, and taken as zero when the learner has no attempts in the week.

The screen condemns at most one standing: the figures above are all drawn from the preliminary top learner, and only that learner is tested. A standing is flagged when either of two compound conditions holds. The first is that the fast-reference count on the benchmark node has reached eight or more and the aggression score has reached at least two thousand. The second is that the aggression score has reached at least two thousand and the accuracy break has fallen below one half. A standing flagged this way moves `GOOD_STANDING → FLAGGED` and records the reason; every other eligible standing keeps `GOOD_STANDING`. The screen runs while the league is `SETTLING`, so both figures are computed against the frozen pre-demotion candidate set. When the sweep is replayed at week-open time after demotions have landed, the fast-reference count is recounted against the post-demotion set, so a learner demoted earlier in the same settlement can record a lower count and escape a flag they would otherwise have carried.

Flags and demotions are both reversible at the next round. The week-open event returns a `CLOSED` league to `ACTIVE` once every demoted standing has been re-ranked with weekly experience above the demotion cutoff, restores those standings `DEMOTED → GOOD_STANDING`, advances `week_start` to the current clock, and re-runs the ranking; re-ranked weekly experience is rounded once over the summed exact totals rather than per attempt, which is why a standing can fall across the promotion boundary on the re-rank. Flagged standings are likewise restored `FLAGGED → GOOD_STANDING` at week-open, their `flag_reason` cleared and their eligibility restored.

### 3.8. Certification Exams, Proctoring and Institutional Reporting

A learner who has proven their level can purchase a proctored credential. A **CertificationExam** records the candidate through `learner_id`, a `product_code` drawn from `DET_BASIC`, `DET_PRO` or `DET_INSTITUTIONAL`, its `status`, the `purchased_at` timestamp, the assigned `proctored_by_user_id`, the `proctor_started_at` timestamp, the derived `score_band`, the candidate's `raw_score`, the derived `report_id` linking to the issued report, and the `expires_at` timestamp at which an unreported purchase lapses.

Purchase follows directly from a placement session that has already been scored and calibrated. A learner buys an exam by supplying `learner_id`, a `product_code`, a `proctor_user_id`, a `proctor_window_hours` and the `expected_learner_version` guard. The purchase is admitted when the learner's placement session sits in `SCORED` or `CALIBRATED` state, its calibrated band is `B2` or `C1`, the named `proctor_user_id` is a non-empty operator identifier, and the learner is on the `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` tier; the session then transitions `SCORED → CERTIFIED` (or, from a `CALIBRATED` session, `CALIBRATED → CERTIFIED` when its assigned index is at least 5) and the learner's `xp_total` is recomputed. The version guard is checked before the tier and placement qualifications, so a purchase that is both stale and otherwise ineligible reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. On success a new exam row is created in `PURCHASED`, its `expires_at` is set to the logical clock plus the supplied `proctor_window_hours` converted to seconds, and the learner's `version` increments by one. A purchase whose learner version guard is stale is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Should the window lapse unreported, the exam-expiry sweep moves a `PURCHASED` exam to the terminal `EXPIRED` state.

Proctoring opens the examination window. An assigned proctor advances the exam out of `PURCHASED` and into `PROCTORING`, which requires a non-empty `proctor_user_id` — supplied on the call or else carried forward from the purchase's `proctored_by_user_id` — and a clock at or before the exam's `expires_at`; a proctor attempt past that instant is refused with `ERR_PROCTOR_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at HTTP 422, and one aimed at an exam not in `PURCHASED` is refused with `ERR_EXAM_NOT_PROCTORING` at HTTP 409. The operation records the `proctor_user_id` and stamps `proctor_started_at` with the logical clock. The submission of a raw score moves the exam from `PROCTORING` to `SUBMITTED` and stores the `raw_score`, and it is admitted only while the clock is at or before `proctor_started_at` plus the proctoring window of `PROCTOR_WINDOW_HOURS`; submitting after that window closes is refused with `ERR_PROCTOR_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at HTTP 422. Submitting against an exam that is not `PROCTORING` is refused with `ERR_EXAM_NOT_PROCTORING` at HTTP 409.

Finalisation converts the raw score into a band through a threshold rule. A normalised score is formed by adding any proctor-supplied discipline penalty to the raw score and constraining the result to lie between 0 and 100 inclusive. The band is `DISTINCTION` at a normalised score of 85 or above, `STRONG_PASS` at 75 or above, `PASS` at 60 or above, and `NOT_CERTIFIED` below that. The operation moves the exam out of `SUBMITTED` and into `SCORED` and writes the band onto `score_band`. The penalty lands differently depending on how the exam was delivered. For a proctored session the penalty is added to the raw score before the band lookup, so a raw score of 78 with no penalty lands on `STRONG_PASS`. For a self-serve session, or whenever the discipline penalty is at or below the one-step demotion threshold of minus ten, the band is looked up against the unadjusted raw score and then demoted exactly one step, so the same raw score of 78 with a penalty of minus ten yields `PASS` instead of `STRONG_PASS`. The threshold constant governing band boundaries is inclusive at the lower bound.

An integrity failure voids the exam rather than scoring it. A proctor who supplies a `void_reason` — together with the `discipline_score`, `raw_score` and `discipline_penalty` fields the operation accepts — moves the exam out of `PROCTORING` and into the terminal `VOID` state, sets `score_band` to `NOT_CERTIFIED`, and moves every `ISSUED` engagement offer for that learner to `SUPPRESSED`, so a candidate who cheated neither passes nor continues to receive conversion messaging. A void, a score submission and a finalisation of the band are each addressed to an exam in a particular state: submission and void to `PROCTORING`, and the band finalisation to `SUBMITTED`, the state a submitted raw score leaves behind. An operation aimed at an exam that is not in the state it expects is refused with `ERR_EXAM_NOT_PROCTORING` at HTTP 409.

Passing exams are reported outward. A proctor files an institutional report by supplying an `institution_name` and the institution's own `institution_learner_code`; the operation is admitted only when the exam sits in `SCORED` state with a `score_band` of `PASS`, `STRONG_PASS` or `DISTINCTION`. It creates a **ScoreReport** carrying the exam's `learner_id`, its `raw_score` and its `score_band` alongside the receiving institution's identifiers and a `reported_at` timestamp, moves the exam from `SCORED` into the terminal `REPORTED` state, sets the exam's `report_id` to the newly minted record, and recomputes the learner's `xp_total`. The report itself is readable individually and listable with narrowing by `exam_id`, by `learner_id`, by `institution_name` or by its own `id`. Exams are likewise listable with narrowing by `learner_id`, by `report_id` or by `status`, and individually readable by a learner, a certifying proctor or an operator.

### 3.9. Conversion Offers, Pricing and Notification Fatigue

The product's revenue motion is to catch a learner at a moment of investment and offer them a paid plan. An **EngagementOffer** records the targeted `learner_id`, the `milestone_code` that qualified them — one of `STREAK_7`, `STREAK_30`, `PLACEMENT_SKIP`, `LEAGUE_PROMOTION` or `HEART_DEPLETION` — the derived `discount_pct`, a `status` of `ISSUED`, `REDEEMED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPPRESSED`, the `issued_at` timestamp, the `expires_at` timestamp closing the issue window, and the derived `scored_tier` of `PLUS` or `PLUS_FAMILY`. Offers are owned by their learner and listable across the learner's history.

An operator issues an offer for a `learner_id` and a `milestone_code`. Minting one stamps `issued_at` with the logical clock and sets `expires_at` to that instant plus an offer window of `OFFER_WINDOW_DAYS`. The discount is then computed, the notification cadence is evaluated for the learner, and the offer's initial status is decided by that cadence: when the cadence resolves to suppressed the offer is created in `SUPPRESSED` status and never reaches the learner, and otherwise it is created `ISSUED`. Offers also arise implicitly from the behavioural milestones described earlier — a streak reaching 7 or 30, a placement that skips at least 5 units, a league promotion, and a heart depletion at the moment a streak breaks — and each is minted and priced by the same rule.

Pricing is tiered and additive. A milestone bonus is looked up from the triggering code: 5 percent for `STREAK_7`, 15 percent for `STREAK_30`, 10 percent for `PLACEMENT_SKIP`, 10 percent for `LEAGUE_PROMOTION`, and 20 percent for `HEART_DEPLETION`. A streak bonus rewards consistency: subtract 7 from the learner's `current_length`, floor that at zero, divide by 7, truncate toward zero, multiply by a 2 percent step, and cap the result at 10 percent. The three components — a base discount of 10 percent, the milestone bonus and the streak bonus — are summed and capped at a maximum discount of 40 percent. The amount due is that discount applied to a base monthly fee of 6999 cents multiplied by the subscription's `seats_total`, rounded half up to whole cents, with `seats_total` read before any cascade that might change it. The `scored_tier` the offer is priced at is `PLUS_FAMILY` when `seats_total` is 2 or more and `PLUS` below that.

Rounding is applied once, at the end, in the ordinary family-plan flow: the seat multiplier is applied to the fee before the discount is taken and the result rounded once, so a 17 percent discount on a four-seat family yields 23237 cents. Re-quoting the same offer through the family-plan seat preview instead rounds each seat's price first and multiplies afterwards, which yields 23236 cents for the identical inputs — a one-cent divergence implementers should reproduce rather than normalise away.

Redemption happens through the subscription-modification write. Supplying an `offer_id` alongside `expected_subscription_version` admits the redemption only when the offer is still `ISSUED`, the subscription's `plan_code` is `PLUS_FAMILY` or `CERTIFICATION_ELITE`, and the clock has not passed the offer's `expires_at`; otherwise the write is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_REDEEMABLE` at HTTP 409. A successful redemption re-prices the plan under the discount schedule above, preserves `seats_total`, and moves the offer `ISSUED → REDEEMED`.

Offers that are never taken lapse. The offer-expiry sweep moves any `ISSUED` offer whose `expires_at` has passed to `EXPIRED`. Offers belonging to a learner whose subscription has expired are swept to `EXPIRED` as part of that cascade, and offers belonging to a learner whose exam is voided for integrity are moved to `SUPPRESSED` as §3.8 describes.

Reaching the learner at all is itself rationed, because over-messaging drives uninstalls. The cadence rule begins with a daily notification budget of 3 slots, raised by 2 for a learner on the `CERTIFICATION_ELITE` tier and by a further 1 when the learner already has an offer sitting in `ISSUED` status. It then measures how many practice attempts the learner has already recorded inside the current local focus day, subtracts that from the budget, and floors the remainder at zero. When at least one slot remains the cadence resolves to allowed and the offer proceeds as `ISSUED`; when the remainder is zero the cadence resolves to suppressed and the offer is created `SUPPRESSED`. Independently of the cadence, an offer that is still `ISSUED` is moved to `SUPPRESSED` by a fatigue sweep once the learner has recorded 3 or more practice attempts inside the trailing 24 hours. A separate urgency rank orders the messaging: 0 when the streak is `BROKEN`, 1 when the learner is `EVALUATING` with no `freeze_tokens` left, 2 when `hearts_available` has fallen to 1 or below, and 3 otherwise.

The cadence window itself shifts with streak state, and the shift is observable. For a learner whose streak sits in `EVALUATING`, `QUALIFIED`, `FROZEN` or `REPAIRED`, the attempt count is taken over the local focus day only, so a learner who practised twice before local midnight and twice after is measured at 2 for the new day. For a learner whose streak is `BROKEN` — the win-back flow — the same count is taken over a trailing 24-hour rolling window instead, so that learner measures 4, the remaining slots fall to zero, and the offer is suppressed.
