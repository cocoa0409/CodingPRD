# Truck Body Upfitting & Final Stage Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A commercial truck upfitter lives in the gap between two manufacturers. The chassis maker ships an *incomplete vehicle* — a cab, a frame and a driveline with no cargo-carrying structure — accompanied by an incomplete vehicle document that explicitly transfers the duty of certifying conformity to whoever finishes the truck. The upfitter then mounts a service, dump, flatbed or van body, integrates cranes, liftgates and power-take-off-driven hydraulics, taps the vehicle's electrical architecture for aftermarket controls, and signs the certification label that goes on the door jamb. Every one of those steps is constrained by the chassis maker's body builder book: how much weight each axle may carry, how long a body the frame will accept, how much continuous auxiliary current the electrical system will tolerate, and how much hydraulic flow the factory power take-off can source. Getting any of it wrong is not a paperwork problem — it is an overloaded axle, a voided chassis warranty, or a truck that cannot legally be registered.

This API is the system of record for that final-stage manufacturing process. It tracks incoming chassis from receipt through scale-ticket inspection to allocation; it holds the standardized build configurations that let pool-stock trucks be produced repeatably while still permitting customer-specific one-offs; it computes axle load distribution and payload capacity from first principles on two distinct bases — what the truck will weigh as drawn, and what it weighs on the scale right now; it schedules bay time against both chassis availability and the customer's contracted in-service date; and it produces the compliance label whose type determines whether the upfitter, or the chassis manufacturer, carries conformity responsibility. After delivery it continues to serve the warranty relationship, apportioning each repair claim between the chassis maker, the upfitter and customer goodwill according to which system failed and whether the upfitter's own electrical work contributed.

Two structural properties of the domain shape the whole specification and should be understood before reading further. First, production progress and certification progress are genuinely independent: a truck can be physically complete and uncertified, or certified on paper and not yet in a bay, and the system therefore carries two state dimensions on the same order rather than one. Second, almost every number the system reports is derived, not stored by a user — committed hydraulic flow, committed amperage, axle loads, invoice totals and liability ledgers are all recomputed from the current component set, which means that removing a single crane from a finished truck must ripple all the way back through the certification and the build state. The specification below is written so that both properties are enforced deterministically rather than left to implementation taste.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The entire API is mounted under the `/upfit` prefix, with the single exception of the test-driving clock endpoint described in §2.4. All request and response bodies are JSON.

### 2.1. Shop Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers, spelled exactly `X-User-ID` (identifying the caller) and `X-User-Role` (naming the caller's role). If either header is missing or empty, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names defined below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes every other check in the system. When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the target operation (see the Authorized Role(s) column of §4), the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionScheduler`, `UpfitEngineer`, `InstallTechnician`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `WarrantyAdministrator` and `Customer`.

A `PlantAdmin` is the plant operations administrator. This role owns the reference catalog — chassis models, body specifications, equipment specifications, work bays and customer accounts — plus custody of incoming vehicles (receipt, quarantine, return), cancellation of orders, and the logical clock. A `PlantAdmin` may not author engineering packages, approve compliance artifacts, or adjudicate warranty claims. A `ProductionScheduler` owns the order book and bay capacity: opening orders, allocating chassis, releasing packages to the floor, booking and cancelling bay slots, and delivering finished trucks; it may not author or approve engineering or compliance artifacts. An `UpfitEngineer` authors the technical package — build configurations, the body mount position, equipment installations, electrical interface proposals and draft weight certifications — and nothing this role creates is approved by itself. An `InstallTechnician` is the shop floor: recording the incoming scale ticket, mounting the body, installing, commissioning, decommissioning and removing equipment, and starting and closing bay slots. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the independent approver: approving or rejecting electrical interfaces, approving weight certifications, sending packages back for rework, and issuing compliance labels. A `WarrantyAdministrator` runs the post-delivery liability split: inspecting, adjudicating, disputing and paying claims. Creating an equipment installation is shared between `UpfitEngineer` and `InstallTechnician`; creating and disputing a warranty claim is shared between `WarrantyAdministrator` and `Customer`; reading reference and order data is open to the operational roles as tabulated in §4.

Identity semantics differ between the operational roles and the customer role, and the distinction matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionScheduler`, `UpfitEngineer`, `InstallTechnician`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `WarrantyAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `engineering_submitted_by_user_id` on an order, as `proposed_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on an electrical interface, as `created_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a weight certification, and as `issued_by_user_id` on a compliance label — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`.

That customer scoping rule is enforced globally. A `Customer` caller may reach an upfit order directly, or reach an equipment installation, electrical interface, build slot, weight certification, compliance label or warranty claim and from there reach the owning order. In every case the owning order's `customer_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The rule applies to every read operation under the API and to opening a warranty claim. A `Customer` may also read only its own customer record.

Segregation of duties is the second authorization concern, and it is enforced on exactly four operations. Approving a weight certification requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` differ from that certification's `created_by_user_id`. Approving an electrical interface requires that the caller differ from the interface's `proposed_by_user_id`. Releasing an order to the floor requires that the caller differ from the order's `engineering_submitted_by_user_id`. Issuing a compliance label requires that the caller differ from the `approved_by_user_id` recorded on the weight certification the label cites. Any of these four collisions is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be less than 1, and may not be greater than 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest record appears first and paging is stable. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` set true when records remain beyond the current page.

Each list endpoint also accepts the domain filters named in §4's description column; filters combine conjunctively, and a filter naming a value outside its enum is a malformed request.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. This applies to `ChassisModel`, `Chassis`, `BodySpec`, `EquipmentSpec`, `WorkBay`, `Customer`, `BuildConfiguration`, `UpfitOrder`, `EquipmentInstallation`, `ElectricalInterface`, `BuildSlot`, `WeightCertification` and `WarrantyClaim`. There is one deliberate exception: a `ComplianceLabel` is an immutable artifact — once issued, its content never changes — so a label row carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `ComplianceLabel`, and a label is never mutated in place. A label is retired by stamping its `voided_at`, and a correction is issued as a brand-new label row that names its predecessor.

Because several rules in this specification depend on the passage of time, the server does not read the host's wall clock for any value it stores on an entity. It maintains a **logical clock** that all date- and time-sensitive behavior reads: the feasibility date computed at release, the `published_at` stamp on a configuration, the `promoted_from_queue_at` stamp on a promoted slot, the `voided_at` stamp on a superseded label, and every `created_at` and `updated_at` stamp described above — a row is stamped, on insertion and again on each mutation, with the logical instant then in force rather than with the host's real time. A test-driving capability restricted to `PlantAdmin` sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; it returns 200 and modifies no entity. Before that capability is ever invoked the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. The single timestamp outside the logical clock's authority is the `api_request_timestamp` of the §2.2 envelope, which belongs to the transport envelope rather than to any entity and is taken from the host's real clock.

Because the logical clock moves only when that capability is invoked, rows created without an intervening clock change carry identical `created_at` values, which makes the tie-break observable. Wherever this specification orders rows by ascending `created_at` — list pagination, the hydraulic duty ranking of §3.4, the queue ranking of §3.7, or the search for an order's most recently created `CLOSED` slot — rows sharing a `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which the server created them, earlier insertion first, and a row's `id` never participates in any ordering.

### 2.5. Reference Resolution and Path Validation Order

Identifiers embedded in a URL are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. Only after every path identifier has resolved does the server look at foreign keys carried in the request body, and those are resolved in the order in which the endpoint declares them. This ordering is observable: a request naming a nonexistent order and a nonexistent equipment specification reports the order failure, because the order identifier is resolved first.

Each reference class has its own 404. A `chassis_model_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_CHASSIS_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`; this is checked when a chassis is received and when a build configuration is created. A `body_spec_id` yields `ERR_BODY_SPEC_NOT_FOUND` on build-configuration creation. An `equipment_spec_id` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, and the same code covers every individual member of a configuration's `standard_equipment_spec_ids` list, so a configuration naming one bad specification among five is refused; the check applies to build-configuration creation and to equipment-installation creation. A `work_bay_id` yields `ERR_WORK_BAY_NOT_FOUND` on build-slot creation and on the bay status change. A `customer_id` yields `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` on order creation, and a `build_configuration_id` yields `ERR_BUILD_CONFIGURATION_NOT_FOUND` on the same operation. A `chassis_id`, whether it arrives in the path or in a body, yields `ERR_CHASSIS_NOT_FOUND`, which covers chassis reads, all chassis lifecycle operations, and chassis allocation. An `upfit_order_id` yields `ERR_UPFIT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` on every order-scoped path and on every child create that names an order. An `equipment_installation_id` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INSTALLATION_NOT_FOUND`, an `electrical_interface_id` yields `ERR_ELECTRICAL_INTERFACE_NOT_FOUND` (on interface reads, approval and rejection), a `build_slot_id` yields `ERR_BUILD_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, a `weight_certification_id` yields `ERR_WEIGHT_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` (on certification reads, certification approval, and compliance-label creation), a `compliance_label_id` or a `supersedes_label_id` yields `ERR_COMPLIANCE_LABEL_NOT_FOUND`, and a `warranty_claim_id` yields `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Many operations in this system can fail several different ways at once, so the order in which checks run is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. When a single request violates more than one rule, the server reports the violation that comes earliest in the following ladder, and it reports exactly one error.

1. **Authentication.** Header presence and role-name validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role must be listed for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Customer scope.** A `Customer` caller reaching outside its own account — `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. **Segregation of duties.** Approver, releaser or issuer colliding with the recorded author — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because this check sits above every existence and state check, a self-approval attempt on a certification that is already `APPROVED` reports the duties violation rather than the state violation.
5. **Request shape.** Required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, integers inside their declared ranges, dates in ISO calendar form — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The one date comparison excluded here is the slot's own span, which is checked much later at step 64 below.
6. **Existence**, in the resolution order of §2.5 — the various not-found codes, ranked among themselves as: chassis model, body spec, equipment spec, work bay, customer, build configuration, chassis, upfit order, equipment installation, electrical interface, build slot, weight certification, compliance label, warranty claim.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** The order-version check on certification drafting, then the chassis-version check on allocation — `ERR_STALE_UPFIT_ORDER_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_CHASSIS_VERSION`. These sit above uniqueness and above every state check, so a stale version is reported even when the target is also in the wrong state.
8. **Uniqueness.** In order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIG_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_NUMBER`.
9. **State preconditions.** In order: the order's own dimension (`ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`), the chassis (`ERR_CHASSIS_INVALID_STATE`), the installation (`ERR_INSTALLATION_INVALID_STATE`), the interface (`ERR_INTERFACE_INVALID_STATE`), the slot (`ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`), the certification (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_INVALID_STATE`), the claim (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`), the configuration (`ERR_CONFIG_INVALID_STATE`), and finally the already-superseded label check (`ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_SUPERSEDED`).
10. **Regulatory weight safety**, which outranks every remaining business rule. In order: `ERR_GVWR_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_REAR_GAWR_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_FRONT_GAWR_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_PAYLOAD_SHORTFALL`, then the measured-basis requirement for labeling (`ERR_MEASURED_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`).
11. **Temporal feasibility.** In order: in-service date feasibility (`ERR_IN_SERVICE_DATE_INFEASIBLE`), chassis occupancy (`ERR_CHASSIS_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`), bay capacity (`ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`), chassis availability against the slot start (`ERR_CHASSIS_NOT_AVAILABLE_BY_SLOT_START`), and the slot's date span (`ERR_SLOT_DATE_RANGE_INVALID`). Note the consequence for slot creation: because chassis occupancy is ranked above bay capacity, a request that collides on both the truck and the bay reports the occupancy conflict, and it does so even when the request asked to be queued.
12. **Body builder book constraints**, in this order: cab-to-axle sufficiency (`ERR_CAB_TO_AXLE_INSUFFICIENT`), wheelbase sufficiency (`ERR_WHEELBASE_INSUFFICIENT`), auxiliary electrical ceiling (`ERR_AUX_ELECTRICAL_LOAD_EXCEEDED`), power-take-off flow ceiling (`ERR_PTO_FLOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), power-take-off provisioning (`ERR_PTO_NOT_PROVISIONED`), hydraulic circuit coherence (`ERR_HYDRAULIC_CIRCUIT_REQUIRED`), unresolved hydraulic deficit (`ERR_HYDRAULIC_DEFICIT_UNRESOLVED`), warranty-voiding interface without a waiver (`ERR_CHASSIS_WARRANTY_VOIDING_INTERFACE`), network gateway prerequisite (`ERR_CAN_GATEWAY_REQUIRED`), configuration publication (`ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PUBLISHED`), bay body-type eligibility (`ERR_BAY_BODY_TYPE_UNSUPPORTED`), pool-stock standardization (`ERR_NON_STANDARD_POOL_EQUIPMENT`), body already installed (`ERR_BODY_ALREADY_INSTALLED`), chassis model match (`ERR_CHASSIS_MODEL_MISMATCH`), mount zone exclusivity (`ERR_MOUNT_ZONE_CONFLICT`), and finally the hydraulic power source requirement (`ERR_HYDRAULIC_SOURCE_MISSING`).
13. **Referential integrity between certification artifacts**, in this order: the cited certification must belong to the same order (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_ORDER_MISMATCH`), the cited certification must be approved (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_APPROVED`), an order may hold only one standing label (`ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_ACTIVE`), and a chassis may not be pulled off a committed order (`ERR_CHASSIS_ALREADY_ALLOCATED`).
14. **Fiscal ceilings, checked last of all.** The warranty liability cap — `ERR_WARRANTY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.7. The Parallel Order State Model and Its Enumerations

An `UpfitOrder` carries three independent state fields on the same row, and understanding why is the key to the whole system. `build_status` tracks production progress and takes one of `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_BUILD`, `BUILD_COMPLETE`, `DELIVERED`, `CHASSIS_HOLD` or `CANCELLED`, beginning at `DRAFT`; `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. `compliance_status` tracks final-stage certification progress and takes one of `NOT_STARTED`, `DESIGN_CERTIFIED`, `MEASURED_CERTIFIED` or `LABELED`, beginning at `NOT_STARTED`; no value on this dimension is terminal, because certification can be undone by a physical change to the truck. `body_install_status` tracks whether the body itself is bolted down and takes `PLANNED` or `INSTALLED`, beginning at `PLANNED`, with `INSTALLED` terminal.

Each dimension advances on its own triggers and neither waits on the other. Sending a package back for rework moves the production dimension and leaves the certification dimension exactly where it stood. Approving a design-basis certification advances the certification dimension no matter where production sits. There is exactly one join point: handing the finished truck to the customer requires the production dimension to be at `BUILD_COMPLETE` *and* the certification dimension to be at `LABELED` simultaneously.

The remaining state enumerations are as follows. A `Chassis` moves through `RECEIVED`, `INSPECTED`, `QUARANTINED`, `ALLOCATED`, `IN_UPFIT`, `COMPLETED` and `RETURNED`, starting at `RECEIVED`, with `COMPLETED` and `RETURNED` terminal. An `EquipmentInstallation` moves through `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED`, `COMMISSIONED` and `REMOVED`, starting at `PLANNED`, with `REMOVED` terminal; the pair `INSTALLED` and `COMMISSIONED` forms a cycle that may be traversed any number of times. An `ElectricalInterface` moves through `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED`, starting at `PROPOSED`, with `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` terminal. A `BuildSlot` moves through `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_BUILD`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, ordinarily starting at `SCHEDULED`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` terminal — although a `CLOSED` slot may be reopened by a compensating cascade described in §3.7. A `WeightCertification` moves through `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` and `VOIDED`, starting at `DRAFT`, with `VOIDED` terminal. A `WarrantyClaim` moves through `SUBMITTED`, `INSPECTED`, `ADJUDICATED`, `DISPUTED` and `PAID`, starting at `SUBMITTED`, with only `PAID` terminal; the sequence `ADJUDICATED` to `DISPUTED` to `INSPECTED` and back to `ADJUDICATED` is a legitimate re-adjudication cycle. A `BuildConfiguration` moves through `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and `DEPRECATED`, starting at `DRAFT`. A `WorkBay` carries `status` of either `ACTIVE` or `DOWN`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`.

Other enumerated fields are fixed as follows. A chassis model's `electrical_bus` is `J1939_CAN` or `DISCRETE`; its `pto_provision` is `NONE`, `CLUTCH_SHIFT` or `FULL`. A body's `body_type` is `SERVICE`, `DUMP`, `FLATBED` or `VAN`. An equipment specification's `equipment_class` is `CRANE`, `LIFTGATE`, `PTO_HOIST` or `PTO_PUMP`, and its `mount_zone` is `BEHIND_CAB`, `BODY_FLOOR` or `REAR_OVERHANG`. A chassis's `ownership` is `POOL_STOCK` or `CUSTOMER_ASSIGNED`, defaulting to `POOL_STOCK`. A configuration's `standardization_tier` is `TIER_A`, `TIER_B` or `CUSTOM`. An order's `order_type` is `POOL_STOCK` or `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC`, and its `chassis_warranty_status` is `INTACT`, `CONDITIONAL` or `VOIDED`, defaulting to `INTACT`. An installation's `hydraulic_circuit` is `NONE`, `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY`, defaulting to `NONE`, and its `hydraulic_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `OK` or `DEFICIT`, defaulting to `NOT_APPLICABLE`. An interface's `interface_method` is `BODY_BUILDER_MODULE`, `CAN_GATEWAY` or `DIRECT_SPLICE`; its `signal_source` is `BODY_CONTROLLER`, `J1939_BUS` or `DISCRETE_CIRCUIT`; its `warranty_impact` is `NONE`, `CONDITIONAL` or `VOIDS`. A slot's `slot_type` is `BODY_MOUNT`, `EQUIPMENT_INTEGRATION` or `FINAL_QC`. A certification's `basis` is `AS_DESIGNED` or `AS_MEASURED`. A label's `label_type` is `FINAL_STAGE`, `ALTERER` or `INCOMPLETE_PASSTHROUGH`, and its `responsible_party` is `UPFITTER` or `CHASSIS_MANUFACTURER`. A claim's `failure_system` is one of `CHASSIS_ENGINE`, `CHASSIS_TRANSMISSION`, `CHASSIS_BRAKES`, `CHASSIS_ELECTRICAL`, `BODY_STRUCTURE`, `BODY_ELECTRICAL` or `HYDRAULICS`, and its `split_basis` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, defaulting to `PROVISIONAL`.

### 2.8. Units, Precision and Rounding Discipline

All weights are expressed in whole pounds, all lengths in whole inches, all currency in integer cents, all electrical loads in whole amperes, and all hydraulic flows in whole gallons per minute. Where a computation produces a fraction, rounding is **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of rounding steps is specified exactly, because the difference between rounding once and rounding twice is observable in the results.

Axle loads are accumulated at full precision and rounded exactly twice: once for the front axle total and once for the rear axle total. Gross vehicle weight is then the sum of those two already-rounded integers, not a fresh rounding of the unrounded sum. Payload capacity takes the minimum across its unrounded candidate ceilings and rounds that minimum once, then floors the result at zero so a negative capacity is reported as zero rather than as a negative number. Electrical loads and hydraulic flows are integer sums with no rounding at all. Monetary computations round half up exactly once when applying a standardization discount, and exactly twice — first the chassis share, then the upfitter share, in that order — when apportioning a warranty claim, with the goodwill share taken as the arithmetic remainder rather than rounded independently. Calendar arithmetic adds whole days to a date and compares dates without any time component.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Chassis and Orders

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy work that a client never observed, and both are therefore guarded by an explicit version check. In each case the guarded request carries a non-optional field naming the version the client last read, and a mismatch against the stored value is rejected outright rather than merged.

Drafting a weight certification — `POST /upfit/weight-certifications` — requires `expected_upfit_order_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `UpfitOrder`. If that value does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UPFIT_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a newer certification of the same basis legitimately voids the previous one, so a client working from a stale view of the order would supersede numbers computed from a component set it never saw; the version check turns that into a deterministic refusal.

Allocating a chassis — `POST /upfit/upfit-orders/{upfit_order_id}/allocate-chassis` — requires `expected_chassis_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Chassis` named in the request. If that value does not equal the chassis's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHASSIS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Pool-stock re-allocation is a legitimate operation: an already-allocated unit may be rebound to a different order, putting the incumbent order on hold. Precisely because that is legitimate, a client holding a stale view of the unit would otherwise pull it off an order it never inspected; the version check makes the outcome deterministic.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be required to carry one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.10. Derived Field Reconciliation and Cascade Discipline

A substantial set of fields is never written by a client. On an `UpfitOrder` these are `chassis_warranty_status`, `committed_pto_flow_gpm`, `committed_aux_amp_draw`, `invoice_total_cents`, `chassis_liability_cents`, `upfitter_liability_cents` and `customer_goodwill_cents`; on an `EquipmentInstallation` they are `allocated_flow_gpm` and `hydraulic_status`; on an `ElectricalInterface` it is `warranty_impact`; on a `WeightCertification` they are `front_axle_load_lb`, `rear_axle_load_lb`, `gvw_lb`, `payload_capacity_lb` and `included_component_count`; on a `WarrantyClaim` they are `split_basis`, `chassis_pct`, `upfitter_pct`, `goodwill_pct`, `chassis_liability_cents`, `upfitter_liability_cents` and `customer_goodwill_cents`; and on a `ComplianceLabel` they are `label_type`, `responsible_party`, `certified_gvwr_lb`, `certified_front_gawr_lb`, `certified_rear_gawr_lb` and `certified_payload_lb`. Each is recomputed from scratch at the moments named in §3, and each recomputation reads the current component set rather than adjusting a running total.

Because derived values span entities, a single operation frequently mutates several rows. The governing principle is that every row a cascade touches has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, in addition to the row the request nominally addressed. Two cascades are deep enough to have earned explicit hop-by-hop treatment in §3: returning a chassis to its manufacturer, and stripping an equipment item off a truck. In both cases the hops run to completion within the same request — this system has no deferred or eventually-consistent reconciliation. A read issued immediately after a cascading write observes every hop of that cascade.

### 2.11. Body Builder Book Authority and the Governing Chassis Model

Every dimensional, electrical and hydraulic constraint in this system originates in one place: the `ChassisModel` row transcribed from the chassis maker's body builder book. Because an order may exist before a physical unit is bound to it, the specification is explicit about which model governs at any moment. When the order's `chassis_id` is not null, the governing model is the model of the allocated unit, reached through that chassis's `chassis_model_id`. When `chassis_id` is still null, the governing model is the one named by the order's build configuration. Axle load computation, the auxiliary electrical ceiling and the power-take-off provisioning test all resolve the governing model this way.

One constraint deliberately behaves differently. The hydraulic flow pool belongs to the physical truck, not to the book: while no unit is bound, the available flow is treated as exactly zero, and the flow-capacity ceiling is not enforced at all. Provisioning level, by contrast, is a pure book property and *is* checked before allocation using the configuration's model. The consequence is spelled out in §3.4 and is intentional: hydraulic equipment planned before a unit is allocated starts out in deficit and is repaired when allocation re-runs the allocation pass against a real chassis.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Catalog: Books, Bodies, Equipment and Bays

Nothing can be built until the plant's reference catalog exists, and a `PlantAdmin` owns all of it. Registering a chassis model is the act of transcribing one entry from the chassis maker's body builder book into the system. The entry is keyed by a `code` such as `F550-DRW-84CA`, which must be unique across all chassis models and must be between 1 and 40 characters long, and it names the chassis `make`. It then carries the three ratings that bound everything downstream — `gvwr_lb`, the gross vehicle weight rating taken from the incomplete vehicle document, together with `front_gawr_lb` and `rear_gawr_lb`, the per-axle ratings — each of which must be at least 1. Two dimensions follow: `wheelbase_in`, the distance in inches from the front-axle centerline to the rear-axle centerline, and `cab_to_axle_in`, the distance from the back of the cab to the rear-axle centerline; both must be at least 1. The wheelbase deserves special mention because it is the denominator of every moment calculation in this system, which is why it can never be zero.

The same entry records what the incomplete vehicle already weighs before anything is added: `curb_front_axle_lb` and `curb_rear_axle_lb`, the book curb weights carried by each axle, each at least 0. Three provisioning attributes complete the record. `max_aux_amp_draw`, at least 0, is the book ceiling on continuous auxiliary current. `electrical_bus` states which vehicle network aftermarket controls must interface with, either `J1939_CAN` or `DISCRETE`. `pto_provision` states the factory power-take-off provision level as `NONE`, `CLUTCH_SHIFT` or `FULL`, and `pto_max_flow_gpm` states the hydraulic flow in gallons per minute that the factory power take-off can source; it defaults to 0, must be at least 0, and must be exactly 0 whenever `pto_provision` is `NONE`, because a chassis with no provision can source no flow. Finally, `body_builder_book_rev` records the revision label of the book these constraints were read from, and `incomplete_vehicle_document_rev` records the revision label of the incomplete vehicle document that transfers labeling duty to the final-stage manufacturer; when omitted the latter defaults to `IVD-2026.1`. Chassis models can be listed and filtered by `make` and by `pto_provision`.

A body catalog entry describes one mountable body. Its `code`, such as `SERVICE-11FT`, is unique across body specifications, and its `body_type` places it in one of four families — `SERVICE`, `DUMP`, `FLATBED` or `VAN` — which drives both labor hours and bay eligibility later on. Its `weight_lb`, at least 1, is the catalog dry weight. Two centroids matter: `cg_from_body_front_in` locates the body's own center of gravity measured rearward from the body's front edge, and `cargo_cg_from_body_front_in` locates the cargo centroid the same way; both are at least 0, and the second one is what drives the payload capacity axle terms. `length_in`, at least 1, is the overall body length in inches. Two book minimums guard the pairing: `required_min_ca_in` is the minimum chassis cab-to-axle dimension the body may be mounted on, and `required_min_wheelbase_in` is the minimum chassis wheelbase, both at least 0. `aux_amp_draw`, at least 0, is the continuous current the body's own lighting and controls draw. `requires_hydraulics`, defaulting to false, is set true when the body cannot function without a power-take-off-sourced hydraulic supply — a dump body being the obvious case. `list_price_cents`, at least 0, is the catalog price and the first term of every build invoice. Body specifications can be filtered by `body_type`.

Equipment catalog entries work the same way. A unique `code` such as `CRANE-6000` identifies the item, and `equipment_class` places it in one of `CRANE`, `LIFTGATE`, `PTO_HOIST` or `PTO_PUMP`. `weight_lb` is at least 1. `requires_pto`, defaulting to false, marks items that draw hydraulic flow from the chassis power take-off, and `required_flow_gpm` states how much flow the item needs in gallons per minute; it defaults to 0, must be at least 0, and must be exactly 0 whenever `requires_pto` is false. `aux_amp_draw`, at least 0, is the continuous current the item draws. `duty_priority`, at least 1, is the item's hydraulic duty rank, with 1 being served first when flow is scarce. `mount_zone` says where on the chassis the item sits — `BEHIND_CAB`, `BODY_FLOOR` or `REAR_OVERHANG` — and only one non-removed item may occupy a given zone on a given truck. `requires_can_gateway`, defaulting to false, marks items whose controls must be read through an approved network gateway rather than spliced into vehicle wiring. `list_price_cents`, at least 0, is the catalog price. Equipment specifications can be filtered by `equipment_class` and by `requires_pto`.

Bays and customers close out the catalog. A work bay has a unique `code` such as `BAY-1`, a `concurrent_capacity` of at least 1 — how many live slots may share any instant in that bay — and a non-empty `supported_body_types` array whose members are drawn from the body-type enumeration. A bay's `status` starts at `ACTIVE`, and a `PlantAdmin` may take a bay `ACTIVE` or `DOWN` by supplying a new `status`; the change increments the bay's `version` by 1. A `DOWN` bay accepts no new slots and is skipped entirely by the queue promotion pass described in §3.7. Bays can be filtered by `status`. A fleet customer account carries the customer's legal `name`, a unique `account_code`, and a `fleet_contact_email` used for in-service date coordination; the `id` returned when the account is created is exactly the value a `Customer`-role caller must present as its `X-User-ID`. Customers can be filtered by `account_code`.

### 3.2. Incoming Vehicle Custody: Receipt, Inspection, Quarantine and Return

When an incomplete vehicle arrives on a transporter, a `PlantAdmin` receives it against a body builder book entry. Receipt records the unit's `vin`, which must be exactly 17 characters and unique across all chassis — a second attempt at the same number is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN` — plus the `chassis_model_id` supplying every book constraint that will apply to builds on this unit, an optional `ownership` of `POOL_STOCK` or `CUSTOMER_ASSIGNED` that defaults to `POOL_STOCK`, and an `availability_date` giving the calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which the unit is free to enter a build bay. A newly received unit sits at `RECEIVED` with `measured_front_axle_weight_lb` and `measured_rear_axle_weight_lb` both null, `allocated_upfit_order_id` null and `quarantine_reason` null.

Inspection is an `InstallTechnician` task and it is where the scale ticket enters the system, moving the unit `RECEIVED → INSPECTED`. The request must carry both `measured_front_axle_weight_lb` and `measured_rear_axle_weight_lb`, each at least 0 when present; if either is absent the request is malformed. Both values are stored on the chassis, `quarantine_reason` is cleared back to null, and the unit's `version` increments by 1 with `updated_at` refreshed. These two numbers are the curb weights that every measured-basis weight certification is built on, so an uninspected unit can never carry a measured certification. Inspection also accepts a unit sitting at `QUARANTINED`, moving it `QUARANTINED → INSPECTED` and overwriting the stored axle weights with the re-inspection ticket while again clearing `quarantine_reason`; re-inspection is the only route by which a quarantined unit re-enters the allocatable pool. Inspection attempted from any state other than `RECEIVED` or `QUARANTINED` is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_INVALID_STATE`.

Transit damage and document discrepancies are handled by quarantine, which a `PlantAdmin` performs from either `RECEIVED` or `INSPECTED`, taking the unit to `QUARANTINED` and stamping the supplied `quarantine_reason` free text. Quarantine and allocation are branch siblings out of `INSPECTED`: the same inspected unit either goes forward to `ALLOCATED` or backward to `QUARANTINED`, and which edge is taken depends entirely on what the inspector found. Quarantining a unit that is already `ALLOCATED`, `IN_UPFIT`, `COMPLETED` or `RETURNED` is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_INVALID_STATE`.

Sending a unit back to its manufacturer is the most disruptive operation in the custody workflow. A `PlantAdmin` may return a unit from `INSPECTED`, `ALLOCATED` or `IN_UPFIT`, supplying a `reason`, and the unit lands at the terminal `RETURNED` state with its `version` incremented. When the unit was never allocated — the `INSPECTED → RETURNED` case, where `allocated_upfit_order_id` is null — nothing else happens; the return is a single-row change. When the unit *was* bound to an order, on either the `ALLOCATED → RETURNED` or the `IN_UPFIT → RETURNED` edge, a four-hop cascade runs to completion in the same request, and both edges run the identical cascade. Hop one clears the chassis's `allocated_upfit_order_id` and, because that order has just lost its truck, sets the order's `chassis_id` to null and moves its `build_status` to `CHASSIS_HOLD` — an order sitting at `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` takes the `RELEASED → CHASSIS_HOLD`, `SCHEDULED → CHASSIS_HOLD` or `IN_BUILD → CHASSIS_HOLD` edge respectively. Hop two, because that order is no longer schedulable, transitions every one of its build slots sitting at `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` to `CANCELLED`. Hop three, because hop two just freed bay windows, runs the deterministic queue promotion pass of §3.7 for each affected bay, promoting at most one queued slot per freed window to `SCHEDULED` and stamping that slot's `promoted_from_queue_at` from the logical clock. Hop four, because a promoted slot has become `SCHEDULED`, moves that slot's own order `RELEASED → SCHEDULED`. Every row touched at every hop has its `version` incremented by 1.

Chassis can be listed and filtered by `chassis_model_id`, by `allocated_upfit_order_id`, by `ownership` and by `status`, which makes it straightforward to ask the pool-stock question — which inspected, unallocated units of a given model are on the yard.

### 3.3. Standardized Configurations and Opening an Upfit Order

Standardization is the upfitter's main lever against cost, and a build configuration is where it lives. An `UpfitEngineer` pairs a chassis model with a body by supplying a `config_code` unique across configurations — a collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIG_CODE` — plus `chassis_model_id`, `body_spec_id`, a `standardization_tier` of `TIER_A`, `TIER_B` or `CUSTOM`, and an optional `standard_equipment_spec_ids` array naming the equipment specifications a pool-stock order on this configuration may install; that array defaults to empty. The two book fit rules are enforced here, before an unbuildable pairing can ever reach an order: the chassis model's `cab_to_axle_in` must be greater than or equal to the body's `required_min_ca_in`, otherwise `ERR_CAB_TO_AXLE_INSUFFICIENT`, and the model's `wheelbase_in` must be greater than or equal to the body's `required_min_wheelbase_in`, otherwise `ERR_WHEELBASE_INSUFFICIENT`. A new configuration is created at `DRAFT` with `published_at` null.

Publication is what makes a configuration usable, taking it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and stamping `published_at` from the current logical-clock instant; retirement takes it `PUBLISHED → DEPRECATED`, leaving existing orders untouched while refusing new ones. Both operations increment the configuration's `version` by 1. Publishing anything other than a `DRAFT`, or retiring anything other than a `PUBLISHED` configuration, is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_INVALID_STATE`. Configurations can be filtered by `chassis_model_id`, `body_spec_id`, `standardization_tier` and `status`.

A `ProductionScheduler` opens an upfit order against a published configuration. The order carries an `order_number` unique across orders — a collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_NUMBER` — and an `order_type` of either `POOL_STOCK`, meaning a standardized build not yet promised to anybody, or `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC`, meaning a build cut to one customer's in-service date. That choice governs `customer_id`: it is required when `order_type` is `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC` and must be null when `order_type` is `POOL_STOCK`. The `build_configuration_id` must name a configuration whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` at the moment the order is created; a `DRAFT` or `DEPRECATED` configuration is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The order also fixes `body_mount_offset_from_front_axle_in`, the rearward distance in inches from the front-axle centerline to the body's front edge and, at at least 0, the single geometric input the shop controls. Three optional inputs round out creation: `required_payload_lb`, the payload the customer contracted for, defaulting to 0 and at least 0; `requested_in_service_date`, the calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which the customer needs the truck in service, defaulting to null and left null for pool-stock orders; and `warranty_liability_cap_cents`, the ceiling on cumulative upfitter warranty liability the order may absorb without an explicit override, defaulting to 5,000,000 cents — that is, fifty thousand dollars — and at least 0.

Order creation seeds a substantial amount of derived state, and every one of these initial values is part of the contract. The system copies `body_spec_id` from the configuration's own body reference and never re-derives it afterward, so a later configuration change cannot silently move a live order onto a different body. The three state fields start at `build_status` `DRAFT`, `compliance_status` `NOT_STARTED` and `body_install_status` `PLANNED`. `chassis_warranty_status` starts `INTACT`, because no interface has been approved yet. `chassis_id` and `body_actual_weight_lb` start null. `committed_aux_amp_draw` is seeded to the body's own `aux_amp_draw`, because the body's lighting draws current from the moment the build is planned. `committed_pto_flow_gpm` starts 0, `rework_round` starts 0, `invoice_total_cents` starts 0, and the three liability ledger fields `chassis_liability_cents`, `upfitter_liability_cents` and `customer_goodwill_cents` all start 0.

The engineering review loop is short and deliberately repeatable. An `UpfitEngineer` submits the package, taking the order `DRAFT → ENGINEERING_REVIEW` provided `body_mount_offset_from_front_axle_in` is present, and the submitter's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `engineering_submitted_by_user_id` — the value that later blocks the same person from releasing the order. A `ComplianceOfficer` may send the package back with a `reason`, taking it `ENGINEERING_REVIEW → REWORK` and incrementing `rework_round` by 1; the certification dimension is untouched by this, since the two dimensions advance independently. Resubmission from rework takes the order `REWORK → ENGINEERING_REVIEW` and overwrites `engineering_submitted_by_user_id` with the resubmitter's identity, closing the revision cycle. Submitting from any state other than `DRAFT` or `REWORK`, or sending back a package that is not in `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

Cancellation is a `PlantAdmin` capability and accepts an order at `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CHASSIS_HOLD`, taking it to the terminal `CANCELLED` with a supplied `reason`; a `BUILD_COMPLETE`, `DELIVERED` or already-`CANCELLED` order cannot be cancelled and yields `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation carries a three-part conditional cascade, identical on every accepted from-state. Every build slot of the order sitting at `QUEUED` or `SCHEDULED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, and because that frees bay windows the queue promotion pass runs on each affected bay. Every equipment installation of the order still sitting at `PLANNED` transitions to `REMOVED`, and the order's `committed_pto_flow_gpm` is recomputed accordingly. The omission of `committed_aux_amp_draw` from that recomputation is deliberate and observable: unlike the removal cascade of §3.4, which explicitly re-derives both committed figures, cancellation re-derives only the committed flow and leaves `committed_aux_amp_draw` holding exactly the value it carried before the cascade, stripped equipment included. And when `chassis_id` is not null, the bound unit returns to `INSPECTED` with its `allocated_upfit_order_id` cleared, so the truck goes back into the allocatable pool rather than being stranded. The order's `version` increments by 1.

Orders can be listed and filtered by `customer_id`, `build_configuration_id`, `body_spec_id`, `chassis_id`, `order_type`, `build_status` and `compliance_status`, and a single-order read returns every derived ledger field described in §2.10.

### 3.4. Equipment Integration and the Hydraulic Flow Waterfall

Planning a crane, liftgate, hoist or pump onto a truck is shared between `UpfitEngineer` and `InstallTechnician`, and the request names the order through its `upfit_order_id`, the catalog item through its `equipment_spec_id`, the item's `mount_cg_from_front_axle_in` — the rearward distance in inches from the front-axle centerline to that item's own center of gravity, at least 0 — and the `hydraulic_circuit` the item is plumbed onto, which is `NONE`, `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY` and defaults to `NONE`. Equipment may be planned while the order's `build_status` is `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD`; on a `CHASSIS_HOLD`, `BUILD_COMPLETE`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` order the create is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. A new row is created at `PLANNED` with `actual_weight_lb` null.

Every body-builder-book capacity gate fires at this moment, in the precedence order of §2.6. The auxiliary electrical ceiling is checked first, with the candidate item's own `aux_amp_draw` counted in, and an overrun yields `ERR_AUX_ELECTRICAL_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. Next comes the hydraulic flow ceiling: when the order already has a chassis bound, the sum of `required_flow_gpm` across the order's non-removed power-take-off installations with the candidate included must not exceed the allocated model's `pto_max_flow_gpm`, otherwise `ERR_PTO_FLOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; while no unit is bound this test is deferred entirely, for the reason given in §2.11. Provisioning is checked next and is never deferred, because it is a pure book property: an item whose specification has `requires_pto` true cannot be planned at all when the governing chassis model's `pto_provision` is `NONE`, and the attempt yields `ERR_PTO_NOT_PROVISIONED`. Circuit coherence follows: an item with `requires_pto` true must be submitted with `hydraulic_circuit` `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY`, and an item with `requires_pto` false must be submitted with `hydraulic_circuit` `NONE`; either mismatch yields `ERR_HYDRAULIC_CIRCUIT_REQUIRED`. The network gateway prerequisite comes next: an item whose specification has `requires_can_gateway` true may only be planned once the order already holds an `APPROVED` electrical interface whose `interface_method` is `CAN_GATEWAY` or `BODY_BUILDER_MODULE`, since such controls must be read through an approved gateway rather than spliced; otherwise `ERR_CAN_GATEWAY_REQUIRED`. Pool standardization is checked after that: on an order whose `order_type` is `POOL_STOCK` the `equipment_spec_id` must be a member of the configuration's `standard_equipment_spec_ids` list, otherwise `ERR_NON_STANDARD_POOL_EQUIPMENT`; a `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC` order may install any specification. Last comes mount-zone exclusivity: at most one non-removed installation may occupy a given `mount_zone` on a given order, because two bodies of iron cannot share one mounting point, and a second attempt yields `ERR_MOUNT_ZONE_CONFLICT`.

Once the gates pass, the system reallocates hydraulic flow across the whole order. The available pool is the `pto_max_flow_gpm` of the governing chassis model of the allocated unit, and it is exactly 0 when the order's `chassis_id` is null — no truck, no hydraulic supply. The candidate set is every installation of the order whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` and whose specification has `requires_pto` true. Those candidates are served in a strictly deterministic sequence: ascending `duty_priority` first, so rank 1 is served before rank 2, and among items sharing a duty rank, ascending `created_at`, so the older row is served first. The row's `id` never participates in the ordering. The order's `committed_pto_flow_gpm` is set to the sum of `required_flow_gpm` across that candidate set. The pool is then paid out in sequence: each candidate is granted the lesser of its own demand and whatever flow remains unallocated, that grant is written to the installation's `allocated_flow_gpm`, the installation's `hydraulic_status` is set to `OK` when the grant equals the demand exactly and to `DEFICIT` when the grant falls short, and the granted amount is subtracted from the remaining pool before the next candidate is considered. A demand exactly equal to the remaining pool is fully granted and reads `OK`. Every other installation on the order — anything whose specification does not require the power take-off, and anything already `REMOVED` — is reset to `allocated_flow_gpm` 0 and `hydraulic_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Planning also re-derives the order's `committed_aux_amp_draw`, and the order's `version` increments by 1.

Because the flow ceiling makes over-subscription unreachable at planning time whenever a unit is already bound, a `DEFICIT` row can arise in only two ways: equipment planned before any unit was allocated, which starts against a pool of zero, or a rebind that moves the order onto a model with a smaller `pto_max_flow_gpm`. Both are repaired the same way — the next allocation re-runs the waterfall against the newly bound model and any candidate whose demand is now fully served flips `DEFICIT → OK`.

An `InstallTechnician` records an item as physically mounted, moving it `PLANNED → INSTALLED`. This requires the parent order to be at `build_status` `IN_BUILD`, requires the installation's own `hydraulic_status` to be anything other than `DEFICIT`, and requires `actual_weight_lb` in the request, which must be at least 1 when present. That scale-verified weight is stored and is what the measured weight basis reads in preference to the catalog figure. Installing also has a compliance consequence that is easy to miss: because the truck's installed component set has just changed, every weight certification of the order carrying `basis` `AS_MEASURED` and `status` `APPROVED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` set to `COMPONENT_SET_CHANGED`, and the certification dimension's own step-back cascade follows from that. Both the installation's `version` and the order's `version` increment by 1.

Commissioning follows a function test, moving the item `INSTALLED → COMMISSIONED`. It requires the item's `hydraulic_status` to be `NOT_APPLICABLE` or `OK`; a `DEFICIT` item cannot be commissioned and the attempt is refused with `ERR_HYDRAULIC_DEFICIT_UNRESOLVED`. Decommissioning is the closing edge of that cycle, returning the item `COMMISSIONED → INSTALLED` with a supplied `reason` so it can be reworked, and it requires the parent order to be at `build_status` `IN_BUILD` or `BUILD_COMPLETE`. The pair may be traversed any number of times on the same item. Installing something that is not `PLANNED`, commissioning something that is not `INSTALLED`, decommissioning something that is not `COMMISSIONED`, or removing something that is not `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` is refused with `ERR_INSTALLATION_INVALID_STATE`.

Removal is the deepest cascade in the build workflow. An `InstallTechnician` may strip an item off from `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED`, supplying a `reason`, and the row lands at the terminal `REMOVED` with `allocated_flow_gpm` 0 and `hydraulic_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Removing a merely `PLANNED` item — the `PLANNED → REMOVED` edge — recomputes the order's `committed_pto_flow_gpm` and `committed_aux_amp_draw` and re-runs the flow waterfall, and stops there, because nothing was ever bolted to the truck. Removing an item that was physically present, on either the `INSTALLED → REMOVED` or the `COMMISSIONED → REMOVED` edge, runs a four-hop cascade, and both edges run the identical cascade. Hop one recomputes `committed_pto_flow_gpm` and `committed_aux_amp_draw` and, because the committed flow has fallen, re-runs the waterfall so that any sibling installation whose demand is now fully served flips `DEFICIT → OK`. Hop two, because the order's installed component set changed, transitions every weight certification of the order with `basis` `AS_MEASURED` and `status` `APPROVED` to `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `COMPONENT_SET_CHANGED`. Hop three, because that certification was voided, stamps `voided_at` on every compliance label of the order whose `voided_at` is still null and moves the order's `compliance_status` `LABELED → DESIGN_CERTIFIED`. Hop four, because the certification dimension has left `LABELED`, reverts an order sitting at `build_status` `BUILD_COMPLETE` back to `IN_BUILD` and reopens that order's most recently created `CLOSED` slot — the one with the highest `created_at`, in practice the final quality-control slot — from `CLOSED` back to `IN_BUILD`, so that the reversal is observable on the slot as well as on the order. The installation's `version` and the order's `version` both increment by 1, as do the versions of every certification, label and slot the cascade touched.

Installations can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, by `equipment_spec_id`, by `status` and by `hydraulic_status`, the last of which is the shop's deficit worklist.

### 3.5. Electrical Integration and the Chassis Warranty Posture

Tapping the vehicle's electrical architecture is the step most likely to cost the customer its chassis warranty, so the system treats every tap as a proposal that a second party must accept. An `UpfitEngineer` proposes an interface against an order by supplying `upfit_order_id`, the `interface_method` describing how the aftermarket control is joined to the vehicle network, the `circuit_amp_draw` in whole amps that the interface's circuits add to the continuous auxiliary load (at least 0), the `signal_source` naming where the interface reads vehicle signals from, and an optional `customer_warranty_waiver_ack` flag that defaults to false and is set true only when the customer has signed off on a warranty-voiding method. Proposals are accepted while the order's `build_status` is `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` — the same window as equipment planning — and refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` outside it. A new row is created at `PROPOSED`, with the proposer's `X-User-ID` recorded in `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` left null.

The interface's `warranty_impact` is computed at proposal time from exactly two inputs — the chosen `interface_method` and the governing chassis model's `electrical_bus` — and the mapping is exhaustive. A `BODY_BUILDER_MODULE` method yields `NONE` on both a `J1939_CAN` bus and a `DISCRETE` bus, because the module is the manufacturer's own sanctioned takeoff point. A `CAN_GATEWAY` method yields `CONDITIONAL` on a `J1939_CAN` bus, since reading the network through a gateway is tolerated but places conditions on any subsequent claim, and yields `NONE` on a `DISCRETE` bus, where there is no network to gateway into. A `DIRECT_SPLICE` method yields `VOIDS` on a `J1939_CAN` bus, because cutting into a multiplexed network is exactly what the chassis maker refuses to stand behind, and yields `CONDITIONAL` on a `DISCRETE` bus. A proposal whose computed `warranty_impact` is `VOIDS` is refused outright unless `customer_warranty_waiver_ack` is true, in which case the refusal carries `ERR_CHASSIS_WARRANTY_VOIDING_INTERFACE`.

The auxiliary electrical ceiling is the other gate at proposal time, and it is worth stating the load computation in full because three different operations consult it. The order's `committed_aux_amp_draw` is the sum of three terms: the body's own `aux_amp_draw`, which counts from the moment the order is created; plus the `aux_amp_draw` of the specification behind every equipment installation of the order whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED`; plus the `circuit_amp_draw` of every electrical interface of the order whose `status` is `APPROVED`. Interfaces sitting at `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing, and installations sitting at `REMOVED` contribute nothing. The result is a whole number of amps with no rounding. The ceiling test is evaluated with the candidate row counted in and accepts when the total is less than or equal to the governing chassis model's `max_aux_amp_draw`; a total strictly greater than that ceiling is refused with `ERR_AUX_ELECTRICAL_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. This same test guards three moments: proposing an interface, approving an interface, and planning an equipment item.

A `ComplianceOfficer` approves an interface, moving it `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, and this is the moment its amps and its warranty impact become binding on the truck. Approval requires the interface to be at `PROPOSED` — anything else yields `ERR_INTERFACE_INVALID_STATE` — requires the parent order's `build_status` to be `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD`, requires the auxiliary load computed with this interface counted in to remain within the model's `max_aux_amp_draw`, and requires the approver's `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `proposed_by_user_id`. On success the approver's identity is written to `approved_by_user_id`, the order's `committed_aux_amp_draw` is recomputed, and the order's `chassis_warranty_status` is re-derived. Approval also supersedes: every other electrical interface of the same order that is currently `APPROVED` and shares the same `signal_source` transitions `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, since one signal source can only be read one way, and `committed_aux_amp_draw` is then recomputed again without the superseded rows' `circuit_amp_draw`. A superseded row's own `version` increments by 1, as do the interface's and the order's.

Rejection is the simpler branch out of `PROPOSED`. A `ComplianceOfficer` may reject with a supplied `reason`, moving the interface `PROPOSED → REJECTED`; a rejected interface contributes no amps and no warranty impact, and its `version` increments by 1. Both `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal, and rejecting anything not at `PROPOSED` yields `ERR_INTERFACE_INVALID_STATE`.

The order's `chassis_warranty_status` is a pure derivation over the approved set, ranked with `INTACT` lowest, `CONDITIONAL` above it and `VOIDED` highest. When the order has no `APPROVED` interfaces at all, the posture is `INTACT` — and an empty approved set is the only thing that produces `INTACT` once any interface work has happened. Otherwise the posture is the worst impact present among the approved rows, where an impact of `NONE` maps to `INTACT`, `CONDITIONAL` maps to `CONDITIONAL`, and `VOIDS` maps to `VOIDED`. This single field is what later decides whether a chassis-side warranty claim can be shifted onto the upfitter. Interfaces can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, by `status`, by `warranty_impact` and by `interface_method`.

### 3.6. Binding a Truck to the Order and Releasing It to the Floor

Allocation is the moment an abstract order becomes a specific truck. A `ProductionScheduler` performs it by naming the `chassis_id` of the unit and the `expected_chassis_version` it last observed, and the version guard of §2.9 is evaluated before any of the business rules below. The unit must be at `INSPECTED` or `ALLOCATED`; any other chassis state, including `QUARANTINED`, `RECEIVED`, `IN_UPFIT`, `COMPLETED` and `RETURNED`, is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_INVALID_STATE`. The order must be at `build_status` `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK`, `RELEASED` or `CHASSIS_HOLD`. The unit's `chassis_model_id` must equal the `chassis_model_id` named by the order's build configuration, since the whole package was engineered against that model's book; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_MODEL_MISMATCH`.

Whether an already-bound unit may be taken is governed by the state of the order that currently holds it. The allocation is accepted when the unit's `allocated_upfit_order_id` is null, or already equals the requesting order, or when the incumbent order's `build_status` is `DRAFT`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `REWORK` or `RELEASED` — an incumbent in any of those four is still re-plannable, so pool-stock rebinding is legitimate. The allocation is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_ALREADY_ALLOCATED` when the incumbent order's `build_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_BUILD`, `BUILD_COMPLETE` or `DELIVERED`: a unit already committed to a bay window, or already built, cannot be pulled out from under it.

Successful allocation writes both sides of the binding and reconciles everything the new model governs. The unit's `allocated_upfit_order_id` is set to the order and the order's `chassis_id` is set to the unit, taking the chassis `INSPECTED → ALLOCATED`. When the order's `order_type` is `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC` the unit's `ownership` is set to `CUSTOMER_ASSIGNED`, since it is no longer pool stock. The order's `committed_pto_flow_gpm` is recomputed and the flow waterfall of §3.4 is re-run against the newly bound model, restamping `allocated_flow_gpm` and `hydraulic_status` on every candidate installation — this is the repair path by which pre-allocation `DEFICIT` rows become `OK`. The auxiliary electrical load is likewise re-evaluated against the newly bound model. An order that was sitting on hold moves `CHASSIS_HOLD → RELEASED`, because the reason for the hold has been resolved. Both the chassis's `version` and the order's `version` increment by 1. On the rebinding edge, where the unit stays at `ALLOCATED` but changes hands, the previously allocated order additionally has its `chassis_id` cleared to null and its `build_status` set to `CHASSIS_HOLD`, and that order's `version` increments too.

Before discussing release, the weight mathematics must be stated precisely, because they are the heart of this system and are evaluated in two distinct variants. Both variants answer the same question — how does the truck's weight divide between the two axles — by balancing moments about the axle pair, and both use the governing model's `wheelbase_in` as the denominator.

The computation begins by assembling an **inclusion set** of weighted items, each paired with its distance rearward of the front-axle centerline. When the body is part of the set, its distance is the order's `body_mount_offset_from_front_axle_in` plus the body's own `cg_from_body_front_in`. Each included equipment installation contributes its own weight at its own `mount_cg_from_front_axle_in`. The number of items assembled is recorded as `included_component_count` — one for the body when the body is included, plus one for each included installation. Two accumulators are then seeded with the curb weights of the bare incomplete vehicle, one for the front axle and one for the rear. For each item in the set, the share of that item's weight landing on the rear axle is the item's weight multiplied by its distance behind the front axle and divided by the wheelbase, and the share landing on the front axle is the item's whole weight minus that rear share. Both quantities are signed: an item mounted behind the rear axle produces a rear share exceeding its own weight and a negative front share, which correctly models a rear overhang lifting load off the steer axle. Contributions accumulate at full precision with no per-item rounding, and because the accumulation is a plain sum, the order in which items are processed cannot change the result. Only at the end is rounding applied, exactly twice: `front_axle_load_lb` is the front accumulator rounded half up to whole pounds and `rear_axle_load_lb` is the rear accumulator rounded half up to whole pounds. `gvw_lb` is then the sum of those two already-rounded integers — deliberately not a fresh rounding of the unrounded total.

What differs between the variants is which numbers feed that machine. Under the `AS_DESIGNED` basis the body is always included whether or not it is physically mounted, and its weight is the catalog `weight_lb` from the body specification; the included installations are those whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED`, each contributing its catalog `weight_lb` from its equipment specification; and the curb accumulators are seeded from the chassis model's book figures `curb_front_axle_lb` and `curb_rear_axle_lb`. This basis answers "what will this truck weigh when built as drawn", and it is the only basis the release safety gate consults. Under the `AS_MEASURED` basis the body is included only when the order's `body_install_status` is `INSTALLED`, and its weight is `body_actual_weight_lb` when that has been recorded and the catalog `weight_lb` otherwise; the included installations are only those whose `status` is `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED`, with `PLANNED` rows excluded entirely because nothing is on the truck yet, and each contributes its own `actual_weight_lb` when recorded and its catalog `weight_lb` otherwise; and the curb accumulators are seeded from the unit's scale ticket, `measured_front_axle_weight_lb` and `measured_rear_axle_weight_lb`. This basis answers "what does this truck weigh right now", and it is the only basis a compliance label may cite. Installations at `REMOVED` are excluded under both bases. For identical stored data the two bases return different `front_axle_load_lb`, `rear_axle_load_lb`, `gvw_lb` and `payload_capacity_lb`, and that divergence is the point.

Payload capacity is computed immediately afterward from the axle loads of the same basis, so it too is basis-specific. The cargo centroid's distance rearward of the front axle is the order's `body_mount_offset_from_front_axle_in` plus the body's `cargo_cg_from_body_front_in`. The fraction of any added payload that lands on the rear axle is that distance divided by the wheelbase, and the fraction landing on the front axle is one minus the rear fraction — a value that goes negative when the cargo centroid sits behind the rear axle. Three candidate ceilings are then considered. The first is gross-weight headroom: the model's `gvwr_lb` minus the computed `gvw_lb`. The second is rear-axle headroom: the model's `rear_gawr_lb` minus the computed `rear_axle_load_lb`, divided by the rear fraction. The third is front-axle headroom: the model's `front_gawr_lb` minus the computed `front_axle_load_lb`, divided by the front fraction — and this third candidate is included **only when the front fraction is strictly greater than zero**. When the front fraction is zero or negative, added payload unloads the steer axle rather than loading it, so no front-axle ceiling exists and the third candidate is omitted from consideration entirely rather than being treated as infinite or as zero. `payload_capacity_lb` is the smallest of the candidates actually considered, taken while all candidates are still unrounded, then rounded half up to whole pounds exactly once, and finally floored at zero so that an overloaded truck reports zero remaining capacity rather than a negative number.

Both computations are available for inspection without persisting anything. A read-only weight projection capability, open to `PlantAdmin`, `ProductionScheduler`, `UpfitEngineer` and `ComplianceOfficer`, takes a `basis` and returns exactly the numbers a certification of that basis would stamp, as an inline object containing `basis`, `front_axle_load_lb`, `rear_axle_load_lb`, `gvw_lb`, `payload_capacity_lb`, `included_component_count`, `front_gawr_lb`, `rear_gawr_lb` and `gvwr_lb`. It writes no entity and advances no state.

Moving the body along the frame is an `UpfitEngineer` capability that supplies a new `body_mount_offset_from_front_axle_in`. It is the single input that shifts the entire weight distribution, which is why it has a compliance consequence: the order's approved `AS_DESIGNED` certification transitions to `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `BODY_MOUNT_CHANGED`, and the certification dimension steps back `DESIGN_CERTIFIED → NOT_STARTED`. When the order's `build_status` is `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` the move also re-runs the invoice computation and restamps `invoice_total_cents`; outside those three states the invoice is left alone. The order's `version` increments by 1.

**Release** is the compliance gate of the whole production dimension, performed by a `ProductionScheduler` and taking the order `ENGINEERING_REVIEW → RELEASED`. Every one of the following must hold, and when several fail the report follows the precedence ladder of §2.6. The releasing `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `engineering_submitted_by_user_id`, so nobody releases their own package. The order's `chassis_id` must not be null and the bound unit must be at `ALLOCATED`. The `AS_DESIGNED` axle computation must satisfy three regulatory ceilings, checked in this order: `gvw_lb` at most the model's `gvwr_lb`, otherwise `ERR_GVWR_EXCEEDED`; `rear_axle_load_lb` at most the model's `rear_gawr_lb`, otherwise `ERR_REAR_GAWR_EXCEEDED`; and `front_axle_load_lb` at most the model's `front_gawr_lb`, otherwise `ERR_FRONT_GAWR_EXCEEDED` — a front-axle overrun being the classic signature of a heavy crane hung just behind the cab. The `AS_DESIGNED` `payload_capacity_lb` must be greater than or equal to the order's `required_payload_lb`; a strictly smaller capacity is refused with `ERR_PAYLOAD_SHORTFALL`. When `requested_in_service_date` is not null it must not fall before the earliest feasible date computed below, otherwise `ERR_IN_SERVICE_DATE_INFEASIBLE`; a null requested date skips the check entirely. The book fit rules are re-verified against the bound model, so a release also reports `ERR_CAB_TO_AXLE_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_WHEELBASE_INSUFFICIENT` if the pairing no longer holds. No installation of the order may carry `hydraulic_status` `DEFICIT`, otherwise `ERR_HYDRAULIC_DEFICIT_UNRESOLVED`. If the order's body has `requires_hydraulics` true, at least one installation at `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` must have a specification with `requires_pto` true, because a dump body needs a hoist to lift it; otherwise `ERR_HYDRAULIC_SOURCE_MISSING`. Every installation whose specification has `requires_can_gateway` true must be matched by an `APPROVED` electrical interface of the order whose `interface_method` is `CAN_GATEWAY` or `BODY_BUILDER_MODULE`, otherwise `ERR_CAN_GATEWAY_REQUIRED`. And on a `POOL_STOCK` order every installation's `equipment_spec_id` must be a member of the configuration's `standard_equipment_spec_ids`, otherwise `ERR_NON_STANDARD_POOL_EQUIPMENT`. Releasing an order that is not at `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` yields `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

The feasibility date the release gate compares against is built from body family and equipment count. The base build allowance is three weeks — 21 days — for a `SERVICE` body, 18 days for a `DUMP` body, 12 days for a `FLATBED`, and 25 days for a `VAN`, reflecting how much fabrication each family needs. Each hydraulic item on the order adds five days of integration work, and each non-hydraulic item adds two; both counts are taken over the order's installations at `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED`, split by whether the specification's `requires_pto` is true or false, so a truck with one crane and two liftgates carries five plus four additional days. The clock starts on the later of two dates: the calendar date of the current logical-clock instant in UTC, and the bound unit's `availability_date`. Adding the total day count to that start date gives the earliest feasible in-service date. Release accepts when `requested_in_service_date` is on or after that date; the boundary date itself is feasible. Because release already requires a bound unit, `availability_date` is always defined at this point.

Release also prices the build, stamping `invoice_total_cents` in four steps. The subtotal is the body's `list_price_cents`, plus the `list_price_cents` of the specification behind every installation at `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` — removed rows drop out, which is why stripping an item during the build changes the invoice on the next recompute — plus shop labor. Labor is budgeted by body family at 18 hours for a `SERVICE` body, 14 for a `DUMP`, 10 for a `FLATBED` and 22 for a `VAN`, billed at a shop rate of 115 dollars per hour, which the system holds as 11,500 cents per hour. Next, a standardization discount applies to `POOL_STOCK` orders only, at 8 percent when the configuration's `standardization_tier` is `TIER_A`, 4 percent when it is `TIER_B`, and nothing at all when it is `CUSTOM`; a `CUSTOMER_SPECIFIC` order receives no discount regardless of tier. The discount amount is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents exactly once, and the discounted subtotal is the subtotal minus that rounded discount. Third comes the final-stage certification fee, and the evaluation order here is pinned deliberately to resolve what would otherwise be circular: the fee tier is selected from the **discounted subtotal**, never from the pre-discount subtotal and never from the total that the fee itself is part of. A discounted subtotal below 20,000 dollars — that is, strictly less than 2,000,000 cents — draws a fee of 45,000 cents; a discounted subtotal below 40,000 dollars, strictly less than 4,000,000 cents, draws 72,500 cents; anything at or above that draws 98,000 cents. The comparisons are strictly-less-than against the exclusive upper bounds, so a discounted subtotal of exactly 2,000,000 cents lands in the middle tier. The invoice total is the discounted subtotal plus that fee, and every figure in the chain is integer cents.

### 3.7. Bay Capacity, the Waitlist, and Work on the Floor

Bay time is the plant's scarcest resource, and booking it is a `ProductionScheduler` task. A booking names the order through `upfit_order_id`, the bay through `work_bay_id`, the stage of work through `slot_type` — `BODY_MOUNT`, `EQUIPMENT_INTEGRATION` or `FINAL_QC` — and the window through `start_date` and `end_date`, both calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. An optional `queue_if_full` flag, defaulting to false, says what to do when the bay is already at capacity. Booking is permitted while the order's `build_status` is `RELEASED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD`, and one order commonly holds several slots — a body-mount window, an equipment-integration window and a final quality-control window. The system copies `chassis_id` onto the slot from the order's allocated unit at creation and never re-derives it afterward.

Two different overlap semantics apply to the same booking, and the difference between them is deliberate. The **chassis** is an exclusive resource evaluated over a **closed** interval: no other build slot holding the same `chassis_id` and sitting at `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` may have a window that touches or overlaps the requested one, where touching means the existing slot's `start_date` is on or before the new `end_date` and the new `start_date` is on or before the existing `end_date`. A shared endpoint date therefore collides, because one physical truck cannot be in two bays on the changeover day, and a collision is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. This rule is never queued around; `queue_if_full` has no effect on it. The **bay** is an aggregate resource evaluated over a **half-open** interval running from `start_date` up to but not including `end_date`: the count of slots in the same `work_bay_id` sitting at `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` whose window satisfies existing `start_date` strictly before the new `end_date` and new `start_date` strictly before the existing `end_date` must remain strictly less than that bay's `concurrent_capacity`. Adjacency does not collide — an existing slot whose `end_date` equals the new `start_date` is not counted. When the count reaches capacity, the request is refused with `ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` unless `queue_if_full` is true, in which case the slot is created at `QUEUED` instead of being rejected.

Two further checks apply to a booking. The bound unit's `availability_date` must be on or before the slot's `start_date`; a unit that will not be free until after the window opens yields `ERR_CHASSIS_NOT_AVAILABLE_BY_SLOT_START`. And the window must span at least one day: `end_date` must be strictly after `start_date`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_DATE_RANGE_INVALID`. Bay eligibility is checked as well — the order's `body_type` must appear in the bay's `supported_body_types` and the bay's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a body family the bay cannot mount, or a bay that has been taken `DOWN`, yields `ERR_BAY_BODY_TYPE_UNSUPPORTED`. A slot that lands at `SCHEDULED` on an order still sitting at `RELEASED` drives the order `RELEASED → SCHEDULED`; only the first slot to reach `SCHEDULED` moves the order, and a slot created at `QUEUED` does not move it at all.

When bay time frees up, the waitlist is drained by a strictly deterministic selector, and the same selector is used everywhere a window is released. Its candidate pool is every slot in the freed bay that is currently `QUEUED`, whose bay `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose own order is still at `build_status` `RELEASED`; slots belonging to orders that have gone to `CHASSIS_HOLD` or `CANCELLED` are skipped. Candidates are ranked by ascending `start_date`, and among candidates sharing a start date by ascending `created_at`; the row `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. The selector then walks that ranking and, for each candidate in turn, re-evaluates both overlap rules: the half-open bay count over the other slots in the bay at `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` must be strictly below the bay's `concurrent_capacity`, and no other slot at `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` may hold the same `chassis_id` over a closed interval that touches this candidate's window. The first candidate that satisfies both is promoted `QUEUED → SCHEDULED`, has `promoted_from_queue_at` stamped from the current logical-clock instant, and moves its own order `RELEASED → SCHEDULED`; the scan then stops. A candidate that fails either test is skipped and the scan continues with the next one. At most one slot is promoted per freed window even when several would fit, and when the candidate pool is empty the freeing operation simply completes with no promotion — that is a normal outcome, never an error.

An `InstallTechnician` starts work in the bay, taking the slot `SCHEDULED → IN_BUILD`. Starting requires the bound unit to be at `ALLOCATED` and the bay to be `ACTIVE`, and it moves two other rows: the unit goes `ALLOCATED → IN_UPFIT` and the order goes `SCHEDULED → IN_BUILD`. All three rows have their `version` incremented. Closing a slot takes it `IN_BUILD → CLOSED` and frees the bay window, so the promotion selector runs on that bay. Closing is also the bubble-up point for the production dimension: when, after this closure, the order holds zero slots at `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` and the order's `body_install_status` is `INSTALLED`, the order moves `IN_BUILD → BUILD_COMPLETE` — in practice this is the closure of the final quality-control window. The bound unit is deliberately left at `IN_UPFIT`, because custody does not pass to the customer until delivery. If any live slot remains, or if the body is not yet mounted, the order stays at `IN_BUILD`.

Cancelling a slot is a `ProductionScheduler` capability that supplies a `reason` and accepts a slot at `QUEUED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD`, taking it to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Cancelling a slot that was merely `QUEUED` releases nothing, so no promotion follows and the only effect is on that slot's own `version`. Cancelling a slot that was `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD` does release a window, so the promotion selector runs on that bay and may promote one waiting slot; additionally, when the order is then left with zero slots at `SCHEDULED` or `IN_BUILD`, the order steps back `SCHEDULED → RELEASED`, returning it to the pool of releasable-but-unscheduled work. Starting a slot that is not `SCHEDULED`, closing one that is not `IN_BUILD`, or cancelling one that is already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`.

One compensating edge runs the other way. As described in §3.4, stripping a physically present equipment item off a truck whose order had reached `BUILD_COMPLETE` reopens that order's most recently created `CLOSED` slot from `CLOSED` back to `IN_BUILD`, because the build is demonstrably no longer complete. Slots can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, `work_bay_id`, `chassis_id`, `slot_type` and `status`.

### 3.8. Weight Certification and Final-Stage Labeling

Certification is where the upfitter accepts legal responsibility for the finished vehicle, and the workflow is built around two rules: nobody certifies their own numbers, and a label may only reproduce numbers taken off a scale. An `UpfitEngineer` drafts a certification by naming the `upfit_order_id`, the `basis` to compute — `AS_DESIGNED` or `AS_MEASURED` — and the `expected_upfit_order_version` required by §2.9. The new row is created at `DRAFT` with the drafter's `X-User-ID` recorded in `created_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` null and `voided_reason` null, and the axle and payload computations of §3.6 run immediately for the requested basis, stamping `front_axle_load_lb`, `rear_axle_load_lb`, `gvw_lb`, `payload_capacity_lb` and `included_component_count`. The three regulatory ceilings are enforced here exactly as they are at release and in the same order — `ERR_GVWR_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_REAR_GAWR_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_FRONT_GAWR_EXCEEDED` — so a certification that would document an illegal truck is never persisted. The order's `version` increments by 1, which is precisely why the next draft needs a freshly read `expected_upfit_order_version`.

Drafting also supersedes. When a newer certification is created for the same order on the same `basis`, any earlier certification of that basis transitions to `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_DRAFT`, whether that earlier row was still `DRAFT` or had already been `APPROVED`. Its own `version` increments by 1, and when the voided row was `APPROVED` the compliance-dimension step-back described below follows.

A `ComplianceOfficer` approves a certification, taking it `DRAFT → APPROVED`, and the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `created_by_user_id`. Approving anything that is not `DRAFT` yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INVALID_STATE` — but only once the basis-dependent order precondition stated next has passed, because tier 9 of §2.6 ranks the order's own dimension ahead of the certification's; consequently an attempt to re-approve a certification that already stands `APPROVED` reports `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, since the order's `compliance_status` has already moved past the value that basis requires. Which order states permit approval depends on the basis, and this is what makes the certification dimension advance in exactly two steps. Approving an `AS_DESIGNED` certification requires the order's `compliance_status` to be `NOT_STARTED`, any other value being refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, and advances it `NOT_STARTED → DESIGN_CERTIFIED`; this happens regardless of where the production dimension sits, which is exactly what makes the two dimensions independent. Approving an `AS_MEASURED` certification requires the order's `compliance_status` to be `DESIGN_CERTIFIED` and additionally requires that something the upfitter added is actually bolted onto the truck — either the order's `body_install_status` is `INSTALLED`, or at least one of the order's equipment installations sits at `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` — because a measured basis is meaningless on a bare chassis; a violation of either condition is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Success advances the order `DESIGN_CERTIFIED → MEASURED_CERTIFIED`. In both cases the approver's identity is written to `approved_by_user_id`, and both the certification's `version` and the order's `version` increment by 1.

Voiding runs the dimension backward. Whenever an `APPROVED` certification is voided — with `voided_reason` drawn from `COMPONENT_SET_CHANGED` when the installed component set changed, `BODY_MOUNT_CHANGED` when the body was moved along the frame, or `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_DRAFT` when a newer certification of the same basis was drafted — the order's `compliance_status` steps back one level and any standing compliance label is voided with it. Concretely: voiding the approved `AS_MEASURED` certification while a label stands takes the order `LABELED → DESIGN_CERTIFIED` and stamps `voided_at` on every label of the order whose `voided_at` was still null; voiding it while no label stands takes the order `MEASURED_CERTIFIED → DESIGN_CERTIFIED`; and voiding the approved `AS_DESIGNED` certification takes the order `DESIGN_CERTIFIED → NOT_STARTED`. `VOIDED` is terminal for a certification — the path back is a new draft, not a revival. Certifications can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, by `basis` and by `status`.

Issuing the label is a `ComplianceOfficer` act and the terminal step of the certification dimension. The request names the `upfit_order_id`, the `weight_certification_id` whose numbers the label reproduces, and optionally a `supersedes_label_id` naming the label this one replaces. Issuance is accepted while the order's `compliance_status` is `MEASURED_CERTIFIED` or `LABELED`. A first issuance requires `MEASURED_CERTIFIED` and carries no `supersedes_label_id`, moving the order `MEASURED_CERTIFIED → LABELED`. A correction on an order already at `LABELED` must name the standing label — the one whose `voided_at` is null — in `supersedes_label_id`; the correction stamps `voided_at` on that predecessor from the current logical-clock instant, creates a replacement row with `label_type` and `responsible_party` recomputed, and leaves the order at `LABELED`, since a label row is immutable and a correction is a new row plus a voided predecessor rather than an edit.

Five refusals guard issuance, and they are evaluated in the precedence order of §2.6. A `supersedes_label_id` naming a label whose `voided_at` is already stamped is refused with `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_SUPERSEDED`. A cited certification whose `basis` is `AS_DESIGNED` is refused with `ERR_MEASURED_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, because a design-basis certification can never carry a final-stage label. A cited certification whose `upfit_order_id` differs from the label's own order is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_ORDER_MISMATCH`. A cited certification whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` rather than `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_APPROVED`. And an order that already holds a label with `voided_at` null, when no `supersedes_label_id` was supplied, is refused with `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_ACTIVE` — an order may hold at most one standing label. Issuance additionally requires that the issuer's `X-User-ID` differ from the `approved_by_user_id` recorded on the cited certification, so the officer who approved the numbers is not the officer who signs the plate.

Which label goes on the truck is decided by what the upfitter actually put on it, counting only equipment installations at `INSTALLED` or `COMMISSIONED` — `PLANNED` and `REMOVED` rows do not count. When the order's `body_install_status` is `INSTALLED` and that installed-equipment count is at least 1, the upfitter has completed the vehicle: `label_type` is `FINAL_STAGE` and `responsible_party` is `UPFITTER`. When the body is installed and the installed-equipment count is zero, the work amounts to a mere alteration of the incomplete vehicle: `label_type` is `ALTERER` and `responsible_party` is still `UPFITTER`. When the body is not installed at all — equipment bolted to a still-bodiless chassis — the vehicle remains incomplete and the incomplete vehicle document passes conformity certification back to the chassis maker: `label_type` is `INCOMPLETE_PASSTHROUGH` and `responsible_party` is `CHASSIS_MANUFACTURER`. All three outcomes are reachable precisely because the measured-basis approval gate accepts either a mounted body or at least one mounted equipment item. The label also snapshots the ratings it certifies: `certified_gvwr_lb`, `certified_front_gawr_lb` and `certified_rear_gawr_lb` are copied as integers from the governing chassis model's `gvwr_lb`, `front_gawr_lb` and `rear_gawr_lb` at the moment of issuance, and `certified_payload_lb` is copied from the cited certification's `payload_capacity_lb`. The issuer's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `issued_by_user_id`, `voided_at` starts null, and the order's `version` increments by 1. Labels can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, by `weight_certification_id`, by `supersedes_label_id`, by `label_type` and by `responsible_party`.

### 3.9. Handover and the Warranty Liability Ledger

Delivery is the one transition that requires both dimensions to be finished at once, and it is a `ProductionScheduler` act. The order's `build_status` must be `BUILD_COMPLETE`, its `compliance_status` must be `LABELED`, and the bound unit must still be at `IN_UPFIT`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. On success the order moves `BUILD_COMPLETE → DELIVERED` and the unit moves `IN_UPFIT → COMPLETED`, which is the terminal state on the custody dimension. Both rows have their `version` incremented. A truck that is physically finished but not yet labeled cannot leave the plant, and neither can a labeled truck whose final quality-control window is still open — this is the single point where the parallel model of §2.7 is forced back together.

After handover, failures in the field are recorded as warranty claims, and the interesting question is never whether the claim is valid but who pays for it. A claim is opened either by a `WarrantyAdministrator` or by the owning `Customer`, naming the `upfit_order_id`, a `claim_number` that must be unique across the whole plant — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_NUMBER` — the `failure_system` that failed, and the repair cost in `claim_cost_cents`, which must be an integer of at least 1. A `Customer` caller may only open a claim against an order whose `customer_id` matches their own `X-User-ID`. The new claim is created at `SUBMITTED` with `split_basis` `PROVISIONAL`, `taint_confirmed` false, `dispute_round` 0, and a provisional liability split already stamped so the parties can see an opening position. That provisional split is deliberately kept out of the order's ledger: a claim at `SUBMITTED` contributes nothing to the order's running liability totals.

The base split depends only on which system failed, and it apportions the cost in whole percentage points across three parties — the chassis manufacturer in `chassis_pct`, the upfitter in `upfitter_pct`, and a customer-goodwill share in `goodwill_pct`. Failures in the drivetrain and running gear sit with the chassis maker: `CHASSIS_ENGINE`, `CHASSIS_TRANSMISSION` and `CHASSIS_BRAKES` each split 95 to the chassis maker, 0 to the upfitter and 5 to goodwill. Chassis electrical failures are inherently shared, because the upfitter's auxiliary circuits ride on the same architecture: `CHASSIS_ELECTRICAL` splits 60 to the chassis maker, 35 to the upfitter and 5 to goodwill. Everything the upfitter built is the upfitter's: `BODY_STRUCTURE`, `BODY_ELECTRICAL` and `HYDRAULICS` each split 0 to the chassis maker, 95 to the upfitter and 5 to goodwill. The goodwill share is 5 points in every case.

A `WarrantyAdministrator` inspects the claim next, taking it `SUBMITTED → INSPECTED` — or `DISPUTED → INSPECTED` when a settled claim has been reopened — and recording the technical finding in `taint_confirmed`: true when the inspection concluded that the upfitter's integration work contributed to a nominally chassis-side failure, false otherwise. Inspection stores the finding and nothing else; it does not move money and does not touch the order's ledger.

Adjudication is where the split becomes binding. A `WarrantyAdministrator` takes the claim `INSPECTED → ADJUDICATED`, or `DISPUTED → ADJUDICATED` when re-settling a reopened claim, optionally passing `override_liability_cap`, which defaults to false. Adjudication sets `split_basis` to `FINAL` and restamps all six split fields, and the final computation differs from the provisional one in exactly one respect: a taint shift may move liability from the chassis maker to the upfitter. The shift applies only when all three of the following hold — `taint_confirmed` is true, `failure_system` is one of `CHASSIS_ENGINE`, `CHASSIS_TRANSMISSION`, `CHASSIS_BRAKES` or `CHASSIS_ELECTRICAL`, and the order's `chassis_warranty_status` is `CONDITIONAL` or `VOIDED`. When all three hold, the shift is 45 percentage points or the whole of `chassis_pct`, whichever is smaller, and that many points are subtracted from `chassis_pct` and added to `upfitter_pct`; `goodwill_pct` is never altered by the shift. Because the shift is capped at the surviving chassis share, a `CHASSIS_ENGINE` claim on a `VOIDED` posture reads 50 to the chassis maker, 45 to the upfitter and 5 to goodwill, while a `CHASSIS_ELECTRICAL` claim on the same posture reads 15, 80 and 5 — the electrical case shifts the full 45 from a starting share of 60. A body-side or hydraulic failure never shifts, because its `chassis_pct` is already 0 and the `failure_system` is outside the shift's scope; a confirmed taint on an `INTACT` chassis posture never shifts either, because a warranty that is still whole leaves the chassis maker on the hook for its own hardware. The provisional split stamped at creation always uses the base table with no shift, whatever `taint_confirmed` may later say.

Cents are apportioned from `claim_cost_cents` and the final percentages with exactly two roundings, performed in a pinned order so that the arithmetic is reproducible. First `chassis_liability_cents` is the cost multiplied by `chassis_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. Then `upfitter_liability_cents` is the cost multiplied by `upfitter_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. Finally `customer_goodwill_cents` is the plain remainder — the cost minus the two rounded figures — and is never rounded independently, which guarantees that the three components always sum exactly to `claim_cost_cents` with no stray cent created or destroyed.

Adjudication also refreshes the order's liability ledger, and that ledger is a derived total in the sense of §2.10: the order's `chassis_liability_cents`, `upfitter_liability_cents` and `customer_goodwill_cents` are each recomputed as the sum of the corresponding field over every claim on that order whose `status` is `ADJUDICATED` or `PAID`. Claims at `SUBMITTED`, `INSPECTED` or `DISPUTED` are excluded from the posted set. The order's `warranty_liability_cap_cents`, which defaults to 5,000,000 cents when the order is opened, is then checked against the recomputed upfitter figure alone: if the order's `upfitter_liability_cents` would exceed the cap, adjudication is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, unless the caller passed `override_liability_cap` as true, in which case the total is allowed to stand above the cap. The cap is checked at adjudication and nowhere else — never at creation, never at inspection, and never at payment — and it is compared against the recomputed order-level total rather than the individual claim.

Either a `WarrantyAdministrator` or the owning `Customer` may dispute a settled claim, taking it `ADJUDICATED → DISPUTED` with a `reason`; a `Customer` caller must own the order, matching `customer_id` against `X-User-ID`. Disputing increments the claim's `dispute_round` by 1 and removes the claim from the posted set, so the order's three ledger fields are recomputed downward without it — and consequently a dispute can bring an order that was over its cap back under it. The claim's own `chassis_liability_cents`, `upfitter_liability_cents`, `customer_goodwill_cents` and `split_basis` are deliberately left untouched while it sits at `DISPUTED`, so the settlement being argued about remains visible on the record; only the order-level rollup changes. A disputed claim can be inspected again or re-adjudicated, and each round through the loop raises `dispute_round` again. Paying a claim is a `WarrantyAdministrator` act taking it `ADJUDICATED → PAID`, which is terminal; because `PAID` is inside the posted set exactly as `ADJUDICATED` is, payment leaves the order's ledger totals unchanged. Inspecting, adjudicating, disputing or paying a claim from any state other than those listed is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Claims can be listed and filtered by `upfit_order_id`, by `failure_system`, by `split_basis` and by `status`.
