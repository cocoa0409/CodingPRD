# School Boundary Redistricting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Redrawing school attendance boundaries is the most politically exposed piece of work a school district ever undertakes, and it is almost always done in spreadsheets. A planner projects enrollment by hand from a housing pipeline that changes weekly, draws candidate maps in a GIS tool that knows nothing about program placement or bus ride times, and presents the board with utilization percentages nobody can reproduce a month later. Meanwhile guardians file comments that vanish into an inbox, siblings get split across schools because nobody encoded the sibling rule, and the transfer window closes with a stack of undecided requests still on a desk. When the board finally adopts a map, the district discovers that two overlapping adoptions now claim the same school years.

This document specifies the School Boundary Redistricting service, mounted under `/redistricting`. The service carries a boundary study from first setup through closure: it holds the school and planning-area inventory and the adjacency graph that defines which areas touch; it tracks the housing development pipeline and gates new development against each area's rolling absorption capacity; it runs enrollment projections under two deliberately different algorithm variants; it lets planners build candidate scenarios by assigning areas to schools and placing district programs, then scores each scenario on contiguity, capacity utilization, walkability, transportation cost and socioeconomic impact; it runs the community engagement cycle of comment intake, triage and scenario revision; it records the board vote, the phase-in and grandfathering rules that soften the transition, and the transfer requests families file against the new map; and it follows the adopted regime into post-implementation enrollment variance monitoring.

Three commitments shape every rule below. **Derived facts are computed, never supplied**: projections, utilization percentages, dissimilarity indices, seat pools, quorum outcomes and variance flags are all recomputed by the server from primary data, so a board member can always ask how a number was produced. **Ordering is pinned**: every cascade, every batch promotion and every projection loop walks its input set in a stated order, because the results differ if it does not. **Time is explicit**: the service reads a logical clock that test harnesses drive directly, and every date gate — the effective date of an adoption, a transfer deadline, a housing delivery window — is evaluated against that clock rather than against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Identity, Roles and Segregation of Duties

Every request to this service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present but the named role is not permitted to invoke the endpoint, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Both rules apply to every endpoint under `/redistricting` and to the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.5. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- **`DistrictPlanner`** — owns study setup, the school and planning-area inventory, the adjacency graph, the housing pipeline, enrollment projection runs, scenario construction, area assignments and program placement. A planner authors scenarios and submits them for review, but may never review, advance or adopt a scenario it authored.
- **`EngagementCoordinator`** — owns the community engagement cycle: opening comment intake, triaging and dismissing public comment, pushing a reviewed scenario back for revision, and certifying a reviewed scenario onto the board agenda.
- **`BoardMember`** — owns the board decision: recording the adopting vote, attaching grandfathering and phase-in rules to an adoption, pushing a board-ready scenario back for revision, and rescinding an adoption that is already in effect.
- **`TransferOfficer`** — owns implementation: activating the adopted regime, deciding transfer requests against each school's seat pool, closing the transfer window, recording post-implementation variance observations and closing monitoring.
- **`Guardian`** — a member of the public. Submits public comment while intake is open, files transfer requests, and reads the public-facing study, school, planning-area, scenario, comment, adoption, phase-in-rule and transfer records. A `Guardian` cannot read internal planning artifacts — projection runs, adjacency listings, housing pipelines, assignment tables and variance monitoring are all closed to that role.

All five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `DistrictPlanner`, `EngagementCoordinator`, `BoardMember`, `TransferOfficer` or `Guardian`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never resolves it as a foreign key or compares it to any row's `id`. The server does record it where a transition captures the acting operator: as `author_user_id` on a boundary scenario, as `guardian_user_id` on a public comment and on a transfer request, as `adopted_by_user_id` on an adoption record, and as `decided_by_user_id` on a decided transfer request. Those stored values are only ever compared for equality against a later request's `X-User-ID`. `X-User-ID` never influences ordering and never participates in a business calculation.

Two segregation-of-duties rules follow from those stored identities, and holding the correct role is necessary but never sufficient. First, the planner who authored a scenario is separated from every downstream judgement on it: an operator whose `X-User-ID` equals the scenario's `author_user_id` may not push that scenario back for revision and may not certify it for the board agenda — both are rejected with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403) — and may not record the board vote adopting it, nor rescind an adoption of it, which are rejected with `ERR_SELF_ADOPTION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Second, the operator who filed a transfer request is separated from its decision: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the request's `guardian_user_id`, the decision is rejected with `ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), even though the caller legitimately holds the `TransferOfficer` role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum effective value is 100 — a supplied `limit` above 100 is served as 100. `offset` is the zero-based index of the first record returned; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 200, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, the filter query parameters a specific endpoint declares are applied as equality predicates against the stored field, and the enum-membership check of §2.10 does not reach them: a filter value outside the filtered field's declared enum member list is not a schema failure, it simply matches no record, so the endpoint still answers HTTP 200 with an empty page. A filter parameter that is omitted or supplied empty is no filter at all and narrows nothing.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and Version Semantics

Every record the service persists carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix or other decoration — and a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits taken from the logical clock at creation time.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that is initialised to 1 at creation and incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record — including mutations the record's owner never requested, such as a recomputed roll-up total or a state change driven by a cascade from another entity. Boundary studies, schools, planning areas, housing developments, projection runs, boundary scenarios, area assignments, public comments, adoption records and transfer requests all behave this way.

Three record types are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`; for these, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely: **area adjacencies**, **phase-in rules** and **variance observations**. None of the three has an update endpoint.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this domain — the effective-date gate on activation, transfer-deadline expiry, the `data_locked_at` and `superseded_at` stamps, the projection horizon and the housing delivery windows — reads a single server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time. On a fresh server the clock stands at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never advances on its own between requests. The only way to move it is `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, a test-driving endpoint that accepts a `current_utc` instant, sets the clock to it, and answers with the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` value it replaced. The call creates and transitions nothing; it changes only what later calls read as "now". All five roles may drive the clock, and the two identity headers are still required.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Boundary Inclusivity

Wherever a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a tie rounds away from zero. The decimal scale depends on the quantity: monetary and percentage values are carried to 2 decimal places; the free/reduced-lunch dissimilarity index is carried to 4 decimal places; per-development student contributions are carried to 2 decimal places; and student counts are whole integers. Percent fields supplied on input must lie within 0 through 1000, and a value outside that range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The **order** of rounding is not uniform across the service, and the differences are deliberate. Enrollment projection rounds every per-development contribution to 2 decimal places before summing those contributions; transportation cost accumulates every per-assignment cost unrounded and rounds the scenario total exactly once at the end; the housing absorption calculation carries per-month delivery rates and window overlaps unrounded and rounds only the final peak. Each subsection of §3 states the order that governs its own calculation, and summing unrounded terms where the specification calls for rounded terms produces a different — and wrong — result.

Threshold comparisons are stated in §3 with their inclusivity spelled out, because most of them are inclusive on one side only. As a general habit of this service, capacity and tolerance bands include their lower edge, the critical utilization ceiling is reached rather than exceeded, walk-distance limits include the exact limit, grade bands include both endpoints, and the housing absorption cap and the supermajority trigger are the two comparisons that are strict.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations mutate a child collection whose meaning depends on the parent record the client last read, and each is guarded by optimistic concurrency against that parent's `version`. The guarding field is a required, non-optional member of the request body in all three cases; omitting it is a schema failure (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).

- `POST /redistricting/assignments` requires `expected_scenario_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `BoundaryScenario`. Creating an assignment legitimately supersedes any live assignment for the same scenario-and-area pair, so a second create carrying the version observed before the first would silently discard that supersede. If `expected_scenario_version` does not equal the scenario's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCENARIO_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created.
- `PUT /redistricting/scenarios/{id}/program-placements` requires `expected_scenario_version` on the same terms. The replacement overwrites the prior placement set wholesale, so a replace carrying a stale scenario `version` would erase placements the client never saw; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCENARIO_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is changed.
- `POST /redistricting/studies/{id}/projection-runs` requires `expected_study_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `BoundaryStudy`. A new run supersedes the study's prior run of the same variant, so a create carrying a stale study `version` is acting on an outdated picture of which run is authoritative; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Note that the two guarded scenario writes both increment the scenario's `version` on success, so a client issuing several assignment creates in sequence must re-read the scenario — or track the increment itself — between calls.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Single-Study Referential Integrity

Identifiers are resolved left to right and outside in. When a path names a parent and then a child, the parent is resolved first and its absence is reported before the child is looked at; when a request body names several references, the missing one reported is the first one that fails to resolve. Each entity type has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVELOPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADOPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`. A school id is resolved wherever it appears — in a path, in `current_school_id`, `assigned_school_id`, `requested_school_id` or `school_id`, and inside a program placement entry — and a planning-area id is resolved wherever it appears, including `neighbor_area_id`.

A boundary study is a closed world. Any request whose references straddle two studies is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422): a planning area whose school of record belongs to another study, two adjacency endpoints in different studies, a scenario whose projection run belongs to another study, an assignment whose area and school do not share the scenario's study, a program placement naming a school outside the scenario's study, an adoption whose scenario belongs to another study, and a variance observation naming a school outside the adoption's study.

### 2.9. Cascade Determinism and Processing Order

Several operations in this service touch many records. Where they do, the walk order is part of the specification and is always `created_at` ascending over the affected collection: planning areas are re-pointed in `PlanningArea.created_at` order, schools recompute their enrollment in `School.created_at` order, sibling scenarios are rejected in `BoundaryScenario.created_at` order, comments are addressed or dismissed in `PublicComment.created_at` order, projection runs are staled or superseded in `ProjectionRun.created_at` order, and transfer requests are revisited in `TransferRequest.created_at` order. Where a cascade recomputes a shared quantity, it does so **between** records rather than once at the end, so the second record in the walk sees the effect of the first.

Cascades are atomic with the operation that triggers them. If any link fails a validation, the whole request is rejected and no link is persisted — including the first one. Every record a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, so a client that pinned a `version` before a cascade ran will find that pin stale afterwards. That is the intended serialization point: the housing-pipeline cascade in §3.2 and the assignment concurrency guard in §2.7 are designed to interact exactly this way.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failing check wins:

1. **Identity** — missing or empty identity headers: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — the role is not permitted on this endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_SELF_ADOPTION_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN`.
4. **Existence** — every not-found code listed in §2.8.
5. **Schema** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a required input field absent or null, an enum value outside its declared member list, a positive-integer field (`total_units`, `buildout_months`, `regime_years`, `core_capacity_seats`, `board_member_count`) at zero or below, a negative vote count or negative `seats`, a `student_grade` outside 0 through 12, a `grade_low` greater than `grade_high`, a percent field outside 0 through 1000, a `limit` outside 1 through 200, a negative `offset`, an empty `travel_options` or `program_placements` array, a travel option entry missing `school_id`, `walk_distance_miles`, `bus_minutes` or `has_safe_route`, or a program placement entry missing `program_type`, `school_id` or `seats`.
6. **Study phase** — `ERR_STUDY_PHASE_INVALID`.
7. **Study readiness** — `ERR_STUDY_SETUP_INCOMPLETE` and `ERR_NO_SCENARIO_UNDER_REVIEW`.
8. **Seed-data lock** — `ERR_STUDY_DATA_LOCKED`.
9. **Entitlement stage reachability** — `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_STAGE_INVALID`.
10. **Scenario state** — `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EDITABLE`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_BOARD_READY`.
11. **Referenced-record state** — `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACTIVE` and `ERR_COMMENT_STATE_INVALID`.
12. **Adoption and transfer state** — `ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
13. **Projection freshness** — `ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`.
14. **Concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_SCENARIO_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION`.
15. **Temporal gates** — `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, then `ERR_INVALID_DATE_WINDOW`.
16. **Referential business rules** — `ERR_TRAVEL_OPTION_MISSING`, then `ERR_SCHOOL_LEVEL_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE`, `ERR_SELF_ADJACENCY` and `ERR_PROGRAM_HOST_INCAPABLE`, then `ERR_SIBLING_LINK_INVALID`.
17. **Student safety** — `ERR_RIDE_TIME_EXCEEDED`.
18. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADJACENCY` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`.
19. **Scenario completeness and structure** — `ERR_AREA_UNASSIGNED`, then `ERR_CONTIGUITY_BROKEN`, then `ERR_PROGRAM_PLACEMENT_INCOMPLETE` and `ERR_PROGRAM_HOST_UNASSIGNED`.
20. **Interval and cycle constraints** — `ERR_PHASE_IN_BAND_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_REGIME_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_AREA_ABSORPTION_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_NO_ELIGIBILITY_BASIS` and `ERR_UNADDRESSED_COMMENTS`.
21. **Capacity and fiscal gates** — `ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_CRITICAL`, then `ERR_NO_TRANSFER_SEATS`, then `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, then `ERR_MAJORITY_NOT_MET`.
22. **Computation preconditions** — `ERR_ZERO_PROJECTION_BASE`.

The consequence worth internalising is that a state failure always masks a business failure, a concurrency failure always masks a temporal or business failure, and a structural failure on a scenario always masks the capacity ceiling. A submission that both breaks contiguity and drives a school to critical utilization reports `ERR_CONTIGUITY_BROKEN`, never `ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_CRITICAL`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Study and Assembling the Seed Inventory

A redistricting effort begins when a `DistrictPlanner` opens a **boundary study**. The study is created with a `name` such as "2028 Elementary Boundary Study", the `district_name` of the owning district, and a `target_school_year` — the first school year the redrawn boundaries could apply to, which must be 2026 or later. The planner also sets the projection defaults and the policy band the study will be judged against: `horizon_years` is the default projection horizon in years, between 1 and 10 inclusive, defaulting to 5 when omitted; `capacity_target_low_pct` and `capacity_target_high_pct` bracket the district's capacity-utilization target band in percent, where the low bound must be greater than 0 and strictly less than the high bound, and the high bound must be strictly less than the critical ceiling of 110.00 percent. Both are carried to 2 decimal places. The boolean `portables_count_toward_capacity` decides whether relocatable classrooms are treated as real seats and defaults to false. Finally, `required_program_types` lists the district programs every scenario must place before it can be reviewed; its members are drawn from `DUAL_LANGUAGE`, `SPECIAL_ED_INTENSIVE` and `GIFTED_MAGNET`, and it defaults to an empty array.

A new study opens in `SETUP` with `comment_intake_open` false, `data_locked_at` null, `projection_stale` false, and both roll-up totals — `resident_student_total` and `enrolled_of_record_total` — at 0. The study's `status` is the master gate for the whole workflow: it advances `SETUP → DATA_LOCKED → SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT → ENGAGEMENT → BOARD_DECISION → IMPLEMENTATION → CLOSED`, with two cyclic reverts (`BOARD_DECISION → ENGAGEMENT` and `ENGAGEMENT → SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT`) and one compensating revert (`IMPLEMENTATION → BOARD_DECISION`) described later. `CLOSED` is terminal. Any operation attempted from the wrong phase is rejected with `ERR_STUDY_PHASE_INVALID`.

While the study is in `SETUP`, the planner builds its seed inventory. **Schools** are registered with a `name` that must be unique within the study, a `level` of `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` or `HIGH`, a `core_capacity_seats` count of at least 1, an optional `portable_capacity_seats` count of at least 0 defaulting to 0, an optional `program_types` array naming the programs the building can physically host (defaulting to empty), and an optional operational `status` of `ACTIVE` or `MOTHBALLED` that defaults to `ACTIVE`. The study is taken from the path, not the body. On creation the server computes `effective_capacity_seats` as `core_capacity_seats` plus, only when the study's `portables_count_toward_capacity` is true, `portable_capacity_seats`; otherwise the portable seats are ignored entirely. The derived `enrolled_students_of_record` starts at 0. A duplicate school name inside the study is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`. Schools may later be adjusted — capacity, hostable programs or operational status — and only fields present in that request change; omitted fields keep their stored values, and `effective_capacity_seats` is recomputed from the new seat counts and the study's portables flag. Adjustment is also the only route to `MOTHBALLED`: a mothballed school may not receive new area assignments, may not host a program placement and may not be the requested school of a transfer (all rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACTIVE`), and it drops out of the input set of both the utilization maximum and the contiguity evaluation.

**Planning areas** are the atoms of a boundary map. Each is registered against a `study_id` with an `area_code` unique within the study, a `grade_band` of `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` or `HIGH` naming the school level the area feeds, and a `current_school_id` identifying its school of record before any adoption takes effect. Its demographic profile is supplied at registration: `resident_student_count` (at least 0) is the students of record living there today, `frl_student_count` and `ell_student_count` count the residents qualifying for free or reduced lunch and those classified as English-language learners respectively — each at least 0 and no greater than `resident_student_count` — and `median_household_income_usd` records the area's median household income in whole US dollars, at least 0, as equity context for the board packet. The `annual_absorption_cap_units` value, at least 1 and defaulting to 250, is the ceiling on housing delivery described in §3.2. The `previous_school_id` field is initialised to null and is written only by the activation cascade in §3.8. Registering an area recomputes the study's `resident_student_total` as the sum of `resident_student_count` over the study's areas, recomputes `enrolled_students_of_record` on the school named as school of record, and then recomputes the study's `enrolled_of_record_total` from the study's schools. A duplicate `area_code` inside the study is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`.

Each planning area also carries its own travel table, `travel_options`, which is where walkability and ride time enter the system. The array must be non-empty, and each entry names a `school_id` together with `walk_distance_miles` (at least 0), `bus_minutes` (at least 1) and the boolean `has_safe_route`. Every referenced school must belong to the same study and its `level` must equal the area's `grade_band`, and the table must ultimately contain exactly one entry for each such school — completeness is what the seed lock verifies below.

The adjacency graph is declared separately. An **area adjacency** states that two planning areas share a physical boundary, naming `area_id`, `neighbor_area_id` and the `shared_boundary_length_ft` of the shared edge in feet (at least 1), which serves as the compactness weight of that edge. An area may not be declared adjacent to itself (`ERR_SELF_ADJACENCY`), both endpoints must live in the same study, and a pair already declared in either direction is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADJACENCY`. The row the client creates carries `is_reciprocal` false; in the same request the server mints a mirror row with the two endpoints swapped, the same `shared_boundary_length_ft` and `is_reciprocal` true, so that the graph is stored symmetrically and contiguity can be evaluated as an undirected traversal. Both rows are immutable, and adjacency listings for an area return every row on which that area appears as either endpoint, filterable by `is_reciprocal`.

Locking the seed data is the planner's declaration that the map's inputs are final, and it moves the study `SETUP → DATA_LOCKED`. It succeeds only from `SETUP` and only when the inventory is complete: the study must have at least 2 `ACTIVE` schools and at least 3 planning areas, every planning area must appear as the `area_id` of at least one adjacency row, and every planning area's `travel_options` must contain exactly one entry for each `ACTIVE` school of the study whose `level` equals that area's `grade_band` — no missing pair, no duplicate pair. Any shortfall is rejected with `ERR_STUDY_SETUP_INCOMPLETE`. On success the server stamps `data_locked_at` with the current logical-clock instant, recomputes `resident_student_total`, recomputes `enrolled_students_of_record` for every school of the study and then `enrolled_of_record_total`, and increments the study's `version`. From that moment the three seed-creation operations — adding a school to the study, registering a planning area and declaring an adjacency — are frozen and answer `ERR_STUDY_DATA_LOCKED`. School listings for a study are filterable by `level` and `status`; planning-area listings are filterable by `study_id`, `current_school_id`, `previous_school_id` and `grade_band`.

### 3.2. The Housing Pipeline and the Rolling Absorption Cap

Enrollment growth in this domain comes from housing, so each planning area carries a pipeline of **housing developments**. A development is added to an area with a `name`, a `unit_type` of `SINGLE_FAMILY`, `TOWNHOME` or `MULTI_FAMILY`, a `total_units` count of at least 1, a `first_occupancy_date` giving the ISO 8601 UTC instant of first expected occupancy, and a `buildout_months` count of at least 1 measuring the months from first occupancy to full buildout. The owning area is taken from the path. An optional `student_yield_override`, between 0 and 2 inclusive and carried to 4 decimal places, replaces the standard per-unit-type yield for that development alone; it defaults to null. A new development always begins at `entitlement_status` `PROPOSED`. Creating a development never stales an existing projection run — only advancing its entitlement stage does that.

Creation is gated by the area's **rolling absorption capacity**, which asks whether the area could physically absorb this much housing inside any 12-month window. The candidate set is every development already in the area whose `entitlement_status` is not `WITHDRAWN`, plus the development being created; a withdrawn development is excluded entirely, so withdrawing one frees absorption capacity for a later creation. Each candidate occupies a delivery window that begins at its `first_occupancy_date` and ends after `buildout_months` multiplied by 30 days, closed at the start and open at the end, and it delivers units evenly across that window at a monthly rate of `total_units` divided by `buildout_months`, carried unrounded. The server then probes one window per distinct candidate `first_occupancy_date`, examining those start instants in ascending date order with ties broken by creation order; each probe covers the 360 days — 12 thirty-day months — beginning at that start instant, again closed at the start and open at the end. For a given probe, the units delivered inside it are the sum, over every candidate, of that candidate's monthly rate multiplied by the length of the intersection between its delivery window and the probe, expressed in 30-day months and carried unrounded; two windows that merely touch, where one's end instant equals the other's start instant, intersect in zero length and contribute exactly nothing. The area's absorption peak is the largest of those probe totals, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the creation is rejected with `ERR_AREA_ABSORPTION_EXCEEDED` when that peak is strictly greater than the area's `annual_absorption_cap_units`. A peak exactly equal to the cap is accepted, and a rejected creation persists nothing.

Advancing a development through the pipeline is the single most disruptive operation in the service, because the entitlement stage is an input to enrollment projection. The advance names a `to_status`, which must be the immediate successor of the development's current stage along `PROPOSED → ENTITLED → PERMITTED → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → COMPLETE`, or `WITHDRAWN` from any non-terminal stage. Stages may not be skipped and may not be walked backwards, and a development already at `COMPLETE` or `WITHDRAWN` has no further moves; every other request is rejected with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_STAGE_INVALID`. Each of the four forward advances and each of the four withdrawal edges — from `PROPOSED`, `ENTITLED`, `PERMITTED` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` — behaves identically with respect to the cascade below; the only distinction is that a `WITHDRAWN` development thereafter contributes zero development students under both projection variants.

The advance fires a four-link cascade, and every link is part of the same atomic request. Link one sets `entitlement_status` to the requested stage and increments the development's `version`. Link two recognises that the projection inputs have changed: every projection run of the study that owns the development's area whose `status` is `COMPLETE` moves to `STALE` with `stale_reason` set to `DEVELOPMENT_PIPELINE_CHANGED`, walked in run creation order, and the study's `projection_stale` flag is recomputed to true. Link three follows the runs to the scenarios that cite them: every boundary scenario referencing one of those runs whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION` sets its own `projection_stale` to true, and each such scenario that was in `UNDER_REVIEW` additionally transitions `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION` with `revision_reason` set to `PROJECTION_STALE` and its `revision_round` incremented by 1, walked in scenario creation order. Link four notices the consequence for the study: if link three emptied the study's `UNDER_REVIEW` and `BOARD_READY` sets and the study is in `ENGAGEMENT`, the study transitions `ENGAGEMENT → SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT` and `comment_intake_open` is recomputed to false, because there is no longer a live scenario for the public to comment on. Housing pipeline listings for an area are filterable by `unit_type` and `entitlement_status`.

### 3.3. Enrollment Projection Runs and Their Two Variants

Once a study's data is locked, a `DistrictPlanner` executes **projection runs**. A run is created against a `study_id` with a `variant` of either `COHORT_SURVIVAL` or `DEVELOPMENT_WEIGHTED`, an optional `horizon_years` between 1 and 10 that defaults to the study's own `horizon_years` when omitted, and the mandatory `expected_study_version` described in §2.7. Runs may be created while the study is in `DATA_LOCKED`, `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT` or `ENGAGEMENT`. A successful run is stored with `status` `COMPLETE` and `stale_reason` null; any earlier run of the same study and the same `variant` whose `status` is `COMPLETE` or `STALE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, walked in run creation order. The study's `version` is incremented and its `projection_stale` flag recomputed, and if the study was in `DATA_LOCKED` it advances automatically to `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT` — the planner never posts that transition directly. Run listings are filterable by `variant` and `status`.

The run computes two outputs: `area_projections`, an array with one entry per planning area of the study ordered by area creation time, each entry carrying `area_id`, `base_students`, `development_students` and `projected_students`; and `projected_total_students`, the plain sum of every area's `projected_students`, which is never re-rounded. No status filter applies to areas — planning areas have no lifecycle, so every area of the study is projected.

The projection reads two instants. The **projection epoch** is the logical-clock instant at which the run is created, and the **horizon end** is that epoch advanced by `horizon_years` multiplied by 365 days, treating every year as exactly 365 days with no leap-day adjustment. Areas are then walked in creation order. An area's baseline is its `resident_student_count` multiplied by the cohort survival rate of 0.98 raised to the power of `horizon_years`, rounded half up to a whole student; that value is the entry's `base_students`. This is where the two variants diverge, and identical study data produces materially different totals under each.

Within an area, its developments are walked in creation order and each is either admitted or skipped according to the run's variant. Under `COHORT_SURVIVAL` — the conservative demographer view used for board-facing baselines — only developments whose `entitlement_status` is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` or `COMPLETE` are admitted; every other stage, including `WITHDRAWN`, is skipped, and each admitted development enters at full entitlement weight of 1.00 and full occupancy of 1.00 regardless of its `first_occupancy_date` or `buildout_months`. Under `DEVELOPMENT_WEIGHTED` — the planning-department view used to size capacity against the pipeline — every development except a `WITHDRAWN` one is admitted, but each is discounted twice. Its entitlement weight is 0.25 while `PROPOSED`, 0.60 while `ENTITLED`, 0.85 while `PERMITTED`, 1.00 while `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, 1.00 once `COMPLETE`, and 0.00 for `WITHDRAWN`. Its occupancy factor is the number of whole 30-day months from its `first_occupancy_date` to the horizon end — that elapsed span divided by 30 days and rounded down to a whole month — divided by its `buildout_months`, then clamped so that a negative result becomes 0.0 and a result above 1.0 becomes 1.0.

An admitted development's per-unit yield is its `student_yield_override` when that field is not null, and otherwise the standard yield for its `unit_type`: 0.42 students per single-family unit, 0.25 per townhome and 0.13 per multi-family unit. Its contribution is then its `total_units` multiplied by that yield, multiplied by its entitlement weight, multiplied by its occupancy factor — **and rounded half up to 2 decimal places before it is added to the running total**. The area's `development_students` is the sum of those already-rounded per-development contributions; summing the raw contributions and rounding the sum once is a different and incorrect result. The area's `projected_students` is `base_students` plus `development_students`, rounded half up to a whole student.

The practical consequence is a systematic gap between the two variants that the board is expected to see: identical study data yields a strictly larger `projected_total_students` under `DEVELOPMENT_WEIGHTED` whenever any `PROPOSED`, `ENTITLED` or `PERMITTED` development exists, and a strictly smaller one for an `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` development whose buildout extends past the horizon end, because the occupancy factor discounts it below the full weight `COHORT_SURVIVAL` would have granted.

### 3.4. Drafting a Boundary Scenario: Assignments and Program Placement

A **boundary scenario** is one candidate map. A `DistrictPlanner` creates it against a `study_id` while the study is in `DATA_LOCKED` or `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT`, naming the `projection_run_id` of the run whose per-area projections the scenario will be scored against, a `name` unique within the study, an initial `program_placements` array, and an `impact_variant` of either `RESIDENT_BASED` or `PROJECTED_BASED` that governs which equity view the impact analysis reports and defaults to `RESIDENT_BASED`. The run must belong to the same study as the scenario. On creation the scenario's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `revision_round` is 0, and `revision_reason` and `rejection_reason` are both null. The `author_user_id` is stamped from the `X-User-ID` header and is never accepted from the request body — it is the anchor of the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.1. The scenario's `projection_stale` flag is computed from the referenced run's `status`, true exactly when that run is `STALE`. Every objective score is initialised to its empty-scenario value: `contiguity_ok` true, `noncontiguous_school_ids` an empty array, `total_assigned_students` 0, `max_utilization_pct` 0.00, `walk_eligible_pct` 0.00, `transport_cost_annual_usd` 0.00, `students_reassigned_count` 0, `students_reassigned_pct` 0.00 and `frl_dissimilarity_index` 0.0000. A duplicate scenario name inside the study is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`. Scenario listings are filterable by `study_id`, `projection_run_id`, `status` and `impact_variant`.

A scenario's `status` walks `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, then cycles `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION → UNDER_REVIEW` as engagement proceeds, branches `UNDER_REVIEW → BOARD_READY`, can fall back `BOARD_READY → REVISION`, and terminates at either `ADOPTED` or `REJECTED`. A scenario's content — its assignments and its program placements — may be edited only while its `status` is `DRAFT` or `REVISION`; in any other state the content is frozen and an edit or a resubmission is rejected with `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EDITABLE`.

The map itself is built one **area assignment** at a time. An assignment names the `scenario_id` it belongs to, the `area_id` being assigned, the `assigned_school_id` receiving it, and the `expected_scenario_version` guard from §2.7. Assignments are never edited: re-assigning an area that already has a live assignment in the same scenario creates a new row and marks the previous one superseded, setting its `is_superseded` to true and stamping `superseded_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Every downstream calculation and constraint reads only the non-superseded rows, and a row whose `is_superseded` is true is invisible to scoring while remaining readable as history — assignment listings are filterable by `scenario_id`, `area_id`, `assigned_school_id` and `is_superseded`. At creation the server snapshots `projected_students` from the `area_projections` entry for this `area_id` on the scenario's projection run, taking that entry's `projected_students` value; the snapshot is what every later score uses, so it does not drift when a newer run is created. The scenario's `version` is incremented, but no objective score is recomputed here.

Four business rules guard an assignment beyond the state and concurrency checks. The area and the school must belong to the scenario's study (`ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE`). The school must not be `MOTHBALLED` (`ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACTIVE`). The area's `travel_options` must contain an entry whose `school_id` is the assigned school, because without it walkability and ride time for the pair are unknown; a missing entry is rejected with `ERR_TRAVEL_OPTION_MISSING`. The school's `level` must equal the area's `grade_band`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_LEVEL_MISMATCH`. Finally, student safety caps the commute: when the matching travel option is **not** walk-eligible, its `bus_minutes` may not exceed the maximum ride time for the school's level — 45 minutes for `ELEMENTARY`, 55 minutes for `MIDDLE` and 60 minutes for `HIGH` — and a longer ride is rejected with `ERR_RIDE_TIME_EXCEEDED`. A walk-eligible pair is exempt from the ride-time cap, since no student would board a bus.

District programs are placed separately, and the placement set is replaced wholesale rather than edited entry by entry: a replacement carries the full `program_placements` array plus `expected_scenario_version`, and every entry absent from the request body is dropped. Each entry names a `program_type` drawn from `DUAL_LANGUAGE`, `SPECIAL_ED_INTENSIVE` and `GIFTED_MAGNET`, a `school_id` and a `seats` count. The named school must belong to the scenario's study (`ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE`), must not be `MOTHBALLED` (`ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACTIVE`), and must be physically able to host the program — that is, the program type must appear in the school's `program_types` array, or the replacement is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_HOST_INCAPABLE`. A successful replacement increments the scenario's `version`; it deliberately does not recompute `max_utilization_pct`, even though program seats feed that number, because utilization is recomputed only when the scenario is submitted for review.

### 3.5. Scoring a Scenario Against Every Objective

Submitting a scenario for review is the moment the map is judged. A `DistrictPlanner` submits it, optionally attaching `planner_notes`, and the transition is either `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION → UNDER_REVIEW`. Either way the study must be in `DATA_LOCKED`, `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT` or `ENGAGEMENT`, the scenario must be in `DRAFT` or `REVISION` (`ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EDITABLE`), and the projection run it cites must still be `COMPLETE` — a scenario whose `projection_stale` is true, whether because its run went `STALE` or because a newer run superseded it, cannot have its scores certified and is rejected with `ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`. When the scenario is returning from `REVISION`, its `revision_reason` is cleared back to null and its `revision_round` is left unchanged, because that counter was already incremented when the scenario entered `REVISION`.

Four calculations run before the structural gates are evaluated, because the gates read their results — but the results are persisted **only if the transition itself succeeds**. A rejected submission, at any precedence level, leaves `status`, `revision_round`, `version` and every stored score field byte-identical to their pre-call values. The freshly computed numbers are observable in a rejected case only through the scenario's impact analysis, a computed read that recomputes on demand under the scenario's stored `impact_variant` and never writes anything. That read answers with the `scenario_id`, the `impact_variant` in force, `total_assigned_students`, `frl_dissimilarity_index`, `students_reassigned_count`, `students_reassigned_pct`, `max_utilization_pct`, `critical_school_ids` (the `ACTIVE` schools at or above the critical ceiling, ordered by school creation time), `walk_eligible_pct`, `transport_cost_annual_usd`, `contiguity_ok`, `noncontiguous_school_ids`, and a `per_school` breakdown giving each `ACTIVE` school's utilization and the band it falls in.

**Contiguity.** The study's planning areas form an undirected graph whose edges are the adjacency rows, both the client-created row and the server-minted reciprocal, so an edge exists between two areas whenever an adjacency row names one as `area_id` and the other as `neighbor_area_id`. For each `ACTIVE` school of the study, the server collects the set of areas assigned to it by the scenario's non-superseded assignments. A school with zero assigned areas is vacuously contiguous and never reported. Otherwise the traversal starts at the member of that set with the earliest `created_at` and walks the graph breadth-first, following an edge only when **both** of its endpoints are members of that school's own set; the school is contiguous exactly when the traversal reaches every member of the set. `contiguity_ok` is true when every `ACTIVE` school of the study is contiguous, and `noncontiguous_school_ids` lists the ids of the schools that are not, ordered by school creation time. Entity ids never influence the traversal or the ordering. A submission whose evaluation reports `contiguity_ok` false is rejected with `ERR_CONTIGUITY_BROKEN`.

**Capacity utilization.** A school's effective capacity is its `core_capacity_seats` plus, only when the study's `portables_count_toward_capacity` is true, its `portable_capacity_seats`. Its load under a scenario is the sum of `projected_students` over the scenario's non-superseded assignments pointing at it, plus the sum of `seats` over the scenario's `program_placements` entries naming it. Utilization in percent is 100 multiplied by that load, divided by effective capacity, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The band a school falls in is `UNDER` when utilization is strictly below the study's `capacity_target_low_pct`, `IN_TARGET` when it is at or above `capacity_target_low_pct` and at or below `capacity_target_high_pct`, `OVER` when it is strictly above `capacity_target_high_pct` and strictly below the critical ceiling of 110.00 percent, and `CRITICAL` when it reaches or exceeds 110.00 percent — a school at exactly 110.00 is `CRITICAL`, one at 109.99 is `OVER`. The scenario's `total_assigned_students` is the sum of `projected_students` over all its non-superseded assignments, and `max_utilization_pct` is the largest utilization across the study's `ACTIVE` schools, or 0.00 when the study has no `ACTIVE` school. `MOTHBALLED` schools never contribute to the maximum. A submission whose `max_utilization_pct` reaches or exceeds 110.00 is rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_CRITICAL`.

**Transportation cost and walkability.** The annual transportation bill is accumulated over the scenario's non-superseded assignments in assignment creation order. For each one the server takes the area, the assigned school, the single `travel_options` entry whose `school_id` is that school, and the assignment's `projected_students` as the head count. The pair is walk-eligible when the entry's `walk_distance_miles` is less than or equal to the walk limit for the school's `level` — 1.0 mile for `ELEMENTARY`, 1.5 miles for `MIDDLE`, 2.0 miles for `HIGH` — **and** its `has_safe_route` is true; a distance exactly equal to the limit is walk-eligible. Walk-eligible head counts accumulate into the walking population and generate no riders; otherwise the whole head count rides. The billed ride length is the greater of the entry's `bus_minutes` and a 15-minute minimum. The bus count is the rider count divided by a bus capacity of 71 seats, rounded **up** to a whole bus, so zero riders means zero buses. That assignment's cost is the bus count multiplied by the sum of a daily base of 95.00 US dollars and 1.85 US dollars per billed minute, multiplied by 180 school days per year. Those per-assignment costs are accumulated **unrounded**, and only the accumulated scenario total is rounded half up to 2 decimal places, exactly once, to give `transport_cost_annual_usd`. This is deliberately the opposite rounding order from enrollment projection. Alongside it, `walk_eligible_pct` is 100 multiplied by the walking population divided by `total_assigned_students`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and is 0.00 when `total_assigned_students` is 0.

**Socioeconomic impact.** The equity measure is a free/reduced-lunch dissimilarity index computed under the scenario's `impact_variant`. Two running tallies — one of FRL students, one of non-FRL students — are kept per `ACTIVE` school of the study, and the scenario's non-superseded assignments are walked in creation order, each contributing its area's split to its assigned school. Under `RESIDENT_BASED`, the view the board sees at first reading, the split is today's head count unchanged: the area's `frl_student_count` as the FRL part and its `resident_student_count` minus its `frl_student_count` as the non-FRL part, and the housing pipeline is ignored entirely. Under `PROJECTED_BASED`, the view used for the final adoption packet, the split is scaled to the projected horizon. The server reads the area's `area_projections` entry on the scenario's run and computes two rates: the area's resident FRL rate, its `frl_student_count` divided by its `resident_student_count`, taken as 0.0 when the resident count is 0; and a development FRL rate, being the sum over the area's admitted developments of that development's already-rounded 2-decimal contribution multiplied by the FRL rate for its `unit_type` — 0.18 for `SINGLE_FAMILY`, 0.32 for `TOWNHOME`, 0.54 for `MULTI_FAMILY` — divided by the entry's `development_students`, taken as 0.0 when `development_students` is 0. The admitted set and the per-development rounded contributions are inherited verbatim from the run's own projection variant, which is why a `COHORT_SURVIVAL` run and a `DEVELOPMENT_WEIGHTED` run yield different indices for identical assignments. The FRL part is then the entry's `base_students` multiplied by the resident FRL rate plus its `development_students` multiplied by the development FRL rate, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the non-FRL part is the entry's `projected_students` minus that FRL part, likewise rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Both parts are rounded per assignment before they are accumulated.

Once every assignment has been folded in, the district FRL total and the district non-FRL total are the sums of the per-school tallies. If either total is zero, `frl_dissimilarity_index` is 0.0000. Otherwise it is one half of the sum, over the `ACTIVE` schools, of the absolute difference between that school's share of the district FRL population and its share of the district non-FRL population, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Two further numbers come out of the same walk: `students_reassigned_count` is the sum of `projected_students` over the non-superseded assignments whose `assigned_school_id` differs from the area's `current_school_id`, and `students_reassigned_pct` is 100 multiplied by that count divided by `total_assigned_students`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places and taken as 0.00 when `total_assigned_students` is 0. That percentage later decides whether the board needs a supermajority.

Three structural gates complete the submission, and their relative order matters because each masks the ones below it. First, the scenario must partition the study exactly: every planning area of the study must have exactly one non-superseded assignment in this scenario — none unassigned, none assigned twice — or the submission is rejected with `ERR_AREA_UNASSIGNED`. Then contiguity is checked (`ERR_CONTIGUITY_BROKEN`). Then program placement is checked in two ways: every member of the study's `required_program_types` must appear exactly once across the scenario's `program_placements`, and appearing zero times or more than once is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_PLACEMENT_INCOMPLETE`; and every school named by a placement must itself have at least one non-superseded assignment in the scenario, since a host school with no attendance area has no students to draw from, which is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_HOST_UNASSIGNED`. Only then is the capacity ceiling applied (`ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_CRITICAL`). On success the scenario's `status` becomes `UNDER_REVIEW`, all four calculations are persisted, and its `version` is incremented.

### 3.6. Community Engagement: Comment Intake, Triage and Revision Cycles

Public engagement opens on the study, not on a scenario. An `EngagementCoordinator` opens it, moving the study `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT → ENGAGEMENT`, and the operation succeeds only from `SCENARIO_DEVELOPMENT` and only when at least one scenario of the study is in `UNDER_REVIEW` — with nothing under review there is nothing for the public to react to, and the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_SCENARIO_UNDER_REVIEW`. Opening engagement recomputes the study's `comment_intake_open` to true and increments the study's `version`. That single boolean is what admits public comment: `comment_intake_open` is true exactly while the study's `status` is `ENGAGEMENT`, and a comment submitted at any other time is rejected with `ERR_STUDY_PHASE_INVALID`.

A `Guardian` submits a **public comment** naming the `scenario_id` being commented on, an optional `area_id` giving the commenter's residence area (null when not stated), and a `body` of comment text between 1 and 4000 characters. The `guardian_user_id` is stamped from the `X-User-ID` header and never accepted from the body. A new comment sits in `SUBMITTED` with `theme`, `priority` and `dismissal_reason` all null. Comment listings are filterable by `scenario_id`, `area_id`, `status`, `theme` and `priority`.

Triage is the coordinator's first pass. Triaging a comment moves it `SUBMITTED → TRIAGED` and stores a `theme` — one of `TRANSPORTATION`, `SIBLING_SPLIT`, `EQUITY`, `CAPACITY`, `PROGRAM_ACCESS` or `OTHER` — together with a `priority` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, both taken from the request body. Triage requires the comment to be in `SUBMITTED` and the commented scenario to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `BOARD_READY`; otherwise it is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A coordinator may instead dismiss a comment outright, with optional `dismissal_notes`: dismissal is permitted from either `SUBMITTED` or `TRIAGED`, moving the comment `SUBMITTED → DISMISSED` or `TRIAGED → DISMISSED`, and records `dismissal_reason` as `OFFICER_DISMISSAL`. Both `ADDRESSED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal for a comment, and any operation attempted from a state that does not admit it is rejected with `ERR_COMMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Pushing a scenario back for revision is how comment turns into a map change, and it is the trigger of a three-link cascade. The request carries a non-empty `cited_comment_ids` list and optional `revision_notes`; every cited id must resolve to a comment (`ERR_COMMENT_NOT_FOUND`) that belongs to the target scenario and is currently in `TRIAGED` (`ERR_COMMENT_STATE_INVALID`). Two source states are accepted and each has its own actor: an `EngagementCoordinator` pushes back a scenario in `UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires the study to be in `ENGAGEMENT` or `BOARD_DECISION`; a `BoardMember` pushes back a scenario in `BOARD_READY`. A scenario in any other state is rejected with `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, and the acting operator may not be the scenario's author (`ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`). Link one moves the scenario `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION` or `BOARD_READY → REVISION`, sets `revision_reason` to `COMMENT_CYCLE`, increments `revision_round` by 1 and increments the scenario's `version`. Link two moves every comment named in `cited_comment_ids` `TRIAGED → ADDRESSED`, walked in comment creation order, incrementing each comment's `version`. Link three reacts to the study: when the scenario left `BOARD_READY` and no scenario of the study remains in `BOARD_READY`, the study reverts `BOARD_DECISION → ENGAGEMENT` and `comment_intake_open` is recomputed to true, reopening intake for another round.

The revision loop is bounded. `revision_round` counts the completed passes through `REVISION`, and the maximum is 3: a scenario whose `revision_round` has already reached 3 may not be pushed back again, and the request is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; the same limit blocks a further resubmission of such a scenario. Note that the counter is also advanced by the housing-pipeline cascade of §3.2, which pushes an `UNDER_REVIEW` scenario into `REVISION` with `revision_reason` set to `PROJECTION_STALE` rather than `COMMENT_CYCLE` — a district with a volatile pipeline can therefore burn its revision budget without a single public comment.

Certifying a scenario onto the board agenda moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → BOARD_READY` and is reserved to an `EngagementCoordinator`, who may attach `agenda_notes`. It requires the study to be in `ENGAGEMENT`, the scenario to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` (`ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_REVIEWABLE`), the scenario's `projection_stale` to be false (`ERR_PROJECTION_STALE`), and the public record to be clean: no comment of that scenario may still be in `SUBMITTED`, since every comment must be triaged or dismissed before the board reads the map, and an untriaged comment is rejected with `ERR_UNADDRESSED_COMMENTS`. The coordinator must not be the scenario's author (`ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`). On success the owning study moves `ENGAGEMENT → BOARD_DECISION` and `comment_intake_open` is recomputed to false, closing intake for the reading.

### 3.7. The Board Decision: Vote Arithmetic, Regime Windows and Adoption

An **adoption record** is the minute of the board vote. A `BoardMember` records it against a `study_id` and the `scenario_id` being adopted, supplying the `effective_date` on or after which the new boundaries may be switched on, the `effective_school_year` that begins the boundary regime, an optional `regime_years` length defaulting to 3 school years, a `transfer_deadline_date`, an optional `transfer_cap_pct` defaulting to 95.0, an optional `variance_tolerance_pct` defaulting to 3.0, the three tallies `votes_for`, `votes_against` and an optional `votes_abstain` defaulting to 0, and the `board_member_count` of seated members. The study must be in `BOARD_DECISION` and the scenario must be in `BOARD_READY`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_BOARD_READY`; the scenario must belong to the named study (`ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE`); and the adopting member must not be the scenario's author (`ERR_SELF_ADOPTION_FORBIDDEN`). The date window must be ordered — `transfer_deadline_date` strictly after `effective_date`, and `effective_school_year` not below the study's `target_school_year` — or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_WINDOW`. The `adopted_by_user_id` is stamped from the `X-User-ID` header.

**Vote arithmetic.** The cast vote count is `votes_for` plus `votes_against` plus `votes_abstain`. The quorum threshold is `board_member_count` divided by two, rounded **up** to a whole member, and quorum is met when the cast count reaches or exceeds that threshold — abstentions **do** count toward quorum. A failed quorum is rejected with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. The `supermajority_required` flag is then computed: it is true exactly when the scenario's `students_reassigned_pct`, as recomputed at its most recent submission for review, is **strictly greater than** 15.00 percent, so a scenario at exactly 15.00 percent needs only a simple majority. When `supermajority_required` is false, the majority is met when twice `votes_for` is strictly greater than the sum of `votes_for` and `votes_against`. When it is true, the majority is met when three times `votes_for` is greater than or equal to twice the sum of `votes_for` and `votes_against`, which is the two-thirds threshold. Abstentions are excluded from the majority denominator in both branches, so they help reach quorum and then vanish from the decision. Both thresholds are stated as whole-number comparisons precisely so that no floating-point rounding can flip an outcome. A failed majority is rejected with `ERR_MAJORITY_NOT_MET`.

**The regime window.** A district may not run two overlapping boundary regimes, nor two regimes that crowd each other. Every adoption occupies, across the whole district, the half-open school-year interval that begins at its `effective_school_year` and ends before its `effective_school_year` plus `regime_years`. The candidate is compared against every existing adoption whose owning study has the same `district_name` as the candidate's study and whose `implementation_status` is not `RESCINDED`. The candidate is rejected with `ERR_REGIME_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when the two intervals intersect — that is, when the candidate begins before the existing interval ends and the existing interval begins before the candidate's ends — **or** when the intervals are disjoint but the gap between them is smaller than the 2-school-year quiet-period buffer, meaning the later interval begins fewer than 2 school years after the earlier one ends. Exactly abutting regimes, with a gap of zero, are therefore rejected, and a one-school-year gap is rejected too; the first legal gap is 2 school years. Because the comparison keys on `district_name` rather than on `study_id`, the reachable conflict is a second study of the same district trying to claim adjacent school years. Note that this is deliberately different interval arithmetic from the rolling absorption cap of §3.2, which lets windows touch and only objects to an aggregate peak. A rescinded adoption releases its interval, so a replacement adoption may reuse exactly the same school years. All of this arithmetic is on whole school years.

Creating the adoption is a three-link cascade, applied atomically. Link one moves the named scenario `BOARD_READY → ADOPTED`. Link two rejects the field: every sibling scenario of the same study still in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION` or `BOARD_READY` moves to `REJECTED` — `DRAFT → REJECTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, `REVISION → REJECTED` and `BOARD_READY → REJECTED` are all reachable only this way — with `rejection_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_ADOPTION`, walked in scenario creation order. Link three closes the public record on those losers: every comment attached to a scenario rejected by link two that is still in `SUBMITTED` or `TRIAGED` moves to `DISMISSED` with `dismissal_reason` set to `SCENARIO_REJECTED`, walked in comment creation order. Comments already `ADDRESSED` or `DISMISSED` are untouched, and comments on the adopted scenario are untouched. Finally the owning study moves `BOARD_DECISION → IMPLEMENTATION`. The new record starts with `implementation_status` `PENDING_EFFECTIVE` and `transfer_window_status` `TRANSFER_WINDOW_OPEN`, and those two dimensions move independently from that moment on — the window may close while the adoption is still `PENDING_EFFECTIVE`, and the adoption may reach `IN_EFFECT` with the window still `TRANSFER_WINDOW_OPEN`. `MONITORING_CLOSED` and `RESCINDED` are the terminal values of the implementation dimension.

**Phase-in rules.** While the adoption is still `PENDING_EFFECTIVE`, a `BoardMember` attaches the grandfathering and phase-in entitlements that soften the transition. Each rule carries a `rule_type` of `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER`, `SIBLING_LINK` or `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND`, a closed grade band from `grade_low` to `grade_high` with both endpoints inclusive and both within grades 0 through 12, and a `sunset_school_year` that is the last school year in which the rule may be invoked. A `grade_low` above `grade_high` is a malformed request (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and a `sunset_school_year` below the adoption's `effective_school_year` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_WINDOW`. The rule's `consumes_seat_pool` is computed rather than supplied: false for `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER` and `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND`, which are entitlements that bypass the seat pool entirely, and true for `SIBLING_LINK`. Bands may not overlap **within the same rule type**: a candidate is rejected with `ERR_PHASE_IN_BAND_OVERLAP` when an existing rule of the same adoption and the same `rule_type` has a band that intersects it, which is the case when the existing `grade_low` is at or below the candidate `grade_high` and the candidate `grade_low` is at or below the existing `grade_high`. Bands that merely abut, where the existing `grade_high` plus one equals the candidate `grade_low`, are accepted. Bands of *different* rule types may overlap freely, and that overlap is exactly what makes the eligibility waterfall's ordering observable. An attempt to attach a rule to an adoption that is no longer `PENDING_EFFECTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`. Rules are immutable once written and carry only an `id` and a `created_at`. Rule listings are filterable by `rule_type`.

### 3.8. Implementation: Activation, Transfers and the Seat Pool

**Activation.** A `TransferOfficer` switches the adopted boundaries on. Activation requires `implementation_status` to be `PENDING_EFFECTIVE` (`ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`), the adopted scenario to be in `ADOPTED`, and the logical clock to have reached the `effective_date` — an attempt while the clock is strictly before that instant is rejected with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, and an attempt at exactly that instant succeeds. Activation is a four-link cascade, applied atomically. Link one moves `implementation_status` `PENDING_EFFECTIVE → IN_EFFECT` and increments the adoption's `version`. Link two rewrites the map of record: for every planning area of the study, walked in area creation order, `previous_school_id` is set to the area's outgoing `current_school_id` and then `current_school_id` is set to the `assigned_school_id` of that area's non-superseded assignment under the adopted scenario. Link three recomputes the head counts that link two invalidated: every school of the study recomputes `enrolled_students_of_record` as the sum of `resident_student_count` over the planning areas now pointing at it, walked in school creation order, and then the study's `enrolled_of_record_total` is recomputed as the sum over its schools. Link four revisits the waitlist, because the new head counts changed every seat pool: every `WAITLISTED` transfer request of this adoption is reconsidered in request creation order, and each one whose requested school now has at least one free transfer seat moves `WAITLISTED → APPROVED` with `promotion_source` set to `WAITLIST_AUTO` and `decided_by_user_id` left null, the pool being recomputed after each promotion before the next candidate is considered. Because of that recomputation, an earlier candidate can consume the last seat and leave a later one waitlisted, which is why the creation-order walk is normative rather than incidental.

**Filing a transfer request.** A `Guardian` files a request against an `adoption_id`, naming the `area_id` of the student's residence, the `requested_school_id`, a `student_reference` unique within the adoption, a `student_grade` between 0 and 12 where 0 is kindergarten, a `reason` of `GRANDFATHER`, `SIBLING`, `PHASE_IN`, `HARDSHIP` or `PROGRAM`, and, when the reason is `SIBLING`, the `sibling_transfer_id` of the already-approved sibling request. The `guardian_user_id` is stamped from the `X-User-ID` header and never accepted from the body. The `origin_school_id` is derived by the server, not supplied: it is the `assigned_school_id` of the residence area's non-superseded assignment in the adopted scenario. Intake is gated twice over: a request filed when the logical clock is strictly after the adoption's `transfer_deadline_date` is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, and a request filed once `transfer_window_status` is `TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED` regardless of the deadline date. The requested school must not be `MOTHBALLED` (`ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACTIVE`), and a repeated `student_reference` within the same adoption is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NAME`. When the reason is `SIBLING`, the link must be usable: `sibling_transfer_id` must be present, must resolve (`ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`), must belong to the same adoption, must itself be `APPROVED`, must name the same `requested_school_id`, and must not be the request itself; any failure is rejected with `ERR_SIBLING_LINK_INVALID`. A new request sits in `SUBMITTED` with `promotion_source`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_notes` all null. Listings are filterable by `adoption_id`, `area_id`, `origin_school_id`, `requested_school_id`, `sibling_transfer_id`, `status` and `resolved_reason`.

**The eligibility waterfall.** At submission, and again at decision time, the server resolves which entitlement the student actually holds by walking four rungs in exactly this order and stopping at the first match. Rung one, `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER`: some phase-in rule of this adoption has `rule_type` `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER`, a band whose `grade_low` is at or below and whose `grade_high` is at or above the `student_grade`, and a `sunset_school_year` at or above the adoption's `effective_school_year`; the `resolved_reason` becomes `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER` and `consumes_seat_pool` becomes false. Rung two, `SIBLING_LINK`: the `reason` is `SIBLING`, the `sibling_transfer_id` is present and names an `APPROVED` request of the same adoption requesting the same school, **and** some `SIBLING_LINK` rule of this adoption covers the grade with an unexpired sunset; the `resolved_reason` becomes `SIBLING_LINK` and `consumes_seat_pool` becomes true. Rung three, `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND`: some `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND` rule of this adoption covers the grade with an unexpired sunset; the `resolved_reason` becomes `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND` and `consumes_seat_pool` becomes false. Rung four, `HARDSHIP_REVIEW`: the `reason` is `HARDSHIP`; the `resolved_reason` becomes `HARDSHIP_REVIEW` and `consumes_seat_pool` becomes true. If no rung matches, `resolved_reason` is `NONE` and `consumes_seat_pool` is false; such a request may be created and sits in `SUBMITTED`, but it can only ever be denied. Every phase-in rule of the adoption is a candidate, since rules have no lifecycle state of their own. The rung order is observable in ordinary use: a grade-5 student covered by both a `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER` band and a `PHASE_IN_GRADE_BAND` band resolves to `TERMINAL_GRADE_GRANDFATHER` and therefore consumes no seat, whereas the reverse order would have consumed a seat and could have produced a waitlisting instead of an approval. Because the waterfall is re-evaluated at decision time, a rule attached after submission can legitimately change a request's `resolved_reason` and `consumes_seat_pool` before it is decided.

**The seat pool.** The pool looks circular — it bounds which requests may be approved, and approved requests shrink it — and is resolved by defining it purely over state that already exists when a decision is evaluated. For a school under an adoption, the post-boundary enrollment is the sum of `projected_students` over the adopted scenario's non-superseded assignments pointing at that school. The inbound count is the number of transfer requests of this adoption whose `requested_school_id` is that school, whose `status` is already `APPROVED` and whose `consumes_seat_pool` is true; the outbound count is the same count taken over `origin_school_id`. The school's current head count is the post-boundary enrollment plus the inbound count minus the outbound count. The pool itself is the school's effective capacity multiplied by the adoption's `transfer_cap_pct`, divided by 100, rounded **down** to a whole seat — a fractional seat is not a seat — minus that current head count. The pool is a signed whole number and may be negative when the adopted scenario already over-fills the school. Requests in `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED` or `DENIED` never affect it, and the request being decided is never counted in its own pool.

**Deciding a request.** A `TransferOfficer` supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `WAITLIST` with optional `decision_notes`. The request must be in `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED`, or the decision is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_DECIDABLE`; the adoption's `transfer_window_status` must still be `TRANSFER_WINDOW_OPEN` (`ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`); and the officer must not be the filing guardian (`ERR_SELF_DECISION_FORBIDDEN`). `APPROVE` and `WAITLIST` both require a real entitlement: a request whose re-evaluated `resolved_reason` is `NONE` may only be denied, and an attempt to approve or waitlist it is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBILITY_BASIS`. For a pool-consuming request, approval requires the requested school's pool to be at least 1 free seat and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_NO_TRANSFER_SEATS`, while waitlisting is the correct outcome when that pool is 0 or negative; a request whose `consumes_seat_pool` is false bypasses the pool entirely and can be approved however full the school is. `APPROVE` moves the request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` or `WAITLISTED → APPROVED` and sets `promotion_source` to `OFFICER_DECISION`; `DENY` moves it `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, which is terminal; `WAITLIST` moves it `SUBMITTED → WAITLISTED`, from which a later officer approval or an activation auto-promotion can still rescue it. Waitlisting is only reachable from `SUBMITTED`, and a request already `WAITLISTED` can only be approved by an officer, auto-promoted, or denied when the window closes. Every decision stamps `decided_by_user_id` from the header, stores `decision_notes` when supplied, and increments the request's `version`. Each approval raises the requested school's usage inside the pool for every later decision, so a batch of decisions is order-sensitive by design.

**Closing the transfer window.** A `TransferOfficer` shuts intake once the logical clock is strictly past the `transfer_deadline_date`; an earlier attempt, or an attempt when the window is already `TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, is rejected with `ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`. The window dimension moves `TRANSFER_WINDOW_OPEN → TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `implementation_status` is untouched, and the undecided queue is cleared: every transfer request of this adoption still in `SUBMITTED` or `WAITLISTED` moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED` or `WAITLISTED → DENIED` with `decision_notes` set to `WINDOW_CLOSED` and `decided_by_user_id` left null, walked in request creation order, each request's `version` incremented. The adoption's `version` is incremented once.

**Rescission.** Rescinding is the exact compensating action for activation, and a `BoardMember` performs it. It requires `implementation_status` to be `IN_EFFECT` (`ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`), carries a `rescind_reason`, and forbids the scenario's author from acting (`ERR_SELF_ADOPTION_FORBIDDEN`). Compensation link one moves `implementation_status` `IN_EFFECT → RESCINDED` and increments the adoption's `version`. Link two restores the old map: for every planning area of the study whose `previous_school_id` is non-null, walked in area creation order, `current_school_id` is set back to `previous_school_id` and `previous_school_id` is reset to null. Link three recomputes every school's `enrolled_students_of_record` and the study's `enrolled_of_record_total`, which returns the district total to its exact pre-activation value. Link four reverses only the automatic promotions: every request of this adoption in `APPROVED` whose `promotion_source` is `WAITLIST_AUTO` moves `APPROVED → WAITLISTED` with `promotion_source` reset to null, walked in request creation order; approvals whose `promotion_source` is `OFFICER_DECISION` survive the rescission untouched, because an officer's judgement is not an artefact of activation. The owning study reverts `IMPLEMENTATION → BOARD_DECISION`, and the adoption's regime interval is released for reuse. The district head-count invariant therefore holds identically before activation, after activation and after rescission.

### 3.9. Post-Implementation Monitoring and Closure

Once boundaries are in effect, the district checks its projections against reality. A `TransferOfficer` records a **variance observation** against an adoption whose `implementation_status` is `IN_EFFECT`, naming the `school_id` observed, the `observed_on` instant of the head-count snapshot, the `observed_enrollment` actually counted and the `projected_enrollment` the adopted scenario had predicted, both non-negative. The school must belong to the adoption's study (`ERR_CROSS_STUDY_REFERENCE`), and recording against an adoption in any other implementation state is rejected with `ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`.

A `projected_enrollment` of 0 leaves the variance percentage undefined, so the request is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_PROJECTION_BASE` and no observation row is created. Otherwise `variance_pct` is 100 multiplied by the signed difference of `observed_enrollment` minus `projected_enrollment`, divided by `projected_enrollment`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — negative when the school under-enrolled. The `flag` is then set by comparing the **absolute value** of `variance_pct` against the adoption's `variance_tolerance_pct`: `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` when the absolute variance is at or below the tolerance, `WATCH` when it is above the tolerance but at or below twice the tolerance, and `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` when it exceeds twice the tolerance. Both band edges belong to the gentler band, so an absolute variance exactly equal to the tolerance is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` and one exactly equal to twice the tolerance is `WATCH`. Each observation is flagged independently — prior observations for the same school are never aggregated — and observations are immutable, carrying only an `id` and a `created_at` alongside their data. Observation listings are filterable by `adoption_id`, `school_id` and `flag`.

Closing monitoring is the last act of the whole workflow and is reserved to a `TransferOfficer`, who may attach `closure_notes`. It requires four conditions at once, and any failure is rejected with `ERR_ADOPTION_STATE_INVALID`: the adoption's `implementation_status` must be `IN_EFFECT`, its `transfer_window_status` must already be `TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, every `ACTIVE` school of the study must have at least one variance observation recorded for this adoption, and no school may currently stand flagged `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`. A school stands flagged when the most recently recorded observation for that school under this adoption, taken by observation creation order, carries the flag `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`. `MOTHBALLED` schools are exempt from the coverage requirement. Because observations are immutable, a `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` observation is permanent history, and the only way past it is to record a later, cleaner observation for that same school; the earlier bad row remains readable and stops blocking closure once superseded by a later one. On success `implementation_status` moves `IN_EFFECT → MONITORING_CLOSED`, the adoption's `version` is incremented, and the owning study moves `IMPLEMENTATION → CLOSED`, which is its terminal state. `CLOSED` admits no further transition: nothing may be created under a closed study and no scenario of it may be edited.
