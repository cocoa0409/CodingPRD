# Bakery Ingredient Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Wholesale bakery distribution sits at an awkward intersection of commodity trading and food safety. The flour a commercial bakery buys on Tuesday is priced off a wheat index that moved on Monday; the yeast in the same drop expires in eleven days; the tree-nut inclusion on line three cannot ride the same trailer as the gluten-free blend two stops later. Today those three concerns live in three unrelated systems, and the reconciliation between them is a spreadsheet maintained by whoever happens to be on shift. This document specifies a single service, mounted under `/bakery-distribution`, that carries a bakery order from forward-price negotiation through warehouse rotation, allergen-segregated routing, and settlement against a published commodity index.

Two commitments shape every requirement that follows. The first is **contractual determinism in pricing**: a forward pricing contract fixes a basis over an index and an observation-date convention, and the price a bakery is invoiced must be reproducible from persisted inputs alone — no wall-clock drift, no re-derivation ambiguity. Contracts are therefore negotiated through a bounded, auditable round trip, and the operator who authors terms is never the operator who activates them. The second is **physical segregation as a hard gate rather than a warning**: allergen class governs which warehouse zone may hold a lot, which trailer may carry a drop, and which SKU may reach which bakery, and every one of those checks rejects the write rather than flagging it.

Because the same order carries an independent commercial life and an independent physical life, the service models the order as two orthogonal status dimensions that gate each other at specific points. Readers should expect cross-dimension conditions throughout §3 — inventory cannot be reserved for an order nobody has priced, and nobody may settle a final price for an order that has not physically moved.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight roles enumerated below, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401. When the headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `CustomerBuyer`, `AccountManager`, `PricingController`, `WarehouseOperator`, `QualityManager`, `TechnicalSpecialist`, `LogisticsPlanner`, and `PlatformAdmin`. A `CustomerBuyer` is the purchasing contact at a bakery: they open and submit orders, counter forward offers, cancel orders, and report silo levels. An `AccountManager` is the commercial owner of the account: they register bakeries, author and offer forward contracts, adjust credit limits, and waive support fees. A `PricingController` governs pricing: publishing index quotes and SKU list prices, activating and terminating contracts, repricing, finalizing and invoicing orders. A `WarehouseOperator` runs the distribution-centre floor: zones, silos, lot receipt, allocation and deallocation, and drop confirmation. A `QualityManager` is the food-safety and allergen authority: zone sanitation lockouts, lot quarantine, release and disposal, and substitution decisions. A `TechnicalSpecialist` provides formulation support: engagements, bench hours, fee attachment, and substitution proposals. A `LogisticsPlanner` owns transport: route construction, stop management, sealing, unsealing, dispatch, and abort. A `PlatformAdmin` drives the logical clock and holds read access to every collection for audit.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — an order's `customer_id`, or a forward contract's `customer_id`. A `CustomerBuyer` acting on a resource whose owning key differs from their `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check applies to opening an order, adding a line to an order, submitting an order, cancelling an order, countering a forward contract, and reading a single customer record.

For the other seven roles, identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountManager`, `PricingController`, `WarehouseOperator`, `QualityManager`, `TechnicalSpecialist`, `LogisticsPlanner`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `authored_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a forward contract, as `requested_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a substitution request, or as `assigned_specialist_user_id` on a support engagement — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Recorded operator identifiers exist to enforce segregation of duties: the `PricingController` who activates a contract must not be the operator recorded in `authored_by_user_id`, and the `QualityManager` who claims, approves, or rejects a substitution request must not be the operator recorded in `requested_by_user_id`. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest accepted value is 100. A `limit` below 1 or above 100 is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint description in §4 states a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending; the collections that deviate — lots ordered by `expires_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending, index quotes ordered by `effective_on` descending, routes ordered by `service_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and route stops ordered by `window_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending — say so in their descriptions.

List responses populate four fields inside `meta`: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of matching rows before the page window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC notation with three fractional digits. Clients never supply either. Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; `updated_at` is refreshed to the mutation instant whenever `version` advances. The single exception is the published commodity index quote: a `CommodityIndexQuote` is an immutable market observation and carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and no endpoint mutates one once published. Customers, products, warehouse zones, lots, silos, forward contracts, contract lines, orders, order lines, substitution requests, delivery routes, route stops, and support engagements all carry the full set.

Time in this service is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any operator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real system time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant through the clock-setting capability described in §4. Advancing the clock is not a passive act: it evaluates two families of automatic transition. Every forward pricing contract whose status is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` and whose `term_end_on` is now strictly earlier than the logical clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, incrementing that contract's `version` by 1; orders whose `pricing_status` is `UNPRICED` and which reference that contract keep their `contract_id` but can no longer be priced against it. Every lot whose status is `AVAILABLE` or `QUARANTINED` and whose `expires_on` is on or before the logical clock date moves to `EXPIRED`, releasing its reservations through exactly the four-hop cascade described in §3.4.

Because dates such as `order_placed_on`, `delivered_on`, `service_on`, `manufactured_on`, `expires_on`, `effective_on`, `term_start_on`, `term_end_on` and `requested_delivery_on` are UTC dates at midnight, comparisons involving them are whole-day comparisons, and any day count between two of them is a count of whole UTC days with the fractional remainder discarded.

Those business-date fields, together with the window-instant fields `lockout_start_at`, `lockout_end_at`, `last_fill_window_start_at`, `last_fill_window_end_at`, `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, are rendered in responses as ISO 8601 UTC to whole-second precision, with no fractional digits at all: a midnight date reads `2026-09-15T00:00:00Z` and a client-supplied window instant is echoed as `2026-09-15T10:30:00Z`. The three-fractional-digit form is reserved for `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`. Any of those date or window-instant fields that is not yet set serialises as null.

### 2.5. Money, Pounds and Rounding Discipline

All money in this service is integer cents. Field names ending in `_cents` hold whole cents and are never fractional in a response. Where a monetary quantity is derived from a percentage or a fractional multiplier, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using round-half-up: a computed value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied at the specific points named in §3 and nowhere else — in particular, the order subtotal is produced by rounding each line to whole cents and then summing the rounded line values, never by summing exact fractional line values and rounding once at the end. Those two procedures give different subtotals for the same inputs, and only the first is correct.

Weights are pounds carried at two decimal places, and equality or threshold comparisons on pounds are exact at that precision. Percentages that are exposed as fields — the trailer's `utilization_pct` — are rounded half-up to two decimal places. The allergen risk score exposed as `allergen_risk_score` is likewise carried at two decimal places with round-half-up applied once, at the point §3.4 specifies. Bench hours on a support engagement are carried at one decimal place. Settled per-pound prices are whole cents per pound and require no rounding, because every input to them is already whole cents.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Orders and Routes

Two write operations in this service require the client to prove it is acting on a current view of a parent resource, and only those two.

A request to `POST /bakery-distribution/substitution-requests` must carry `expected_order_version` as a required input field, holding the `version` of the parent `Order` — reached from the supplied `order_line_id` — as the specialist last observed it. If that value does not equal the order's current `version` at the moment of the write, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Nothing in the substitution state machine forbids the write itself: raising a request legitimately supersedes any earlier `REQUESTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` request on the same order line, moving that earlier request to `WITHDRAWN`. Optimistic concurrency on the order's `version` is the only mechanism that stops a specialist from superseding another specialist's proposal on the basis of an out-of-date view of the order.

Likewise, a request to `POST /bakery-distribution/routes/{id}/stops` must carry `expected_route_version` as a required input field, holding the `version` of the parent `DeliveryRoute` as the planner last observed it. A value that differs from the route's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Appending a stop is always permissible while the route's status is `PLANNING` and the trailer is under capacity, so no state check rejects it; optimistic concurrency on the route's `version` is what prevents a planner from appending against a stale view of `planned_load_lb`, `utilization_pct` and `allergen_class_lock`.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to any other write endpoint that include one are simply operating outside the contract; the endpoints listed in §4 without such a field do not require and do not check one.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Nested resources are addressed hierarchically, and existence is verified strictly left to right: the route before the stop, the contract before the contract line, the order before the order line. The first path segment whose referenced row does not exist produces `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and no deeper segment is examined. Foreign keys arriving in a request body are a separate concern: `customer_id`, `product_id`, `zone_id`, `contract_id`, `order_id`, `order_line_id`, `proposed_product_id` and `route_id` must each resolve to an existing row, and a key that does not resolve yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, also HTTP 404. That body-level check applies when opening an order, adding an order line, drafting a forward contract, adding a contract line, receiving a lot, registering a silo, appending a route stop, raising a substitution request, opening a support engagement, and attaching a support engagement to an order.

When more than one rule could reject the same request, the rejection is deterministic and follows a single global precedence order. Authentication comes first: header presence, then role recognition, then endpoint role authorization. Schema validation of the request body and query string follows — required fields present, types correct, enum values drawn from the declared set, `quantity_lb` strictly greater than zero, `volume_committed_lb` at least 1000, `basis_cents_per_lb` no less than −500 and no greater than 500, `measured_residual_lb` no less than zero and no greater than the silo's `capacity_lb`, `hours` strictly greater than zero, and `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive — all failures reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Next comes path-resource existence, then body foreign-key existence, then the resource-ownership check for the `CustomerBuyer` role, then the two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6 (order version before route version, since they never co-occur on one endpoint).

Only after all of that do state and business checks run, in this order: the target resource's own state-transition legality; the cross-dimension gate on an order; the state of related entities; segregation of duties; allergen-program compatibility; allergen zone dedication; trailer allergen lock; shelf-life rotation availability; entity-link agreement; stop-window separation; silo fill-window collision; zone sanitation lockout; contract term coverage; index-quote availability; route service-date agreement; uniqueness; route compartment capacity; bulk capability; silo headroom; minimum pneumatic fill; negotiation and revision round caps; contract volume headroom; minimum drop value; credit exposure; and finally engagement attachment before invoicing.

Three collisions are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. A submit request against an order that is already `PROVISIONAL` *and* whose lines breach the customer's allergen program returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not `ERR_ALLERGEN_PROGRAM_CONFLICT`, because state legality outranks allergen compatibility. An order that both breaches the allergen program *and* would exceed the customer's credit limit returns `ERR_ALLERGEN_PROGRAM_CONFLICT`, because allergen compatibility outranks the fiscal checks. And a route stop that both collides in time with an existing stop *and* overflows the trailer returns `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, because the temporal check outranks the capacity check.

### 2.8. Allergen Segregation from Zone to Trailer to Bakery

Allergen class is the spine of the safety model, and it is declared in two vocabularies that must be read as distinct. Every SKU carries an `allergen_class` drawn from `NONE`, `GLUTEN`, `TREE_NUT`, `PEANUT`, `DAIRY`, `EGG`, or `SOY`, and every warehouse zone and every trailer lock uses that same set of seven values. Separately, every bakery declares an `allergen_program` drawn from `STANDARD`, `GLUTEN_FREE_CERTIFIED`, or `NUT_FREE_CERTIFIED`, describing the handling regime at the receiving site.

Three segregation gates run at three different moments. At receipt, a lot may only enter a zone whose `allergen_class` is exactly equal to the `allergen_class` of the lot's product; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ALLERGEN_ZONE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). On the trailer, once a route's `allergen_class_lock` holds any value other than `NONE`, every subsequently added stop's order lines must all carry that same `allergen_class`; a trailer may never hold two different non-`NONE` classes at once. That check guards both appending a stop and sealing the load plan, and in either case the rejection is `ERR_ROUTE_ALLERGEN_LOCK_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). At the bakery, program compatibility governs: a customer whose `allergen_program` is `GLUTEN_FREE_CERTIFIED` may not receive a product whose `allergen_class` is `GLUTEN`, and a customer whose `allergen_program` is `NUT_FREE_CERTIFIED` may not receive a product whose `allergen_class` is `TREE_NUT` or `PEANUT`. Independently of the declared program, a customer whose `risk_tier` has been derived as `RED` may not receive a product whose `allergen_class` is `TREE_NUT` or `PEANUT`. All three of those bakery-level breaches return `ERR_ALLERGEN_PROGRAM_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422), and the check is enforced at exactly three moments: when a line is added to an order, when an order is submitted, and when a substitution is approved.

### 2.9. Two Conserved Quantities

The service maintains two named conservation invariants, and every mutation that touches either quantity is expected to leave the invariant intact.

The first conserved quantity is **pounds of inventory**. For every lot, the sum of `quantity_on_hand_lb`, `quantity_reserved_lb` and `quantity_consumed_lb` equals `quantity_received_lb`, exactly, at two decimal places. That equality is asserted after every mutation that touches a lot: allocation, deallocation, order cancellation, quarantine, release, disposal, full delivery, short delivery, and the lot re-pick that follows a substitution approval. A forward action such as reserving pounds during allocation and its compensating action such as deallocation or cancellation must leave not only the sum but each of the three buckets individually at their pre-action values. Lots that have been disposed hold their written-off pounds in `quantity_consumed_lb`; every lot row participates in the invariant regardless of status.

The second conserved quantity is **committed contract pounds**. For every contract line, `volume_drawn_lb` equals the total of `quantity_lb` over all order lines bound to that contract line whose owning order's `pricing_status` is anything other than `VOID`, less the pounds of that product that came back through short shipments on orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHORT_SHIPPED`. Voided orders contribute nothing. That figure is never negative, and a contract's `drawn_volume_lb` equals the total of `volume_drawn_lb` across its lines. The invariant is asserted after order submission, order cancellation, short delivery, and each automatic movement between `ACTIVE` and `EXHAUSTED`. Stated in business terms: drawing pounds on submission and returning them through cancellation or a short drop must leave the remaining headroom — the commitment less the drawn volume — at its pre-action value.

### 2.10. Cascades, Compound States and Recomputation

Several operations in this service reach well beyond the resource named in the request, and the cascades are specified as ordered hops in §3 rather than left to implementer discretion. Two are deep: quarantining a lot unwinds reservations, orders, stops and routes across four hops, and approving a substitution rewrites a line, re-picks inventory, reprices the order, revisits credit standing and re-derives allergen risk across five hops. Each hop that mutates a resource increments that resource's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`.

Two parent resources hold **compound** state derived from their children. A delivery route's `status` advances partly from explicit planner actions and partly from the aggregate condition of its stops: it completes when no stop remains in `PLANNED`, and it falls back from `SEALED` to `PLANNING` when its `stop_count` reaches zero. A forward contract's `status` oscillates between `ACTIVE` and `EXHAUSTED` purely from the aggregate drawn volume of its lines, with no operator action involved. Computed aggregate fields — `on_hand_lb` on a zone, `committed_volume_lb` and `drawn_volume_lb` on a contract, `planned_load_lb`, `utilization_pct` and `stop_count` on a route, `open_exposure_cents` and `allergen_risk_score` on a customer — are always full recomputations from their current inputs, never incremental adjustments applied to a stored running total.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Account, a Catalogue and an Index Board

An `AccountManager` registers a bakery by supplying its `legal_name`, its declared `allergen_program`, and the `credit_limit_cents` the commercial team is willing to extend. The legal name must be between 1 and 120 characters and must be globally unique when compared case-insensitively; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409). The credit limit is a whole number of cents and may not be negative. A fourth input, `min_drop_cents`, is optional and expresses the smallest priced value a single delivery drop to this bakery may carry; when the account manager does not supply it, the account is created with a two hundred fifty dollar floor — that is, 25000 cents. Registration initialises the remaining fields deterministically: `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `open_exposure_cents` becomes 0, `allergen_risk_score` becomes 0.00, and `risk_tier` becomes `GREEN`. The account `status` is only ever `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD`, and only the exposure recomputation described in §3.8 moves it between the two.

Credit terms get renegotiated, and the account manager has a dedicated adjustment surface for that: supplying a `new_credit_limit_cents` together with a free-text `reason` overwrites `credit_limit_cents`, then immediately re-runs the exposure recomputation, which may move the account from `ACTIVE` to `CREDIT_HOLD` if outstanding exposure now sits above the lowered limit, or from `CREDIT_HOLD` back to `ACTIVE` if a raised limit now covers it. The customer's `version` increments by 1.

Catalogue authorship belongs to the `PricingController`, not to the account team, because a SKU's price and index linkage are pricing-governance artefacts. Creating a SKU requires a `sku` code of 3 to 32 characters, globally unique; a `category` drawn from `FLOUR`, `LEAVENING`, `FAT`, `INCLUSION`, or `PACKAGING`; an `allergen_class`; a `shelf_life_days` count of at least 1, which is what turns a lot's manufacture date into an expiry date; and a `list_price_cents_per_lb` of at least 1 cent, which is the fallback per-pound price whenever no contract line applies. Three further inputs are optional. `bulk_capable` states whether the SKU can be blown into a silo as a pneumatic fill rather than delivered as palletised cases, and defaults to false. `index_code` names the commodity index the SKU prices against — `HRW_WHEAT`, `SRW_WHEAT`, `SOFT_OIL`, or `CANE_SUGAR` — and defaults to `NONE`, meaning the SKU is priced from the fixed list only. `min_rotation_days` is the minimum remaining shelf life, in days, a lot must still hold to be pickable, and defaults to 0.

Physical storage is modelled as allergen-dedicated zones, created by a `WarehouseOperator` from a `zone_code` of 2 to 16 characters, globally unique, and the single `allergen_class` the zone is dedicated to. A new zone opens with `status` equal to `OPEN` and `on_hand_lb` at 0. Zones do not stay open forever: a `QualityManager` schedules an allergen-changeover sanitation lockout by supplying `lockout_start_at` and `lockout_end_at`, or clears an existing one by passing the optional `clear` flag. The lockout is a **closed** interval — both endpoints belong to it. When the logical clock instant falls inside that closed interval the zone's `status` reads `SANITATION_LOCKOUT`, and otherwise `OPEN`; the zone's `version` increments by 1 on either scheduling or clearing. While a zone is inside its lockout window, no lot may be received into it and no quarantined lot in it may be released, and both attempts are rejected with `ERR_ZONE_LOCKOUT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

The index board is a sequence of published market observations. A `PricingController` publishes a quote by naming the `index_code` being quoted, the `effective_on` UTC date from which the quote becomes prevailing, and the `price_cents_per_lb` published for that date, which must be at least 1. The pair of index code and effective date is unique — republishing the same index for the same date is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — and consequently there is never a tie when the pricing engine looks for the prevailing quote. A published quote governs every observation date from its `effective_on` onward until a later quote for the same index supersedes it. Quotes are immutable and are listed newest effective date first, optionally narrowed to a single `index_code`, with the usual `limit` and `offset` window.

### 3.2. Forward Pricing: Authorship, Counter-Offers and Activation

Forward pricing exists because a bakery buying forty tonnes of hard red winter flour a month cannot run its own margin off a daily index print. An `AccountManager` drafts a contract for a bakery by naming the `customer_id`, the inclusive term boundaries `term_start_on` and `term_end_on`, and a `pricing_mode` that fixes which observation date settles the contract's lines — either `ORDER_DATE_INDEX` or `DELIVERY_DATE_INDEX`. A freshly drafted contract has `status` equal to `DRAFT`, `negotiation_round` at 0, and both `committed_volume_lb` and `drawn_volume_lb` at 0.

Commitment lives on the contract's lines. Each line names a `product_id`, a `volume_committed_lb` of at least 1000 pounds that the bakery undertakes to draw over the term, and a `basis_cents_per_lb` — the differential added to the prevailing index print, which may be negative to express a discount and must lie between −500 and 500 inclusive. An optional `cap_cents_per_lb` puts a ceiling on the settled per-pound price; when it is omitted the line is uncapped. A line's `volume_drawn_lb` starts at 0. At most one line may exist per combination of owning contract and SKU, and a second line for the same SKU on the same contract is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Adding a line recomputes the parent contract's `committed_volume_lb` as the total of `volume_committed_lb` across all its lines and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Offering the contract moves it from `DRAFT` to `OFFERED`. The account manager may only do so when the contract is genuinely in `DRAFT`, when at least one commitment line exists, and when `term_end_on` is strictly later than `term_start_on`. The offer records the acting operator in `authored_by_user_id`, recomputes `committed_volume_lb` from the contract's lines, and increments `version` by 1. A bakery's `CustomerBuyer` may then counter, moving the contract from `OFFERED` to `COUNTERED`; countering is permitted only while the contract is `OFFERED` and only by the buyer whose `X-User-ID` equals the contract's `customer_id`. The counter carries a single `counter_basis_cents_per_lb`, and that value replaces `basis_cents_per_lb` on **every** line of the contract, not merely on one. The contract's `version` increments by 1.

Negotiation is a genuine cycle: `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED`, and it is bounded. Re-offering a `COUNTERED` contract is permitted only while `negotiation_round` is strictly less than 3; the re-offer increments `negotiation_round` by 1, re-records `authored_by_user_id` as the operator who re-offered, and increments `version` by 1. A fourth attempt — that is, an attempt made when `negotiation_round` has already reached 3 — is rejected with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).

Activation is where segregation of duties bites. Only a `PricingController` may move a contract from `OFFERED` to `ACTIVE`, and four conditions must hold: the contract is in `OFFERED`; the acting operator's `X-User-ID` differs from the contract's `authored_by_user_id`; the bakery's own `status` is `ACTIVE` rather than `CREDIT_HOLD`; and no other contract for the same bakery that is already `ACTIVE` overlaps the interval from `term_start_on` to `term_end_on` on any shared `product_id`. A same-operator collision returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; an overlapping active term on a shared SKU returns `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Activation records the acting controller in `approved_by_user_id` and increments `version` by 1; that field is null on every contract that has not yet been activated.

A `PricingController` may terminate a contract that sits in `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, or `ACTIVE`, supplying a `reason`; the contract moves to `TERMINATED` and its `version` increments by 1. Termination does not retroactively rewrite invoices, but it does change future arithmetic: every order referencing that contract whose `pricing_status` is `PROVISIONAL` will fall back to list pricing at its next repricing event. `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` are the two terminal states of the contract lifecycle; a contract reaching either is done, and any further action on it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

Two of the contract's transitions have no human actor at all. When every one of a contract's lines has a `volume_drawn_lb` that has reached or passed its `volume_committed_lb`, the contract moves itself from `ACTIVE` to `EXHAUSTED`, recomputing `drawn_volume_lb` and incrementing `version` by 1; this is evaluated at the end of every order submission that draws volume. The reverse also happens: when returned volume leaves at least one line with `volume_drawn_lb` strictly below its `volume_committed_lb`, an `EXHAUSTED` contract moves back to `ACTIVE`, provided its `term_end_on` is on or after the logical clock date, again recomputing `drawn_volume_lb` and incrementing `version`. The pair `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` may therefore cycle as many times as volume is drawn and returned.

### 3.3. Order Intake, Submission and Drawing Contract Volume

An order carries two independent status dimensions. `fulfillment_status` tracks the physical life of the load through `NEW`, `ALLOCATED`, `ROUTED`, `LOADED`, and then one of the three terminal dispositions `DELIVERED`, `SHORT_SHIPPED`, or `CANCELLED`. `pricing_status` tracks the commercial life through `UNPRICED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL`, and then one of the two terminal dispositions `INVOICED` or `VOID`. Neither dimension is derived from the other; each has its own transitions, and the points at which one gates the other are named explicitly below and in §3.9.

A `CustomerBuyer` opens an order by supplying their own `customer_id`, a `delivery_mode` of either `BULK` for a pneumatic silo fill or `BAGGED` for palletised cases, and a `requested_delivery_on` date. An optional `contract_id` nominates the forward contract the order should price against; when it is omitted the order prices entirely from the catalogue list. A new order begins with `fulfillment_status` at `NEW`, `pricing_status` at `UNPRICED`, `surge_window` false, `short_ship_lb` at 0, and every money field — `subtotal_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `tech_attach_fee_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents` and `total_cents` — at 0. Both `order_placed_on` and `delivered_on` are null until the respective events stamp them.

Lines are added to an order while it remains `UNPRICED`, each naming a `product_id` and a `quantity_lb` strictly greater than zero. A new line begins with `allocation_status` at `UNALLOCATED`, `unit_price_cents` and `extended_cents` at 0, and `lot_id`, `silo_id`, `contract_line_id` and `substituted_from_product_id` all null. Adding a line increments the parent order's `version` by 1, which matters for anyone holding an order version for optimistic-concurrency purposes. The allergen-program compatibility gate of §2.8 runs at this moment as well as at submission, so a nut inclusion cannot even be added to an order for a `NUT_FREE_CERTIFIED` bakery. If the order's `delivery_mode` is `BULK`, every line's product must have `bulk_capable` set to true and the bakery must have a silo registered for that SKU; a line that fails either test is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_BULK_CAPABLE` (HTTP 422), and that same check runs again at allocation time.

Submission is the buyer's commitment, and it is the most heavily guarded transition in the service, moving `pricing_status` from `UNPRICED` to `PROVISIONAL`. It requires that the order is still `UNPRICED`, that its `fulfillment_status` is still `NEW`, that the acting buyer's `X-User-ID` equals the order's `customer_id`, that the order carries at least one line, and that the bakery's own `status` is `ACTIVE` — a bakery on `CREDIT_HOLD` cannot submit, which surfaces as `ERR_RELATED_ENTITY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). When the order nominates a contract, that contract's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the order's `requested_delivery_on` must fall on or after `term_start_on` and on or before `term_end_on`, both endpoints inclusive; a delivery date outside the term is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_NOT_COVERING` (HTTP 422). Where an index quote is required and none has been published with an `effective_on` at or before the applicable observation date, the submission is rejected with `ERR_INDEX_QUOTE_MISSING` (HTTP 422).

Drawing volume is part of submission rather than a separate act. Each line matched to a contract line adds its `quantity_lb` to that line's `volume_drawn_lb`, and the contract's `drawn_volume_lb` is recomputed from its lines. A draw may bring a line's `volume_drawn_lb` up to exactly its `volume_committed_lb`, which is permitted and exhausts that line; a draw that would push it strictly above the commitment is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_VOLUME_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Once every line of the contract has reached its commitment, the contract auto-transitions `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED` at the end of the submission, as §3.2 describes.

Two fiscal gates close the submission. The order's computed `total_cents` must reach the bakery's `min_drop_cents`; a total exactly equal to the floor passes, and a total strictly below it is rejected with `ERR_MIN_DROP_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). Then the bakery's existing `open_exposure_cents` plus this order's computed `total_cents` must be no greater than its `credit_limit_cents`; a sum that strictly exceeds the limit is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Note the ordering consequence spelled out in §2.7: an order that breaches both the allergen program and the credit limit reports the allergen breach. A successful submission stamps `order_placed_on` with the logical clock date, runs the three pricing calculations of §3.4 in their pinned order, recomputes the bakery's `open_exposure_cents`, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Repricing is a self-loop on `PROVISIONAL`: the state does not change, but the totals do. A `PricingController` reprices an order after a new index quote lands or after a contract is terminated, and the same self-loop fires automatically when a substitution is approved or a support fee is attached. Repricing is permitted only while `pricing_status` is `PROVISIONAL` and only while `fulfillment_status` is none of `LOADED`, `DELIVERED`, `SHORT_SHIPPED`, or `CANCELLED` — once the trailer is loaded the commercial figures stop moving. Each reprice re-runs the settlement price for every line at the order-date observation, then re-extends the lines, then re-runs the order-level waterfall, then recomputes `open_exposure_cents`; if the recomputed exposure now exceeds `credit_limit_cents`, the bakery moves `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD`. The order's `version` increments by 1.

A bakery may cancel its own order while `fulfillment_status` is `NEW` or `ALLOCATED`, optionally recording a `cancellation_reason`, and only when the acting buyer's `X-User-ID` matches the order's `customer_id`. Cancellation drives both dimensions at once: `fulfillment_status` becomes `CANCELLED` and `pricing_status` becomes `VOID` — a transition available from either `UNPRICED` or `PROVISIONAL`. All reservations are released exactly as in a deallocation, every contract line's `volume_drawn_lb` is reduced by the pounds this order drew, and `subtotal_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents`, `tech_attach_fee_cents` and `total_cents` all reset to 0. The bakery's `open_exposure_cents` is recomputed, and if the owning contract was `EXHAUSTED` and a line now falls below its commitment, the contract auto-transitions `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE`. The order's `version` increments by 1.

Orders are readable individually and as a filtered collection — narrowable by `customer_id`, by `contract_id`, and by either `fulfillment_status` or `pricing_status` — and a dedicated computed read exposes the pricing summary: each line's unit and extended price alongside the order's surcharge, rebate and total, plus the index quote that priced each line. Order lines are likewise readable per order and across orders, filterable by `order_id`, `product_id`, `substituted_from_product_id`, `lot_id`, `silo_id`, `contract_line_id` and `allocation_status`.

### 3.4. The Pricing Waterfall: Unit Price, Line Extension and Order Settlement

Every pricing event — the initial submission, each provisional reprice, and the final settlement — runs three calculations in a fixed sequence: first the settled per-pound price for each line, then the extension of each line into cents and the order subtotal, then the order-level surcharge, rebate and total. The sequence never runs in a different order and never re-enters an earlier step.

**Step one: the settled per-pound price.** For each line, the engine looks for the commitment line belonging to the order's nominated contract whose `product_id` equals the line's `product_id`. At most one such line can exist, because a contract may hold only one line per SKU. If the order nominates no contract, or no matching commitment line exists, or the contract's `status` is neither `ACTIVE` nor `EXHAUSTED`, the line prices from the catalogue: its `contract_line_id` is set to null and its `unit_price_cents` becomes the product's `list_price_cents_per_lb`. Otherwise the line binds to that commitment line, recording its identifier in `contract_line_id`, and prices from the contract. Within the contract path there are two branches. When the product's `index_code` is `NONE` the SKU has no index linkage at all, so the settled price is the product's `list_price_cents_per_lb` plus the commitment line's `basis_cents_per_lb`, and if that sum falls below one cent per pound it is lifted to one cent — one cent per pound is the absolute floor on any settled price. When the product names a real index, the engine resolves the **prevailing quote**: among the published quotes for that index, the one with the greatest `effective_on` that is on or before the observation date. No tie is possible because index code and effective date are unique together. If no such quote exists the pricing event is rejected with `ERR_INDEX_QUOTE_MISSING` — and that applies to all three pricing events alike: submission, a provisional reprice, and final settlement. From the prevailing quote the engine takes `price_cents_per_lb`, adds the commitment line's `basis_cents_per_lb`, and then applies cap and floor in exactly that order: if the commitment line carries a `cap_cents_per_lb` the result becomes the lesser of the two, and if the capped result still falls below one cent per pound it is lifted to one cent. The outcome is written to `unit_price_cents`.

The observation date differs between two variants of that calculation, and this is the single most consequential subtlety in the pricing model. During submission and during every provisional reprice, the observation date is the order's `order_placed_on`, regardless of what the contract's `pricing_mode` says. During final settlement, the observation date depends on the mode: under `DELIVERY_DATE_INDEX` it is the order's `delivered_on`, and under `ORDER_DATE_INDEX` it remains `order_placed_on`. Consequently, when a contract prices on the delivery date and a new quote is published between the order's placement and its delivery, that later quote becomes prevailing at final settlement and the final per-pound price legitimately differs from the provisional one for identical order inputs.

**Step two: extension into cents.** Each line earns a quantity discount, and the discount is a single rate applied to the whole line rather than a marginal rate applied to bands. A line of fewer than 2000 pounds earns no discount. A line of 2000 pounds or more but fewer than 10000 pounds earns one and a half percent. A line of 10000 pounds or more — the boundary is inclusive — earns three percent. The line's gross value is its `quantity_lb` multiplied by its `unit_price_cents`, held as an exact value; the net value is that gross reduced by the discount rate. The rounding order is pinned: each line's net value is rounded half-up to whole cents **first** and written to `extended_cents`, and only then are the rounded line values summed, in `created_at` ascending order, to produce the order's `subtotal_cents`. Summing exact unrounded line values and rounding once at the end is forbidden and yields a different subtotal for the same inputs. Every line of the order participates, including lines whose `allocation_status` is `UNALLOCATED` or `RELEASED`; nothing is excluded.

**Step three: surcharge, rebate and total.** The order-level waterfall reads the subtotal produced by step two and proceeds through four further stages whose order is likewise pinned, with no iteration to a fixed point. First, `surge_window` is determined: it is true when the order's `requested_delivery_on` falls inside a declared holiday surge window, with both endpoints of the window inclusive. Two surge windows are declared — the spring baking build-up running from 15 March 2026 through 15 April 2026, and the winter holiday peak running from 15 November 2026 through 31 December 2026. Second, the fuel surcharge rate is chosen from that flag: four percent of subtotal inside a surge window, and two and a half percent outside one. That rate is applied to `subtotal_cents` and the product is rounded half-up to whole cents to give `fuel_surcharge_cents`.

Third — and this is where the arithmetic looks circular but is not — the volume rebate tier is keyed on a base that already contains the fuel surcharge, while the rebate percentage itself is applied to the subtotal alone and never to the surcharge. The rebate base is `subtotal_cents` plus `fuel_surcharge_cents`. If the order sits inside a surge window the rebate rate is zero, because rebates are suppressed during holiday peaks no matter how large the order. Otherwise, a rebate base below five thousand dollars — that is, below 500000 cents — earns no rebate; a base of 500000 cents or more but below 1500000 cents earns one percent; and a base of 1500000 cents or more, the boundary being inclusive, earns two and a half percent. The chosen rate is applied to `subtotal_cents` and rounded half-up to whole cents to give `volume_rebate_cents`. The two roundings, at the surcharge stage and at the rebate stage, are performed independently.

Finally, `total_cents` is the subtotal plus the fuel surcharge plus the formulation-support attach fee, less the volume rebate. The attach fee is read from `tech_attach_fee_cents` as it stands at the moment of the pricing event, and it is 0 until a support engagement has been attached to the order as described in §3.9.

Open exposure is a separate aggregation, recomputed in full from scratch — never incrementally — after submission, after each provisional reprice, after final settlement, after invoicing, and after voiding. A bakery's `open_exposure_cents` is the total of `total_cents` over every one of its orders whose `pricing_status` is either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`; orders that are `UNPRICED`, `INVOICED` or `VOID` contribute nothing. Immediately afterwards the account standing is re-derived: an `ACTIVE` bakery whose recomputed exposure strictly exceeds its `credit_limit_cents` moves to `CREDIT_HOLD`, and a bakery on `CREDIT_HOLD` whose recomputed exposure is no greater than its limit returns to `ACTIVE`. There is no time window on this aggregation and no rounding, since every input is already whole cents.

### 3.5. Receiving Lots, First-Expired-First-Out Rotation and Reservation

Yeast and chemical leavening do not forgive sloppy rotation, so inventory in this service is tracked at lot granularity with an explicit freshness rule. A `WarehouseOperator` receives a supplier lot by supplying a `lot_code` of 4 to 32 characters that is globally unique, the `product_id` the lot holds, the `zone_id` it is being put away into, the `manufactured_on` production date, and the `quantity_received_lb` strictly greater than zero. Receipt computes `expires_on` as the manufacture date advanced by the product's `shelf_life_days`, landing at midnight UTC, and initialises the three pound buckets: `quantity_on_hand_lb` equals `quantity_received_lb`, while `quantity_reserved_lb` and `quantity_consumed_lb` are both 0. The lot's `status` becomes `AVAILABLE` and its `quarantine_reason` is null. Two gates guard the receipt: the destination zone's `allergen_class` must equal the product's `allergen_class`, failing which the receipt is rejected with `ERR_ALLERGEN_ZONE_MISMATCH`, and the logical clock must not lie inside the zone's closed sanitation lockout interval, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_LOCKOUT_CONFLICT`. A successful receipt recomputes the zone's `on_hand_lb`, which is the total of `quantity_on_hand_lb` over the lots stored in that zone whose status is `AVAILABLE` or `QUARANTINED`.

Lot selection is deterministic, and the rule that chooses which lot fills a line is worth stating as an explicit predicate rather than as an instinct about freshness. For a given order line, a lot is a candidate when all six of the following hold: the lot's `product_id` equals the line's `product_id`; the lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE`; the lot's `quantity_on_hand_lb` is at least the line's `quantity_lb`; the number of whole UTC days from the logical clock date to the lot's `expires_on` is at least the product's `min_rotation_days`; the lot's storage zone has `status` equal to `OPEN`; and that zone's `allergen_class` equals the product's `allergen_class`. Lots that are `QUARANTINED`, `EXPIRED`, `DISPOSED` or `DEPLETED` are never candidates. Among the candidates, ranking is First-Expired-First-Out: the earliest `expires_on` wins. Only where two candidates share the same expiry does a tie-break apply, and then the larger `quantity_on_hand_lb` wins; if that is also equal, the earlier `created_at` wins. Lot identifiers are never used as a tie-break. Splitting a line across two lots is not permitted — one lot must cover the whole line by itself — which is precisely why the on-hand test above is stated against the full line quantity. If the candidate set is empty for any line, the operation is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT` (HTTP 409). Pound comparisons in this predicate are exact at two decimal places, and the day count to expiry discards any fractional remainder.

Reserving inventory advances the order's `fulfillment_status` from `NEW` to `ALLOCATED`, and it is a `WarehouseOperator` action. Six preconditions apply. The order must still be `NEW` on the fulfillment dimension. Its `pricing_status` must be either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — this is the cross-dimension gate, and an attempt to reserve inventory for an `UNPRICED` order is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_DIMENSION_GATE` (HTTP 409), because the warehouse does not pick against a load nobody has priced. The bakery's own `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The rotation selector must return a non-empty candidate set for every line. Every candidate lot's zone must have `status` equal to `OPEN`. And if the order's `delivery_mode` is `BULK`, every line's product must carry `bulk_capable` set to true and a silo must exist for the pairing of the order's `customer_id` and the line's `product_id`.

Allocation's effects run across three entities. Each line's `lot_id` is set to the lot the selector chose and its `allocation_status` becomes `RESERVED`. On each picked lot, `quantity_reserved_lb` increases by the line's `quantity_lb` and `quantity_on_hand_lb` decreases by the same amount, so the conservation invariant of §2.9 holds unchanged. Every touched zone's `on_hand_lb` is recomputed. On a `BULK` order, each line's `silo_id` is set to the bakery's silo for that SKU. Finally the bakery's `allergen_risk_score` is recomputed on the in-flight basis and its `risk_tier` re-derived, and the order's `version` increments by 1.

Deallocation is the exact compensating action, returning `fulfillment_status` from `ALLOCATED` to `NEW`. It is permitted only while the order is `ALLOCATED` and only while no route stop for that order is still in `PLANNED` — an order already sitting on a planned stop must have that stop cancelled first. Each line's `allocation_status` returns to `UNALLOCATED` with `lot_id` and `silo_id` cleared to null; on each released lot, `quantity_reserved_lb` decreases by the line's `quantity_lb` and `quantity_on_hand_lb` increases by the same amount, while `quantity_received_lb` is untouched. Every affected zone's `on_hand_lb` is recomputed, the bakery's `allergen_risk_score` and `risk_tier` are recomputed on the in-flight basis, and the order's `version` increments by 1. The point of stating this as a compensating action is that each of the three pound buckets, individually, must return to the value it held before the allocation.

### 3.6. Bulk Delivery: Silo Registration and the Fill Plan

Bulk flour does not arrive on pallets; it is blown from a pneumatic trailer into a dedicated silo at the bakery. A `WarehouseOperator` registers such a silo from a `silo_code` of 2 to 16 characters that is globally unique, the `customer_id` whose site hosts it, the `product_id` it is dedicated to — which must be a SKU whose `bulk_capable` is true — and a `capacity_lb` of at least 1000 pounds. An optional `min_fill_lb` states the smallest fill the pneumatic trailer can economically blow and defaults to 2000 pounds. A new silo has `residual_lb` at 0 and both `last_fill_window_start_at` and `last_fill_window_end_at` null, since it has never been filled. At most one silo may exist per pairing of bakery and SKU; a second registration for the same pairing is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Bakeries report their own consumption between fills. A level-probe reading, submitted by either a `WarehouseOperator` or the bakery's own `CustomerBuyer`, carries a `measured_residual_lb` that overwrites `residual_lb` outright rather than adjusting it; the value must be no less than zero and no greater than the silo's `capacity_lb`, and a reading outside that range is a schema failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Each accepted reading increments the silo's `version` by 1.

When a `BULK` order is allocated, each line runs a fill plan against the single silo matching the order's `customer_id` and the line's `product_id`; no other silo is ever considered. If no such silo exists the allocation is rejected with `ERR_NO_SILO_FOR_PRODUCT` (HTTP 422). Four quantities then interact. Headroom is the silo's `capacity_lb` less its current `residual_lb`. Contract room is the line's `quantity_lb` outright when the line has no `contract_line_id`, and otherwise the commitment line's `volume_committed_lb` less its `volume_drawn_lb` plus this line's own `quantity_lb`, floored at zero so it can never go negative. The third quantity is the line's own `quantity_lb`, and the fourth is the trailer compartment ceiling of 48000 pounds — twenty-four tonnes is the most a single compartment run will carry. The fillable amount is the smallest of those four. If the fillable amount is less than the line's `quantity_lb`, the allocation is rejected with `ERR_SILO_HEADROOM_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). If the fillable amount is less than the silo's `min_fill_lb`, it cannot be blown pneumatically at all and the allocation is rejected with `ERR_BULK_FILL_BELOW_MIN` (HTTP 422). Only when both tests pass is the line's `silo_id` set to that silo. Pounds here are compared at two decimal places and the comparisons are exact.

One referential check accompanies the fill plan: the silo's `product_id` must agree with the order line's `product_id`, and a disagreement is reported as `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The same error code covers two sibling mismatches elsewhere — a support engagement whose `customer_id` differs from the target order's `customer_id` at attach time, and a substitution request whose `order_line_id` belongs to a different order than the one whose version the requester supplied.

### 3.7. Quality Holds, Write-Offs, Expiry and the Allergen Risk Score

A lot's disposition runs through five values. It begins `AVAILABLE`; a quality intervention can move it to `QUARANTINED` and back; and it ends in one of three terminal dispositions — `DISPOSED`, `EXPIRED`, or `DEPLETED`.

Consider a scenario where a supplier notifies the distribution centre of a mill contamination. A `QualityManager` quarantines the affected lot, supplying a `quarantine_reason` of between 1 and 200 characters, and the lot moves `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED`. That single action fires a four-hop cascade, and each hop exists because the previous one changed something. **Hop one:** every order line whose `lot_id` is this lot and whose `allocation_status` is `RESERVED` flips back to `UNALLOCATED`; the lot's `quantity_reserved_lb` decreases by that line's `quantity_lb` and its `quantity_on_hand_lb` increases by the same amount, conserving the lot's totals. Unlike the deallocation of §3.5, this hop does not clear the line's references: `lot_id` and `silo_id` keep the values they held, standing as the audit record of what had been picked until some later operation writes them. **Hop two:** because those lines became `UNALLOCATED`, each parent order whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED` or `ROUTED` reverts to `NEW`. **Hop three:** because those orders left `ROUTED`, each of their route stops still in `PLANNED` flips to `CANCELLED`, and the owning route recomputes `planned_load_lb`, `utilization_pct` and `stop_count`. **Hop four:** because `stop_count` has reached zero on a route whose `status` was `SEALED`, that route reverts to `PLANNING` and its `allergen_class_lock` resets to `NONE`. The quarantine also recomputes the storage zone's `on_hand_lb`.

Release is the reverse arc, `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`, and the cycle may repeat as often as quality decides. Release requires that the lot is genuinely `QUARANTINED`, that its zone's `status` is `OPEN` — a zone inside its sanitation lockout blocks release with `ERR_ZONE_LOCKOUT_CONFLICT`, and a zone otherwise not open surfaces as `ERR_RELATED_ENTITY_STATE_INVALID` — and that the lot's `expires_on` is strictly later than the logical clock date, since there is no point returning expired stock to the pick face. Release clears `quarantine_reason` and recomputes the zone's `on_hand_lb`.

Write-off is the other exit from quarantine. A `QualityManager` disposes of a `QUARANTINED` lot, optionally recording a `disposal_note`, but only when the lot's `quantity_reserved_lb` is exactly zero — pounds still promised to an order cannot be written off underneath it. Disposal moves the whole of `quantity_on_hand_lb` into `quantity_consumed_lb`, which is what keeps the conservation sum equal to `quantity_received_lb`, and recomputes the zone's `on_hand_lb`. The lot's status becomes `DISPOSED`.

Two lot transitions have no human actor. Advancing the logical clock to a date on or after a lot's `expires_on` moves that lot from `AVAILABLE` or `QUARANTINED` to `EXPIRED`, and the reserved pounds are released through exactly the same four hops as the quarantine cascade — lines to `UNALLOCATED`, orders back to `NEW`, planned stops to `CANCELLED`, and a sealed route with no stops left back to `PLANNING` with its allergen lock cleared. Separately, when a delivery consumes the last on-hand pound so that an `AVAILABLE` lot holds `quantity_on_hand_lb` of zero and `quantity_reserved_lb` of zero, the lot moves itself to `DEPLETED` and the zone's `on_hand_lb` is recomputed.

Allergen exposure at the bakery is scored, and the score is what drives the hardest of the three segregation gates. The score is a pounds-weighted average of allergen severity, expressed on a nought-to-one-hundred scale. Each allergen class carries a weight: `NONE` weighs nothing at all, `SOY` weighs 0.20, `EGG` weighs 0.35, `DAIRY` weighs 0.40, `GLUTEN` weighs 0.55, `TREE_NUT` weighs 0.90, and `PEANUT` weighs the full 1.00. For the input set of order lines described below, the engine totals each line's weight multiplied by its `quantity_lb`, divides that by the total of `quantity_lb` across the same lines — a divisor that is always positive, because every line quantity is strictly greater than zero — and multiplies by one hundred. To that raw figure it adds a fifteen-point conflict penalty when the bakery's `allergen_program` is `GLUTEN_FREE_CERTIFIED` and any line in the input set carries a product whose `allergen_class` is `GLUTEN`; otherwise it adds nothing. The sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places, once, after the penalty has been added, and then held down to a ceiling of 100.00. When the input set is empty the score is 0.00 rather than undefined. The result is written to `allergen_risk_score`, and `risk_tier` is then re-derived: `GREEN` below 25.00, `AMBER` from 25.00 up to but not including 60.00, and `RED` at 60.00 and above.

The input set has two variants, and the same bakery with the same orders therefore carries two different scores at different points in one order's life. The **in-flight** variant is used on allocation, on deallocation, and on substitution approval; its input set is the order lines whose owning order has `fulfillment_status` in `NEW`, `ALLOCATED`, `ROUTED` or `LOADED`. The **closed-book** variant is used when a stop is delivered or shorted; its input set is the order lines whose owning order has `fulfillment_status` of `DELIVERED` or `SHORT_SHIPPED`, so the audited score never includes in-flight pounds. Both variants exclude the same two things: order lines belonging to orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `CANCELLED`, and order lines whose `allocation_status` is `RELEASED`.

### 3.8. Substitution Proposals and Quality Adjudication

When a committed SKU cannot be shipped — a mill is short, a lot is quarantined, a formulation changes — a `TechnicalSpecialist` proposes a replacement rather than silently swapping stock. Raising a proposal names the `order_line_id` whose SKU is to change, the `proposed_product_id` offered in its place, a `rationale` of between 1 and 500 characters justifying the swap on formulation grounds, and the `expected_order_version` required by §2.6. The new request starts with `status` at `REQUESTED`, `revision_round` at 0, `requested_by_user_id` set to the acting specialist's `X-User-ID`, and `decided_by_user_id` null. Creating a request supersedes any earlier request on the same order line that is still open in `REQUESTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, moving that earlier one to `WITHDRAWN`, and it increments the parent order's `version` by 1 — which in turn invalidates any other specialist's held order version.

Adjudication belongs exclusively to a `QualityManager`, and never to the specialist who raised the proposal. Claiming a request moves it `REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and records the claiming manager in `decided_by_user_id`; it requires that the request is in `REQUESTED`, that the claimant's `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`, and that the parent order's `fulfillment_status` is still `NEW` or `ALLOCATED`. That last condition is the reason a dispatched load is beyond substitution: once an order reaches `LOADED` it is immutable to substitution approval, and any claim or approval against it is rejected with `ERR_RELATED_ENTITY_STATE_INVALID`.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` a manager may send the proposal back for rework. Requesting a revision moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, carries a `revision_note` explaining what must change, and clears `decided_by_user_id` so the next claim starts clean. It is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 2; a third revision attempt is rejected with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. The originating specialist — and only that specialist, identified by an `X-User-ID` equal to `requested_by_user_id` — resubmits, moving the request `REVISION_REQUESTED → REQUESTED`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1, and replacing `proposed_product_id` and `rationale` with the revised values. The full cycle `REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → REQUESTED` may therefore run at most twice.

Approval is the deepest cascade in the service. It requires that the request is in `UNDER_REVIEW`, that the deciding manager's `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`, that the parent order's `fulfillment_status` is `NEW` or `ALLOCATED`, that the parent order's `pricing_status` is not `INVOICED`, and that the proposed product's `allergen_class` is compatible with the bakery's `allergen_program` under the rules of §2.8 — an incompatible swap is rejected with `ERR_ALLERGEN_PROGRAM_CONFLICT`. On success the request moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and five hops fire in order. **Hop one:** the order line's current `product_id` is copied into `substituted_from_product_id`, and `product_id` becomes the request's `proposed_product_id`. **Hop two:** because the line's product changed, any existing `RESERVED` allocation is released — the line's `allocation_status` becomes `RELEASED` and the old lot returns the line's pounds from `quantity_reserved_lb` to `quantity_on_hand_lb` — and the rotation selector of §3.5 re-picks a lot of the new product and re-reserves it; if no eligible lot of the new product exists, the approval is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT`. **Hop three:** because the line's product and unit price changed, the order is repriced through the `PROVISIONAL` self-loop, changing `subtotal_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents` and `total_cents`. **Hop four:** because `total_cents` changed, the bakery's `open_exposure_cents` is recomputed, and if it now exceeds `credit_limit_cents` the bakery moves `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD`. **Hop five:** because the line's allergen class changed, the bakery's `allergen_risk_score` is recomputed on the in-flight basis and `risk_tier` is re-derived. The parent order's `version` increments by 1.

Rejection is the sibling branch out of `UNDER_REVIEW`, and its specification is deliberately narrow. It requires that the request is in `UNDER_REVIEW` and that the rejecting manager's `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`; it optionally carries a `decision_note`; and it moves the request to `REJECTED`. Nothing else changes: the order line keeps its original `product_id`, its `substituted_from_product_id` stays null, its existing reservation and `lot_id` are untouched, the order is not repriced, and neither `open_exposure_cents` nor `allergen_risk_score` is recomputed. No cascade of any kind runs on rejection.

The specialist retains one exit of their own. Withdrawing a request moves it to `WITHDRAWN` from either `REQUESTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and is permitted only when the acting `X-User-ID` equals `requested_by_user_id`; like rejection, it leaves the order line entirely untouched. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the three terminal states of the proposal lifecycle. Requests are readable individually — exposing `revision_round` and both actor identifiers — and as a collection filterable by `order_line_id`, `proposed_product_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Building a Multi-Stop Route and Sealing the Load Plan

A `LogisticsPlanner` opens a route for a service date by supplying a `route_code` of 2 to 16 characters that is globally unique, the `service_on` date the trailer runs, and a `vehicle_capacity_lb` payload capacity of at least 1000 pounds. An optional `service_buffer_minutes` states the minimum idle minutes required between the end of one stop's window and the start of the next, and defaults to 30; it may be zero but never negative. A new route has `status` at `PLANNING`, `allergen_class_lock` at `NONE`, and `planned_load_lb`, `utilization_pct` and `stop_count` all at 0.

Appending a stop names the `order_id` being dropped, the `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` bounding the delivery window, and the `expected_route_version` required by §2.6. Delivery windows are **half-open**: the interval runs from `window_start_at` inclusive to `window_end_at` exclusive. The order must be sitting at `ALLOCATED`, and appending the stop advances it `ALLOCATED → ROUTED`; the route itself must be in `PLANNING`, and a route in any other status rejects the append with `ERR_RELATED_ENTITY_STATE_INVALID`. The new stop begins with `status` at `PLANNED` and `sequence_no` at 0. When the route's `allergen_class_lock` is still `NONE`, appending the first stop sets it from that order's allergen class; once set it is immutable, and every later stop's order lines must all carry that same class or the append is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_ALLERGEN_LOCK_CONFLICT`. Each accepted append re-runs the load waterfall below and increments the route's `version` by 1.

Three temporal checks guard the append, and their adjacency semantics deliberately differ. First, stop windows on one route may not overlap, and they must additionally be separated by at least the route's `service_buffer_minutes`: because windows are half-open, a new window beginning exactly at an existing window's `window_end_at` does not technically overlap, yet it is still rejected unless the gap between that existing end instant and the new start instant is at least the buffer. Either failure is reported as `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Second, a silo's accepted fill window is a **closed** interval running from `last_fill_window_start_at` to `last_fill_window_end_at` with both endpoints included, and a bulk stop whose window shares even a single instant with it — endpoints included, with no buffer applied — is rejected with `ERR_SILO_FILL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Touching endpoints collide here, where on the route they merely fail the buffer test. Third, every stop window must fall inside the route's `service_on` UTC day, and a window outside that day is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_DATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

The load waterfall recomputes three route fields whenever a stop is appended, a stop is cancelled, the route is sealed, or any cascade changes a stop's order. Only stops whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` participate — that is, stops in `PLANNED`, `DELIVERED` or `SHORTED` — and they are processed in `window_start_at` ascending order, then `created_at` ascending; stop identifiers are never used for ordering, and cancelled stops contribute nothing. The route's `stop_count` is the number of participating stops. Its `planned_load_lb` is the total, across those stops, of the `quantity_lb` on every line of each stop's order. The trailer is then modelled as two compartments of 24000 pounds each, and each stop's pounds are poured in sequence: as much as fits goes into the first compartment, the remainder spills into the second, and a single stop's pounds may straddle the two. If any pounds remain unplaced after both compartments are full, the operation is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); the same error is raised when `planned_load_lb` would exceed the route's `vehicle_capacity_lb`. Finally `utilization_pct` is the planned load divided by the vehicle capacity, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Cancelling a planned stop is available on a route that is either `PLANNING` or `SEALED`. The stop's `status` becomes `CANCELLED` and its order steps back `ROUTED → ALLOCATED`, retaining its reservations. The waterfall re-runs, and if `stop_count` reaches zero on a route that was `SEALED`, that route reverts `SEALED → PLANNING` and its `allergen_class_lock` resets to `NONE`. The route's `version` increments by 1.

Sealing the load plan moves the route `PLANNING → SEALED` and locks the stop order in. Six conditions must hold: the route is in `PLANNING`; `stop_count` is at least 1; `utilization_pct` is no greater than 100, with exactly 100.00 permitted to seal; every stop's order has `fulfillment_status` equal to `ROUTED`; every stop's order has a `total_cents` that reaches that bakery's own `min_drop_cents`, failing which the seal is rejected with `ERR_MIN_DROP_NOT_MET`; and every bulk stop's order agrees with the trailer, meaning the route's `allergen_class_lock` equals that order's single allergen class, failing which the seal is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_ALLERGEN_LOCK_CONFLICT`. A seal that would breach the compartment waterfall is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Sealing assigns each stop's `sequence_no` as a one-based position ordered by `window_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and increments the route's `version` by 1. Unsealing reverses it: a `SEALED` route returns to `PLANNING`, every stop's `sequence_no` is cleared back to 0, and `version` increments by 1. The same reversal is what fires automatically as the fourth hop of the quarantine cascade, and when it is triggered by `stop_count` reaching zero the `allergen_class_lock` resets to `NONE` as well.

### 3.10. Dispatch, the Full Drop and the Short Drop

Dispatch happens on the day, not before it. A `LogisticsPlanner` moves a route `SEALED → DISPATCHED` only when the logical clock date equals the route's `service_on`; any other date is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_DATE_MISMATCH`. Every stop's order must also carry a `pricing_status` of `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` at that moment. Dispatch advances each stop's order `ROUTED → LOADED` and increments the route's `version` by 1. From `LOADED` onward the order is immutable to substitution approval, as §3.8 explains.

A dispatched route can still be recalled, but only before anything has been dropped. Aborting requires the route to be `DISPATCHED` and requires that no stop has yet reached `DELIVERED` or `SHORTED`; the planner supplies an `abort_reason`. The route moves to `ABORTED`, every stop still in `PLANNED` becomes `CANCELLED`, every affected order steps back `LOADED → ALLOCATED` with its reservations retained rather than released, and `stop_count`, `planned_load_lb` and `utilization_pct` are all recomputed to 0. The route's `version` increments by 1. `ABORTED` and `COMPLETED` are the two terminal states of the route lifecycle.

At the bakery's dock, a `WarehouseOperator` confirms each drop, and the two possible confirmations are genuine siblings with materially different consequences. **A full drop** requires the order to be `LOADED`, the stop to be `PLANNED`, and the route to be `DISPATCHED`. It moves the stop to `DELIVERED` and the order `LOADED → DELIVERED`. Every line's `allocation_status` becomes `SHIPPED`, and each line's picked lot moves the line's pounds out of `quantity_reserved_lb` and into `quantity_consumed_lb`. On bulk lines, the destination silo's `residual_lb` increases by the line's `quantity_lb`, and the silo's `last_fill_window_start_at` and `last_fill_window_end_at` are set from that stop's window — which is what makes future stops on that silo subject to the closed-interval collision test of §3.9. The order's `delivered_on` is stamped with the logical clock date, at which point the order becomes eligible for final pricing. Any lot that now holds zero on-hand and zero reserved pounds auto-transitions to `DEPLETED`. The bakery's `allergen_risk_score` is recomputed on the closed-book basis and its `risk_tier` re-derived. When every non-cancelled stop on the route has reached `DELIVERED` or `SHORTED` — equivalently, when no stop remains in `PLANNED` — the route moves `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED`, recomputing `planned_load_lb` and `utilization_pct` from delivered pounds only and incrementing `version` by 1.

**A short drop** shares those three preconditions — order `LOADED`, stop `PLANNED`, route `DISPATCHED` — and adds one of its own. The operator supplies a `delivered_lb_total` and a `short_reason`; the delivered total must be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than the total of `quantity_lb` across the order's lines, since a zero drop is an abort and a complete drop is a full delivery. A `delivered_lb_total` outside that open range is rejected with `ERR_SHORT_QUANTITY_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The stop moves to `SHORTED` and the order `LOADED → SHORT_SHIPPED`, with `short_ship_lb` set to the order's total requested pounds less the delivered total. Every line's `allocation_status` becomes `SHIPPED` here too, but the pound movement differs from the full drop in a way that matters: only the delivered pounds move from `quantity_reserved_lb` into `quantity_consumed_lb`, while the shorted pounds return to `quantity_on_hand_lb` and become pickable again. Additionally — and this has no counterpart in the full drop — each affected contract line's `volume_drawn_lb` is reduced by the shorted pounds for that product, restoring the committed-pounds invariant of §2.9. The order's `delivered_on` is stamped with the logical clock date, the bakery's `allergen_risk_score` is recomputed on the closed-book basis, and the route moves `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED` under the same aggregate condition as above. Both `DELIVERED` and `SHORT_SHIPPED` are terminal on the fulfillment dimension, and both leave the commercial dimension open for settlement.

Routes and stops are readable throughout: routes individually and as a collection filterable by `service_on` and `status`, stops per route and across routes filterable by `route_id`, `order_id` and `status`, with each stop exposing its assigned `sequence_no`.

### 3.11. Formulation Support, Final Settlement and Invoicing

Dawn's technical team earns its keep on bench work, and that work is billed through engagements rather than absorbed. A `TechnicalSpecialist` opens an engagement by naming the `customer_id` receiving support and the `product_id` the formulation work targets. The engagement begins with `status` at `OPEN`, `hours_logged` at 0, `attach_fee_cents` at 0, `order_id` null, and `assigned_specialist_user_id` set to the acting specialist's `X-User-ID`.

Bench time is posted incrementally: each posting carries an `hours` figure strictly greater than zero and an optional `work_note`, and it adds to the running `hours_logged`, which is carried at one decimal place. The first four hours are the qualifying threshold — the moment `hours_logged` reaches 4.0, the boundary being inclusive, an `OPEN` engagement moves itself to `BILLABLE` and the attach fee is computed. Every subsequent posting while the engagement is `BILLABLE` recomputes the fee.

The fee itself is marginally tiered. The first two logged hours are included at no charge, so billable hours are `hours_logged` less two, floored at zero. Of those billable hours, the first eight bill at one hundred eighty-five dollars per hour — 18500 cents — and every billable hour beyond the eighth bills at the lower rate of one hundred forty-five dollars per hour, or 14500 cents. The gross fee is the sum of those two bands. A risk multiplier then applies: when the owning bakery's `risk_tier` as persisted at computation time reads `RED`, the gross is multiplied by one and a quarter, reflecting the additional oversight a high-exposure allergen account demands; for `GREEN` and `AMBER` accounts the multiplier is one. The result is rounded half-up to whole cents, once, after the multiplier has been applied, and written to `attach_fee_cents`.

Attaching the fee to an order moves the engagement `BILLABLE → ATTACHED` and is the specialist's own act: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `assigned_specialist_user_id`. The target order is named by an `order_id` in the request, must belong to the same bakery as the engagement — a mismatch between the engagement's `customer_id` and the order's `customer_id` is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` — and must have `pricing_status` exactly `PROVISIONAL`. Attaching to a `FINAL` or `INVOICED` order is a cross-dimension violation rejected with `ERR_ORDER_DIMENSION_GATE`, because a settled order's totals no longer move. Attachment sets the engagement's `order_id`, copies `attach_fee_cents` into the order's `tech_attach_fee_cents`, reprices the order through the `PROVISIONAL` self-loop so the fee lands inside `total_cents`, recomputes the bakery's `open_exposure_cents`, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Not every engagement gets billed. An `AccountManager` may waive one, supplying a `waiver_reason`; waiving is permitted from either `OPEN` or `BILLABLE`, requires the engagement's `order_id` to be null so nothing already billed is being retracted, and sets `attach_fee_cents` to 0. `ATTACHED` and `WAIVED` are the two terminal states of the engagement lifecycle. Engagements are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `customer_id`, `product_id`, `order_id` and `status`.

Final settlement is the `PricingController`'s move, taking an order `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`. It requires the order to be `PROVISIONAL` on the commercial dimension and to have physically closed — `fulfillment_status` of `DELIVERED` or `SHORT_SHIPPED` — and finalizing before the load has moved is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_DIMENSION_GATE`. When the order's contract prices on the delivery date, a published quote with an `effective_on` at or before the order's `delivered_on` must exist for every index the order's lines price against, failing which the finalization is rejected with `ERR_INDEX_QUOTE_MISSING`. Finalization re-runs the settlement price for every line under the delivery-date observation described in §3.4 — producing per-pound prices that may legitimately differ from the provisional ones — then re-extends the lines, re-runs the order-level waterfall, recomputes the bakery's `open_exposure_cents`, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

Invoicing closes the book, moving the order `FINAL → INVOICED`. The order must be `FINAL`, the acting controller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `customer_id`, and every support engagement billed onto this order must have reached `ATTACHED`; an engagement still sitting in `OPEN`, `BILLABLE` or any other status blocks the invoice with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ATTACHED` (HTTP 409). Invoicing freezes `total_cents` — any subsequent repricing attempt is rejected — and recomputes `open_exposure_cents`, from which the now-invoiced order drops out entirely. That recomputation is often what rescues an account: a bakery sitting on `CREDIT_HOLD` whose recomputed exposure has fallen to or below its `credit_limit_cents` returns to `ACTIVE`, and its buyer can submit again. The order's `version` increments by 1, and `INVOICED` is terminal on the commercial dimension.
