# Recreational Tennis League API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community tennis leagues run on trust that nobody can quite audit. A player declares a level, a captain fills a lineup, a scorecard arrives by text message, and by September nobody can reconstruct which of those decisions was legitimate. The administrative burden falls on volunteers: section administrators who own the rating epoch, league coordinators who own the calendar, captains who own their rosters, and a review committee that has to adjudicate the inevitable complaints without any of its members quietly deciding a matter they are personally involved in. This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets and email threads with an auditable system of record.

The service administers a **Section** — the rating and discipline authority for one playing year — and everything beneath it: a self-referential ladder of leagues progressing from local play through district play to section play, the facilities whose courts constrain the calendar, the players whose NTRP levels gate every roster decision, the teams and roster spots that bind the two together, the fixtures and their two independent status dimensions, the scorecards that report them, the strikes that punish violations, and the committee machinery that hears rating appeals and grievances. Three properties are non-negotiable throughout. First, **provenance**: every rating a player carries records how it was obtained, and every change to it appends to an immutable history. Second, **conservation**: the strike ledger and the league result ledger are both conserved quantities, so an action and its compensating action must leave the books exactly where they started. Third, **segregation of duties**: a captain may not verify their own scorecard, a committee member may not vote on a matter in which they are an interested party, and a chair may not decide a subject on which they cast a vote.

Everything the service exposes is mounted under the `/tennis` prefix. Time is a first-class input rather than an ambient fact — registration windows, scorecard submission deadlines, dispute windows and grievance windows are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock, which makes the entire temporal surface of the system deterministically testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-Role`, naming the role the caller is acting in, and `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller. There is no session, no token and no login; the headers are the whole of the authentication story.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `SectionAdmin`, `LeagueCoordinator`, `Captain`, `Player`, `CommitteeMember` and `CommitteeChair`. If `X-User-Role` is absent or empty the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER` (HTTP 401). If it is present but is not one of those six exact strings, the refusal is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but the endpoint being called does not list it among its authorised roles, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide into two identity families, and the distinction determines whether `X-User-ID` participates in ownership checks at all.

`SectionAdmin` and `LeagueCoordinator` are **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `SectionAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `LeagueCoordinator`, the same holds: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier, not resolved against the `Player` table and not used in any ownership comparison. A section administrator owns section setup, facility creation, league creation, opening registration, player enrolment and the two-phase year-end rating recalculation, and additionally drives the logical clock; the administrator never reports a result and never decides an appeal or a grievance. A league coordinator owns the competition calendar — fixtures, slots, marking matches played, recording defaults — plus the league lifecycle from roster lock onward, coordinator-assigned computer ratings, and coordinator-issued strikes; the coordinator cannot create sections, leagues or facilities, and cannot decide committee matters.

The remaining four roles are **entity-backed** by the `Player` table. When `X-User-Role` is `Captain`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Player` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a captain may only act for a team whose `captain_player_id` equals `X-User-ID` (and, for match-level and scorecard-level actions, only where `X-User-ID` captains one of the two sides of the match). When `X-User-Role` is `Player`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player`, and a `Player`-role actor may only touch its own player record. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player`, used to enforce one-vote-per-subject and the interested-party bar. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player`, recorded onto decided subjects as `decided_by_chair_player_id` and used to enforce the vote-then-decide bar.

Because those four roles resolve to a real row, `X-User-ID` must be present and must resolve to an existing `Player` whenever `X-User-Role` is `Captain`, `Player`, `CommitteeMember` or `CommitteeChair`. A missing or unresolvable identifier under one of those four roles is refused with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401). A further gate applies to writes: an entity-backed actor whose own `Player` row is in status `DISQUALIFIED` may not perform any write operation, and is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_DISQUALIFIED` (HTTP 403). Note carefully that `Captain` and `Player` are distinct roles even when the same `Player` row is behind both: the `Player` role carries no team authority whatsoever, and a person who captains a team must present `X-User-Role: Captain` to exercise roster and reporting rights.

Three ownership refusals recur across the specification and are named here once. A `Player`-role actor addressing a player record other than its own is refused with `ERR_NOT_SELF` (HTTP 403) — this guards the self-rating operation and the filing of a rating appeal. A `Captain`-role actor addressing a team it does not captain, or a match neither of whose sides it captains, is refused with `ERR_NOT_TEAM_CAPTAIN` (HTTP 403) — this guards roster-spot creation, roster-spot activation, roster-spot removal, team registration, team withdrawal, match confirmation, scorecard submission, scorecard review, scorecard amendment, grievance filing and grievance withdrawal. A `CommitteeMember` voting on a subject in which it is an interested party is refused with `ERR_SOD_INTERESTED_PARTY` (HTTP 403), and a `CommitteeChair` deciding a subject on which it has itself cast a vote is refused with `ERR_CHAIR_VOTED_ON_SUBJECT` (HTTP 403). Section 2.9 fixes where these checks sit relative to each other and to everything else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the league standings listing, which is ordered by the playoff ranking keys described in §3.9 rather than by creation time.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` (the page size actually applied), `offset` (the starting position actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses carry only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` in `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client; a client that invents one is simply describing a row that does not exist. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped once at insertion and never rewritten.

Eleven of the twelve resource kinds additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each subsequent mutation — including mutations the client did not directly request, such as a counter recomputation triggered by a cascade from another entity. `Section`, `League`, `Facility`, `Player`, `Team`, `RosterSpot`, `Match`, `Scorecard`, `Strike`, `RatingAppeal` and `Grievance` all behave this way.

`CommitteeVote` is the exception. A committee vote is an append-only record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, because it is never modified after it is cast.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Temporal Evaluation

Every temporal rule in this specification is evaluated against a single server-held logical clock, referred to throughout as the current instant. The server starts with the logical clock at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before the clock has ever been set, the current instant reads that default, and at no point does the service fall back to wall-clock time. This makes registration windows, submission deadlines, dispute windows and grievance windows reproducible.

A section administrator may move the clock forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the administrative clock-setting capability. The clock may only ever move forward: if the supplied instant is earlier than the clock's present value, the request is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Supplying an instant equal to the present value is not a backwards move and is accepted. This capability returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than a resource.

Every stored instant in the system — `registration_opens_at`, `registration_closes_at`, `roster_lock_at`, `season_end_at`, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at`, `verified_at`, `decided_at`, `created_at`, `updated_at` — is an ISO 8601 UTC instant. Two renderings are in play and which one a field carries is fixed. Every instant the service writes itself — `created_at` and `updated_at`, `verified_at`, `decided_at`, the `recorded_at` of a `rating_history` entry, and the `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` a scheduling request writes onto a match — is rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a `Z` suffix, so a start instant supplied as `2026-08-15T18:00:00Z` is stored and returned as `2026-08-15T18:00:00.000Z`. The four league window instants — `registration_opens_at`, `registration_closes_at`, `roster_lock_at` and `season_end_at` — are the exception: they are retained exactly as the creating request supplied them. Comparisons always read the instant itself and never its spelling. Window comparisons throughout the specification are stated with explicit inclusivity, and that inclusivity is part of the contract, not a rounding detail.

### 2.6. Level Vocabulary, Rating Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

NTRP levels in this system are drawn from a fixed six-member vocabulary: 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5 and 5.0. Any level supplied or requested by a client must be a multiple of 0.5 lying within 2.5 and 5.0 inclusive; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RATING_LEVEL` (HTTP 422). The floor of the vocabulary is 2.5 and its ceiling is 5.0, and every arithmetic operation that moves a level — an appeal grant, a committee-assigned bump, a year-end recalculation step — is clamped inside those bounds rather than allowed to run past them.

Alongside the coarse level, players carry a fine-grained `dynamic_rating`. Dynamic ratings are computed to **3 decimal places using round-half-up**. The rounding *order* is part of the contract and is stated in full in §3.2: each individual line rating is rounded to three decimals before any averaging occurs, and the resulting weighted mean is then rounded to three decimals again. Averaging unrounded line ratings and rounding only once at the end produces a different number and is wrong.

Two other numeric domains behave differently and are called out so that no implementer applies decimal rounding where none belongs. Standings arithmetic — matches, lines, sets, games and points — is pure integer arithmetic with no rounding of any kind, and the ledger invariants of §2.11 are exact equalities with no tolerance. Court and calendar arithmetic works in whole minutes, with instants compared as UTC instants; the calendar-date form of the team conflict rule truncates an instant to its UTC calendar date. Where the appeal auto-grant evaluation of §3.8 computes a gap and a threshold, both are rounded to three decimals with round-half-up *before* being compared, and the comparison is inclusive, so a gap exactly equal to the threshold qualifies.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Rosters and Slots

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent entity. Both require the client to declare which version of the parent it believes it is acting against, and both reject a declaration that no longer matches.

Adding a player to a team roster — `POST /tennis/roster-spots` — requires the non-optional input field `expected_team_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Team`. Roster composition is a property of the whole roster rather than of one spot: an earlier accepted add, an activation, a removal or a cascaded ineligibility has already moved `active_roster_size`, `roster_compliant` and the headroom left under `max_roster_size`. A request whose `expected_team_version` does not equal the target team's current `version` is therefore deciding against a roster that no longer exists, and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TEAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Writing the slot of a match — `POST /tennis/matches/{id}/schedule` — requires the non-optional input field `expected_match_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Match`. Scheduling legitimately supersedes whatever slot the match previously held, so this operation cannot be filtered out by a state precondition the way most conflicting writes can; the version declaration is the only thing that distinguishes a client rescheduling the slot it actually read from a client overwriting a slot written after its read. A request whose `expected_match_version` does not equal the match's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

If either of those two operations omits its version field entirely, the request is refused earlier, with `ERR_MISSING_EXPECTED_VERSION` (HTTP 400) — a missing declaration is a malformed request, not a stale one. No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and supplying one where it is not defined is an unknown field.

### 2.8. The Section-Wide Rating Epoch

A `Section` holds a `rating_period_status` that acts as a section-wide posture gate on rating writes and roster edits. It takes exactly three values: `OPEN_SEASON`, `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS` and `RECALC_PUBLISHED`. A newly created section starts at `OPEN_SEASON`.

While the section sits at `OPEN_SEASON`, ratings may be written and rosters may be edited. Once the section leaves `OPEN_SEASON` — in either of the two later postures — the rating period is closed, and five operations are refused with `ERR_RATING_PERIOD_CLOSED` (HTTP 409): assigning a rating to a player, adding a roster spot, activating a roster spot, a captain removing a roster spot, and granting a rating appeal. Both `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS` and `RECALC_PUBLISHED` close the period; there is no partial reopening.

The posture also gates the two phases of the year-end recalculation themselves, in the opposite direction. Beginning a recalculation requires the section to be at `OPEN_SEASON`, and publishing one requires the section to be at `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS`; a request that arrives against the wrong posture is refused with `ERR_RECALC_PHASE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Alongside the posture the section keeps `recalc_run_index`, a monotone counter that increases by 1 each time a recalculation is published, and it starts at 0.

One review operation is deliberately permitted in a partially closed epoch: opening a rating appeal to committee review is allowed while the section is at either `OPEN_SEASON` or `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS`, so that appeals filed before a recalculation began can still reach the committee. Only the *grant* is blocked once the period closes.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence — Which Error the Client Actually Sees

Many requests fail more than one check at once. The service does not report the first failure it happens to notice; it reports the failure with the **lowest priority number** in the ordering below, and the priority ordering is a fixed part of the contract. The ordering runs in ten groups: authentication and authorisation first, then existence, then payload shape, then concurrency, then state preconditions, then business safety, then business referential integrity, then business constraints, then temporal windows, and finally quota and capacity rules. Priorities are contiguous, and the lowest matching one wins.

The full ladder, from highest precedence to lowest, is:

1. `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER` — `X-User-Role` present and non-empty on every request.
2. `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` — `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles.
3. `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` — `X-User-ID` present and resolving to a `Player` for the four entity-backed roles.
4. `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list.
5. `ERR_ACTOR_DISQUALIFIED` — the acting entity-backed player is not `DISQUALIFIED`, on every write.
6. `ERR_NOT_SELF` — a `Player`-role actor acts only on its own record.
7. `ERR_NOT_TEAM_CAPTAIN` — a `Captain`-role actor acts only for a team it captains.
8. `ERR_SOD_INTERESTED_PARTY` — a committee member is not an interested party in the subject it votes on.
9. `ERR_CHAIR_VOTED_ON_SUBJECT` — a chair has not voted on the subject it is deciding.
10. `ERR_NOT_FOUND` — every path identifier resolves, validated left to right.
11. `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` — every foreign key carried in the payload resolves.
12. `ERR_SECTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` — a resolved payload foreign key belongs to the same section as the target.
13. `ERR_VALIDATION` — required fields present, no unknown fields, every enum value a declared member.
14. `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` — `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0.
15. `ERR_INVALID_RATING_LEVEL` — any supplied or requested level is a multiple of 0.5 within 2.5 to 5.0.
16. `ERR_SCORECARD_LINE_INVALID` — scorecard line structure and set scores are legal.
17. `ERR_MISSING_EXPECTED_VERSION` — the OCC field is present on the two guarded writes.
18. `ERR_STALE_TEAM_VERSION` — `expected_team_version` equals the team's `version`.
19. `ERR_STALE_MATCH_VERSION` — `expected_match_version` equals the match's `version`.
20. `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — a transition from the resource's current state is declared for this action.
21. `ERR_LEAGUE_PHASE_FORBIDS_ROSTER_CHANGE` — the league's lifecycle stage admits this registration or roster change.
22. `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_IN_PLAY` — the league's lifecycle stage admits match creation, scheduling, confirmation or marking played.
23. `ERR_LEAGUE_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — the league is not `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, and the seeding or advancement action matches the current stage.
24. `ERR_MATCH_SCHEDULE_STATE_INVALID` — `schedule_status` admits the action.
25. `ERR_MATCH_RESULT_STATE_INVALID` — `result_status` admits the action.
26. `ERR_SCORECARD_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION` — `Scorecard.status` admits the action.
27. `ERR_RECALC_PHASE_INVALID` — the section's recalculation posture admits the phase being run.
28. `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION` — `RatingAppeal.status` admits the action.
29. `ERR_GRIEVANCE_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION` — `Grievance.status` admits the action.
30. `ERR_SUBJECT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` — a vote's subject is `UNDER_REVIEW`.
31. `ERR_TEAM_STATE_INVALID` — `Team.status` admits the action.
32. `ERR_TEAM_ROSTER_NONCOMPLIANT` — both sides of a match are roster-compliant at confirmation.
33. `ERR_RATING_PERIOD_CLOSED` — the section's rating epoch is `OPEN_SEASON` for a rating write, roster change or appeal grant.
34. `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_PLAYABLE` — a suspended or disqualified player is neither rostered nor fielded.
35. `ERR_AGE_GROUP_NOT_MET` — the player meets the league's age-group minimum.
36. `ERR_RATING_LEVEL_EXCEEDS_LEAGUE` — the player is rated and sits inside the league's rating band.
37. `ERR_FORMAT_GENDER_MISMATCH` — the player's gender is admissible for the league's format.
38. `ERR_SELF_RATING_LOCKED` — a self-rating is only accepted on an unrated or self-rated record.
39. `ERR_INELIGIBLE_LINEUP` — every named player holds an active spot on the side fielding the line, and appears once.
40. `ERR_PLAYER_DISQUALIFIED_PERMANENT` — a rescission is not asked to undo a disqualification.
41. `ERR_TEAM_LEAGUE_MISMATCH` — both sides of a fixture belong to the match's league and differ from each other.
42. `ERR_FACILITY_SECTION_MISMATCH` — the facility belongs to the same section as the league.
43. `ERR_LEAGUE_LADDER_INVALID` — the parent-league reference satisfies the ladder rule.
44. `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROSTER_SPOT` — the player holds at most one live spot per league.
45. `ERR_SUBJECT_TYPE_MISMATCH` — a vote's subject identifier resolves inside the entity its subject type names.
46. `ERR_GRIEVANCE_SUBJECT_INVALID` — the grievance payload is coherent for its kind.
47. `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_ADMISSIBLE` — the remedy chosen at uphold is admissible for the grievance kind and target state.
48. `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN` — the player holds at most one open rating appeal.
49. `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE` — a committee member votes at most once per subject.
50. `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` — the scorecard has not exhausted its amendments.
51. `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN` — review is by the non-submitting captain and amendment by the submitting captain.
52. `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_OPEN` — the match has at most one open scorecard.
53. `ERR_FACILITY_COURTS_EXCEEDED` — the facility has court capacity for the requested slot.
54. `ERR_TEAM_DATE_CONFLICT` — neither side already holds a conflicting fixture.
55. `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` — a committee decision that needs votes has them.
56. `ERR_APPEAL_BASELINE_STALE` — the appellant's level still equals the level pinned at filing.
57. `ERR_ROSTER_SPOT_HAS_RESULTS` and `ERR_TEAM_HAS_VERIFIED_RESULTS` — nothing carrying a verified result is unwound by removal or withdrawal.
58. `ERR_AUTO_GRANT_MUST_BE_GRANTED` — an auto-grant-eligible appeal is not denied.
59. `ERR_NO_PLAYOFF_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS` — seeding finds at least two candidates and advancement follows a completed seeding.
60. `ERR_OUTSIDE_REGISTRATION_WINDOW` — team registration lands inside the registration window.
61. `ERR_MATCH_START_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` — the requested start instant is future-dated and on the correct side of the season end.
62. `ERR_SCORECARD_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — the scorecard lands inside the submission window.
63. `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — a dispute lands inside the dispute window.
64. `ERR_MATCH_NOT_YET_PLAYABLE` — marking played or defaulting is not premature.
65. `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — the grievance lands inside the grievance window.
66. `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` — the logical clock never moves backwards.
67. `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_EXCEEDED` — the roster stays within its maximum size.
68. `ERR_LEAGUE_TEAM_CAPACITY_REACHED` — the league stays within its team quota.
69. `ERR_ROSTER_QUOTA_NOT_MET` — team registration, roster lock and start of play meet their minimum active-spot and team counts.
70. `ERR_ADVANCEMENT_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` — advancement fits within the parent league's remaining capacity.

Three of these pairings deserve explicit attention because a single malformed request can trip both members, and only the lower number is reported. Submitting a scorecard against a match whose `result_status` forbids reporting *and* doing so after the submission window has closed reports `ERR_MATCH_RESULT_STATE_INVALID`, not `ERR_SCORECARD_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, because the state check at rank 25 outranks the temporal check at rank 62. Activating a spot for an under-age player on a roster that is already at its maximum size reports `ERR_AGE_GROUP_NOT_MET`, not `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_EXCEEDED`, because the safety check at rank 35 outranks the capacity check at rank 67. And scheduling a match at a facility belonging to another section on a date where the team already plays reports `ERR_FACILITY_SECTION_MISMATCH`, not `ERR_TEAM_DATE_CONFLICT`, because the referential check at rank 42 outranks the calendar check at rank 54.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution and Section Scoping

Several read and action paths nest one resource inside another. Path identifiers are always resolved **left to right**, and the first identifier that fails to resolve is the one reported: a request naming a section that does not exist is refused with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before anything nested beneath it is examined. The same code covers any unresolvable path identifier at any depth.

Payload references are treated separately. A foreign key carried in a request body that does not resolve to an existing row of the entity it names is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A payload foreign key that *does* resolve but belongs to a different `Section` than the target resource is refused with `ERR_SECTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 404): players, leagues, facilities and teams never cross section boundaries. This scoping rule guards league creation, facility creation, team creation, roster-spot creation, fixture creation and strike issuance.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and the Two Conserved Ledgers

A great deal of this system's behaviour is cascade. One accepted request routinely mutates several entities: a strike moves a player's status, which moves roster spots, which move team counters, which move a league counter. Three rules govern all of it. Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` increased by 1, even when the client never named that entity. Cascades are applied within the same request as the action that triggered them, so a caller never observes a half-propagated state. And every derived counter named in this document is recomputed from its underlying rows rather than incrementally patched, so a recomputation is always idempotent.

Two quantities in this system are **conserved**, and they conserve different things.

The first is the **weighted strike ledger** of a player, described in §3.7. It conserves violation weight: issuing a strike and then rescinding that same strike must leave the player's `strike_count` exactly where it started, must leave no roster spot pointing at the rescinded strike, must return the count of spots made ineligible by suspension to its pre-issue value, and must return each affected team's `active_roster_size` and `roster_compliant` to their pre-issue values. There is exactly one documented asymmetry, and it is deliberate: once a player has reached `DISQUALIFIED`, that status floor is permanent. A later rescission still lowers `strike_count` correctly, but the player's status is not restored and neither are the roster spots.

The second is the **league result ledger**, described in §3.6. It conserves match, line, set and game credit across every team of a league, and it holds as four exact equalities at every observation point. Consider the set of *decided* matches of a league — those whose `result_status` is `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED` and whose `winner_team_id` is not null. First, summing `matches_won` across every team of the league and adding the sum of `matches_lost` across every team yields exactly twice the number of decided matches. Second, the sum of `lines_won` across every team equals the sum of `lines_lost` across every team. Third, the sum of `sets_won` equals the sum of `sets_lost`, and the sum of `games_won` equals the sum of `games_lost`. Fourth, the sum of `standings_points` across every team equals three times the number of decided matches plus the sum of `lines_won` across every team. These four equalities are asserted after every result-status transition, after every recorded default, and after every forfeit remedy, and they are exact — no tolerance is permitted. A double default is the interesting boundary: because it leaves `winner_team_id` null, it does not increase the decided count and it adds zero to every sum, so all four equalities continue to hold.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Section: The Rating Authority, Its Facilities and Its Progression Ladder

Everything begins with a `Section`, which is at once a geographic administrative body and a rating authority for one playing year. A section administrator creates it with a `code` — a short identifier such as `SOCAL-2026`, between 3 and 32 characters long and unique across all sections — a human-readable `name`, and a `season_year` between 2000 and 2100 inclusive, which is the playing year the section administers. A player's *league age* is derived arithmetically from that year: it is the section's `season_year` less the player's `birth_year`.

The rest of a section's fields are policy dials, and each has a default that applies when the creating request omits it. `min_matches_for_playoff_eligibility` is the number of verified team matches a roster spot must accumulate before it counts toward playoff eligibility; it defaults to 3 and must be at least 1. `strike_limit` is the weighted strike total at which a player is suspended; it defaults to 3 and must be at least 1. `disqualification_strike_limit` is the weighted total at which a player is disqualified for the season; it defaults to 6 and must be strictly greater than `strike_limit`. Three window dials govern deadlines and are all expressed in hours: `scorecard_submission_window_hours` defaults to 48 (two days after the scheduled end of a match, within which a scorecard may be submitted), `scorecard_dispute_window_hours` defaults to 24 (one day after a scorecard is submitted, within which the opposing captain may dispute it), and `grievance_window_hours` defaults to 72 (three days, within which a grievance may be filed). Two further counters are maintained by the service rather than supplied: `recalculated_player_count` records how many players had a proposed level written by the most recent year-end recalculation run, and `published_level_change_count` records how many players' `rating_level` actually changed when that run was published. Both start at 0. Sections may be listed and filtered by `code`, by `season_year` and by `rating_period_status`.

**Facilities** hold the courts. A section administrator creates a `Facility` against a `section_id`, giving it a `name` and a `court_count` of at least 1 — the aggregate capacity that every scheduling request is measured against. Two optional fields shape its behaviour: `turnaround_buffer_minutes`, defaulting to 30 and required to be zero or greater, is appended to the end of every match occupancy interval at that facility, and `surface` is one of `HARD`, `CLAY` or `INDOOR_HARD`, defaulting to `HARD`. The facilities of a section can be listed as a filtered collection, narrowable by `surface`.

**Leagues** are where the competitive structure lives, and a league is a point on a self-referential ladder. Each `League` belongs to a `section_id` and declares a `tier` of `LOCAL`, `DISTRICT` or `SECTION`, an `ntrp_level` drawn from the six-member level vocabulary, an `age_group` of `AGE_18_OVER`, `AGE_40_OVER` or `AGE_55_OVER`, and a `format` of `MENS_DOUBLES`, `WOMENS_DOUBLES` or `MIXED_DOUBLES`. The `parent_league_id` names the next rung up the ladder, and the rule binding it is strict: it must be null exactly when `tier` is `SECTION`, and non-null otherwise; the referenced league must live in the same section, carry the identical `ntrp_level`, the identical `age_group` and the identical `format`, and sit exactly one step above the child — a `LOCAL` league's parent is a `DISTRICT` league, and a `DISTRICT` league's parent is a `SECTION` league. Any violation of that composite rule — wrong tier distance, mismatched level, mismatched age group, mismatched format, a null where one is required or a value where null is required — is refused with `ERR_LEAGUE_LADDER_INVALID`. Leagues may be listed filtered by `section_id`, by `parent_league_id`, by `tier`, by `ntrp_level`, by `age_group`, by `format` and by `status`, which makes every rung of the ladder reachable in both directions.

A league's competitive shape comes from eight optional dials. `lines_per_match` is the number of doubles lines contested in one team match; it defaults to 3 and must be an odd integer between 3 and 5, which is to say either 3 or 5 — the oddness matters, because it is what prevents a played match from ending level on lines. `min_active_roster_size` defaults to 8 and is the number of active roster spots a team needs in order to be considered roster-compliant. `max_roster_size` defaults to 14 and caps how many roster spots a single team may hold in the pending or active states combined. `min_playoff_eligible_players` defaults to 6 and is the number of playoff-eligible active spots a team needs before it can enter the seeding candidate set. `max_teams` defaults to 8 and is the registration quota, counting teams in the registered, roster-locked, in-play, eliminated and advanced states. `advancement_slots` defaults to 1 and is how many teams may be promoted into the parent league. `match_duration_minutes` defaults to 180 — three hours — and is the nominal court occupancy of one team match, excluding facility turnaround. Finally `allow_same_day_doubleheader` is a boolean feature flag defaulting to false, and it switches the team calendar-conflict rule between two entirely different geometries, as §3.5 explains.

Four instants complete the league definition and they must be supplied in a consistent order: `registration_opens_at` and `registration_closes_at` bracket the registration window, with the close strictly after the open; `roster_lock_at` is the earliest instant from which rosters may be locked and must be at or after `registration_closes_at`; and `season_end_at` is the last instant at which a regular-season match may be scheduled to start and must be strictly after `roster_lock_at`. A newly created league begins at `status` `DRAFT` with every derived counter at 0. Those counters are `registered_team_count` (teams of this league in the registered, roster-locked, in-play, eliminated or advanced states), `noncompliant_team_count` (teams of this league in the roster-locked or in-play states whose `roster_compliant` is false), `finalized_match_count` (matches of this league whose `result_status` is `FINALIZED`) and `seeded_team_count` (teams of this league whose `playoff_seed` is not null).

**Players** are enrolled by either a section administrator or a league coordinator against a `section_id`, with a `full_name`, a `birth_year` between 1900 and 2020 inclusive, and a `gender` of `M` or `F` — the latter feeding the format eligibility rule of §3.3. A freshly enrolled player is deliberately incomplete: `rating_type` is `UNRATED`, `rating_level` is null, `dynamic_rating` is null, `strike_count` is 0, `rating_history` is an empty array, and `status` is `ACTIVE`. Players may be listed filtered by `section_id`, by `status`, by `rating_type` and by `gender`.

### 3.2. Rating Provenance and the Dynamic Rating Engine

A player's effective NTRP level is `rating_level`, and every eligibility check in the system reads it. Standing beside it is `rating_type`, which records *how* that level was obtained and takes one of five values: `UNRATED`, `SELF_RATED`, `COMPUTER_RATED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED` or `COMMITTEE_ASSIGNED`. While `rating_type` is `UNRATED` the `rating_level` is null. The pair is never allowed to drift: provenance and value move together, always.

Assigning a rating requires a `source` and a `level`. Two sources exist, and they belong to different roles. `source` `SELF` is the one-time player self-rating: it may only be exercised by a `Player`-role actor on its own record, and it is only admissible while the target's `rating_type` is `UNRATED` or already `SELF_RATED`. Once a player has become `COMPUTER_RATED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED` or `COMMITTEE_ASSIGNED`, a self-rating attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_RATING_LOCKED`; from that point only a coordinator-sourced computer rating may overwrite the level. A successful self-rating writes the declared value into both `rating_level` and `self_rated_level`, the latter retained permanently for audit even after a later reassignment moves the effective level away from it, and sets `rating_type` to `SELF_RATED`. `source` `COMPUTER` is the coordinator-assigned computer rating: it requires the `LeagueCoordinator` role, it may overwrite any provenance whatsoever, it writes `rating_level` and sets `rating_type` to `COMPUTER_RATED`. Either way the section must be at `OPEN_SEASON` or the write is refused with `ERR_RATING_PERIOD_CLOSED`, the supplied `level` must satisfy the level vocabulary rule of §2.6, and `Player.version` increases by 1.

Every level movement appends to `rating_history`, an append-only array ordered by `recorded_at` ascending. Each entry carries a `source`, a `level`, a `dynamic_rating`, an `effective_season_year` and a `recorded_at` instant. The `source` of a history entry is one of `SELF`, `COMPUTER`, `APPEAL`, `COMMITTEE` or `YEAR_END_RECALC`, so the provenance of every level a player has ever held remains reconstructible. Nothing ever removes or rewrites an entry.

Alongside the coarse level, the system maintains `dynamic_rating` — a fine-grained provisional measure of how the player is actually performing. It stays null until the player has at least one counted line, and null is genuinely null, never 0.0. Two supporting counters sit beside it: `verified_match_count` is the number of distinct matches on which the player appears on a counted line of the verifying scorecard, restricted to matches whose `result_status` is `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED`; and `verified_line_count` is the number of counted lines the player appears on across those same matches. A player who plays two lines of one match therefore adds 1 to `verified_match_count` and 2 to `verified_line_count`.

**The counted-line set.** A line contributes to a player's dynamic rating only when three conditions all hold: the owning match's `result_status` is `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED`; the entry's `credit_source` in the match's `line_results` array is `PLAYED`; and the player appears among the `home_player_ids` or the `away_player_ids` of the scorecard line whose `line_number` matches that entry's `line_number`. Everything else contributes nothing. Matches sitting at `PENDING`, `REPORTED`, `DISPUTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing at all, and a line credited by any mechanism other than actual play — that is, a `credit_source` of `LINE_DEFAULT`, `TEAM_DEFAULT` or `FORFEIT` — contributes nothing even when the surrounding match is `VERIFIED`. Walkovers do not move ratings.

**The per-line rating.** For each counted line, the player's side is home when the player is listed among that line's `home_player_ids` and away otherwise. The two opponents are the two player identifiers on the opposite side of the same line. Their level is taken not from their live records but from the values pinned when they joined their roster: the average of the `level_at_add` of the opposing roster spot held by the first opponent on the opposing team of that match and the `level_at_add` of the opposing roster spot held by the second opponent, divided by two and rounded to three decimals with round-half-up. This is the *pinned* opponent level, and pinning is the whole point — a rating change months later never rewrites the arithmetic of a match already played. The games for the player's side are that line's home games when the player is on the home side and its away games otherwise, and the games against are the mirror image. The game-difference ratio is the games for minus the games against, divided by the total of games for plus games against, kept exact and unrounded at this step. The line's rating is then the pinned opponent level plus 0.50 multiplied by that game-difference ratio, rounded to three decimals with round-half-up. The coefficient 0.50 is the full width of the credit a dominant result can earn: winning every game of a line moves the line rating half a level above the opponents' pinned average, and losing every game moves it half a level below.

**The aggregate.** The dynamic rating is the weighted mean of the included line ratings — the sum, over the included lines, of each line's weight multiplied by its already-rounded line rating, divided by the sum of those same weights — and that mean is then rounded to three decimals with round-half-up. The rounding order is fixed and not negotiable: each line rating is rounded to three decimals *before* it enters the mean, and the mean is rounded again afterwards. Carrying unrounded line ratings into the average and rounding only once at the end yields a different number, and that number is wrong. When the counted-line set is empty, the result is null rather than zero, and `Player.dynamic_rating` remains null.

Two **variants** of this calculation exist, and they differ in exactly two respects: which counted lines are included, and what weight each carries.

The **in-season provisional** variant is the one written to `Player.dynamic_rating`. It is recomputed on every scorecard verification and on every match void, and its value at the moment an appeal is filed is what gets pinned into that appeal's `dynamic_rating_at_filing`. It includes only the six most recent counted lines, where recency is determined by ordering the counted lines by the owning match's `scheduled_start_at` descending, then by the owning match's `created_at` descending, then by the line's `line_number` ascending, and taking the first six. Every included line carries a weight of 1.0, so the provisional variant is an unweighted mean of at most six line ratings. A player with fewer than six counted lines simply averages all of them.

The **year-end final** variant is computed during the year-end recalculation and written to `Player.proposed_recalc_dynamic`. It has no recency cap — every counted line of the whole season is included — and it weights lines by competitive stage: a line whose owning match has `stage` `PLAYOFF` carries a weight of 1.5, and a line whose owning match has `stage` `REGULAR` carries a weight of 1.0. Playoff tennis therefore counts half again as much as regular-season tennis in the year-end number. Over identical stored data the two variants disagree whenever the player has more than six counted lines, or has any playoff line at all; that divergence is intentional and is the reason the year-end run exists as a separate exercise rather than as a read of the live field.

Two more player fields belong to the year-end machinery and are described fully in §3.9: `proposed_recalc_level`, the level a recalculation run proposes, and `proposed_recalc_dynamic`, the year-end final dynamic rating that run computed. Both are null on a freshly enrolled player and both are only written by a recalculation.

### 3.3. Team Formation, Roster Composition and the Eligibility Gates

A `Captain` creates a `Team` by naming the `league_id` it will compete in, a `name` that must be unique within that league, and a `home_facility_id` that becomes the default venue for its home matches. The acting captain's identifier is recorded as `captain_player_id` at creation and is **never reassigned** for the life of the team — there is no captaincy transfer operation in this system. The nominated facility must belong to the same section as the league; a facility from another section is a payload foreign key crossing a section boundary, so it is refused with the section-scoping code of §2.10, `ERR_SECTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, whose rank 12 outranks the rank-42 `ERR_FACILITY_SECTION_MISMATCH`. A new team starts at `status` `DRAFT`, with every standings and roster counter at 0 and `roster_compliant` false. Its `advanced_from_team_id` is null, because it was registered directly rather than promoted; only teams created by the advancement cascade of §3.9 carry a non-null value there, pointing at the lower-tier team they came from. Teams may be listed filtered by `league_id`, by `captain_player_id`, by `home_facility_id`, by `advanced_from_team_id` and by `status`.

Roster membership is expressed by a `RosterSpot`, which is the join between a team and a player and carries its own small lifecycle. Adding a spot requires a `team_id`, a `player_id` and the `expected_team_version` declaration described in §2.7. The new spot arrives at `status` `PENDING`, with `level_at_add` null, `verified_match_count` 0 and `playoff_eligible` false, and the parent team's `version` increases by 1 as a consequence. Two structural rules bite at add time. A player may hold at most one roster spot in the `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `INELIGIBLE` states per league, counted across *every* team of that league — a second attempt anywhere in the same league is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROSTER_SPOT`. And the team may not exceed its league's `max_roster_size`, counting spots in `PENDING` and `ACTIVE` together; an add that would push the combined count past that maximum is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_EXCEEDED`, and the same cap is re-checked at activation. Roster spots may be listed filtered by `team_id`, by `player_id`, by `source_strike_id`, by `status`, by `ineligible_reason` and by `playoff_eligible`.

**Activation is where eligibility is enforced.** Taking a spot from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` is the single most heavily gated operation in the specification, and the captain performing it must be the team's own captain. The section must be at `OPEN_SEASON`. The team must be at `DRAFT`, or at `REGISTERED` with its league at `REGISTRATION_OPEN`; a league that has moved past registration no longer admits captain-driven roster change, and the refusal is `ERR_LEAGUE_PHASE_FORBIDS_ROSTER_CHANGE`. Then five player-level gates apply in the precedence order of §2.9:

- The player's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. A player who is `STRIKE_SUSPENDED` or `DISQUALIFIED` may not be rostered, and the refusal is `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_PLAYABLE`.
- The player's league age — the section's `season_year` less the player's `birth_year` — must be at least the minimum implied by the league's `age_group`: 18 for `AGE_18_OVER`, 40 for `AGE_40_OVER` and 55 for `AGE_55_OVER`. The boundary is inclusive, so a league age exactly equal to the minimum qualifies. A shortfall is refused with `ERR_AGE_GROUP_NOT_MET`.
- The player's `rating_type` must not be `UNRATED`, and the player's `rating_level` must sit inside the league's band: at least the league's `ntrp_level` less 1.0, and at most the league's `ntrp_level`. Both ends of that band are inclusive. An unrated player, a player rated above the league's level, or a player rated more than a full level below it is refused with `ERR_RATING_LEVEL_EXCEEDS_LEAGUE`.
- The player's `gender` must be admissible for the league's `format`. A `MENS_DOUBLES` league admits only players whose gender is `M`; a `WOMENS_DOUBLES` league admits only players whose gender is `F`; a `MIXED_DOUBLES` league admits both. Inadmissibility is refused with `ERR_FORMAT_GENDER_MISMATCH`. The mixed format carries a second, line-level obligation described in §3.6.

On success, the accepted level is **pinned** onto the spot as `level_at_add`, copying the player's `rating_level` at that instant. This pinned value is what the dynamic-rating engine of §3.2 reads as the opponent level, which is precisely why later rating changes cannot retroactively rewrite the ratings of matches already played. Activation then recomputes the team's `active_roster_size` (its count of spots at `ACTIVE`), its `playoff_eligible_roster_size` (its count of spots at `ACTIVE` whose `playoff_eligible` is true) and its `roster_compliant` flag, which is true exactly when `active_roster_size` is at least the league's `min_active_roster_size`. The team's `version` and the spot's own `version` each increase by 1, and the league's `noncompliant_team_count` is recomputed.

A spot that has become unusable moves to `INELIGIBLE` rather than disappearing, and `ineligible_reason` records why. It takes one of four values — `PLAYER_SUSPENDED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED`, `YEAR_END_RECALC` or `GRIEVANCE_REMEDY` — and is null in every other status. Four different drivers push a spot into `INELIGIBLE`, each stamping its own reason: a strike issuance that carries the player to the section's suspension threshold, an upward appeal grant that lifts the player above the league's band, a published year-end recalculation that does the same, and a grievance remedy that either disqualifies the team or strikes the player. When the driver is a strike, the spot also records `source_strike_id`, naming the specific strike whose issuance caused the ineligibility; for every other driver `source_strike_id` stays null. That pointer is what makes the rescission of §3.7 surgical rather than sweeping.

Captain-driven removal takes a spot from `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` to `REMOVED`, which is terminal. The captain must own the team, the league must be at `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, and the section must be at `OPEN_SEASON`. A spot that has accumulated any verified match may not be removed by a captain at all: if its `verified_match_count` is greater than 0 the removal is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_SPOT_HAS_RESULTS`, because removing it would silently unwind results already credited to the standings ledger. A successful removal recomputes the team's `active_roster_size` and `roster_compliant`, recomputes the league's `noncompliant_team_count`, and increases the team's `version` by 1.

A spot's own `verified_match_count` counts the distinct matches with `result_status` `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED` on which that spot's player appears on a counted line for that spot's team, and `playoff_eligible` is true exactly when that count is at least the section's `min_matches_for_playoff_eligibility`. Both are derived, both are recomputed as part of the verification cascade of §3.6, and neither is ever set directly by a client.

### 3.4. The League Lifecycle: Opening, Locking, Starting Play and Unwinding

A league walks a seven-value lifecycle: it begins at `DRAFT` and may reach `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, `IN_PLAY`, `PLAYOFFS`, and finally either `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. The last two are terminal — a league in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` accepts no further lifecycle transition of any kind, and an attempt on any lifecycle operation is refused with `ERR_LEAGUE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. That same code also covers the two playoff operations aimed at the wrong stage: asking to seed playoffs while the league is still at `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, or to advance teams while it is still `IN_PLAY`, is a stage mismatch, and those two operations report `ERR_LEAGUE_ALREADY_TERMINAL` on a stage mismatch whatever stage the league actually holds. Seeding requires `IN_PLAY`; advancement requires `PLAYOFFS`. The other three lifecycle operations — opening registration, locking rosters and starting play — behave differently on a stage mismatch: a league that is neither `COMPLETED` nor `CANCELLED` but is not sitting at the stage the operation departs from has no such transition declared at all, so the refusal is the rank-20 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; only a league already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` turns those three into `ERR_LEAGUE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Opening registration takes the league from `DRAFT` to `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and belongs to the section administrator. Four preconditions apply. The owning section's `rating_period_status` must be `OPEN_SEASON`. The current instant must be at or after `registration_opens_at` and strictly before `registration_closes_at` — the window is half-open, and a league whose window has already elapsed can never be opened. And if the league declares a `parent_league_id`, that parent must itself be at `DRAFT`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, `IN_PLAY` or `PLAYOFFS`; a parent that has already completed or been cancelled has nowhere to promote anyone into. A league with a null `parent_league_id` skips that check. The league's `version` increases by 1.

Team registration is a captain's act, taking the team from `DRAFT` to `REGISTERED`. The acting captain must be the team's own `captain_player_id`. The league must be at `REGISTRATION_OPEN` — otherwise the refusal is `ERR_LEAGUE_PHASE_FORBIDS_ROSTER_CHANGE`. The current instant must lie at or after `registration_opens_at` and strictly before `registration_closes_at`, and a request landing outside that half-open window is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_REGISTRATION_WINDOW`. The league's `registered_team_count` must be strictly below its `max_teams`, and a league already at quota refuses the registration with `ERR_LEAGUE_TEAM_CAPACITY_REACHED`. Finally the team must already carry at least two active roster spots — a team with a single activated player is not a doubles team — and a shortfall there is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_QUOTA_NOT_MET`, the same rank-69 quota code the lock and the start of play use. On success the league recomputes `registered_team_count` and increases its own `version` by 1, and the team's `version` increases by 1.

Locking rosters is the coordinator's act and takes the league from `REGISTRATION_OPEN` to `ROSTERS_LOCKED`. It requires that the current instant has reached `roster_lock_at`, and that `registered_team_count` is at least 2; fewer than two registered teams refuses the lock with `ERR_ROSTER_QUOTA_NOT_MET`. The cascade is broad. Every team of the league at `REGISTERED` transitions to `ROSTER_LOCKED`. For each of those teams, every roster spot still at `PENDING` transitions to `REMOVED` — pending spots that were never activated are simply dropped at lock time, and this is the moment where a captain's unfinished paperwork stops mattering. Each affected team then recomputes `active_roster_size`, `playoff_eligible_roster_size` and `roster_compliant` from the surviving active spots, the league recomputes `noncompliant_team_count`, and the league's `version` plus each affected team's `version` each increase by 1. Note what the lock does *not* do: a team whose `roster_compliant` is false still locks. It is not rejected here; it is held out of the next cascade instead, and remains counted in `noncompliant_team_count`.

Starting play takes the league from `ROSTERS_LOCKED` to `IN_PLAY`, and this is where compliance finally bites. The precondition is that at least two teams of the league are simultaneously at `ROSTER_LOCKED` and have `roster_compliant` true; if fewer than two teams satisfy both, the request is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_QUOTA_NOT_MET`. The cascade is *conditional*: every team at `ROSTER_LOCKED` whose `roster_compliant` is true transitions to `IN_PLAY`, and every team at `ROSTER_LOCKED` whose `roster_compliant` is false stays exactly where it is, remaining at `ROSTER_LOCKED` and remaining inside `noncompliant_team_count`. Such a team is stranded: it never plays, because match confirmation requires both sides to be `IN_PLAY`. The league's `version` and each promoted team's `version` each increase by 1.

**Withdrawal is the unwinding path, and it is the most far-reaching cascade in the system.** A captain may withdraw its own team from `REGISTERED`, from `ROSTER_LOCKED` or from `IN_PLAY`, optionally supplying a `reason`, and the destination in all three cases is `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. No withdrawal edge is declared from any other team state, so a withdrawal aimed at a team that is not `REGISTERED`, `ROSTER_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY` is refused with the rank-20 `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than the rank-31 `ERR_TEAM_STATE_INVALID`, and the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE` answers a withdrawal whose league sits outside the stages the next sentence pairs with the team's state. Withdrawing from `REGISTERED` requires the league to be at `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, `ROSTERS_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY`; withdrawing from `ROSTER_LOCKED` requires the league to be at `ROSTERS_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY`; withdrawing from `IN_PLAY` requires the league to be at `IN_PLAY` and additionally requires that **no** match involving the team sits at `result_status` `VERIFIED`. A team that owns even one verified result may not withdraw, and the refusal is `ERR_TEAM_HAS_VERIFIED_RESULTS` — once a result is on the books the standings ledger of §2.11 can no longer be unwound by a withdrawal, and the coordinator must use the grievance path instead.

All three withdrawal edges run the identical cascade. Every roster spot of the team at `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `INELIGIBLE` transitions to `REMOVED`. Every match naming the team as either `home_team_id` or `away_team_id` whose `schedule_status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, releasing whatever facility occupancy it held. The league then recomputes `registered_team_count`, and two further league-level edges may fire off the back of that recount. If the count has dropped below 2 while the league sits at `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, the league **reverts** to `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and every remaining team still at `ROSTER_LOCKED` transitions back to `REGISTERED`; this revert is a repair path, so it fires regardless of whether `registration_closes_at` has passed, and it makes the league lifecycle genuinely cyclic between `REGISTRATION_OPEN` and `ROSTERS_LOCKED`. If instead the count has reached 0 while the league sits at `REGISTRATION_OPEN`, the league transitions to `CANCELLED` and every match of the league whose `schedule_status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. In every case `noncompliant_team_count` is recomputed, and the team's `version`, the league's `version` and each affected match's `version` each increase by 1.

`WITHDRAWN` and `DISQUALIFIED` teams are frozen. Neither may be named on a new fixture, neither may be seeded, and neither may receive or activate a roster spot; an attempt is refused with `ERR_TEAM_STATE_INVALID`. That same code covers every other team-state mismatch, withdrawal alone excepted: a roster spot may only be added to or activated on a team at `DRAFT` or `REGISTERED`, registration departs only from `DRAFT`, and both sides of a match must be at `IN_PLAY` for a confirmation to be accepted. A team also reaches the terminal states `ELIMINATED` and `ADVANCED` through the playoff machinery of §3.9, and `DISQUALIFIED` through the grievance machinery of §3.8.

### 3.5. The Competition Calendar: Fixtures, Court Capacity and Team Availability

A `Match` in this system carries **two independent status dimensions**, and understanding that they advance on entirely different triggers is the key to the whole reporting workflow. `schedule_status` tracks the logistics of the fixture and runs across `UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `PLAYED`, `DEFAULTED` and `CANCELLED`, with the last two terminal. `result_status` tracks the outcome and runs across `PENDING`, `REPORTED`, `DISPUTED`, `VERIFIED`, `FINALIZED` and `VOIDED`, with the last two terminal. Neither dimension is derived from the other. A match may be `PLAYED` on the schedule side while still `PENDING` on the result side; a defaulted match jumps to `VERIFIED` on the result side while its schedule side rests at `DEFAULTED`. Where a required state is not met, the schedule dimension refuses with `ERR_MATCH_SCHEDULE_STATE_INVALID` and the result dimension refuses with `ERR_MATCH_RESULT_STATE_INVALID`, and the two codes are never interchangeable.

A coordinator creates a fixture by naming a `league_id`, a `home_team_id`, an `away_team_id`, an optional `stage` of `REGULAR` (the default) or `PLAYOFF`, and a `round_number` of at least 1, which is the one-based round index within that stage. Both sides must belong to the named league and must differ from each other; a side drawn from another league, or the same team named twice, is refused with `ERR_TEAM_LEAGUE_MISMATCH`. Fixture creation requires the league to be at `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, `IN_PLAY` or `PLAYOFFS`; a league at any other stage refuses with `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_IN_PLAY`, and the same code guards scheduling, confirmation and marking played. A new fixture arrives at `schedule_status` `UNSCHEDULED` and `result_status` `PENDING`, with `facility_id` null, `scheduled_start_at` null, `scheduled_end_at` null, `courts_required` null, every credited counter at 0, an empty `line_results` array, `winner_team_id` null, `default_side` null, `default_kind` null and `verified_at` null. Matches may be listed filtered by `league_id`, by `home_team_id`, by `away_team_id`, by `facility_id`, by `winner_team_id`, by `stage`, by `round_number`, by `schedule_status`, by `result_status`, and by a `scheduled_from` and `scheduled_to` instant pair bounding `scheduled_start_at`.

**Writing a slot** is the coordinator's act and is the single richest validation in the specification. It takes a match from `UNSCHEDULED` to `SCHEDULED`, and it also exists as a self-loop from `SCHEDULED` back to `SCHEDULED` — the reschedule, which supersedes whatever slot the match previously held. The request carries a `facility_id`, a `scheduled_start_at`, a `courts_required` and the `expected_match_version` declaration of §2.7. Both edges enforce the identical rule set; the difference is that on a reschedule the match's own prior slot is **excluded** from both geometric checks before the new slot is measured, so a match never collides with the slot it is in the act of releasing.

The rules, in order of the precedence ladder of §2.9, are these. The league must be at `ROSTERS_LOCKED`, `IN_PLAY` or `PLAYOFFS`. The `expected_match_version` must match. The requested `facility_id` must belong to the same section as the match's league and must be marked active, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SECTION_MISMATCH`. The `scheduled_start_at` must be strictly after the current instant, and its relationship to the league's `season_end_at` depends on the fixture's `stage`: a `REGULAR` match must start at or before `season_end_at`, while a `PLAYOFF` match must start strictly after it. A start instant in the past, or on the wrong side of the season boundary for its stage, is refused with `ERR_MATCH_START_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. The `courts_required` must be at least 1, must not exceed the facility's `court_count`, and must not exceed the league's `lines_per_match`. Then the two geometric calculations run. On success `facility_id`, `scheduled_start_at` and `courts_required` are written (overwritten, on a reschedule), `scheduled_end_at` is derived as `scheduled_start_at` advanced by the league's `match_duration_minutes` — three hours by default, and note that `scheduled_end_at` deliberately *excludes* the facility turnaround buffer — and the match's `version` increases by 1.

**Court capacity.** The first geometric check asks whether the facility has enough courts free for the whole time the match will occupy them. Every match occupies a **half-open interval** that begins at its `scheduled_start_at` and ends at that instant advanced by its own league's `match_duration_minutes` *plus* the buffer named in `turnaround_buffer_minutes` on the facility it occupies. The interval includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. The requested interval is constructed the same way, from the requested start, the requesting league's `match_duration_minutes` and the requested facility's `turnaround_buffer_minutes`. Two such intervals intersect exactly when the first begins strictly before the second ends and the second begins strictly before the first ends. The occupants measured against are the matches at that same facility whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `PLAYED`, excluding the match being scheduled. The service sums the `courts_required` of every occupant whose interval intersects the requested interval, and requires that sum plus the requested `courts_required` to be at most the facility's `court_count`; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_COURTS_EXCEEDED`. Because the turnaround buffer is part of every match's occupancy, the effective gap two matches need at the same facility is the duration plus the buffer — with the defaults, three and a half hours. Two consequences follow from the half-open geometry and should be relied upon. Matches whose `schedule_status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, `DEFAULTED` or `CANCELLED` hold no interval whatsoever, so a cancelled match releases its courts immediately and a defaulted one likewise. And a request whose start instant is exactly equal to an occupant's interval end — that is, exactly the occupant's start plus the occupant's duration plus the buffer — does **not** intersect that occupant, and is admitted even when the facility is otherwise at exactly its `court_count`.

**Team availability.** The second geometric check asks whether either side is already committed, and it uses a *different* geometry from the court check — a distinction that matters because the two rules produce different verdicts on the same data. It is evaluated once for the `home_team_id` and once for the `away_team_id`, and either side failing refuses the request with `ERR_TEAM_DATE_CONFLICT`. A team's peers are all matches naming that team on either side whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `PLAYED`, excluding the match being scheduled — and crucially, peers are counted across **every league the team plays in**, so a team promoted into a district league can collide with its own local-league fixture.

Which geometry applies depends on the league's `allow_same_day_doubleheader` flag. When the flag is false, which is the default, the rule is a pure **UTC calendar-date bucket**: the request is rejected if any peer's `scheduled_start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested start. Duration plays no part, buffers play no part, and a fixture at 08:00 conflicts with one at 22:00 on the same date. When the flag is true, the rule becomes a **start-to-start separation** rule with a duration overlap check: the request is rejected if any peer's start is within 240 minutes — four hours — of the requested start in either direction, that bound being **inclusive** so that a separation of exactly 240 minutes still conflicts, or if the peer's own interval, running from its start for its own league's `match_duration_minutes`, intersects the requested interval running from the requested start for the requesting league's `match_duration_minutes`. Note that neither form of this rule uses the facility turnaround buffer at all; that omission is exactly what distinguishes team availability from court capacity. Calendar dates are computed by truncating an instant to its UTC calendar date, and separations are measured in whole minutes.

**Confirmation** is the home captain's act, taking the match from `SCHEDULED` to `CONFIRMED`. The acting captain must be the captain of the `home_team_id` — the away captain has no confirmation right. The league must be at `IN_PLAY` or `PLAYOFFS`. Both teams must be at `IN_PLAY`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_TEAM_STATE_INVALID`. Both teams must have `roster_compliant` true, failing which the refusal is `ERR_TEAM_ROSTER_NONCOMPLIANT` — this is the check that strands a team whose roster has fallen below its league's `min_active_roster_size`, whether through removals or through a cascade of ineligibility. And the current instant must be strictly before `scheduled_start_at`: a match cannot be confirmed after it was due to begin. The match's `version` increases by 1.

**Marking a match played** is the coordinator's act, taking it from `CONFIRMED` to `PLAYED`, and it is what opens the scorecard submission window. The only additional requirement is temporal: the current instant must have reached `scheduled_end_at`. Marking a match played before its scheduled end is refused with `ERR_MATCH_NOT_YET_PLAYABLE`, and the same code covers a default recorded before the match's `scheduled_start_at`. The match's `version` increases by 1.

Finally, a match may reach `CANCELLED` on the schedule dimension from `UNSCHEDULED`, from `SCHEDULED` or from `CONFIRMED`, always as a system cascade rather than a direct request — the drivers are a team withdrawal, a team disqualification and a league cancellation. In every case the precondition is that the match's `result_status` is still `PENDING`; a match whose result has been reported or verified is never cancelled by a cascade. From `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`, cancellation retains `facility_id`, `scheduled_start_at` and `courts_required` for audit while releasing the occupancy interval for court-capacity purposes. The match's `version` increases by 1.

### 3.6. Reporting the Result: Scorecards, Disputes, Line Credit and the Standings Ledger

Reporting is deliberately adversarial: one captain submits, the other captain checks. A `Scorecard` is submitted for a match whose `schedule_status` has reached `PLAYED`, by a captain of either the home or the away side, and it carries a `match_id` and an array of `lines`. The submission drives the match's `result_status` from `PENDING` to `REPORTED` and the scorecard itself arrives at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `amendment_count` 0, `dispute_reason` null, an empty `disputed_line_numbers` array, `verified_by_captain_player_id` null and `verified_at` null. The acting captain's identifier is recorded as `submitted_by_captain_player_id`, and the team that captain leads is recorded as `submitted_by_team_id`. Temporally, the submission must land while the current instant is at or before the match's `scheduled_end_at` advanced by the section's `scorecard_submission_window_hours` — two days by default. A submission landing exactly on that boundary instant is still accepted; anything later is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A match may have at most one scorecard in the `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED` or `AMENDED` states at any time, and a second attempt while one is open is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_OPEN`. Scorecards may be listed filtered by `match_id`, by `submitted_by_team_id`, by `submitted_by_captain_player_id`, by `verified_by_captain_player_id` and by `status`.

**The shape of a reported line.** The `lines` array is ordered by `line_number` ascending, and each entry carries a `line_number`, a `home_player_ids` pair, an `away_player_ids` pair, a `set_scores` array of objects each holding a `home_games` and an `away_games` count, and a `default_side`. The structural rules are exact and their violation is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_LINE_INVALID`. There must be exactly as many entries as the league's `lines_per_match`, their `line_number` values must run within 1 through `lines_per_match` with no duplicates, and each side of each line must name two *distinct* player identifiers. The `set_scores` array must describe legal tennis sets: a set is won either at 6 games with the loser on at most 4, or at 7 games with the loser on 5 or 6; a line is best of three sets; and a third set may instead be a 10-point tiebreak, which is recorded as a set of 1 games to 0. Crucially, `set_scores` is empty **exactly** when `default_side` on that line is non-null — a line that was defaulted carries no scores, and a line that was played carries scores.

Two further gates apply to who may be named. Every player identifier on a line must hold a roster spot at `status` `ACTIVE` on the team that fields that side of that line, and no player may appear on two lines of the same match; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_INELIGIBLE_LINEUP`. A named player whose own `status` is `STRIKE_SUSPENDED` or `DISQUALIFIED` may not appear at all, and is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_PLAYABLE`. And in a `MIXED_DOUBLES` league, each side of each line must pair one player whose `gender` is `M` with one whose `gender` is `F`; a same-gender pairing in a mixed league is refused with `ERR_FORMAT_GENDER_MISMATCH`. All three of these checks apply identically to a submission and to an amendment.

**Review is the opposing captain's act,** and it carries a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `DISPUTE`, plus an optional `dispute_reason` and an optional `disputed_line_numbers` array used only on the dispute branch. The scorecard must be at `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED`; `VERIFIED` and `VOIDED` are terminal and admit nothing, and a review aimed at the wrong scorecard state is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION`. The reviewer must be the captain of the side that did **not** submit — the submitting captain attempting to verify their own scorecard is refused with `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN`, and so is a non-submitting captain attempting an amendment.

A `DISPUTE` decision takes the scorecard from `SUBMITTED` or from `AMENDED` to `DISPUTED`, and takes the match's `result_status` from `REPORTED` to `DISPUTED`. It must land while the current instant is at or before the scorecard's `updated_at` advanced by the section's `scorecard_dispute_window_hours` — one day by default; later is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Because the window is anchored on `updated_at` rather than on `created_at`, each amendment restarts the disputing captain's clock. The supplied `disputed_line_numbers` must be a non-empty subset of the `line_number` values actually reported, and a set containing anything else is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The `dispute_reason` and `disputed_line_numbers` are recorded, and they are **retained for audit even after a later amendment overwrites the lines**. The standings consequence is immediate: a disputed match is excluded from *both* standings bases, so whatever provisional contribution the submission created is withdrawn. Both the scorecard's `version` and the match's `version` increase by 1.

**Amendment is the submitting captain's reply.** It takes the scorecard from `DISPUTED` to `AMENDED` and simultaneously returns the match's `result_status` from `DISPUTED` to `REPORTED`, carrying a fresh `lines` array that overwrites the previous one wholesale. Only the captain recorded as `submitted_by_captain_player_id` may amend. `DISPUTED` is the only state an amendment departs from, and because no amendment transition is declared from any other scorecard state the refusal there is the rank-20 `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than the rank-26 `ERR_SCORECARD_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION`, which is reserved for review. `amendment_count` increases by 1 with each amendment and is capped at 2: once it has reached 2 a further amendment is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`. This is what bounds the revision cycle — a scorecard may travel `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → AMENDED → DISPUTED → AMENDED` and no further, at which point the opposing captain's only remaining choices are to verify or to let the grievance machinery handle it. Every amendment recomputes the provisional standings of both teams, and the scorecard's `version` and the match's `version` each increase by 1.

**Verification is where the ledger moves,** and it is the deepest cascade in the specification. A `VERIFY` decision takes the scorecard from `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED` to `VERIFIED` and the match's `result_status` from `REPORTED` to `VERIFIED`, writing `verified_by_captain_player_id`, stamping `verified_at` on both the scorecard and the match — the latter being the anchor for the grievance filing window — and then propagating through five levels. At the first level, the line-credit resolution described below runs, writing the match's `line_results`, `home_lines_won`, `away_lines_won`, `home_sets_won`, `away_sets_won`, `home_games_won`, `away_games_won`, `winner_team_id`, `default_side` and `default_kind`. At the second level, both teams recompute `matches_won`, `matches_lost`, `lines_won`, `lines_lost`, `sets_won`, `sets_lost`, `games_won`, `games_lost` and `standings_points` on the official basis. At the third level, every player appearing on a credited line recomputes `verified_match_count`, `verified_line_count` and `dynamic_rating`. At the fourth level, every corresponding roster spot recomputes its own `verified_match_count` and its `playoff_eligible` flag. At the fifth level, each affected team recomputes `playoff_eligible_roster_size`. The match's `version` increases by 1, and so does the `version` of every entity the cascade touched.

**Line credit resolution** is the arithmetic that turns a set of reported or synthesised lines into credited totals, and its evaluation order is **pinned**: line credits depend on the team-default verdict, and the team-default verdict depends on the per-line default counts, so the steps may not be reordered or fused. Let the league's `lines_per_match` be the line count, and let the *team-default threshold* be that count divided by two and rounded **up** to a whole number — 2 when there are 3 lines, 3 when there are 5.

The first step classifies every line from 1 to the line count. When the driver is a recorded default with a defaulting side of `BOTH`, every line is classified as defaulted by both sides. When the driver is a recorded default naming one side, every line is classified as defaulted by that side. Otherwise, for each line, the classification is *defaulted by home* when that scorecard line's `default_side` is `HOME`, *defaulted by away* when it is `AWAY`, and *played* when it is null.

The second step counts, per side, how many lines that side defaulted — a line defaulted by both sides counts toward both tallies. The third step converts each of those two counts into a team-default verdict: a side has team-defaulted exactly when its defaulted-line count is greater than or equal to the team-default threshold. The fourth step credits every line, using that verdict:

- **Both sides team-defaulted.** Every line is credited to nobody: `credited_to` is null, all four of the line's set and game counts are 0, and `credit_source` is `TEAM_DEFAULT`. The match's `default_side` is `BOTH` and its `winner_team_id` stays null.
- **Only the home side team-defaulted.** Every line from 1 to the line count — *including lines that were actually played* — is credited to the away side at the standard default line score, with `credit_source` `TEAM_DEFAULT`. The games actually played are discarded. The match's `default_side` is `HOME`.
- **Only the away side team-defaulted.** The mirror image of the previous branch: every line, including played ones, is credited to the home side at the standard default line score with `credit_source` `TEAM_DEFAULT`, played games are discarded, and `default_side` is `AWAY`.
- **Neither side team-defaulted.** Each line is credited on its own terms. A line classified as played is credited with the set scores exactly as reported, and `credit_source` is `PLAYED`. A line defaulted by home is credited to the away side at the standard default line score with `credit_source` `LINE_DEFAULT`; a line defaulted by away is credited to the home side the same way. The match's `default_side` is then `HOME` when the home side defaulted at least one line and the away side defaulted none, `AWAY` when the reverse holds, `BOTH` when both defaulted at least one line, and null when neither defaulted any.

The **standard default line score** is fixed: the credited side is recorded as winning two sets six games to love and six games to love, which is 2 sets and 12 games for the credited side and 0 sets and 0 games against.

The fifth step rolls the credited lines up, and only at this point are the match totals defined. `home_lines_won` is the count of lines credited to the home side and `away_lines_won` the count credited to the away side; `home_sets_won`, `away_sets_won`, `home_games_won` and `away_games_won` are the sums of the corresponding per-line figures across the credited lines. The `winner_team_id` becomes `home_team_id` when `home_lines_won` exceeds `away_lines_won`, `away_team_id` when `away_lines_won` exceeds `home_lines_won`, and null otherwise — and because `lines_per_match` is always odd, the null case arises only on a double default. Finally `default_kind` records how the default arose: `NO_SHOW` or `SHORT_ROSTER` as supplied when the driver was a recorded default, `LINE_MAJORITY` when a team default emerged from scorecard line defaults reaching the threshold, and null when no default occurred at all. The `line_results` array itself is ordered by `line_number` ascending, and each entry carries a `line_number`, a `credited_to`, a `home_sets`, an `away_sets`, a `home_games`, an `away_games` and a `credit_source` drawn from `PLAYED`, `LINE_DEFAULT`, `TEAM_DEFAULT` or `FORFEIT`.

**Recording a default** is the coordinator's act when a match simply does not happen as scheduled. It takes the match's `schedule_status` from `SCHEDULED` or from `CONFIRMED` to `DEFAULTED`, and requires that the current instant has reached `scheduled_start_at` and that `result_status` is still `PENDING`. The request carries a `defaulting_side` of `HOME`, `AWAY` or `BOTH`, a `default_kind` of `NO_SHOW` or `SHORT_ROSTER`, and an optional `note`. No scorecard exists in this path, so every line is synthesised and run through the resolution above; `default_side` and `default_kind` are written from the request. The result dimension advances directly from `PENDING` to `VERIFIED` in the same request, with `verified_at` stamped. One strike of kind `NO_SHOW_DEFAULT` is issued to the captain of each defaulting side, so a `BOTH` default issues two strikes — one to each captain. Both teams then recompute their standings fields, the ledger invariants of §2.11 are re-established, and the match's `version` increases by 1.

**Standings.** A team's official standings are persisted onto its own fields on every result transition, and the same arithmetic can also be read on a provisional basis through the `basis` query parameter of the standings listing, which accepts `official` (the default) and `provisional`. The counted match set for a team is the matches of the team's league naming that team on either side whose `result_status` falls in the basis's inclusion set. The official inclusion set is `VERIFIED` and `FINALIZED`. The provisional inclusion set is `REPORTED`, `VERIFIED` and `FINALIZED`. Both bases exclude `PENDING` and `DISPUTED` entirely, and both exclude a `VOIDED` match from the win-and-loss arithmetic, but one narrow contribution survives voiding under either basis: a match voided by the `MATCH_FORFEIT` remedy of §3.8, which is to say one whose `line_results` entries all carry `credit_source` `FORFEIT`, still contributes its per-line, per-set and per-game figures for and against to both sides, so the forfeit credit that remedy awarded stays on the books. It contributes neither a match win nor a match loss, because §2.11 does not count a voided match as decided.

For each counted match, the team's lines, sets and games for are the home-side figures when the team is the home side and the away-side figures otherwise, and the figures against are the mirror image. The team is credited one match win when the match's `winner_team_id` is the team itself, and one match loss when `winner_team_id` is non-null and is the other side. `matches_won` and `matches_lost` are the sums of those two indicators; `lines_won` and `lines_lost`, `sets_won` and `sets_lost`, `games_won` and `games_lost` are the corresponding sums of the per-match figures for and against. `standings_points` is then 3 points for each match won plus 1 point for each line won — a team that sweeps a three-line match earns 3 plus 3, that is 6 points, while a team that loses two lines to one still banks 1 point for the line it took. All of this is integer arithmetic with no rounding anywhere. A double default, whose `winner_team_id` is null, adds neither a win nor a loss to either side, and its credited lines are 0 for both, so it contributes nothing but is not an error.

The two bases differ in exactly one respect and that difference is observable. The official basis counts only `VERIFIED` and `FINALIZED` matches, alongside the surviving forfeit credit just described, so a match sitting at `REPORTED` but not yet verified contributes nothing at all; the official basis is also the *only* basis the playoff seeding of §3.9 ever reads. The provisional basis additionally counts matches at `REPORTED`, deriving their tentative line, set and game credits and their tentative winner by running the line-credit resolution over the scorecard `lines` currently on file. The provisional figures are computed per request and are never persisted. For the same stored rows the two bases return different `standings_points` whenever any match of the league sits at `REPORTED`.

Two more result-dimension edges complete the picture. A match at `VERIFIED` with `stage` `REGULAR` transitions to `FINALIZED` as a cascade of playoff seeding, which freezes the standings input set; §3.9 covers it. And a match at `VERIFIED` or at `FINALIZED` transitions to `VOIDED` as a cascade of a forfeit remedy, described in §3.8. `FINALIZED` and `VOIDED` admit no further action whatsoever.

### 3.7. Discipline: The Weighted Strike Ledger, Suspension and the Compensating Rescission

Rating violations and competitive misconduct are recorded as `Strike` rows, and a strike is never a simple counter increment — it is an entry in a weighted ledger with automatic status consequences. Either a league coordinator or a committee chair may issue one, naming the `player_id` charged, an optional `match_id` identifying the match that occasioned it (null for rating-only violations), a `kind` and a free-text `reason` recording the issuer's narrative. Strikes may be listed filtered by `player_id`, by `match_id`, by `source_grievance_id`, by `kind` and by `status`.

The `kind` fixes the `weight`, which the client never supplies and never overrides. A `SELF_RATE_VIOLATION` carries weight 2. An `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP` carries weight 2. A `NO_SHOW_DEFAULT` carries weight 1. A `SCORECARD_FALSIFICATION` carries weight 3 — falsifying a result is the gravest offence in the catalogue, and a single instance is enough to suspend a player under the default `strike_limit` of 3. A new strike arrives at `status` `ACTIVE` with `source_grievance_id` null and `rescinded_reason` null; the `source_grievance_id` is populated only when the strike was issued by a grievance remedy rather than directly by an operator.

**The ledger.** A player's `strike_count` is not a tally of strike rows; it is the **sum of the `weight` of every strike charged to that player whose `status` is `ACTIVE`**. Rescinded strikes contribute nothing. That sum is recomputed from scratch after every issuance, every rescission, every auto-issued strike arising from a recorded default, and every strike arising from a grievance remedy. Immediately after each recompute, three status thresholds are evaluated against the section's two limits, and they are all inclusive comparisons. If `strike_count` has reached or passed the section's `disqualification_strike_limit`, the player's `status` becomes `DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal. Otherwise, if `strike_count` sits at or above the section's `strike_limit` but below the disqualification limit, the player's `status` becomes `STRIKE_SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if `strike_count` has fallen below `strike_limit` and the player was `STRIKE_SUSPENDED`, the player returns to `ACTIVE`. With the default `strike_limit` of 3, a `NO_SHOW_DEFAULT` weighing 1 landing on top of an `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP` weighing 2 suspends the player at exactly 3 — the threshold is reached, not merely passed.

Suspension cascades into roster composition. When a player crosses into `STRIKE_SUSPENDED`, every roster spot of that player at `ACTIVE` transitions to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligible_reason` set to `PLAYER_SUSPENDED` and `source_strike_id` set to the identifier of the strike that just crossed the threshold. Each affected team then recomputes `active_roster_size`, `playoff_eligible_roster_size` and `roster_compliant`, and each affected league recomputes `noncompliant_team_count`. The player's `version` and every affected team's `version` each increase by 1. Disqualification cascades the same way from either `ACTIVE` or `STRIKE_SUSPENDED`: from `ACTIVE`, every active roster spot moves to `INELIGIBLE` with reason `PLAYER_SUSPENDED` and the same team recomputations follow; from `STRIKE_SUSPENDED` the spots are already ineligible, so only the player's own `status` and `version` move.

**Rescission is the compensating action,** available to a committee chair, taking a strike from `ACTIVE` to `RESCINDED` and requiring a `rescinded_reason` narrative that the chair supplies and the system retains. The ledger is recomputed, and if it has fallen back below the section's `strike_limit` the player returns to `ACTIVE`. The roster restoration is deliberately **surgical**: only those roster spots at `INELIGIBLE` whose `ineligible_reason` is `PLAYER_SUSPENDED` *and* whose `source_strike_id` names the strike just rescinded return to `ACTIVE`, and on doing so both `ineligible_reason` and `source_strike_id` are cleared to null. Two further conditions guard the restoration of an individual spot: the player's `status` must genuinely be `ACTIVE` after the recompute, and the player's `rating_level` must not exceed the `ntrp_level` of the league the spot's team competes in. A spot made ineligible for any *other* reason — `APPEAL_GRANTED`, `YEAR_END_RECALC` or `GRIEVANCE_REMEDY` — is **not** restored by a rescission, and neither is a spot whose `source_strike_id` names some different strike. Each affected team recomputes `active_roster_size` and `roster_compliant`, and the player's `version`, the strike's `version` and each affected team's `version` each increase by 1.

The conservation property of §2.11 is what all of this is for. Issuing a strike and then rescinding that same strike must leave `strike_count` exactly where it started, must leave no roster spot pointing at the rescinded strike, must return the number of spots suspended across the player's teams to its pre-issue value, and must return each affected team's `active_roster_size` and `roster_compliant` to their pre-issue values. The one documented asymmetry is the permanence of disqualification: once a player has reached `DISQUALIFIED`, a rescission still lowers `strike_count` correctly and is fully recorded, but the player's `status` remains `DISQUALIFIED` and no roster spot is restored. A rescission asked to undo a disqualification is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_DISQUALIFIED_PERMANENT`.

### 3.8. Committee Review: Appeals, Votes, Grievances and Remedies

The section committee is split in two by design. A `CommitteeMember` may vote but never decide; a `CommitteeChair` may decide but is barred from deciding anything it voted on. Both are backed by `Player` rows, which is what makes the interested-party tests possible at all.

**Filing a rating appeal** is a `Player`-role act on the actor's own record, carrying a `player_id` that must equal `X-User-ID`, a `direction` of `UP` or `DOWN`, a `requested_level` and a `reason` statement. The requested level must be the appellant's current `rating_level` plus 0.5 for an `UP` appeal and minus 0.5 for a `DOWN` appeal, and it must satisfy the level vocabulary rule of §2.6. A player may hold at most one appeal at a time in the `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` states, and a second filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN`. The appeal arrives at `status` `SUBMITTED` with both vote counts at 0, `decided_by_chair_player_id`, `decided_at`, `decision_note` and `granted_level` all null.

Filing **pins four snapshots**, and the pinning is the whole mechanism by which an appeal cannot be gamed after the fact. `rating_level_at_filing` captures the appellant's `rating_level`, `dynamic_rating_at_filing` captures the `dynamic_rating` (null when the player has no counted line), `verified_match_count_at_filing` captures the `verified_match_count`, and `strike_count_at_filing` captures the `strike_count`. Every subsequent evaluation reads those pinned values and never the live player record, so a rating change or a new strike arriving after filing cannot flip the verdict. Appeals may be listed filtered by `player_id`, by `decided_by_chair_player_id`, by `direction`, by `status` and by `auto_grant_eligible`.

**The auto-grant evaluation** runs once at filing and writes the boolean `auto_grant_eligible`, and it is re-read at decision time rather than recomputed. It reads only the four pinned snapshots plus the requested `direction` and `requested_level`. If `dynamic_rating_at_filing` is null there is nothing to measure against, and `auto_grant_eligible` is false immediately. Otherwise the *gap* is the pinned dynamic rating less the pinned level, rounded to three decimals with round-half-up. The *required match count* is 3 when the appellant's `rating_type` is `COMPUTER_RATED`, and 5 for every other provenance — that is, for `SELF_RATED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED` and `COMMITTEE_ASSIGNED`; a computer-rated player has already been measured by the system and needs a shorter track record to challenge it than a self-rated one does. The *threshold* is built from that required count and the pinned strike total: take 0.10 multiplied by the required match count, subtract 0.05 multiplied by the smaller of the pinned strike total and 3, then clamp the result so that it is never above 0.50 and never below 0.20. Both the gap and the threshold are rounded to three decimals with round-half-up before any comparison, and every comparison is inclusive, so a gap exactly equal to the threshold qualifies.

For an `UP` appeal, `auto_grant_eligible` is true when all three of the following hold: the pinned verified match count is at least the required match count; the gap is at least the threshold; and the requested level is exactly the pinned level plus 0.5. For a `DOWN` appeal, four conditions must hold: the pinned verified match count is at least the required match count; the *negation* of the gap — that is, how far the dynamic rating sits *below* the pinned level — is at least the threshold plus a further 0.10 margin; the requested level is exactly the pinned level minus 0.5; and the pinned strike total is exactly 0. Downward appeals are held to a stricter standard than upward ones and are unavailable to any player carrying an active strike, because a player who wants to be re-rated *down* has an obvious incentive to lose.

**Opening an appeal to review** is a chair's act, taking it from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and it is the only committee operation permitted in a partially closed rating epoch: the section's `rating_period_status` may be either `OPEN_SEASON` or `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS`. Votes may only be cast while the appeal is `UNDER_REVIEW`. The appeal's `version` increases by 1. An appeal at `GRANTED` or `DENIED` is terminal, and any action aimed at the wrong appeal state is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION` — including a start-review aimed at an appeal that is not `SUBMITTED`, and a decision aimed at one that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`.

**Casting a vote** is a `CommitteeMember` act. A `CommitteeVote` carries a `subject_type` of `RATING_APPEAL` or `GRIEVANCE`, a polymorphic `subject_id` resolving to a `RatingAppeal` when the subject type is `RATING_APPEAL` and to a `Grievance` when it is `GRIEVANCE`, a `vote` of `SUPPORT`, `OPPOSE` or `ABSTAIN`, and an optional `note`. The acting member's identifier is recorded as `voter_player_id`. A subject identifier that does not resolve inside the entity its subject type names is refused with `ERR_SUBJECT_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The subject must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_SUBJECT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. A member may cast at most one vote per subject type and subject identifier pair, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`. Interested parties are barred outright, with `ERR_SOD_INTERESTED_PARTY`: a member may not vote on a rating appeal whose `player_id` is the member itself, and may not vote on a grievance whose `filed_by_captain_player_id` is the member, whose `remedy_target_player_id` is the member, or whose `against_team_id` is a team the member captains. Casting a vote recomputes the subject's `support_vote_count` (its votes of `SUPPORT`) and `oppose_vote_count` (its votes of `OPPOSE`) and increases the subject's `version` by 1; an `ABSTAIN` counts toward neither tally, though it is recorded and it does consume the member's one vote. Votes may be read individually and listed per voting player, filterable by `subject_type`.

**Deciding an appeal** is a chair's act taking it from `UNDER_REVIEW` to either `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, carrying an `outcome` of `GRANT` or `DENY` and an optional `decision_note`. Four gates precede either branch. The chair must not have cast a vote on this subject, failing which the refusal is `ERR_CHAIR_VOTED_ON_SUBJECT`. On the grant branch the section must be at `OPEN_SEASON`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_RATING_PERIOD_CLOSED`. Also on the grant branch, either `auto_grant_eligible` must be true, or the committee must have delivered a quorum — `support_vote_count` at least 2 *and* strictly greater than `oppose_vote_count`; failing both, the refusal is `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. And the appellant's live `rating_level` must still equal the appeal's `rating_level_at_filing`; a level moved since filing by a published recalculation or a committee assignment invalidates the baseline and the refusal is `ERR_APPEAL_BASELINE_STALE`. The `DENY` branch has its own gate in the opposite direction: an appeal whose `auto_grant_eligible` is true may not be denied, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUTO_GRANT_MUST_BE_GRANTED`.

The two outcomes have **disjoint side-effect sets**, and both deserve equal attention. On `GRANT`, `granted_level` is set to the `requested_level`, the appellant's `rating_level` is set to that same value, `rating_type` becomes `APPEAL_GRANTED`, and a `rating_history` entry of source `APPEAL` is appended carrying the granted level and the player's current `dynamic_rating`. Then, **for an `UP` direction only**, every roster spot of that player at `ACTIVE` whose team's league has an `ntrp_level` below the player's new `rating_level` transitions to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligible_reason` `APPEAL_GRANTED` and `source_strike_id` left null; each affected team recomputes `active_roster_size`, `playoff_eligible_roster_size` and `roster_compliant`; and each affected league recomputes `noncompliant_team_count`. Winning an upward appeal can therefore cost a player their place on a lower-band team, and can push that team out of compliance. `decided_by_chair_player_id` and `decided_at` are written, and both the player's `version` and the appeal's `version` increase by 1. On `DENY`, by contrast, `granted_level` stays null and the appellant's `rating_level`, `rating_type` and `rating_history` are all left completely untouched; only `decided_by_chair_player_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` are written, and only the appeal's `version` increases by 1. No roster spot moves on a denial.

**Filing a grievance** is a captain's act against another team of the same league. It carries a `league_id`, an `against_team_id`, an optional `subject_match_id`, a `kind`, an optional `remedy_target_player_id` and a `statement` narrative; the filing captain's identifier is recorded as `filed_by_captain_player_id`. The accused team must belong to the named league and must not be the filer's own team. The `kind` is one of `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP`, `SCORE_FALSIFICATION`, `DEFAULT_ABUSE` or `RATING_VIOLATION`, and it constrains the payload: `subject_match_id` is **required** for `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP`, `SCORE_FALSIFICATION` and `DEFAULT_ABUSE`, and **forbidden** for `RATING_VIOLATION`. Where a subject match is named it must belong to the same league and must name the accused team on one of its sides. Where a `remedy_target_player_id` is named it must hold a roster spot at `ACTIVE` or `INELIGIBLE` on the accused team. Any incoherence among these is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_SUBJECT_INVALID`. Temporally, a grievance must be filed within the section's `grievance_window_hours` — three days by default — of its subject anchor: the subject match's start instant for the three match-based kinds, or the appeal decision it contests for a `RATING_VIOLATION`; later is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The grievance arrives at `status` `FILED` with `remedy` null, both vote counts 0, and `decided_by_chair_player_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` null. Grievances may be listed filtered by `league_id`, by `filed_by_captain_player_id`, by `against_team_id`, by `subject_match_id`, by `remedy_target_player_id`, by `decided_by_chair_player_id`, by `kind` and by `status`.

From `FILED` a grievance may go two ways. The filing captain may **withdraw** it, taking it to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal; only the captain recorded as `filed_by_captain_player_id` may do so. Or a chair may **open it to review**, taking it to `UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires the affected league to be at `IN_PLAY`, `PLAYOFFS` or `COMPLETED`. Either way the grievance's `version` increases by 1. `UPHELD`, `DISMISSED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal, and any action aimed at the wrong grievance state is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_STATE_FORBIDS_ACTION`.

**Deciding a grievance** is a chair's act carrying an `outcome` of `UPHOLD` or `DISMISS`, an optional `remedy` used only on the uphold branch, and an optional `decision_note`. As with appeals, a chair that voted on this subject is refused with `ERR_CHAIR_VOTED_ON_SUBJECT`. An `UPHOLD` requires the committee quorum — `support_vote_count` at least 2 and strictly greater than `oppose_vote_count` — failing which the refusal is `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; a `DISMISS` requires the converse posture, namely at least one `OPPOSE` vote or fewer than 2 `SUPPORT` votes. An `UPHOLD` also requires that the chosen `remedy` be admissible for the grievance `kind`, and inadmissibility is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`. The admissibility rules are: `MATCH_FORFEIT` requires the kind to be `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP`, `SCORE_FALSIFICATION` or `DEFAULT_ABUSE`, requires `subject_match_id` to be non-null, and requires that match's `result_status` to be `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED`; `PLAYER_STRIKE` requires `remedy_target_player_id` to be non-null; and `TEAM_DISQUALIFICATION` requires the kind to be `INELIGIBLE_LINEUP` or `SCORE_FALSIFICATION` and requires the accused team's `status` to be `ROSTER_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY`. In every case `decided_by_chair_player_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` are written and the grievance's `version` increases by 1.

A `DISMISS` touches nothing outside the grievance row itself: `remedy` stays null, and no match, team, player or strike is modified. An `UPHOLD` writes the `remedy` and then forks into one of three entirely different cascade families.

**The `MATCH_FORFEIT` remedy** takes the subject match's `result_status` to `VOIDED`, from either `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED`. First the previously credited contributions are subtracted from both teams. Then a forfeit credit is applied to the team that is *not* the accused team: exactly `lines_per_match` lines, each at two sets six games to love and six to love — which is 2 sets and 12 games per line, so with three lines the aggrieved team gains 3 lines, 6 sets and 36 games and the forfeiting team the mirror-image losses. The match's `line_results` is rewritten with every entry carrying `credit_source` `FORFEIT`, `winner_team_id` is set to the aggrieved team, and `default_side` is set to `HOME` or `AWAY` according to which side forfeited, while `default_kind` is left unchanged. The scorecard of that match transitions to `VOIDED`, retaining its `lines` for audit while ceasing to contribute to any calculation. Every player and every roster spot that lost a credited line recomputes `verified_match_count`, `verified_line_count`, `dynamic_rating` and `playoff_eligible`; each affected team recomputes `playoff_eligible_roster_size` and `standings_points`; and the four ledger invariants of §2.11 must hold after the reversal plus the forfeit credit. If the match being voided was `FINALIZED` while its league sits at `PLAYOFFS`, the league additionally recomputes `finalized_match_count` and **every** `playoff_seed` in the league is recomputed by the seeding calculation of §3.9. The match's `version` increases by 1.

**The `PLAYER_STRIKE` remedy** creates a `Strike` for the `remedy_target_player_id` with `source_grievance_id` set to the deciding grievance, running the full ledger cascade of §3.7. The `kind` of that strike depends on the grievance: it is `SELF_RATE_VIOLATION` when the grievance `kind` is `RATING_VIOLATION`, and `SCORECARD_FALSIFICATION` for every other grievance kind — so a falsification grievance costs the target 3 weight while a rating-violation grievance costs 2. When the grievance `kind` is `RATING_VIOLATION` there is a second, distinct effect: the target player's `rating_level` is additionally raised by 0.5, capped at the ceiling of 5.0, `rating_type` is set to `COMMITTEE_ASSIGNED`, and a `rating_history` entry of source `COMMITTEE` is appended. A player who sandbagged their self-rating therefore both accumulates strike weight and is moved up to where the committee believes they belong.

**The `TEAM_DISQUALIFICATION` remedy** takes the accused team to `DISQUALIFIED` from either `ROSTER_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY`. Every roster spot of that team at `ACTIVE` transitions to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligible_reason` `GRIEVANCE_REMEDY`. Every match involving that team whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `result_status` is still `PENDING` has its `schedule_status` taken to `CANCELLED`. Matches already `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED` **keep** their standings contributions — a disqualification is forward-looking and does not rewrite history. The league recomputes `registered_team_count` and `noncompliant_team_count`, and the team's `version` increases by 1.

### 3.9. Playoffs, Advancement to the Parent Tier and the Year-End Rating Recalculation

**Seeding is the moment the season's arithmetic is frozen.** A league coordinator seeds the playoffs of a league at `IN_PLAY`, and two preconditions guard it. First, no regular-stage match of the league may still be open: there must be zero matches at `stage` `REGULAR` whose `schedule_status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `PLAYED`, and zero whose `result_status` is `PENDING`, `REPORTED` or `DISPUTED`. Every regular fixture must have reached a settled place — verified, defaulted, cancelled or voided — before seeds may be written. Second, the candidate set must hold at least two teams. A league not at `IN_PLAY` is refused with `ERR_LEAGUE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and a league whose teams are frozen at `WITHDRAWN` or `DISQUALIFIED` cannot have them seeded, which the team-state gate reports as `ERR_TEAM_STATE_INVALID`.

**The candidate set** is defined by four explicit predicates evaluated over the teams of the league. A team is a candidate when its `status` is `IN_PLAY`, its `roster_compliant` is true, its `playoff_eligible_roster_size` is at least the league's `min_playoff_eligible_players`, and the sum of its `matches_won` and `matches_lost` is at least 1. That last predicate excludes a team that played nothing decisive all season: a team every one of whose matches was cancelled or voided has no wins and no losses, and takes no seed no matter how compliant its roster. If fewer than two teams satisfy all four predicates the entire request is rejected with `ERR_NO_PLAYOFF_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS`, **no seed is written at all**, and the league's `status` stays `IN_PLAY` — seeding is all-or-nothing.

**The ranking expression applies six keys in a fixed order,** each breaking whatever ties the previous key left. The first key is `standings_points` descending, read on the official basis only — the provisional basis of §3.6 never seeds anything. The second is `matches_won` descending. The third is head-to-head lines won, descending. The fourth is the set differential, that is `sets_won` less `sets_lost`, descending. The fifth is the game differential, `games_won` less `games_lost`, descending. The sixth and final key is `created_at` ascending, so the team that registered earliest wins an otherwise perfect tie; the team's `id` is **never** consulted as a tie-break. All five statistical comparisons are integer comparisons, and `created_at` is compared at millisecond precision.

The head-to-head key deserves its own explanation because it is computed against a *tie group* rather than the whole league. The tie group is recomputed at each level: it is the set of candidates still tied after the first two keys have been applied. For a team in that group, the head-to-head figure is the sum, over every match whose `result_status` is `VERIFIED` or `FINALIZED` and whose `home_team_id` and `away_team_id` are **both** inside the tie group and one of which is the team itself, of that team's line count in the match — `home_lines_won` when it is the home side and `away_lines_won` when it is the away side. Two consequences follow directly. A two-team tie group whose members never actually met each other scores 0 for both teams, so the third key is a no-op and the set differential decides. A tie group of three or more teams becomes a mini-league, scored by summing lines across only those matches played among the tied teams.

**Seeds are then assigned** 1 through the number of candidates in the sorted order, written into each candidate's `playoff_seed`. Every team of the league whose `status` is `IN_PLAY` and which is *not* in the candidate set transitions to `ELIMINATED`, which is terminal; its `playoff_seed` is left as it was, which for a first-time non-candidate means null. Every match of the league at `stage` `REGULAR` whose `result_status` is `VERIFIED` transitions to `FINALIZED`, which is what freezes the standings input set: a finalized match still counts toward the official basis but no longer admits any amendment, dispute or re-verification. The league recomputes `seeded_team_count` and `finalized_match_count`, its `status` becomes `PLAYOFFS`, and its `version` increases by 1. Each affected team's and each affected match's `version` increases by 1 as well.

Seeding is not permanently frozen against later correction. When a `FINALIZED` match is voided by an upheld forfeit remedy while the league is still at `PLAYOFFS`, the league's `finalized_match_count` is recomputed and **every** `playoff_seed` in the league is recomputed by this same ranking expression over the same candidate predicates. A forfeit awarded in week three of the playoffs can therefore reorder the bracket.

**Advancement closes the league.** A coordinator advances teams from a league at `PLAYOFFS`, and four preconditions apply. The league's `seeded_team_count` must be at least 1, and advancing before a completed seeding — that is, with no `playoff_seed` populated — is refused with `ERR_NO_PLAYOFF_ELIGIBLE_TEAMS`. No playoff-stage match may remain open: there must be zero matches at `stage` `PLAYOFF` whose `result_status` is `PENDING`, `REPORTED` or `DISPUTED`, and zero whose `schedule_status` is `UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `PLAYED`. If the league has a parent league, that parent must be at `REGISTRATION_OPEN` — a parent tier that has already locked its rosters cannot receive promotions. And the parent must have room: the parent's `registered_team_count` plus the number of teams about to be promoted must not exceed the parent's `max_teams`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_ADVANCEMENT_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`. A league with no parent — a `SECTION`-tier league at the top of the ladder — skips both parent conditions entirely and simply completes.

The number promoted is the **smaller of the league's `advancement_slots` and its `seeded_team_count`**, and the teams promoted are that many teams taken in `playoff_seed` ascending order, that is, the best seeds first. Each promoted team transitions to `ADVANCED`, which is terminal. The promotion then runs a four-level cascade. At level two, for each advanced team, when the league's `parent_league_id` is not null a **new** `Team` row is created in the parent league at `status` `REGISTERED`, with `advanced_from_team_id` set to the source team's identifier, and with the same `captain_player_id`, the same `home_facility_id` and the same `name` as the source. At level three, for each newly created team, one `RosterSpot` is created at `status` `PENDING` for each roster spot of the source team whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — the promoted squad arrives at the higher tier unactivated, so the new captain must activate each spot afresh under the parent league's own `ntrp_level` band and its own age and gender rules. At level four the parent league recomputes `registered_team_count` and its `version` increases by 1. Every remaining team of the league whose `playoff_seed` is not null — the seeded teams that were not promoted — transitions to `ELIMINATED`. The league's `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, which is terminal, and its `version` increases by 1. A team promoted this way is a genuinely new row; the source team stays in its own league at `ADVANCED` as the historical record of the season it won.

**The year-end rating recalculation is a two-phase, section-wide operation** and the only thing in the system that rewrites ratings in bulk. Both phases are section-admin acts on the section, and both are gated by the section's `rating_period_status`: phase one requires `OPEN_SEASON` and phase two requires `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS`. Either mismatch is refused with `ERR_RECALC_PHASE_INVALID`, which makes the two phases strictly ordered and each of them exactly-once per epoch.

**Phase one proposes and applies nothing.** Its scope is every player of the section whose `status` is not `DISQUALIFIED` and whose `verified_line_count` is at least 1, processed in `created_at` ascending order; the processing order is deterministic and the player's `id` is never consulted. For each player in scope, `proposed_recalc_dynamic` is set to the year-end-final dynamic rating described in §3.2 — no recency cap, every counted line of the season included, and playoff-stage lines weighted at 1.5 against 1.0 for regular-stage lines. The *delta* is that proposed dynamic rating less the player's current `rating_level`, rounded to three decimals with round-half-up. If the delta is at or above the upward threshold of 0.25, `proposed_recalc_level` becomes the player's `rating_level` plus 0.5, capped at the ceiling of 5.0. Otherwise, if the delta is at or below the downward threshold of negative 0.35, `proposed_recalc_level` becomes the `rating_level` minus 0.5, floored at 2.5. Otherwise `proposed_recalc_level` is set equal to the player's existing `rating_level` — in scope, evaluated, and proposed unchanged. Both boundaries are inclusive: a delta of exactly 0.25 bumps up, a delta of exactly negative 0.35 bumps down, and a delta of negative 0.34 leaves the level alone. Players outside the scope keep `proposed_recalc_level` at null and are untouched at publish. The section's `recalculated_player_count` is set to the number of players in scope, `rating_period_status` becomes `RECALC_IN_PROGRESS`, and the section's `version` increases by 1. No player's `rating_level`, `rating_type` or `dynamic_rating` moves in phase one, and no roster spot moves.

**Phase two publishes.** Its scope is every player of the section whose `proposed_recalc_level` is not null — which is the phase-one scope, since a player evaluated in phase one always received a non-null proposal even when it equalled the existing level — again processed in `created_at` ascending order. For each such player, first note whether the proposal actually differs from the current `rating_level`. Then `rating_level` is set to `proposed_recalc_level`; `rating_type` is set to `COMPUTER_RATED` **even when the level is unchanged**, so a player who was `SELF_RATED` and whose level held steady still ends the epoch computer-rated; `dynamic_rating` is set to `proposed_recalc_dynamic`, making the year-end figure the new season's baseline; and a `rating_history` entry is appended with source `YEAR_END_RECALC`, the new level, the year-end dynamic rating, an `effective_season_year` of the section's `season_year` plus 1, and a `recorded_at` of the current logical clock instant. The section's `published_level_change_count` counts only the players whose level genuinely changed, so it is generally smaller than `recalculated_player_count`.

Publishing then cascades into roster composition exactly as an upward appeal does. For each published player, every roster spot of that player at `ACTIVE` whose team's league has an `ntrp_level` **below** the player's new `rating_level` transitions to `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligible_reason` `YEAR_END_RECALC` and `source_strike_id` null; each affected team recomputes `active_roster_size`, `playoff_eligible_roster_size` and `roster_compliant`; and each affected league recomputes `noncompliant_team_count`. Because the reason recorded is `YEAR_END_RECALC` and not `PLAYER_SUSPENDED`, no strike rescission will ever restore these spots; only a captain's roster work in the next registration window will. Finally the section's `rating_period_status` becomes `RECALC_PUBLISHED`, `recalc_run_index` increases by 1, and the section's `version` increases by 1. From `RECALC_PUBLISHED` the rating epoch is closed for every purpose described in §2.8, and a fresh epoch must be opened before any rating write or roster activation is accepted again.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are rooted at the `/tennis` prefix. Creation endpoints return HTTP 201 with the created resource; every state transition, rating assignment, recalculation phase and read returns HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource; the captain's removal of a roster spot returns HTTP 204 with no body. All list endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` parameters of §2.3 and return their page in `data` with the pagination fields populated in `meta`.
