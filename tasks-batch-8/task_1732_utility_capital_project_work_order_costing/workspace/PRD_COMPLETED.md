# Utility Capital Costing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Electric and gas utilities do not expense the poles, transformers, and conductor they install; they capitalize them. Between appropriation and the fixed-asset register, however, lies a long and heavily audited pipeline: a capital program is budgeted under a budget code, individual work orders are estimated, authorized, and charged in the field, an allowance for funds used during construction accrues while the asset is being built, customer contributions offset the utility's own outlay, and at closeout the accumulated construction-work-in-progress balance is unitized across standardized asset classes so depreciation can begin from the in-service date. This document specifies the Utility Capital Costing API, a book-of-record service mounted under `/ucp` that governs that pipeline end to end — programs, work orders, estimates, cost charges, CIAC agreements, fixed-asset units, regulatory assets, and the accounting-period close that produces the AFUDC, depreciation, and amortization entries.

Two properties dominate the design. First, the ledger is always reconciled: every posting immediately recomputes the derived balances it touches, every side effect lands atomically inside the triggering request, and nothing is left for a nightly batch outside the period close itself. Second, authority is segregated and tiered: the engineer who creates a work order cannot authorize it, the analyst who proposes a regulatory deferral cannot approve it, the supervisor who completes a job cannot close it out, and decisions above defined dollar and percentage thresholds escalate from the field supervisor to the corporate controller. The sections that follow give the global contract first and then walk the business workflows in lifecycle order.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide rules, envelope formats, and cross-cutting principles that apply to every endpoint of the API.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request is authenticated through two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming their capacity. The system recognizes exactly seven roles, spelled precisely as follows: `SystemAdmin`, `ProjectEngineer`, `WorkOrderSupervisor`, `Controller`, `FieldClerk`, `RateAnalyst`, and `Auditor`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is missing or not one of these seven values, or whose `X-User-ID` header is missing or empty on any mutating endpoint, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is not in the endpoint's permitted role list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). All seven roles may read any resource; write capabilities are partitioned as described below and summarized per endpoint in §4.

The `SystemAdmin` administers the utility's reference data and calendar: creating capital programs, maintaining the unit catalog and overhead schedules, opening accounting periods, and setting the logical clock. The `ProjectEngineer` scopes capital work: creating work orders, submitting and resubmitting them, and writing estimates. The `WorkOrderSupervisor` runs field execution — returning, starting, suspending, resuming, cancelling, and completing work orders, authorizing orders whose approved estimate falls below the controller threshold, and deciding estimates within the supervisor routing tier. The `Controller` is the corporate authority: authorizing work orders of any amount, deciding estimates of any tier, closing and reopening accounting periods, closing out work orders, approving or rejecting regulatory assets, retiring fixed-asset units, and closing capital programs. The `FieldClerk` posts and reverses labor, equipment, and material charges. The `RateAnalyst` manages CIAC pledges and receipts and proposes regulatory asset deferrals. The `Auditor` is read-only.

For every one of these seven roles, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on the resources the operator creates or decides — for example as `created_by_user_id` on a work order, `submitted_by_user_id` on an estimate, `posted_by_user_id` on a charge, or `proposed_by_user_id` on a regulatory asset — and compares those recorded identifiers against the incoming `X-User-ID` exclusively for the segregation-of-duties checks described in §3. It never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints paginate through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned: it defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. The `offset` parameter skips a number of items from the start of the result set and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that fails to parse as an integer, a `limit` outside its range, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Unless otherwise specified, every list endpoint returns resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with remaining ties broken by creation (insertion) order; fixed-asset-unit lists additionally break `created_at` ties by `line_no` ascending before that insertion fallback, so the lines of a single closeout appear in their submitted order. The `meta` object of a list response carries four pagination fields: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every top-level resource carries a standard set of system-managed fields. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` records when the resource was first created, and `updated_at` the instant of its most recent modification; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. The `version` is a system-managed integer that starts at 1 on creation — with `updated_at` initially equal to `created_at` — and increments with every modification to the resource, including the state transitions and derived-field recomputations called out in §3, and serves as the basis for the optimistic concurrency control of §2.7.

Three resources are append-only ledger entries and deliberately deviate from this pattern: an AFUDC entry, a depreciation entry, and an amortization entry each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them, because an entry is never mutated in place — when a period is reopened, compensation flips the entry's `status` rather than editing the row.

### 2.5. Monetary Values and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer cents; every such field is named with the `_cents` suffix (for example `authorized_amount_cents`, `base_amount_cents`, `pledged_cents`). Rates and weights are numbers expressing percentages, so a value of 12.0 means twelve percent. Whenever a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — zero decimal places — and this rounding is applied per individual row before any summation, never on the aggregate. The rounding order is pinned: overhead on a charge is rounded per charge row first and only then summed into work-order totals; computing overhead on the sum of the bases is forbidden because it yields different cents. By way of example, three charge rows of 100 cents each at a two-and-a-half-percent rate accrue 3 cents of overhead apiece — 9 cents in total — whereas rounding the aggregate 7.50 cents would yield 8. Threshold comparisons throughout the system (the variance hold, the estimate routing tier, the authorization tier, the closeout variance gate) are exact integer comparisons performed without any floating-point arithmetic.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a logical clock rather than trusting wall-clock time. Before it has ever been set, the clock reads exactly 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, and it never falls back to wall-clock time afterward. A `SystemAdmin`-only test-driving capability advances the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant — the clock keeps whole-second resolution, truncating any fractional seconds — and the response reports the new reading as `current_utc`. The logical clock is the single time authority for three behaviors in this document: it decides when a CIAC pledge expires, it supplies the informational date stamped on reversal charge rows, and it is recorded as `closed_at` when an accounting period closes.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write operations in the API carry a version field: creating an estimate on a work order, and approving an estimate. Both require a non-optional `expected_work_order_version` input carrying the work-order `version` the client last observed. If that value no longer equals the work order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). For estimate creation the rejection protects the caller from acting on a stale picture of which estimate is still `PROPOSED` and what the current sequence number is; for approval it protects the caller from deciding on a stale picture of which estimate is currently approved and what budget commitment the work order carries. No other endpoint accepts a version field; on every other operation the server processes the mutation without an optimistic-concurrency check.

### 2.8. Request Validation Order

When several checks could reject the same request, the server evaluates them in a fixed order and reports the first failure encountered; the error a client observes is therefore deterministic. The order is:

1. Authentication headers — missing or unknown role, or a missing `X-User-ID` on a mutating endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint permission — the role is not in the endpoint's role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN`.
3. Existence — path identifiers first, then foreign-key references in the body, validated left to right — `ERR_NOT_FOUND`.
4. Request shape — a required field is missing, an enum field takes an undeclared member, a numeric range is violated (amounts greater than zero, `quantity` at least one, `amortization_months` at least one, rate bounds, a debt weight between 0 and 100 inclusive), a date fails to parse, an `end_date` precedes its `start_date`, or a program `code` or catalog `asset_class` duplicates an existing one — `ERR_VALIDATION`.
5. Tiered authorization — the role is endpoint-allowed but below the required tier for the amount or delta in play — `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_REQUIRED`.
6. Stale version — the optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
7. State legality — the target entity is not in a legal from-state for the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` or the domain-specific state error (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED`, `ERR_CHARGE_ALREADY_REVERSED`, `ERR_REVERSAL_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_RETIRED`, `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_OPEN`, `ERR_PERIOD_REOPEN_NOT_LATEST`).
8. Segregation of duties — the actor repeats a prior step's actor — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. Temporal rules — period adjacency and containment, in-service and retirement dating, CIAC pledge expiry — the 422 family `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ADJACENT`, `ERR_IN_SERVICE_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_RETIREMENT_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_CIAC_EXPIRED`.
10. Cross-entity referential rules — overhead rate resolution, schedule overlap, program-close completeness, closeout's CIAC and unitization requirements, the approved-estimate requirement at authorization — the 422 family `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVERHEAD_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_WORK_ORDERS`, `ERR_CIAC_PLEDGED_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_UNITIZATION_EMPTY`, `ERR_UNITIZATION_ZERO_TARGET`, `ERR_NO_APPROVED_ESTIMATE`.
11. Fiscal rules — budget at authorization, the variance hold on charging, the below-actuals floor on estimate approval, the closeout variance gate, and the write-off disposition requirement — `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_VARIANCE_HOLD`, `ERR_ESTIMATE_BELOW_ACTUALS`, `ERR_CLOSEOUT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`, `ERR_REGULATORY_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_WRITEOFF_DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth internalizing. A tier failure always surfaces before any state, segregation, referential, or fiscal complaint on the same request, and the segregation-of-duties check is evaluated before the budget test at authorization and before the variance gate at closeout — the closeout variance gate is the last check evaluated on that operation.

### 2.9. Resource References and Existence Checking

The API exposes flat peer collections under `/ucp`; resources reference one another by identifier in request bodies rather than through nested URL hierarchies. Existence is validated in a strict left-to-right order: every identifier appearing in the path is resolved first — `program_id`, `catalog_id`, `schedule_id`, `period_id`, `work_order_id`, `estimate_id`, `charge_id`, `ciac_id`, `unit_id`, `reg_asset_id`, `entry_id` — and only then are the body's foreign-key references checked, such as `program_id` on work-order creation, `work_order_id` on estimate, charge, and CIAC creation, each `unit_catalog_id` inside a closeout's `unitization_lines`, and `source_work_order_id` on a regulatory-asset proposal. The first identifier that does not correspond to an existing resource rejects the request with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and no later reference is examined.

### 2.10. Derived Balances and Atomic Side Effects

Every derived balance named in this document — a program's committed, capitalized, written-off, and contra amounts, a work order's actuals, AFUDC total, and CWIP balance, a unit's accumulated depreciation, a regulatory asset's remaining balance — is recomputed immediately inside the request that triggered the change, and all cascaded side effects of a single request apply atomically: the request either lands with every cascade complete or is rejected with nothing persisted. A mutation that recomputes a parent resource's derived fields refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version` just as a direct edit would. The three ledger-entry collections (AFUDC, depreciation, amortization) are the system's audit trail: they are read-only through the API, created only by period closes, and compensated only by period reopens.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Capital Programs, the Unit Catalog, and Overhead Schedules

A `SystemAdmin` opens a **Capital Program** by supplying its budget `code` — unique across all programs, in a form such as "CAP-2026-DIST" — together with the `fiscal_year` and the `authorized_amount_cents`, the total authorized capital budget, which must be greater than zero. The program also carries the parameters that drive capitalized interest: `afudc_debt_rate_pct` and `afudc_equity_rate_pct` are the annual AFUDC debt and equity rates as percentages (each zero or greater), and `afudc_debt_weight_pct` is the debt weighting between 0 and 100 inclusive, with the equity weight implicitly one hundred minus the debt weight. Two method flags change downstream calculation behavior: `afudc_method` takes `HALF_MONTH` or `FULL_MONTH` and selects the AFUDC base convention for current-period charges (§3.9), while `ciac_method` takes `NET_OF_CIAC` or `GROSS_WITH_CONTRA` and selects how customer contributions offset capitalization and depreciation (§3.8). A new program starts `OPEN` with all four derived counters — `committed_amount_cents`, `capitalized_actual_cents`, `expense_writeoff_cents`, and `ciac_contra_balance_cents` — at zero, and its `available_amount_cents` is defined at every moment as the authorized amount minus the committed amount. A duplicate `code` is a request-shape failure rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Programs may be listed filtered by `fiscal_year` or `status`.

Closing a program is a `Controller` action valid only while the program stands `OPEN` — closing an already-`CLOSED` program is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and is only possible once every work order raised against it has reached a terminal state: if any work order of the program still stands at `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED`, the close is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_WORK_ORDERS`. Once the program is `CLOSED`, attempting to create a further work order against it fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED`.

The **Unit Catalog** supplies the standardized asset classes used at unitization. A `SystemAdmin` creates each entry with an `asset_class` — one of `POLE`, `TRANSFORMER`, `CONDUCTOR`, `METER`, or `REGULATOR`, unique across the catalog, with duplicates rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION` — a `standard_cost_cents` per unit that serves as the unitization weight and may be zero (a closeout whose lines all weight to zero is nonetheless rejected; see §3.8), and an `annual_depreciation_rate_pct`, the straight-line annual depreciation rate percentage, which must be greater than zero. Catalog entries may be listed filtered by `asset_class`.

**Overhead Schedules** hold the effective-dated loading rates applied to field charges. Each row, created by a `SystemAdmin`, pairs a `cost_type` (`LABOR`, `EQUIPMENT`, or `MATERIAL`) with an `order_type` (`CAPITAL` or `EXPENSE`) identifying which work-order type the rate loads, a `rate_pct` of zero or greater, and a half-open effective interval: `effective_from` is the inclusive lower bound and `effective_to` the exclusive upper bound, with a null `effective_to` meaning open-ended. Intervals for the same cost-type-and-order-type pair must never overlap; a new schedule whose interval intersects an existing one for the same pair is rejected with `ERR_OVERHEAD_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`, while intervals that merely touch — one row's `effective_to` equal to another's `effective_from` — do not constitute an overlap. A schedule's `status` is `ACTIVE` from creation, and schedules are immutable: the API offers no update or delete for them. Schedules may be listed filtered by `cost_type` or `order_type`.

### 3.2. The Accounting Calendar

An **Accounting Period** is the calendar unit to which every charge, accrual, and close belongs. A `SystemAdmin` creates each period with its `fiscal_year`, a `period_no` of at least one, and an inclusive date range whose `end_date` must fall on or after its `start_date`. The first period of the calendar may begin on any date. Every subsequent period must adjoin its predecessor exactly: its `start_date` must be precisely one day after the latest existing period's `end_date`, with gaps and overlaps alike rejected as `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ADJACENT`. Creation additionally requires that every existing period already be `CLOSED`; attempting to open a new period while another stands `OPEN` or `REOPENED` fails with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_OPEN`, since at most one period may occupy `OPEN` or `REOPENED` at any time.

A period's `status` cycles `OPEN` → `CLOSED` → `REOPENED` → `CLOSED` under the `Controller`'s close and reopen actions, whose full mechanics — accrual, compensation, and the CIAC sweep — are detailed in §3.9. The period's `closed_at` stays null until the first close and thereafter records the logical-clock instant of the most recent close. The single currently open period is the only container that accepts new charge postings, in-service declarations, and retirements, and it is the period whose close advances the accounting epoch. Periods may be listed filtered by `fiscal_year` or `status`.

### 3.3. Work Order Origination and Authorization

A `ProjectEngineer` originates a **Work Order** under an `OPEN` program by supplying the `program_id`, an `order_type` of `CAPITAL` or `EXPENSE`, a `title`, and the `initial_estimated_cost_cents`; targeting a `CLOSED` program is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_CLOSED`. Creation does two things at once: the work order itself starts at `DRAFT`, and the system seeds estimate number one from it — an Estimate carrying `estimate_seq` 1, `status` `PROPOSED`, an `estimated_cost_cents` copied from the `initial_estimated_cost_cents`, and the creator recorded as `submitted_by_user_id`. The order's own `estimate_seq` tracks the most recently created estimate and therefore begins at 1. Its remaining fields start empty or zeroed: `approved_estimate_cents` null until the first approval, `actual_direct_cents`, `afudc_total_cents`, and `cwip_cents` at zero, `variance_posture` at `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, `ciac_applied_cents` at zero, and `in_service_date`, `in_service_period_id`, `gross_cwip_cents`, `capitalizable_total_cents`, `cancel_writeoff_disposition`, `authorized_by_user_id`, `completed_by_user_id`, and `closed_by_user_id` all unset. The creator's header value is recorded as `created_by_user_id`, which anchors the segregation-of-duties check at authorization; the submitted `initial_estimated_cost_cents` is not retained as a work-order field — it lives on the seeded estimate alone. An `EXPENSE` order earns no AFUDC while it is being built and unitizes zero lines at closeout.

Submission is the engineer's handshake to supervision: submitting moves `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`, and a returned order resubmits along `RETURNED` → `SUBMITTED`, a loop that may repeat as often as review requires; each submission increments the work order's `version` by exactly one. A `WorkOrderSupervisor` who is not satisfied returns a `SUBMITTED` order to the engineer — `SUBMITTED` → `RETURNED` — supplying a `reason` (required, but not persisted on the order), again incrementing the version.

Authorization (`SUBMITTED` → `AUTHORIZED`) is the first act that commits program money, and it is guarded by four checks evaluated in the §2.8 order. The tier rule comes first: when the `approved_estimate_cents` is strictly below 10,000,000 cents — one hundred thousand dollars — a `WorkOrderSupervisor` may authorize, while at or above that amount only a `Controller` may, and exactly 10,000,000 cents belongs to the `Controller`; a supervisor reaching above the tier is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_REQUIRED`. Next, the from-state must be `SUBMITTED`. Then segregation of duties: the authorizer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `created_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The referential check follows — an estimate in `APPROVED` must exist, else `ERR_NO_APPROVED_ESTIMATE` — and the fiscal test comes last: the program's `available_amount_cents` must be at least the `approved_estimate_cents`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On success the program's `committed_amount_cents` increases by the approved estimate, `authorized_by_user_id` records the authorizer, the order's `variance_posture` is re-derived against the newly committed estimate per the rule of §3.5, and the order's `version` increments by one.

Once authorized, field execution belongs to the `WorkOrderSupervisor`: starting work moves `AUTHORIZED` → `IN_PROGRESS`, suspending moves `IN_PROGRESS` → `SUSPENDED`, and resuming returns `SUSPENDED` → `IN_PROGRESS`, each transition incrementing the version by one. While an order stands `SUSPENDED`, any attempt to post a charge against it is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_SUSPENDED`. Throughout these states the program's budget accounting obeys a conservation law: `committed_amount_cents` is the sum of `approved_estimate_cents` over the program's work orders in `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED` — a completed order stays committed until its closeout — and `available_amount_cents` is always the authorized amount minus the committed amount. Authorizing and then cancelling (or closing out) the same order therefore leaves the program's commitment identical to its pre-authorization value. The `capitalized_actual_cents` counter behaves differently: it accumulates each closed order's capitalizable total at closeout and never decreases. Work orders may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `status`, or `order_type`.

### 3.4. Estimating and the Re-Estimate Decision

Estimates version a work order's cost expectation. A `ProjectEngineer` records a new estimate against an order in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `SUSPENDED` by supplying the `work_order_id`, an `estimated_cost_cents` greater than zero, the mandatory `expected_work_order_version` implementing the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.7, and an optional free-text `notes` justification. Creating an estimate legitimately withdraws the order's currently `PROPOSED` estimate — a system transition to `WITHDRAWN` — assigns the newcomer the order's next `estimate_seq`, and increments the work order's `version` by one; precisely because creation mutates the order, a request whose `expected_work_order_version` no longer matches the order's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). On a fresh estimate, `supersedes_estimate_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, and `routing_tier` are all null until a decision occurs.

Deciding an estimate — approval or rejection — is permitted only while the work order stands at `SUBMITTED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `SUSPENDED`; any other order state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the estimate itself must still be `PROPOSED`. The decision routes by tier, computed at decision time rather than at submission: when the order has no currently approved estimate — the first estimate is always such a case — the tier is `SUPERVISOR`; otherwise the absolute difference between the new `estimated_cost_cents` and the current `approved_estimate_cents`, scaled by one hundred, is compared against the current approved amount scaled by ten, and reaching or exceeding that bound routes the decision to `CONTROLLER` — the boundary is inclusive, so a change of exactly 10.00% requires the `Controller` — while anything smaller stays `SUPERVISOR`. A `WorkOrderSupervisor` who attempts a `CONTROLLER`-tier decision is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_REQUIRED`; a `Controller` may decide any tier. Segregation of duties applies to both decisions: the decider's `X-User-ID` must differ from the estimate's `submitted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval alone carries two further guards — the OCC field `expected_work_order_version` from §2.7, and the fiscal floor that an estimate may not be approved below the order's `actual_direct_cents`, else `ERR_ESTIMATE_BELOW_ACTUALS`. Per the §2.8 ordering, the tier check is evaluated first on a decision request, then the stale-version check, then the state checks, then segregation of duties, and the below-actuals floor last.

Approval rewires the order's financial picture in one atomic step. The previously `APPROVED` estimate, when one exists, becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and the newly approved estimate's `supersedes_estimate_id` points back at that predecessor; the order's `approved_estimate_cents` takes the new `estimated_cost_cents`; and the estimate records its `routing_tier` (`SUPERVISOR` or `CONTROLLER`) and its `decided_by_user_id`. When the order stands at `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `SUSPENDED`, the program's `committed_amount_cents` shifts by the difference between the newly approved amount and the previously approved one. The order's `variance_posture` is then recomputed against the new approved amount, so a `RE_ESTIMATE_REQUIRED` hold clears once the actuals scaled by one hundred no longer exceed the approved estimate scaled by one hundred ten. The work order's `version` increments by one.

Rejection is the parallel decision with equal rigor and no financial rewiring: the same order-state set, the same tier routing computed the same way, and the same segregation-of-duties rule against `submitted_by_user_id`, but no version field on the request and no budget or variance adjustment — the estimate simply becomes `REJECTED` with `decided_by_user_id` recorded. Estimates may be listed filtered by `work_order_id`, `status`, or `supersedes_estimate_id`, which makes the supersession chain directly traversable.

### 3.5. Field Cost Capture: Charges, Overhead Loading, Reversals, and the Variance Hold

A `FieldClerk` posts a **Cost Charge** against an order with the `work_order_id`, a `cost_type` of `LABOR`, `EQUIPMENT`, or `MATERIAL`, a `base_amount_cents` greater than zero, and a `charge_date`. The order must stand at `AUTHORIZED` or `IN_PROGRESS`: posting against a `SUSPENDED` order is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_SUSPENDED`, and any other state — including `COMPLETED`, whose charges are frozen — is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The `charge_date` must fall inside an existing accounting period, else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, and that period must currently be `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`; the containing period's id is then recorded on the row as `accounting_period_id`.

Overhead loading resolves through the overhead schedules. The applicable rate is the unique schedule row whose `cost_type` matches the charge, whose `order_type` equals the work order's `order_type`, and whose half-open interval covers the charge date — on or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`, or open-ended when `effective_to` is null. Because schedule creation forbids overlaps, at most one row can match; when none covers the date, the posting fails with `ERR_OVERHEAD_RATE_NOT_FOUND`. The resolved rate is pinned onto the charge as `overhead_rate_pct`; the `overhead_amount_cents` is the base multiplied by that rate and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent per the pinned per-row rounding of §2.5; and the `total_cents` is the base plus the overhead. The row is born with `kind` `ORIGINAL`, `status` `POSTED`, a null `reverses_charge_id`, and the clerk's identity in `posted_by_user_id`.

Every posting recomputes the order's ledger in place. The `actual_direct_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` over all of the order's charge rows, in both kinds — an `ORIGINAL` row remains in the sum even after it flips to `REVERSED`, because its compensating `REVERSAL` row carries the exact negation — and it excludes AFUDC entirely, making it the variance base. The `afudc_total_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the order's AFUDC entries whose `status` remains `ACCRUED`. The `cwip_cents` — the construction-work-in-progress balance — is the sum of those two. The `variance_posture` is then re-derived from the new actuals: while no estimate is approved it stays `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`; once an `approved_estimate_cents` exists, actuals scaled by one hundred strictly greater than the approved estimate scaled by one hundred ten flips the posture to `RE_ESTIMATE_REQUIRED`, while exactly 110.00% remains within tolerance. While the posture stands at `RE_ESTIMATE_REQUIRED`, any further charge posting is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409) — though the charge that itself crosses the threshold is allowed to post, flipping the posture only for the next charge — and the hold clears when an approved re-estimate or a reversal brings the comparison back within bound. Following the §2.8 order, a posting that would fail several of these guards reports the state error first, then the temporal period errors, then the missing overhead rate, and the variance hold last.

Reversal corrects the ledger without erasing it. A `FieldClerk` reverses a charge only while it is a `POSTED` row of `kind` `ORIGINAL`: attempting to reverse a `REVERSAL` row is rejected with `ERR_REVERSAL_NOT_ALLOWED`, and reversing an original already in `REVERSED` is `ERR_CHARGE_ALREADY_REVERSED`. An accounting period must currently stand `OPEN` or `REOPENED` to receive the reversal row, else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`. The reversal itself is a brand-new charge row, created with `kind` `REVERSAL`, its `reverses_charge_id` pointing at the reversed original, its base, overhead, and total amounts exactly negated, its `overhead_rate_pct` copied from the original, its `posted_by_user_id` recording the reversing clerk, and its `charge_date` stamped with the current logical-clock date as an informational marker; as a prior-period adjustment, its `accounting_period_id` is the single currently `OPEN` or `REOPENED` period regardless of that clock date, because the clock date never re-enters a `CLOSED` period. The original flips `POSTED` → `REVERSED`, while reversal rows stay `POSTED` forever and can never themselves be reversed. The pair nets to zero, so the order's `actual_direct_cents` and `cwip_cents` return to their pre-charge values — posting a charge and then reversing it leaves the ledger identical to its state before the posting — and the `variance_posture` is recomputed, which may clear a `RE_ESTIMATE_REQUIRED` hold. Charges may be listed filtered by `work_order_id`, `accounting_period_id`, `cost_type`, `kind`, or `reverses_charge_id`.

### 3.6. Contributions in Aid of Construction

A customer who funds part of a construction job does so through a **CIAC Agreement**. A `RateAnalyst` records the pledge with the `work_order_id`, a `pledged_cents` greater than zero, and a `pledge_expiry_at` instant. Pledges attach only while the order is neither terminal nor completed — that is, while it stands at `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `SUSPENDED`. The agreement is born `PLEDGED` with `received_cents`, `applied_cents`, `forfeited_cents`, and `refunded_cents` all unset.

Receipt is all-or-nothing: recording receipt moves `PLEDGED` → `RECEIVED` and sets `received_cents` equal to the full `pledged_cents` — partial receipts do not exist. Two guards apply. The logical clock must not have passed `pledge_expiry_at`; a receipt attempted after expiry is rejected with `ERR_CIAC_EXPIRED`, and the agreement flips to `EXPIRED` as a side effect of that failed attempt. And the work order must stand at `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED`, or `COMPLETED` — funds may legitimately arrive after the in-service date but before closeout — with any other order state rejected as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Expiry otherwise advances lazily and by sweep: a `PLEDGED` agreement becomes `EXPIRED` when the logical clock has passed its `pledge_expiry_at`, observed either on a receive attempt or at every period close (§3.9).

The remainder of the agreement's lifecycle is driven by the work order's fate. When the order is cancelled before receipt, a `PLEDGED` agreement becomes `CANCELLED`. When the order is cancelled after receipt, the agreement becomes `REFUNDED`, with a forfeiture that depends on how far the work advanced (§3.7). At closeout, a `RECEIVED` agreement becomes `APPLIED` against the CWIP balance (§3.8). And while the money is held, CIAC-financed CWIP earns no AFUDC — received contributions reduce the AFUDC base at every period close (§3.9). Agreements may be listed filtered by `work_order_id` or `status`.

### 3.7. Completion, Cancellation, and Regulatory Deferral

Completion declares the asset ready for service. A `WorkOrderSupervisor` completes an order (`IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETED`) by supplying the `in_service_date`, which must fall inside the single accounting period currently `OPEN` or `REOPENED`; a date outside that period is rejected with `ERR_IN_SERVICE_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. On success the containing period's id is recorded as the order's `in_service_period_id`, the caller is recorded as `completed_by_user_id`, and the version increments. Completion freezes the ledger going forward: any further charge posting against the order is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and AFUDC stops accruing for the order at the in-service date — subsequent period closes produce no further AFUDC entries for it.

Cancellation is the supervisor's exit ramp, available from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, and `SUSPENDED`, with consequences that scale with how committed the order was. From `DRAFT`, the only cascade is that any `PLEDGED` CIAC agreements become `CANCELLED`. From `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED`, the order's `PROPOSED` estimates are additionally withdrawn alongside the CIAC cancellation. In all three early states no budget has been committed and no CWIP has accrued, so no write-off arises; the version simply increments.

From `AUTHORIZED` the consequences deepen. The committed budget is released — the program's `committed_amount_cents` decreases by the order's `approved_estimate_cents` — and whenever the order's `cwip_cents` is greater than zero, the cancellation request must carry a `writeoff_disposition`, else it is rejected with `ERR_WRITEOFF_DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 400); choosing `REGULATORY_DEFERRAL` additionally requires a `writeoff_amortization_months` term. With disposition `EXPENSE`, the program's `expense_writeoff_cents` increases by the order's `cwip_cents`. With disposition `REGULATORY_DEFERRAL`, a Regulatory Asset is created instead: born `PROPOSED`, its `original_cents` equal to the order's `cwip_cents`, its `source_work_order_id` pointing at the cancelled order, its amortization term taken from `writeoff_amortization_months`, its `proposed_by_user_id` set to the caller, and its `description` system-generated as "Cancelled work order write-off: " followed by the order's title. The chosen disposition is recorded on the order as `cancel_writeoff_disposition`, taking `EXPENSE` or `REGULATORY_DEFERRAL`; a `writeoff_disposition` supplied while `cwip_cents` is zero is accepted but not recorded, the field remaining unset. Because an order cancelled from `AUTHORIZED` never reached `IN_PROGRESS`, its `RECEIVED` CIAC agreements are refunded in full — `forfeited_cents` of zero and `refunded_cents` equal to the `received_cents`, the agreement ending `REFUNDED` — while any still `PLEDGED` become `CANCELLED`. The version increments.

From `IN_PROGRESS`, the same budget release, write-off, and CIAC cascades apply with one sharpening: the refund now carries a ten percent forfeiture. The `forfeited_cents` is the `received_cents` multiplied by ten hundredths, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the `refunded_cents` is what remains of the receipt. Cancellation from `SUSPENDED` behaves identically to cancellation from `IN_PROGRESS` in every respect, including the ten percent forfeiture. At closeout, by contrast, no forfeiture ever applies — excess contributions are simply refunded (§3.8).

A `RateAnalyst` may also propose a regulatory deferral directly, without a cancelling order — storm restoration and cancelled-project write-offs are typical descriptions — by supplying a `description`, an `original_cents` greater than zero, an `amortization_months` term of at least one, and optionally a `source_work_order_id` when the deferral arises from a specific work order, whether from a cancellation write-off or from closeout variance coverage (§3.8). The proposal is born `PROPOSED` with `remaining_cents` initialized to the full `original_cents`, the caller recorded as `proposed_by_user_id`, and `approved_by_user_id` unset. Only a `Controller` may decide it, and never the proposer: both approval and rejection require the decider's `X-User-ID` to differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval sets `approved_by_user_id` and moves `PROPOSED` → `APPROVED`; rejection moves `PROPOSED` → `REJECTED`. Amortization begins at the first period close after approval and runs on the schedule described in §3.9. Regulatory assets may be listed filtered by `source_work_order_id` or `status`.

### 3.8. Closeout and the Unitization Waterfall

Closeout (`COMPLETED` → `CLOSED`) is the `Controller`'s act that converts a finished job into fixed assets, and its request carries the `unitization_lines` describing the physical units installed. Its guards evaluate in the §2.8 order. Segregation of duties comes first among them: the closer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `completed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then the referential checks: no CIAC agreement of the order may still stand `PLEDGED` (`ERR_CIAC_PLEDGED_OUTSTANDING`); a `CAPITAL` order requires a non-empty `unitization_lines` while an `EXPENSE` order requires it empty — an omitted `unitization_lines` reads as an empty array — either violation rejected as `ERR_UNITIZATION_EMPTY`; every line must carry an integer `quantity` of at least one and reference an existing `unit_catalog_id`; and the lines' total target — the sum over the lines of each `quantity` multiplied by its catalog row's `standard_cost_cents` — must be greater than zero, else `ERR_UNITIZATION_ZERO_TARGET`, a condition reachable only when every referenced catalog row has a zero standard cost.

The fiscal variance gate is evaluated last. The overrun is the order's `actual_direct_cents` minus its `approved_estimate_cents`, and the tolerance is the greatest whole-cent amount not exceeding fifteen percent of the `approved_estimate_cents`. The gate passes outright whenever the overrun scaled by one hundred is less than or equal to the approved estimate scaled by fifteen, so underruns never block closeout. Beyond tolerance, the order must carry regulatory coverage for the excess: the coverage needed is the overrun minus the tolerance, and the coverage available is the sum of `original_cents` over regulatory assets whose `source_work_order_id` is this order and whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `AMORTIZING`, or `FULLY_AMORTIZED`. With no qualifying coverage at all the closeout is rejected `ERR_CLOSEOUT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`; coverage that exists but sums below the needed amount is `ERR_REGULATORY_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`; and coverage at least equal to the need passes — the boundary is inclusive. A concrete instance: an approved estimate of 100,000 cents against 116,000 cents of actuals yields a 15,000-cent tolerance and a 1,000-cent coverage requirement.

On success the order's construction balance is snapshotted and the customer money settles. The `gross_cwip_cents` becomes the final `cwip_cents`, frozen at this instant. The `ciac_applied_cents` becomes the lesser of the total `received_cents` across the order's `RECEIVED` agreements and that gross CWIP. Each `RECEIVED` agreement moves to `APPLIED` in creation order, recording its `applied_cents` as the lesser of what it received and the gross CWIP still unabsorbed after earlier-created agreements have applied, and its `refunded_cents` as the received amount minus the applied amount — the closeout excess refund, with no forfeiture, its `forfeited_cents` remaining unset. The `capitalizable_total_cents` then follows the program's `ciac_method`: under `NET_OF_CIAC` it is the gross CWIP minus the applied contribution, so depreciation later runs on the net basis; under `GROSS_WITH_CONTRA` it is the full gross CWIP, and the program's `ciac_contra_balance_cents` increases by the `ciac_applied_cents`, establishing the contra-asset that subsequent depreciation contra credits will draw down (§3.9).

The unitization waterfall then spreads the capitalizable total across the listing lines. Each line's target is its `quantity` multiplied by the referenced catalog row's `standard_cost_cents`; call the sum of all targets S. Each line's raw allocation is the capitalizable total multiplied by its target and divided by S, rounded half up to a whole cent — per-line rounding first, exactly as §2.5's pinned discipline requires. The remainder, defined as the capitalizable total minus the sum of the raw allocations and therefore possibly negative, is applied in full to the line with the greatest target; a tie breaks toward the greatest `standard_cost_cents`, and a further tie breaks toward the `asset_class` that sorts earliest alphabetically. One **Fixed Asset Unit** is created per line, carrying the final `allocated_cost_cents`, `status` `IN_SERVICE`, the `in_service_period_id` copied from the work order, the order reference recorded as its `work_order_id`, the line's `unit_catalog_id` and `quantity`, and a `line_no` equal to the line's zero-based index within the request's `unitization_lines` array. A pinned worked example: with a total of 100,003 cents and line targets of 50,000, 20,000, and 30,000 (an S of 100,000), the raw allocations are 50,002, 20,001, and 30,001; the remainder of minus one cent lands on the `TRANSFORMER` line as the greatest target, producing final allocations of 50,001, 20,001, and 30,001 that sum to exactly 100,003.

The program rollup completes the order's ledger: the program's `committed_amount_cents` decreases by the `approved_estimate_cents` and its `capitalized_actual_cents` increases by the `capitalizable_total_cents`; the order records its `closed_by_user_id` and its version increments by one. The new units enter service with `accumulated_depreciation_cents` at zero and both `retirement_period_id` and `retirement_nbv_cents` unset; they may be listed filtered by `work_order_id`, `unit_catalog_id`, or `status`. A unit's service life ends when a `Controller` retires it (`IN_SERVICE` → `RETIRED`) by supplying a `retirement_date` that must fall inside the single accounting period currently `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_RETIREMENT_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`; retiring a unit already `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_RETIRED`. Retirement records the `retirement_period_id`; the retirement period's own close then posts the unit's final half-month depreciation entry and only afterward sets the `retirement_nbv_cents` to the allocated cost minus the accumulated depreciation including that final entry (§3.9).

### 3.9. The Period Close and Reopen: AFUDC, Depreciation, Amortization, and the CIAC Sweep

Closing a period is the system's ops-run checkpoint — a `Controller` action valid from `OPEN` or `REOPENED` that executes five steps in order, atomically, and then marks the period `CLOSED`. Re-closing a `REOPENED` period recomputes every accrual fresh from the state current at that moment, so charges posted during the reopen window are included in the new close. Periods accrue in chronological close order: each period's beginning position is the prior period's ending position.

**Step 1 — AFUDC accrual.** For each `CAPITAL` work order standing at `AUTHORIZED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `SUSPENDED` whose program is still `OPEN`, the close accrues an allowance for funds used during construction; `EXPENSE` orders never accrue AFUDC, and accrual stops at completion. The composite monthly rate percentage derives from the program's parameters: the `afudc_debt_weight_pct` multiplied by the `afudc_debt_rate_pct`, plus one hundred minus the debt weight multiplied by the `afudc_equity_rate_pct`, the total divided by one hundred and then by twelve. Each order's accrual then resolves in a pinned evaluation order. First, the beginning CWIP is the order's carry-in balance from prior periods — the charge rows attributed to earlier periods plus all still-accrued AFUDC — which includes all prior periods' AFUDC, because AFUDC compounds across periods, and excludes both this period's charge rows (they enter only at the third step) and this period's own accrual. Second, the net beginning is the greater of zero and the beginning CWIP minus the CIAC received to date — the sum of `received_cents` over the order's agreements in `RECEIVED` or `APPLIED` — because CIAC-financed CWIP earns no AFUDC. Third, the period's charges are the sum of `total_cents` over the charge rows counted by the CWIP aggregation whose `accounting_period_id` is this period. Fourth, the base follows the program's `afudc_method`: under `HALF_MONTH` it is the net beginning plus one half of the period's charges, a mid-period convention; under `FULL_MONTH` it is the net beginning plus the period's full charges. Fifth, the entry's `amount_cents` is the base multiplied by the composite monthly rate percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent; that multiplication uses the exact base, which under the half-month convention may end in a half cent, while the `base_cents` recorded on the entry is the base itself rounded half up to a whole cent; when that result rounds to zero, no entry is created for the order at all. Each created AFUDC entry records its `work_order_id`, the closing period's id as `accounting_period_id`, a snapshot of the program's convention in `method` (`HALF_MONTH` or `FULL_MONTH`), the `base_cents` used, the `rate_pct_applied`, and a `status` of `ACCRUED`; the order's `afudc_total_cents` and `cwip_cents` update accordingly. The two conventions diverge predictably: 200,000 cents of current-period charges at a one-percent monthly rate accrue 1,000 cents under the half-month convention, while identical inputs accrue 2,000 cents under the full-month convention — exactly double the half-month result when the beginning CWIP is zero.

**Step 2 — Depreciation.** The close posts one depreciation entry for each unit `IN_SERVICE` whose in-service period's `start_date` falls on or before the closing period's `end_date`, plus one final entry for each unit `RETIRED` whose `retirement_period_id` is the closing period; periods strictly before a unit's in-service period and strictly after its retirement period receive nothing. The computation rounds at each step. The full monthly amount is the unit's `allocated_cost_cents` multiplied by the `annual_depreciation_rate_pct` of its catalog asset class and divided by twelve hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent. A factor then applies: one half when the closing period is the unit's in-service period, one half when it is the retirement period, and one otherwise — and a unit placed in service and retired within the same period receives a single half-month entry, not a full one. The entry's `amount_cents` is the full monthly amount multiplied by the factor, again rounded half up. Each entry records its `fixed_asset_unit_id`, the closing period as `accounting_period_id`, and a `status` of `POSTED`, and the unit's `accumulated_depreciation_cents` — defined as the sum of `amount_cents` over the unit's entries still `POSTED` — rises accordingly. For units retired in this period, the close then sets `retirement_nbv_cents` to the `allocated_cost_cents` minus the accumulated depreciation including this final entry. Under a `GROSS_WITH_CONTRA` program each entry additionally carries a `ciac_contra_credit_cents`: the entry's amount multiplied by the order's `ciac_applied_cents` and divided by its `gross_cwip_cents`, rounded half up — and zero whenever the order's gross CWIP is zero, where that ratio is undefined — and the credits draw down the program's `ciac_contra_balance_cents`; under `NET_OF_CIAC` the credit is always zero. The two methods diverge on identical physical data: a net basis of 100,000 cents at a twelve-percent annual rate yields a full month of 1,000 cents and an in-service month of 500 cents with no contra credit, while the gross basis of 120,000 cents at the same rate yields a full month of 1,200 cents and an in-service month of 600 cents, with a 600-cent entry carrying a 100-cent contra credit when 20,000 cents of the 120,000-cent gross was CIAC-financed.

**Step 3 — Amortization.** Each regulatory asset standing at `APPROVED` or `AMORTIZING` at close time receives one amortization entry. The standard monthly installment is the `original_cents` divided by the `amortization_months`, rounded half up to a whole cent. For the final scheduled month — when the number of entries already `POSTED` for the asset is one less than the amortization term — the entry is whatever `remaining_cents` remains, so the final entry absorbs the rounding remainder; in every other month the entry is the lesser of the monthly installment and the remaining balance. The entry records its `regulatory_asset_id`, the closing period as `accounting_period_id`, and a `status` of `POSTED`, and the asset's `remaining_cents` falls by the entry amount. The first such entry moves the asset `APPROVED` → `AMORTIZING`, and an entry that brings `remaining_cents` to exactly zero moves it `AMORTIZING` → `FULLY_AMORTIZED`; an entry that is simultaneously the first and the zeroing one — as with a one-month term — moves the asset directly from `APPROVED` to `FULLY_AMORTIZED`. A worked instance: an original 10,000 cents over three months amortizes 3,333, then 3,333, then 3,334, the final scheduled month plugging the remainder.

**Step 4 — CIAC sweep.** Every agreement still `PLEDGED` whose `pledge_expiry_at` lies earlier than the current logical clock flips to `EXPIRED`.

**Step 5 — Seal.** The period's `status` becomes `CLOSED` and its `closed_at` records the logical-clock instant of the close.

Reopening is the compensating mirror, available to a `Controller` under two guards: no accounting period may currently stand `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_OPEN`, and the target must be the most recently created `CLOSED` period, else `ERR_PERIOD_REOPEN_NOT_LATEST`. The reopen compensates everything the period's close produced: every AFUDC, depreciation, and amortization entry posted by that close and still `ACCRUED` or `POSTED` flips to `REVERSED`, and the affected balances — each order's `afudc_total_cents` and `cwip_cents`, each unit's `accumulated_depreciation_cents` and `retirement_nbv_cents`, each asset's `remaining_cents`, and each program's `ciac_contra_balance_cents` — are recomputed without the reversed entries. Regulatory-asset status follows the restored balance: an asset whose `remaining_cents` returns to its `original_cents` stands again at `APPROVED`, one left strictly between zero and the original stands at `AMORTIZING`, and a `FULLY_AMORTIZED` asset whose final entry is reversed returns to `AMORTIZING` with a positive remainder. The step-4 CIAC sweep is not compensated: an agreement flipped to `EXPIRED` by the close remains `EXPIRED` after the reopen. The period itself ends at `REOPENED`, from which a fresh close repeats the same five steps. All three entry logs are read-only through the API: AFUDC entries filter by `work_order_id`, `accounting_period_id`, or `status`; depreciation entries by `fixed_asset_unit_id`, `accounting_period_id`, or `status`; amortization entries by `regulatory_asset_id`, `accounting_period_id`, or `status`.
