# Data Labeling Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Turning a customer's raw labeling specification into shipped training data is, in practice, an exercise in coordinating a large, distributed, multilingual workforce against deadlines that do not move. This document specifies the API surface of an operations platform that owns that coordination end to end. A customer engagement — an "engagement" is registered here as a **CustomerProgram** — begins as a ticket-like code such as `program_code` `AX-4410`, carries a single working `language_code`, and declares one labeling task family through its `instruction_model`. From that engagement the platform decomposes the specification into hierarchical **SpecUnit** records, attaches customer payloads as **LabelingItem** pieces, dispatches consensus panels of independent workers, and measures the resulting quality against customer-supplied ground truth.

The pressure this system is designed to absorb comes from four directions at once. Quality has to be defended without human supervision of every answer, so the platform leans on multi-worker consensus, gold-standard audits, and a reviewer tier that can break ties and adjudicate worker disputes. Money has to be defended without over-paying for mediocre work, so payouts are computed from a dynamic rate that responds to the worker's skill tier and their recent failure streak, guarded by a weekly ceiling. Capacity has to be defended against a fixed customer deadline, so every unit of work carries a points cost that scales with confidentiality class and with accuracy. And confidentiality has to be defended absolutely, so a single serious breach can restrict a worker or an entire engagement to a stop.

The API itself is a REST service mounted under `/api`. Its resource graph is intentionally deep: engagements own units, units own pieces, pieces own assignments and audits, and disputes cut across all of them. Several lifecycles in this system are cyclic rather than one-directional — a unit can return from `COMPLETE` to `ACTIVE`, a qualification can fall from `QUALIFIED` back to `PROBATION`, and a piece can leave `COMPLETED` for `REWORK` and come back again — and the specification that follows pins each of those cycles precisely enough that an implementation has no room to guess.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every request the platform serves, regardless of which resource is being touched. They cover identity and authorization, the shape of every response body, pagination, the invariants that hold across the resource hierarchy, and the concurrency and precision conventions the rest of the document assumes.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares their operational capacity. The platform recognizes exactly four role names, spelled as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `PlatformReviewer`, `Worker`, and `Anonymous`. The two headers must both be present and non-empty on every request; when either is missing or empty the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The single endpoint that is exempt from this requirement is worker registration. Once headers are present, the acting role must be one of the roles the endpoint declares; a well-formed request from a role the endpoint does not admit is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

The two roles carry their identity differently, and implementations must not conflate them. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `adjudicator_id` on an **AppealClaim** where the appeal lifecycle writes the acting reviewer's identifier) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same is true when `X-User-Role` is `PlatformReviewer`: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server records it where a transition stamps it but never resolves it against a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque identifier that is not resolved against anything. When `X-User-Role` is `Worker`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting **WorkerIdentity**; ownership checks on any resource that references a `WorkerIdentity` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In particular, the `worker_id` carried by a **Commit**, by an **AppealClaim**, and by a **ConfidentialityIncident** must equal the `X-User-ID` of the acting `Worker` for that worker to act on it. A worker who reaches a resource owned by another worker is treated exactly as though the resource did not exist: reading or committing against another worker's assignment is `ERR_COMMIT_NOT_FOUND` (404), and reading or settling against another worker's **WorkerIdentity** is `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND` (404). The one ownership refusal reported differently is a rework hand-in that names an assignment another worker owns, which is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) because it is the same rule that keeps a reviewer out of a panel they answered.

A further restriction runs *within* the reviewer role and is a property of the data rather than of the role table: the platform enforces segregation of duties between labeling and judging. Any reviewer action that evaluates, breaks, or adjudicates a specific piece is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) when the acting reviewer holds a **Commit** on that piece. That single guard covers reviewer tie-breaking on a held panel, gold-audit grading, and both the proposal and concurrence steps of the appeal lifecycle. A reviewer who is the claim's own `adjudicator_id` is additionally barred from concurring with that claim, and that case is reported under the same error. `PlatformAdmin` is the counterpart role: it decomposes specifications, opens and freezes engagements, dispatches panels, reports and resolves confidentiality incidents, settles worker payouts, and drives the logical clock. `Worker` commits labels, abstains, hands in corrected rework answers, settles receivables, and files disputes.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` query parameter giving the maximum number of resources to return, and an `offset` query parameter giving the number of resources to skip. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10, and it must fall between a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 inclusive; a limit below the minimum, a limit above the maximum, a negative offset, or a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0. Every list endpoint takes that same default of 10 and none of them overrides it, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus `limit` is strictly below `total_count`. Unless a statement in §3 says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources ordered by creation timestamp ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Two list endpoints in this system deviate from the default ordering. The commit list for a piece returns assignments ordered by `dispatch_rank` ascending rather than by creation time, because the rank is what the tie-break logic reads. The item list for a unit returns pieces ordered by `item_seq` ascending, which is the 1-based insertion sequence assigned when the piece was attached to its unit. Each deviation is scoped to the filtered list a statement names and to no other: the ordering deviates only when the list is the commit list of a single piece, or the item list already narrowed to one unit by its `spec_unit_id` filter. Every other list — including the item list queried by `program_id` or `consensus_status` alone, without `spec_unit_id` — keeps the default creation-timestamp order.

### 2.4. Decomposition Depth and the Instruction Contract

The specification hierarchy is four levels deep and every level depends on the one above it. An engagement is decomposed into **SpecUnit** records through the `parent_unit_id` field, which may be left null for a root unit or set to the `id` of another unit to declare that unit as this one's decomposition parent. The graph is acyclic; a submission whose `parent_unit_id` would close a cycle is refused with `ERR_SPEC_UNIT_CYCLE` (409), and this cycle check is reported ahead of the `unit_code` scope check, so a submission that both names a cycle-closing parent and reuses a code already carried in that parent chain — as a cycle-closing submission necessarily does — is refused for the cycle rather than for the duplicate. Uniqueness is scoped rather than global: `unit_code` must be unique within its owning engagement, `item_key` must be unique within its spec unit, and `handle` must be unique across the whole workforce. A violation of any of those three is reported identically as `ERR_UNIT_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409).

Each unit carries an instruction document whose current revision is `instruction_revision` and whose status is `instruction_status`, which moves `DRAFT` → `PUBLISHED` → `SUPERSEDED`. The document enumerates an ordered list of payload field names in `field_schema`, and it declares whether it publishes edge-case guidance through `has_edge_case_guidance` and how many edge cases it enumerates through `edge_case_count`. The two must agree: a unit that claims to publish guidance while asserting an enumerated count above zero is contradictory and is refused with `ERR_EDGE_CASE_GUIDANCE_CONFLICT` (409). Every answer a worker submits — a panel commit or a corrected rework answer alike — must carry a payload whose key set equals the unit's `field_schema` key set exactly; a payload that adds a key the document does not enumerate or drops one it does is refused with `ERR_FIELD_SCHEMA_MISMATCH` (400).

`max_items` is the ceiling on how many pieces a unit may hold, and `item_count` is the live count of pieces attached to it; `item_count` may never exceed `max_items`, and the remaining headroom is exposed as `remaining_item_slots`, the difference between the two. A piece carries `required_units`, the number of certified items the unit must already hold before the piece may be released; the field defaults to 1 and a value of zero would mean the piece publishes with no detection coverage at all. Attachment of a piece assigns its `item_seq` as the next insertion sequence inside the unit and raises `item_count`. A piece attaches in container status `DRAFT` only while its unit is not yet in production; when the owning unit is already `ACTIVE` with a `piece_status` of `IN_PRODUCTION`, `CONSENSUS`, or `REWORK`, the new piece is attached directly at container status `RELEASED` with its `item_status` still `PENDING`, because the release cascade that §3.3 describes applies to it the moment it exists. Attaching a piece to a unit whose `item_count` has already reached its `max_items` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), and a reused `item_key` within the unit is `ERR_UNIT_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409). Pieces may be filtered on the item list by `spec_unit_id`, by `program_id`, or by `consensus_status`.

### 2.5. Confidentiality as a Containment Boundary

Confidentiality is modeled as an incident register rather than as a flag, because the platform has to be able to tell the difference between a program that is *classed* sensitive and a program that is *currently in trouble*. An engagement declares a `sensitivity_class` of `STANDARD`, `SENSITIVE`, or `RESTRICTED` at registration, defaulting to `STANDARD`. Incidents are reported against a worker through the **ConfidentialityIncident** entity with a `category` of `PII_EXPOSURE`, `DATASET_DOWNLOAD`, `SCREEN_RECORDING`, or `UNAUTHORIZED_SHARE`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `CATASTROPHIC`, a free-text `description_text`, and the `program_code` of the exposed engagement. An incident starts `OPEN` and later becomes `RESOLVED` or `DISMISSED`.

Two derived containment flags track the *unresolved* portion of the register. On the engagement, `open_incident_count` counts the incidents on that program whose status is `OPEN`, and `operational_restricted` is true while any incident on that program has status `OPEN` *and* a severity that is `MAJOR` or `CATASTROPHIC`. Both are recomputed whenever an incident is reported against or resolved on the program, which is the distinction the `incident_action` field carries: its value is `REPORT` or `RESOLVE`, the reporting action opens a new incident against the worker and recomputes both flags, while the resolving action closes the worker's outstanding incident on that engagement — the most recently filed one still `OPEN` — and recomputes both flags again. The engagement also carries `incident_severity`, the worst unresolved severity observed on it, defaulting to `NONE`. On the worker, the mirror flag is `restricted`, true while any incident naming that worker has status `OPEN`. A restricted worker may not commit labels, may not be dispatched work, and may not be paid — an attempt to do any of those is refused with `ERR_WORKER_RESTRICTED` (403). An operationally restricted engagement admits no labeling work at all: dispatching a panel, creating a gold audit, or decomposing a further unit under it is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_OPERATIONALLY_RESTRICTED` (409).

One more confidentiality rule crosses from the register into routing. An engagement whose `sensitivity_class` is `RESTRICTED` may only be served by workers whose qualification carries `restricted_accessible` true, which is a condition that holds while the qualification's `tier` is `EXPERT` *and* its status is `QUALIFIED` simultaneously. A worker on the `SENSITIVE` or `STANDARD` classes is not gated by this flag.

### 2.6. Certification, Degrees of Skill, and the Ability to Work At All

A **WorkerQualification** is the join between a worker and one `language_code`, unique per worker-and-language pair, and it is the record that decides whether the worker is routable, at what rate, and at what tier. The tier is `NOVICE`, `PROFICIENT`, or `EXPERT` and sets both the minimum score the worker must clear and the pay multiplier. The qualification's `qualification_score` is not an input and not an audit result: it is derived deterministically from the worker's `handle` and is expressed as the sum of 5000 and the remainder, modulo 5000, of the quantity `len(handle)` multiplied by 977 plus the sum of the handle's character codes multiplied by 31 plus the sum of the language code's character codes multiplied by 17. The result is therefore always an integer between 5000 and 9999 inclusive. `tier_min_score` is 5000 for `NOVICE`, 6500 for `PROFICIENT`, and 8000 for `EXPERT`, and the multiplier `tier_multiplier` is 1.0, 1.25, and 1.5 respectively.

Progress toward a qualification is tracked by two counters. `training_passed_count` is the number of training tasks passed, `training_required_count` is how many must pass before the qualification may be verified and defaults to 3, and `gold_audits_dispatched` is the running count of `GOLD` commits ever dispatched to this qualification. A worker's `handle` is an immutable workforce handle unique across the entire workforce, and it is the value the deterministic scoring reads; a worker may also carry an optional `display_name` for readability, which no rule in this document consults. Field workers hold a `payout_currency` of `USD`, `MXN`, `PHP`, `VND`, `BRL`, or `INR`, defaulting to `USD`, and a `settlement_min_cents` floor, defaulting to 1000, below which a settlement request is not accepted.

The two-field enrollment surface is worth stating plainly because the action name is carried in the payload. Enrolling, verifying, or resetting a qualification is expressed through `qualification_action`, whose value is one of `ENROLL`, `VERIFY`, or `RESET`, combined with `language_code` and `tier`, and `training_required_count` may be supplied alongside. The `ENROLL` action creates the qualification in `IN_TRAINING`. The `VERIFY` action enforces two gates: `training_passed_count` must be at least `training_required_count`, and `qualification_score` must be at or above `tier_min_score`; the score gate alone is reported as `ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_TIER` (422). Verification is evaluated at the `tier` supplied with the action, and success adopts that tier when it differs from the one on record, so the score gate reads the declared tier's minimum. Verification is additionally refused outright when the owning worker has an open confidentiality incident. The `RESET` action is the reviewer path: it suspends the qualification when an open incident names the worker, and otherwise returns it to `IN_TRAINING` when training is still incomplete.

### 2.7. Common Resource Properties

Every entity in this system carries `common_fields: true`, so the full standard property set applies uniformly to **CustomerProgram**, **SpecUnit**, **LabelingItem**, **Commit**, **WorkerIdentity**, **WorkerQualification**, **ConfidentialityIncident**, **AppealClaim**, and **WeekEpoch**. Each resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID string with no type prefix embedded in it. Each carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with exactly three fractional digits. Each carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every modification of the resource. And each carries `version`, an integer that starts at its initial value and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource.

Because derived and computed fields are still mutations of their owning resource, recomputing one of them is not a silent side effect: any operation that changes `demand_points`, `capacity_requirement_points`, `capacity_feasible`, `projected_miss_deadline`, `accepted_accuracy_pct`, `provisional_accuracy_pct`, `qa_sample_rate`, `on_time`, `worker_receivable_cents`, `accuracy_pct`, or `agreement_ratio` counts as a change to the resource that owns that field, and therefore refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version`. Where §3 names an explicit `version` increment for a transition, that increment is the one that applies; when a transition's description does not name one, the general rule of one increment per mutation stands. The rule is scoped to writes, and a read is never a mutation: a `GET` that recomputes a derived field only to serialize it — the plan metrics recomputed on a read of an engagement, the receivable recomputed on a read of a worker, or the accuracy, slot, and group-rank fields recomputed on a read of a unit, a piece, or a qualification — returns the freshly computed value without refreshing `updated_at` and without bumping `version`, so repeated reads leave every counter exactly where the last write left it.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding, and the Single-Rounding-Site Rule

Precision in this platform is pinned per quantity, and the rounding site matters as much as the rounding method. Every rounding operation anywhere in the system is **round half up**. Money is carried as integer cents and rounded exactly once, at the site §3 names, with the ceiling clamp applied only after that single rounding — rounding the product after each multiplicative factor produces a different and wrong answer. `agreement_ratio` is rounded half up to exactly 4 decimal places. The accuracy percentages `accepted_accuracy_pct`, `provisional_accuracy_pct`, `accuracy_pct`, `provisional_accuracy_pct` on a qualification, `unit_accuracy_pct`, and `unit_provisional_accuracy_pct` are rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places, and each of them reads 0 when its denominator is zero rather than raising. `qualification_score` is an exact integer and is never rounded. The point totals `demand_points`, `capacity_points`, `capacity_requirement_points`, `unit_ceiling_points`, and `unit_effective_points` are integers, with the multiplier applied and the product rounded half up. Comparisons of `accuracy_pct` inside the routing selector are made at 2 decimal places. Two derived quantities the engagement and the unit expose are plain JSON numbers rather than pre-rounded values: the engagement's `capacity_multiplier` echoes the plan's accuracy multiplier as it stands, and a unit's `rework_point_multiplier` echoes its rework multiplier as it stands, so neither is rounded at any site.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock and the Weekly Epoch

The platform does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical clock that starts at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and only ever moves when a `PlatformAdmin` supplies a new instant as `current_utc` through the clock-setting capability. The clock never falls back to wall-clock time. Every deadline, every elapsed-time measurement, and every window comparison in this document is evaluated against that logical clock.

Time is divided into week epochs, persisted as **WeekEpoch** rows, each with a 0-based `index` unique across the table, an optional human `name`, a `starts_at_utc` instant, and a `status` of `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. Exactly one epoch is `OPEN` at any moment. No epoch row is created until the clock is first moved or its index is first read; at that moment an initial epoch is minted from the current logical clock with `index` 0. Advancing the logical clock past a week boundary closes the open epoch and opens the next one with an `index` one greater than the closed epoch's and a `starts_at_utc` equal to the supplied instant; the first epoch is created from the supplied instant when none exists. A supplied instant is treated as crossing a boundary when it sits at least seven days — 604800 seconds, that is a full week — beyond the open epoch's `starts_at_utc`, and the roll itself only fires when the supplied instant is strictly later, so an instant inside the week, an equal instant, or an instant in the past leaves the epoch untouched and the clock simply moves to it. The engagement exposes the current epoch as `current_week_epoch_index`, and every weekly accumulator resets when that index advances: the weekly ceiling accumulator restarts because the settled amount counts only accepted rows whose `week_epoch_index` equals the new index, every piece's `gold_audits_this_week` returns to 0, and rehabilitation eligibility is measured against the new epoch index. Advancing the clock across a boundary also re-evaluates the consensus resolver for every piece sitting in `AWAITING_CONSENSUS` or `HELD_QUORUM`, so hold expiries, quorum-rejection timeouts, rework-window timeouts, and the appeal deadlock sweep all fire deterministically rather than waiting for another request.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations carry an explicit optimistic-concurrency guard. In each case the request body must include the named field as a non-optional input carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent entity, and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) — and with no mutation applied — when that value does not equal the parent entity's current `version`. The guard is an observation guard, not a timing guard: it exists so that a client cannot write against a snapshot that the server has since superseded.

Creating a unit under an engagement carries `expected_program_version`, compared against the **CustomerProgram**'s current `version`; this catches the case where an embargo, reopen, or freeze cascade has already moved the program and every one of its units, so a decomposition submitted against the stale snapshot would otherwise resurrect a superseded structure. Grading a gold audit carries `expected_item_version`, compared against the **LabelingItem**'s current `version`; this catches the case where the audited piece was re-dispatched onto a new instruction revision between the reviewer's read and the grade submission, so the grade would otherwise be attributed to the wrong revision. Concurring with an appeal carries `expected_claim_version`, compared against the **AppealClaim**'s current `version`; this catches the case where the claim moved to adjudication or was withdrawn between the reviewer's read and the concurrence, so the reviewer would otherwise be concurring with a proposal the claim no longer carries. No other endpoint accepts an `expected_..._version` field, and supplying one anywhere else has no effect on processing.

### 2.11. Ordered Validation and Request Admissibility

Validation runs in a fixed order and the first failing check is the one reported, so an implementation that reorders the ladder will surface a different `error_id` than the tests expect. Two classes share the first rank and are checked first: the request-body completeness check, which refuses a body that is missing a required input field or supplies one with a value of the wrong type, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400); and the header check of §2.1, which refuses a request missing either identity header, reported as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The header check exempts worker registration, while the body check applies only to endpoints that declare required inputs. Next, at rank 2, the acting role must be one of the roles the endpoint declares, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

Rank 3 is existence, and it resolves path parameters strictly from left to right: the first identifier in the path that does not resolve to an existing resource determines the error, and later identifiers are never examined. A missing engagement yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (404), a missing unit yields `ERR_SPEC_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` (404), a missing piece yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404), a missing assignment yields `ERR_COMMIT_NOT_FOUND` (404), a missing audit yields `ERR_GOLD_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — this one also covers an audit that exists but does not target the item named alongside it — a missing worker yields `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND` (404), which likewise covers a qualification or incident that does not name the worker named alongside it, a missing qualification yields `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404), and a missing appeal case yields `ERR_APPEAL_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). On the routes whose path carries an identifier for a resource the endpoint acts on, this existence check is performed before the request body is parsed, so a request that names a non-existent resource is refused at rank 3 rather than at rank 1 even when its body is also incomplete; the body-completeness check of rank 1 is reported first only on the routes that must create a resource from that body or act on the body before a path identifier is needed.

Rank 4 is the concurrency guard of §2.10, reported as `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Rank 5 is state admissibility: a target resource that is in a state which does not admit the operation, or that has an ancestor engagement in `FROZEN`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); an assignment that is no longer `DISPATCHED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409); and activating a unit whose instruction document has not reached `PUBLISHED` is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). Rank 6 is the safety band: `ERR_PROGRAM_OPERATIONALLY_RESTRICTED` (409), `ERR_WORKER_RESTRICTED` (403), `ERR_FIELD_SCHEMA_MISMATCH` (400), and `ERR_EDGE_CASE_GUIDANCE_CONFLICT` (409).

Rank 7 is referential integrity: `ERR_UNIT_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409), `ERR_SPEC_UNIT_CYCLE` (409), and `ERR_INSTRUCTION_REVISION_MISMATCH` (409), the last covering an answer whose instruction revision differs from the unit's pinned `frozen_revision`. Rank 8 is the temporal band: `ERR_COMMIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), `ERR_REQUIRED_UNITS_GAP` (409), `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_WORKER_LANE_CONFLICT` (409), and `ERR_REWORK_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Rank 9 collects the remaining business constraints: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WORKER` (422), `ERR_GOLD_ROTATION_ACTIVE` (409), `ERR_PANEL_ALREADY_DISPATCHED` (409), `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_REACHED` (409), `ERR_REWORK_ROUND_LIMIT` (409), `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), `ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_TIER` (422), and `ERR_CAPACITY_INFEASIBLE` (409). A check whose error belongs to one band is reported with that error even when, on the route that carries it, it runs at a different point in the sequence: panel dispatch evaluates the lane conflict before the empty-pool refusal, so a pool that is populated but wholly lane-blocked reports `ERR_WORKER_LANE_CONFLICT` (409) while a genuinely empty pool reports `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WORKER` (422); gold-audit creation evaluates the weekly gold rotation ahead of the engagement's operational restriction, so a piece that has taken its weekly quota reports `ERR_GOLD_ROTATION_ACTIVE` (409) even when the engagement is also restricted; and the reviewer panel decision reports `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_REACHED` ahead of the piece's own state admissibility. Rank 10 is the fiscal band: `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (409), and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECEIVABLE` (422).

Several checks apply to more than one operation, and the full scope of each is worth stating so that no guard is implemented on only part of its surface. The engagement-restricted guard reaches panel dispatch, gold-audit creation, and unit decomposition under the engagement alike. The worker-restricted guard reaches label commitment, panel dispatch (through the excluded candidate), payout acceptance, and settlement requests. The field-schema guard reaches both label commitment and rework hand-in, since both submit an answer payload. The instruction-revision guard reaches those same two operations. The lane-conflict and detection-gap guards reach panel dispatch. The coverage-window, instruction-published, and edge-case-guidance guards reach unit activation. The round-limit guard reaches the rework-opening action on a piece. The segregation-of-duties guard reaches reviewer panel decisions, gold-audit grading, appeal proposals, and appeal concurrence. The currency guard reaches payout acceptance and settlement requests. And the receivable guard reaches settlement requests and the compensation concurrence of the appeal ladder.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Engagement Intake and the Decomposition Tree

A `PlatformAdmin` opens an engagement by registering a **CustomerProgram** with a unique `program_code`, a `name`, the sole task `language_code` of the engagement, an `instruction_model` drawn from `IDEAL_RESPONSE`, `RANKED_PREFERENCE`, `SPAN_EXTRACTION`, or `SAFETY_CLASSIFICATION`, and the three scheduling instants `deadline_utc`, `blackout_start_utc`, and `blackout_end_utc`. The `sensitivity_class` is optional here and takes its `STANDARD` default when omitted, as §2.5 states. The registration also accepts `coverage_hours`, the wall-clock span of the engagement's delivery window, which defaults to 168 hours. The engagement is created in `DRAFT`, which is the state in which it can still be decomposed but cannot yet produce work.

Several tuning values may be supplied at registration or left to their defaults, and all of them are engagement-scoped. Consensus is governed by `min_consensus_agreement`, the fraction of the non-abstaining committed panel that must share the modal value before a piece certifies on its own, defaulting to 0.6; by `commitment_quorum`, the number of non-abstaining committed assignments required before a piece may certify or be tie-broken, defaulting to 3; by `panel_size` on the unit, the number of distinct assignments dispatched when a piece enters consensus, defaulting to 3, where a supplied value is taken as given rather than raised to any floor, though a value below 1 is treated as absent and replaced by the default; and by `patience_seconds`, the length of the deadlock window a piece or an appeal may wait for its remaining quorum before the hold fires, defaulting to 600. Timing of an individual answer is governed by `commit_deadline_seconds`, the number of seconds after dispatch during which a committed answer is still admissible, defaulting to 7200. Compensation is governed by `weekly_earning_cap_cents`, the per-worker ceiling on accepted payouts inside one week epoch, defaulting to 9000 cents.

Planning is governed by five more values. `capacity_points` is the total workforce points available inside the coverage window, defaulting to 100000. `accuracy_floor_pct` is the hard floor the accepted accuracy variant must clear for a plan to stay feasible, defaulting to 85 percent, and `accuracy_high_watermark_pct` is the accuracy at or above which the cheapest per-item point cost applies, defaulting to 95 percent. `point_cost_high_accuracy`, `point_cost_medium_accuracy`, and `point_cost_fallback` are the per-item point charges for the three accuracy bands, defaulting to 2, 3, and 4 points respectively.

Decomposition proceeds from the engagement downward. A `PlatformAdmin` submits a **SpecUnit** naming its owning `program_id` as a body reference, along with a program-unique `unit_code`, `max_items`, `instruction_revision`, `instruction_status`, the ordered `field_schema`, and the unit's `coverage_start_utc` and `coverage_end_utc`. The submission must also carry `expected_program_version`, and the OCC guard of §2.10 applies here: when the engagement's `version` has advanced past the supplied value the decomposition is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) and nothing is created. Optional fields are `parent_unit_id`, which makes the new unit a child in the decomposition tree; `has_edge_case_guidance`, defaulting false, and `edge_case_count`, defaulting 0, which must not contradict each other as §2.4 requires; `panel_size`; and `gold_rate`, the fraction of dispatched commits on this unit that are to be served as gold audits, which reads as intended between 0 and 1 inclusive and defaults to 0. Supplying `gold_rate` outside that span is not itself refused; every plan number that consults the value is unchanged by it beyond the span. The optional inputs each take their documented default when omitted, and none of them is required. The creation raises the engagement's `demand_points` and increments the engagement's `version`.

Pieces attach to units. A `PlatformAdmin` submits a **LabelingItem** with its `item_key`, which is unique within the unit, and its `payload_uri`, the storage handle of the raw customer payload, plus an optional `required_units`. The piece is created in container status `DRAFT` and production status `PENDING`, with consensus status `PENDING`, and the server assigns its `item_seq`, copies the unit's current `instruction_revision` onto it, and raises the unit's `item_count`. The `payload_uri` is additionally subject to the confidentiality class of the owning engagement: while the engagement's `sensitivity_class` is `SENSITIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, the field is omitted from every response that carries the piece, even though it remains a required input at creation.

### 3.2. Opening an Engagement and Putting Units into Production

An engagement in `DRAFT` becomes operational when a `PlatformAdmin` drives it to `ACTIVE`. The transition requires that the engagement already has at least one **SpecUnit** whose status is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; with no such unit, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Opening the engagement sets its status to `ACTIVE` and increments its `version` by 1. It then sweeps the engagement's root units — those whose `parent_unit_id` is null — and activates every root unit that already satisfies the unit activation gates, leaving any unit that fails a gate in `DRAFT`. Activation of a unit is not unconditional, and the same gates guard the explicitly requested activation below, so it is worth stating them once: the parent engagement must not be `FROZEN`, which is the only engagement state that blocks the path; every unit whose `parent_unit_id` equals this unit's `id` must itself be in `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETE`; this unit's `instruction_status` must be `PUBLISHED`; `edge_case_count` must be 0 whenever `has_edge_case_guidance` is true; and the unit's coverage window must not overlap the engagement's blackout window under the half-open rule.

A unit's own production status, the `piece_status` field, is a separate vocabulary from its lifecycle status and begins at `DECOMPOSED`, the state a freshly decomposed unit holds before any of its pieces are in production. A unit may also be activated directly, by a `PlatformAdmin` supplying `unit_action` with the value `ACTIVATE`, and it may be reopened from a completed state by supplying `unit_action` with the value `REOPEN`. The direct `ACTIVATE` path applies the identical gate set and additionally requires the unit's own status to be `DRAFT`, so it is the transition a freshly decomposed unit takes; the engagement-sweep arm of opening activates a root unit without that self-state requirement, since each swept unit is already `DRAFT`. Either path is deliberately open while the engagement is still `DRAFT` — only the terminal `FROZEN` blocks it — so a unit may be put into production before its engagement is opened. The `ACTIVATE` path sets the unit's status to `ACTIVE` and its `piece_status` to `IN_PRODUCTION`, increments the unit's `version` by 1, and releases the unit's pieces: every attached **LabelingItem** still in container status `DRAFT` transitions to container status `RELEASED` while its `item_status` remains `PENDING`. This is the first arm of the release cascade described in §3.3. The `REOPEN` path instead requires the unit to be `COMPLETE`, the engagement to be `ACTIVE` or `QUALITY_EMBARGO`, and at least one attached piece to carry `consensus_status` `REWORK`; it sets the unit's status back to `ACTIVE` and its `piece_status` to `REWORK`, and it releases no pieces. When the gate that fails is a non-`PUBLISHED` instruction document, the refusal is the specific `ERR_INSTRUCTION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409); when the failure is the contradiction between published guidance and a positive enumerated edge-case count, it is `ERR_EDGE_CASE_GUIDANCE_CONFLICT` (409); and when the failure is a positive-length overlap between the unit's coverage window and the engagement's blackout window it is `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). A unit with an unfinished child — one whose `parent_unit_id` equals this unit's `id` and whose status is anything other than `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETE` — blocks activation with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

### 3.3. The Piece Lifecycle and Its Two Parallel Statuses

A piece carries three status vocabularies at once, and the distinctions matter. Its container status — the `status` field — moves through `DRAFT`, `RELEASED`, `IN_PANEL`, and `RESOLVED`. Its production status — `item_status` — moves through `PENDING`, `ROUTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `COMPLETED`, and `REWORK`. Its consensus status — `consensus_status` — moves through `PENDING`, `AWAITING_CONSENSUS`, `CERTIFIED`, `HELD_QUORUM`, `REJECTED`, and `REWORK`. A freshly attached piece sits at `DRAFT` / `PENDING` / `PENDING`.

Release is a system-driven cascade triggered by a unit activation, whether that activation came from opening the engagement or from a directly requested activation. When the owning unit transitions from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, every attached piece still at container status `DRAFT` moves to container status `RELEASED` with `item_status` remaining `PENDING`, and the piece receives its `item_seq`. Nothing consensual has happened yet; the piece is merely available to be dispatched.

Dispatch is what turns a piece into work. A `PlatformAdmin` triggers panel dispatch on a released piece, and this is the single most guarded operation in the system. The owning unit's status must be `ACTIVE`, its `piece_status` must be `IN_PRODUCTION`, `CONSENSUS`, or `REWORK`, and the engagement's `operational_restricted` must be false, else the request is refused — the restriction case specifically with `ERR_PROGRAM_OPERATIONALLY_RESTRICTED` (409). The piece's container status must be `DRAFT`, `RELEASED`, or `IN_PANEL`; a piece already at `RESOLVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). A piece still at container status `DRAFT` is treated as already released — it is held back only by the release cascade having run before the piece was attached — and is dispatched the same way. The piece's own `gold_rotation` must be false, so a piece that has already taken its weekly quota of gold audits cannot be put back into a production panel, which is reported as `ERR_GOLD_ROTATION_ACTIVE` (409). The detection-gap guard is the subtle one: `required_units` must be at least 1, and the count of items already certified inside the unit must be at or above `required_units` minus 1, because releasing a piece that still carries a required-detection shortfall would publish the unit with a zero gap; a violation is `ERR_REQUIRED_UNITS_GAP` (409). The panel must not already be full — the committed production assignment count must be strictly below the unit's `panel_size` — else `ERR_PANEL_ALREADY_DISPATCHED` (409), and this is counted over `COMMITTED` production rows alone, so a panel whose members are still `DISPATCHED` does not yet count against the ceiling. And dispatch must produce a candidate: when the selector described in §3.4 returns an empty pool the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WORKER` (422), a code reserved for that case alone; a pool that is non-empty but whose every member is blocked by the lane conflict of §3.4 is instead refused with `ERR_WORKER_LANE_CONFLICT` (409).

When the guards pass, the piece's container status becomes `IN_PANEL`, its `item_status` becomes `IN_REVIEW`, and its `consensus_status` becomes `AWAITING_CONSENSUS`. The selector is evaluated once for this dispatch, and one **Commit** row per selected member is created. Each commit is created with `status` `DISPATCHED`, with `kind` `PRODUCTION`, with `dispatch_rank` set from the ranked order the selector produced, so a fresh dispatch on a piece numbers its members upward from 1 in ranked order while a further dispatch onto the same piece continues the panel's ranking rather than restarting it, keeping the ranks monotonic, with `worker_id` resolved server-side to the **WorkerIdentity** matching the selected candidate and never supplied by the client, with `qualification_id` resolved to the **WorkerQualification** matching that worker and the engagement's `language_code`, with `dispatched_at` set to the current logical clock, and with `commit_deadline_at` computed as `dispatched_at` plus the engagement's `commit_deadline_seconds`. The unit's `version` is incremented by 1 by the dispatch. Dispatching does not move the owning unit's `piece_status` to `CONSENSUS`; a live panel leaves the unit's production status to be derived from its pieces rather than from the panel itself, so `CONSENSUS` is a value no ordinary panel path writes. If the pool held fewer eligible members than the requested `panel_size`, every member found is dispatched; the piece simply waits longer for quorum.

A `Worker` answers through the assignment surface. Submitting a label on a dispatched commit requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the commit's `worker_id` — the acting worker may only answer their own assignment — and requires `instructions_ack` to be true, since the acknowledgement of the instruction document is mandatory on every commit. An assignment that has not yet been answered carries payment status `UNPAID` with a `paid_amount_cents` of 0, so no payout exists until a record is accepted. The clock must be strictly before `commit_deadline_at`; at or past it the submission is refused with `ERR_COMMIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). The commit's `dispatched_at` and its computed deadline are both exposed on the assignment, as is its 1-based `dispatch_rank`. An attempt against an assignment that is no longer `DISPATCHED` — already committed, already abstained, or already expired — is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). The submitted `payload` must satisfy the exact-schema rule of §2.4, and its key set is compared against the unit's `field_schema`, with `ERR_FIELD_SCHEMA_MISMATCH` (400) on any divergence. When the answer is a genuine label, `abstained` is false, the commit's `status` becomes `COMMITTED`, and `committed_value`, `value_group_rank`, and `is_modal_agent` are computed by the value-grouping rule of §3.4; the piece's `non_abstaining_commit_count` is recomputed and the consensus resolver is re-evaluated. When the worker declines to label, `abstained` is true, the commit's `status` becomes `ABSTAINED`, `committed_value` is set to null, and `non_abstaining_commit_count` is recomputed in a way that leaves the abstainer entirely out of the denominator. An assignment still `DISPATCHED` when the engagement freezes expires to `COMMIT_EXPIRED`, and that too forces a recomputation of the piece's `non_abstaining_commit_count`.

### 3.4. Selecting the Panel and Counting the Agreement

Not every qualified worker is a candidate for every dispatch, and the selection consists of a filter, an ordering, and a truncation in that sequence. This is the engagement's worker-routing policy, and it is evaluated in full exactly once per panel dispatch, producing both the candidate pool and the ranked order the panel is cut from. A **WorkerQualification** is eligible for a dispatch only when all of the following hold: its `language_code` equals the engagement's `language_code`; its status is `QUALIFIED` or `PROBATION`; its `qualification_score` is at or above the tier minimum implied by its `tier`; if the engagement's `sensitivity_class` is `RESTRICTED`, then its `restricted_accessible` must be true; the owning **WorkerIdentity**'s `restricted` must be false; its owner must not already own any existing **Commit** row on this piece; its owner must not appear among the owners of the piece's previous rework revisions; and the candidate must hold no `DISPATCHED` commit on a different piece whose closed dispatch-to-deadline interval touches or overlaps this dispatch's interval. That last condition is a lane exclusion, and it is deliberately conservative about adjacency: because the intervals are treated as closed, an interval ending exactly when the next begins is a conflict, and the offending dispatch is reported as `ERR_WORKER_LANE_CONFLICT` (409).

Eligibility is then narrowed by a cold-start accommodation. A qualification is selected only when it has fewer than three graded gold audits, which is the reading of `gold_denominator_count` strictly below 3, **or** when its accepted `accuracy_pct` is at or above 60.0 percent. The exemption keeps a brand-new qualification routable until it has accumulated three graded audits, and the 60.0 percent floor applies only once that history exists. The pool of candidates is the set of qualifications whose `language_code` matches the engagement and which satisfy the selection rule, and the panel is the first `panel_size` members of that pool in ranked order.

The ranking is a three-key sort and it is exact. Members are ordered by `accuracy_pct` descending — equivalently, by the negated accuracy ascending — then by `qualification_score` descending, then by `created_at` ascending. The tie-break is deliberately the qualification's creation timestamp and never the server-minted `id`, so a pool whose members share a creation instant still has a total order, and `qualification_score` is an exact integer so no rounding ambiguity enters the middle key. One variant applies to this ordering: when the engagement's `accuracy_denominator_count` is strictly below 3 — that is, while the engagement itself is cold-starting — the first key becomes the qualification's `provisional_accuracy_pct` descending rather than its accepted `accuracy_pct` descending. Identical worker data can therefore produce a different panel depending on how much audit history the engagement has accumulated.

Once answers exist, they are grouped by value. The grouping operates only over **Commit** rows whose `status` is `COMMITTED` and whose `abstained` is false; dispatched, abstained, and expired rows sit outside both the numerator and the denominator. The count of those rows is the agreement denominator. Each distinct `committed_value` forms a group whose size is the number of members carrying it and whose minimum rank is the smallest `dispatch_rank` among its members. The modal value is the value of the group that maximizes size first and, among equally sized groups, minimizes the group's minimum rank; in prose, larger groups win and, between groups of equal size, the group containing the earliest-dispatched member wins. The agreement ratio is the modal group's size divided by the agreement denominator, rounded half up to exactly 4 decimal places, and it reads 0 when the denominator is 0. Each commit's `value_group_rank` is the 1-based rank of its own group under that same ordering, and `is_modal_agent` is true for exactly one member: the single commit inside the winning group carrying that group's minimum `dispatch_rank`. Because the ordering keys are the integer `dispatch_rank` assigned at dispatch time and the group sizes, no random or arrival-order field is consulted anywhere in this computation, and the same answers always yield the same modal agent. Abstainers are excluded from the denominator entirely, so an abstaining member can never lower the agreement ratio.

The instruction revision pin is a derived protection against later decomposition edits. On the first occasion a piece reaches `CERTIFIED` while its unit's `frozen_revision` is still null, the unit's `frozen_revision` is set to the minimum of that piece's `instruction_revision` and the `instruction_revision` of every other item attached to the unit. Once set, it never moves backward, so a later activation cannot drag the pinned revision down; and every answer submitted afterward is checked against it, with any disagreement reported as `ERR_INSTRUCTION_REVISION_MISMATCH` (409).

### 3.5. Consensus Resolution and the Held Panel

Consensus is decided on the shape of the committed answer set rather than on any single member's answer, and the resolver branches on two quantities: the piece's `non_abstaining_commit_count` and its `agreement_ratio`. The resolver is driven automatically — it runs at the end of every successful label commit, and it is re-evaluated when the clock advances across a week boundary — and it may only act at all when the piece has at least one non-abstaining committed assignment. Its outcome also depends on the assembly of the commit list itself, because the panel may be short: the piece's panel size is fixed, but workers can abstain or never answer, so the denominator the resolver sees may be smaller than the dispatched panel.

One branch joins the panel. When `non_abstaining_commit_count` is at or above the engagement's `commitment_quorum` **and** the `agreement_ratio` is at or above the engagement's `min_consensus_agreement`, the piece's `consensus_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `modal_label` and `agreement_ratio` are stamped onto the piece, `value_group_rank` and `is_modal_agent` are stamped on every committed member, the piece's container `status` becomes `RESOLVED` and its `item_status` becomes `COMPLETED`. This is the join branch, and it is also what drives the unit's completion in §3.11.

A second branch holds the panel instead of resolving it. When `non_abstaining_commit_count` is at or above the quorum but the `agreement_ratio` is strictly below `min_consensus_agreement`, the piece's `consensus_status` becomes `HELD_QUORUM` and `holds_quorum` becomes true, while the container `status` stays `IN_PANEL` and the `item_status` stays `IN_REVIEW`. The disagreement is not resolved by the panel; it is routed to a human. This is the drop-out fork that the reviewer tie-break in §3.6 picks up.

A third branch waits. When `non_abstaining_commit_count` is strictly below the quorum and the hold window that §3.1 describes as `patience_seconds` has not yet expired, the piece's `consensus_status` stays `AWAITING_CONSENSUS` and the piece simply waits for its remaining members. And when the count is strictly below the quorum and the seconds elapsed since the piece's first commit are at or above `patience_seconds`, the wait is abandoned: the `consensus_status` becomes `REJECTED`, `holds_quorum` becomes false, the container `status` becomes `RESOLVED`, and the `item_status` becomes `COMPLETED`. The distinction between waiting and rejecting is therefore the expiry of a single window measured from the first commit, not the arrival of any new answer.

A piece sitting in `HELD_QUORUM` is resolved by a human. A `PlatformReviewer` submits `reviewer_action`, whose value is `RESOLVE_PANEL_DECISION` or `OPEN_REWORK`, to record a panel decision, which requires at least `commitment_quorum` non-abstaining commits to exist — tying the reviewer's authority to the same denominator the resolver used — and is refused with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_REACHED` (409) when that floor is not met, a refusal reported ahead of the piece's own state admissibility so a piece short of quorum is refused for the quorum rather than for its consensus state. The decision carries a `decision_value`, and recording it certifies the piece. The `decision_value` is optional: when a value is supplied it is stamped onto the piece as `modal_label`, and when it is omitted the piece keeps whichever modal label its committed value groups already produced. Recording the decision also re-stamps `value_group_rank` and `is_modal_agent` across the committed members under the value-grouping rule of §3.4. Only a piece sitting in `HELD_QUORUM` admits this recording; a piece in any other consensus state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). `RESOLVE_PANEL_DECISION` is the tie-break arm, and `OPEN_REWORK` is the second reviewer capability, which §3.6 describes. The reviewer who breaks a held panel is subject to the segregation rule of §2.1 and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) if they hold a commit on the piece.

### 3.6. Rework: Sending a Certified Piece Back and Taking It Back

Rework is the cyclic arm of the piece lifecycle: a piece can leave `COMPLETED` for `REWORK` and return to `CERTIFIED` or to `HELD_QUORUM`. A `PlatformReviewer` opens a rework round against a piece whose `consensus_status` is `CERTIFIED` or `HELD_QUORUM`. The round is refused when the piece's `rework_cycles` has already reached its `rework_round_limit`, reported as `ERR_REWORK_ROUND_LIMIT` (409), and it is refused for a reviewer who holds a commit on the piece, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

Opening rework rewrites a great deal of piece state at once. The `consensus_status` becomes `REWORK`, the `item_status` becomes `REWORK`, the container `status` returns to `IN_PANEL`. The `rework_cycles` counter is incremented by 1, `rework_started_at` is set to the current logical clock, and the piece's `version` is incremented by 1. The piece's `gold_audits_this_week` is reset to 0, because a rework epoch restarts the weekly gold rotation counter. And the panel itself is mutated: the lowest ranked member of the winning value group is marked `is_superseded`, and the piece is re-dispatched to one fresh member drawn from the selector of §3.4, with the new member's `dispatch_rank` set to the incremented `rework_cycles` plus the unit's `panel_size`. The piece is thereby back in the hands of a worker, with one member's answer marked as replaced.

The superseding worker hands the corrected answer back. A `Worker` submits a rework answer naming the `commit_id` of the superseded assignment, together with the revised `payload` and an `instructions_ack`. The acting worker is resolved from the submitting identity, so the `worker_id` on the assignment must match it; three further conditions guard the submission. The acting worker must be the owner of the superseded commit, so no other worker may correct a member's answer. The rework window must not have lapsed: the seconds elapsed since `rework_started_at` must be strictly below the piece's `rework_window_seconds`, and at or past that length the submission is refused with `ERR_REWORK_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). And the revised payload must satisfy the exact-schema rule, with `ERR_FIELD_SCHEMA_MISMATCH` (400) on any divergence. When the submission is accepted, the superseded commit is replaced in place — its `is_superseded` flag cleared and its `committed_value` refreshed — and the value-group computation is re-evaluated over the refreshed member set. The outcome of the re-evaluation then branches: when the refreshed modal value equals the modal value of the previous round, the piece's `consensus_status` becomes `CERTIFIED` and the piece rejoins the previous value as though it had never moved; when the refreshed modal value differs, the `consensus_status` becomes `HELD_QUORUM`, `holds_quorum` becomes true, and the disagreement is routed to a reviewer. Either way the piece's `version` is incremented by 1. The rework window itself is `rework_window_seconds`, defaulting to 900 seconds, and its remaining time is exposed as `rework_window_remaining_seconds`, which is the window length minus the elapsed time since `rework_started_at` and reads null unless the piece is actually in `REWORK`.

An unattended round does not stall forever. When the rework window lapses without a hand-in — that is, the remaining seconds reach 0 or below, meaning the elapsed time since `rework_started_at` is at or above `rework_window_seconds` — and `rework_cycles` is still strictly below `rework_round_limit`, the round is rolled automatically: `rework_cycles` is incremented by 1, `rework_started_at` is reset to the current logical clock, the superseded commit is restored with `is_superseded` cleared, and the piece's `version` is incremented by 1. The piece stays in `REWORK` and is given another window. But when the same timeout occurs and `rework_cycles` is already **at** the round limit, the piece is not given another window: its `consensus_status` becomes `REJECTED`, `holds_quorum` becomes false, the container `status` becomes `RESOLVED`, and the `item_status` becomes `COMPLETED`. The `rework_round_limit` defaults to 3, so a piece can be handed back at most that many times before the timeout rejects it outright.

### 3.7. Gold-Standard Audits and Ground-Truth Anchoring

Consensus measures agreement; gold audits measure correctness, and the two mechanisms are kept separate by design. A `PlatformReviewer` creates a **Commit** of kind `GOLD` over a piece that already has committed panel members, supplying a `gold_task_key`. A gold audit is never part of a consensus panel — the value-grouping rule of §3.4 counts only `PRODUCTION` commits — so auditing a piece cannot move its agreement ratio. The audit inherits the customer's ground truth rather than receiving it from the client: the server selects an anchor among the piece's committed members by the anchor rule — the committed member carrying the lowest `dispatch_rank`, that is the earliest-dispatched answer, with any tie broken by creation order — and copies that member's committed value into the audit's `expected_value`, recording the reference in `selected_anchor_commit_id`. The audit may be served by any qualification eligible for the engagement's language, including the anchor member's own, and the acting reviewer's identifiers are not resolved against the auditor drawn here; when no eligible qualification exists at all the creation is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WORKER` (422). That identifier is a server-side reference and is never client-supplied, which is what makes the ground truth reproducible and independent of the order in which answers arrived. The audit's outcome vocabulary is carried on the audit itself as `eligible_outcomes`; the base vocabulary is `PASSED`, `FAILED`, and `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY`, enumerated in that order, and an engagement whose `instruction_model` is `SAFETY_CLASSIFICATION` replaces that list with the four-value enumeration `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `FLAGGED`, `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY`. The audit joins the piece's assignment ranking: it is created with its `dispatch_rank` one greater than the highest rank already present on the piece, so it sorts after every production member and is never counted as one. Creating a gold audit raises the piece's `gold_audits_this_week` by 1, increments the serving qualification's `gold_audits_dispatched` by 1, and creates the audit with its `gold_outcome` null, because an audit is dispatched before it is graded. Gold-auditing a piece whose engagement is operationally restricted is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_OPERATIONALLY_RESTRICTED` (409), and creating a further gold audit on a piece whose `gold_rotation` flag has already turned true is refused with `ERR_GOLD_ROTATION_ACTIVE` (409), the same weekly quota that bars a piece from a production panel. The two restrictions are evaluated in that order, so a piece that has taken its weekly quota reports the rotation refusal even when its engagement is also restricted; a piece with no committed member to anchor on at all is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

The weekly quota travels with the piece rather than with the worker. `gold_audits_this_week` counts the gold audits dispatched against this piece since the current week epoch began, and `gold_rotation` is derived from it: the flag is true while `gold_audits_this_week` is at or above 3 and the piece's `consensus_status` is neither `REWORK` nor `REJECTED`, so it also clears on a terminal piece. A piece with `gold_rotation` true cannot be dispatched back into a production panel, which is the `ERR_GOLD_ROTATION_ACTIVE` (409) refusal of §3.3. The escape hatch is the rework reset of §3.6, which returns `gold_audits_this_week` to 0, and the weekly epoch roll of §2.9, which does the same for every piece at once.

Grading is where the audit becomes a quality measurement. A `PlatformReviewer` grades a dispatched audit by supplying a `gold_outcome` — one of `PASSED`, `FLAGGED`, `FAILED`, or `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY` — together with `expected_item_version`. The OCC guard of §2.10 applies: when the piece's `version` has moved past the supplied value the grade is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412), because the audited piece was re-dispatched onto a new instruction revision between the reviewer's read and the grade and the grade would otherwise be attributed to the wrong revision. The reviewer who committed the audited panel member is barred from grading it by the segregation rule of §2.1, with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Grading admits any of the four outcome values whatever the engagement's `instruction_model` — the audit's `eligible_outcomes` list guides the client but is not itself enforced as a gate — so a `FLAGGED` grade is accepted on a non-`SAFETY_CLASSIFICATION` engagement. An audit already expired to `COMMIT_EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Grading records the outcome, updates the qualification streak and cyclic status of the author of the anchored answer whose payload the audit inherited as §3.9 describes, recomputes the program-level and unit-level accuracy metrics of §3.8, and increments the piece's `version` by 1.

Cataloguing is the deliberate exit from the accuracy computation, and it is the reason `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY` exists as a first-class outcome. Because both accuracy variants draw their denominator from audits graded `PASSED` or `FAILED`, an audit graded `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY` sits outside both the numerator and the denominator, and so does an audit whose `gold_outcome` is still null. Grading an audit `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY` therefore removes it from both variants rather than counting it as a failure or a success.

### 3.8. Measuring Quality Against Ground Truth

Every accuracy number in this system comes in two variants, and the variants differ only in how they treat a `FLAGGED` audit. The **accepted** variant counts only `PASSED` as a success, so a flagged audit lowers it. The **provisional** variant counts `PASSED` and `FLAGGED` together, so it is greater than or equal to the accepted variant on every possible input. Both variants are computed over the same denominator: the audits graded `PASSED` or `FAILED`, with `EXCLUDED_FROM_ACCURACY` and null outcomes outside it as §3.7 states. Both are expressed as a percentage rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places, and both read 0 when the denominator is 0 rather than raising. The accepted variant is the one the feasibility gate, the embargo trigger, and every acceptance decision read. The provisional variant is the one the cold-start ranking of §3.4 reads and the one the plan's `AT_RISK` reading uses.

The engagement carries the pair as `accepted_accuracy_pct` and `provisional_accuracy_pct`, and alongside them the shared denominator as `accuracy_denominator_count`. The same pair is computed at unit granularity, where `unit_accuracy_pct` is the accepted variant over the unit's gold-audited items and `unit_provisional_accuracy_pct` is the provisional variant over the same set. The same pair is also computed per qualification as `accuracy_pct` and `provisional_accuracy_pct`, both over that qualification's `gold_denominator_count`.

The denominator-exclusion rule has one further consequence that the routing definition depends on. Because an engagement with no graded audits has no denominator, its accepted accuracy is 0 and the cold-start exemptions keyed to `accuracy_denominator_count` strictly below 3 are what keep its units dispatchable. A brand-new qualification is likewise measured only against its own `gold_denominator_count`, `accepted_gold_count`, and `provisional_gold_count`, and its `gold_audits_dispatched` counter tracks how many audits it has ever received.

### 3.9. The Cyclic Qualification Lifecycle

A qualification's status is `IN_TRAINING`, `QUALIFIED`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `REVOKED`, and the progression is genuinely cyclic: a qualification that has fallen can climb back, and one that has been suspended can be returned to training. The cycle is driven by two inputs — the audit streak and the training counters — and the platform tracks the streak as `gold_fail_streak`, the number of consecutive `FAILED` gold audits on this qualification since its last non-`FAILED` grade. A qualification begins in `IN_TRAINING` when it is enrolled.

Verification is the ascent from training. A `PlatformAdmin` verifies a qualification out of `IN_TRAINING`, which requires that `training_passed_count` is at least `training_required_count` and that `qualification_score` is at or above `tier_min_score`; the score gate is reported on its own as `ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_TIER` (422), and as §2.6 states, verification is also refused outright when the owning worker has an open confidentiality incident. Success sets the status to `QUALIFIED`. The verification action admits a qualification in `IN_TRAINING` or in `PROBATION`, and no other state — a `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED`, or already-`QUALIFIED` qualification is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and the training shortfall on this path is likewise reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than as the score error.

Falling is automatic. When a gold audit is graded `FAILED` and the resulting `gold_fail_streak` is at or above 2 — that is, the grade that pushes the streak to two clears the threshold — the qualification's status becomes `PROBATION`. The descent is therefore earned by repetition: a single failed audit does not demote a qualified worker.

Ascent from probation is deliberate and time-boxed. A `PlatformAdmin` may verify a qualification out of `PROBATION` back to `QUALIFIED`, which requires that `training_passed_count` is at least `training_required_count` and, crucially, that the qualification was rehabilitated in an earlier week epoch — meaning the current program week index differs from the epoch in which the last rehabilitation happened. A qualification cannot be rehabilitated twice inside the same week: the epoch the rule compares against is the one stamped when the qualification was last lifted, and it is stamped as null on enrollment, so a qualification never yet rehabilitated is not blocked by the same-week test. Success returns the status to `QUALIFIED` and resets `gold_fail_streak` to 0. The same lift, whether it starts from `IN_TRAINING` or from `PROBATION`, stamps the current week epoch as the qualification's last-rehabilitated epoch.

Falling further is a reviewer decision. A `PlatformReviewer` resets a qualification out of `PROBATION`, and that action requires that a **ConfidentialityIncident** naming the qualification's owner exists with status `OPEN`. Success sets the status to `SUSPENDED` and resets `gold_fail_streak` to 0, but it also carries a withdrawal side effect on live work: every **Commit** owned by that worker which is still `DISPATCHED` on a non-terminal piece is withdrawn, the piece's `non_abstaining_commit_count` is decremented accordingly, and the piece is returned to `AWAITING_CONSENSUS` when the withdrawn assignment was the last outstanding one on it. The same reset action serves a second purpose from the suspended state: a `PlatformReviewer` resets a `SUSPENDED` qualification when `training_passed_count` is strictly below `training_required_count`, which returns the status to `IN_TRAINING` and sets `training_passed_count` back to 0 — the worker starts training over. A `PROBATION` qualification that has no open incident, a `SUSPENDED` qualification whose training is already complete, a `REVOKED` qualification, and any qualification in `IN_TRAINING` or `QUALIFIED` are all outside the reset action's reach and are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

Revocation is the terminal outcome and it does not require a human. When the deterministic `qualification_score` is strictly below `tier_min_score` for the declared tier, the qualification's status becomes `REVOKED`, and every uncommitted **Commit** on that qualification is withdrawn. This judgment is applied on the grading path: every time a gold audit is graded, the qualification that carries the anchored answer's streak is re-examined, and if its status is still `IN_TRAINING` while its score sits strictly below the declared tier's minimum it is revoked there and then, its outstanding `DISPATCHED` assignments withdrawn. Because `qualification_score` depends only on the worker's `handle` and the language code, this outcome is fully predictable from the enrollment inputs, and it is also the gate that `ERR_QUALIFICATION_SCORE_BELOW_TIER` (422) protects on the verification path. `REVOKED` is terminal: no action of this ladder lifts it, and a revoked qualification is never eligible for a dispatch or an audit.

### 3.10. Dynamic Pay Rates, the Weekly Ceiling, and Settlement

Compensation for an accepted assignment is computed from a dynamic rate, not from a fixed price, and the arithmetic is pinned to a single rounding site. The base rate for a commit is 9 cents when the assignment's `kind` is `GOLD` and 6 cents when it is `PRODUCTION`. Two multipliers then apply: a streak multiplier keyed to the answering qualification's `gold_fail_streak`, and a tier multiplier keyed to the qualification's `tier`. The streak multiplier is 1.0 when the streak is 0 or 1, 0.8 when it is exactly 2, and 0.5 when it is 3 or higher. The tier multiplier is 1.0 for `NOVICE`, 1.25 for `PROFICIENT`, and 1.5 for `EXPERT`, and it is the same `tier_multiplier` the qualification exposes. The unrounded amount is the base rate multiplied by the streak multiplier and then by the tier multiplier; that product is then rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and that rounding is the **only** rounding site in the calculation. Rounding after each factor produces a different and wrong answer.

The weekly ceiling is applied after that single rounding and never before it. The engagement's `weekly_earning_cap_cents` is a per-worker ceiling on accepted payouts inside one week epoch, and the amount already settled inside the current epoch is the sum of `paid_amount_cents` over the **Commit** rows belonging to this worker and this epoch that are already in payment status `ACCEPTED`. The remaining allowance is the ceiling minus that settled sum, floored at zero so it can never be negative. The accepted amount is the smaller of the rounded uncapped product and that remaining allowance, and the commit's `payment_capped` flag is true exactly when the rounded uncapped product is strictly greater than the remaining allowance — that is, when the ceiling rather than the formula bound the payment. The week epoch the payment lands in is stamped onto the commit as `week_epoch_index`, and it is the single `OPEN` week epoch.

A `PlatformAdmin` accepts a payment by asserting `amount_cents` and a `currency`. The assertion must match the computed amount exactly: an under-stated or over-stated claim is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422) rather than being silently corrected. The currency must be the worker's `payout_currency`, else `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (409). A payment is accepted only against an assignment whose status is `COMMITTED`; a `DISPATCHED`, `ABSTAINED`, or `COMMIT_EXPIRED` assignment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Accepting the payment credits the worker's `worker_receivable_cents` and their `lifetime_earned_cents`, sets the commit's `paid_amount_cents` and `payment_status` to `ACCEPTED`, and — as §3.11 describes — may fire the engagement's quality embargo. The commit's `payout_complete` flag is a derived convenience: it is true when every `COMMITTED` commit on that piece has a payment status of `ACCEPTED` or `REVERSED`. Paying a worker who is `restricted` is refused with `ERR_WORKER_RESTRICTED` (403).

The same rate may be read on a provisional basis. Supplying an `earnings_variant` of `PROVISIONAL` on the worker read inserts an additional decay factor into the same computation, and the factor is what makes the reading provisional rather than authoritative; omitting the parameter yields the base computation described above, and an `earnings_variant` of `ACCEPTED` or `BASE` is accepted as a synonym for the base reading. The provisional read adds a single field, `provisional_earnings_cents`, to the worker object: it is the sum over every assignment owned by that worker, production and gold alike, of that assignment's provisional rate, and it is present only when the variant is requested. Supplying any other value for `earnings_variant` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The decay factor is the larger of 0.5 and the quantity one minus 0.05 multiplied by the whole number of times the worker's `lifetime_earned_cents` exceeds each 10000-cent step — that is, one minus 0.05 times the floor of lifetime earnings divided by 10000. It is multiplied in **before** the tier factor, so the multiplication order changes even though the single rounding site does not. The divergence is real and small: a `PROFICIENT` worker with zero lifetime earnings earns 6 times 1.0 times 1.25, which is 7.5 rounded half up to 8, on a production commit; the same worker having crossed 10000 lifetime cents earns 6 times 1.0 times 0.95 times 1.25, which is 7.125 rounded half up to 7. That one-cent divergence exists only under the provisional variant and never under the base computation.

Workers are paid into a receivable balance and withdraw from it. Paying a commit raises `worker_receivable_cents`, the conserved quantity carried on the **WorkerIdentity** and computed as the sum of accepted payouts minus settled cents plus reversed cents, which is never negative. A `Worker` — or a `PlatformAdmin` on their behalf — requests a settlement of `amount_cents`, which is accepted only when the worker's `worker_receivable_cents` meets or exceeds their `settlement_min_cents` floor, and only when the requested currency is the worker's `payout_currency`, else `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (409). The request's `currency` is optional and takes the worker's own `payout_currency` when omitted, so an omitted currency never trips the currency guard. A settlement decrements `worker_receivable_cents` by the settled amount, and any settlement or reversal that would drive the balance strictly negative is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECEIVABLE` (422). A settlement request from a worker whose `restricted` flag is set is refused with `ERR_WORKER_RESTRICTED` (403). Lifetime earnings are deliberately excluded from the settlement path: `lifetime_earned_cents` is a monotonic sum of accepted payout amounts that is never decremented, not even by a reversal, so it is an accurate record of what the worker has ever earned while `worker_receivable_cents` reflects what the worker may still withdraw. The worker also carries `total_accepted_payout_cents`, the sum of accepted payouts over all epochs, against which the settlement ceiling is measured.

### 3.11. Capacity Planning and the Waterfall Allocation

Every engagement is planned against a fixed pool of workforce points, and the plan's demand side is built from the ceiling of each unit rather than from its actual usage. A unit's ceiling cost in points is its `max_items` multiplied by the unit's `capacity_weight`, and the weight depends on the engagement's `sensitivity_class`: it is 1 for `STANDARD`, 2 for `SENSITIVE`, and 3 for `RESTRICTED`. A unit's **effective** cost applies a rework multiplier to that ceiling: the multiplier is 1.0 normally and 1.2 while the unit holds at least one item whose `consensus_status` is `REWORK`, and the effective cost is the ceiling multiplied by that multiplier and rounded half up to a whole number of points. Both derived values are exposed on the unit as `unit_ceiling_points`, `unit_effective_points`, and `rework_point_multiplier`.

Demand is deliberately blind to lifecycle. The engagement's `demand_points` is the sum of `unit_effective_points` over **every** **SpecUnit** under the engagement, including units still in `DRAFT` and units already in `REJECTED`, because collapsing a unit never refunds its ceiling. The requirement is then `demand_points` scaled by an accuracy multiplier: the multiplier is 1.0 under the `ACCEPTED` variant, 1.15 under `PROVISIONAL`, and 1.35 under `AT_RISK`, and the engagement exposes both the multiplier as `capacity_multiplier` and the resulting requirement as `capacity_requirement_points`, the product rounded half up to a whole number of points.

The per-item point charge is selected by the plan's accuracy band, and the two boundaries are inclusive in the specific directions stated here. Accuracy strictly below `accuracy_floor_pct` takes the fallback charge `point_cost_fallback`, defaulting to 4 points, and sets a below-floor indicator. Accuracy at or above `accuracy_floor_pct` but strictly below `accuracy_high_watermark_pct` takes the medium charge `point_cost_medium_accuracy`, defaulting to 3 points, and is **not** below floor. Accuracy at or above `accuracy_high_watermark_pct` takes the cheap charge `point_cost_high_accuracy`, defaulting to 2 points. Two boundary readings follow directly: accuracy exactly equal to `accuracy_floor_pct` takes the medium branch and is not below the floor, and accuracy exactly equal to `accuracy_high_watermark_pct` takes the high branch. Feasibility itself is the conjunction of two conditions and is exposed as `capacity_feasible`: `capacity_points` must be at or above `capacity_requirement_points`, and the requested accuracy variant must be at or above `accuracy_floor_pct`. The engagement's `projected_miss_deadline` is true exactly when `capacity_feasible` is false, and the companion flag `accuracy_floor_variant_below` is true exactly when the requested accuracy variant is strictly below `accuracy_floor_pct` — equality with the floor is **not** below it. A plan is therefore infeasible whenever the accuracy variant sits strictly below the floor, even when capacity is plentiful, and assessing such a plan is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_INFEASIBLE` (409). The accuracy band is read from the accepted variant unless a variant is requested, which is why §3.8 insists that the accepted variant is the one every acceptance decision reads.

Planning also produces a staffing signal. The engagement's `qa_sample_rate` is the highest `gold_rate` declared by any unit under it, so the audit load the engagement must carry is driven by its most heavily audited unit rather than by an average. Delivery performance is tracked by `on_time`, which is true when every piece that entered `REWORK` was eventually certified at or before the engagement's `deadline_utc`; a piece still sitting in rework, or one rejected rather than certified, leaves the flag false. The flag is minted false when the engagement is registered and is not advanced by any of the recomputations named below: no write path rewrites `on_time`, and a read of the engagement recomputes the plan metrics without persisting this one, so it holds its stored value throughout.

Asking for the plan is an explicit act. A `PlatformAdmin` drives the engagement's lifecycle through a `program_action`, whose value is `OPEN`, `ASSESS_CAPACITY`, `RECALL`, or `FREEZE`; opening, recalling, and freezing are described in §3.2 and §3.13, and the `ASSESS_CAPACITY` action recomputes `demand_points`, `capacity_requirement_points`, `capacity_feasible`, and `projected_miss_deadline` and refuses an infeasible plan with `ERR_CAPACITY_INFEASIBLE` (409). Reading the engagement with a `plan_variant` recomputes the same derived points under the requested variant, which is how the `PROVISIONAL` and `AT_RISK` readings are obtained; both the `plan_variant` query parameter and the `program_action` body field are accepted on their respective surfaces, and `plan_variant` is likewise accepted when the plan is assessed. A variant value is accepted in any letter case and is read for its accuracy by a single rule: `ACCEPTED` and an omitted variant both read the accepted variant, while `PROVISIONAL` and `AT_RISK` both read the provisional variant, so `AT_RISK` is distinguished from `PROVISIONAL` only by the multiplier it applies to the demand. An unrecognised `plan_variant` on the read surface is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400); on the assess surface an unrecognised value falls back to the accepted reading rather than being refused.

The bubble-up from finished work into the plan is automatic. When the consensus resolver certifies the last child piece of a unit, that unit's own completion check runs: the unit completes when every attached piece has a `consensus_status` of `CERTIFIED` or `REJECTED`, at least one attached piece is `CERTIFIED`, and every `COMMITTED` commit on the unit's pieces is paid so that `payout_complete` is true on each certified piece. Completion sets the unit's status to `COMPLETE` and its `piece_status` to `CERTIFIED`, recomputes `certified_item_count` as the count of attached pieces whose `consensus_status` is `CERTIFIED`, recomputes `unit_accuracy_pct` and `unit_provisional_accuracy_pct` over the unit's gold-audited pieces, and then pushes upward: the engagement's `demand_points` is recomputed from `unit_effective_points`, and `capacity_requirement_points`, `capacity_feasible`, and `projected_miss_deadline` are recomputed with it, while `accepted_accuracy_pct`, `provisional_accuracy_pct`, and `on_time` are recomputed on the engagement as well. This is a three-hop bubble: the panel resolver certifies the last child piece, the unit completes as a consequence, and the engagement's demand and accuracy metrics then recompute. A completed unit can also be reopened: a `PlatformAdmin` reopens a `COMPLETE` unit when at least one attached piece has `consensus_status` `REWORK` and the parent engagement is in `ACTIVE` or `QUALITY_EMBARGO`, which returns the unit's status to `ACTIVE` and its `piece_status` to `REWORK`, increments the unit's `version` by 1, and lets the cycle turn again.

### 3.12. Worker Disputes and the Appeal Ladder

A rejected or disputed piece can be challenged by the worker who answered it, and the challenge is carried by an **AppealClaim** whose status ladder is `ADVOCACY`, `PROPOSED`, `CONCURRENCE`, `ADJUDICATION`, `ACCEPTED`, `REVIEWER_SUSPENSION`, `REJECTED`, and `DEADLOCK`, of which the last four are terminal. A `Worker` files a claim naming the disputed `item_id`, a `grounds` value of `AMBIGUOUS_INSTRUCTIONS`, `INSUFFICIENT_CONTEXT`, or `TECHNICAL_ERROR`, and a free-text `statement_text`, with an optional `requested_reviewer_id`. The filing resolves its own references rather than trusting the client: the claim's `program_id` is resolved as the engagement owning the piece through the piece's unit, its `spec_unit_id` is denormalized from that piece, and its `worker_id` is resolved as the **WorkerIdentity** whose `id` equals the submitting `X-User-ID`. The `requested_reviewer_id` is a preference only and is never binding on who adjudicates. The claim is created in `ADVOCACY`. A reviewer may also file on a worker's behalf, and that filing carries an optional `worker_id` naming the worker the claim is filed for; when the reviewer omits it, the claim is recorded against the reviewer's own identifier. A piece that cannot be resolved — or whose unit or engagement cannot be resolved from it — is refused at filing with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404).

Moving a claim forward is expressed through an `appeal_action` field, whose value is `PROPOSE`, `ESCALATE`, `SUSPEND`, or `WITHDRAW`, together with an optional `proposed_outcome`. A `PlatformReviewer` proposes an outcome with the `PROPOSE` action on an `ADVOCACY` claim, which requires that the acting reviewer holds no commit on the disputed piece and is otherwise refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403); a `PROPOSE` that names no `proposed_outcome` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The proposal sets the claim's status to `PROPOSED`, records `adjudicator_id` as the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID`, and records the submitted `proposed_outcome`, which is one of `PAY_REVERSAL`, `QUALIFICATION_RESET`, or `OUTCOME_STANDS`. One compensation rule fires on proposal: when the claim's `grounds` is `AMBIGUOUS_INSTRUCTIONS` and the `proposed_outcome` is `PAY_REVERSAL`, the claim's `revised_amount_cents` is set to the disputed commit's `paid_amount_cents`; the disputed commit is the filing worker's own assignment on the disputed piece, preferring one already in payment status `ACCEPTED` and taking the lowest-ranked such assignment, and when the proposal is anything other than that one combination `revised_amount_cents` is left clear rather than carried over. That is the only combination that qualifies a later outcome as compensating; `INSUFFICIENT_CONTEXT` and `TECHNICAL_ERROR` never do.

Concurrence is the second reviewer's agreement, and it is a separate act from the proposal. A `PlatformReviewer` concurs on a `PROPOSED` claim, which requires three things: the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `adjudicator_id`, the acting reviewer must hold no commit on the disputed piece, and the claim's `proposed_outcome` must be `PAY_REVERSAL` while its `grounds` is `AMBIGUOUS_INSTRUCTIONS`. A concurrence carries `expected_claim_version` and is subject to the OCC guard of §2.10, so concurring against a claim that has moved to adjudication or been withdrawn is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) — the reviewer would otherwise be concurring with a proposal the claim no longer carries. A concurrence rewrites a great deal at once. The claim's status becomes `CONCURRENCE`. The disputed commit's `payment_status` moves from `ACCEPTED` to `REVERSED` and its `reversed_amount_cents` is set to `revised_amount_cents`, so the accepted-payout sum drops the row while the reversed-cents term adds the revised amount back, which is how the disputed worker's `worker_receivable_cents` is increased by `revised_amount_cents` against a balance the reversal has already reduced; the row keeps its recorded paid amount, and the worker's `lifetime_earned_cents` is deliberately left unchanged, so the reversal is compensating rather than a rewinding of lifetime earnings. The disputed qualification's `gold_fail_streak` is reset to 0, and the `gold_fail_streak` this and the `QUALIFICATION_RESET` concurrence clear belongs to the qualification of the disputed commit's author for the engagement's language. And the disputed piece's `consensus_status` becomes `REWORK` with `rework_cycles` incremented by 1 and `rework_started_at` set to the current logical clock, which returns the piece to the panel under §3.6. A reversal that would drive the worker's `worker_receivable_cents` strictly negative is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECEIVABLE` (422). Once the concurrence completes without disagreement, the claim transitions automatically to `ACCEPTED` and the disputed piece is returned to the panel under the same re-dispatch rules.

Not every proposal is concurred with, and the ladder has two further directions. A `PlatformReviewer` escalates with the `ESCALATE` action a `PROPOSED` claim whose `proposed_outcome` is `QUALIFICATION_RESET`, which requires that the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `adjudicator_id`; that moves the claim to `ADJUDICATION` and enters it into the reviewer-to-reviewer cycle. From `ADJUDICATION`, a `PlatformReviewer` who holds no commit on the disputed piece may concur on a `QUALIFICATION_RESET` proposal, which moves the claim to `CONCURRENCE` and resets the disputed qualification's `gold_fail_streak` to 0 — this is the correction path that rehabilitates a worker whose fail streak was caused by a mis-graded audit, and unlike a `PAY_REVERSAL` concurrence it touches no money. Alternatively, a `PlatformReviewer` may escalate a claim out of `ADJUDICATION` again with the `ESCALATE` action, which requires that the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `adjudicator_id`, and which returns the claim to `PROPOSED` with `adjudicator_id` set to the acting reviewer's `X-User-ID` and `proposed_outcome` set to the resubmitted outcome, recomputing `revised_amount_cents` for that new outcome; the escalation that reopens the claim also clears its adjudication entry instant. In this way a claim can cycle between `PROPOSED` and `ADJUDICATION` while reviewers keep failing to agree, which is exactly the condition the deadlock window measures.

Two outcomes terminate a claim without agreement. A `PlatformAdmin` suspends a claim with the `SUSPEND` action out of `ADJUDICATION` when it has spent at least one full `patience_seconds` window in that state without agreement, which sets the status to `REVIEWER_SUSPENSION`, sets `lock_reason` to `REVIEWER_SUSPENSION`, and freezes the disputed piece with its outcome unchanged and `holds_quorum` set to true. The reference instant the suspension window measures from is the claim's entry into `ADJUDICATION`; a claim that has not yet spent a full window there is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), as is a claim in any state other than `ADJUDICATION`. And when the seconds elapsed since the claim entered `ADJUDICATION` are at or above `patience_seconds` with no concurrence recorded, the claim transitions automatically to `DEADLOCK`, its `consensus_outcome` is filled with `DEADLOCKED`, and the disputed piece is released with its outcome unchanged and `holds_quorum` set to false; this sweep advances the claim's status and stamps its `updated_at` but does not bump its `version`. The `consensus_outcome` field draws its value from `UPHELD`, `DENIED`, `DEADLOCKED`, or null, and although only the deadlock value is ever written by the transition specified here, the field's vocabulary is fixed at that enumeration. The field is filled only on the deadlock terminal path and is null everywhere else; the same is true of `lock_reason`, which is informational and set only by the suspension path. Both terminal paths leave the disputed piece's `consensus_status` exactly as it stood, so a `REWORK` piece stays in rework and a `CERTIFIED` piece stays certified.

Withdrawal is the worker's own exit. A `Worker` withdraws a claim with the `WITHDRAW` action out of `PROPOSED`, which requires that the acting `worker_id` equals the claim's `worker_id` and that the claim is still within one `patience_seconds` window of filing. Withdrawal sets the status to `REJECTED`, sets `lock_reason` to null, and applies no payment or qualification mutation whatsoever — the disputed state is left exactly as it was. Every `advance-appeal` action on a claim whose engagement is `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) before the action itself is examined, and an action whose ladder edge does not exist — a `WITHDRAW` by a reviewer, or an `ESCALATE` of something other than a `QUALIFICATION_RESET` proposal — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

### 3.13. Quality Embargo, Recall, and the Terminal Freeze

An engagement in `ACTIVE` can be stopped by its own quality record, and the trigger is a payout acceptance rather than a review. When a payout record is accepted on an engagement whose accepted accuracy variant is strictly below its `accuracy_floor_pct`, and whose `accuracy_denominator_count` is at least 1 so that an empty denominator never triggers the embargo, the engagement's status becomes `QUALITY_EMBARGO` and its `version` is incremented by 1. The embargo is not a passive flag: every **SpecUnit** under the engagement whose status is `ACTIVE` transitions to `piece_status` `REWORK` and has its own `version` incremented by 1, and for each such unit every attached **LabelingItem** whose `consensus_status` is `AWAITING_CONSENSUS` transitions to `consensus_status` `REWORK` and `item_status` `REWORK` with its `rework_cycles` incremented by 1 and its `rework_started_at` set to the current logical clock. The chain is three hops deep — the payout acceptance is the first hop, the engagement embargo is the second, the unit rework sweep is the third, and the piece consensus demotion is the third hop's own child mutation — which is why a single accepted payment can move an entire engagement's worth of state.

An embargo is recoverable. A `PlatformAdmin` recalls the engagement out of `QUALITY_EMBARGO` when its accepted accuracy variant has recovered to at or above `accuracy_floor_pct`, which returns the status to `ACTIVE`, increments the `version` by 1, and recomputes `operational_restricted` and `open_incident_count` from the engagement's unresolved incidents. A recall attempted while accuracy is still below the floor is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), as is one attempted from any state other than `QUALITY_EMBARGO`.

Freezing is the terminal outcome and it is deliberately unconditional on quality. A `PlatformAdmin` freezes an engagement in `ACTIVE` when no **SpecUnit** under it has `piece_status` `CONSENSUS` — an engagement with a live panel cannot be frozen, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). An engagement in `QUALITY_EMBARGO` may be frozen with no precondition at all. Both paths apply the same cascade. The engagement's status becomes `FROZEN` and its `version` is incremented by 1. Every **SpecUnit** under the engagement transitions to status `REJECTED` and `piece_status` `REJECTED`. Every **LabelingItem** under those units transitions to container `status` `RESOLVED`, `item_status` `COMPLETED`, and `consensus_status` `REJECTED`, and has its own `version` incremented by 1. And every **Commit** on those pieces still in `DISPATCHED` transitions to `COMMIT_EXPIRED`. `FROZEN` is terminal: no transition leaves it, and every write path that reaches a resource whose ancestor engagement is `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new stand-alone resource, or that add a new distinct resource to a collection, return `201 Created`; the registrations of a worker, an engagement, a unit, a piece, a gold audit, a confidentiality incident, and an appeal claim all fall in that class. Operations that modify an existing resource — including lifecycle advances, panel dispatch, grading, payment acceptance, settlement, and clock advancement — return `200 OK`. Two status details cut across that split. A qualification operation is not treated as a stand-alone registration: enrolling, verifying, or resetting a qualification returns `200 OK` rather than `201`, because the qualification is addressed through its worker. And the confidentiality-incident endpoint returns `201 Created` for both of its arms — the `REPORT` arm that files a new incident and the `RESOLVE` arm that closes an outstanding one alike. Every endpoint returns the standard envelope of §2.2, and unless a statement elsewhere in this document says otherwise, a create or modify operation returns the created or updated resource object in the `data` field. The clock-setting capability returns an empty JSON object `{}` as its data.
