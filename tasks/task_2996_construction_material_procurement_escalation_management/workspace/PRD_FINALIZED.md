# Construction Material Procurement & Escalation Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

On a large construction project, the material buyout is where the estimate either holds or quietly disintegrates. The general contractor's procurement group must identify long-lead scope early enough that a mill or a switchgear plant can still fit it into a production slot, negotiate escalation clauses that reference published commodity indices without handing the supplier an unbounded price ratchet, hold quoted prices firm long enough for the owner to approve them, and — when the schedule pulls left or a tariff lands mid-negotiation — buy material out early, park it in a bailed warehouse, and keep the insurance and lien-waiver paperwork clean enough that the owner will actually pay for it. Every one of those moves has a dollar consequence and a schedule consequence, and today the two are tracked in different spreadsheets by different people.

This document specifies the Construction Material Procurement & Escalation Management API, the system of record for that work. It is mounted under `/procurement` and it owns the full chain from long-lead identification through release-for-fabrication, escalation recognition, off-site storage, engineered substitution, and owner change-order substantiation. The API is deliberately opinionated about two things. First, the **buyout log** — the running comparison of estimated cost against committed cost, and of escalation accrued against the escalation contingency set aside to absorb it — is derived, never hand-maintained: every read of a project re-derives it from the underlying packages, purchase orders and change orders. Second, escalation is **recognized twice**: provisionally when material is released for fabrication, and finally when it is accepted on site, against different index readings selected by different rules. A contractor who bills the owner from the provisional number is billing a guess, and the system refuses to let a change order be substantiated from anything other than the final figure.

Because almost every rule in this domain is a function of *when* — a price hold expires, an index period is published, a document lapses, a warehouse month elapses — the server keeps an explicit logical clock that the procurement group advances, rather than reading wall-clock time. Advancing that clock is a first-class business event with a documented cascade: quotes expire, un-executed escalation clauses lapse, pending purchase orders attached to those clauses are voided, and the affected scope packages fall back into sourcing. The specification below defines that cascade, the five operator roles that may drive it, the derived arithmetic each read must reproduce, and the exact order in which a malformed or ill-timed request is refused.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, audit fields, the logical clock, rounding discipline, version pinning, and the precedence order in which validation failures are reported.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The five roles are `BuyoutManager`, `SupplierRep`, `ProjectExecutive`, `DesignEngineer`, and `OwnerRep`.

The `BuyoutManager` is the general contractor's procurement buyer. This role registers scope packages and suppliers, publishes index readings, drafts and proposes escalation terms, issues purchase orders, drives the delivery posture, arranges off-site storage, revokes documents, raises and resubmits substitutions, prepares and submits owner change orders, and advances the logical clock. The `SupplierRep` speaks for one fabricator or vendor: it submits and revises quotes, counters proposed escalation terms, and may file storage documents. The `ProjectExecutive` is the contractor officer with signing authority: it executes escalation clauses, countersigns and cancels purchase orders, and suspends suppliers. This is **not** a hierarchical override — a `ProjectExecutive` cannot register a package, issue a purchase order, move a delivery posture, or advance the clock. These are peer roles with disjoint authority over the same rows. The `DesignEngineer` is the engineer of record and the only role that may dispose of a substitution request. The `OwnerRep` is the owner's representative: it has full read access, including the project buyout log and the change-order records, and it is the only role that may dispose of a submitted change order.

Only one role is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare that resource's supplier foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SupplierRep` submitting a quote must supply its own supplier identifier, may only revise a quote it itself issued, may only counter escalation terms on a quote it itself issued, and may only file storage documents for material it itself supplied. A mismatch here is **not** an authorization failure against the endpoint's role list — it is `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `BuyoutManager`, `ProjectExecutive`, `DesignEngineer`, or `OwnerRep`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it in several places — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a substitution request, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` on a change order request — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers exist so that segregation of duties can be enforced *within* the `BuyoutManager` role: the operator who prepares an owner change order may not be the operator who submits it to the owner. Both are `BuyoutManager` users, and a self-submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by their creation timestamp, `created_at`, ascending — oldest first. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

Filters offered on a list endpoint are combined conjunctively: a request that supplies both a package identifier and a status returns only the rows matching both. Filters are also the only reverse-navigation channel in this API — because parentage is expressed through foreign-key fields rather than nested collection paths, a caller finds a package's quotes by filtering the quote list on `material_package_id`, a supplier's quotes by filtering on `supplier_id`, a revision chain by filtering on `supersedes_quote_id`, a project's packages by filtering on `project_id`, a commitment's storage arrangements by filtering on `purchase_commitment_id`, and so on.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Version Discipline

Every resource the API mints carries a server-generated `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example, `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources — projects, scope packages, suppliers, quotes, escalation agreements, purchase commitments, storage arrangements, storage documents, substitution requests, change order requests and disruption events — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation. Published index readings are the sole exception: a reading is append-only configuration data with no update endpoint and no delete endpoint, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field at all. Corrections to a published index are issued as a new period, never as an edit.

Projects deserve a specific note, because their `version` is the token that guards the buyout log. A project's money roll-ups are all derived on read, and a pure re-derivation never touches `version`. A project's `version` increments by exactly 1 only on the five events that genuinely enlarge or unwind a commitment: issuing a purchase commitment, countersigning it, cancelling it, releasing its material for fabrication, and accepting its delivery. Reading a project a hundred times leaves its `version` where it was.

### 2.5. The Logical Procurement Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time. It keeps a single logical UTC clock that every window, expiry, float, month-count and index-period rule consults. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this fixed startup default must not be replaced by the host's system time. A `BuyoutManager` moves the clock forward by supplying a target instant in `to_iso8601`. The clock is monotonic: a target earlier than the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to precisely its current value is not a rewind and is accepted.

Advancing the clock is a business event, not a test convenience, and it runs a four-hop cascade in a fixed order. Hop one expires price holds: every quote still `OPEN` whose `expires_at` is at or before the supplied instant becomes `EXPIRED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Hop two lapses negotiations: every escalation agreement attached to a quote that just expired, and whose own status is `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, becomes `LAPSED`. Hop three voids un-countersigned buyouts: every purchase commitment whose `commitment_status` is `PENDING` and whose attached escalation agreement just lapsed becomes `VOIDED`. Hop four returns scope to the market: each of those voided commitments releases its scope package from `BOUGHT_OUT`. Only after all four hops have run does a single stall sweep pass over the affected packages and mark every one that has no `OPEN` quote left as `SOURCING_STALLED`; the sweep runs once, not once per hop.

The clock response reports the resulting instant in `current_utc` and returns four audit lists — `expired_quote_ids`, `lapsed_agreement_ids`, `voided_commitment_ids` and `stalled_package_ids` — each ordered by the `created_at` of the rows it names, ascending. Beyond those recorded transitions, advancing the clock silently changes a great deal of derived data that is recomputed on the next read rather than written now: every package's `float_days` and `rff_risk_band`, every supplier's snapshot exposure, and every storage arrangement's `documentation_status` and `carrying_cost_accrued`.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are JSON numbers, never strings, and are stored and returned with at most 2 decimal places using **round half up**. Percentage fields carry the precision their own rule declares — escalation deadbands, caps and sharing bands to one decimal place, monthly storage rates to as many as four — and are never coerced to a coarser precision on the way in.

Two calculations pin an intermediate precision that the implementer must honour exactly, because rounding at a different step yields a different cent. The index movement percentage that drives escalation is rounded to **4** decimal places once, and only the final dollar figure is rounded to 2; the deadband subtraction, the cap clamp and the sharing split in between are carried unrounded. Storage carrying cost is the mirror image: the two per-month charges are each rounded to 2 decimal places *before* they are added, and the resulting per-month total is multiplied by the month count and rounded to 2 again. Summing unrounded monthly amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong.

Day counts and month counts are exact integers with no rounding. Where a day count is derived from two instants, the count is the number of whole days between them, taken toward negative infinity, so a release date that has already slipped into the past yields a negative float rather than zero.

### 2.7. Version Pinning at Buyout (Optimistic Concurrency Control)

Exactly one write in this API takes a concurrency token. A `POST /procurement/purchase-commitments` request must carry `expected_project_version`, a non-optional field whose value is the client's last observed `version` of the project that owns the package being bought out. If that value does not equal the project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created.

The reason this single write is guarded is fiscal rather than structural. A project legitimately carries many purchase commitments, so a second buyout is not blocked by any state precondition — but each successful buyout consumes part of a shared escalation contingency and advances the project's `version`, so a client working from a stale view of the buyout log would be committing scope against a contingency balance that no longer exists. Pinning the version forces that client to re-read the project first.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Countersigning, cancelling, moving a delivery posture, filing a document, disposing of a substitution and submitting a change order are all unguarded by optimistic concurrency control, and supplying a version field to any of them is an unknown field and therefore a malformed request.

### 2.8. Reference Resolution: Path First, Then Body

Identifiers are resolved in a fixed order, and the first one that fails to resolve decides the error. Path identifiers are validated left to right before any request body is inspected; only once every path identifier resolves does the server resolve the foreign keys carried in the body. Each identifier resolves against a specific 404 code, so the failure is always attributable: a missing project yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing scope package `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing supplier `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing quote `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing escalation agreement `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing purchase commitment `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The remaining record types — storage arrangements, storage documents, substitution requests, change order requests and disruption events — share the generic `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. All six of these codes are HTTP 404.

The same mapping applies to body foreign keys. A `project_id` that names no project is `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; a `material_package_id` that names no package is `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`; a `supplier_id` or a `proposed_supplier_id` that names no supplier is `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`; a `quote_id` that names no quote is `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`; an `escalation_agreement_id` that names no agreement is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `purchase_commitment_id` that names no commitment is `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and a `disruption_event_id` that names no event is `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Many requests violate more than one rule at once, and an implementation that reports the wrong one is wrong even though it correctly refused. Checks are therefore evaluated in a strict precedence order, and evaluation stops at the first failure. From highest precedence to lowest:

1. **Header authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, non-empty, with `X-User-Role` naming one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The declared role appears in the called endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint, all required fields are present; each field carries its declared JSON type; each enum field carries a declared member; monetary fields are non-negative with at most 2 decimal places; percentage fields fall within 0 through 100; day-count fields are integers of at least 0; datetime fields parse as ISO-8601 UTC instants; period fields are four digits, a hyphen, and a month from 01 through 12; and unknown fields are rejected outright. A required string field that is empty once surrounding whitespace is trimmed also fails. All of these are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, per §2.8 (404).
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key in the body resolves, per §2.8 (404).
6. **Supplier scope.** When the caller is a `SupplierRep`, the supplier it acts for must be its own. This rule guards exactly four operations: submitting a quote (`POST /procurement/quotes`, where the body's `supplier_id` must equal `X-User-ID`), revising a quote (`POST /procurement/quotes/{id}/revise`, where the path quote's `supplier_id` must equal `X-User-ID`), countering escalation terms (`POST /procurement/escalation-agreements/{id}/counter`, where the `supplier_id` of the agreement's quote must equal `X-User-ID`), and filing a storage document (`POST /procurement/storage-arrangements/{id}/documents`, where the `supplier_id` of the quote behind the arrangement's commitment must equal `X-User-ID`). A violation is `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
7. **Segregation of duties.** On `POST /procurement/change-order-requests/{id}/submit`, `X-User-ID` must differ from the change order's `prepared_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. **Version pinning.** On `POST /procurement/purchase-commitments`, `expected_project_version` must equal the current `version` of the project owning the package — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412).
9. **Own-state legality.** The requested transition must be a declared edge out of the target row's current state. A quote must be `OPEN` to be revised or bought out (`ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`, 409). An escalation agreement's propose, counter or execute must match its current status, a commitment's countersign or cancel must match its `commitment_status`, a substitution's disposition, resubmit or rescind must match its status, and a change order's submit or disposition must match its status (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409); the same code covers a substitution being dispositioned or rescinded while its scope package is already `CLOSED`. A delivery-posture move must be a declared edge of the posture axis (`ERR_INVALID_POSTURE_TRANSITION`, 409), and an early-buyout commitment may not jump from `FABRICATING` straight to `DELIVERED` (`ERR_STORAGE_STEP_REQUIRED`, 409). This rung guards every transition endpoint plus commitment creation and quote revision.
10. **Cross-entity state gates,** evaluated only once the target row's own state is legal, and all carrying HTTP 409. Executing a clause requires the underlying quote to still be `OPEN` (`ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`). Countersigning a commitment requires any attached clause to be `EXECUTED` (`ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_EXECUTED`) and the quote's supplier to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE`). Attaching a clause at commitment creation requires that clause to be `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ATTACHABLE`). Moving a posture to `STORED_OFFSITE`, whether from `FABRICATING` or back from `STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT`, requires a storage arrangement that is both `ACTIVE` and documentation-complete (`ERR_STORAGE_NOT_DOCUMENTED`). Creating a storage arrangement requires its commitment to be `COMMITTED` with a posture of `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` or `FABRICATING` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`). Submitting a change order requires its commitment's `escalation_variant` to be `FINAL` (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`). This rung guards clause execution, commitment creation, countersigning, delivery-posture moves, storage-arrangement creation and change-order submission.
11. **Referential coherence between rows that both exist.** A clause attached at commitment creation must reference the very quote being bought out (`ERR_AGREEMENT_QUOTE_MISMATCH`, 422). A clause's `index_code` must equal the `commodity_index_code` of its quote's package (`ERR_INDEX_CODE_MISMATCH`, 422) and its `baseline_index_period` must already have a published reading (`ERR_INDEX_READING_MISSING`, 409). A substitution's `proposed_supplier_id` must not be the supplier already carried by a `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` commitment on that package, and the `disruption_event_id` rule must hold — supplied when the driver is contingency sourcing and naming an event against the package's currently quoted or committed supplier, omitted for every other driver (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422). This rung guards commitment creation, escalation-agreement creation and substitution creation.
12. **Bailment and exposure safety,** evaluated before any temporal or fiscal check. An insurance certificate's `coverage_amount` must be at least the arrangement's `stored_value` (`ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, 422). A commitment may not be issued against a quote whose supplier carries a snapshot exposure band of `HIGH` (`ERR_SUPPLIER_EXPOSURE_TOO_HIGH`, 409). A substitution may not be approved, with or without conditions, when the proposed supplier's forward-looking exposure band is `HIGH` (`ERR_SUBSTITUTE_SUPPLIER_EXPOSED`, 409). This rung guards document filing, commitment creation and substitution disposition.
13. **Temporal windows,** all carrying HTTP 422. The clock may not be rewound (`ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`). A quote's `received_at` must be at or before the current logical clock and its `price_hold_days` at least 1 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). A clause's `price_hold_through` must be at or before the quote's `expires_at` — note this is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not as a storage-window error. A storage arrangement's `stored_from` must be at or after its commitment's `released_for_fab_at` and its `planned_release_date` must be strictly after `stored_from` (`ERR_STORAGE_WINDOW_INVALID`). A storage document's `expires_at` must be strictly after its `effective_date` (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). This rung guards the clock endpoint, quote creation, quote revision, escalation-agreement creation, storage-arrangement creation and document filing.
14. **Uniqueness and interval conflicts.** A package's `scope_code` is unique within its project (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPE_CODE`, 409); a commitment's `po_number` is globally unique (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PO_NUMBER`, 409); an index reading's index and period pair is globally unique (`ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_READING`, 409); a package may carry at most one commitment that is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` (`ERR_PACKAGE_ALREADY_COMMITTED`, 409); a new quote's price-hold interval may not overlap a live interval for the same package and supplier (`ERR_PRICE_HOLD_OVERLAP`, 409); a new arrangement's buffered occupancy interval may not collide with another live arrangement in the same warehouse (`ERR_WAREHOUSE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409); a counter offered once the negotiation rounds are used up is refused with no mutation at all (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 409); a counter that improves nothing for the supplier is refused (`ERR_COUNTER_NOT_RESPONSIVE`, 422); a further revision request beyond the substitution revision limit is refused (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409); and an approval-with-conditions that carries no conditions text is refused (`ERR_ENGINEER_CONDITIONS_REQUIRED`, 422). This rung guards package creation, quote creation, quote revision, index-reading publication, commitment creation, storage-arrangement creation, escalation counters and substitution dispositions.
15. **Fiscal ceilings, evaluated last.** A quote's `base_price` may not exceed its package's `estimated_cost` by more than the permitted buyout overrun (`ERR_BUYOUT_OVERRUN_EXCEEDED`, 422). The provisional escalation computed at release must fit inside the project's remaining escalation contingency (`ERR_CONTINGENCY_EXCEEDED`, 409). An arrangement's `stored_value` may not exceed its commitment's `committed_base_cost` (`ERR_STORED_VALUE_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`, 422). A change order requires a commitment whose escalation is final and greater than zero, and a `claimed_amount` greater than zero (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`, 409). This rung guards commitment creation, delivery-posture moves, storage-arrangement creation and change-order creation.

Two collisions are called out explicitly, because they are the ones most likely to be reported inconsistently. When a commitment is attempted against an expired quote whose `base_price` *also* overruns the package estimate, the own-state check wins and the answer is `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`, never `ERR_BUYOUT_OVERRUN_EXCEEDED`. When a storage arrangement is created whose `stored_from` precedes the commitment's `released_for_fab_at` *and* whose occupancy window also collides with another arrangement in the same warehouse, the temporal check wins and the answer is `ERR_STORAGE_WINDOW_INVALID`, never `ERR_WAREHOUSE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

### 2.10. Cascades, Derived Aggregates, and Where They Are Written

Two different mechanisms keep this system consistent, and confusing them is the most common way to get the buyout log wrong.

The first is **derivation on read**. A project's `estimated_total_cost`, `committed_total_cost`, `buyout_variance`, `bought_out_variance`, `escalation_contingency_committed`, `escalation_contingency_remaining` and `change_order_recovered_amount` are all recomputed from the current package, commitment and change-order rows every time the project is read. A package's `lead_time_days`, `release_for_fabrication_date`, `is_long_lead`, `float_days`, `rff_risk_band` and `open_quote_count` are recomputed on every read of that package. A supplier's `open_disruption_count`, `exposure_score_snapshot` and `exposure_band_snapshot` are recomputed on every read of that supplier. A storage arrangement's `documentation_status`, `verified_doc_type_count` and `carrying_cost_accrued` are recomputed on every read of that arrangement. Nothing about a derivation counts as a mutation: it never refreshes `updated_at` and never increments `version`.

The second is **cascading transitions**, where one operation moves rows other than the one addressed. These are genuine mutations of the affected rows, and each affected row's own `updated_at` is refreshed. Three cascades matter. Advancing the clock runs the four-hop quote-expiry chain described in §2.5. Revoking a storage document runs a three-hop chain: the document is marked revoked, its arrangement falls from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, and the commitment holding that material moves its delivery posture from `STORED_OFFSITE` to `STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT` — while its `commitment_status` is deliberately left alone. Approving a substitution runs a chain that mutates the scope package's cost and duration and then re-derives that package's whole release-for-fabrication schedule, and, when the substitution was raised to mitigate a logged disruption, closes that disruption event as well.

Where a cascade and a derivation meet, the derivation always reflects the post-cascade state. A project read taken immediately after a commitment is voided reports the smaller `committed_total_cost`, because the voided row no longer qualifies for the sum.

### 2.11. The Two State Axes of a Purchase Commitment

A purchase commitment is unusual in carrying two independent state machines on the same row. `commitment_status` is the **contractual** axis: `PENDING` at creation, `COMMITTED` once countersigned, `CLOSED` once the material is accepted, `CANCELLED` when an officer cancels it, `VOIDED` when the escalation clause behind it lapses. `delivery_posture` is the **physical** axis: `SCHEDULED` at creation, then `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, `FABRICATING`, optionally `STORED_OFFSITE` (which may cycle out to `STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT` and back), and finally `DELIVERED`.

The two state-name sets are disjoint, so no transition request is ambiguous across the axes. The contractual axis advances only through countersigning, cancellation, and the lapsed-clause cascade. The physical axis advances only through explicit posture moves plus the document-revocation cascade. Reaching a terminal value on one axis does not by itself move the other — with exactly two documented couplings: a posture reaching `DELIVERED` closes the contractual axis, and a cancellation resets the physical axis to `SCHEDULED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Setting Up the Job: Projects, Scope Packages and the Buyout Log

A job enters the system as a **Project**, opened either by a `BuyoutManager` or by a `ProjectExecutive`. Opening one requires a display `name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, the prime contract's `contract_number` of 1 to 40 non-blank characters, the contracting `owner_name`, the baseline `schedule_data_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, and an `escalation_contingency_budget` — the dollars the contractor has set aside to absorb material escalation it cannot pass through. The budget must be strictly greater than zero and carry at most 2 decimal places. A freshly opened project has `version` 1 and reports every money roll-up as 0.00, with the single exception of `escalation_contingency_remaining`, which equals the budget just supplied.

Everything else on a project is arithmetic. `estimated_total_cost` is the sum of `estimated_cost` over every scope package belonging to the project — every package, with no status filter at all, because there is no soft delete here and a package sitting in `IDENTIFIED` is still scope somebody has to buy. `committed_total_cost` is the sum, over the project's commitments whose `commitment_status` is `COMMITTED` or `CLOSED`, of each commitment's `committed_base_cost` plus its `escalation_accrued`; commitments that are `PENDING`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing. `escalation_contingency_committed` is the sum of `escalation_accrued` over that same `COMMITTED`-or-`CLOSED` set, and `escalation_contingency_remaining` is the budget less that figure, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `change_order_recovered_amount` is the sum of `substantiated_amount` over the project's change orders that the owner has approved. Each addend is already carried to 2 decimal places, so the order in which the sums are accumulated cannot change the answer, and no as-of filter applies — these are all-time figures.

The project reports variance twice, on purpose, because procurement groups argue about which number is honest. `buyout_variance` is the full-programme figure: `estimated_total_cost` across every package, less `committed_total_cost`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `bought_out_variance` is the apples-to-apples figure: the sum of `estimated_cost` restricted to packages whose status is `BOUGHT_OUT` or `CLOSED`, less the same `committed_total_cost`, rounded the same way. Consider a project with one bought-out package estimated at 1200000.00 against which 1182941.98 has been committed, plus one package still in `SOURCING` estimated at 300000.00: the full-programme variance reads 317058.02 while the bought-out variance reads 17058.02. A negative variance in either flavour means the buyout came in over the estimate. The owner's representative sees this log alongside the procurement group and the signing officer; a supplier's representative and the engineer of record do not.

Scope arrives as a **MaterialPackage**, registered by a `BuyoutManager` against a `project_id`. The package carries a `scope_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique within its project, compared case-sensitively — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPE_CODE` — plus a free-text `description`, an `estimated_cost` greater than zero with at most 2 decimal places, and a `commodity_index_code` naming the published price index this commodity escalates against. That code is one of `PPI_STEEL_MILL`, `PPI_COPPER_WIRE`, `PPI_LUMBER`, `PPI_CEMENT` or `PPI_SWITCHGEAR`. The schedule side of the package is four numbers: the `required_onsite_date` the material must be on site per the schedule, a `fabrication_duration_days` shop duration between 0 and 720 calendar days inclusive, a `delivery_transit_days` transit duration between 0 and 365 calendar days inclusive, and a `submittal_review_days` submittal-and-review duration between 0 and 180 calendar days inclusive that defaults to 21 when omitted. At creation the server also snapshots `baseline_estimated_cost` from the supplied `estimated_cost` and `baseline_fabrication_duration_days` from the supplied `fabrication_duration_days`; those two snapshots anchor the money and schedule halves of the substitution compensation invariant and are never mutated by a substitution.

From those inputs the release-for-fabrication schedule falls out. `lead_time_days` is the sum of `submittal_review_days`, `fabrication_duration_days` and `delivery_transit_days`. `release_for_fabrication_date` is the `required_onsite_date` moved backwards by `lead_time_days` calendar days. `is_long_lead` is a deliberately narrower test: it is true when `fabrication_duration_days` plus `delivery_transit_days` alone reaches at least 90 days — three months of pure market lead time — and it excludes `submittal_review_days` entirely, even though that duration *is* included in `lead_time_days`. A package with a sixty-day fabrication, a thirty-day transit and a sixty-day review has a hundred-and-fifty-day lead time and is long-lead; a package with a fifty-day fabrication, a thirty-day transit and the same sixty-day review has a hundred-and-forty-day lead time and is not.

`float_days` measures how much room is left before the release date arrives: it is the number of whole days from the current logical clock forward to `release_for_fabrication_date`, taken toward negative infinity, and it goes negative once that date is behind us. `rff_risk_band` reads `CRITICAL` when `float_days` is zero or less, `AT_RISK` when it is between 1 and 14 inclusive, and `ON_TRACK` when it is 15 or more. Both figures are re-derived against the clock on every read, so the same untouched package reports a different band tomorrow than it does today. `lead_time_days`, `release_for_fabrication_date` and `is_long_lead`, by contrast, are clock-independent. `open_quote_count` counts the package's quotes that are currently `OPEN`.

A package's status narrates its buyout. It begins `IDENTIFIED` with an `open_quote_count` of 0. The first quote landing on it moves it to `SOURCING`, and a quote landing on a package already in `SOURCING` leaves the status untouched. When a commitment is issued it becomes `BOUGHT_OUT`; when its commitment reaches delivery it becomes `CLOSED`; and when its last `OPEN` quote lapses it becomes `SOURCING_STALLED`, from which a fresh quote revives it back to `SOURCING`. A `CLOSED` package is terminal, and its `estimated_cost` is thereby frozen for variance purposes: no substitution may be applied against it afterwards.

As design develops, a `BuyoutManager` may revise a package's `required_onsite_date`, `fabrication_duration_days`, `delivery_transit_days`, `submittal_review_days` or `estimated_cost` — each optional, with the omitted ones left as they stand. Such a revision re-runs the whole release-for-fabrication derivation over the new durations, and it also rebaselines `baseline_estimated_cost` and `baseline_fabrication_duration_days` to the revised values, so that the conservation invariant between an applied substitution and its later rescission continues to hold exactly.

### 3.2. The Supply Base: Suppliers, Country of Origin and Disruption Exposure

A `BuyoutManager` registers each fabricator or vendor as a **Supplier**, carrying a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters that must be globally unique compared case-sensitively, and a `country_of_origin` given as exactly two uppercase letters identifying the producing plant's country. From that code the server derives `is_domestic`, which is true exactly when the country is `US`. A supplier may also declare a `declared_tariff_rate_pct` — the tariff rate it says applies to its imported content — as a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive, defaulting to 0 when omitted. Suppliers start with `status` `ACTIVE` and a null `suspension_reason`. A `ProjectExecutive` may suspend one by supplying a `reason`, which sets `status` to `SUSPENDED` and records that text in `suspension_reason`; existing commitments are left entirely untouched, but no new commitment may be countersigned against a suspended supplier's quote. Suppliers can be listed with filters on `status`, `country_of_origin` and `exposure_band_snapshot`.

Trouble in the supply base is logged as a **DisruptionEvent** against a specific supplier, recorded by a `BuyoutManager`. The event's `supplier_id` is taken from the addressed supplier rather than from the body. Its `event_type` is one of `TARIFF_IMPOSED`, `PLANT_SHUTDOWN`, `PORT_CONGESTION` or `FORCE_MAJEURE`; its `description` is 1 to 500 non-blank characters; and its `effective_date` is the instant the disruption bites. That instant **may sit in the future** relative to the logical clock — an announced tariff that takes effect next quarter is real information, and this is precisely the fact the two exposure variants disagree about. A `tariff_rate_pct` between 0 and 100 may accompany the event, defaulting to 0, and it is read only when the event type is `TARIFF_IMPOSED`; on any other event type the value is stored but ignored by every rule. Events start `OPEN`, with `mitigated_at` null, and they can be listed per supplier with filters on `status` and `event_type`.

Exposure is scored out of 100 from three components. The **tariff component** starts from an effective tariff rate defined as the greatest value among the supplier's `declared_tariff_rate_pct` and the `tariff_rate_pct` of every in-scope `OPEN` event whose `event_type` is `TARIFF_IMPOSED`, with an absent rate treated as zero. That effective rate is multiplied by 1.6, and the product is capped at 40 points. The **event component** is the count of in-scope `OPEN` events of any type, whatever their kind, multiplied by 20 points, also capped at 40 — so two open events already max this component out. The **geographic component** is zero for a supplier whose `country_of_origin` is `US` and a flat 20-point penalty for every other origin. The three components are added and the total is rounded half up to 2 decimal places to yield the exposure score. The band is then `HIGH` when the score is at least 60, `ELEVATED` when it is at least 30 but below 60, and `LOW` below 30; each threshold is an inclusive lower bound. Only `OPEN` events participate in either component — a `MITIGATED` event is excluded entirely, not down-weighted — and because the arithmetic uses only greatest-of, counting and capping, the order in which events are visited is irrelevant.

What differs between the two readings of that same formula is which events are "in scope". The **snapshot** reading, exposed on the supplier row as `exposure_score_snapshot` and `exposure_band_snapshot` and counted in `open_disruption_count`, admits only events that are `OPEN` *and* whose `effective_date` is at or before the current logical clock. A disruption dated next month is invisible to it, which is why a supplier can be committable today and `HIGH` tomorrow purely because the clock moved with nobody writing to the supplier at all. The **forward-looking** reading admits every `OPEN` event regardless of `effective_date`, so known future tariffs and announced shutdowns count now. On identical stored data the forward score is therefore always greater than or equal to the snapshot score.

Each reading gates a different decision, and this asymmetry is deliberate. Issuing a purchase commitment consults the **snapshot** band and is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_EXPOSURE_TOO_HIGH` when the quote's supplier reads `HIGH`. Approving a substitution — with or without conditions — consults the **forward-looking** band of the proposed supplier and is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_SUPPLIER_EXPOSED` when that reads `HIGH`, even if the same supplier's snapshot band is `LOW`. The engineer of record is being asked to bless a supplier for work that has not started yet, so announced future trouble counts against it; a buyer signing today against a firm price is judged on today's facts.

### 3.3. Quotes, Price Holds and the Expiry Sweep

A **Quote** is a price-hold instrument, and only a `SupplierRep` may submit one. The submission names the `material_package_id` being quoted and the `supplier_id` doing the quoting — which must be the caller's own supplier identity — plus a `base_price` greater than zero with at most 2 decimal places, the `received_at` instant the quote was received, and a `price_hold_days` firm-price period between 1 and 120 calendar days inclusive. The `received_at` instant may not be later than the current logical clock: a quote cannot be received in the future. From those two temporal inputs the server derives `expires_at` as `received_at` advanced by `price_hold_days` calendar days, and the price-hold interval is **half-open** — the price is holdable while the clock is strictly before `expires_at`, and the instant `expires_at` itself is already outside the hold. New quotes start `OPEN` with `supersedes_quote_id` null, and their arrival is what lifts a package out of `IDENTIFIED` or revives it from `SOURCING_STALLED` into `SOURCING`, recomputing that package's `open_quote_count`.

The same supplier may not hold two live prices on the same package for overlapping periods. A new quote's half-open interval, running from `received_at` up to but excluding `expires_at`, must not overlap the interval of any existing quote for the same package and supplier whose status is `OPEN` or `CONVERTED`; a collision is `ERR_PRICE_HOLD_OVERLAP`. Intervals that merely touch — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not overlap, precisely because the interval is half-open at its upper end.

A supplier that wants to change its number revises its own quote rather than editing it. Revision requires the addressed quote to be `OPEN` and the caller to be that quote's supplier; it moves the addressed quote from `OPEN` to `SUPERSEDED` and creates a brand-new quote carrying its own `base_price`, `received_at` and `price_hold_days`, with `supersedes_quote_id` set to the superseded quote's identifier. The new quote is what the response returns. For this create alone, the quote being directly superseded is exempted from the price-hold overlap test — otherwise no supplier could ever re-price inside its own live hold — but every *other* live quote from the same supplier on that package is still tested. Both quotes' effect on the package's `open_quote_count` is recomputed. Revision chains are discoverable by filtering the quote list on `supersedes_quote_id`, and the list also filters on `material_package_id`, `supplier_id` and `status`.

A quote's status tells the rest of the story. It is `OPEN` while live, becomes `EXPIRED` when the clock sweep passes its `expires_at`, becomes `SUPERSEDED` when its supplier re-prices, and becomes `CONVERTED` at the moment a commitment against it is countersigned. `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal. `CONVERTED` is not: if that commitment is later cancelled, the quote goes back to `OPEN` when the clock is still strictly inside its hold, and straight to `EXPIRED` when the clock has already reached or passed `expires_at`. That conditional return is how a cancelled buyout puts a still-valid price back on the market instead of resurrecting a dead one.

Expiry itself is driven only by the clock. When a `BuyoutManager` advances the clock, every `OPEN` quote whose `expires_at` is at or before the new instant becomes `EXPIRED`, oldest `created_at` first, and each such identifier is reported in `expired_quote_ids`. Each expiry recomputes the affected package's `open_quote_count`, and each also drags any un-executed escalation clause attached to that quote into `LAPSED` — the second hop of the cascade described in §2.5.

### 3.4. Published Index Readings and the Negotiated Escalation Clause

Escalation in this domain is never a negotiated guess about the future; it is a formula pinned to a published number. Those numbers arrive as **IndexReading** rows, published by a `BuyoutManager`. Each reading names its `index_code` — one of `PPI_STEEL_MILL`, `PPI_COPPER_WIRE`, `PPI_LUMBER`, `PPI_CEMENT` or `PPI_SWITCHGEAR` — the calendar month it covers in `period`, written as four digits, a hyphen and a month from 01 through 12, a `value` greater than zero with at most 2 decimal places, and a `published_at` instant. The pair of index code and period is globally unique; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_READING`. Readings are immutable append-only configuration: there is no revision and no deletion, so a correction is published as a later period rather than as an edit, and consequently a reading carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. The `published_at` instant is recorded purely for audit — **no rule reads it** — which means readings covering future periods may be loaded at any clock position without disturbing anything. Readings can be listed with filters on `index_code` and `period`.

An **EscalationAgreement** is the clause itself, drafted by a `BuyoutManager` against exactly one quote. Drafting it requires the `quote_id` it escalates, the `index_code` it references, the `baseline_index_period` whose published reading will serve as the escalation baseline, a `cap_pct` greater than 0 and no greater than 25 with one decimal place, a `contractor_share_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive with one decimal place, and a `price_hold_through` instant through which the supplier holds the base price firm. Optionally it carries a `deadband_pct` between 0 and 20 inclusive with one decimal place, which defaults to 2.0 when omitted, and a free-text `proposed_terms_note`. The clause's `index_code` **must** equal the `commodity_index_code` of the package behind its quote — a copper clause on a steel package is `ERR_INDEX_CODE_MISMATCH` — and the `baseline_index_period` must already have a published reading, failing which the draft is refused with `ERR_INDEX_READING_MISSING`. The `price_hold_through` instant may not be later than the quote's `expires_at`; a clause cannot promise a firm price beyond the hold the quote itself grants, and that violation is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A new clause starts in `DRAFT` with `round_number` 0, `counter_note` null, `executed_at` null and `baseline_index_value` null — the baseline value stays null until execution. Clauses can be listed with filters on `quote_id`, `status` and `index_code`.

The three commercial terms are what the two parties actually argue over. `deadband_pct` is the band of index movement the supplier absorbs before any escalation is payable at all. `cap_pct` is the ceiling on escalation payable after the deadband is cleared. `contractor_share_pct` is the sharing band: the percentage of the capped escalation the contractor eats itself, with the remainder — one hundred less that percentage — being the owner-borne portion that actually accrues on the commitment. A clause with a fifty-percent contractor share hands half the capped movement to the owner's account and swallows the other half.

Negotiation is a bounded cycle. A `BuyoutManager` puts terms on the table, moving the clause from `DRAFT` to `PROPOSED`, and this same act is also how it comes back from `COUNTERED` to `PROPOSED` — the cycle `PROPOSED → COUNTERED → PROPOSED` is the whole negotiation. Either way the underlying quote must still be `OPEN`, and any of `cap_pct`, `contractor_share_pct`, `deadband_pct` or `proposed_terms_note` supplied with the proposal overwrite what was stored. The supplier's side of the cycle is the counter, available only to the `SupplierRep` whose supplier is the one behind the clause's quote. A counter must supply `cap_pct` and `contractor_share_pct`, and may supply `deadband_pct` and a `counter_note`; the supplied values overwrite the stored terms and `round_number` increments by exactly 1.

Two conditions bound the counter. First, it must be **responsive**: it has to move at least one term in the supplier's favour, meaning the offered `cap_pct` is strictly greater than the currently stored `cap_pct`, or the offered `contractor_share_pct` is strictly less than the currently stored `contractor_share_pct`. A counter that raises neither ceiling nor lowers neither share is `ERR_COUNTER_NOT_RESPONSIVE`. Second, the negotiation is capped at three rounds: a counter is only permitted while `round_number` is still below 3, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and — importantly — **no mutation whatsoever**, so the stored terms and `round_number` are exactly what they were before the rejected attempt.

Execution is reserved for the `ProjectExecutive`, and it is the moment the clause stops being a proposal. A clause may only be executed from `PROPOSED`; a clause still in `DRAFT` or sitting in `COUNTERED` cannot be executed, and neither can one already `EXECUTED` or `LAPSED`. The underlying quote must still be `OPEN` — a clause on a dead price is worthless, and this cross-entity gate reports `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`. A published reading must exist for the clause's `index_code` and `baseline_index_period`, otherwise `ERR_INDEX_READING_MISSING`. Execution then freezes `baseline_index_value` from that reading's `value`, pinning the reference number for the rest of the clause's life so that a later correction to the index cannot retroactively change the baseline, and stamps `executed_at` with the current logical clock. `EXECUTED` is terminal.

The other terminal outcome is failure by timeout. When the clock sweep expires the quote behind a clause, any clause still in `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` becomes `LAPSED` and its identifier is reported in `lapsed_agreement_ids`. Lapsing then propagates: every purchase commitment still `PENDING` that had this clause attached becomes `VOIDED`, and those commitments' packages fall back out of `BOUGHT_OUT`. A clause that has already reached `EXECUTED` is immune — the deal was struck while the price was live and stays struck.

### 3.5. Buying Out a Package: Issuing, Countersigning and Cancelling the Purchase Order

The **PurchaseCommitment** is the buyout record — the purchase order. A `BuyoutManager` issues one by naming the `quote_id` being bought out, a `po_number` of 1 to 40 characters that must be globally unique compared case-sensitively (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PO_NUMBER` otherwise), and the `expected_project_version` token described in §2.7. Two optional inputs shape what follows. An `escalation_agreement_id` attaches a negotiated clause; omitting it, or passing null, issues a flat purchase order that will never accrue escalation. An `is_early_buyout` boolean, false by default, declares that the order is being placed ahead of the schedule-driven need purely to lock the price in — and the server mirrors that declaration into `requires_storage_arrangement`, which always equals `is_early_buyout`. When that flag is true, the material must physically pass through off-site storage before it can be accepted on site.

Issuing an order snapshots the commercial facts so that later churn cannot disturb them. `committed_base_cost` is copied from the quote's `base_price` at this instant and is immune to any subsequent revision of that quote, and `material_package_id` is copied from the quote rather than supplied by the caller. The new row starts with `commitment_status` `PENDING` and `delivery_posture` `SCHEDULED`, with `released_for_fab_at`, `delivered_at`, `escalation_index_period` and `cancellation_reason` all null, `escalation_variant` `NONE` and `escalation_accrued` 0. The addressed package leaves `SOURCING` — or `SOURCING_STALLED`, since a stalled package can still be bought out from a quote created after the stall, provided that quote is `OPEN` — and becomes `BOUGHT_OUT`. The owning project's `version` increments by 1, and its `committed_total_cost`, `buyout_variance` and `bought_out_variance` all shift on the next read.

Several gates guard that create, and §2.9 fixes the order in which they are reported. The quote must be `OPEN`: an `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CONVERTED` quote yields `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`. The package may not already carry another commitment whose `commitment_status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` — one live purchase order per package, and a second is `ERR_PACKAGE_ALREADY_COMMITTED`. An attached clause must be in `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED`; attaching a `LAPSED` clause is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ATTACHABLE`, and note that the clause need *not* yet be executed at this point — execution is only required later, to countersign. An attached clause must also belong to the very quote being bought out: a clause whose own `quote_id` differs from the supplied `quote_id` is `ERR_AGREEMENT_QUOTE_MISMATCH`. The quote's supplier must not read `HIGH` on its snapshot exposure band, per §3.2. And finally the fiscal ceiling: the quote's `base_price` may not exceed the package's `estimated_cost` by more than fifteen percent — that is, a price above one-and-fifteen-hundredths times the current estimate is `ERR_BUYOUT_OVERRUN_EXCEEDED`. When a quote is both expired *and* over that ceiling, the answer is `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_OPEN`.

Countersigning belongs to the `ProjectExecutive`, and it is what makes the order contractually binding: `commitment_status` moves from `PENDING` to `COMMITTED`. The commitment must still be `PENDING`; a second countersignature is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The quote must still be `OPEN` *and* the clock must still be strictly before its `expires_at`, so a price that lapsed between issue and countersignature cannot be consumed. If a clause is attached, that clause must by now be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_EXECUTED`; a commitment with no clause attached skips this gate entirely. The quote's supplier must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success the quote itself moves from `OPEN` to `CONVERTED`, which removes it from the package's `open_quote_count`, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

Cancellation also belongs to the `ProjectExecutive` and always records a `cancellation_reason`. Its preconditions differ by the axis position it is cancelling from, and so do its consequences. Cancelling a `PENDING` commitment has no posture precondition at all — nothing has been released, no quote was ever converted, no escalation was ever recognized — so the effects are simply the recorded reason, the package leaving `BOUGHT_OUT`, and a project `version` increment. Cancelling a `COMMITTED` commitment is permitted **only** while `delivery_posture` is `SCHEDULED` or `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`; once the material is in fabrication or beyond, the order can no longer be unwound and the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When it is permitted, the compensation is complete: `escalation_accrued` returns to 0, `escalation_variant` returns to `NONE`, `escalation_index_period` returns to null, `released_for_fab_at` returns to null, and `delivery_posture` resets to `SCHEDULED`. The quote comes back to the market as `OPEN` if the clock is still strictly inside its hold and goes to `EXPIRED` if it is not. The project's `version` increments by 1, and its `escalation_contingency_committed` and `escalation_contingency_remaining` return to exactly the values they carried before the release — the contingency conservation invariant, which must hold to the cent.

In both cancellation cases the package leaves `BOUGHT_OUT` for `SOURCING` when it still has at least one `OPEN` quote after the cancellation, and for `SOURCING_STALLED` when it has none. The same fork applies to the void path: when the clock sweep voids a `PENDING` commitment because its clause lapsed, that commitment's package likewise goes to `SOURCING` if an `OPEN` quote survives and to `SOURCING_STALLED` if none does. A voided commitment retains its `escalation_agreement_id` for audit even though the clause behind it is dead, and its identifier is reported in `voided_commitment_ids`. `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal on the contractual axis. Commitments can be listed with filters on `material_package_id`, `quote_id`, `escalation_agreement_id`, `commitment_status` and `delivery_posture`.

### 3.6. The Delivery Axis and the Twofold Recognition of Escalation

The physical progress of bought-out material is driven by a `BuyoutManager` supplying a `to_posture` value, and every such move requires the commitment to be `COMMITTED` on its contractual axis. A posture move requested on a commitment that is `PENDING`, `CANCELLED`, `VOIDED` or `CLOSED`, or a `to_posture` that is not a declared edge out of the current posture — asking to jump from `SCHEDULED` directly to `STORED_OFFSITE`, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POSTURE_TRANSITION`.

**Releasing for fabrication** moves the posture from `SCHEDULED` to `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` and is the single most consequential step in the workflow, because it is where escalation is first recognized. It stamps `released_for_fab_at` with the current logical clock, runs the provisional escalation arithmetic described below, and increments the project's `version` by 1; the project's `escalation_contingency_committed`, `escalation_contingency_remaining` and `committed_total_cost` all move on the next read. Two preconditions guard it beyond the posture edge itself. If the commitment has a clause attached, a published reading must exist for that clause's `index_code` covering some period at or before the current clock month, failing which the release is refused with `ERR_INDEX_READING_MISSING`. And the provisional escalation just computed must fit inside the project's `escalation_contingency_remaining`; if it would overrun the remaining contingency the release is refused with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_EXCEEDED` and nothing is stamped.

**Starting fabrication** moves the posture from `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` to `FABRICATING` and does nothing else at all — it refreshes `updated_at` and touches no money, no project version, and no other row. **Moving into storage** takes the posture from `FABRICATING` to `STORED_OFFSITE`, and it requires that a storage arrangement for this commitment already be `ACTIVE` with a `documentation_status` of `COMPLETE`, and that the current logical clock have reached that arrangement's `stored_from`. Either failure is `ERR_STORAGE_NOT_DOCUMENTED`. The same code and the same requirement govern the **return from non-compliance**, the move from `STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT` back to `STORED_OFFSITE`, which additionally requires the arrangement to have climbed back to `ACTIVE`; this is the cycle `STORED_OFFSITE → STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT → STORED_OFFSITE` on the delivery axis, and like the fabrication step it refreshes `updated_at` only.

**Accepting delivery** reaches `DELIVERED` from either `FABRICATING` or `STORED_OFFSITE`, and the two entry paths are guarded differently. From `FABRICATING`, delivery is only permitted when `requires_storage_arrangement` is false; an early-buyout order may not skip the storage step on its way to site, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_STORAGE_STEP_REQUIRED`. From `STORED_OFFSITE` there is no such restriction. Both paths require, when a clause is attached, a published reading for that clause's `index_code` whose `period` is **exactly** the current clock month — no look-back is permitted here — and a missing reading for that precise month is `ERR_INDEX_READING_MISSING`, because a delivery cannot be posted into an unpublished month. Both paths then stamp `delivered_at` with the current logical clock, run the final escalation arithmetic, increment the project's `version` by 1, close the contractual axis by moving `commitment_status` from `COMMITTED` to `CLOSED`, and close the scope package by moving it from `BOUGHT_OUT` to `CLOSED`. Delivery from `STORED_OFFSITE` does one thing more: the `ACTIVE` storage arrangement moves to `RELEASED` and its `carrying_cost_frozen_at` is stamped with the current clock, freezing its carrying-cost accrual. `DELIVERED` is terminal on the physical axis.

Escalation itself is a chain of four operations over five stored numbers, and it is computed identically at release and at delivery except for which index reading is used. The starting point is the movement of the index: the reading's `value` for the selected period, less the clause's frozen `baseline_index_value`, divided by that same `baseline_index_value`, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half up to **four** decimal places. From that movement the deadband is deducted: the eligible movement is the greater of zero and the index movement less `deadband_pct`, so movement wholly inside the deadband yields zero and a falling index yields zero rather than a credit to the owner. The eligible movement is then clamped by the ceiling: the capped movement is the lesser of the eligible movement and `cap_pct`. The capped movement is then split: the shared movement is the capped movement multiplied by the owner-borne fraction, which is one minus `contractor_share_pct` divided by one hundred. Finally, `escalation_accrued` is `committed_base_cost` multiplied by the shared movement, divided by one hundred, and rounded half up to **two** decimal places.

The rounding discipline here is not decorative. There is exactly one intermediate rounding — the index movement to four decimal places — and exactly one final rounding, to two. The deadband deduction, the cap clamp and the share split are each carried at full precision; re-rounding any of them to two decimal places produces a different cent in the final figure and is wrong. The accrued figure is never negative and never below zero dollars. A commitment with no clause attached accrues 0.00 and keeps `escalation_variant` at `NONE` throughout its life.

What differs between the two recognitions is index selection, and that difference is the whole point of recognizing twice. At **release**, the reading used is the one for the clause's `index_code` with the **greatest period at or before** the current clock month — a look-back to the newest published period that is not in the future. Since the index-and-period pair is unique, no tie is possible. Release sets `escalation_variant` to `PROVISIONAL` and records the selected reading's period in `escalation_index_period`. At **delivery**, the reading used is the one whose `period` equals the current clock month exactly. Delivery overwrites `escalation_accrued`, overwrites `escalation_index_period`, and sets `escalation_variant` to `FINAL`; the difference between the provisional and final amounts flows into the project's `escalation_contingency_committed` on the next project read. A commitment's `escalation_variant` therefore reads `NONE` before release, `PROVISIONAL` between release and delivery, `FINAL` afterwards, and `NONE` again after a cancellation has unwound it.

### 3.7. Bailed Off-Site Storage: Warehouse Windows, Documentation and Carrying Cost

Material bought out early has to live somewhere, and the arrangement under which a third-party warehouse holds it on the contractor's behalf is a bailment — which means the owner will not pay for stored material unless the paperwork proving title, insurance and lien release is on file and current. An **OffsiteStorageArrangement** is created by a `BuyoutManager` against a `purchase_commitment_id`, and only while that commitment is `COMMITTED` with a `delivery_posture` of `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` or `FABRICATING`; creating one against a commitment in any other position is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It carries the bailee's `warehouse_name` — 1 to 120 non-blank characters, compared case-sensitively, and the key against which warehouse occupancy is checked — a `warehouse_address`, a declared `stored_value` greater than zero with at most 2 decimal places, a `monthly_storage_rate_pct` greater than zero and no greater than 5 with up to four decimal places, and the occupancy window itself as `stored_from` and `planned_release_date`.

Three window rules apply to that occupancy. The declared `stored_value` may not exceed the commitment's `committed_base_cost` — the contractor cannot insure more material than it bought — and a breach is `ERR_STORED_VALUE_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`. The `stored_from` instant may not precede the commitment's `released_for_fab_at`, and `planned_release_date` must be strictly after `stored_from`; either violation is `ERR_STORAGE_WINDOW_INVALID`. And the occupancy interval — which is **closed** at both ends, so both `stored_from` and `planned_release_date` are themselves inside it — must not collide with the interval of any other arrangement in the same `warehouse_name` whose status is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, once both intervals are inflated by a three-day handling buffer at each end to allow for inbound and outbound handling. A collision is `ERR_WAREHOUSE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. When a proposed arrangement both starts before its commitment was released *and* collides with a neighbour, the answer is `ERR_STORAGE_WINDOW_INVALID`.

A new arrangement starts in `DRAFT` with `documentation_status` `INCOMPLETE`, `verified_doc_type_count` 0 and `carrying_cost_frozen_at` null. Its `status` is compound — the server, not the caller, decides it, purely as a function of the arrangement's documents. The moment the mandatory document set becomes complete, the arrangement climbs from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`; the moment it falls out of completeness, an `ACTIVE` arrangement drops to `SUSPENDED`; a replacement document restores it from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`; and when the commitment's material is finally accepted on site, the `ACTIVE` arrangement moves to the terminal `RELEASED` with its carrying cost frozen. Arrangements can be listed with filters on `purchase_commitment_id`, `status`, `documentation_status` and `warehouse_name`.

Evidence is filed as **StorageDocument** rows against a specific arrangement, and either a `BuyoutManager` or the `SupplierRep` behind that material may file one. The arrangement is taken from the addressed resource and recorded as `storage_arrangement_id`. Each document declares a `doc_type` — one of `BAILMENT_AGREEMENT`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE`, `LIEN_WAIVER` or `INVENTORY_TAG_PHOTO` — a `reference_number` of 1 to 60 non-blank characters holding the document or policy number, an `effective_date` from which it is effective inclusive, and an `expires_at` at which it ceases to be effective. That validity window is **half-open**: the document is effective while the clock is at or after `effective_date` and strictly before `expires_at`. An `expires_at` that is not strictly after `effective_date` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two optional inputs complete the filing: a `coverage_amount` of at least zero with at most 2 decimal places, defaulting to 0, which records insured value; and a `verified` boolean, false by default, asserting that the procurement group has checked the filing against the original. A document filed as an `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` must carry a `coverage_amount` of at least the arrangement's `stored_value`; a lower figure, or an omitted one, is rejected outright with `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Documents are never hard-deleted, so a filed document also carries `revoked` (false initially), `revoked_at` (null) and `revocation_reason` (null). Documents for an arrangement can be listed with filters on `doc_type` and `revoked`, and that list is the only way to discover a document's identifier.

Completeness is a distinct-type count over qualifying documents. A document qualifies when it is not revoked, its `verified` flag is true, the current logical clock is at or after its `effective_date` and strictly before its `expires_at`, and — for an insurance certificate only — its `coverage_amount` is at least the arrangement's `stored_value`. `verified_doc_type_count` is then the number of *distinct* `doc_type` values, drawn from the three mandatory types `BAILMENT_AGREEMENT`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` and `LIEN_WAIVER`, for which at least one qualifying document exists. Two qualifying lien waivers still count as one type, and a qualifying `INVENTORY_TAG_PHOTO` is never counted at all, because an inventory photograph is useful evidence but not a mandatory instrument. `documentation_status` is `COMPLETE` when that count is exactly 3, and `INCOMPLETE` otherwise. Because the qualification test reads the clock, advancing the clock past a mandatory document's `expires_at` flips the arrangement to `INCOMPLETE` on the very next read without anybody writing to the documents at all.

Revocation is the head of the documentation cascade, and it belongs to the `BuyoutManager`. Revoking a document records the supplied `revocation_reason`, sets `revoked` to true and stamps `revoked_at` with the current clock. If losing that document leaves the mandatory set incomplete, the arrangement drops from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, its `documentation_status` becomes `INCOMPLETE` and its `verified_doc_type_count` decrements accordingly; and if the commitment holding that material is sitting at `STORED_OFFSITE`, its `delivery_posture` moves to `STORAGE_NONCOMPLIANT` while its `commitment_status` is deliberately left at `COMMITTED` — the paperwork lapsing does not unwind the purchase order. Filing a replacement that restores the third mandatory type reverses the first two hops: the arrangement returns to `ACTIVE` with `documentation_status` `COMPLETE` and `verified_doc_type_count` back at 3, after which the `BuyoutManager` may move the posture back to `STORED_OFFSITE`.

Carrying cost accrues by whole elapsed months and is reported in `carrying_cost_accrued`. The evaluation instant is `carrying_cost_frozen_at` when that has been stamped, and otherwise the current logical clock. The month count is the number of whole months from `stored_from` up to that instant: counting from one upward, each month step is included while `stored_from` advanced by that many months still falls at or before the evaluation instant, with the day-of-month clamped to the target month's length when necessary — advancing the 31st of January by one month lands on the 28th of February in a non-leap year. A partial month contributes nothing. The charge itself has two components per month: the storage fee, being `stored_value` multiplied by `monthly_storage_rate_pct` and divided by one hundred; and the insurance fee, being `stored_value` multiplied by one hundred and seventy-five ten-thousandths of a percent — that is, by 0.0175 as a percentage — and divided by one hundred. Each of those two components is rounded half up to 2 decimal places **before** they are added, the two rounded figures are summed into a per-month charge, and the per-month charge is multiplied by the whole-month count and rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. For a stored value of 862500.00 at a monthly rate of 0.1875 percent over three whole months, the storage component rounds from 1617.1875 to 1617.19 and the insurance component rounds from 150.9375 to 150.94, giving 1768.13 per month and 5304.39 in total; summing the unrounded monthly values and rounding once at the end would give 5304.38, and that is the wrong answer.

Two further facts about accrual. Suspended periods are **not** deducted — the bailee charges for possession whether or not the contractor's paperwork is in order — and an arrangement still in `DRAFT`, or one whose `stored_from` the clock has not yet reached, reports 0.00.

### 3.8. Engineered Substitutions and Contingency Sourcing

A **SubstitutionRequest** is how the procurement group proposes a different product than the one specified, and it doubles as the vehicle for sourcing around a tariff or a supply disruption. A `BuyoutManager` raises one against a `material_package_id`, naming the `proposed_supplier_id` of the substitute's supplier, a `driver` explaining why, a `proposed_product` description of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a signed `cost_delta` and a signed `schedule_delta_days`. The `driver` is one of `VALUE_ENGINEERING`, `DISRUPTION_CONTINGENCY` or `AVAILABILITY`. The `cost_delta` is the dollar impact on the package estimate, carried to at most 2 decimal places, with a negative figure representing a credit and an absolute value no greater than five million. The `schedule_delta_days` is the calendar-day impact on the package's `fabrication_duration_days`, between negative 180 and positive 180 inclusive, with a negative figure representing recovery. The caller's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`, and the request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0, `engineer_conditions` null, and both `applied_cost_delta` and `applied_schedule_delta_days` at 0.

Two coherence rules bind the request to the world it claims to describe, and both are reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. First, a substitution may not propose the supplier the package is already committed to: the `proposed_supplier_id` must not equal the supplier behind any commitment on that package whose `commitment_status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED`. Second, the `disruption_event_id` is conditional on the driver: when the `driver` is `DISRUPTION_CONTINGENCY` it is **required**, and the event it names must belong to the supplier the package is currently quoted or committed against — that is what makes the substitution genuinely a contingency response rather than an unrelated swap. With any other driver, `disruption_event_id` must be omitted. Requests can be listed with filters on `material_package_id`, `proposed_supplier_id`, `disruption_event_id` and `status`.

Disposition belongs exclusively to the `DesignEngineer`, who supplies a `disposition` of `APPROVED`, `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`, `REJECTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, optionally with `engineer_conditions` text. All four branches require the request to be currently `SUBMITTED`; a disposition on an already-dispositioned request is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Beyond that the four branches diverge sharply, and the divergence is the substance of the rule.

An **`APPROVED`** disposition requires the scope package not to be `CLOSED` and requires the proposed supplier's forward-looking exposure band — the one that counts announced future disruptions, per §3.2 — not to be `HIGH`; a `HIGH` forward band is `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_SUPPLIER_EXPOSED` even when that same supplier's snapshot band is lower. On approval both deltas are applied: `applied_cost_delta` is set to `cost_delta` and `applied_schedule_delta_days` to `schedule_delta_days`, the package's `estimated_cost` is increased by the applied cost delta and its `fabrication_duration_days` is increased by the applied schedule delta, and the package's `lead_time_days`, `release_for_fabrication_date`, `float_days` and `rff_risk_band` are all re-derived from the mutated durations — a substitution that adds fabrication time therefore pulls the release date earlier and can push a package from `ON_TRACK` into `AT_RISK` or `CRITICAL` in the same breath. The project's `estimated_total_cost` and `buyout_variance` move on the next project read.

An **`APPROVED_AS_NOTED`** disposition carries the same package-not-`CLOSED` and forward-exposure preconditions, and adds one of its own: `engineer_conditions` must be supplied and non-blank, otherwise `ERR_ENGINEER_CONDITIONS_REQUIRED`. Its side effects deliberately diverge from a clean approval. `applied_cost_delta` is set to `cost_delta` exactly as before, but `applied_schedule_delta_days` is set to **0** — the engineer's conditions forbid any schedule impact — so the package's `estimated_cost` is increased by the applied cost delta while its `fabrication_duration_days` is left completely untouched, and the release-for-fabrication schedule consequently shifts only insofar as nothing about it changed. This is the single most commonly mis-implemented branch in the workflow: the money moves, the schedule does not.

A **`REJECTED`** disposition has no precondition beyond the request being `SUBMITTED` — no package-status check, no exposure check. Both `applied_cost_delta` and `applied_schedule_delta_days` stay at 0, and neither the scope package nor any disruption event is touched in any way. `REJECTED` is terminal.

A **`REVISION_REQUESTED`** disposition sends the request back for rework and is permitted only while `revision_round` is still below 2; a third revision request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It records `engineer_conditions` when supplied and mutates nothing on the package. A `BuyoutManager` then resubmits, supplying a fresh `proposed_product`, `cost_delta` and `schedule_delta_days`, which overwrite the stored values along with `submitted_by_user_id`; `revision_round` increments by exactly 1 and the request returns to `SUBMITTED`. That is the cycle `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and it can run at most twice before the engineer must approve or reject outright.

Approval of a contingency-driven substitution also closes the loop on the disruption that prompted it. When the `driver` is `DISRUPTION_CONTINGENCY` and the disposition is either `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`, the referenced disruption event moves from `OPEN` to `MITIGATED` and its `mitigated_at` is stamped with the current logical clock. Because only `OPEN` events feed either exposure reading, that single transition lowers the offending supplier's `open_disruption_count` and both exposure figures on the next read of that supplier — a supplier can therefore become committable again purely because the contingency plan around it was blessed.

An approval can be withdrawn. A `BuyoutManager` rescinds an approved substitution by supplying a `rescission_reason`, which is permitted from `APPROVED` or from `APPROVED_AS_NOTED`, and in both cases only while the scope package is not yet `CLOSED`. Rescinding an `APPROVED` request reverses both applications exactly: the package's `estimated_cost` is reduced by `applied_cost_delta` and its `fabrication_duration_days` is reduced by `applied_schedule_delta_days`, the release-for-fabrication schedule is re-derived, and both applied deltas are reset to 0. The conservation requirement is strict — after the rescission the package's cost and duration must equal exactly what they were before the approval, to the cent and to the day. Rescinding an `APPROVED_AS_NOTED` request reduces `estimated_cost` by `applied_cost_delta` and resets `applied_cost_delta` to 0; there is no duration to restore, because `applied_schedule_delta_days` was already 0. `RESCINDED` is terminal, and a rescission does not reopen the disruption event it mitigated.

### 3.9. Substantiating Documented Escalation to the Owner

The last act of the buyout is asking the owner to pay for escalation the contractor has actually incurred and documented. A **ChangeOrderRequest** is prepared by a `BuyoutManager` against a `purchase_commitment_id`, with a `claimed_amount` greater than zero carried to at most 2 decimal places and a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters substantiating the claim. The caller's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `prepared_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `owner_note` start null, and the request opens in `PREPARED`. Change orders can be listed with filters on `purchase_commitment_id` and `status`, and they are visible to the procurement group, the signing officer and the owner's representative — the supplier's representative and the engineer of record have no access to them at all.

Preparation is only possible against a commitment whose escalation is **final**. The claim's `gross_documented_amount` is the commitment's `escalation_accrued`, and only when that commitment's `escalation_variant` is `FINAL`; a commitment still at `PROVISIONAL`, or one at `NONE`, yields a gross of 0.00 and the preparation is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`. The same code covers a commitment whose final `escalation_accrued` is 0.00 and a `claimed_amount` that is not strictly greater than zero. Once captured, `gross_documented_amount` is **frozen** at the value the commitment carried at the instant the change order was created — a later cancellation or recomputation upstream does not rewrite a claim already prepared. Only that one commitment's escalation enters the claim; sibling commitments on the same package or project are excluded entirely.

The substantiation arithmetic then runs in five pinned steps, in exactly this sequence. First, the gross documented amount is captured as above. Second, the administrative fee tier is selected **from that gross amount**: 5.0 percent when the gross is below 10000.00, 3.5 percent when it is at least 10000.00 but below 50000.00, and 2.0 percent when it is 50000.00 or more; each tier boundary is an inclusive lower bound on the tier above it, and the selected percentage is persisted as `admin_fee_pct`. Third, `admin_fee_amount` is the gross amount multiplied by that percentage, divided by one hundred, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Fourth, a running total is formed as the gross amount plus the fee amount. Fifth, `substantiated_amount` is the lesser of that running total and the `claimed_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — the owner is never asked for more than the contractor claimed, and a `claimed_amount` below the running total silently caps the claim rather than raising an error.

The order of those steps is load-bearing. The fee tier is keyed on the step-one gross amount and **never** on the running total produced by step four; keying it on the total would be circular, since a total of 49000.00 plus its fee lands in a different tier than the amount it was derived from, and that recursion has no fixed point. Three examples fix the behaviour. A gross of 55200.00 with a claim of 60000.00 selects the 2.0 percent tier, yields a fee of 1104.00, and substantiates 56304.00. A gross of 49000.00 with a claim of 52000.00 selects the 3.5 percent tier, yields a fee of 1715.00, and substantiates 50715.00. The same gross of 49000.00 with a claim of only 45000.00 substantiates 45000.00, because the claim cap binds.

Submission is where segregation of duties bites. A `BuyoutManager` submits the change order to the owner, moving it from `PREPARED` to `SUBMITTED` and recording that caller's `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id` — but the submitting operator's `X-User-ID` **must differ** from the recorded `prepared_by_user_id`, and a self-submission is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Submission re-checks that the commitment's `escalation_variant` is still `FINAL` with a gross documented amount above zero, refusing with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` otherwise, and a submit attempted on an already-`SUBMITTED` change order is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Disposition belongs to the `OwnerRep`, who supplies a `disposition` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and may attach an `owner_note`. It is permitted only while the change order is `SUBMITTED`: a disposition attempted against one still `PREPARED`, or against one already `OWNER_APPROVED` or `OWNER_REJECTED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Either way the note is recorded and the change order reaches a terminal status — `SUBMITTED` becomes `OWNER_APPROVED` on an approval and `OWNER_REJECTED` on a rejection. An approval feeds the project's `change_order_recovered_amount` on the next project read; a rejection leaves that figure exactly where it was. One consequence deserves emphasis, because it looks like a bug and is not: an approved change order does **not** reduce the project's `escalation_contingency_committed`. Owner recovery and contractor contingency are separate ledgers — the contingency records what the contractor has committed to absorb, and the recovery records what the owner has agreed to reimburse, and a project can legitimately show both figures at once.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint lives under the `/procurement` prefix.
