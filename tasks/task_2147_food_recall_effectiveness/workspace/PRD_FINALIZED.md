# Food Recall Effectiveness API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a pathogen, an undeclared allergen or a foreign-material finding forces food off the market, the regulator's problem is rarely the decision to recall — it is proving, weeks later, that the recall actually worked. Product moves through distributors to retailers to restaurants; the firm's shipping records disagree with what the consignees say they received; some consignees never answer the phone; some product is destroyed on site under a certificate nobody witnessed; and the insurance file grows a line item every day. This service is the system of record for that proof. It carries a recall case from the first health hazard evaluation through classification, distribution list reconstruction, consignee notification, product retrieval accounting, disposition verification, periodic status reporting, cost accumulation and insurance settlement, and finally to root cause and termination.

The system is deliberately adversarial about who may assert what. A firm's recall officer reconstructs the distribution list, dispatches notifications and executes disposition; an agency case officer classifies the hazard, authorizes disposition and decides termination; an independent panel — and only that panel — ratifies a hazard evaluation; a field investigator — and only a field investigator — verifies the firm's physical claims; a carrier adjuster — and only the adjuster — pays or denies a claim. No actor may both create an artifact and attest to it, and the coordinator who classified a recall may not be the coordinator who terminates it.

Two measurement philosophies coexist in the same case file. FDA scores a recall on **contact coverage** — did enough of the sampled consignees actually respond? — while USDA scores it on **quantity accounted for** — how much product physically came back or was verifiably destroyed? Both figures are computed continuously and exposed side by side, but only one of them is the official number for any given recall, selected by the owning agency. Because effectiveness gates termination, and because reporting cadence tightens when a firm misses its filing windows, the two numbers and the compliance posture derived from them are the spine of the whole workflow.

Every collection in this API is mounted at the root path. Time is not read from the wall clock: the server keeps a logical clock that tests and operators drive explicitly, and every recorded instant in the system is stamped from it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Acting Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal acts. Exactly five role strings are recognised, and they are spelled `FirmRecallOfficer`, `RecallCoordinator`, `HealthHazardPanel`, `FieldInvestigator` and `InsuranceAdjuster`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string outside those five, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` and HTTP 401. If both headers are well formed but the acting role does not appear in the authorized-role list published for the endpoint in §4, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` with HTTP 403.

The two rejections are distinct and are not interchangeable: an unrecognised role string is an authentication failure, not an authorization failure.

Identity binding differs by role. When `X-User-Role` is `FirmRecallOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Firm`; ownership checks on any resource that references a firm compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a recall officer may only touch recalls whose `firm_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, and that scoping applies to reads exactly as it applies to writes; a violation is rejected with `ERR_FIRM_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `RecallCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow step demands attribution — as `classified_by_user_id` on the recall, as `drafted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a public warning, as `authorized_by_user_id` on a disposition order, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a cost entry — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `HealthHazardPanel`, whose `X-User-ID` is opaque and is recorded as `ratified_by_user_id`; for `FieldInvestigator`, whose opaque `X-User-ID` is recorded as `verified_by_user_id` on a retrieval receipt and as `witness_user_id` on a disposition order; and for `InsuranceAdjuster`, whose opaque `X-User-ID` is recorded as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an insurance claim. In none of those four cases is the header value looked up as an entity primary key.

The division of labour behind those five names is the reason the workflow has as many hand-offs as it does. A `FirmRecallOfficer` owns everything the firm must physically do: declaring shipment lines, dispatching notifications and recording their outcomes, reporting retrieval accounting, proposing and executing disposition, filing status reports and cost lines, opening and submitting insurance claims, and requesting termination. A `RecallCoordinator` is the agency case officer: registering firms, classifying and re-classifying the recall, running the recall checkpoint, drafting, reviewing and publishing public warnings, authorizing disposition, adjudicating cost entries and status reports, and deciding termination. A `HealthHazardPanel` has exactly one write power — ratifying or returning a submitted hazard evaluation — and may never classify a recall itself, which is precisely what forces evaluation and classification apart. A `FieldInvestigator` verifies the firm's physical assertions (retrieval receipts and witnessed disposition execution) but may never create the artifacts it verifies. An `InsuranceAdjuster` adjudicates submitted claims only, and may neither file a claim, file a cost line, nor adjudicate one. Several operations are shared: opening a recall case and filing or submitting a hazard evaluation are open to both `FirmRecallOfficer` and `RecallCoordinator`, as are adding a consignee and reconciling a distribution line. Every read endpoint is open to all five roles, subject to the firm scoping described above, and every role may drive the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so pagination through a growing collection is stable for already-created rows. List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and populate four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is taken, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Filters on list endpoints are exact-match and combine conjunctively. Recall listings accept `firm_id`, `status` and `hazard_class`; consignee listings accept `tier`; notification listings accept `delivery_state` and `response_state`; status-report listings accept `status` and `report_kind`; cost-entry listings accept `category` and `status`; hazard-evaluation, distribution-record, retrieval-receipt, public-warning, disposition-order and insurance-claim listings accept `status`.

### 2.4. Case Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system carries four common properties. Its `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by a client. Its `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Its `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation came from a direct client write or from a cascade triggered elsewhere in the case file. All twelve resource types — `Firm`, `Recall`, `HazardEvaluation`, `Consignee`, `DistributionRecord`, `ConsigneeNotification`, `RetrievalReceipt`, `PublicWarning`, `DispositionOrder`, `StatusReport`, `CostEntry` and `InsuranceClaim` — carry all four properties; none of them is an append-only log exempt from `updated_at` or `version`.

Time in this system is logical, not observed. The server keeps a single logical clock whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any client sets it, the server must read that default and must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A clock-setting capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and jumps the clock to it, returning the new `current_utc`; no entity is mutated by the jump and no aging, cadence or overdue rule fires until the next checkpoint or write touches the case. Every instant the server itself stamps is read from this clock: `sent_at`, `delivery_recorded_at` and `response_recorded_at` on notifications, `submitted_at` on status reports, `executed_at` on disposition orders, `published_at` on public warnings, `terminated_at` on the recall, and the `period_start`, `period_end` and `due_at` of any report the server mints on its own. Client-supplied instants — a shipment's `ship_date`, an explicitly filed report's `period_start` and `period_end` — are taken as given and must parse as ISO 8601: each such string is persisted and echoed back exactly as the client wrote it, and is not re-rendered into the three-fractional-digit form, so an explicitly filed report's `due_at`, which is taken from its `period_end`, carries that same client string verbatim. Only when the server itself later re-dates an existing report — the re-dating hop of Cascade A, or the two-day extension granted on a rejected report — is the replacement instant rendered in the three-fractional-digit form.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write paths are guarded against acting on a stale view of their parent, because in both cases the new child legitimately displaces something the client may never have seen.

A request to `POST /recalls/{id}/hazard-evaluations` must carry `expected_recall_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Recall`. If that value does not equal the recall's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECALL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The rationale is that a freshly filed evaluation, once ratified, supersedes the recall's existing ratified evaluation and can therefore change the recall's classification basis; a client working from an out-of-date read of the recall would displace an evaluation posture it never observed. A successful filing itself increments the recall's `version` by one, so a client filing two evaluations in sequence must re-read the recall between them.

A request to `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications` must carry `expected_consignee_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Consignee` named in the body. If that value does not equal that consignee's current `version`, the dispatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONSIGNEE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A new dispatch moves the consignee's `last_contact_at` and restarts its cooling-off clock, so a client acting on a stale consignee view would silently overwrite contact history it never saw. A successful dispatch increments the consignee's `version` by one.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints that are not named in one of these two rules must not require, and must not honour, any `expected_..._version` input.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated strictly in the ascending order listed below; the first violated rung wins and short-circuits every later rung. This ordering is normative — an implementation that reports a later rung's error while an earlier rung is also violated is wrong even though both conditions genuinely hold.

1. Both auth headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The `Firm` addressed by a path segment or by the `firm_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `GET /firms/{id}` and `POST /recalls`.
4. The `Recall` addressed by the leading `/recalls/{id}` path segment, or reached through a sub-resource's `recall_id`, exists — otherwise `ERR_RECALL_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint under `/recalls/{id}`.
5. The `Consignee` addressed by a `/consignees/{id}` path segment or named by a `consignee_id` body field exists **and** belongs to the recall in the path — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNEE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the `/consignees/{id}` reads and their sub-listings, to `POST /recalls/{id}/distribution-records`, `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications`, `POST /recalls/{id}/retrieval-receipts`, and to the `parent_consignee_id` supplied on `POST /recalls/{id}/consignees`. A consignee that exists but hangs off a different recall is a 404, not a 403.
6. The flat-addressed sub-resource exists — otherwise `ERR_SUBRESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every action endpoint addressed as `/hazard-evaluations/{id}`, `/distribution-records/{id}`, `/notifications/{id}`, `/retrieval-receipts/{id}`, `/public-warnings/{id}`, `/disposition-orders/{id}`, `/status-reports/{id}`, `/cost-entries/{id}` or `/insurance-claims/{id}`.
7. Firm tenant scoping: when the acting role is `FirmRecallOfficer`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `firm_id` of the recall the request resolves to — otherwise `ERR_FIRM_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint reachable by that role that resolves to a recall, reads included.
8. The request body conforms to the endpoint's input schema: required fields present, enum values in range, integers non-negative where §3 says non-negative, quantity fields strictly positive where §3 says positive, `lot_codes` carrying at least one element, ISO 8601 instants parseable, and `period_start` strictly earlier than `period_end` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
9. `expected_recall_version` equals the current recall `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_RECALL_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/hazard-evaluations`.
10. `expected_consignee_version` equals the current `version` of the consignee named in the body — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONSIGNEE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications`.
11. The recall's `status` permits the operation, including the total lockdown of a `TERMINATED` recall and the block on terminating while the compliance posture is `DELINQUENT` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE` (409). Applies to every write endpoint that resolves to a recall. Creation endpoints have no state transition of their own and are gated exactly as follows: hazard evaluations may be filed while the recall's `status` is anything other than `TERMINATED`; consignees may be added while it is `DRAFT`, `CLASSIFIED` or `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`; distribution records may be declared while it is `DRAFT`, `CLASSIFIED`, `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS` or `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING`; notifications may be dispatched while it is `CLASSIFIED` or `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`; retrieval receipts may be reported while it is `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` or `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`; disposition orders may be proposed while it is `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` or `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`; public warnings, status reports, cost entries and insurance claims may be created while it is anything other than `TERMINATED`; and a checkpoint may be run while it is anything other than `DRAFT` or `TERMINATED`.
12. The hazard evaluation's `status` permits submit or decide — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EVALUATION_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /hazard-evaluations/{id}/submit` and `POST /hazard-evaluations/{id}/decide`.
13. The public warning's `status` permits submit, review or publish — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WARNING_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /public-warnings/{id}/submit`, `/review` and `/publish`.
14. The disposition order's `status` permits authorize, execute or verify — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /disposition-orders/{id}/authorize`, `/execute` and `/verify`.
15. The notification's `delivery_state` permits the delivery call, and its `response_state` together with its `delivery_state` permit the response call — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /notifications/{id}/delivery` and `POST /notifications/{id}/response`.
16. The distribution record's `status` permits the requested reconcile outcome, and the retrieval receipt's `status` permits the requested verify decision — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNTING_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /distribution-records/{id}/reconcile` and `POST /retrieval-receipts/{id}/verify`.
17. The status report's `status` permits submit or adjudicate — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /status-reports/{id}/submit` and `POST /status-reports/{id}/adjudicate`.
18. The insurance claim's `status` permits submit or adjudicate, and at submit time the recall's accepted insurable loaded cost is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /insurance-claims/{id}/submit` and `POST /insurance-claims/{id}/adjudicate`.
19. The cost entry's `status` permits the requested adjudication decision — accept is allowed from `PENDING` or `REJECTED`, reject is allowed from `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COST_ENTRY_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /cost-entries/{id}/adjudicate`.
20. Classification requires at least one ratified hazard evaluation on the recall, and re-classification additionally requires one ratified strictly later than the evaluation that backed the previous classification — otherwise `ERR_NO_RATIFIED_EVALUATION` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/classify`.
21. A press release or public warning may only be published when the recall's hazard class is one of the two most serious; a social-media-only notice is exempt — otherwise `ERR_PUBLIC_WARNING_NOT_WARRANTED` (422). Applies to `POST /public-warnings/{id}/publish`.
22. Segregation of duties: the reviewer of a public warning must differ from its `drafted_by_user_id`, and the coordinator deciding termination must differ from the recall's `classified_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /public-warnings/{id}/review` and `POST /recalls/{id}/decide-termination`.
23. The consignee named on a retrieval receipt must have at least one distribution record on this recall in a reconciled state — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNEE_NOT_ON_DISTRIBUTION_LIST` (422). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/retrieval-receipts`.
24. No notification may be dispatched while any distribution record of the recall is still unreconciled — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_LIST_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications`.
25. The cooling-off buffer since the same consignee's most recent notification must have fully elapsed — otherwise `ERR_NOTIFICATION_COOLING_OFF` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications`.
26. A filed reporting window must not overlap any existing status-report window of the same recall — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/status-reports`.
27. The same consignee may not hold two notifications whose delivery dimension is still in flight — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_NOTIFICATION` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/notifications`.
28. Termination may not be requested while any disposition order of the recall is still open — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPOSITION_ORDERS` (409). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/request-termination`.
29. A consignee's computed `tier` must not exceed the three-level cap — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNEE_TIER_LIMIT` (422). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/consignees`.
30. A retrieval receipt must not push the consignee's cumulative reported units past its reconciled shipped total — otherwise `ERR_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED` (422). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/retrieval-receipts`.
31. A disposition order's `quantity` must not exceed the units currently awaiting disposition — otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_EXCEEDS_ACCOUNTED_UNITS` (422). Applies to `POST /disposition-orders/{id}/authorize`.
32. The recall's official effectiveness must have reached the termination floor — otherwise `ERR_EFFECTIVENESS_BELOW_TERMINATION_THRESHOLD` (422). Applies to `POST /recalls/{id}/request-termination`.
33. At claim submission the firm's remaining policy aggregate must be strictly greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_POLICY_AGGREGATE` (422). Applies to `POST /insurance-claims/{id}/submit`.

Note the ordering consequences that matter most in practice. Because rung 5 precedes rung 8, naming a consignee that belongs to a different recall yields a 404 even when the rest of the body is also malformed. Because rungs 9 and 10 precede rung 11, a stale parent version is reported before any recall-state objection. Because rung 11 precedes rungs 20 through 33, a recall in the wrong lifecycle state masks every downstream business objection — a termination request against a recall that is not yet in effectiveness review is a recall-state error, not an effectiveness-threshold error. And because rung 22 precedes rung 28, a coordinator who classified the recall and then tries to decide its termination is rejected for segregation of duties even if disposition orders are also still open.

### 2.7. Arithmetic Discipline: Cents, Percentages and Rounding

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency amounts anywhere in the API surface, and every multiplication that produces money is closed by rounding half up to whole cents before the value is stored or summed further.

All effectiveness figures are percentages carried to exactly two decimal places, rounded half up. Every threshold comparison stated in this document uses the inclusivity written into the sentence, and those inclusivities are load-bearing: where a rule says "at least", the boundary value satisfies it, and where a rule says "strictly below" or "strictly greater", the boundary value does not.

Two rounding orders are pinned and must not be rearranged. First, overhead loading on a cost line is rounded to whole cents on that single line, before any line participates in a sum; summing unrounded products and rounding the total afterwards produces a different — and wrong — figure. Second, in the insurance waterfall the deductible is subtracted before the coinsurance rate is applied, the aggregate clamp is applied after coinsurance and before the handling fee, and the coinsurance tier is selected from the sublimit-capped subtotal rather than from the final claim value.

Where a rule calls for rounding a quotient up to a whole number, that is a mathematical ceiling applied to the exact quotient, not a rounding of a decimal approximation.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived Case Counters

A `Recall` is a hub whose derived fields are recomputed, not incrementally patched, whenever the facts underneath them change. Whenever a cascade touches a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one, exactly as for a direct write.

Cascades in this system are single-pass and finite. A recomputation that would, in principle, feed back into one of its own inputs is deliberately not iterated to a fixed point: each cascade uses the values that were in effect when it started, and the resulting figure stands until the next event re-triggers the computation. Two cascades are long enough to be named in this document and are referred to by name in the workflows below. **Cascade A** is the re-classification chain, in which a changed reporting cadence re-dates every pending report, which pushes some of those reports overdue, which raises the recall's overdue counter, which in turn recomputes the compliance posture. **Cascade B** is the retrieval chain, in which verifying a receipt raises the accounted-unit total, which recomputes effectiveness, which may carry the recall into effectiveness review, which closes out unresponsive consignees and mints a final effectiveness report.

Two round trips are exactly compensating and must be implemented so that they are. Reconciling a distribution line as confirmed, then disputing it, then confirming it again restores the recall's shipped-unit total to exactly its pre-cycle value. Accepting a cost line and then rejecting it restores both the recall's total loaded cost and its insurable loaded cost to exactly their pre-cycle values. A third, narrower compensation applies to custody: executing a disposition order and then having that execution rejected restores the units awaiting disposition exactly.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Sub-Resource Addressing

Paths are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment is validated before the next is considered. A request addressed to a sub-resource under `/recalls/{id}` is first checked for the existence of that recall; only then is the sub-resource considered. Where a body field names a consignee, that consignee must both exist and belong to the recall named in the path — cross-recall references are not silently tolerated and are not treated as authorization failures.

Most action endpoints are addressed flatly — a hazard evaluation, a notification, a disposition order or a claim is acted on through its own top-level collection rather than through the recall that owns it. The owning recall is nonetheless reached through the sub-resource's `recall_id`, and every recall-state and firm-scoping rule described above applies as if the recall had been named in the path.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Case: Firms, Recalls and the Distribution Tree

Before any recall exists there must be a firm. A `RecallCoordinator` registers one by supplying its legal `name` and its food-facility `registration_number`, which must be a non-empty string and must be unique across all firms. Two insurance policy terms may be supplied at registration and both have defaults if they are not: `policy_deductible_cents`, which defaults to 2,500,000 cents — twenty-five thousand dollars — and must not be negative, and `policy_aggregate_cents`, which defaults to 100,000,000 cents — one million dollars — and must be strictly positive. The firm's two running counters are server-owned and always start at zero: `policy_paid_to_date_cents`, which accumulates the amounts actually paid across every claim of this firm that reached partial or full payment, and `open_recall_count`, which tracks how many of the firm's recalls have not yet terminated.

Opening a recall case requires the `firm_id` of the recalling firm, a `product_description`, the owning `agency`, the `initiation_type`, and `lot_codes` — an array of affected lot code strings that must contain at least one element. The `agency` is either `FDA` or `USDA`, and this single choice decides for the whole life of the case which effectiveness basis is the official one. The `initiation_type` is one of `FIRM_INITIATED`, `AGENCY_REQUESTED` or `MANDATED`. A newly opened case has `status` `DRAFT` and no `hazard_class` at all — the field is null until the recall is classified — and its whole derived apparatus (`effectiveness_check_level`, `sample_tier_scope`, `eligible_consignee_count`, `required_sample_size`, `responded_count`, `report_cadence_days`, `overdue_report_count`, `total_shipped_units`, `accounted_units`, `units_awaiting_disposition`, `units_pending_verification`, `units_disposed_verified`, `contact_effectiveness_pct`, `quantity_effectiveness_pct`, `official_effectiveness_pct`, `total_loaded_cost_cents`, `insurable_loaded_cost_cents` and `termination_denied_count`) starts at zero, with `compliance_flag` starting at `COMPLIANT`. Opening the case also increments the firm's `open_recall_count` by one, which is a mutation of the firm row and therefore advances the firm's `version`. Both `root_cause_code` and `capa_summary` remain null until termination is requested, `classified_by_user_id` remains null until classification, and `terminated_at` remains null until the case closes.

Reconstructing where the product went is modelled as a tree of consignees hanging off the recall. Adding one requires the consignee's `name` and its `consignee_type`, which is `DISTRIBUTOR`, `RETAILER`, `RESTAURANT` or `INSTITUTION`. Omitting `parent_consignee_id` declares a direct consignee — one the firm shipped to itself — and gives it a `tier` of 1. Supplying `parent_consignee_id` declares that an upstream consignee re-shipped to this one, and the new consignee's `tier` is one greater than its parent's. The tree is capped at three levels: a computed `tier` that would exceed 3 is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNEE_TIER_LIMIT`, so a consignee two hops below a direct consignee is the deepest the distribution list can go. Each consignee starts with `escalation_level` at 0, `last_contact_at` null, `response_state` `AWAITING`, `closed_without_response` false and `in_effectiveness_sample` false until the sampling scope is known. Every addition re-runs the sampling arithmetic described in §3.3, so `eligible_consignee_count`, `required_sample_size` and each consignee's `in_effectiveness_sample` stay current as the tree grows. Consignees may be added while the recall is in `DRAFT`, `CLASSIFIED` or `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`, and no later.

### 3.2. Health Hazard Evaluation and Panel Ratification

An evaluation is the formal argument that a hazard exists and how bad it is. Filing one is open to both a `FirmRecallOfficer` and a `RecallCoordinator`, and the filer supplies five substantive inputs plus the concurrency guard. `probability_of_adverse_health` is `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW` or `REMOTE`. `severity_tier` is an integer clinical severity band from 1 through 4 inclusive. `exposure_population` is the estimated number of consumers exposed and must not be negative. `vulnerable_population` is a boolean, defaulting to false, that is set true when infants, elderly, pregnant or immunocompromised consumers are in the exposed set. `evidence_strength` is `CONFIRMED`, `PROBABLE` or `SUSPECTED`. The sixth input, `expected_recall_version`, is the optimistic-concurrency guard described in §2.5.

Scoring is a weighted calculation over exactly those five inputs of the single evaluation being scored — no other evaluation, and no recall or consignee data, participates — and its evaluation order is fixed. First, a base figure is taken as the severity points for the evaluation's `severity_tier` multiplied by the probability multiplier for its `probability_of_adverse_health`. Severity points are 12 for tier 1, 24 for tier 2, 40 for tier 3 and 58 for tier 4. The probability multipliers are 1.30 for `HIGH`, 1.00 for `MEDIUM`, 0.70 for `LOW` and 0.40 for `REMOTE`. Second, an exposure factor is taken as the smaller of 12 and the `exposure_population` divided by 25,000 and rounded up to a whole number — so a hundred thousand exposed consumers contributes 4, and any population beyond three hundred thousand is capped at 12. Third, a vulnerability bonus of 9 is added when `vulnerable_population` is true, and 0 otherwise. Fourth, a scale is selected according to the evaluation's `variant`. Finally, `hazard_score` is the sum of the base figure, the exposure factor and the vulnerability bonus, multiplied by that scale, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `recommended_class` follows directly: `CLASS_I` when `hazard_score` is at least 70.00, otherwise `CLASS_II` when it is at least 35.00, otherwise `CLASS_III`. Both thresholds are inclusive.

The `variant` is what makes the panel's judgement bite. While the evaluation is still the firm's own assertion — at creation, and at every submission and resubmission — the `variant` is `PRELIMINARY`, `evidence_strength` is ignored entirely and the scale is forced to 1.00. That is the worst-case reading, in which every reported link is treated as though it were confirmed. Once the panel ratifies, the `variant` becomes `RATIFIED` and the scale becomes the evidence scale for the recorded `evidence_strength`: 1.00 for `CONFIRMED`, 0.80 for `PROBABLE`, 0.55 for `SUSPECTED`. Discounting a merely suspected link to 0.55 can move `recommended_class` down one class or even two for identical inputs, which is exactly the effect the two-variant design is meant to produce.

A newly filed evaluation is in `status` `DRAFT`. Submitting it advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, recomputes `hazard_score` and `recommended_class` under the `PRELIMINARY` variant and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE` if the recall has already terminated. The `HealthHazardPanel` then decides, supplying a `decision` and optionally a `panel_note` that is recorded on the evaluation. A `decision` of `RATIFY` advances `SUBMITTED → RATIFIED`, flips the `variant` to `RATIFIED`, recomputes `hazard_score` and `recommended_class` with the evidence scaling now applied, stamps `ratified_by_user_id`, and — this is the cross-entity part — moves every *other* evaluation of the same recall that was in `RATIFIED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one ratified evaluation is ever live. A `decision` of `RETURN` advances `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and increments `return_count` by one. An evaluation sitting in `RETURNED` is resubmitted along the `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` edge, with exactly the same side effects as a first submission — the `variant` reverts to `PRELIMINARY`, the score is recomputed without evidence scaling, and `submitted_by_user_id` is restamped. There is no edge back to `DRAFT`: the review cycle runs `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` for as many rounds as the panel wants. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and the only way into it is another evaluation of the same recall being ratified. Deciding an evaluation that is not currently `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVALUATION_STATE`.

### 3.3. Classification, Sampling Scope and the Reporting Cadence

Classification is the coordinator's act, and it is gated on the panel's. A `RecallCoordinator` classifying a recall supplies `hazard_class` in the body, one of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`; the coordinator is not obliged to adopt the evaluation's `recommended_class`, but there must be something to adopt. A first classification requires at least one evaluation of this recall in `RATIFIED`, and without one the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_RATIFIED_EVALUATION`. A first classification advances the recall `DRAFT → CLASSIFIED`, sets `hazard_class` from the body and `classified_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Classification then fixes the effectiveness sampling scope. The `effectiveness_check_level` is `A` when the recall is `CLASS_I`; it is `B` when the recall is `CLASS_II` and at least one consignee of this recall sits at `tier` 2 or deeper; it is `C` when the recall is `CLASS_II` and every consignee of this recall is a direct, tier-1 consignee; and it is `D` when the recall is `CLASS_III`. The level then fixes `sample_tier_scope` — the deepest consignee tier included in the sample — at 3 for level `A`, 2 for level `B`, 2 for level `C` and 1 for level `D`. The `eligible_consignee_count` is the number of consignees of this recall whose `tier` is at or below `sample_tier_scope`, counted regardless of their `response_state` or notification history: consignees are never excluded from the denominator for being unresponsive. The `required_sample_size` is zero when there are no eligible consignees at all; otherwise it is the eligible count multiplied by the level percentage — 100 percent for level `A`, 50 percent for level `B`, 10 percent for level `C` and 2 percent for level `D` — rounded up to a whole number, and then floored at 1 so that a non-empty eligible set always demands at least one response. Finally, every consignee of the recall has `in_effectiveness_sample` refreshed to reflect whether its `tier` is at or below the new `sample_tier_scope`.

Classification also starts the reporting clock. The `report_cadence_days` is the escalated cadence of 3 days whenever the recall's `compliance_flag` is `ESCALATED` or `DELINQUENT`, and otherwise it is the cadence for the hazard class: 7 days for `CLASS_I`, 14 days for `CLASS_II`, 30 days for `CLASS_III`. On a first classification the server mints the recall's first status report — `report_kind` `PERIODIC`, `period_index` 1, `period_start` at the current logical instant, and both `period_end` and `due_at` at that instant plus `report_cadence_days` days — in `status` `PENDING`.

Re-classification is permitted, and it is where the case file gets interesting. A recall already in `CLASSIFIED` may be classified again along a `CLASSIFIED → CLASSIFIED` self-edge, and a recall already in `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS` may be classified again along the equivalent `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS → NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS` self-edge; the two behave identically. Either way the precondition is stricter than for a first classification: there must exist an evaluation in `RATIFIED` whose `created_at` is strictly later than the `created_at` of the evaluation that backed the previous classification, and if there is not, the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_RATIFIED_EVALUATION`. Re-classification runs **Cascade A** in four hops, in order. Hop one sets `hazard_class` from the body, restamps `classified_by_user_id`, and recomputes `report_cadence_days` from the new hazard class. Hop two, because the cadence changed, re-dates every status report of this recall that is still `PENDING`: each one's `due_at` becomes its own `period_start` plus the cadence in effect at the start of this cascade, and its `period_end` is set equal to that `due_at`. Hop three observes that a re-dated `due_at` may now be at or before the current logical instant, and every such report flips `PENDING → OVERDUE`. Hop four, because reports flipped, increments the recall's `overdue_report_count` by one per flipped report, recomputes `compliance_flag` from the new count, and then recomputes `report_cadence_days` once more against that new posture, so a recall whose posture has just risen to `ESCALATED` or `DELINQUENT` is left carrying the escalated 3-day cadence in its stored field. Hop four fires only when hop three actually flipped at least one report; if nothing flipped, neither the posture nor the cadence is recomputed again in this cascade. Re-classification also refreshes `effectiveness_check_level`, `sample_tier_scope`, `eligible_consignee_count`, `required_sample_size` and every consignee's `in_effectiveness_sample`, exactly as a first classification does.

One subtlety in Cascade A is deliberate and must not be "fixed". The escalated 3-day cadence that hop four's new posture implies applies only to reports minted *after* the escalation; hop two uses the cadence pinned at hop one. There is no fixed-point iteration, and the cascade runs exactly once.

### 3.4. Reconstructing and Reconciling the Distribution List

A shipment line is declared by a `FirmRecallOfficer` and names the `consignee_id` it went to, the `lot_code` shipped, the `quantity_shipped` — an integer that must be strictly greater than zero — and a `ship_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. A newly declared line is in `status` `DECLARED` with `effective_quantity` 0, `quantity_confirmed` null and `reconcile_round` 0. A declared line contributes nothing at all to the recall's `total_shipped_units`; only reconciliation gives a line weight.

Reconciliation is driven by an `outcome` supplied by a `FirmRecallOfficer` or a `RecallCoordinator`, and it is a small cyclic machine rather than a one-shot approval. `CONFIRM` moves `DECLARED → CONFIRMED` or `DISPUTED → CONFIRMED`, sets `effective_quantity` to `quantity_shipped`, and — on the return from `DISPUTED` — clears `quantity_confirmed` back to null. `DISPUTE` moves `DECLARED → DISPUTED`, `CONFIRMED → DISPUTED` or `CORRECTED → DISPUTED` and sets `effective_quantity` to 0. `CORRECT` is only available from `DISPUTED`, moves `DISPUTED → CORRECTED`, and requires a `quantity_confirmed` in the body that is present and strictly greater than zero — omit it or send zero and the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the supplied value is stored as `quantity_confirmed` and becomes the line's `effective_quantity`. `VOID` is reserved to a `RecallCoordinator`, is only available from `DISPUTED`, moves `DISPUTED → VOIDED`, and sets `effective_quantity` to 0; `VOIDED` is terminal. Every reconciliation, whatever the outcome, increments `reconcile_round` by one and re-sums the recall's `total_shipped_units` from the `effective_quantity` of all its lines, then recomputes `quantity_effectiveness_pct` and `official_effectiveness_pct`. An outcome the line's current `status` does not permit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNTING_STATE`, and no reconciliation of any kind is permitted once the recall has terminated. Because a dispute zeroes a line and a re-confirm restores it to `quantity_shipped`, the confirm-dispute-confirm round trip returns `total_shipped_units` to exactly the value it had before the cycle.

### 3.5. Consignee Notification: Two Independent Dimensions

Dispatching a notification is a `FirmRecallOfficer` operation and takes three inputs: the `consignee_id` being notified, the `method` — `EMAIL`, `PHONE` or `CERTIFIED_MAIL` — and `expected_consignee_version`. Three gates stand in front of it, and their relative order is the ladder in §2.6. The distribution list must be complete: if any distribution record of this recall is still in `DECLARED`, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_LIST_INCOMPLETE`. The cooling-off buffer must have elapsed: the buffer is 24 hours for `EMAIL`, 12 hours for `PHONE` and 72 hours for `CERTIFIED_MAIL`, and the buffer that applies is the larger of the buffer for the method now being used and the buffer for the method of this consignee's most recent prior notification — the one with the greatest `sent_at`, ties broken by the greatest `created_at`, considered across all of that consignee's notifications including undeliverable ones. If the time elapsed since that prior notification's `sent_at` is strictly less than the applicable buffer, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_COOLING_OFF`; a dispatch exactly at the buffer edge is allowed. And no consignee may hold two notifications whose `delivery_state` is still `SENT`, so a second dispatch before the first one's delivery is recorded is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_NOTIFICATION`.

A dispatched notification records `attempt_no` as one more than the count of pre-existing notifications for the same consignee, stamps `sent_at` from the logical clock, and starts life in `delivery_state` `SENT` and `response_state` `AWAITING`, with `delivery_recorded_at`, `response_recorded_at` and `response_note` all null and `counts_toward_effectiveness` false. The consignee's `last_contact_at` is set to that `sent_at`. If this is the first notification of the recall, the recall itself advances `CLASSIFIED → NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`.

The two state dimensions on a notification evolve independently and neither waits for the other. On the delivery dimension, a `FirmRecallOfficer` records an `outcome` of `DELIVERED`, advancing `SENT → DELIVERED`, or `UNDELIVERABLE`, advancing `SENT → UNDELIVERABLE`; either way `delivery_recorded_at` is stamped from the clock. Recording delivery when `delivery_state` is no longer `SENT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_STATE`. An `UNDELIVERABLE` outcome additionally increments the consignee's `escalation_level` by one, and it freezes the response dimension of that row at `AWAITING` forever, because the response call requires a `delivery_state` of `SENT` or `DELIVERED`. On the response dimension, the same officer records an `outcome` of `ACKNOWLEDGED`, advancing `AWAITING → ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `REFUSED`, advancing `AWAITING → REFUSED`, optionally attaching a `response_note`; `response_recorded_at` is stamped. That call requires the row's `response_state` to still be `AWAITING` and its `delivery_state` to be `SENT` or `DELIVERED`, and a violation of either is `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_STATE`. There is a third way out of `AWAITING`: the checkpoint aging rule described in §3.8 moves `AWAITING → NO_RESPONSE` on its own.

Only the conjunction of the two dimensions counts. A notification's `counts_toward_effectiveness` is true when, and only when, its `delivery_state` is `DELIVERED` and its `response_state` is either `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `REFUSED` — a refusal is a response, and it counts exactly as much as an acknowledgement. A consignee's own `response_state` is the strongest state across all of its notifications, with `ACKNOWLEDGED` outranking `REFUSED`, `REFUSED` outranking `NO_RESPONSE`, and `NO_RESPONSE` outranking `AWAITING`. Recording either dimension recomputes that rollup, the recall's `responded_count`, `contact_effectiveness_pct` and `official_effectiveness_pct`. Whenever the recall is in `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS` and its `required_sample_size` is greater than zero and its `responded_count` has reached at least that size, the recall automatically advances `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS → RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING`; that check is evaluated at the end of a delivery recording, at the end of a response recording, and during a checkpoint.

### 3.6. The Press Release and Public Warning Decision

Public communication is entirely a `RecallCoordinator` affair, and it is a four-state pipeline with a mandatory second pair of eyes. Drafting takes a `warning_type` of `PRESS_RELEASE`, `PUBLIC_WARNING` or `SOCIAL_MEDIA_ONLY`, a `headline` and a `body`; the draft is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `drafted_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `reject_count` 0, `review_note` null and `published_at` null. Sending the draft into review advances `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` and requires that the recall has actually been classified — a recall still in `DRAFT` refuses the submission with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`.

Review takes a `decision` and an optional `review_note`, and the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the draft's `drafted_by_user_id`; a coordinator reviewing their own draft is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `decision` of `APPROVE` advances `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`; a `decision` of `REJECT` sends the draft back along `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` and increments `reject_count` by one. Both branches stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing coordinator, and both are refused with `ERR_INVALID_WARNING_STATE` when the warning is not currently `IN_REVIEW`.

Publication advances `APPROVED → PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` from the logical clock; `PUBLISHED` is terminal. Publishing anything that is not `APPROVED` is `ERR_INVALID_WARNING_STATE`. One substantive gate applies at this last step: a `PRESS_RELEASE` or a `PUBLIC_WARNING` may only be published when the recall's `hazard_class` is `CLASS_I` or `CLASS_II`, because a class-three hazard does not warrant broad public alarm; attempting it on a `CLASS_III` recall is refused with `ERR_PUBLIC_WARNING_NOT_WARRANTED`. A `SOCIAL_MEDIA_ONLY` notice is exempt from that gate and may be published at any hazard class.

### 3.7. Retrieval Accounting, the Two Effectiveness Bases, and Disposition Custody

Product accounting is reported per consignee by a `FirmRecallOfficer` and splits the consignee's stock four ways, each of which is an integer that must not be negative: `quantity_returned` for units physically sent back to the firm, `quantity_destroyed_onsite` for units destroyed at the consignee under its own certificate, `quantity_held_segregated` for units still at the consignee but blocked from sale, and `quantity_unrecoverable` for units already consumed or sold through. The receipt's `accounted_units` is the sum of exactly the first three of those; `quantity_unrecoverable` is deliberately excluded, because product that reached consumers is precisely what the recall failed to capture. Two gates guard the filing. The named consignee must appear on the reconciled distribution list — it must have at least one distribution record on this recall in `CONFIRMED` or `CORRECTED` — and otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNEE_NOT_ON_DISTRIBUTION_LIST`. And the arithmetic must not exceed what was shipped: take the cumulative sum of `quantity_returned`, `quantity_destroyed_onsite`, `quantity_held_segregated` and `quantity_unrecoverable` across that consignee's existing receipts in `REPORTED` or `VERIFIED` — receipts in `DISPUTED` are excluded from that running total — add the same four-way sum for the incoming receipt, and the result must not exceed the sum of `effective_quantity` over that consignee's `CONFIRMED` and `CORRECTED` distribution records on this recall. Exceeding it is `ERR_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED`.

A filed receipt is in `status` `REPORTED` with `verified_by_user_id` and `verification_note` null, and it contributes nothing to the recall until a `FieldInvestigator` adjudicates it with a `decision` and an optional `verification_note`. A `decision` of `VERIFY` advances `REPORTED → VERIFIED` and opens **Cascade B**. Hop one stamps `verified_by_user_id`, re-sums the recall's `accounted_units` over its receipts in `VERIFIED`, and adds this receipt's `quantity_returned` plus its `quantity_held_segregated` to the recall's `units_awaiting_disposition` — the two buckets of product that are still physically somewhere and still need a disposition decision, as distinct from product already destroyed at the consignee. Effectiveness is then recomputed. Hop two, if the effectiveness threshold is now met, advances the recall `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING → EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`, and hop three follows from that entry: every consignee of this recall whose `response_state` is `AWAITING` or `NO_RESPONSE` has `closed_without_response` set to true, and the server mints a status report with `report_kind` `FINAL_EFFECTIVENESS`, a `period_index` one greater than the largest existing index on the recall, `period_start` at the current logical instant, `period_end` and `due_at` three days later, and `status` `PENDING`. A `decision` of `DISPUTE` advances `REPORTED → DISPUTED`, stamps `verified_by_user_id`, and leaves the receipt contributing zero to `accounted_units`. A `decision` of `REOPEN` is available only from `DISPUTED`, moves `DISPUTED → REPORTED` and clears `verified_by_user_id` back to null. A decision the receipt's `status` does not permit is `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNTING_STATE`; verification also requires the recall to be in `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` or `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`, and is otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`.

Effectiveness itself is computed two ways at all times. The **contact basis** counts consignees, not notifications. Take the consignees of this recall whose `tier` is at or below `sample_tier_scope`, and count those having at least one notification whose `delivery_state` is `DELIVERED` and whose `response_state` is `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `REFUSED`; that count is `responded_count`, and a consignee with three qualifying notifications still contributes exactly one. Then `contact_effectiveness_pct` is 100.00 when `required_sample_size` is zero; otherwise it is the smaller of `responded_count` and `required_sample_size`, divided by `required_sample_size`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **quantity basis** counts units. `shipped_units` is the sum of `effective_quantity` over this recall's distribution records in `CONFIRMED` or `CORRECTED` — records in `DECLARED`, `DISPUTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing — and `accounted_units` is the sum of `accounted_units` over this recall's receipts in `VERIFIED`, with `REPORTED` and `DISPUTED` receipts contributing nothing. Then `quantity_effectiveness_pct` is 0.00 when `shipped_units` is zero; otherwise it is the smaller of `accounted_units` and `shipped_units`, divided by `shipped_units`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. Both numerators are clamped by that "smaller of" so neither percentage can ever exceed 100.00, and the two empty-set boundaries deliberately disagree: an empty required sample scores 100.00 while zero shipped units scores 0.00.

Which of the two is official is decided by the recall's `agency`. When `agency` is `FDA` the `official_basis` is `CONTACT` and `official_effectiveness_pct` is `contact_effectiveness_pct`; when `agency` is `USDA` the `official_basis` is `QUANTITY` and `official_effectiveness_pct` is `quantity_effectiveness_pct`. For the same fixture data the two variants routinely differ by tens of percentage points, and only the official one gates anything. Both are recomputed whenever a delivery or response outcome is recorded, whenever a distribution line is reconciled, whenever a receipt is verified, and at every checkpoint. A dedicated computed read exposes the whole picture in one payload: `recall_id`, `agency`, `effectiveness_check_level`, `sample_tier_scope`, `eligible_consignee_count`, `required_sample_size`, `responded_count`, `contact_effectiveness_pct`, `shipped_units`, `accounted_units`, `quantity_effectiveness_pct`, `official_basis` and `official_effectiveness_pct`, computed fresh at read time. The recall automatically advances `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING → EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW` as soon as `official_effectiveness_pct` reaches 95.00 or more; that check is evaluated at the end of a receipt verification and during a checkpoint, and — importantly — it is *not* re-evaluated when a termination decision is made, so a denied termination leaves the recall parked in `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` until the next verification or checkpoint.

Product in the firm's custody then has to be disposed of, and the custody ledger is the point of the exercise. A `FirmRecallOfficer` proposes an order naming a `method` — `DESTROY`, `REWORK`, `RELABEL` or `RETURN_TO_FIRM` — a `quantity` that must be strictly greater than zero, and the `facility_name` performing the work; the order is created in `status` `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` stamped, and `authorized_by_user_id`, `certificate_reference`, `executed_at`, `witness_user_id` and `verification_note` all null with `reject_count` 0. A `RecallCoordinator` authorizes it, advancing `PROPOSED → AUTHORIZED` and stamping `authorized_by_user_id`; authorization requires the recall to be in `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` or `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`, and it requires the order's `quantity` to be no greater than the recall's `units_awaiting_disposition` at that moment — an order for more product than the firm actually holds is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_EXCEEDS_ACCOUNTED_UNITS`. The firm then executes, advancing `AUTHORIZED → EXECUTED`, supplying a `certificate_reference` that must be a non-empty string (an empty or missing one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), stamping `executed_at` from the clock, and moving the order's `quantity` out of `units_awaiting_disposition` and into `units_pending_verification`. Finally a `FieldInvestigator` supplies a `decision`: `VERIFY` advances `EXECUTED → VERIFIED`, stamps `witness_user_id`, and moves the `quantity` out of `units_pending_verification` and into `units_disposed_verified`; `REJECT` sends the order back along `EXECUTED → AUTHORIZED`, increments `reject_count`, clears both `certificate_reference` and `executed_at` back to null, and moves the `quantity` out of `units_pending_verification` and back into `units_awaiting_disposition`, so an execute-then-reject pair restores the custody ledger exactly. `VERIFIED` is terminal. Any authorize, execute or verify against an order in the wrong `status` is `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE`.

### 3.8. Reporting Cadence, Checkpoints and Compliance Posture

Status reports come from two sources: the server mints them on its own at classification, on entry into effectiveness review, when a termination is denied and at the end of a checkpoint, and a `FirmRecallOfficer` may file one explicitly at any time before the recall terminates. An explicit filing supplies a `report_kind` — `PERIODIC`, `INTERIM` or `FINAL_EFFECTIVENESS`, defaulting to `INTERIM` when omitted — together with an explicit `period_start` and `period_end`. Reporting windows are half-open, covering `period_start` inclusive up to `period_end` exclusive, and `period_start` must be strictly earlier than `period_end`. A new window must not overlap any existing window on the same recall: overlap means the new start falls before an existing end *and* that existing start falls before the new end, and any such collision is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Exact adjacency, where a new window starts precisely at an existing window's end, is explicitly allowed. Every report — minted or filed — takes a `period_index` one greater than the largest index currently on the recall, so indices are strictly increasing within a recall and never repeat even after rejections; `due_at` is always equal to `period_end` for every report kind; and the report starts in `status` `PENDING` with `submitted_at`, `narrative` and `adjudication_note` null, `reported_effectiveness_pct` 0 and `reject_count` 0.

Submitting a report is the firm's act and is permitted from `PENDING` and from `OVERDUE` alike — a late report is still a report — advancing `PENDING → SUBMITTED` or `OVERDUE → SUBMITTED`. Submission stamps `submitted_at` from the clock, stores the supplied `narrative`, and snapshots `reported_effectiveness_pct` from the recall's `official_effectiveness_pct` at that exact instant, so the report preserves what was true when it was filed rather than what is true when it is read. A `RecallCoordinator` then adjudicates with a `decision` and an optional `adjudication_note`. `ACCEPT` advances `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal. `REJECT` sends the report back along `SUBMITTED → PENDING`, increments `reject_count`, clears `submitted_at` and `narrative` back to null, and grants a two-day extension by adding 2 days to `due_at` and setting `period_end` equal to the new `due_at`. Submitting a report that is neither `PENDING` nor `OVERDUE`, or adjudicating one that is not `SUBMITTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`.

The checkpoint is the `RecallCoordinator`'s way of advancing the recall's reporting and notification epoch after the logical clock has moved, and it does five things in order. First, every status report of the recall whose `due_at` is at or before the current logical instant flips `PENDING → OVERDUE`, and each flip increments the recall's `overdue_report_count` by one and recomputes `compliance_flag`. Second, notification aging runs: over this recall's notifications in ascending `sent_at`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`, every notification whose `delivery_state` is `DELIVERED` and whose `response_state` is still `AWAITING` and whose time since `delivery_recorded_at` has reached the response window for the recall's hazard class moves `AWAITING → NO_RESPONSE`, stamps `response_recorded_at`, increments that consignee's `escalation_level` by one, and refreshes the consignee's rolled-up `response_state`. The response window is 24 hours for `CLASS_I`, 72 hours for `CLASS_II` and 168 hours — a full week — for `CLASS_III`, and the comparison is inclusive, so a notification sitting exactly at the window edge does age out. Third, `compliance_flag`, `report_cadence_days` and all three effectiveness figures are recomputed. Fourth, the recall may auto-advance `NOTIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS → RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING` or `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING → EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW` under the conditions already described. Fifth and last, if the recall's `status` is neither `DRAFT` nor `TERMINATED`, and no status report of the recall is left in `PENDING`, and at least one status report of the recall exists, the server mints the next one: `report_kind` `PERIODIC`, `period_index` one above the current maximum, `period_start` at the current logical instant, `period_end` and `due_at` at that instant plus `report_cadence_days` days, `status` `PENDING`. A checkpoint against a recall in `DRAFT` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`. Day and hour arithmetic on the logical clock is exact — a day is precisely twenty-four hours.

Compliance posture is a single derived label evaluated top-down, first matching clause wins. The `compliance_flag` is `DELINQUENT` when `overdue_report_count` has reached 3 or more. Otherwise it is `ESCALATED` when `overdue_report_count` has reached 1 or more. Otherwise it is `WATCH` when `official_effectiveness_pct` is strictly below 60.00 *and* the recall's `status` is `RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING`, `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW` or `TERMINATION_REQUESTED`. Otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. The strictness of that effectiveness comparison matters: an official effectiveness of exactly 60.00 does not raise `WATCH`. Because `overdue_report_count` is cumulative and never decreases, a posture that has been raised by an overdue report never returns to `COMPLIANT`. The posture feeds straight back into the cadence: once the recall is `ESCALATED` or `DELINQUENT`, `report_cadence_days` becomes 3 regardless of hazard class, and the cadence is recomputed at classification, at every checkpoint and on the denial branch of a termination decision.

### 3.9. Cost Accumulation, Insurance Settlement and Closing the Case

Every incurred cost is filed as its own line by a `FirmRecallOfficer`, carrying a `category`, an `amount_cents` that must be strictly greater than zero, and a free-text `description` of what the cost was for. Six categories exist — `NOTIFICATION`, `LOGISTICS`, `DESTRUCTION`, `LEGAL`, `PRODUCT_VALUE` and `LAB_TESTING` — and the category alone decides two derived attributes at filing time. `insurable` is true for `NOTIFICATION`, `LOGISTICS`, `DESTRUCTION` and `PRODUCT_VALUE`, and false for `LEGAL` and `LAB_TESTING`. The `overhead_rate` is 11.5 percent for `NOTIFICATION`, 8.5 percent for `LOGISTICS`, 14.5 percent for `DESTRUCTION`, zero for `LEGAL`, 5.5 percent for `PRODUCT_VALUE` and 22.5 percent for `LAB_TESTING`. From those, `line_loaded_cost_cents` is the line's `amount_cents` multiplied by one plus its `overhead_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents — and that rounding happens on this single line, before the line ever enters a sum. The order is load-bearing: three notification lines of 3,333 cents each load to 3,716 cents apiece for a total of 11,148 cents, whereas summing first and rounding once would give 11,149. The line is created in `status` `PENDING` with `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudication_note` null.

A `RecallCoordinator` adjudicates each line with a `decision` and an optional `adjudication_note`, stamping `adjudicated_by_user_id`. `ACCEPT` is permitted from `PENDING` and from `REJECTED`, moving `PENDING → ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED → ACCEPTED`; `REJECT` is permitted from `PENDING` and from `ACCEPTED`, moving `PENDING → REJECTED` or `ACCEPTED → REJECTED`. A decision the line's current `status` does not permit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COST_ENTRY_STATE`. Every adjudication re-sums the recall's ledger from scratch: `total_loaded_cost_cents` is the sum of `line_loaded_cost_cents` over this recall's lines in `ACCEPTED`, and `insurable_loaded_cost_cents` is that same sum restricted to lines whose `insurable` is true. Lines in `PENDING` and `REJECTED` contribute nothing, which is exactly why accepting a line and then rejecting it restores both totals to their pre-cycle values.

An insurance claim starts as a shell: a `FirmRecallOfficer` opens one with nothing but a `claim_reference` from the carrier, and it is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `claimed_cents`, `capped_total_cents`, `coinsurance_rate`, `settlement_payable_cents`, `handling_fee_cents`, `total_claim_value_cents` and `paid_cents` all zero, `supplement_round` 0, and `denial_reason` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` null. Submitting the claim advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is permitted only while the recall is in `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`, `TERMINATION_REQUESTED` or `TERMINATED`; the recall's `insurable_loaded_cost_cents` must be greater than zero, and a submission against a recall with no accepted insurable cost is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The firm's remaining policy aggregate — its `policy_aggregate_cents` less its `policy_paid_to_date_cents` — must be strictly greater than zero, and a submission when it is zero or less is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_POLICY_AGGREGATE`; note that a merely *small* positive remainder never rejects, it only clamps.

Submission runs a settlement waterfall whose evaluation order is pinned; there is no back-substitution and no iteration. Step one snapshots `claimed_cents` from the recall's current `insurable_loaded_cost_cents`, uncapped. Step two applies a per-category sublimit: for each insurable category, sum `line_loaded_cost_cents` over this recall's accepted lines of that category, then take the smaller of that subtotal and the category's sublimit — 2,000,000 cents for `NOTIFICATION`, 5,000,000 cents for `LOGISTICS`, 3,000,000 cents for `DESTRUCTION` and 25,000,000 cents for `PRODUCT_VALUE` — and `capped_total_cents` is the sum of those four capped figures. Lines in `LEGAL` and `LAB_TESTING` never enter any step of this waterfall, although they do count toward the recall's `total_loaded_cost_cents`. Step three selects `coinsurance_rate` from `capped_total_cents` and from nothing else: 0.90 when `capped_total_cents` is at least 50,000,000 cents, otherwise 0.85 when it is at least 10,000,000 cents, otherwise 0.80. Step four subtracts the deductible *before* applying coinsurance: take `capped_total_cents` less the firm's `policy_deductible_cents`, floor that difference at zero, multiply by `coinsurance_rate`, and round half up to whole cents to obtain `settlement_payable_cents`. Step five clamps that figure down to the firm's remaining policy aggregate if the aggregate is smaller. Step six computes `handling_fee_cents` as `settlement_payable_cents` multiplied by 3.0 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. Step seven sets `total_claim_value_cents` to `settlement_payable_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents`. The dependency chain only looks circular — the tier is pinned to step two's `capped_total_cents`, never to `total_claim_value_cents`, and that pinning is what breaks the loop.

An `InsuranceAdjuster` then adjudicates a submitted claim with a `decision`. `PAY_FULL` advances `SUBMITTED → PAID` and sets `paid_cents` to `settlement_payable_cents`. `PAY_PARTIAL` advances `SUBMITTED → PARTIALLY_PAID` and requires a `paid_cents` in the body that is strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `settlement_payable_cents`; anything outside that open interval is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `DENY` advances `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and requires a non-empty `denial_reason`, which is recorded on the claim. All three stamp `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and both paying branches add the paid amount to the firm's `policy_paid_to_date_cents`. `PAID` and `DENIED` are terminal; `PARTIALLY_PAID` is not. A partially paid claim may be resubmitted as a supplement along `PARTIALLY_PAID → SUBMITTED`, which increments `supplement_round` by one and recomputes every settlement field from scratch against the recall's *current* `insurable_loaded_cost_cents` and the firm's *current* remaining aggregate. Adjudicating a claim that is not `SUBMITTED`, or submitting one that is neither `DRAFT` nor `PARTIALLY_PAID`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Closing the case is a two-actor act. The `FirmRecallOfficer` requests termination from `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW`, supplying `root_cause_code` — one of `SUPPLIER_INGREDIENT`, `SANITATION`, `PROCESS_DEVIATION`, `LABELING`, `PACKAGING`, `CROSS_CONTACT` or `UNKNOWN` — and a `capa_summary` narrating the corrective and preventive action taken; both are persisted on the recall and the recall advances `EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW → TERMINATION_REQUESTED`. Two conditions guard the request. No disposition order of the recall may be in `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED` or `EXECUTED` — any open order refuses the request with `ERR_OPEN_DISPOSITION_ORDERS` — and the recall's `official_effectiveness_pct` must be at least 90.00, below which the request is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVENESS_BELOW_TERMINATION_THRESHOLD`. Note that the termination floor of 90.00 is lower than the 95.00 that carried the recall into effectiveness review in the first place, so a recall whose effectiveness has slipped since entering review can still be closed out.

The `RecallCoordinator` then supplies a `decision`, and in either branch the deciding coordinator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recall's `classified_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `decision` of `TERMINATE` additionally requires that `compliance_flag` is not `DELINQUENT` — a delinquent recall may not be closed, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_STATE`. Termination advances `TERMINATION_REQUESTED → TERMINATED`, stamps `terminated_at` from the logical clock, moves every status report of the recall still in `PENDING` or `OVERDUE` to `ACCEPTED`, and decrements the firm's `open_recall_count` by one. `TERMINATED` is terminal for the whole case file: no write of any kind is accepted against the recall or any of its sub-resources afterwards. A `decision` of `DENY` sends the recall back along `TERMINATION_REQUESTED → RETRIEVAL_ACCOUNTING`, increments `termination_denied_count` by one, raises `compliance_flag` from `COMPLIANT` to `WATCH` while leaving any stronger posture untouched, and mints a fresh status report with `report_kind` `PERIODIC`, `period_index` one above the current maximum, `period_start` at the current logical instant, `period_end` and `due_at` at that instant plus `report_cadence_days` days, and `status` `PENDING`.
