# Office Coffee Service & Equipment Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Farmer Brothers' office coffee service business sells very little in a single transaction and almost everything over a multi-year relationship. A corporate customer signs one program agreement, then accumulates dozens of break rooms across several buildings, each with its own headcount, its own occupancy pattern, its own brewer, and its own weekly replenishment stop. The commercial value of the program lives in the tension between two promises: we put a brewer in the break room at little or no capital cost to the customer, and the customer keeps buying coffee and allied products through us. When one side of that bargain drifts — purchases fall below the committed minimum, a route misses three stops in a row and the creamer runs dry, a loaner brewer never comes back from a repair, or the green coffee market moves eight percent against the price we locked at signing — somebody has to notice, quantify it in cents, and put it on an invoice.

This document specifies the service that carries that burden. It models the whole program: account setup with headcount-based consumption forecasting and equipment sizing, loan-versus-rental placement terms tied to a minimum purchase commitment, route replenishment against par levels with stockout escalation, brewer preventive maintenance with descaling and loaner swaps, allied-product breadth attachment, consolidated invoicing for multi-site corporate accounts, equipment retrieval on cancellation with unreturned-asset billing, and price adjustment negotiation driven by green coffee index movement. Every one of those concerns is a calculation, a state transition, or a gate, and most of them interact: a missed delivery becomes a stockout, which becomes a site escalation, which becomes an account risk posture, which freezes a price negotiation that was already on the customer's desk.

Two design commitments shape the whole API. First, **time is explicit**: every deadline, trailing window, depreciation clock and service window is evaluated against a server-side logical clock that test harnesses and operators drive directly, never against real wall-clock time. Second, **money is deterministic**: every monetary quantity is an integer, every rounding step is pinned to a named point in a named evaluation order, and no downstream reader is ever allowed to recompute a price that was already snapshotted upstream. Implementations that honour those two commitments produce byte-identical invoices from identical histories; implementations that do not will disagree by cents, and cents are what this business is made of.

All resources described here are mounted under the `/ocs` path prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the two test-driving endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. There is no anonymous surface and no session mechanism; the headers are the entire authentication story. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system, so a malformed body on an unauthenticated request still surfaces as an authentication failure.

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `AccountManager`, `BillingSpecialist`, `RouteTechnician`, and `CustomerContact`. Spelling is exact and case-sensitive.

**`ProgramAdmin`** is the operations owner of the fleet, the product catalog and the route network, and is additionally the only role permitted to void a completed visit. **`AccountManager`** owns the customer program lifecycle: accounts, sites, demand planning, par-level overrides, brewer placements, retrieval, and visit scheduling. **`BillingSpecialist`** owns money — minimum-purchase compliance evaluation, invoice assembly, issuance, credits, and green-coffee price proposals. Each of these three roles has an **opaque** identity: when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `AccountManager`, or `BillingSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may accept and log it, but it never resolves it against any entity's `id` and never uses it in an ownership comparison. An opaque identifier is therefore never the cause of a 401 beyond the presence check.

**`RouteTechnician`** is the field technician of exactly one route, and its identity is entity-backed by `Route`. When `X-User-Role` is `RouteTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Route`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Route` compare the resource's route foreign key to `X-User-ID`. **`CustomerContact`** is the customer-side signer of exactly one account, and its identity is entity-backed by `Account`. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's account foreign key to `X-User-ID`. For these two entity-backed roles the identifier must actually resolve — a `RouteTechnician` whose `X-User-ID` matches no existing route, or a `CustomerContact` whose `X-User-ID` matches no existing account, is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR_IDENTITY` (HTTP 401). Opaque roles are never resolved and therefore never raise this error.

Once the actor is authenticated and its identity resolves, the endpoint's own authorization list applies. If the acting role does not appear on the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Two further ownership gates then apply, and they are distinct from the coarse role check because they compare identity to data. A `RouteTechnician` may only act on a service visit whose route matches its own `X-User-ID`, and on a maintenance work order whose site sits on the route matching its own `X-User-ID`; a violation of either yields `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ON_ROUTE` (HTTP 403). A `CustomerContact` may only respond to a price adjustment belonging to the account matching its own `X-User-ID`; a violation yields `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ACCOUNT_CONTACT` (HTTP 403). Both ownership gates are evaluated only after the referenced resources have been confirmed to exist, because you cannot compare against the route of a visit that is not there.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a list endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first. The route listing is the one documented exception: it presents routes newest first.

List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and populate four pagination members inside `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied), and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses set `response_type` to `"object"` and omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id` on creation, and no endpoint accepts one in a request body.

Most resources also carry three common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was written, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. `updated_at` follows the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row; it is the value the stale-read protection of §2.7 compares against. Ten of the thirteen resource types carry all four: `Account`, `Site`, `Route`, `EquipmentAsset`, `EquipmentPlacement`, `ParLevel`, `ServiceVisit`, `MaintenanceWorkOrder`, `ConsolidatedInvoice`, `InvoiceLine`, and `PriceAdjustment`.

Two resource types are deliberately immutable catalog records and carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: `EquipmentModel` and `ProductItem`. Neither exposes `updated_at` and neither exposes `version`, because neither has an update endpoint. A brewer model cannot be edited once registered, and a catalog product cannot be repriced by editing it — repricing happens through the account-level green-coffee factor described in §3.9, never by mutating the product row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Green Coffee Market Quote

Nothing in this system reads real wall-clock time. Every deadline, service window, trailing aggregation window, depreciation age and protection period is evaluated against a single server-side logical instant, referred to throughout this document as the current instant. Before the logical clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an implementation must never fall back to host wall-clock time. An authorized operator moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; the clock may move forward **or** backward, and setting it mutates no entity and fires no lazy transition. Lazy state promotions — an overdue loaner becoming overdue, a reconditioning hold expiring — are evaluated when the next business call touches the affected row, never at the moment the clock moves.

A second global, the green coffee market index, works the same way. It is a whole number of micro-dollars per pound, it reads `2000000` at startup, and an authorized operator replaces it by supplying an `index_micros` value to the market-quote capability. Replacing the quote reprices nothing on its own: no account's effective prices change until a price adjustment is proposed against that quote and the customer accepts it.

### 2.6. Money, Quantities and Rounding Discipline

Three units of measure appear in this specification, and they never mix silently. Money on invoices, fees, balances and equipment values is expressed in whole **cents** as an integer. Unit prices for catalog products are expressed in whole **micro-dollars** as an integer, where one cent equals 10000 micro-dollars. Proportions — discount rates, index movements, pass-through percentages, allied attachment shares, depreciation rates — are expressed in whole **basis points** as an integer, where 10000 basis points is unity, so a factor of `10000` means "unchanged" and `500` means five percent.

Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is **round half up**: the fractional part is dropped when it is below one half and the magnitude is increased by one when it is one half or above. Wherever this document says a value is rounded up, the method is ceiling — any non-zero fractional part increases the integer by one. Wherever it says rounded down, the method is floor. Each of these operations is applied exactly at the point the relevant calculation in §3 names it, and never a second time on an aggregate that has already been rounded: an implementation that sums unrounded micro-dollar products across several lines and rounds once at the end will produce totals that differ by cents from the specification, and is wrong.

The one non-integer stored value in the system is a site's escalation score, which is a number carried to one decimal place. Its component weights are all multiples of one half, so the score is exact and requires no rounding.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Placement and Invoice Creation

Two write operations are guarded against acting on data the client has not observed, and both use the same mechanism: the client echoes the version it last read of a **parent** resource, and the server refuses the write if that parent has moved on.

Creating an equipment placement requires a non-optional `expected_site_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Site`. If it does not equal the site's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because creating a placement legitimately retires the site's prior active placement — a brewer upgrade or swap — so a writer holding a stale view of the site would supersede a placement it never saw. The rejection forces it to re-read the site first.

Creating a consolidated invoice draft requires a non-optional `expected_account_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Account`. If it does not equal the account's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Draft assembly is emphatically not a read-only projection: it writes unreturned-asset charges, loaner write-offs and per-site discount lines. A writer holding a stale account view — one that has not seen a cancellation, an accepted price adjustment, or a posture change — would bill against superseded prices and balances.

No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind. Sending one where it is not specified is not required to be honoured, and omitting one where it **is** specified is a missing-required-field failure yielding `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

### 2.8. The Request Admission Ladder

When a single request could fail several checks, the outcome is not a matter of implementation taste. Checks are evaluated in a fixed global order, and the **first** failing check determines the response. The ladder, from highest precedence to lowest, is:

1. **Header presence and role membership** — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. **Endpoint role authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Entity-backed actor identity resolution** — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR_IDENTITY`, applied on every endpoint invoked as `RouteTechnician` or as `CustomerContact`.
4. **Request shape** — `ERR_VALIDATION`. Every input field declared without a default must be present; declared types must match; enum-typed values must be members of their declared value set; integers must respect their declared ranges; ISO 8601 instants must parse; and `limit` and `offset` must respect the pagination bounds of §2.3.
5. **Path identifier existence**, left to right (see §2.9) — the corresponding not-found error.
6. **Body foreign-key existence** (see §2.9) — the corresponding not-found error.
7. **Route-technician ownership** — `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ON_ROUTE`, on visit line append, visit completion, work-order advance, loaner swap and loaner return.
8. **Customer-contact ownership** — `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ACCOUNT_CONTACT`, on price-adjustment response.
9. **Stale site version** — `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION`, on placement creation.
10. **Stale account version** — `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION`, on invoice draft creation.
11. through 20. **State preconditions** — the posture and status gates catalogued in §2.10 and detailed in §3.
21. through 23. **Safety gates** — sanitation hold, descale overdue, asset-role mismatch.
24. through 30. **Temporal gates** — window validity, route window collision, loaner window collision, premature completion, invoice period validity, invoice period overlap, price protection.
31. through 36. **Referential coherence gates** — site planned, product stocked, duplicate visit line, credit target, site-in-account, billing-mode scope.
37. through 42. **Feasibility gates** — model fit, trailing usage availability, awaiting-parts cycle limit, index movement threshold, revision bounds, delivered-versus-requested.
43. through 46. **Fiscal and capacity gates** — loan minimum floor, route day capacity, billable lines, credit ceiling.
47. through 51. **Uniqueness and conditionally-required transition fields** — duplicate model code, duplicate serial number, duplicate SKU, duplicate par line, and missing transition field.

The consequences of this ordering are load-bearing and are restated wherever they matter in §3. Two examples worth internalising now: identity resolution for entity-backed roles precedes request-shape validation, so a `RouteTechnician` with an unresolvable `X-User-ID` and a malformed body gets `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR_IDENTITY` rather than `ERR_VALIDATION`; and stale-version rejection precedes every state, safety, temporal and fiscal gate, so a placement create with both a stale `expected_site_version` and an unplanned site gets `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION`.

Note also that the two safety gates sit **above** every temporal and fiscal gate. A loaner swap that names an asset still inside its sanitation hold **and** whose requested window collides with another work order surfaces the sanitation hold, not the collision.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution: Path First, Then Body

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved before anything in the body is examined, and when a path carries more than one templated segment they are resolved strictly left to right. Each identifier has its own dedicated error: `account_id` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `site_id` yields `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `route_id` yields `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `model_id` yields `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `asset_id` yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `placement_id` yields `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `product_id` yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `par_level_id` yields `ERR_PAR_LEVEL_NOT_FOUND`, `visit_id` yields `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `work_order_id` yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `invoice_id` yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, and `adjustment_id` yields `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve are HTTP 404. A `line_id` naming a non-existent invoice line, and a `target_line_id` that resolves to nothing at all, both yield `ERR_INVOICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a `loaner_asset_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after all path identifiers, and among themselves in the order the fields are declared on the owning resource. Placement creation therefore checks `site_id` before `asset_id`, and visit scheduling checks `site_id` before `route_id`. Each body foreign key raises the same error identifier its path counterpart would.

### 2.10. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Two resources in this system carry **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and conflating them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. An `EquipmentPlacement` carries `placement_status` (where the brewer physically is, in the installation lifecycle) and `compliance_status` (whether the site is meeting its purchase commitment). Neither constrains the other: a placement scheduled for retrieval can still be evaluated for compliance, and a placement in a billed-shortfall state is still physically installed. A `MaintenanceWorkOrder` likewise carries `wo_status` (the service dimension) and `loaner_status` (the substitute-brewer dimension), and again they advance independently — except at one deliberate join point, where a work order cannot be resolved while its loaner is still out.

Three of these dimensions are **cyclic**. A placement's `compliance_status` can travel `MEETING_MINIMUM` → `SHORTFALL_WARNED` → `SHORTFALL_BILLED` → `MEETING_MINIMUM` and around again without bound. A work order's `wo_status` can travel `IN_PROGRESS` → `AWAITING_PARTS` → `IN_PROGRESS` repeatedly, bounded only by a cap on entries into `AWAITING_PARTS`. A price adjustment can travel `PROPOSED` → `DISPUTED` → `PROPOSED` with no round limit at all. A site's `service_posture` cycles between `ACTIVE` and `ESCALATED` any number of times, and an account's `program_posture` cycles between `ACTIVE` and `AT_RISK` in step with it.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and synchronous**. When one operation moves several resources, all of those writes are visible by the time the response is returned; there is no deferred worker and no eventual convergence. Every resource whose fields change as part of a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — including resources the caller never named, such as the account whose posture flipped because a site three levels down ran out of creamer. Two cascade chains dominate the domain and are described in full in §3: the four-hop replenishment chain that runs shelf ledger → site posture → account posture → price adjustments, and the four-hop cancellation chain that runs placements → sites → scheduled visits, with draft invoices voided in the same pass.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Account Onboarding, Program Posture and Cancellation

A corporate program begins when an `AccountManager` registers the customer. The registration payload carries the customer's `legal_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), a `billing_mode` of either `CONSOLIDATED` or `PER_SITE` that decides whether one invoice may span several of the account's break rooms, and a `green_index_baseline_micros` value of at least 1 — the green coffee index in micro-dollars per pound, locked at contract signing, against which all future market movement is measured for this customer and this customer only. An optional `price_protection_days` may be supplied; it defaults to 90 and must not be negative. The new account is born in `program_posture` `ONBOARDING` with `green_price_factor_bps` at `10000` (meaning green-linked prices are unmodified), `receivable_balance_cents` at 0, `escalated_site_count` at 0, `allied_attachment_bps` at 0, `price_protection_until` null, and `version` 1. The three cancellation fields `cancel_reason`, `retrieval_due_by` and `cancelled_at` are all null until the program is cancelled.

**Program posture** is the account-level gate on everything below it, and it is computed — never client-supplied. Its five values are `ONBOARDING`, `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK`, `CANCELLATION_PENDING` and `CLOSED`, of which `CLOSED` is terminal. The account leaves onboarding the moment equipment is installed anywhere: when the first placement of any site of the account reaches an active installation state, and the account is still `ONBOARDING`, the system moves `program_posture` from `ONBOARDING` to `ACTIVE` and increments the account's `version` by 1. This is a bubble-up, driven by a write against a placement rather than against the account.

Sites, placements and service visits may only be created while `program_posture` is `ONBOARDING`, `ACTIVE`, or `AT_RISK`. A `CANCELLATION_PENDING` or `CLOSED` account rejects all three with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPERATING` (HTTP 409). The same error and the same rule guard cancellation itself: cancellation is permitted from `ONBOARDING`, `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK`, so a second cancellation attempt against an already-cancelling or closed account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPERATING`.

**The risk posture** reflects trouble in the field. Whenever a site of the account changes its own service posture, the account's `escalated_site_count` is recomputed as the number of that account's sites currently in service posture `ESCALATED`. If the recomputed count reaches 2 or more and the account is `ACTIVE`, the account moves `ACTIVE` → `AT_RISK`, increments its `version` by 1, and — as the fourth hop of the replenishment cascade — moves every price adjustment of that account currently in `PROPOSED` to `DEFERRED`. The recovery direction closes the cycle: if the recomputed count falls below 2 and the account is `AT_RISK`, the account moves `AT_RISK` → `ACTIVE`, increments its `version` by 1, and moves every `DEFERRED` price adjustment of that account back to `PROPOSED`. Two escalated sites is the trigger in both directions, and the comparison is inclusive going up (two escalated sites is enough) and strict coming down (one escalated site restores the account).

**Cancellation** is the head of a four-hop unwind, and an `AccountManager` drives it by supplying a `cancel_reason` and a `retrieval_due_by` deadline. The account moves to `CANCELLATION_PENDING` from whichever of `ONBOARDING`, `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK` it was in — the three source postures behave identically — and `cancel_reason`, `retrieval_due_by` and `cancelled_at` are stamped, the last with the current instant. Hop one: every equipment placement belonging to any site of this account whose installation state is `ACTIVE` moves to `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, and each such placement's own `retrieval_due_by` is copied from the account's. Hop two: each site owning one of those placements moves its service posture to `SUSPENDED`, whether it was `ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED` at the time — and when the suspended site had been `ESCALATED`, the account's `escalated_site_count` is recomputed in the same pass, because a suspended site no longer counts as escalated. Hop three: every service visit of those sites still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and each cancellation releases that visit's booked minutes back to its route. In the same pass, every consolidated invoice of the account still in `DRAFT` moves to `VOID`. The account's `version` increments by 1. When the account was in `ONBOARDING` with no active placement, the retrieval cascade is simply a no-op and the account still reaches `CANCELLATION_PENDING`.

The program **closes** only when the equipment is accounted for. When a placement retrieval is recorded and, after that retrieval, every placement of the account is in one of the terminal-or-billed installation states `RETRIEVED`, `UNRETURNED_BILLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and the account is in `CANCELLATION_PENDING`, then the account moves `CANCELLATION_PENDING` → `CLOSED`, every site of the account still in `SUSPENDED` moves to `CLOSED`, and the account's `version` increments by 1.

**Allied attachment** is a health metric an `AccountManager` or `BillingSpecialist` can refresh on demand, and it is also refreshed automatically whenever an invoice is issued. It measures the non-coffee share of product revenue over the trailing 90 days and is stored on `allied_attachment_bps`. The window is the 90 days immediately preceding the current instant, treated as left-exclusive and right-inclusive: a visit exactly 90 days old is outside the window, and a visit at the current instant is inside it. Across every completed visit of every site of the account whose service window opened inside that window, and for every line of those visits with a delivered quantity above zero, the line's cents amount is computed as its delivered units multiplied by its snapshotted effective unit price in micro-dollars, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Those per-line amounts are then split into two buckets by the product's `category`: amounts whose product category is `COFFEE` form the coffee bucket, and amounts of every other category form the allied bucket. The metric is the allied bucket multiplied by 10000 and divided by the sum of both buckets, rounded half up. When the sum of both buckets is zero, the metric is 0 rather than an error — an account that has taken no delivery in the window reports zero attachment and the recompute succeeds.

### 3.2. Break-Room Sites, Demand Modelling and Par Planning

**Catalog prerequisites.** Before any customer work is possible, a `ProgramAdmin` populates three catalogs. A **brewer model** is registered with a `model_code` (non-empty, and unique across the catalog — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_MODEL_CODE`, HTTP 409), a `brewer_class` drawn from `AIRPOT`, `THERMAL`, `SINGLE_CUP` and `ESPRESSO`, a `cups_per_hour_capacity` of at least 1, an inclusive headcount band given by `min_headcount` (at least 1) and `max_headcount` (not below `min_headcount`), a `monthly_rent_cents` of at least 0 that is charged only under rental terms, a `descale_interval_days` of at least 1 that is the maximum number of days permitted between descale services, and a `list_value_cents` of at least 0 that is the replacement value used when an asset is never returned. A **catalog product** is registered with a `sku` (non-empty, unique — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, HTTP 409), a `category` drawn from `COFFEE`, `CREAMER`, `TEA`, `WATER` and `PANTRY`, a `base_unit_price_micros` of at least 1, a `pack_units` count of at least 1 recording how many sales units make up a shipping case (a reporting figure only — every par level and every delivery in this system is counted in sales units, never cases), and an optional `green_coffee_linked` flag defaulting to false that decides whether the account's green-coffee factor applies to this product's price. Every category other than `COFFEE` counts as allied breadth. A **route** is registered with a `name` (non-empty, at most 100 characters), a `service_day` drawn from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU` and `FRI`, a `capacity_minutes` between 1 and 960 inclusive that bounds technician minutes per calendar service date, and an optional `travel_buffer_minutes` between 0 and 120 inclusive that defaults to 30. A new route starts with `booked_minutes_total` at 0.

**Site registration.** An `AccountManager` adds a break room by supplying the owning `account_id`, the `route_id` of the replenishment route that will service it, a `site_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), the on-site `headcount` (at least 1), the number of occupied `days_per_week` (between 1 and 7 inclusive), a `consumption_model` of either `HEADCOUNT_MODEL` or `TRAILING_USAGE`, and a `service_minutes_estimate` between 1 and 480 inclusive recording how many technician minutes a standard visit to this site consumes. The site starts in service posture `ACTIVE`, with `escalation_score` at 0.0, `forecast_cups_per_week` at 0, `required_peak_cups_per_hour` at 0, `consecutive_shortfall_visit_count` at 0, `recommended_model_id` null, `brew_style` null, and `version` 1. A site's demand attributes — `headcount`, `days_per_week`, `consumption_model` and `route_id` — may later be revised individually by an `AccountManager`; each supplied field replaces the stored value and the site's `version` increments by 1, but revising them deliberately does **not** re-run the forecast: `forecast_cups_per_week`, `required_peak_cups_per_hour`, `recommended_model_id` and every existing par line are left exactly as they were until planning is run again.

**Planning a site** is the operation that turns headcount into numbers. An `AccountManager` triggers it with a `brew_style` of `DRIP`, `SINGLE_CUP` or `ESPRESSO`, which is stamped onto the site. Planning requires the site's service posture to be `ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED`; a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` site refuses it with `ERR_SITE_NOT_SERVICEABLE` (HTTP 409). Planning then runs the consumption forecast in the variant named by the site's own stored `consumption_model` — the variant is **never** a request field — and afterwards runs the equipment sizing selector. On success the site's `version` increments by 1.

Under the **`HEADCOUNT_MODEL`** variant, the weekly cup forecast is the site's headcount multiplied by 1.8 cups per person per day, multiplied by the number of occupied days per week, multiplied by the brew-style factor, with the product rounded up to a whole number of cups. The brew-style factor is 1.00 for `DRIP`, 1.15 for `SINGLE_CUP`, and 0.85 for `ESPRESSO` — single-cup brewing wastes a little, espresso volumes run lower. Each product's par quantity is then the weekly cup forecast multiplied by a two-week cover period, multiplied by that product's per-cup unit factor, rounded up to a whole number of sales units. The per-cup unit factors are 0.09 for `COFFEE`, 0.50 for `CREAMER`, 0.05 for `TEA`, 0.01 for `WATER`, and 0.40 for `PANTRY`. Because this variant derives par levels from projected rather than observed demand, a site that has never been served still receives non-zero par levels.

Under the **`TRAILING_USAGE`** variant, par levels come from what the site actually took. The observation window is the 28 days ending at the current instant, inclusive of both endpoints. For each product, the observed delivery is the sum of delivered units across that product's lines on service visits of this site whose status is `COMPLETED` and whose service window opened inside the window; visits that were voided or cancelled contribute nothing. If the site has no completed visit at all inside that window, the site cannot be planned and the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_TRAILING_USAGE` (HTTP 422). Otherwise, the product's weekly rate is its observed delivery divided by 4, carried as an exact rational value with no intermediate rounding, and the product's par quantity is that weekly rate multiplied by the two-week cover period, multiplied by a safety uplift of 11000 basis points (a ten percent cushion), divided by 10000, rounded up to a whole number of sales units. The weekly cup forecast under this variant is computed exactly as it is under `HEADCOUNT_MODEL` — headcount times 1.8 times occupied days per week times the brew-style factor, rounded up. Identical site attributes therefore produce materially different par quantities under the two variants, which is the point: a trailing-usage site is planned from its own history, and a site with no history in the window cannot be planned at all.

Both variants share a common tail. Each product's reorder point is its par quantity multiplied by 4000 basis points (forty percent of par), divided by 10000, rounded up to a whole number of units. The site's required peak throughput is the weekly cup forecast divided by the number of occupied days per week, multiplied by a peak factor of 0.35, rounded up to a whole number of cups per hour; intermediate products are exact decimals and are not rounded before the final ceiling.

**Par lines** are the per-product stock definitions of a break room. Planning writes one par line with `source` `FORECAST` for **every** product in the catalog, at that site. If a `FORECAST` line already exists for that product and site, planning overwrites **only** its `par_units` and `reorder_point_units` and leaves every other member alone. If the line is newly created, it starts with `on_hand_units` 0, both `cumulative_delivered_units` and `cumulative_consumed_units` 0, `last_service_at` set to the current instant, and `stock_status` `STOCKOUT`. Lines whose `source` is `MANUAL` are **never** touched by planning, no matter how many times it runs.

A `MANUAL` par line is created directly by an `AccountManager` for a `(site_id, product_id)` pair, to stock a product the forecast did not cover, with a client-supplied `par_units` of at least 0 and a `reorder_point_units` that must be at least 0 and must not exceed `par_units`. At most one par line may exist per site-and-product pair whatever its source; a duplicate yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAR_LEVEL` (HTTP 409). A manually created line begins with `source` `MANUAL`, zero on-hand and zero cumulative counters, `last_service_at` at the current instant, and `stock_status` `STOCKOUT`. An `AccountManager` may also override an existing line's `par_units` and `reorder_point_units` individually; doing so replaces the supplied fields, **pins** the line's `source` to `MANUAL` (so a forecast-generated line that has been overridden is thereafter protected from planning), recomputes `stock_status` against the new reorder point, and increments the line's `version` by 1.

`stock_status` is always derived, never supplied: it is `STOCKOUT` when `on_hand_units` is exactly 0, `LOW` when `on_hand_units` is above 0 and at or below `reorder_point_units`, and `IN_STOCK` otherwise.

**Equipment sizing** runs immediately after the forecast, using the freshly computed required peak throughput. The eligible candidates are the brewer models whose headcount band contains the site's headcount — that is, the model's minimum headcount is at or below the site's headcount and the model's maximum headcount is at or above it, both bounds inclusive — and whose cups-per-hour capacity is at or above the site's required peak throughput. Deliberately, availability of physical assets plays no part in eligibility: the selector ranks the whole model catalog. Candidates are ranked by cups-per-hour capacity ascending, so the **smallest sufficient** brewer wins; ties are broken by monthly rent ascending, and any remaining tie is broken by the model's `created_at` ascending — never by identifier. The first candidate's identifier is written to the site's `recommended_model_id`. If no model satisfies both eligibility conditions, planning is rejected with `ERR_NO_MODEL_FITS_HEADCOUNT` (HTTP 422). The recommendation is computed output only: clients never supply it, and it is never used as a reverse-lookup key.

Planning is a hard prerequisite for field work. Neither a brewer placement nor a service visit may be created for a site whose `recommended_model_id` is still null; both are refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_PLANNED` (HTTP 422).

### 3.3. Fleet Assets, Brewer Placement and Asset Recovery

**Serialized assets.** A `ProgramAdmin` registers each physical brewer in the fleet with the `model_id` of its catalog model, a `serial_number` that is non-empty and unique across the fleet (a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER`, HTTP 409), and an `asset_role` of either `PLACEMENT` or `LOANER`. The role is a hard partition: only a `PLACEMENT` asset may be installed at a site, and only a `LOANER` asset may be attached to a work order as a substitute unit. Using an asset in the wrong capacity in either direction is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A newly registered asset starts with `asset_status` `IN_STOCK`, `reconditioning_until` null, and `last_descale_at` stamped with the current instant — which is why a test that needs a descale-overdue asset registers it at an earlier clock position and then advances the clock.

The fleet state dimension `asset_status` takes the values `IN_STOCK`, `PLACED`, `ON_LOAN`, `RECONDITIONING`, `UNRETURNED` and `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal. Installing a brewer moves it `IN_STOCK` → `PLACED`, and requires the asset to be `IN_STOCK` and to carry the `PLACEMENT` role; an asset in any other fleet state is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). When the placement holding an asset is superseded, that asset returns `PLACED` → `IN_STOCK` and is immediately reusable. Recording retrieval of a serviceable brewer moves it `PLACED` → `RECONDITIONING` with a sanitation hold; recording retrieval of an unserviceable one moves it `PLACED` → `RETIRED`. Invoice draft assembly that bills a placement as unreturned moves the asset `PLACED` → `UNRETURNED`, and if the equipment later turns up, recording its retrieval moves it `UNRETURNED` → `RECONDITIONING` (or, when it comes back unserviceable, to `RETIRED`; retrieval accepts an asset in either `PLACED` or `UNRETURNED`). A loaner asset takes a parallel path: attaching it to a work order moves it `IN_STOCK` → `ON_LOAN`, returning it moves it `ON_LOAN` → `RECONDITIONING`, and writing it off during invoice assembly moves it `ON_LOAN` → `UNRETURNED`. Finally, a `RECONDITIONING` asset whose hold has expired becomes `IN_STOCK` again with `reconditioning_until` cleared to null — and that promotion is **lazy**, evaluated when a loaner swap next reaches for the asset rather than the instant the clock passes the hold.

**The sanitation hold** is one calendar day long. Whenever an asset enters `RECONDITIONING`, its `reconditioning_until` is set to one day after the moment that put it there — the current instant for a retrieved placement asset, the reported return date for a returned loaner. While the current instant is before `reconditioning_until`, the asset is under sanitation hold and may not be re-issued.

**Placement terms.** An `AccountManager` installs a brewer at a planned site by supplying the `site_id`, the `asset_id` of the fleet unit, a `terms_type` of `LOAN` or `RENTAL`, a `minimum_purchase_cents` of at least 0, a `min_term_months` between 1 and 60 inclusive, and the `expected_site_version` required by §2.7. Under `LOAN` terms the brewer is placed free of rent against a minimum purchase commitment; under `RENTAL` terms monthly rent is charged and the shortfall consequence is different. A `LOAN` placement's `minimum_purchase_cents` must be at least 75000 — seven hundred fifty dollars of trailing purchase per compliance window — and a `LOAN` placement declaring less is rejected with `ERR_MINIMUM_BELOW_LOAN_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). `RENTAL` placements have no such floor and may declare a commitment of zero.

Creation stamps `placed_at` with the current instant, starts `placement_status` at `ACTIVE` and `compliance_status` at `MEETING_MINIMUM`, sets `trailing_purchase_cents`, `shortfall_fee_cents`, `rent_charge_cents` and `unreturned_billed_cents` to 0, and leaves the placement's own `retrieval_due_by` null. In the same operation the asset moves to `PLACED`, the site's `version` increments by 1, and — if this is the first installed equipment on the account — the account leaves `ONBOARDING` for `ACTIVE`.

Creation also **supersedes**. If the site already has a placement in `ACTIVE`, that prior placement moves `ACTIVE` → `SUPERSEDED` and its asset returns to `IN_STOCK`. This is exactly why the write is guarded on the site's `version`: a client working from a stale site view would retire a brewer contract it had never observed.

**The installation dimension** `placement_status` takes the values `ACTIVE`, `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`, `RETRIEVED`, `UNRETURNED_BILLED` and `SUPERSEDED`, with `RETRIEVED` and `SUPERSEDED` terminal. Cancellation of the owning account moves an `ACTIVE` placement to `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` and copies the account's `retrieval_due_by` onto the placement, incrementing the placement's `version` by 1 and suspending the site as its second hop. Invoice draft assembly run while the current instant is past a `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` placement's own `retrieval_due_by` moves it `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` → `UNRETURNED_BILLED`, writes the computed charge to `unreturned_billed_cents`, appends an `UNRETURNED_ASSET` invoice line for that amount to the draft, and moves the asset to `UNRETURNED`.

**Recording retrieval** is an `AccountManager` action carrying a single transient input, `retrieved_asset_condition`, whose value is `SERVICEABLE` or `UNSERVICEABLE`. The value is used to route the asset and is not persisted on any entity. Retrieval requires the placement to be in `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` or `UNRETURNED_BILLED`; from any other installation state it is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The placement moves to `RETRIEVED`. When the reported condition is `UNSERVICEABLE` the asset moves to `RETIRED`; otherwise the asset moves to `RECONDITIONING` with `reconditioning_until` set one day past the current instant. If this was the account's last non-terminal placement and the account is `CANCELLATION_PENDING`, the account closes and its suspended sites close with it, as described in §3.1.

One asymmetry is deliberate and must not be smoothed over: retrieving a placement that was already billed as unreturned does **not** automatically credit the issued charge. The `UNRETURNED_ASSET` line stays on the issued invoice, and a `BillingSpecialist` must post a compensating credit line explicitly. Recording retrieval of the equipment and refunding the customer are two separate decisions made by two separate roles.

**Pricing an asset that never came back.** The unreturned-asset charge is computed during invoice draft assembly, both for a `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` placement whose retrieval deadline has passed and for a loaner written off in the same pass. The age anchor differs by case: for a placement asset it is the placement's `placed_at`; for a written-off loaner it is the work order's `loaner_out_date`. The number of months in service is the elapsed time from the anchor to the current instant divided by 30 days, rounded down to a whole number. The depreciated proportion is 10000 basis points reduced by 150 basis points for each whole month in service — one and a half percent per month — but never allowed below a residual floor of 3000 basis points, so an asset never depreciates past thirty percent of its list value no matter how old it is. The asset's residual value in cents is the model's `list_value_cents` multiplied by that depreciated proportion, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The charge finally billed is that residual value plus a flat retrieval trip fee of 7500 cents, and the sum is what lands in the placement's `unreturned_billed_cents` and on the `UNRETURNED_ASSET` line. The model is always reached through the asset's own `model_id`.

### 3.4. Route Capacity and Replenishment Scheduling

Replenishment happens on routes, and a route is a finite thing: one weekday, one pool of technician minutes per calendar service date, and one technician. Scheduling a stop is therefore a capacity negotiation as much as a calendar entry.

An `AccountManager` or a `ProgramAdmin` schedules a stop by supplying the `site_id` together with the service window — an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end` that must be strictly later than the start — and the `planned_minutes` the stop will consume, between 1 and 480 inclusive. The visit's `route_id` is **copied from the site's own route** and is never client-supplied; nor is `visit_status`, which starts at `SCHEDULED`, nor `lines`, which starts as an empty array. Scheduling adds the visit's `planned_minutes` to the route's `booked_minutes_total`. The visit's `shortfall_flag` starts false, and `completed_at` and `void_reason` start null.

Scheduling is gated in a fixed order. The owning account's posture is checked first, so a cancelling account's site cannot receive a new stop (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPERATING`). The site's own posture is checked next: scheduling requires a service posture of `ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED`, and a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` site is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. Window validity comes third: `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, both instants must be minute-aligned, and `window_start` must fall on the route's own `service_day` when evaluated in UTC. Any violation yields `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422) — a Wednesday window on a `MON` route is invalid however much capacity is free. Window collision is checked fourth, then the site-planned prerequisite of §3.2, and route day capacity last. The consequence of that ordering matters: a candidate window that both collides with a neighbouring stop and would blow the day's capacity surfaces `ERR_ROUTE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and a stop on an unplanned site whose window is malformed surfaces `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID` rather than `ERR_SITE_NOT_PLANNED`.

**Window collision** is evaluated against the other visits of the **same route only**; the same site may be visited by another route on the same day without conflict, and visits that are `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` are ignored entirely. The candidate interval is widened symmetrically by the route's `travel_buffer_minutes` on both sides: it begins that many minutes before the requested `window_start` and ends that many minutes after the requested `window_end`, and it is treated as half-open — inclusive of its start instant, exclusive of its end instant. Each existing `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` visit of the route occupies its own un-buffered half-open interval from its `window_start` inclusive to its `window_end` exclusive. The candidate collides with an existing visit when the candidate's start is strictly before that visit's end **and** that visit's start is strictly before the candidate's end. Because both intervals are half-open, exact abutment is never a collision. With the default 30-minute buffer, an existing stop ending at 09:45 leaves a new stop starting at 10:15 free — the buffered candidate begins exactly at 09:45, which abuts rather than overlaps — while a new stop starting at 10:14 collides. A collision is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**Day capacity** is evaluated on the UTC calendar date of `window_start`, and only that date, even when `window_end` falls on the following day. The booked minutes for a day are the sum of `planned_minutes` over the visits of that route whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and whose `window_start` falls on the same UTC date. Scheduling is rejected with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) when those booked minutes plus the new stop's `planned_minutes` would exceed the route's `capacity_minutes`. Exact equality is accepted — a day may be filled to the minute. Minutes are released immediately in both directions: a visit that is `CANCELLED` frees its minutes, and a visit that is `VOIDED` after completion frees them too, so neither status ever contributes to load. The route's `booked_minutes_total` is the same sum taken across every day of the route rather than a single date.

The same day-load arithmetic is exposed as a computed read for a `ProgramAdmin`, `AccountManager` or `RouteTechnician`: supplying a `service_date` returns that date's `service_date`, `booked_minutes_day`, `remaining_minutes` and the route's `capacity_minutes`, where the remaining minutes are the route's capacity minus the day's booked minutes.

**Rescheduling** lets an `AccountManager` or `ProgramAdmin` move a stop's window by supplying a replacement `window_start` and `window_end`. It requires the visit to be `SCHEDULED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Both window validity and window collision are re-evaluated against the new window, and the day capacity check runs again on the new date. Because `planned_minutes` is not changed by a reschedule, the route's `booked_minutes_total` is unchanged by the operation.

**Requested lines** are appended to a scheduled stop, one product at a time, by the `RouteTechnician` on that visit's own route — and by nobody else. The payload carries a `product_id` and a `requested_units` quantity, and appends an element to the visit's `lines` array holding that `product_id` and `requested_units`; the delivery-side members of the element stay unset until completion. Two coherence rules guard the append: the product must actually be stocked at the site, meaning a par line exists for that site-and-product pair (otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_STOCKED`, HTTP 422), and a visit may carry at most one line per product (otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_VISIT_LINE`, HTTP 422). Appending requires the visit to still be `SCHEDULED`, and a technician from a different route is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ON_ROUTE` before either coherence rule is reached.

### 3.5. Visit Completion, the Shelf Ledger and the Escalation Chain

Completing a stop is the busiest operation in the system. It is the head of a four-hop cascade that runs shelf ledger → site posture → account posture → price adjustments, and it is the only place where consumption is modelled and prices are snapshotted.

The `RouteTechnician` on the visit's own route reports the stop with a `delivered_lines` array whose elements each carry a `product_id` and a `delivered_units` count. Only products already present on the visit's `lines` array may be reported, each reported quantity must be at least 0 and must not exceed that line's `requested_units`, and any violation of either — including naming a product absent from the visit's lines — is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERED_EXCEEDS_REQUESTED` (HTTP 422). A stocked line that is simply **omitted** from `delivered_lines` is not an error: it completes with a delivered quantity of 0, which is how a truck that arrived empty is recorded.

Four gates precede the arithmetic, in this order: the technician must own the visit's route (`ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ON_ROUTE`); the site's service posture must be `ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED`, so a suspended site refuses completion with `ERR_SITE_NOT_SERVICEABLE` even for a visit that is otherwise perfectly schedulable; the visit must be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`; and the window must have opened, meaning the current instant is at or after `window_start` — an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_VISIT_NOT_YET_STARTED` (HTTP 422). Only then are the delivered quantities validated.

**Consumption is debited before the delivery is credited**, and that order is pinned. For each element of the visit's `lines`, in ascending order of the referenced par line's `created_at`, the system first computes how much the break room drank since it was last served. The elapsed days are the time from the par line's `last_service_at` to the visit's `window_start`, divided by 86400 seconds, rounded down to a whole number of days. The modelled daily draw is the par line's `par_units` divided by 14, carried as an exact rational value with no intermediate rounding — a two-week cover target consumed evenly. The modelled consumption is the daily draw multiplied by the elapsed days, rounded down to whole units, and then clamped to the shelf quantity: the consumed quantity is the smaller of the current `on_hand_units` and that modelled figure, so the shelf can never be driven negative. The par line's new `on_hand_units` is its previous value less the consumed quantity plus the delivered quantity. Processing order affects only the cumulative counters' observable sequence; it never changes a per-line result.

**Each line element is then stamped** with four computed members. `consumed_units` receives the modelled consumption above. `delivered_units` receives the reported quantity, or 0 when the product was absent from `delivered_lines`. `effective_unit_price_micros` receives the effective price described below. `resulting_on_hand_units` receives the par line's shelf quantity **after** the write.

**Effective pricing** is a snapshot, taken at the instant of completion against the account owning the visit's site. When the product's `green_coffee_linked` flag is true, the effective unit price in micro-dollars is the product's `base_unit_price_micros` multiplied by the account's `green_price_factor_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole micro-dollars. When the flag is false, the effective unit price is the `base_unit_price_micros` unchanged. That snapshot is authoritative forever afterwards: invoice assembly and trailing-purchase rollups **must reuse** it and must **never** recompute a price at invoice time, even if the account's factor has since moved.

**Cascade hop one — the shelf ledger.** For each par line the visit touched, `cumulative_consumed_units` increases by that line's consumed quantity, `cumulative_delivered_units` increases by that line's delivered quantity, `on_hand_units` is re-derived as cumulative delivered less cumulative consumed, `last_service_at` is set to the visit's `window_start`, and `stock_status` is recomputed against the reorder point — becoming `STOCKOUT` whenever the re-derived shelf quantity is exactly 0. The identity that `on_hand_units` always equals `cumulative_delivered_units` minus `cumulative_consumed_units` is an invariant of this system and must hold before and after every operation, with no drift.

The visit's `shortfall_flag` is set true when at least one line completed with a delivered quantity strictly below its requested quantity, and false otherwise. When the flag is true, the site's `consecutive_shortfall_visit_count` increases by 1; when the flag is false, that counter is reset to 0. `completed_at` is stamped with the current instant and `visit_status` becomes `COMPLETED`.

**Cascade hop two — the site posture.** The site's `escalation_score` is recomputed from the new shelf state, and if it crosses the threshold the site moves `ACTIVE` → `ESCALATED`. **Hop three** recomputes the owning account's `escalated_site_count`, which may carry the account `ACTIVE` → `AT_RISK`. **Hop four** defers every `PROPOSED` price adjustment of that account. All four hops complete before the response is returned.

**The escalation score** is a weighted count of things going wrong at a break room, carried to one decimal place. Four counts feed it. The **stockout count** is the number of par lines of the site whose `on_hand_units` is exactly 0 **and** whose `cumulative_delivered_units` is above 0 — a line that has never been delivered to at all is merely unstocked, not a stockout, which is why a freshly planned site never escalates on its own. The **below-reorder count** is the number of par lines whose `on_hand_units` is above 0 and at or below `reorder_point_units`. The **overdue-descale count** is the number of assets installed at the site through a placement whose installation status is `ACTIVE` for which the descale clock has run out, meaning the asset's `last_descale_at` advanced by the model's `descale_interval_days` is at or before the current instant; placements that are `RETRIEVED` or `SUPERSEDED` never contribute. The **missed-visit count** is the number of service visits of the site whose status is `CANCELLED` and whose `window_start` is at or after the current instant less 28 days.

The raw score is the stockout count multiplied by 20.0, plus the below-reorder count multiplied by 7.5, plus the overdue-descale count multiplied by 15.0, plus the missed-visit count multiplied by 10.0. The stored `escalation_score` is the smaller of 100.0 and that raw score. Because every weight is a multiple of one half, the score is exact and needs no rounding. A site moves `ACTIVE` → `ESCALATED` when its recomputed score is at or above 40.0 and it was `ACTIVE`; it moves `ESCALATED` → `ACTIVE` when its recomputed score is strictly below 40.0 and it was `ESCALATED`. The comparison against 40.0 is exact: a score of 39.5 stays `ACTIVE`, and a score of exactly 40.0 escalates. Two stockouts alone (40.0) escalate a site; one stockout plus two below-reorder lines (35.0) do not.

An `AccountManager` or `BillingSpecialist` may also recompute a site's posture directly, without waiting for a visit. Doing so writes the freshly computed `escalation_score`, applies the `ACTIVE` ↔ `ESCALATED` transition in whichever direction the score demands, leaves the posture unchanged when neither direction applies (an idempotent no-op), recomputes the account's `escalated_site_count`, and may therefore move the account `ACTIVE` → `AT_RISK` — deferring its `PROPOSED` adjustments — or `AT_RISK` → `ACTIVE`, restoring them. The site's `version` increments by 1 **only** when the service posture actually changed.

**Voiding a completed stop** is the compensating action, reserved to `ProgramAdmin`, and it exists precisely because the shelf ledger must never drift. It requires the visit to be `COMPLETED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`. It is additionally refused, as a conflict carrying `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), once any consolidated invoice of this site's account whose status is anything other than `VOID` covers the visit's window — specifically, an invoice whose `period_start` is at or before the visit's `window_start` and whose `period_end` is at or after it. Once money has gone out the door on a period, that period's history is frozen.

A void stamps `void_reason` and moves `visit_status` to `VOIDED`. Every par line the visit touched has both cumulative counters **reduced** by that line's recorded amounts — `cumulative_delivered_units` by the line's delivered quantity and `cumulative_consumed_units` by the line's consumed quantity — and `on_hand_units` re-derived from them, so the unit invariant holds with no drift. Each affected par line's `last_service_at` reverts to the `window_start` of the most recent remaining `COMPLETED` visit that touched that line, or, if none remains, to that par line's own `created_at`. The route's `booked_minutes_total` is reduced by the visit's `planned_minutes`. Finally the site's `consecutive_shortfall_visit_count` is recomputed over the remaining `COMPLETED` visits and its `escalation_score` is recomputed, which may move the site `ESCALATED` → `ACTIVE` and, in turn, the account `AT_RISK` → `ACTIVE` with its deferred adjustments restored to `PROPOSED`.

### 3.6. Brewer Maintenance, Descale Discipline and Loaner Circulation

A brewer in a break room needs three kinds of attention, and a work order records all three. A `RouteTechnician` or a `ProgramAdmin` opens one against a placed brewer by supplying the `asset_id` and a `wo_type` of `PREVENTIVE` (the scheduled check), `DESCALE` (which clears the descale clock), or `REPAIR` (corrective work). The work order's `site_id` is **copied from the site of that asset's `ACTIVE` placement** and is never client-supplied. A new work order starts with `wo_status` `OPEN`, `loaner_status` `NONE`, `awaiting_parts_cycles` 0, and `parts_eta`, `resolution_note`, `loaner_asset_id`, `loaner_out_date`, `loaner_due_back_date` and `loaner_returned_on` all null. When a `RouteTechnician` acts on a work order, that technician's route must be the route of the work order's site; otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ON_ROUTE`.

**The service dimension.** `wo_status` takes the values `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_PARTS` and `RESOLVED`, with `RESOLVED` terminal. A `RouteTechnician` drives it by naming a `to_status`, and only four transitions are declared: `OPEN` → `IN_PROGRESS`, `IN_PROGRESS` → `AWAITING_PARTS`, `AWAITING_PARTS` → `IN_PROGRESS`, and `IN_PROGRESS` → `RESOLVED`. Any other requested pair — including anything at all out of `RESOLVED` — is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The middle pair is a true cycle: a technician may bounce between working and waiting for parts repeatedly.

Entering `AWAITING_PARTS` requires a `parts_eta` instant recording when the parts are expected, stamps it, and increases `awaiting_parts_cycles` by 1. That counter is capped: the work order may enter `AWAITING_PARTS` at most three times, so a fourth attempt — one made while `awaiting_parts_cycles` already stands at 3 — is rejected with `ERR_AWAITING_PARTS_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). The `parts_eta` is recorded afresh on **each** entry, so the field always reflects the current wait rather than the first one.

Resolving a work order stamps `resolution_note`. Two preconditions guard it beyond the declared transition. First, the loaner dimension must be settled: resolution requires `loaner_status` to be `NONE`, `RETURNED`, or `WRITTEN_OFF`, and a work order whose substitute brewer is still out is refused with `ERR_LOANER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Second — and only for a `PREVENTIVE` order — the brewer must not be descale-overdue: the elapsed time from the asset's `last_descale_at` to the current instant must not exceed the model's `descale_interval_days`. When it does, the preventive order cannot be signed off and is rejected with `ERR_DESCALE_OVERDUE` (HTTP 422); a `DESCALE` work order must be resolved first. `REPAIR` orders are not subject to the descale gate.

Resolving a `DESCALE` order sets the asset's `last_descale_at` to the current instant, which is the only way the descale clock is ever reset. Any resolution then recomputes the site's `escalation_score`, because clearing an overdue descale removes 15.0 points from it; that recomputation can move the site `ESCALATED` → `ACTIVE` and from there the account `AT_RISK` → `ACTIVE`, restoring the account's deferred price adjustments.

The two conditionally-required transition fields are checked **after** the descale gate and after the cycle cap. `parts_eta` is required when the requested status is `AWAITING_PARTS` and `resolution_note` is required when it is `RESOLVED`; omitting either where the requested transition needs it yields `ERR_MISSING_TRANSITION_FIELD` (HTTP 422).

**The loaner dimension** is independent of the service dimension. `loaner_status` takes the values `NONE`, `OUT`, `RETURNED`, `OVERDUE` and `WRITTEN_OFF`, with `WRITTEN_OFF` terminal. A swap is only permitted from `NONE`, a return only from `OUT` or `OVERDUE`, and a violation of either yields `ERR_LOANER_STATE_INVALID`.

A `RouteTechnician` attaches a substitute brewer by supplying the `loaner_asset_id` together with a `loaner_out_date` — the inclusive first date the loaner occupies, at date granularity, midnight UTC — and a `loaner_due_back_date`, the inclusive last date it may stay out. The work order must be in `OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `AWAITING_PARTS` for a swap to be meaningful. The requested asset must carry the `LOANER` role (otherwise `ERR_ASSET_ROLE_MISMATCH`), and it must not be under sanitation hold: an asset whose fleet status is `RECONDITIONING` and whose `reconditioning_until` is still ahead of the current instant is refused with `ERR_ASSET_UNDER_SANITATION_HOLD` (HTTP 422). Note the precedence — the sanitation hold is checked **before** the role check, so a `RECONDITIONING` asset carrying the wrong role surfaces the sanitation hold. A successful swap stamps `loaner_asset_id`, `loaner_out_date` and `loaner_due_back_date`, moves `loaner_status` to `OUT`, and moves the loaner asset to `ON_LOAN` — passing lazily through `IN_STOCK` first when the asset was `RECONDITIONING` with an expired hold.

**Loaner availability** uses deliberately different interval semantics from route scheduling, and the difference is the point. The candidate occupancy is a **closed** date interval running from `loaner_out_date` through `loaner_due_back_date`, both endpoints included, and `loaner_due_back_date` must not precede `loaner_out_date`. Every other work order that has ever held this same loaner asset is examined, whatever its service status. A work order whose loaner is `OUT`, `OVERDUE` or `WRITTEN_OFF` holds the asset from its own `loaner_out_date` onward with no end — a written-off loaner blocks forever, because the asset is gone. A work order whose loaner is `RETURNED` holds the asset from its `loaner_out_date` through its `loaner_returned_on` advanced by one reconditioning day, so the trailing sanitation day is blocked too. The candidate conflicts with a held interval when the candidate's start is at or before the held interval's end **and** the held interval's start is at or before the candidate's end. Because both intervals are closed, an abutting same-day handoff **is** a conflict — unlike the route rule, where abutment is free. A loaner returned on 2026-08-10 is unavailable on 2026-08-11 and free again on 2026-08-12, and the asset's `reconditioning_until` carries that same instant for readback. Any conflict, or a due-back date before the out date, is rejected with `ERR_LOANER_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).

**Returning a loaner** is reported by the `RouteTechnician` with a `returned_on` date that must not precede `loaner_out_date`. Before the return is applied, an overdue loaner is lazily promoted: if `loaner_status` is `OUT` and the current date is past `loaner_due_back_date`, the row first moves `OUT` → `OVERDUE`, so a late return still lands in `RETURNED` by way of the overdue state. Either way the return stamps `loaner_returned_on` with the reported date, moves `loaner_status` to `RETURNED`, and moves the loaner asset to `RECONDITIONING` with `reconditioning_until` set to the reported return date advanced by one day — blocking the next swap for exactly one calendar day.

**Writing off a loaner** happens during invoice draft assembly, never through a direct call. In that pass, any loaner still `OUT` whose due-back date has passed is first promoted to `OVERDUE`; then any `OVERDUE` loaner whose due-back date is more than 30 days behind the current date moves `OVERDUE` → `WRITTEN_OFF`, its asset moves to `UNRETURNED`, and an `UNRETURNED_ASSET` invoice line for the loaner is appended to the draft, priced by the unreturned-asset charge of §3.3 with the months-in-service measured from `loaner_out_date` rather than from a placement date.

### 3.7. Minimum-Purchase Compliance and Equipment Program Charges

A brewer placed under `LOAN` terms is not a gift; it is collateralised by a purchase commitment. A `BillingSpecialist` tests that commitment by evaluating a placement's compliance, which first measures what the site actually bought and then advances the placement one step around its compliance cycle. Evaluation requires the placement's installation status to be `ACTIVE` or `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED`; from `RETRIEVED`, `UNRETURNED_BILLED` or `SUPERSEDED` it is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A placement scheduled for retrieval is still evaluable, because a customer who is walking away still owes the commitment for the window it did not meet.

**The trailing purchase measure** looks back 90 days from the current instant, over a window that is left-exclusive and right-inclusive: a visit exactly 90 days old is outside the window, a visit at the current instant is inside it. Only the placement's **own site** contributes — a corporate account's other break rooms never subsidise this one — and only visits whose status is `COMPLETED` count. For every such visit, and for every line of that visit whose delivered quantity is above 0, the line's contribution in cents is its delivered units multiplied by its snapshotted `effective_unit_price_micros`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Each line is rounded individually and the rounded amounts are then summed. That total is written to the placement's `trailing_purchase_cents` on every evaluation, whichever way the compliance state moves. Rounding per line rather than on the sum is not a stylistic choice: it makes a placement's trailing purchases equal, to the cent, the sum of the corresponding `PRODUCT` invoice lines, and the product's *current* catalog price is never consulted — only the price snapshotted at completion.

**The compliance dimension** cycles. `compliance_status` takes the values `MEETING_MINIMUM`, `SHORTFALL_WARNED` and `SHORTFALL_BILLED`, with no terminal state, and it evolves entirely independently of the installation dimension. When the measured trailing purchase is strictly below the placement's `minimum_purchase_cents`, a placement in `MEETING_MINIMUM` moves to `SHORTFALL_WARNED` and **no fee at all is accrued on the first miss** — the first shortfall is a warning. A second consecutive miss moves `SHORTFALL_WARNED` → `SHORTFALL_BILLED` and accrues the fee into `shortfall_fee_cents`. When the measured trailing purchase is at or above the commitment, the placement returns to `MEETING_MINIMUM` from either `SHORTFALL_WARNED` or `SHORTFALL_BILLED`, closing the cycle so it can run again. Returning from `SHORTFALL_BILLED` clears `shortfall_fee_cents` back to 0 **only if** no `SHORTFALL` invoice line has yet been emitted for that accrual; once the fee has reached an invoice it is the invoice's business, not the placement's. Returning from `SHORTFALL_WARNED` has no fee to clear. Each evaluation increments the placement's `version` by 1, and an evaluation on a placement already `MEETING_MINIMUM` and still above its commitment is an idempotent no-op that only refreshes `trailing_purchase_cents`.

**The equipment program charge** has two components, and which one is live depends on the placement's `terms_type`. The unmet commitment is the placement's `minimum_purchase_cents` less its `trailing_purchase_cents`, floored at 0 so a site above its commitment has an unmet commitment of exactly zero.

Under **`LOAN`** terms there is no rent whatsoever — `rent_charge_cents` stays 0 and a loan placement never produces a `RENT` invoice line — and the shortfall fee is 3500 basis points, thirty-five percent, of the unmet commitment: the unmet commitment multiplied by 3500, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Because the fee is proportional to the gap, the charge grows as purchases fall.

Under **`RENTAL`** terms both components behave differently. Rent is prorated: it is the model's `monthly_rent_cents` multiplied by the number of days the placement was active in the billed period — capped at 30, the proration denominator — divided by 30, rounded half up. The days active are a plain date difference with no inclusive-day adjustment: the later of the period's start and the placement's `placed_at`, taken as a date, subtracted from the earlier of the period's end and the current instant, taken as a date. The shortfall component under rental terms is 0 when the unmet commitment is exactly 0; otherwise it is a flat surcharge of 2500 basis points, twenty-five percent, of the **full** monthly rent — the model's `monthly_rent_cents` multiplied by 2500, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The surcharge is keyed on the full monthly rent and **never** on the prorated `rent_charge_cents`, which is precisely what makes the shortfall component period-independent and evaluable outside any invoice period. Identical commitment and trailing-purchase figures therefore yield a completely different shortfall fee under the two terms types.

Each component is rounded half up exactly once. The rent component only enters an invoice when the placement's installation status is `ACTIVE` or `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` during the billed period; the shortfall component only enters an invoice when the placement's compliance status is `SHORTFALL_BILLED` and its accrued `shortfall_fee_cents` is above 0.

### 3.8. Invoice Assembly, Issuance and Credits

Invoicing is where every other part of this system settles up. A `BillingSpecialist` assembles a draft for one closed billing period by supplying the `account_id`, the period bounds `period_start` (the inclusive first instant) and `period_end` (the inclusive last instant, which must be strictly later than the start), the `expected_account_version` required by §2.7, and — conditionally — a `site_id`.

**Scope is decided by the account's `billing_mode`.** A `CONSOLIDATED` account is invoiced whole, so `site_id` must be **absent**; a `PER_SITE` account is invoiced one break room at a time, so `site_id` must be **present**. Getting this backwards in either direction is rejected with `ERR_BILLING_MODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). When a `site_id` is supplied it must name a site whose owning account is the invoice's own account; a site belonging to a different customer yields `ERR_SITE_NOT_IN_ACCOUNT` (HTTP 422). For a `PER_SITE` account only the named site's lines are in scope; for a `CONSOLIDATED` account every site of the account is.

**Period rules.** The period must be well-formed and must be **closed**: `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, and `period_end` must be at or before the current instant. A period that has not finished yet cannot be invoiced, and attempting it yields `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID` (HTTP 422). No other invoice whose status is anything but `VOID` may cover an overlapping period for the same scope — the same account for a `CONSOLIDATED` invoice, the same site for a `PER_SITE` one. Periods are treated as closed intervals, so sharing even a single endpoint instant counts as an overlap; a violation yields `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Within a single request these checks run in a fixed order: the stale-account rejection first, then period well-formedness, then period overlap, then site-in-account, then billing-mode scope, and the empty-invoice rejection last.

**Draft assembly is not a read-only projection.** It writes. The pass produces, in scope:

- One **`PRODUCT`** line per delivered visit line, drawn from visits whose status is `COMPLETED`, whose `window_start` falls inside the closed period, and whose delivered quantity is above 0. Voided and cancelled visits contribute nothing. Each such line carries the `product_id`, a `qty_units` equal to the delivered quantity, and a `unit_price_micros` copied straight from the `effective_unit_price_micros` snapshotted on the visit line at completion — never recomputed at invoice time.
- One **`RENT`** line per `RENTAL` placement billable in the period whose prorated `rent_charge_cents` is non-zero. `LOAN` placements never produce a `RENT` line.
- One **`SHORTFALL`** line per placement whose compliance status is `SHORTFALL_BILLED` and whose accrued `shortfall_fee_cents` is above 0.
- One **`UNRETURNED_ASSET`** line for every `RETRIEVAL_SCHEDULED` placement whose own `retrieval_due_by` is behind the current instant — moving that placement to `UNRETURNED_BILLED`, writing `unreturned_billed_cents`, and moving its asset to `UNRETURNED` — and one more for every loaner written off in the same pass, as described in §3.6.
- One negative **`ALLIED_DISCOUNT`** line per site with a non-zero discount result.
- At most one **`FREIGHT`** line, and only when the computed freight is above 0.

Every line records the `invoice_id` of its owning invoice and the `site_id` the charge belongs to. `product_id` is populated on `PRODUCT` lines and null on every other line type, and it is an identity reference only — never a decision input. `qty_units` and `unit_price_micros` are 0 on every line type other than `PRODUCT`. `target_line_id` and `credit_reason` are null on every line type other than `CREDIT`, and `credits_applied_cents` starts at 0 on every line. `amount_cents` is the line's signed amount in cents, negative for `ALLIED_DISCOUNT` and `CREDIT` lines and positive for the rest. A line's `site_id` is null only for a `CREDIT` line that credits a line which itself had no site.

**Per-line rounding, then per-site summation.** A `PRODUCT` line's `amount_cents` is its `qty_units` multiplied by its `unit_price_micros`, divided by 10000, rounded half up — **individually, per line**. Only after each line has been rounded are the rounded amounts summed to form a site's product subtotal, and the site's discount is then rounded once against that subtotal. Summing micro-dollar products across a site and rounding once at the end is explicitly forbidden: it differs by cents whenever two or more lines of a site carry a fractional-cent unit price. Rounding therefore happens exactly twice per site — once per line, once on that site's discount — and never on a cross-site sum.

**The assembly evaluation order is pinned**, because the discount depends on a total and the freight waiver depends on a total while both change the total. It runs in eight steps.

1. Every `PRODUCT`, `RENT`, `SHORTFALL` and `UNRETURNED_ASSET` line is rounded individually as described above.
2. `pre_freight_total_cents` is the sum of all of those line amounts, across all sites and all four line types, before any discount and before any freight.
3. `allied_category_count` is the number of **distinct** product categories other than `COFFEE` appearing among this invoice's `PRODUCT` lines.
4. `allied_discount_bps` is selected from that count and from the step-2 total, in this precedence: 500 basis points when the allied category count is at least 3 **and** the pre-freight total is at least 100000 cents; otherwise 300 basis points when the allied category count is at least 3 **and** the pre-freight total is at least 50000 cents; otherwise 100 basis points when the allied category count is exactly 2; otherwise 0. The tier is keyed on the step-2 value and never on a post-discount total.
5. `allied_discount_cents` is the sum, over the sites in scope, of each site's product subtotal multiplied by `allied_discount_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. One negative `ALLIED_DISCOUNT` line is written per site whose result is non-zero, and the stored `allied_discount_cents` is the absolute value of the sum of those lines.
6. `freight_cents` is 0 when `pre_freight_total_cents` is at or above 50000, and otherwise a flat 2500 cents. This too is keyed on the step-2 value, which has an observable consequence: an invoice that clears the free-freight threshold **before** the discount keeps its free freight even when the discount drops the final total below the threshold. A `FREIGHT` line is written only when the freight is above 0.
7. `total_cents` is `pre_freight_total_cents` less `allied_discount_cents` plus `freight_cents`.
8. `line_count` is the number of invoice lines written. An assembly that produced zero lines is rejected with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES` (HTTP 422) — there is no such thing as an empty invoice.

The draft is written with `invoice_status` `DRAFT`, `issued_at` null, and the account's `version` incremented by 1. Everything below `PRODUCT`, `RENT`, `SHORTFALL`, `UNRETURNED_ASSET`, `ALLIED_DISCOUNT` and `FREIGHT` is synthesized rather than posted by a client, so the invoice-line listing — filterable by `invoice_id`, by `site_id`, by `target_line_id`, by `line_type` and by `product_id` — is the only discovery channel for them.

**The invoice lifecycle.** `invoice_status` takes the values `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`, `FULLY_CREDITED` and `VOID`, of which `VOID` and `FULLY_CREDITED` are terminal. Issuance requires `DRAFT`; crediting requires `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`; `VOID` and `FULLY_CREDITED` accept neither. Any mismatch yields `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A `DRAFT` invoice is additionally moved to `VOID` as a side effect of cancelling its account, as described in §3.1.

**Issuing** a draft moves the invoice to `ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at` with the current instant, adds the invoice's `total_cents` to the account's `receivable_balance_cents`, recomputes the account's `allied_attachment_bps` per §3.1, and clears `shortfall_fee_cents` to 0 on every placement whose `SHORTFALL` line was emitted on this invoice — the accrual has now been billed and must not be billed twice. Issuance additionally requires the owning account not to be `CLOSED`, refusing a closed account with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPERATING` (HTTP 409), and requires the invoice to carry at least one line, refusing an empty one with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES`.

**Crediting** is the only way to reverse a charge on an issued invoice, and it is the only remedy when unreturned equipment turns up after it was written off. A `BillingSpecialist` posts a credit against exactly one line by supplying that line's `target_line_id`, a positive `amount_cents`, and a `credit_reason`. The target line must belong to **this** invoice and must not itself be a `CREDIT` line; a violation of either yields `ERR_CREDIT_TARGET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The credit amount must be above 0, and the cumulative credited amount across the invoice must not exceed the invoice's pre-credit `total_cents`; a violation of either yields `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE_TOTAL` (HTTP 422).

A successful credit appends a new invoice line whose `line_type` is `CREDIT`, whose `amount_cents` is the **negation** of the requested amount, whose `target_line_id` points at the credited line, and whose `credit_reason` carries the supplied reason. The target line's `credits_applied_cents` increases by the requested amount. The invoice's `total_cents` is recomputed as the sum of all of its lines — with the negative `ALLIED_DISCOUNT` and `CREDIT` lines counted as negative — and its `line_count` increases by 1. The account's `receivable_balance_cents` decreases by the credited amount. The invoice then moves to `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` when the cumulative credits after this one are strictly less than the pre-credit total, and to `FULLY_CREDITED` when they exactly equal it, at which point the invoice's total is 0 and the invoice is terminal. A second and subsequent partial credit is permitted and simply keeps the invoice in `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`; the credit operation returns the newly created credit line rather than the invoice.

### 3.9. Green Coffee Repricing and the Negotiation Cycle

Green coffee is a traded commodity, and the price we quoted at signing is a bet on it. When the market moves far enough, a `BillingSpecialist` proposes a price adjustment — and because the customer has to live with the result, the customer, and only the customer, gets to accept or dispute it.

A proposal names the `account_id` and an `effective_at` date on which the new prices take effect if the customer accepts. Everything else is computed. `index_baseline_micros` is snapshotted from the account's own `green_index_baseline_micros`; this is a **per-account** baseline, not a global one, so two customers signed a year apart can face very different movements from the same market quote. `index_current_micros` is snapshotted from the market quote most recently set through the market capability of §2.5.

**Movement** is the signed difference between the current index and the baseline, multiplied by 10000 and divided by the baseline, rounded half up, and is stored in `movement_bps`. Its sign is preserved: a falling market produces a negative movement and, ultimately, a price reduction. A proposal is only admissible when the **magnitude** of that movement is at least 800 basis points — an eight-percent move in either direction. A movement of 799 basis points is not adjustable and is rejected with `ERR_INDEX_MOVEMENT_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422); a movement of exactly 800 basis points is adjustable.

**Pass-through** is deliberately partial: we absorb some of the market. The raw pass-through is the movement multiplied by 6000 basis points — sixty percent — divided by 10000, rounded half up. The stored `adjustment_pct_bps` is that raw pass-through clamped to the closed range from -1500 to 1500 basis points, so no single adjustment can move a customer's prices by more than fifteen percent in either direction. The cap is applied last and never re-rounds, and the raw uncapped figure is not stored anywhere; the cap is observable only as an `adjustment_pct_bps` sitting exactly at plus or minus 1500 while the underlying movement would have justified more. A new proposal is created with `adj_status` `PROPOSED`, `revision_count` 0, `dispute_reason` null and `accepted_at` null, and nothing on the account changes yet.

**Price protection** prevents a customer from being repriced twice in quick succession. No new adjustment may be proposed while the account's `price_protection_until` is still ahead of the current instant; an attempt is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_PROTECTION_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). The protection window is set at acceptance, described below, and is null on a brand-new account.

**The negotiation dimension.** `adj_status` takes the values `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DISPUTED` and `DEFERRED`, and `ACCEPTED` is terminal. A `CustomerContact` responds by supplying a `disposition` of `ACCEPT` or `DISPUTE`; the contact's `X-User-ID` must equal the adjustment's own account, otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ACCOUNT_CONTACT`. Responding to an adjustment that is already `ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Responding to one that is `DEFERRED` is refused with its own dedicated code, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_DEFERRED` (HTTP 409), because the reason is different and worth telling the customer: the account is `AT_RISK` and the negotiation is frozen until service recovers. A `DISPUTE` disposition additionally requires a `dispute_reason`; omitting it yields `ERR_MISSING_TRANSITION_FIELD` (HTTP 422), checked after the state gates.

**Accepting** moves the adjustment `PROPOSED` → `ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` with the current instant, and reprices the account in four coordinated writes. The account's `green_price_factor_bps` becomes 10000 plus the adjustment's `adjustment_pct_bps` — an **absolute** figure measured against the unmodified baseline of 10000, never compounded onto the previous factor, so two successive acceptances of 1000 basis points each leave the factor at 11000 rather than 12100. The resulting factor is additionally never permitted below 5000 basis points, a floor the plus-or-minus 1500 cap makes unreachable in practice. The account's `green_index_baseline_micros` is **re-based** to the adjustment's `index_current_micros`, so the next proposal measures movement from here rather than from the original signing index. The account's `price_protection_until` is set to the current instant advanced by the account's `price_protection_days`. And the account's `version` increments by 1 — which, per §2.7, is exactly why an invoice draft assembled against a stale account view is refused.

**Disputing** moves the adjustment `PROPOSED` → `DISPUTED`, stamps `dispute_reason`, and leaves every price on the account untouched.

**Revising** closes the negotiation cycle. A `BillingSpecialist` supplies a `revised_adjustment_pct_bps`, which replaces `adjustment_pct_bps`, increases `revision_count` by 1, and moves the adjustment `DISPUTED` → `PROPOSED` so the customer can look again. Revision requires the adjustment to be `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The revised figure may only move **toward** the customer: it must lie in the closed interval between 0 and the current `adjustment_pct_bps` inclusive — so for a current increase of 1200 basis points any value from 0 through 1200 is acceptable and 1201 is not, and for a current reduction of -1200 basis points the mirrored bound applies, admitting any value from -1200 through 0. A figure outside that interval is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422). The `PROPOSED` → `DISPUTED` → `PROPOSED` cycle has no fixed round limit; `revision_count` simply records how many rounds it has taken.

**Deferral** is not a customer or operator action at all. When the account moves `ACTIVE` → `AT_RISK` because two or more of its break rooms are escalated, every adjustment of that account in `PROPOSED` moves to `DEFERRED`, and while deferred the customer may not respond. When the account recovers to `ACTIVE`, every `DEFERRED` adjustment of that account moves back to `PROPOSED` and the negotiation resumes exactly where it left off. Nothing about the adjustment's figures changes across a deferral round trip.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Status codes follow one rule throughout: an operation that brings a **new** resource into existence answers with **201 Created**, and every other successful operation — reads, state transitions, computed refreshes, field updates, and the two test-driving capabilities — answers with **200 OK**. Note the two places where this rule is less obvious than it looks: assembling an invoice draft creates a resource and therefore answers 201, while issuing or voiding one modifies an existing resource and answers 200; and posting a credit creates a new invoice line and therefore answers 201, returning the created credit line rather than the invoice. Appending a requested line to a service visit is **not** a resource creation — it mutates the visit's `lines` array and returns the visit with 200 OK. The two test-driving capabilities answer 200 with an empty `data` object.

All collection endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3. In addition, the following filters are available as query parameters, each matching on exact equality: routes by `service_day`; brewer models by `brewer_class`; fleet assets by `model_id`, `asset_status` and `asset_role`; catalog products by `category` and `green_coffee_linked`; accounts by `program_posture` and `billing_mode`; sites by `account_id`, `route_id` and `service_posture`; par lines by `site_id`, `product_id`, `stock_status` and `source`; placements by `site_id`, `asset_id`, `placement_status`, `compliance_status` and `terms_type`; service visits by `site_id`, `route_id`, `visit_status` and `service_date`; work orders by `asset_id`, `site_id`, `loaner_asset_id`, `wo_status`, `loaner_status` and `wo_type`; invoices by `account_id`, `site_id` and `invoice_status`; invoice lines by `invoice_id`, `site_id`, `target_line_id`, `line_type` and `product_id`; and price adjustments by `account_id` and `adj_status`. The day-load read takes a required `service_date`.
