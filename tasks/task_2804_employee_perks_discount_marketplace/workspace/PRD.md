# Employee Perks & Discount Marketplace API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An employee perks programme provider sits between three constituencies whose interests only partly overlap. Merchant partners want distribution to a large, pre-qualified audience and are willing to give up margin for it, but only on terms they have negotiated line by line. Employer clients want a branded discount storefront their staff will actually use, want a cut of the economics where their contract grants one, and are legally and reputationally obliged never to learn what any individual employee bought. Employees want a discount to work at the till, and want recourse when it does not. This API is the system of record that holds all three sets of expectations simultaneously.

The service mounts under `/perks` and governs the whole commercial arc: recruiting a merchant, negotiating a partner agreement through a bounded sequence of proposals and counters, activating it (sometimes with category exclusivity in a region), publishing offers under it, selling storefront placement to those offers, letting employees reserve and merchants verify redemptions, computing the commission and the employer's revenue share on each verified line, settling those amounts into periodic statements, and adjudicating disputes when a merchant fails to honour what it advertised. Every one of those steps writes to a single append-only ledger, so at any instant the provider can say exactly what it is owed and what it owes.

Two design commitments run through the entire specification and should be read as constraints on every requirement that follows. First, **time is explicit**: nothing in this system reads the wall clock. A logical clock is set by the test harness and every window, deadline and time-to-live is evaluated against it, which makes expiry, staleness and decay reproducible rather than incidental. Second, **money is integral**: all monetary quantities are whole cents and all rates are basis points, and rounding happens at named, ordered points in each calculation rather than once at the end. Implementations that fuse arithmetic steps together, or that key a pricing tier on the wrong intermediate value, will produce amounts that differ from this specification by a few cents — and a few cents, repeated across a statement period, is a reconciliation failure.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere in the API: identity, envelope shape, pagination, timekeeping, common resource fields, monetary discipline, concurrency control, cascade semantics, the order in which rules are evaluated, and the privacy floor.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_REQUIRED`. If the declared role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Five roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly: `PlatformAdmin`, `PartnerManager`, `Merchant`, `EmployerAdmin`, and `Employee`.

Two of these roles are **staff roles whose identifier is opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` carries an opaque platform-staff identifier such as `admin-001`; it is not the `id` of any persisted entity and is never compared against one, so no ownership check ever applies to a `PlatformAdmin`. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerManager`, `X-User-ID` likewise carries an opaque provider-staff identifier such as `pm-001`, is never the `id` of a persisted entity, and is never scope-checked. The server may record such an identifier where a workflow captures who acted, but it never resolves it to a row.

The other three roles are **entity-backed**, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Merchant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting merchant partner; ownership checks on any agreement, offer, placement slot, redemption or dispute compare that row's `merchant_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `EmployerAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting employer client; ownership checks on any employer row, eligibility batch, membership or revenue share statement compare that row's `employer_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting employee's membership record; ownership checks on a membership compare the row's own `id`, and ownership checks on a redemption or a dispute compare the redemption's `membership_id` (or, on a dispute, its `raised_by_membership_id`) to `X-User-ID`. When a role is authorised for an endpoint but the targeted row belongs to a different principal under these rules, the rejection is `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, not `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Role separation is deliberate and in several places prevents a principal from completing a transaction alone. A `PartnerManager` may propose negotiation terms but may never counter its own proposal or accept a round it authored; a `Merchant` may counter and may accept the provider's standing proposal, but may never propose the opening round nor activate an agreement. A `Merchant` reserves a placement slot but cannot confirm it — confirmation belongs to the `PartnerManager`, so reservation and billing are split across two peer roles. A `PartnerManager` finalizes a revenue share statement but may never pay one, and a `PlatformAdmin` pays but may never finalize; the segregation of duties is intentional. An `EmployerAdmin` may not suspend its own storefront (only the platform may freeze a programme), may not create or verify redemptions, and may not read an individual redemption or dispute at all. An `Employee` may neither verify nor fulfil its own redemption — only the merchant may — and may never write its own `employment_status`. Each of these refusals surfaces as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` when the role itself is wrong for the operation, and as `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` when the role is right but the row belongs to someone else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of elements returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many elements to skip and defaults to 0; negative offsets are not accepted. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value supplied for either one is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Two collections override this: an agreement's negotiation history is ordered by `round_number` ascending, and the analytics category rows described in §2.10 are ordered by category name ascending.

For a list response, `response_type` is `"array"`, `data` is the bare JSON array of resource objects — so the first element is addressed as `$.data[0]` — and the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` all appear inside `meta` alongside the request identifiers.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Epoch Sweep

Timekeeping in this domain is entirely explicit. A control-plane capability lets any of the five roles set the logical clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response echoes the new clock value as `current_utc`. Before any such call has been made, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Every window bound, deadline, time-to-live and decay interval described anywhere in this document is evaluated against this logical clock and never against the host's wall clock.

Because nothing in this system runs on a timer, all time-triggered state changes are gathered into one administrator-only sweep capability that a `PlatformAdmin` invokes with no request body. The sweep evaluates the current logical clock and performs five passes **in this fixed order**: first, every offer whose `window_end` is at or before the clock and whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` becomes `EXPIRED` with `expired_at` set to the clock; second, every `CONFIRMED` placement slot whose `slot_window_end` is strictly before the clock becomes `COMPLETED`; third, every `PENDING` redemption whose `verification_expires_at` is at or before the clock becomes `VOID` with `void_reason` set to `VERIFICATION_TTL_EXPIRED`; fourth, every employer whose `integration_status` is `SYNCED` and whose `last_sync_at` plus 45 days is at or before the clock moves to `STALE`; fifth, every `OPEN` dispute that has received no merchant response and whose `response_deadline_at` is at or before the clock is default-judged as a merchant fault. The sweep reports what it did as `expired_offer_count`, `completed_slot_count`, `voided_redemption_count`, `stale_employer_count` and `default_judged_dispute_count`. The sweep is idempotent with respect to the clock: running it a second time without advancing the clock reports zero in all five counters.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Append-Only Records

Merchants, partner agreements, employers, eligibility files, employee memberships, offers, placement slots, redemptions, disputes and revenue share statements all carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured decoration.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock when the row is first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations that only rewrite derived fields.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing integer that is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. This is the value that optimistic concurrency control in §2.7 compares against.

Two record types are append-only and therefore deliberately lack both of the mutation-tracking properties. A negotiation round carries only `id` and `created_at`: rounds are immutable historical facts, so they have no `updated_at` and no `version`. A ledger entry likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`: entries are never amended or deleted, there is no endpoint that modifies one, and they have no `updated_at` and no `version` either. Corrections to the ledger are made by writing a compensating entry, never by editing an existing one.

Several derived fields are refreshed on read rather than only on write, because they depend on the logical clock rather than on any request. Reading a merchant recomputes `fulfillment_failure_score` against the current clock, so decay is observable without any write occurring. Reading an employer recomputes `eligible_headcount` and `program_posture`. Reading an offer recomputes `inventory_reserved`, `inventory_redeemed` and `sold_out`.

### 2.6. Monetary Discipline, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents; no monetary field is ever a decimal or a floating-point number. All rates are integers denominated in basis points, where 10,000 basis points is the whole — so a commission of 1,200 basis points is twelve percent of the amount it applies to. Every conversion from a basis-point rate to a cent amount divides by 10,000.

Rounding is **round half up** to a whole number of cents — a computed value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, never to the nearest even. Rounding is applied at the specific, named steps identified in each calculation in §3 and nowhere else: each step rounds its own output to whole cents before that output is used as the input to the next step. This round-then-chain discipline is observable. In particular, an employer's share of a commission is a slice of the **already-rounded** commission, not a slice of the net charged amount; and a statement's employer share total is the sum of the individual redemptions' already-rounded shares, never the result of applying a share rate to a summed commission. Where a calculation calls for a duration in days it uses a ceiling, and where a calculation converts an amount into whole scoring points it uses a floor; those two are called out explicitly at the point of use and are not rounding half up.

### 2.7. Version Echo and Stale-Read Rejection

Four write operations mutate an aggregate that other writers can also reach, and each of them requires the caller to echo the version of the parent row it last read. The echo field is not optional on these four operations; omitting it is a missing required field. If the echoed value does not equal the parent row's current `version`, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is mutated.

- Proposing a negotiation round requires `expected_agreement_version` to equal the target partner agreement's current `version`. Every accepted proposal, counter, decision, activation and termination increments that agreement's `version`, so a proposer holding an older read is refused before any round-count or term validation runs.
- Countering a negotiation round likewise requires `expected_agreement_version` to equal the same agreement's current `version`. The provider's newer proposal has already advanced it, so a counter built on the previous proposal is refused rather than silently overwriting the standing terms.
- Reserving a placement slot requires `expected_offer_version` to equal the parent offer's current `version`. Slot reservation is a write against the offer's aggregate because it consumes storefront capacity and cooldown footprint, and a pause, a resume, a review decision or an earlier slot reservation invalidates the caller's read.
- Loading an eligibility batch requires `expected_employer_version` to equal the parent employer's current `version`. The batch will mutate the employer's membership roster and integration dimension, and a posture change, a storefront transition or an earlier batch application invalidates the caller's read.

Every other write endpoint in the API takes no version field at all, and supplying one has no effect. The version check sits deliberately early in the evaluation order described in §2.9: a caller holding a stale read is told to re-read rather than being told about a state, deadline or capacity limit it cannot yet see.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline Across the Partner and Programme Hierarchies

Consistency in this system is immediate, not eventual. When an operation changes a row whose state other rows depend on, every dependent row is brought into line inside the same request, and each row the server touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1 — including rows the client never named. A single request can therefore legitimately advance half a dozen versions.

Two cascade spines run through the domain and are worth stating up front, because the individual workflows in §3 assume them. The **partner spine** runs from a merchant down through its partner agreements, from each agreement down to that agreement's offers, from each offer down to that offer's placement slots, and from each confirmed slot into the ledger: terminating a merchant terminates its agreements, which expires their offers, which releases those offers' live slots, each confirmed release writing a compensating ledger entry. The **programme spine** runs from an employer's storefront and eligibility dimensions down to its employees' memberships and from each membership to that employee's pending redemptions, and from each voided redemption back up into the reserved-inventory count of the offer it was reserved against. Because the second spine terminates by decrementing a counter on an offer, a purely employer-side action such as freezing a storefront can and does change what a merchant sees as available inventory.

Cascades are never rollbacks. Reinstating a suspended merchant does not un-pause the offers that its suspension paused, and does not re-reserve the placement slots that were released; the merchant must resume each offer explicitly. Un-suspending a storefront does not resurrect the redemptions that the suspension voided. Releasing a confirmed placement slot does not delete its original fee entry; it writes an offsetting one so the slot's ledger contribution nets to zero.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and the Order of Rule Evaluation

Where a URL nests one resource inside another, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first identifier that names no row determines the failure. A request that targets an eligibility batch under a non-existent employer fails with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND` before the batch is looked for; a request that targets a placement slot under a non-existent offer fails with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` before the slot is looked for. The same precedence governs foreign keys supplied in a request body: a referenced row that does not exist yields that entity's own not-found code — `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

Beyond existence, every endpoint evaluates the rules applicable to it in one fixed global order and **returns the first violation it meets; later violations are never reported**. The order is organised into bands, and within a band the sequence below is exact. This ordering is part of the contract: a request that violates two rules must fail with the code of the earlier one.

1. **Identity.** Missing or empty `X-User-ID` (`ERR_IDENTITY_REQUIRED`); then role not permitted for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`); then the row belongs to a different principal (`ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); then the employer-privacy refusal described in §2.10 (`ERR_PRIVACY_RESTRICTED`).
2. **Existence.** Any path identifier or body foreign key that names no row, with the entity's own not-found code.
3. **Schema.** A required input field missing, null, blank, of the wrong type or outside its documented range (`ERR_VALIDATION`); then an enum-typed input carrying a value outside its permitted set (`ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`); then bad pagination parameters (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`); then an interval whose end is at or before its start (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`); then a discount shape that contradicts its declared type (`ERR_DISCOUNT_SHAPE_MISMATCH`); then a region outside the merchant's footprint (`ERR_REGION_NOT_COVERED`); then two eligibility records sharing one employee reference (`ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_REF`).
4. **Concurrency.** The version echo of §2.7 (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`).
5. **Own state.** The target row is not in a state from which the requested transition is declared, using that entity's own invalid-state code: `ERR_MERCHANT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_STATEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Then, still inside this band: an employment-status write against a revoked membership (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_REVOKED`), then a duplicate open negotiation lane (`ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT`).
6. **Cross-entity state.** The offer's agreement is not active (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`); then the acting or referenced merchant is suspended or terminated (`ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`); then the redeeming membership is not eligible (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); then the employer's programme posture is `SETUP` (`ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_LIVE`); then it is `FROZEN` (`ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`); then it is `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`); then the member's region is outside the offer's regions (`ERR_REGION_NOT_COVERED`).
7. **Temporal.** The offer's redemption window is closed (`ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`); then the membership's eligibility window is closed (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`); then the verification time-to-live has elapsed (`ERR_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`); then the dispute filing window has closed (`ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`); then the dispute response deadline has passed (`ERR_DISPUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`); then the statement period is not yet closed (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`); then the requested exclusivity window collides with a live one (`ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_OVERLAP`).
8. **Capacity.** The negotiation round budget is exhausted (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`); then the offer is sold out (`ERR_OFFER_SOLD_OUT`); then the member's per-offer allowance is used up (`ERR_PER_MEMBER_LIMIT`); then the featured-placement capacity is full (`ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`); then the merchant's placement cooldown is not satisfied (`ERR_SLOT_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT`); then the dispute information-request budget is exhausted (`ERR_MAX_INFO_REQUESTS`); then the category coverage floor would be breached (`ERR_CATEGORY_COVERAGE_FLOOR`).
9. **Economics and privacy.** The verified ticket is below the offer's minimum spend (`ERR_MIN_SPEND_NOT_MET`); then the echoed verification token does not match (`ERR_VERIFICATION_TOKEN_INVALID`); then the audit fraud score is at or above its block threshold (`ERR_FRAUD_RISK_BLOCKED`); then a goodwill credit exceeds the redemption's net charge (`ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_NET`); then exclusivity is requested without negotiated exclusivity terms (`ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_TERMS_MISSING`).

Two orderings inside band 9 deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. The minimum-spend check runs **before** the verification-token check, so a verification request that both undershoots the offer's minimum and carries a wrong token fails with `ERR_MIN_SPEND_NOT_MET`. And the fraud-risk check runs **after** the minimum-spend check by design, because the risk score consumes the very ticket amount that the minimum-spend rule has just accepted.

### 2.10. Employee Privacy and Cohort Suppression

Employers pay for a perks programme but are never permitted to see who used it. An `EmployerAdmin` is refused outright — with `ERR_PRIVACY_RESTRICTED` — when it asks for an individual redemption by identifier, when it asks for the redemption collection at all, or when it asks for an individual dispute by identifier. This refusal is evaluated **before** existence is checked, precisely so that a probing employer cannot distinguish a redemption that exists from one that does not. It applies even to redemptions belonging to that employer's own staff, and an employee's free-text dispute `description` is never exposed to the employer under any circumstances. What an employer gets instead is an aggregated engagement report; what a merchant partner gets instead is an aggregated category-demand feed.

Both reports are computed the same way and take the period bounds `period_start` and `period_end` as inputs. The population is every redemption whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `verified_at` falls inside the half-open period — at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. Those redemptions are grouped by the offer's `category`, and every category with at least one redemption in the window produces one element of `category_rows`. Each element carries `category`, a boolean `suppressed`, a `redemption_count`, a `discount_amount_cents` equal to the sum of the included redemptions' own `discount_amount_cents`, and a cohort metric. Rows are ordered by category name ascending.

The two variants differ only in scope and in what the cohort metric counts. The **employer-scoped** report is restricted to the requesting employer's own verified redemptions and its cohort metric is `distinct_redeemer_count`, the number of distinct memberships behind the row; its suppression floor is that employer's own `privacy_min_cohort_size`, which defaults to 8 and may be configured to any integer from 2 through 25 inclusive. The **partner-scoped** feed spans every verified redemption across all employers and its cohort metric is `distinct_employer_count`, the number of distinct employers behind the row; its suppression floor is a fixed platform constant, `partner_min_employer_cohort`, whose value is 2.

When a row's cohort metric is strictly below the applicable floor, the row is suppressed: `suppressed` is true and `redemption_count`, `discount_amount_cents` and the cohort metric are all reported as null. A row whose cohort metric equals the floor is **not** suppressed — the floor is inclusive. Suppression hides the breakdown but never the total: `suppressed_category_count` reports how many rows were withheld, and the period totals are computed over every redemption in the window **including** those belonging to suppressed categories, so an employer can still reconcile total spend. The employer-scoped payload therefore reports `employer_id`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `privacy_min_cohort_size`, `category_rows`, `suppressed_category_count`, `total_redemption_count`, `total_discount_amount_cents` and `total_distinct_redeemer_count`; the partner-scoped payload reports `period_start`, `period_end`, `partner_min_employer_cohort`, `category_rows`, `suppressed_category_count` and `total_redemption_count`. Neither payload ever contains a `membership_id`, an `external_employee_ref`, a redemption identifier or an employer name.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Partner Recruitment and the Negotiation Loop

A `PartnerManager` opens the commercial funnel by registering a merchant partner, supplying the partner's `legal_name` (between 1 and 200 characters), a `contact_email` for negotiation correspondence (which must contain an `@` character), the head-line `primary_category` the partner is being recruited for, and the `region_codes` the partner can actually fulfil in. A category is one of `DINING`, `TRAVEL`, `WELLNESS`, `ELECTRONICS`, `CHILDCARE`, `EDUCATION` or `HOME_SERVICES`; a region is one of `CA`, `TX`, `NY`, `WA` or `IL`. The region list must be non-empty and must not repeat a region. A newly registered partner begins in `PROSPECT` with `fault_event_count`, `fulfillment_failure_score`, `accrued_commission_payable_cents` and `active_agreement_count` all at 0, and with `suspended_at` null. Later on, the same `PartnerManager` may amend a partner's `contact_email` or `region_codes`; shrinking the footprint is allowed and never retro-invalidates agreements or offers that were created while the wider footprint was in force. Partner listings may be narrowed by `status`, by `primary_category`, and by `region_code` — the last of which matches on membership of the partner's `region_codes` rather than on equality.

Commercial terms live on a **PartnerAgreement**, and a `PartnerManager` opens one by naming the `merchant_id`, the `category` the agreement will cover, and the single `region_code` it applies to, optionally setting `exclusivity_requested` to true (it defaults to false). The nominated region must itself be one of the partner's `region_codes`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REGION_NOT_COVERED`. Only one live lane may exist per partner, category and region combination: if an agreement already exists for that same triple in any of `DRAFT`, `PROVIDER_PROPOSED`, `MERCHANT_COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT`. A fresh agreement starts in `DRAFT` with `round_count` at 0 and every standing term — `commission_rate_bps`, `placement_fee_base_cents`, `employer_revenue_share_bps`, `exclusivity_window_start` and `exclusivity_window_end` — null, with `exclusivity_granted` false and with `accepted_by_role`, `activated_at` and `terminated_reason` all null. Agreements can be listed filtered by `merchant_id`, `category`, `region_code`, `status` and `exclusivity_granted`.

Negotiation proceeds as an append-only sequence of **NegotiationRound** records, and the loop is bounded. A `PartnerManager` proposes terms, moving the agreement `DRAFT → PROVIDER_PROPOSED` or, when the merchant has already replied, `MERCHANT_COUNTERED → PROVIDER_PROPOSED`. The proposal carries `expected_agreement_version` (see §2.7), a `commission_rate_bps` between 100 and 4,000 inclusive, a `placement_fee_base_cents` between 0 and 500,000 inclusive, an `employer_revenue_share_bps` between 0 and 5,000 inclusive, optionally an `exclusivity_window_start` and `exclusivity_window_end` pair, and optionally a free-text `note`. If both exclusivity bounds are supplied and the end is at or before the start, the proposal is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Each accepted proposal writes a new round whose `agreement_id` points at the agreement, whose `round_number` is the previous highest round number plus one — the first round is numbered 1 — and whose `proposer_role` is recorded as `PARTNER_MANAGER`. The agreement then republishes its standing terms by copying `commission_rate_bps`, `placement_fee_base_cents`, `employer_revenue_share_bps`, `exclusivity_window_start` and `exclusivity_window_end` from the round with the highest `round_number`, and recomputes `round_count` as the number of rounds attached to it. A first proposal also lifts the partner from `PROSPECT → IN_NEGOTIATION`; if the partner is already `IN_NEGOTIATION` because of another lane, that lift is a no-op. Both the agreement and, when it moves, the merchant have their `version` incremented.

The merchant side answers symmetrically. Acting as `Merchant` on its own agreement — the agreement's `merchant_id` must equal the caller — the partner counters, moving `PROVIDER_PROPOSED → MERCHANT_COUNTERED`. A counter carries exactly the same payload as a proposal: `expected_agreement_version`, `commission_rate_bps`, `placement_fee_base_cents`, `employer_revenue_share_bps`, the optional `exclusivity_window_start` and `exclusivity_window_end`, and the optional `note`. It creates the next round with `proposer_role` recorded as `MERCHANT`, republishes the standing terms from the highest `round_number` exactly as a proposal does, and increments the agreement's `version`. The same `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` guard applies to a counter's exclusivity bounds.

The loop is capped at four rounds in total across both sides. Proposing or countering while `round_count` is already 4 or greater is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` — that is, the fifth round attempt on one agreement fails, whichever side attempts it. The full round history is readable as an append-only list ordered by `round_number` ascending; rounds are immutable, and as noted in §2.5 they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

### 3.2. Acceptance, Activation, Exclusivity and the Coverage Floor

A negotiation closes with a decision, and the decision payload carries a `decision` value plus an optional `note`. Acceptance is asymmetric on purpose: **the side that did not author the standing round is the only side allowed to accept it.** When the standing round was authored by the provider and the agreement therefore sits in `PROVIDER_PROPOSED`, only the `Merchant` may accept, moving the agreement `PROVIDER_PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`; this requires `round_count` to be at least 1, and it records `accepted_by_role` as `MERCHANT`. When the standing round was authored by the merchant and the agreement therefore sits in `MERCHANT_COUNTERED`, only the `PartnerManager` may accept, moving the agreement `MERCHANT_COUNTERED → ACCEPTED` and recording `accepted_by_role` as `PARTNER_MANAGER`. A principal that attempts to accept the proposal it authored itself is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Either acceptance increments the agreement's `version`.

Withdrawal is the other branch of the same decision, and it belongs to the `PartnerManager` alone. A withdrawal is accepted from `DRAFT`, from `PROVIDER_PROPOSED` or from `MERCHANT_COUNTERED`, and it drives the agreement to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state with `terminated_reason` set to the fixed string `WITHDRAWN_IN_NEGOTIATION`. Withdrawal also unwinds the partner's recruitment status: the partner drops from `IN_NEGOTIATION` back to `PROSPECT`, but only when that partner has no other agreement sitting in `PROVIDER_PROPOSED`, `MERCHANT_COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`. If any such agreement survives, the partner stays `IN_NEGOTIATION`. The agreement's `version` is incremented.

Activation is the `PartnerManager`'s privilege and takes no request body. It moves the agreement `ACCEPTED → ACTIVE` and additionally requires the partner to be in `PROSPECT`, `IN_NEGOTIATION` or `ACTIVE`; activation against a `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` partner is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`. On success the agreement stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, the partner is lifted to `ACTIVE` and its `active_agreement_count` is recomputed as the number of that partner's agreements currently in `ACTIVE`, and the agreement's `version` is incremented.

Where `exclusivity_requested` is true, activation runs two additional checks, and — following the evaluation order of §2.9 — the overlap check runs **before** the terms-completeness check. The overlap check looks for any other partner agreement that is `ACTIVE`, that has `exclusivity_granted` true, that covers the same `category` and the same `region_code`, and whose exclusivity window intersects the one being requested. Exclusivity windows are **half-open**: a window runs from `exclusivity_window_start` inclusive to `exclusivity_window_end` exclusive, so two windows that merely touch — where the existing window's end equals the new window's start — do not overlap and activation proceeds. A genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_OVERLAP`. The terms-completeness check then verifies that the standing round actually negotiated an exclusivity window at all: if either `exclusivity_window_start` or `exclusivity_window_end` on the standing terms is null, activation is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_TERMS_MISSING`. When both checks pass, `exclusivity_granted` is set to true. When `exclusivity_requested` is false, neither check runs and `exclusivity_granted` stays false.

Termination of an `ACTIVE` agreement may be initiated either by the `PartnerManager` who owns the relationship or by a `PlatformAdmin`, and it carries a free-text `terminated_reason` that is stored verbatim. It is guarded by the **category coverage floor**, a conditional aggregate that protects employer clients from a partner manager walking away from a category their storefront is actively promoting. Termination is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_COVERAGE_FLOOR` when, after this agreement leaves `ACTIVE`, the number of remaining `ACTIVE` agreements for the same `category` and `region_code` pair would be zero **and** at least one employer still depends on that pairing — meaning an employer whose `program_posture` is `LIVE`, whose `region_code` equals the agreement's region, and whose `featured_categories` list contains the agreement's category. If no such employer exists, the last agreement in a lane may be terminated freely. This floor guards agreement termination only; it does not guard withdrawal from negotiation, and as §3.8 explains it is deliberately bypassed when the platform terminates a partner outright.

A successful termination is the head of the partner cascade spine. The agreement records the supplied `terminated_reason`, resets `exclusivity_granted` to false, and expires every offer it owns whose status is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, stamping each such offer's `expired_at` from the logical clock. Every offer that leaves `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` in this way then releases its own placement slots: each slot whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_at` from the clock and `release_reason` set to the fixed string `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`, and each slot that was `CONFIRMED` additionally writes a compensating placement-fee refund entry to the ledger for the negative of its `placement_fee_cents` and lowers the partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` by that same amount. Finally the partner's `active_agreement_count` is recomputed. The partner's own `status` is deliberately left untouched: losing every agreement does not terminate the partner relationship.

### 3.3. Employer Onboarding: Eligibility Batches and the Membership Roster

Employer clients are onboarded by the platform, not self-served. A `PlatformAdmin` registers an employer with a `name` between 1 and 200 characters (this is the name that appears on the branded storefront), a primary `region_code` drawn from the same five-region set, and an `employee_count_declared` between 1 and 1,000,000 that records the headcount stated at contracting. That declared figure is **reporting only**: it never participates in pricing, in eligibility, or in any other calculation in this system. Two further settings may be supplied at registration or amended afterwards: `revenue_share_enabled`, which defaults to false, and `privacy_min_cohort_size`, which defaults to 8 and accepts any integer from 2 through 25 inclusive. A new employer starts with `integration_status` at `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `storefront_status` at `DRAFT` and the derived `program_posture` at `SETUP`; `brand_color_hex`, `logo_url`, `hero_headline`, `featured_categories`, `last_sync_at` and `suspension_reason` are all null, and `eligible_headcount` and `accrued_share_cents` are both 0. An `EmployerAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` may later adjust `revenue_share_enabled`, `privacy_min_cohort_size` and `employee_count_declared`; because `revenue_share_enabled` is read at verification time, flipping it never rewrites the `employer_share_cents` of a redemption that has already been verified. Employer listings may be filtered by `region_code`, `program_posture`, `integration_status` and `storefront_status`.

The eligibility integration dimension advances through `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `FILE_RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `SYNCED` and `STALE`. An `EmployerAdmin` loads an **EligibilityFile** against its own employer row, supplying `expected_employer_version` (§2.7), an `effective_from` instant from which the batch's memberships are considered current, a client-supplied `checksum` of 1 to 128 characters that is echoed back but **never used in any decision**, and a `records` array of between 1 and 5,000 pseudonymous employee records. Each element of `records` carries an `external_employee_ref`, an `employment_status` of `ACTIVE`, `LEAVE` or `TERMINATED`, a `region_code`, an `eligibility_valid_from` and an `eligibility_valid_until`. No two elements of one batch may share an `external_employee_ref`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_REF`. A batch may be loaded from any integration state — a new batch always pulls the integration dimension back to `FILE_RECEIVED`, whether it was previously `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `FILE_RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `SYNCED` or `STALE` — and loading recomputes the employer's `program_posture` and increments the employer's `version`. The batch itself is created in `RECEIVED` with its `employer_id` set, with `rejection_reason` and `applied_at` null, with `created_membership_count`, `updated_membership_count` and `suspended_membership_count` all 0, and with its three record tallies computed immediately.

Those tallies are the batch's own arithmetic. `record_count` is simply the number of elements in `records`. `invalid_record_count` is the number of elements that fail any one of four tests: a required key is missing (the required keys are `external_employee_ref`, `employment_status`, `region_code`, `eligibility_valid_from` and `eligibility_valid_until`); the `employment_status` value lies outside `ACTIVE`, `LEAVE` and `TERMINATED`; the `region_code` value lies outside `CA`, `TX`, `NY`, `WA` and `IL`; or the record's `eligibility_valid_until` is at or before its `eligibility_valid_from`. `valid_record_count` is `record_count` less `invalid_record_count`.

Validation is a branching decision that either an `EmployerAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` may trigger, with no request body, and it is accepted only from `RECEIVED` — a batch already `VALIDATED`, `REJECTED` or `APPLIED` is refused with `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`. The decision rests entirely on an invalid-record ratio expressed in basis points: multiply `invalid_record_count` by 10,000, divide by `record_count`, and round that quotient half up to a whole number of basis points. If the resulting ratio is **strictly greater than 500 basis points** — five percent — the batch moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED` and its `rejection_reason` is set to the fixed string `INVALID_RECORD_RATIO_EXCEEDED`; the employer's `integration_status` is left where it is, at `FILE_RECEIVED`, and only the batch's `version` advances. Otherwise the batch moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED` and drives the employer's integration dimension `FILE_RECEIVED → VALIDATED`, recomputing `program_posture` and incrementing the employer's `version` as well as the batch's. **The boundary is inclusive: a ratio of exactly 500 basis points validates.** A batch of 20 records with 1 invalid record yields exactly 500 and is accepted; a batch of 19 records with 1 invalid record yields 526 and is rejected.

Applying a `VALIDATED` batch is reserved to the `EmployerAdmin` that owns it — the batch's `employer_id` must equal `X-User-ID` — and takes no request body. Application walks the valid records **in `records` array order** and, for each one, either creates a new **EmployeeMembership** or rewrites the existing membership that already carries that `external_employee_ref` for this employer. `external_employee_ref` is unique per employer, and it is the join key that makes a batch idempotent in identity but authoritative in content: a rewrite overwrites the membership's `employment_status`, `region_code`, `eligibility_valid_from`, `eligibility_valid_until` and `source_file_id`. Because memberships are created in records order and lists default to `created_at` ascending, the first element of a membership listing is the first record of the first batch ever applied. Any membership of this employer whose `external_employee_ref` is **absent** from the batch and whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE` is moved `ELIGIBLE → SUSPENDED`; conversely a membership currently `SUSPENDED` whose `external_employee_ref` reappears in a batch with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` or `LEAVE` is restored `SUSPENDED → ELIGIBLE`. Application then stamps the batch `VALIDATED → APPLIED` with `applied_at` from the logical clock and fills in `created_membership_count`, `updated_membership_count` and `suspended_membership_count`, sets the employer's `last_sync_at` to the logical clock, recomputes `eligible_headcount` and `program_posture`, drives the integration dimension `VALIDATED → SYNCED`, and increments both the employer's and the batch's `version`.

Suspending a membership is not a bookkeeping-only act — it releases inventory. Because a `SUSPENDED` membership is no longer eligible, every redemption of that membership whose `verification_status` is `PENDING` moves to `VOID` with `void_reason` set to `MEMBERSHIP_INELIGIBLE` and `voided_at` from the clock, and each such void lowers the corresponding offer's `inventory_reserved` by 1 and recomputes that offer's `sold_out` flag. The membership's `version` is incremented, as are the versions of every redemption and offer touched.

An `EmployerAdmin` may also write a single membership's employment dimension directly, on a membership whose `employer_id` equals the caller, by supplying an `employment_status`. Writing `ACTIVE` or `LEAVE` leaves the membership `ELIGIBLE`, stores the new `employment_status`, stamps `last_employment_change_at` from the clock, and increments the membership's `version`; a membership sitting on `LEAVE` will subsequently score additional risk points at every verification, as §3.7 describes. Writing `TERMINATED` moves the membership `ELIGIBLE → REVOKED` and runs a three-level cascade: the employment write drives the eligibility dimension to `REVOKED`, which voids the membership's `PENDING` redemptions with `void_reason` `MEMBERSHIP_INELIGIBLE`, which in turn lowers each affected offer's `inventory_reserved` by 1 and recomputes its `sold_out` flag. `REVOKED` is terminal: a further employment-status write against a revoked membership is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_REVOKED`, and rehiring an employee requires a fresh eligibility batch rather than a status flip.

Two rolling counters live on each membership and are evaluated against the logical clock. `redemption_count_7d` counts that membership's redemptions whose `created_at` falls at or after the instant seven days before the clock and strictly before the clock, and whose `verification_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`. `redemption_count_30d` counts that membership's redemptions whose `created_at` falls at or after the instant thirty days before the clock and strictly before the clock, and whose `verification_status` is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `FAILED` or `VOID` — the wider window deliberately also counts the failures and cancellations that the seven-day counter ignores.

The employer's `eligible_headcount` is the number of that employer's memberships that satisfy three conditions at once: `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LEAVE`, and the logical clock falls at or after `eligibility_valid_from` and strictly before `eligibility_valid_until`. A membership whose `eligibility_valid_until` is exactly the current clock instant is therefore excluded. This count — never `employee_count_declared` — is the reach figure that drives placement-fee pricing in §3.6. Membership listings may be filtered by `employer_id`, `source_file_id`, `status`, `employment_status` and `region_code`; eligibility batch listings may be filtered by `status`.

### 3.4. Branded Storefronts and the Derived Programme Posture

The storefront dimension evolves entirely independently of the eligibility dimension, and this independence is deliberate rather than accidental. An `EmployerAdmin` brands its storefront by supplying a `brand_color_hex` accent colour, a `logo_url`, a `hero_headline` and a `featured_categories` array whose elements are drawn from the seven perk categories and which may hold anywhere from zero to seven entries. Branding is accepted while the storefront dimension is `DRAFT` or `BRANDED` — the first submission moves `DRAFT → BRANDED` and a re-submission re-brands in place, `BRANDED → BRANDED`, overwriting all four stored values. Branding a storefront that is already `PUBLISHED` or that is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`. Each branding call recomputes `program_posture` and increments the employer's `version`.

Publishing belongs to the `EmployerAdmin` too and takes no request body. It is accepted from `BRANDED`, moving the storefront `BRANDED → PUBLISHED`, and it is also the way back from a platform freeze, moving `SUSPENDED → PUBLISHED` and clearing `suspension_reason`. Publishing from `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`, since there is nothing to publish. Publishing recomputes `program_posture` and increments `version`. Crucially, publishing does **not** consult the eligibility dimension: an employer that publishes a storefront before its first eligibility batch has ever been applied ends up with a `PUBLISHED` storefront and a posture that is still `SETUP`.

Freezing a programme is a platform prerogative. A `PlatformAdmin` suspends a `PUBLISHED` storefront by supplying a `suspension_reason`, which is stored on the employer, moving the storefront `PUBLISHED → SUSPENDED`. Because the recomputed posture becomes `FROZEN`, every redemption of that employer whose `verification_status` is `PENDING` moves to `VOID` with `void_reason` set to `PROGRAM_FROZEN` and `voided_at` taken from the logical clock; each voided redemption drops out of its offer's reserved count, lowering that offer's `inventory_reserved` by 1 and forcing a recomputation of the offer's `sold_out` flag. The employer's `version` is incremented, as is the version of every redemption and offer the cascade touched. Republishing later restores the storefront but is **not a rollback**: the voided redemptions stay voided and the employee must reserve again.

The eligibility dimension has one time-driven transition of its own. A `SYNCED` employer whose `last_sync_at` plus 45 days has been reached by the logical clock is moved to `STALE` by the epoch sweep of §2.4, which recomputes `program_posture` and increments the employer's `version`. Forty-five days without a refreshed roster is treated as a feed the platform can no longer trust for new commitments, though not as grounds for cancelling commitments already made.

`program_posture` is a **derived** gate over the two dimensions, and it is evaluated as an ordered table in which the first matching row wins:

1. If `storefront_status` is `SUSPENDED`, the posture is `FROZEN` — a suspension dominates even a perfectly healthy `SYNCED` integration.
2. Otherwise, if `storefront_status` is `DRAFT` or `BRANDED`, the posture is `SETUP`.
3. Otherwise, if `storefront_status` is `PUBLISHED` and `integration_status` is `SYNCED`, the posture is `LIVE`.
4. Otherwise, if `storefront_status` is `PUBLISHED` and `integration_status` is `STALE`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise — `storefront_status` is `PUBLISHED` and `integration_status` is any of `NOT_CONFIGURED`, `FILE_RECEIVED` or `VALIDATED` — the posture is `SETUP`.

The four postures gate different operations, and the asymmetry between them is intentional. A `LIVE` posture permits everything. A `SETUP` posture refuses three operations with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_LIVE`: creating a redemption, reserving a placement slot on that employer's storefront, and confirming a reserved slot. A `FROZEN` posture refuses four operations with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`: creating a redemption, verifying a redemption, reserving a placement slot, and confirming a slot. A `RESTRICTED` posture refuses exactly one operation, creating a redemption, with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` — a published storefront running on a stale eligibility feed accepts no new commitments, but a redemption that was already `PENDING` when the feed went stale can still be verified and fulfilled normally, and slots may still be reserved and confirmed against it.

### 3.5. Offers: Drafting, Review, Pausing and Expiry

An **Offer** is the employee-facing artefact, and it inherits its economics rather than declaring them. A `Merchant` drafts one against an `agreement_id` and supplies a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, a `discount_type` of either `PERCENT` or `FIXED`, a `region_codes` array, a `window_start` and a `window_end`. Optionally it supplies a `min_spend_cents` floor (defaulting to 0, accepted from 0 through 1,000,000), an `inventory_limit` (accepted from 1 through 1,000,000, or null to mean unlimited), and a `per_member_limit` (defaulting to 1, accepted from 1 through 50). The offer's `merchant_id` and `category` are **copied from the referenced agreement** at creation and any client-supplied value for either is ignored; `category` is never rewritten thereafter, and neither is `merchant_id`.

The discount shape must be internally consistent. A `PERCENT` offer must carry a `discount_value_bps` between 100 and 7,000 inclusive and must leave `discount_value_cents` omitted or null; a `FIXED` offer must carry a `discount_value_cents` between 100 and 500,000 inclusive and must leave `discount_value_bps` omitted or null. Any other combination — a percentage offer with no basis-point value, a percentage offer that also names a cent value, a fixed offer with no cent value, or a fixed offer that also names a basis-point value — is refused with `ERR_DISCOUNT_SHAPE_MISMATCH`. Separately, `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` or the draft is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, and `region_codes` must be non-empty and a subset of the partner's own `region_codes` or the draft is refused with `ERR_REGION_NOT_COVERED`. Drafting also requires the referenced agreement to be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the partner not to be `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` (otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`). A new offer lands in `DRAFT` with `inventory_reserved` and `inventory_redeemed` at 0, `sold_out` false, and `review_note`, `paused_reason` and `expired_at` all null. Offers can be listed filtered by `agreement_id`, `merchant_id`, `category`, `status`, `region_code` and `sold_out`.

Publication is a two-party affair. The owning `Merchant` submits its own draft, moving the offer `DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW`; submission requires the offer's `merchant_id` to equal the caller, the governing agreement to be `ACTIVE`, and the partner not to be suspended or terminated. A `PlatformAdmin` then rules on it by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `review_note`, and both branches store the `review_note` verbatim and increment the offer's `version`. The two branches are **not** symmetric in what they check. `APPROVE` moves the offer `PENDING_REVIEW → ACTIVE` and additionally requires that the governing agreement is still `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — and that the partner's own `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`. `REJECT` moves the offer `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED` and requires nothing beyond the offer being in `PENDING_REVIEW`: a rejection can always be recorded, even for a partner whose agreement has since lapsed. `REJECTED` is terminal.

An `ACTIVE` offer may be pulled out of circulation and put back. Either the owning `Merchant` or a `PlatformAdmin` pauses it by supplying a `paused_reason`, which is stored, moving the offer `ACTIVE → PAUSED`. Pausing deliberately does **not** void redemptions that are already `PENDING` against the offer; those reservations remain live and may still be verified by the merchant. Only the owning `Merchant` may resume, moving the offer `PAUSED → ACTIVE` and clearing `paused_reason`, and resumption is the most heavily guarded transition on the offer: it requires the partner's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, the governing agreement's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, and the offer's `window_end` to be strictly after the logical clock. A resumption attempt on a suspended partner is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`, one on a lapsed agreement with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and one whose redemption window has already closed with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`. The `ACTIVE → PAUSED → ACTIVE` cycle may be traversed any number of times.

Offers reach the terminal `EXPIRED` state in three ways, and all three are system-driven. The epoch sweep expires any offer in `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` whose `window_end` has been reached by the logical clock, stamping `expired_at`. Terminating the governing agreement expires the offer from any of `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, as described in §3.2. Terminating the partner outright does the same through the agreement, as described in §3.8. In the latter two cases, an offer that was in `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` also releases its own placement slots: every slot in `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`, and every slot that was `CONFIRMED` writes a compensating placement-fee refund entry for the negative of its `placement_fee_cents`.

There is a fourth, indirect route out of `ACTIVE`, and it is a pause rather than an expiry. When a partner is suspended for fulfilment failures (§3.8), each of that partner's `ACTIVE` offers is moved `ACTIVE → PAUSED` with `paused_reason` set to the fixed string `MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`. Because such an offer has left `ACTIVE`, each of its placement slots in `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the fixed string `OFFER_PAUSED` and `released_at` from the clock, and each released slot that had been `CONFIRMED` writes a placement-fee refund entry for the negative of its `placement_fee_cents` and lowers the partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` by that same amount.

Inventory on an offer is maintained as two counts rather than as a decrementing balance. `inventory_reserved` is the number of that offer's redemptions whose `verification_status` is `PENDING`; `inventory_redeemed` is the number whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`. The derived `sold_out` flag is true exactly when `inventory_limit` is not null and the sum of `inventory_reserved` and `inventory_redeemed` is greater than or equal to `inventory_limit`; an offer with a null `inventory_limit` is never sold out. Because verification moves a unit from the reserved count to the redeemed count, the sum across the two is unchanged by verification and a successful verification therefore never frees capacity.

### 3.6. Storefront Placement: Reserving, Pricing and Confirming Slots

A **PlacementSlot** is a paid run of one offer on one employer's storefront for a bounded stretch of time. The owning `Merchant` reserves it against its own offer, supplying `expected_offer_version` (§2.7), the `employer_id` whose storefront the slot will run on, a `placement_tier` of either `STANDARD` or `FEATURED`, and the run bounds `slot_window_start` and `slot_window_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start or the reservation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The slot's `offer_id` is taken from the offer being placed. Reservation requires the offer to be in a state that permits placement — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE` — requires the partner not to be `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` — otherwise `ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED` — and is refused when the employer's `program_posture` is `SETUP` (`ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_LIVE`) or `FROZEN` (`ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`). A `RESTRICTED` posture does not block placement. A new slot lands in `RESERVED` with `confirmed_at`, `released_at` and `release_reason` all null. Slot listings may be filtered by `offer_id`, `employer_id`, `status` and `placement_tier`.

**Slot run intervals are closed on both ends.** A run occupies every instant from `slot_window_start` through `slot_window_end` inclusive, which means a run that begins at exactly the instant another run ends does intersect it. Two capacity rules use this notion of intersection, and they behave differently.

The first is a **concurrency ceiling on featured placement**. At most two `FEATURED` slots whose status is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` may share a single combination of storefront (`employer_id`) and offer `category` over intersecting run intervals. A reservation that would make a third such slot intersect two existing ones is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`. `STANDARD` slots never consume this capacity, neither as the slot being reserved nor as an existing occupant.

The second is a **separation rule**, not a concurrency rule, and it applies per partner. If the same partner already holds a `FEATURED` slot whose status is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` on the same storefront-and-category combination, then the quiet period between that run and the new one must be at least seven days. The gap is measured in whole days as the difference between the later run's `slot_window_start` and the earlier run's `slot_window_end`, divided by 86,400 seconds and floored; a pair of runs that intersect at all has a gap of zero days. A gap of fewer than seven days is refused with `ERR_SLOT_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT`. Note the consequence: two runs that do not overlap in the slightest are still refused when the silence between them is too short, and the rule reaches back over runs that have already finished.

Reserving a slot prices it immediately, in **five ordered steps**, and the resulting figures are all persisted on the slot. The inputs are the governing agreement's `placement_fee_base_cents` (a per-day base rate), the slot's own run bounds, and the employer's `eligible_headcount` read at the moment of reservation.

1. **Duration and base.** `slot_duration_days` is the difference between `slot_window_end` and `slot_window_start` divided by 86,400 seconds and rounded **up** to a whole number of days, floored at a minimum of 1 — a run shorter than a day still bills a day. `base_fee_cents` is the agreement's `placement_fee_base_cents` multiplied by `slot_duration_days`.
2. **Reach surcharge.** `reach_surcharge_bps` is selected from a tier over the employer's `eligible_headcount`: a headcount of 3 or fewer selects 0 basis points; a headcount from 4 through 20 inclusive selects 1,500 basis points; a headcount of 21 or more selects 3,000 basis points. `reach_surcharge_cents` is `base_fee_cents` multiplied by `reach_surcharge_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents.
3. **Gross.** `gross_fee_cents` is `base_fee_cents` plus `reach_surcharge_cents`.
4. **Volume discount.** `volume_discount_bps` is selected from a tier over `gross_fee_cents` — that is, over the step-3 figure **after** the reach surcharge has been added, never over the bare base: a gross below 100,000 cents (one thousand dollars) selects 0 basis points; a gross from 100,000 cents up to but not including 200,000 cents selects 500 basis points; a gross of 200,000 cents (two thousand dollars) or more selects 1,200 basis points. `volume_discount_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents.
5. **Fee.** `placement_fee_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` less `volume_discount_cents`.

The ordering of steps 2 through 4 is observable and must be respected exactly. A reach surcharge can push the gross across a volume-tier floor that the bare base would not have reached, and applying the volume discount before the surcharge — or keying the volume tier on `base_fee_cents` — yields a materially different `placement_fee_cents`.

Confirmation is the `PartnerManager`'s act, not the merchant's, and takes no request body: reservation and billing are split across the two peer roles precisely so that a partner cannot bill itself. Confirmation moves the slot `RESERVED → CONFIRMED` and additionally requires the parent offer to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE` — and the employer's `program_posture` to be `LIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, so a `SETUP` programme is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_LIVE` and a `FROZEN` one with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`. On success the slot stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock, a placement-fee entry is written to the ledger for the positive of `placement_fee_cents` naming this slot and this partner, the partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` is raised by `placement_fee_cents`, and the slot's `version` is incremented. Confirming a slot that is not `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`.

Either the owning `Merchant` or a `PartnerManager` may release a slot, supplying a `release_reason` that is stored verbatim. Release is accepted from `RESERVED` and from `CONFIRMED`, moves the slot to the terminal `RELEASED` state, and stamps `released_at` from the clock; releasing a slot that is already `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`. A released slot no longer contributes to the featured-concurrency ceiling, and no longer contributes to the partner's cooldown footprint either. Releasing a slot that was `CONFIRMED` additionally writes a compensating placement-fee refund entry to the ledger for the negative of `placement_fee_cents` and lowers the partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` by that amount, so the slot's total ledger contribution nets to zero. Releasing a slot that was merely `RESERVED` writes nothing to the ledger, because nothing had been billed.

A `CONFIRMED` slot whose `slot_window_end` is strictly before the logical clock is moved to the terminal `COMPLETED` state by the epoch sweep, and its `version` is incremented. A completed slot stops occupying the featured-concurrency ceiling, since that ceiling counts only `RESERVED` and `CONFIRMED` slots. Its run interval nevertheless continues to count toward the partner's seven-day cooldown separation, which counts `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` and `COMPLETED` runs alike. Completion writes no ledger entry: the fee was earned when the run was confirmed.

### 3.7. Redemption: Reservation, Fraud Screening, Verification and Line Economics

A **Redemption** carries two entirely independent dimensions. `verification_status` tracks whether the discount was actually honoured at the till and moves through `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `FAILED` and `VOID`; `fulfillment_status` tracks whether the merchant then delivered and moves through `AWAITING`, `FULFILLED`, `DISPUTED` and `REFUNDED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions except where explicitly stated, and the fulfilment dimension only ever advances while the verification dimension reads `VERIFIED`.

An `Employee` reserves a redemption by naming the `offer_id`, its own `membership_id` and an `intended_gross_amount_cents` between 100 and 5,000,000 inclusive — the ticket the employee expects to spend. The membership named must be the acting employee's own, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On creation the redemption copies `employer_id` from the membership and `merchant_id` from the offer, leaves `statement_id` null, and starts at `PENDING` on the verification dimension and `AWAITING` on the fulfilment dimension. A fresh opaque `verification_token` is minted and **returned only in the creation response** — it is the single-use secret the merchant must echo back — and `verification_expires_at` is set to the redemption's `created_at` plus 72 hours. `gross_amount_cents`, `discount_amount_cents`, `net_charged_cents`, `commission_amount_cents`, `employer_share_cents`, `provider_net_cents`, `audit_risk_score`, `risk_block_reason`, `verified_at`, `fulfilled_at`, `refunded_at`, `voided_at` and `void_reason` are all null at this point. Creating the redemption reserves one inventory unit on the offer, which is to say it raises that offer's `inventory_reserved` and forces a recomputation of `sold_out`.

Reservation performs exactly this sequence of checks, in this order, stopping at the first failure: the acting or referenced partner must not be `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` (`ERR_MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`); the redeeming membership's `status` must be `ELIGIBLE` and its `employment_status` must not be `TERMINATED` (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); the employer's `program_posture` must not be `SETUP` (`ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_LIVE`), must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`) and must not be `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`), so only a `LIVE` programme accepts a new reservation; the membership's `region_code` must be a member of the offer's `region_codes` (`ERR_REGION_NOT_COVERED`); the logical clock must fall at or after the offer's `window_start` and strictly before its `window_end` (`ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`); the clock must likewise fall at or after the membership's `eligibility_valid_from` and strictly before its `eligibility_valid_until` (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`); the offer must not be sold out, meaning that if `inventory_limit` is not null the sum of `inventory_reserved` and `inventory_redeemed` must be strictly below it (`ERR_OFFER_SOLD_OUT`); and the membership must not already hold `per_member_limit` redemptions on this same offer counting only those whose `verification_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` (`ERR_PER_MEMBER_LIMIT`). The sold-out rule is an aggregate rule: each individual reservation is perfectly valid on its own and it is the collective count that fails.

Reservation also freezes a fraud score. **Fraud risk is scored by one formula in two variants.** The score is the sum of its component point terms, capped at 100. The `SCREENING` variant is computed once at reservation, stored in `screening_risk_score`, and never rewritten; it gates nothing at all and exists purely for analytics. The `AUDIT` variant is computed when the merchant attempts verification, stored in `audit_risk_score` whichever way that attempt resolves, and it is the only variant that blocks anything. The terms are:

- **Employment.** 15 points when the membership's `employment_status` is `LEAVE`, and 0 otherwise. This is why moving an employee onto leave raises the risk of every subsequent verification.
- **Velocity.** 8 points per prior redemption, capped at 40 points in total. The prior count is the number of the **same membership's other** redemptions — the redemption being scored is always excluded — whose `created_at` falls at or after the instant one velocity window before the logical clock and strictly before the clock, and whose `verification_status` lies in the variant's status set. The `SCREENING` variant looks back 24 hours and counts only redemptions in `PENDING`. The `AUDIT` variant looks back 168 hours, which is seven days, and counts redemptions in `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `FAILED`. The two variants therefore routinely disagree on the same data, and that divergence is intended.
- **Seasoning.** 10 points when the logical clock is strictly earlier than the membership's `eligibility_valid_from` plus 7 days, and 0 otherwise — a brand-new membership is riskier than a settled one.
- **Amount** (`AUDIT` only). One point per whole 10,000 cents of `gross_amount_cents`, computed by dividing `gross_amount_cents` by 10,000 and taking the **floor** — never rounding — and capped at 25 points. A ticket of 149,999 cents therefore scores 14 points, not 15.
- **Merchant risk** (`AUDIT` only). 10 points when the partner's `fulfillment_failure_score` is 20 or more, and 0 otherwise.

The `SCREENING` variant sums the employment, velocity and seasoning terms only; the `AUDIT` variant sums all five. All arithmetic here is integral.

Verification is the merchant's act and is where all economics crystallise. The owning `Merchant` supplies the `verification_token` it was shown and the actual `gross_amount_cents` rung up at the till. The checks run in the order §2.9 prescribes: the redemption's `verification_status` must be `PENDING` (`ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`); the redemption's `merchant_id` must equal the caller (`ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); the membership's `status` must still be `ELIGIBLE` with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` or `LEAVE` (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); the employer's `program_posture` must be `LIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, so a `FROZEN` programme is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` — note that a `RESTRICTED` programme still permits verification even though it refuses new reservations; the logical clock must be strictly before `verification_expires_at` (`ERR_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`); the supplied `gross_amount_cents` must be greater than or equal to the offer's `min_spend_cents` (`ERR_MIN_SPEND_NOT_MET`); the supplied `verification_token` must equal the one minted at creation (`ERR_VERIFICATION_TOKEN_INVALID`); and finally the recomputed `AUDIT` score must be strictly below 70 (`ERR_FRAUD_RISK_BLOCKED`). The minimum-spend check precedes the token check, and the fraud check comes last of all because it consumes the very ticket amount the minimum-spend check has just accepted.

A successful verification moves the redemption `PENDING → VERIFIED`, stores `gross_amount_cents`, stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, stores the computed `audit_risk_score`, and computes the five line-economics figures below. It writes a commission accrual entry to the ledger for the positive of `commission_amount_cents` and, only when `employer_share_cents` is greater than zero, an employer-share entry for the negative of `employer_share_cents`; it then recomputes the partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` and the employer's `accrued_share_cents` from those entries. It moves the offer's unit from the reserved count to the redeemed count — since both are counts of redemptions, their sum is unchanged — and recomputes `sold_out`. The redemption's `version` is incremented.

The line economics are **five ordered steps, each rounding to whole cents before feeding the next**. The inputs are the supplied `gross_amount_cents`, the offer's `discount_type` with whichever of `discount_value_bps` or `discount_value_cents` applies, the governing agreement's standing `commission_rate_bps` and `employer_revenue_share_bps`, and the employer's `revenue_share_enabled` flag.

1. **Discount.** For a `PERCENT` offer, `discount_amount_cents` is the lesser of `gross_amount_cents` and the result of multiplying `gross_amount_cents` by `discount_value_bps`, dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up to whole cents — the discount can never exceed the ticket. For a `FIXED` offer, `discount_amount_cents` is the lesser of `discount_value_cents` and `gross_amount_cents`.
2. **Net charged.** `net_charged_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` less `discount_amount_cents`.
3. **Commission.** `commission_amount_cents` is `net_charged_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `commission_rate_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents.
4. **Employer share.** `employer_share_cents` is 0 whenever the employer's `revenue_share_enabled` is false, regardless of what the agreement's `employer_revenue_share_bps` says. Otherwise it is `commission_amount_cents` — the already-rounded figure from step 3, never `net_charged_cents` — multiplied by `employer_revenue_share_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents.
5. **Provider net.** `provider_net_cents` is `commission_amount_cents` less `employer_share_cents`.

Because rounding is half up rather than half to even, a step-3 commission of 806 cents against a 2,500 basis-point share yields a step-4 share of 202 cents, not 201. And because a false `revenue_share_enabled` suppresses only step 4, the provider simply retains the whole commission in that case.

Verification has a second, persisted outcome. When every other precondition holds but the recomputed `AUDIT` score is 70 or above, the redemption moves `PENDING → FAILED` rather than being merely rejected: the system stores `audit_risk_score`, sets `risk_block_reason` to the fixed string `FRAUD_RISK_THRESHOLD`, leaves `gross_amount_cents` null, writes no ledger entry at all, lowers the offer's `inventory_reserved` by 1 and recomputes `sold_out`, and increments the redemption's `version`. The HTTP response is `ERR_FRAUD_RISK_BLOCKED` with status 422 even though this state change has been persisted — the score that justified the refusal is deliberately kept for audit. `FAILED` is terminal on the verification dimension.

The verification dimension has one more terminal state. An `Employee` may cancel its own `PENDING` redemption, and a `PlatformAdmin` may cancel any `PENDING` redemption; neither call takes a request body. Cancellation moves the redemption `PENDING → VOID`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, sets `void_reason` to `MEMBER_CANCELLED`, lowers the offer's `inventory_reserved` by 1, recomputes `sold_out` and increments `version`. Three system-driven paths reach `VOID` from `PENDING` as well, and they differ only in the reason recorded: a storefront freeze records `PROGRAM_FROZEN`, a membership becoming suspended or revoked records `MEMBERSHIP_INELIGIBLE`, and the epoch sweep passing `verification_expires_at` records `VERIFICATION_TTL_EXPIRED`. All three release the reserved inventory unit and recompute `sold_out` in exactly the same way.

Redemption listings may be filtered by `offer_id`, `membership_id`, `employer_id`, `merchant_id`, `statement_id`, `verification_status` and `fulfillment_status`, but the filters never widen visibility: an `Employee` sees only rows belonging to its own membership and a `Merchant` only rows carrying its own `merchant_id`, whatever filters are supplied, and an `EmployerAdmin` is refused outright per §2.10.

### 3.8. Fulfilment, Disputes and Partner Accountability

Fulfilment is the merchant's assertion that the goods or service actually reached the employee. The owning `Merchant` fulfils a redemption whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `fulfillment_status` is `AWAITING`, supplying no request body; a redemption on any other pair of states is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`, and a merchant that does not own the row is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Fulfilment moves the fulfilment dimension `AWAITING → FULFILLED`, stamps `fulfilled_at` from the logical clock — this instant, not `verified_at`, is the settlement date a `CASH`-basis statement keys on — and increments `version`. The verification dimension is untouched.

An `Employee` may dispute its own redemption, and a dispute may be filed either before or after the merchant has claimed fulfilment: the fulfilment dimension moves to `DISPUTED` from `AWAITING` and from `FULFILLED` alike. The redemption's `verification_status` must be `VERIFIED` — a `PENDING`, `FAILED` or `VOID` redemption has no fulfilment to dispute — and the logical clock must be strictly earlier than `verified_at` plus 30 days, or the filing is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The filing window is measured from verification, never from fulfilment, so a merchant that delays fulfilment does not extend the employee's window.

The employee supplies a `reason`, which is one of `NOT_HONORED`, `WRONG_DISCOUNT`, `OFFER_EXPIRED`, `OUT_OF_STOCK` or `OTHER`, and a `description` of 1 to 1,000 characters. The description is the employee's narrative and is never exposed to the employer. The new **Dispute** is created in `OPEN`; it copies `redemption_id` from the path, `raised_by_membership_id` and `merchant_id` from the redemption, sets `response_deadline_at` to the logical clock plus 120 hours, starts `info_request_count` at 0 and `default_judged` at false, and leaves `response_note`, `remedy_offered_cents`, `outcome_credit_cents` and `resolved_at` null. Filing also increments the redemption's `version`. When the dispute is filed against an already-fulfilled redemption its `fulfilled_at` is left exactly as it was.

The owning `Merchant` responds with a `response_note` and, optionally, a `remedy_offered_cents` figure it is prepared to pay. A response is accepted from `OPEN` and from `INFO_REQUESTED`, in both cases moving the dispute to `MERCHANT_RESPONDED`; from any other state it is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE`. The logical clock must be strictly before `response_deadline_at`, or the response is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A second response overwrites `response_note` and `remedy_offered_cents` rather than accumulating; the dispute keeps only the latest response, and `version` is incremented each time.

The platform may push a responded dispute back for more information. A `PlatformAdmin` sends an information request against a dispute whose `status` is exactly `MERCHANT_RESPONDED` — from `OPEN`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or either resolved state the request is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE` — carrying no request body. The request moves the dispute `MERCHANT_RESPONDED → INFO_REQUESTED`, raises `info_request_count` by 1, extends `response_deadline_at` to the logical clock plus a fresh 120 hours, and increments `version`. This is a genuine cycle: response and information request may alternate, but only twice. When `info_request_count` has already reached 2 a third request is refused with `ERR_MAX_INFO_REQUESTS`, and the dispute must then be resolved on the record as it stands.

Resolution is the `PlatformAdmin`'s decision. It carries an `outcome` of `MERCHANT_FAULT` or `NO_FAULT` and an optional `outcome_credit_cents`, which defaults to 0 when omitted. A dispute may be resolved from `OPEN`, `MERCHANT_RESPONDED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` — the platform never has to wait for the merchant — and an already-resolved dispute is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE`. An `outcome` of `MERCHANT_FAULT` drives the dispute to `RESOLVED_MERCHANT_FAULT` and an `outcome` of `NO_FAULT` drives it to `RESOLVED_NO_FAULT`; both of those states are terminal. **The two outcomes diverge on the fulfilment target state, on every ledger write and on partner scoring**, and the divergence is the point of the whole mechanism.

A `MERCHANT_FAULT` resolution first checks the money: the requested `outcome_credit_cents` must be less than or equal to the redemption's `net_charged_cents`, or the resolution is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_NET` — the platform may not hand back more than the employee actually paid. It then sets `resolved_at` from the logical clock, stores `outcome_credit_cents`, increments the dispute's `version`, and runs a cascade five levels deep:

1. **The redemption.** Its fulfilment dimension moves `DISPUTED → REFUNDED`, `refunded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` is incremented. `REFUNDED` is terminal on the fulfilment dimension; the verification dimension stays `VERIFIED` and its economics figures are never rewritten.
2. **The ledger.** A commission-reversal entry is written for the negative of `commission_amount_cents`. When the redemption's `employer_share_cents` is greater than zero, an employer-share-reversal entry is written for the positive of `employer_share_cents` — the provider claws back the slice it paid away. When `outcome_credit_cents` is greater than zero, two further entries are written: a dispute-credit entry for the negative of `outcome_credit_cents`, being the goodwill paid to the employee, and a dispute-credit-recovery entry for the positive of `outcome_credit_cents`, being the same sum recovered from the partner. The partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` and the employer's `accrued_share_cents` are then recomputed from the ledger.
3. **The statement.** If the redemption's `statement_id` is not null and that statement's `status` is `FINALIZED`, the statement moves `FINALIZED → REOPENED`, as §3.9 describes.
4. **The partner.** `fault_event_count` rises by 1, `last_fault_at` is set to the logical clock, and `fulfillment_failure_score` is recomputed.
5. **The partner's offers and slots.** When the recomputed score reaches the suspension threshold and the partner's `status` is `ACTIVE`, that status moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_at` set to the logical clock, every `ACTIVE` offer of the partner is paused with `paused_reason` of `MERCHANT_SUSPENDED`, and every `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` placement slot of those offers is released with `release_reason` of `OFFER_PAUSED`, each formerly `CONFIRMED` slot writing its compensating placement-fee refund.

A `NO_FAULT` resolution takes the opposite branch on every one of those points. It performs no credit check at all, because it **forces `outcome_credit_cents` to 0 regardless of what the request asked for**. It sets `resolved_at` from the logical clock and increments the dispute's `version`. It moves the redemption's fulfilment dimension `DISPUTED → FULFILLED` — completing the cycle `AWAITING` or `FULFILLED` → `DISPUTED` → `FULFILLED` — and stamps `fulfilled_at` from the logical clock only when it was still null, so a redemption that had already been fulfilled before the dispute keeps its original `fulfilled_at`. It writes no ledger entry, never touches any statement, and leaves `fault_event_count`, `fulfillment_failure_score`, `accrued_commission_payable_cents` and `accrued_share_cents` exactly as they were.

The epoch sweep resolves abandoned disputes for the platform. In its fifth pass it finds every dispute whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `response_deadline_at` is at or before the logical clock, and — provided `response_note` is still null — sets `default_judged` to true, sets `outcome_credit_cents` to 0, and applies the identical `MERCHANT_FAULT` cascade described above, including the fault-count increment and any resulting partner suspension. Each default judgment counts once in `default_judged_dispute_count`. **A dispute in `MERCHANT_RESPONDED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` is never default-judged, however far past its deadline it sits**, because the merchant has engaged and the record deserves a human decision.

The partner's `fulfillment_failure_score` is a **decaying sum over the partner's merchant-fault disputes**, and it is recomputed both whenever a dispute of that partner enters `RESOLVED_MERCHANT_FAULT` and on every read of the partner, so decay is observable without any write at all. Each dispute whose `status` is `RESOLVED_MERCHANT_FAULT` contributes 20 points multiplied by a decay factor chosen from the age of its `resolved_at`. The age in whole days is the elapsed time from `resolved_at` to the logical clock divided by 86,400 seconds and floored. The factor is 1.0 when the age is 30 days or less, 0.5 when the age is between 31 and 90 days inclusive, and 0.0 when the age is 91 days or more. The per-event contributions stay exact — a half-decayed event contributes exactly 10 — and **the sum is rounded half up to a whole integer only at the very end**, once every contribution has been added. Two faults resolved within the last 30 days therefore score 40; if one of them has since aged to 35 days and the other to 26, the score is 30; and a lone fault 122 days old scores 0.

Reaching 40 points is what suspends a partner, and the threshold is tested only when the score is recomputed by a merchant-fault resolution. Decay never un-suspends anybody: a `SUSPENDED` partner stays `SUSPENDED` until a `PlatformAdmin` explicitly reinstates it. Reinstatement is accepted only from `SUSPENDED`, is refused from any other status with `ERR_MERCHANT_INVALID_STATE`, and requires that the score recomputed against the current logical clock has fallen strictly below 40 — while it is still 40 or more the call is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_SCORE_NOT_DECAYED`. A successful reinstatement moves the partner `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, clears `suspended_at` back to null and increments `version`. It is emphatically **not a rollback**: the offers that were paused stay `PAUSED` and the slots that were released stay `RELEASED`, and the partner must resume each offer and re-reserve each placement itself.

A `PlatformAdmin` may also terminate a partner outright. Termination is accepted from `PROSPECT`, `IN_NEGOTIATION`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, is refused against an already-`TERMINATED` partner with `ERR_MERCHANT_INVALID_STATE`, and takes a `terminated_reason`. It moves the partner to the terminal `TERMINATED` status and cascades four levels: every agreement of the partner whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PROVIDER_PROPOSED`, `MERCHANT_COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` is terminated with `terminated_reason` set to the fixed string `MERCHANT_TERMINATED`; each terminated agreement expires its `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE` and `PAUSED` offers; each expired offer releases its `RESERVED` and `CONFIRMED` slots, every formerly `CONFIRMED` slot writing its compensating placement-fee refund; and `active_agreement_count` is recomputed, which necessarily leaves it at 0. Platform termination **deliberately bypasses the category-coverage floor** of §3.2: that floor exists to stop a partner manager quietly walking away from a category an employer still features, not to stop the platform from shutting down a partner it no longer trusts.

Dispute listings may be filtered by `redemption_id`, `raised_by_membership_id`, `merchant_id` and `status`. A `Merchant` sees its own disputes and an `Employee` its own; an `EmployerAdmin` cannot read individual dispute detail at all, per §2.10.

### 3.9. Settlement: Revenue Share Statements and the Append-Only Ledger

A **RevenueShareStatement** is the periodic reckoning of what one employer is owed. Either a `PartnerManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` opens one by naming the `employer_id`, an inclusive `period_start`, an exclusive `period_end` and a `basis` of `ACCRUAL` or `CASH`. The `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, or the statement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; a statement for an unknown employer is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`. The statement is created in `DRAFT` with `included_redemption_count`, `gross_commission_cents`, `employer_share_cents`, `provider_retained_cents` and `reversal_adjustment_cents` all at 0 and with `finalized_at`, `reopened_at`, `reopen_reason`, `paid_at` and `payment_reference` all null. **Nothing whatsoever is computed at creation**; a draft statement is an empty container that names a period and a convention. Statements may be listed with the filters `employer_id`, `status` and `basis`.

Finalizing is the `PartnerManager`'s act — the `PlatformAdmin` who may pay a statement may never finalize one, and the reverse. Finalizing is accepted from `DRAFT` and from `REOPENED`; against a `PAID` statement it is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The period must also be closed: the `period_end` must lie strictly in the past relative to the logical clock, and while the clock is at or before `period_end` the call is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. Because state rules outrank temporal rules, a `PAID` statement whose period is also still open reports the invalid-state error, not the period error.

Finalizing recomputes all five totals from scratch against the statement's `basis`, which selects the **inclusion set** — and the two bases genuinely disagree about the same data. Under `ACCRUAL`, the inclusion set is every redemption of the statement's employer whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `verified_at` falls at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`, **regardless of `fulfillment_status`**: the provider recognises the commission when the discount was honoured. Under `CASH`, the inclusion set is every redemption of that employer whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `fulfillment_status` is `FULFILLED`, and whose `fulfilled_at` falls at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`; a verified-but-unfulfilled redemption is excluded even when its `verified_at` sits squarely inside the period, and a redemption verified in one period but fulfilled in the next belongs to the later one. Both windows are half-open at the upper end.

From the chosen inclusion set the totals follow directly, and **no rounding happens at this level at all** — every rounding decision was already taken per redemption in §3.7:

- `included_redemption_count` is the number of redemptions in the inclusion set.
- `gross_commission_cents` is the sum of their `commission_amount_cents`.
- `employer_share_cents` is the sum of their **already-rounded** per-redemption `employer_share_cents`. It is never obtained by applying `employer_revenue_share_bps` to `gross_commission_cents`; per-redemption rounding first, then summation. Two identical redemptions each carrying a 202-cent share sum to 404, whereas rounding a summed commission of 1,612 at 2,500 basis points would give 403 — and 403 is wrong.
- `provider_retained_cents` is `gross_commission_cents` less `employer_share_cents`.
- `reversal_adjustment_cents` is the sum of the `amount_cents` of every employer-share-reversal ledger entry carrying this statement's id. It is necessarily 0 on a first finalize and becomes non-zero only after a reopen.

Finalizing also claims its redemptions. It stamps the statement's id into the `statement_id` of every redemption the inclusion set selected whose `statement_id` is currently null. A redemption that was already stamped by an earlier statement **keeps its original `statement_id` but still counts in these totals**, so overlapping statements can double-count deliberately while the stamp records the first claim only. A reversal entry written later for a stamped redemption carries that same `statement_id`, which is how `reversal_adjustment_cents` finds it. Finalizing sets `finalized_at` to the logical clock, clears `reopen_reason` back to null, moves the statement to `FINALIZED` and increments `version`.

A finalized statement is not immutable, because a dispute can undo one of its lines. When a merchant-fault resolution writes a commission reversal against a redemption whose `statement_id` points at a `FINALIZED` statement, that statement is moved `FINALIZED → REOPENED` by the system: `reopened_at` is set from the logical clock, `reopen_reason` is set to the fixed string `DISPUTE_REVERSAL`, and `version` is incremented. **The previously computed totals are deliberately left in place, stale**, until the next finalize recomputes them — the reopened statement still shows what was published, and it is the re-finalize that nets the reversed redemption out and populates `reversal_adjustment_cents`. The full cycle is therefore `DRAFT → FINALIZED → REOPENED → FINALIZED`, and it can turn more than once.

Payment is the `PlatformAdmin`'s act and is accepted only from `FINALIZED`, carrying a `payment_reference` that is stored on the statement. It sets `paid_at` from the logical clock, moves the statement to the terminal `PAID` state and increments `version`. **Paying writes no ledger entry**: the employer's `accrued_share_cents` is a lifetime accrual of what has been earned, not a balance of what remains outstanding, so remittance does not change it. A `DRAFT` or `REOPENED` statement cannot be paid — the attempt is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — so a reopened statement must be re-finalized before it can be paid again.

The **LedgerEntry** is the single append-only record behind every monetary claim in the system. Entries are never updated and never deleted, they carry no `version` field, and they expose no write endpoint of their own: every entry is a side effect of some other operation. Each entry carries a `merchant_id`, which is always set, and a signed `amount_cents` whose **sign is stated from the provider's perspective — positive is provider inflow, negative is provider outflow** — plus a human-readable `memo` recording its provenance. `redemption_id` is null on placement-fee entries, `placement_slot_id` is null on redemption-economics entries, `employer_id` is null on placement-fee entries that carry no storefront revenue share, and `statement_id` is null until a statement finalization or reopen claims the entry. The eight `entry_type` values are:

- `COMMISSION_ACCRUAL`, positive, written at verification for the redemption's `commission_amount_cents`.
- `EMPLOYER_SHARE`, negative, written at verification for the redemption's `employer_share_cents`, and only when that figure is greater than zero.
- `COMMISSION_REVERSAL`, negative, written on a merchant-fault dispute resolution for the redemption's `commission_amount_cents`.
- `EMPLOYER_SHARE_REVERSAL`, positive, written on the same resolution for the redemption's `employer_share_cents`, and only when that figure is greater than zero.
- `PLACEMENT_FEE`, positive, written when a placement slot is confirmed, for the slot's `placement_fee_cents`.
- `PLACEMENT_FEE_REFUND`, negative, written when a `CONFIRMED` slot is released, for the same `placement_fee_cents`.
- `DISPUTE_CREDIT`, negative, written when a merchant-fault resolution grants a goodwill credit, for `outcome_credit_cents`.
- `DISPUTE_CREDIT_RECOVERY`, positive, written alongside it for the same `outcome_credit_cents`, recovering the credit from the partner.

The entries are paired so that reversals cancel exactly: a confirmed-then-released slot nets to zero, a verified-then-refunded redemption nets its commission to zero, and a goodwill credit and its recovery net to zero. Two entity fields are pure derivations from these rows and are recomputed rather than incremented. A partner's `accrued_commission_payable_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that partner's entries whose `entry_type` is one of `COMMISSION_ACCRUAL`, `COMMISSION_REVERSAL`, `PLACEMENT_FEE`, `PLACEMENT_FEE_REFUND` or `DISPUTE_CREDIT_RECOVERY`. An employer's `accrued_share_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that employer's entries whose `entry_type` is `EMPLOYER_SHARE` or `EMPLOYER_SHARE_REVERSAL`, **multiplied by negative one**, so that a share the provider paid away registers as a positive lifetime accrual to the employer; it is net of reversals and independent of whether any statement has been paid.

A `PlatformAdmin` may read any single entry, and an unknown id is refused with `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Entries may be listed by a `PlatformAdmin` or `PartnerManager` with the filters `redemption_id`, `placement_slot_id`, `statement_id`, `employer_id`, `merchant_id` and `entry_type`. The entries of one redemption may also be listed beneath that redemption by a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `Merchant`, optionally filtered by `entry_type`, and that listing is ordered by `created_at` ASC so the economic history of a single redemption reads top to bottom in the order it happened.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/perks` prefix, apart from the two test-harness control-plane endpoints. Resource creation returns HTTP 201; every other successful call returns HTTP 200, including the action endpoints that drive a state transition and the list endpoints. Path identifiers are resolved left to right as described in §2.9, and the "Authorized Role(s)" column lists the roles for which the endpoint is declared — a caller in any other role is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and a caller in a listed role reaching for another principal's row is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
