# Protective Equipment Reconditioning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Athletic protective equipment does not simply wear out; it *expires*. A football helmet that looks serviceable may be a decade past the manufacturer's service-life ceiling, and a shell that survives a visual check may still fail an impact-attenuation test. Reconditioning plants sit between the school or club that owns the equipment and the manufacturer whose performance standard governs it, and the plant's liability turns entirely on whether it can prove — years later — which technician measured which component, on which calibrated rig the shell was tested, which approved part was installed under which manufacturer warranty claim, and that every condemned shell was physically destroyed rather than quietly resold.

This document specifies the API for that plant. It covers the full custody chain: intake from a program's seasonal drop-off with a physical dock count reconciled against the packing list; model identification and age verification; cleaning, sanitization and teardown followed by component inspection against manufacturer criteria; shell testing on a calibrated impact rig with a pass, repair or condemn determination signed by a certifying inspector; replacement-part installation from an approved-parts cage with independent verification; recertification labelling with a traceable plant-wide daily code; destruction verification for condemned shells; loaner substitution when the plant cannot beat the program's season start; and commercial settlement with record retention.

Two properties dominate the design. The first is **segregation of duties** — the technician who installs a repair may not sign its verification, the officer who witnesses a destruction may not verify it, and determinations and label voids are reserved to distinct roles. The second is **conservation** — approved-part stock and shipment unit dispositions are both tracked as ledgers whose buckets must always re-sum to a fixed total, so that no unit and no part can silently disappear from the record. Every date, expiry and window rule in this system reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that a plant's turnaround commitments and retention deadlines are reproducible under test.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Plant Personnel, Program Contacts and Access Control

Every request identifies its caller with two headers. `X-User-Role` names the acting role and `X-User-ID` identifies the acting individual or program. There are six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `PlantManager`, `IntakeClerk`, `Technician`, `CertifyingInspector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `CustomerContact`.

`PlantManager` owns reference data — model specifications, approved parts, test benches, loaner units and customer accounts — and closes the commercial loop by settling shipments. `IntakeClerk` runs the dock: opening shipments, recording counts, registering units, identifying models, staging and releasing custody, and running loaner logistics. `Technician` performs teardown, component inspection, bench testing and part installation, but may neither sign determinations nor issue labels. `CertifyingInspector` signs pass, repair and condemn determinations, condemns units that cannot be reconditioned, and issues recertification labels, but may not record the impact readings they adjudicate. `ComplianceOfficer` owns destruction witnessing and verification, label voiding, and record retention purges. `CustomerContact` is a read-only representative of a single program.

Identity semantics differ between the plant roles and the program role, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantManager`, `IntakeClerk`, `Technician`, `CertifyingInspector`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `inspector_user_id` on a component inspection, `determined_by_user_id` on a shell test, `opened_by_user_id`, `installed_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a repair order, `issued_by_user_id` and `voided_by_user_id` on a certification label, and `witnessed_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a destruction record — and it compares those recorded values against each other for segregation-of-duties checks, but it never compares an operator identifier to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerContact`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CustomerAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CustomerAccount` compare the resource's account foreign key to `X-User-ID`.

A request that omits `X-User-Role` entirely, or that presents `X-User-Role` as `CustomerContact` without an `X-User-ID`, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This authentication gate applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.4, which is deliberately exempt so that a test harness can position the clock before establishing any identity. A caller whose role is not among those authorized for the endpoint it addressed is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); this role gate does apply to the clock endpoint, whose authorized set is the five plant roles and excludes `CustomerContact`. A `CustomerContact` that reaches an authorized read endpoint but targets an account, a shipment or a loaner assignment belonging to a different `CustomerAccount` is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403); this scope check is deliberately evaluated *after* the role gate, so that a caller with the wrong role for an endpoint still receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than a scope error. The four scoped reads are the single-account read, the shipment list, the single-shipment read, and the loaner-assignment list.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1, and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific workflow in §3 states a different ordering for an internal computation. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Plant Clock

Every persisted resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a shipment `id` and a unit `id` are indistinguishable by shape, and clients must not attempt to infer a resource's kind from its identifier. Each resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation that changes the row. This applies to `CustomerAccount`, `ModelSpec`, `ApprovedPart`, `TestBench`, `LoanerUnit`, `IntakeShipment`, `EquipmentUnit`, `ShellTest`, `RepairOrder`, `CertificationLabel`, `DestructionRecord` and `LoanerAssignment`. There is one deliberate exception: a `ComponentInspection` is an immutable measurement record and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` at all, because a recorded measurement is never edited. Correcting a mis-measured component is not an update; it requires condemning or re-intaking the unit.

Wall-clock time is never consulted. All date arithmetic, expiry comparison, window evaluation and system timestamping read a server-held **logical clock**. An administrative test-driving capability sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back. Before any such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never silently fall back to real time. Timestamps written by workflows — `accepted_at`, `certified_at`, `liability_waiver_signed_at`, `witnessed_at`, `verified_at`, `voided_at`, `returned_at`, `records_purged_at` — are all taken from this clock.

### 2.5. Version Stamping and Stale-Read Rejection

Two write operations in this system consume a client-supplied version of the `EquipmentUnit` they act on, because in both cases the operation legitimately supersedes an earlier view of the unit and therefore cannot be made deterministic by a state precondition alone.

Issuing a recertification label requires `expected_equipment_unit_version` as a mandatory, non-optional field of the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `EquipmentUnit` as the inspector last observed it. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the issue is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A label issued after a repair legitimately supersedes the inspector's earlier reading of the unit, so the stale-version check is what separates the two views and stops the inspector from certifying a unit whose history has moved on since they read it.

Creating a repair order likewise requires `expected_equipment_unit_version` as a mandatory field carrying the technician's last observed `version` of the unit. A mismatch against the unit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A newly created order legitimately supersedes an earlier draft order for the same unit, so without the stale-version check a second create observing an out-of-date unit state would succeed and the outcome would be indeterminate.

No other endpoint in this system takes any expected-version field. In particular, submitting, installing, verifying or cancelling a repair order, recording impacts, signing a determination, voiding a label, witnessing or verifying a destruction, staging or releasing custody, and every intake operation all carry no version input.

### 2.6. Money, Measurement and Rounding Precision

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents: `unit_price_cents`, `unit_price_cents_snapshot`, `line_total_cents`, `reserved_parts_cents`, `installed_parts_cents`, `labor_cents`, `provisional_total_cents` and `final_total_cents`. Wherever a documented computation step produces a fractional amount, that step rounds half up to a whole number of cents. Half-up rounding is applied at *every* documented rounding step, and the number and placement of those steps is itself part of the specification — §3.9 makes this explicit for the settlement calculation, where the same set of units produces two different totals depending on whether rounding happens before or after summation.

Severity indices are decimal numbers carried to exactly one decimal place, rounded half up. Component measurement values are recorded exactly as the technician supplied them, to two decimal places, with no rounding applied by the server. Service ages, unit counters, part quantities and label sequence numbers are all non-negative integers. Date computations operate in whole days against UTC midnight boundaries.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the error returned is always the one belonging to the earliest applicable rung of a fixed ladder. The ladder, from first-evaluated to last-evaluated, is:

1. **Authentication** — missing `X-User-Role`, or `CustomerContact` without `X-User-ID`: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — acting role not authorized for the endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Account scope** — a `CustomerContact` reading another program's data: `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. **Request schema** — a required field absent, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared set, a numeric field out of range (any of the peak-g readings below zero, a part-line `quantity` below 1, a `counted_unit_count` below 1, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, a `record_retention_years` below 1), or a window whose `window_end` is not strictly after its `window_start`: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Existence** — a referenced row does not exist. Within this rung the path identifier is resolved first, and then foreign-key fields in this declaration order: `customer_account_id`, `model_spec_id`, `intake_shipment_id`, `equipment_unit_id`, `test_bench_id`, `approved_part_id`, `loaner_unit_id`. The error returned is the specific not-found code for the first unresolved reference.
6. **Settlement readiness** — a settle request while units remain on the shop floor: `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_READY`. This rung sits deliberately ahead of the generic state rung so that a premature settlement names the specific reason rather than a generic status error; a shipment that has not yet reached `READY_FOR_RETURN` always has a non-zero in-process counter, so this is what a caller settling too early actually sees.
7. **State guards** — the target resource is not in a status from which the requested transition is defined: `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SHELL_TEST_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_VOIDED`, `ERR_DESTRUCTION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_LOANER_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, each on its own family of operations.
8. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
9. **Condemnation mandate** — `ERR_CONDEMN_REQUIRED`.
10. **Determination consistency** — `ERR_DETERMINATION_MISMATCH`.
11. **Determination override direction** — `ERR_DETERMINATION_UPGRADE_FORBIDDEN`.
12. **Part suitability** — `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` and `ERR_PART_NOT_APPROVED`, which share a rung.
13. **Model reconditionability** — `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RECONDITIONABLE`.
14. **Bench calibration currency** — `ERR_BENCH_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`.
15. **Inspection completeness** — `ERR_INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE`.
16. **Duplicate inspection** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_INSPECTION`.
17. **Counting and retest constraints** — `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_UNIT_REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE` and `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, which share a rung.
18. **Part stock availability** — `ERR_PART_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`.
19. **Bench window conflict** — `ERR_BENCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
20. **Bench daily quota** — `ERR_BENCH_DAILY_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`.
21. **Loaner overlap** — `ERR_LOANER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.
22. **Loaner certification window** — `ERR_LOANER_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`.
23. **Label issue window** — `ERR_LABEL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
24. **Retention still active** — `ERR_RETENTION_ACTIVE`.
25. **Liability waiver on file** — `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING`.
26. **Warranty claim attribution** — `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_REQUIRED`.
27. **Billing hold** — `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD`.
28. **Settlement lock** — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_LOCKED`.
29. **Stale unit version** — `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION`, evaluated last of all, after every state and business check, so that a stale expected version on an otherwise invalid request still surfaces the business error rather than masking it.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright because they are easy to get backwards. A label issue against a unit still in `TESTED` (rather than `TEST_PASSED`) whose owning program is simultaneously on a billing hold returns `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), because the state rung precedes the fiscal rung; move that same unit to `TEST_PASSED` and the identical request returns `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 402). Likewise, a loaner booking for a program that has never acknowledged a waiver *and* is on a billing hold returns `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING` (HTTP 409), because the referential rung precedes the fiscal rung; acknowledge the current waiver revision and the same request returns `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 402).

### 2.8. Conservation Ledgers

Two families of counters in this system are ledgers rather than statistics, and both hold an invariant that must be true at every observable moment — meaning after every request completes, not merely eventually.

For every `ApprovedPart`, the quantity originally booked into the parts cage always equals the sum of the three moving buckets: `received_qty` equals `on_hand_qty` plus `reserved_qty` plus `consumed_qty`. No operation in this system ever changes `received_qty` after creation, so the three buckets always re-sum to it. Reserving stock moves quantity out of `on_hand_qty` into `reserved_qty`; installing moves it out of `reserved_qty` into `consumed_qty`; cancelling an order that still holds reservations moves it back out of `reserved_qty` into `on_hand_qty`. A reservation attempt that would drive any part's `on_hand_qty` below zero is rejected before any bucket moves at all, so `on_hand_qty` never goes negative and a rejected reservation leaves all three buckets exactly as they were. The net effect of a reserve followed by a cancel is zero drift.

For every `IntakeShipment` that has reached `ACCEPTED` or any later status, `counted_unit_count` always equals `units_in_process` plus `units_certified` plus `units_condemned` plus `units_released`. At acceptance `units_in_process` is set to `counted_unit_count` and the other three are set to zero. Every subsequent disposition change moves exactly one unit between exactly two buckets: certifying a unit moves one from `units_in_process` to `units_certified`; condemning one moves it from `units_in_process` to `units_condemned`; releasing a certified unit into the program's custody moves it from `units_certified` to `units_released`; and voiding a label moves one back from `units_certified` to `units_in_process`. Because every mutation is a paired decrement and increment, the four counters can never drift away from `counted_unit_count`.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two steps in this system must be performed by two different people, and the check is on `X-User-ID`, not on role. Verifying a repair is refused when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the order's `installed_by_user_id` — the technician who installed the parts cannot sign off on their own work. Verifying a destruction is refused when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the record's `witnessed_by_user_id` — the officer who watched the shell being destroyed cannot also be the officer who certifies the evidence. Both refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

Role separation reinforces the same principle structurally: recording impact readings and signing the resulting determination are split between `Technician` and `CertifyingInspector`, and label voiding and destruction handling are reserved to `ComplianceOfficer` so that neither the technician who worked on a shell nor the inspector who certified it can retroactively unwind that certification.

### 2.10. Filtered Reads and Path Resolution

Path parameters are validated left to right. A read of the inspections recorded on a unit, the labels issued for a unit, or the destruction records raised for a unit resolves the unit identifier first; if no such unit exists, the request fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` before any child collection is considered. The same principle governs every sub-collection read: the parent must exist before the child filter is evaluated.

Sub-collection and top-level list endpoints accept narrow filter parameters in addition to `limit` and `offset`. Approved parts may be filtered by `model_spec_id` and `component_code`. Loaner units accept `model_spec_id`, `status` and `condition_grade`. Shipments accept `customer_account_id` and `status`. Equipment units accept `intake_shipment_id`, `model_spec_id`, `recon_status` and `custody_status` — note that the two state axes are filterable independently, which is the point of keeping them separate. A unit's inspections accept `component_code` and `verdict`. Shell tests accept `equipment_unit_id`, `test_bench_id` and `status`. Repair orders accept `equipment_unit_id` and `status`. A unit's certification labels and its destruction records each accept `status`. Loaner assignments accept `customer_account_id`, `requested_model_spec_id`, `loaner_unit_id` and `status`. Every filter parameter is optional; omitting all of them returns the unfiltered collection.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Accounts, Liability Documentation and Billing Posture

A `PlantManager` opens an account for each program the plant serves, supplying the `org_name` — between 1 and 120 characters, and required — together with an `org_type` drawn from `SCHOOL`, `CLUB`, `COLLEGE` or `MUNICIPAL`, a primary `sport` drawn from `FOOTBALL`, `LACROSSE`, `HOCKEY` or `BASEBALL`, and a `contact_email` used for billing and notification. A newly opened account starts with `status` set to `ACTIVE`, `liability_waiver_status` set to `MISSING`, and `open_shipment_count` at 0. Its `billing_hold_reason`, `liability_waiver_version`, `liability_waiver_signer`, `liability_waiver_signed_at` and `loaner_recall_due_at` all begin as null.

Liability documentation is the gate that lets equipment change hands, and it is recorded by acknowledgment. Either a `PlantManager` or an `IntakeClerk` may record the program's signed waiver, supplying the `waiver_version` the program signed and the `signer_name` of the officer who signed it. The acknowledgment writes `liability_waiver_version` from the supplied revision, `liability_waiver_signer` from the supplied printed name, and `liability_waiver_signed_at` from the logical clock, then recomputes `liability_waiver_status`. That computed posture takes exactly three values: `MISSING` while `liability_waiver_version` is still null; `ON_FILE` when the recorded revision is exactly the revision the plant currently requires, which is the string `2026.1`; and `STALE` when some other, non-null revision is on file. A program that signed last season's paperwork therefore reads `STALE`, not `ON_FILE`, and is treated exactly as harshly as one that never signed at all. Three operations demand an `ON_FILE` posture: accepting a shipment into reconditioning, releasing a finished unit into the program's custody, and booking a loaner. Each of the three rejects a `MISSING` or `STALE` account with `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING` (HTTP 409).

Commercial posture is a separate axis. A `PlantManager` may place or clear a billing hold on an account by supplying a boolean `hold` and, optionally, a `reason`. When `hold` is true the account's `status` becomes `BILLING_HOLD` and `billing_hold_reason` records the supplied reason; when `hold` is false the `status` returns to `ACTIVE` and `billing_hold_reason` is cleared to null. Either way the account's `version` advances by one. A `BILLING_HOLD` account blocks three operations — issuing a recertification label, settling a shipment, and booking a loaner — each rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` and HTTP 402. Note carefully what a hold does *not* block: intake, counting, acceptance, teardown, inspection, testing and repair all proceed normally, because the plant's liability exposure does not pause because an invoice is late. What stops is the issuing of new certifications and the closing of the commercial loop.

Two account fields track the plant's ongoing exposure across shipments. `open_shipment_count` is the number of that program's shipments currently in `ACCEPTED` or `IN_PROCESS` status: it rises by one when a shipment is accepted, falls by one when a shipment reaches `READY_FOR_RETURN`, and rises by one again if a shipment is reopened by a label void. `loaner_recall_due_at` is the deadline by which the program must return borrowed equipment: whenever `open_shipment_count` falls to 0, it is set to the logical clock plus a two-week recall grace period of 14 days; whenever `open_shipment_count` rises above 0 again, it is cleared back to null, because a program with equipment still in the plant has a legitimate continuing need for a loaner.

### 3.2. Plant Reference Data: Models, Approved Parts, Impact Rigs and Loaners

Everything the plant does is anchored to a **model specification** — the manufacturer's statement of what a given shell is, how long it may stay in service, and how its components must be judged. A `PlantManager` registers one by supplying the `manufacturer` name, a `model_code` between 1 and 40 characters that must be unique across all model specifications, and the `standard_code` of the applicable performance standard, which is printed verbatim on every recertification label the plant issues for that model. The `shell_material` is either `POLYCARBONATE` or `ABS`, and it feeds a material offset in the severity computation described in §3.5. Three lifetime numbers follow: `max_service_life_years`, the manufacturer's service-life ceiling in whole years, between 1 and 25; `warranty_years`, the warranty term in whole years counted from manufacture, between 0 and 10; and `record_retention_years`, the number of years records must be retained after a unit is certified or destroyed, between 1 and 10. Two severity bands complete the performance envelope: `pass_si_max` is the highest severity index still certifiable without repair, inclusive, and `repair_si_max` is the highest severity index that can be repaired at all — above that the shell is condemned. The reference bands the plant uses for its standard shells are 300.0 for `pass_si_max` and 360.0 for `repair_si_max`. `max_retest_count`, between 0 and 5, caps how many retests a unit may receive after its first shell test. `reconditionable` is a boolean that defaults to true when omitted from the registration and is set false for models the manufacturer has withdrawn from reconditioning entirely.

The inspection contract for a model comes in two paired parts. `required_component_codes` is a non-empty subset of `SHELL`, `LINER`, `FACEMASK`, `CHINSTRAP` and `HARDWARE`, naming every component that must be individually inspected before a unit may advance past teardown. `component_criteria` is an array carrying exactly one object per entry in `required_component_codes`, and a registration whose criteria do not cover every required code is rejected as an invalid request. Each criteria object names the `code` it governs, the `metric` being measured, the `metric_direction` — either `LOWER_IS_WORSE` or `HIGHER_IS_WORSE` — the `replace_threshold` at which the component must be replaced, the `fail_threshold` at which the component is condemned outright, and the `unit` of measure the criteria are expressed in.

Replacement stock lives in the parts cage. A `PlantManager` books in an approved component by naming its `model_spec_id`, the manufacturer's `part_number` — unique within that model, so two models may legitimately share a part number — the `component_code` the part replaces, a boolean `oem_approved` that is true only for components the manufacturer approves for reconditioning use, a `unit_price_cents` of zero or more, and the `received_qty` of zero or more units physically received into the cage. Booking a part in fixes `received_qty` permanently and initialises the three moving buckets so that `on_hand_qty` equals `received_qty` while `reserved_qty` and `consumed_qty` are both 0, establishing the conservation identity of §2.8 from the very first moment.

Impact rigs are registered with the operating limits that govern their scheduling. A rig carries a `bench_code` unique across all benches, a `test_type` of either `DROP_IMPACT` or `PNEUMATIC_RAM`, a `daily_session_quota` of at least 1 capping how many shell-test sessions it may hold per UTC calendar day, and a `cooldown_minutes` between 0 and 240 that defaults to 45 minutes when the registration omits it. A newly registered rig has a null `calibration_valid_until` and therefore cannot host a single session until a `PlantManager` calibrates it; calibration supplies the `calibration_valid_until` expiry directly and advances the bench's `version` by one.

The loaner pool is the plant's answer to a program whose season starts before its own equipment can come home. A loaner unit belongs to exactly one model — loaners are only interchangeable within a model — and is registered with its `model_spec_id`, a plant `asset_tag` unique across all loaner units, a `condition_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C` capturing cosmetic and fit quality, and a `certification_valid_until` marking the expiry of the loaner's own recertification. A new loaner enters the pool with `status` `AVAILABLE` and `assignment_count` at 0, where `assignment_count` counts every assignment ever created against that loaner. The loaner's `status` is derived rather than set directly: it reads `AVAILABLE` whenever no assignment in `RESERVED` or `OUT_ON_LOAN` status references it, and otherwise mirrors the status of the earliest-starting active assignment — a loaner may legitimately hold several non-overlapping future bookings at once, and it is the first of them that determines what the pool sees.

### 3.3. Dock Intake: Counting, the Discrepancy Fork and Acceptance

A season's work arrives as a shipment. An `IntakeClerk` opens one against a program by supplying the `customer_account_id`, the `sport` the shipment belongs to drawn from `FOOTBALL`, `LACROSSE`, `HOCKEY` or `BASEBALL`, a commercial `service_tier` of `STANDARD`, `SEASONAL_CONTRACT` or `PRIORITY_RUSH`, the `declared_unit_count` of at least one unit that the program wrote on its packing list, and the program's competitive season as a `season_start_date` and a `season_end_date` at UTC midnight, where `season_end_date` must fall strictly after `season_start_date`. A shipment opens in `DRAFT` status with `counted_unit_count`, `discrepancy_count`, `discrepancy_resolution`, `accepted_at`, `raw_due_at`, `committed_due_at`, `provisional_total_cents` and `final_total_cents` all null, its four disposition counters and `units_registered` at zero, `loaner_required` false and `settlement_locked` false.

Counting the pallet is where the packing list meets reality, and it is the shipment machine's fork. An `IntakeClerk` records the physical dock tally as `counted_unit_count`, which must be at least 1, against a shipment that is still in `DRAFT`. The count writes `counted_unit_count`, derives `discrepancy_count` as the counted tally minus the declared tally — so a shortfall is negative and an overage positive — and advances the shipment's `version` by one. The resulting status depends entirely on that difference: when `discrepancy_count` is exactly zero the shipment moves `DRAFT → COUNTED`, and when it is anything other than zero the shipment moves `DRAFT → QUARANTINED` instead. Counting a shipment that has already left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Individual units are registered against a counted shipment, one request per physical shell. An `IntakeClerk` supplies the `intake_shipment_id`, the `serial_number` stamped in the shell, and optionally a `declared_model_code` — the model the program *claimed* on its packing list, which may be null and which the plant treats as a hint rather than as fact. Registration creates the unit with `recon_status` `RECEIVED` and `custody_status` `AT_PLANT`, and increments the shipment's `units_registered` by one. Registration is only defined while the shipment is in `COUNTED` or `QUARANTINED`; against a `DRAFT` shipment, whose tally is not yet known, or against one that has already reached `ACCEPTED`, `IN_PROCESS`, `READY_FOR_RETURN` or `CLOSED`, it is rejected with `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A registration that would push `units_registered` above `counted_unit_count` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — the plant will not carry more units on its books than it physically counted. Serial numbers must not collide: if another unit bearing the same `serial_number` already exists under the same program's account, the registration is rejected with `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409).

Acceptance is the moment the plant takes commercial and legal responsibility, and it is where the count fork rejoins. An `IntakeClerk` accepts a shipment by supplying a `discrepancy_resolution`, and the permitted value depends on which branch the shipment took. From `COUNTED`, where the tallies matched, the only permitted resolution is `MATCHED`. From `QUARANTINED`, where they did not, `MATCHED` is refused and the resolution must be either `CUSTOMER_CONFIRMED` — the program has agreed the plant's tally is correct — or `WRITTEN_OFF`, where the plant absorbs the difference. Supplying a resolution that no accept transition is defined for, given the shipment's branch, is rejected with `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), as is any accept attempted from a status other than `COUNTED` or `QUARANTINED`. Acceptance additionally demands that every counted unit has been registered: when `units_registered` does not equal `counted_unit_count` the request is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_REGISTRATION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). It also demands an `ON_FILE` waiver posture on the owning account, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING` (HTTP 409).

A successful acceptance does a great deal at once, and every part of it matters downstream. The shipment advances `COUNTED → ACCEPTED` or `QUARANTINED → ACCEPTED`, persisting the supplied `discrepancy_resolution` in the process. `accepted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The disposition ledger is initialised, setting `units_in_process` to `counted_unit_count` and `units_certified`, `units_condemned` and `units_released` all to zero. The turnaround commitment described in §3.9 is computed, writing `raw_due_at`, `committed_due_at` and `loaner_required`. The provisional settlement figure, also described in §3.9, is computed and written to `provisional_total_cents`. On the owning account, `open_shipment_count` increases by one and `loaner_recall_due_at` is cleared to null. Finally the shipment's `version` advances by one.

From `ACCEPTED` the shipment slides into active work without any explicit request: the first unit of the shipment to leave `RECEIVED` drives the shipment `ACCEPTED → IN_PROCESS` and advances its `version` by one. The reverse boundary is equally implicit — when a unit-level disposition change brings `units_in_process` down to 0, the shipment moves `IN_PROCESS → READY_FOR_RETURN`, decrements the account's `open_shipment_count` by one, and, if that leaves the account with `open_shipment_count` at 0, sets the account's `loaner_recall_due_at` to the logical clock plus 14 days; the shipment's `version` advances by one. A shipment can also travel that edge backwards, which §3.7 covers.

### 3.4. Identification, Age Verification, Teardown and Component Inspection

Identification binds a physical shell to the manufacturer's paperwork. An `IntakeClerk` identifies a unit still in `RECEIVED` status, belonging to a shipment in `ACCEPTED` or `IN_PROCESS` status, by supplying the authoritative `model_spec_id`, the `manufacture_date` read off the shell at UTC midnight, and an `age_basis` of either `MANUFACTURE_DATE` or `FIRST_SEASON`. The chosen `age_basis` is frozen at identification and never revisited. Identifying a unit that is not in `RECEIVED` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and a `model_spec_id` that resolves to nothing is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_SPEC_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Identification advances the unit `RECEIVED → IDENTIFIED`, advances its `version` by one, and — when the parent shipment was still in `ACCEPTED` — drives that shipment `ACCEPTED → IN_PROCESS`.

Age verification is deliberately dual-basis, because the two industry conventions disagree and the disagreement changes outcomes. Under the `MANUFACTURE_DATE` basis, `service_age_years` is the whole number of elapsed days between the manufacture date and the current date on the logical clock, divided by 365 days per service year and rounded down to a whole number. Under the `FIRST_SEASON` basis, `service_age_years` is instead the calendar year of the logical clock minus the calendar year of the manufacture date, because season-anchored ageing counts competition seasons rather than elapsed days: a shell manufactured in September 2016 has been through ten seasons by August 2026 even though only nine full years have passed. Under either basis, `service_age_years` is never negative and is clamped at 0. `life_expired` is then set to true exactly when `service_age_years` is greater than or equal to the model's `max_service_life_years`, and false otherwise. Consider a unit whose stored `manufacture_date` is 15 September 2016 read against a clock of 1 August 2026, on a model whose `max_service_life_years` is 10: the `MANUFACTURE_DATE` basis yields nine whole years and leaves `life_expired` false, while the `FIRST_SEASON` basis yields ten and sets it true. Identical stored data, opposite outcome — which is precisely why the basis is captured explicitly rather than inferred.

Identification also settles two derived postures. `warranty_coverage`, which reads `PENDING` on an unidentified unit, becomes `IN_WARRANTY` when `service_age_years` is less than or equal to the model's `warranty_years` and `OUT_OF_WARRANTY` otherwise. `condemn_required` is recomputed as true whenever any one of three things holds: `life_expired` is true, or the unit's `inspection_verdict_summary` is `FAIL`, or the unit's model has `reconditionable` set to false. Because `condemn_required` blocks certification outright and forces a `CONDEMN` determination, a life-expired shell is effectively decided at the dock, before a single component is measured.

Teardown follows. A `Technician` records cleaning, sanitization and disassembly on a unit that is in `IDENTIFIED` status and whose `model_spec_id` is populated; the request carries no body. Teardown advances the unit `IDENTIFIED → DISASSEMBLED` and advances its `version` by one. A unit whose model has `reconditionable` set to false cannot be torn down at all: the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RECONDITIONABLE` (HTTP 422). Attempting teardown from any status other than `IDENTIFIED` yields `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Component inspection is the plant's evidentiary core, and each measurement is a permanent record. A `Technician` records one inspection against a `DISASSEMBLED` unit by supplying the `component_code` being inspected, the `measurement_value` observed, the `measurement_unit` as recorded at the bench, and optionally a free-text `note`. At most one inspection may exist per component code per unit; a second attempt on a code the unit already carries is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INSPECTION` (HTTP 409), and because inspection records are immutable there is no correction path short of condemning or re-intaking the unit. Recording an inspection against a unit that is not `DISASSEMBLED` yields `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The recorded row snapshots the manufacturer criteria in force at that moment — `metric_direction_applied`, `replace_threshold_applied` and `fail_threshold_applied` are all copied from the criteria object of the unit's model whose `code` matches the supplied `component_code` — so that a later edit to the model specification cannot retroactively change a verdict already reached. `inspector_user_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`.

The `verdict` follows from the snapshotted criteria, and the direction of the comparison is what the `metric_direction_applied` value decides. When it is `LOWER_IS_WORSE`, the verdict is `FAIL` if `measurement_value` is less than or equal to `fail_threshold_applied`, otherwise `REPLACE` if `measurement_value` is less than or equal to `replace_threshold_applied`, and otherwise `PASS`. When it is `HIGHER_IS_WORSE`, the verdict is `FAIL` if `measurement_value` is greater than or equal to `fail_threshold_applied`, otherwise `REPLACE` if `measurement_value` is greater than or equal to `replace_threshold_applied`, and otherwise `PASS`. Both comparisons are inclusive at the threshold — a liner measured at exactly its replace threshold is `REPLACE`, not `PASS` — and `FAIL` is always evaluated before `REPLACE`, so a value that satisfies both conditions is a `FAIL`.

Each recorded inspection also refreshes the unit's rolled-up posture. `inspection_verdict_summary` is recomputed over every inspection the unit carries, in a strict order of precedence: it is `PENDING` while any code in the model's `required_component_codes` still lacks an inspection; otherwise it is `FAIL` when any `SHELL` inspection carries verdict `FAIL`; otherwise it is `REPLACE_REQUIRED` when any inspection on a non-`SHELL` code carries verdict `REPLACE` or `FAIL`; and otherwise it is `CLEAR`. A `FAIL` summary sets `condemn_required` to true. Once — and only once — the inspection set covers every code in `required_component_codes`, the unit advances `DISASSEMBLED → INSPECTED` automatically, with no separate request; the unit's `version` advances by one on each recorded inspection.

### 3.5. Bench Scheduling, Impact Recording and the Severity Index

Shell testing occupies a physical rig for a window of time, so a session is booked before it is run. A `Technician` books one by supplying the `equipment_unit_id` of the shell under test, the `test_bench_id` of the rig, an inclusive `window_start` and `window_end` for the session — where `window_end` must fall strictly after `window_start` — and the environmental `conditioning` to be applied before the drops, drawn from `AMBIENT`, `HOT` or `COLD`. A booking is only defined for a unit whose `recon_status` is `INSPECTED` or `REPAIRED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A unit whose `inspection_verdict_summary` is still `PENDING` — meaning at least one code in the model's `required_component_codes` has no inspection yet — cannot be booked at all, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). A booking that would take the unit past its model's `max_retest_count` is rejected with `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); the check is that the unit's current `retest_count` has already reached or passed that ceiling.

The rig itself imposes two independent scheduling limits, and they fail differently. The first is a cooldown envelope: the requested window is expanded by the bench's `cooldown_minutes` on *both* sides, and if that expanded interval intersects the window of any existing session on the same bench, the booking is rejected with `ERR_BENCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Both intervals are treated as closed, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide. With the default cooldown of 45 minutes — three quarters of an hour of rig recovery on each side — two sessions on the same rig must therefore be separated by more than an hour and a half of clear time. The second limit is an aggregate daily cap: the number of sessions already booked on that bench whose `window_start` falls on the same UTC calendar day as the requested `window_start` is counted, and if that count has already reached the bench's `daily_session_quota` the booking is rejected with `ERR_BENCH_DAILY_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The two limits are genuinely independent: a set of bookings can each clear the cooldown envelope individually and still exceed the collective daily cap.

A booked session is created in `SCHEDULED` status with `peak_g_front`, `peak_g_side`, `peak_g_crown`, `severity_index`, `computed_result`, `determination`, `override_reason` and `determined_by_user_id` all null. Its `attempt_index` is 1 for the unit's very first session and one greater than the previous session's `attempt_index` for each session after that. The unit's `retest_count` counts sessions beyond the first, so it stays at zero through the first booking and increases by one for every subsequent session — which is exactly the counter the retest cap reads.

Running the session means recording three impact-site readings. A `Technician` supplies `peak_g_front`, `peak_g_side` and `peak_g_crown`, none of which may be negative, against a session in `SCHEDULED` status; a session in any other status is rejected with `ERR_SHELL_TEST_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The rig's calibration must still be current: if the bench's `calibration_valid_until` is null, or falls earlier than the logical clock, the recording is rejected with `ERR_BENCH_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Recording stores the three readings, computes the severity index and the machine result, advances the session `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, advances the unit `INSPECTED → TESTED` or `REPAIRED → TESTED` depending on which side of the recertification cycle the unit is on, copies the session's `severity_index` onto the unit as `current_si`, and advances both the session's and the unit's `version` by one. The unit's `current_si` always reflects the severity index of its most recent session that has left `SCHEDULED`.

The **severity index** weights the three impact sites unequally and caps each one before they are combined. The front site contributes its peak-g reading multiplied by 0.5, the side site its reading multiplied by 0.3, and the crown site its reading multiplied by 0.2; each of the three contributions is then individually capped at 150.0, so a single catastrophic reading cannot dominate the whole index. The three capped contributions are added together to give a raw index. That raw index is multiplied by the conditioning multiplier for the session's `conditioning` — 1.00 for `AMBIENT`, 1.15 for `HOT`, and 1.08 for `COLD` — and then the material offset for the model's `shell_material` is added: 0 for `POLYCARBONATE` and 25 for `ABS`. The result is rounded half up to one decimal place and stored as `severity_index`. The order of these steps is part of the specification: the per-site cap is applied before summation, and the material offset is added after the conditioning multiplier has been applied, never before.

Consider a shell reading 300 g at each of the three sites, tested `AMBIENT` on a `POLYCARBONATE` model. The capped contributions are 150.0, 90.0 and 60.0, summing to a raw index of 300.0; the `AMBIENT` multiplier leaves it unchanged and the `POLYCARBONATE` offset adds nothing, so `severity_index` is 300.0. Against a model whose `pass_si_max` is 300.0 that is a `PASS`, because the band is inclusive at its upper edge. The same three readings taken `HOT` on an `ABS` model instead give 300.0 multiplied by 1.15, which is 345.0, plus an offset of 25, for a `severity_index` of 370.0 — beyond a `repair_si_max` of 360.0, and therefore a `CONDEMN`. Cap saturation is equally consequential: readings of 400, 600 and 900 g would give uncapped contributions of 200.0, 180.0 and 180.0 for a total of 560.0, but the per-site cap pulls each to 150.0 for a capped total of 450.0 — still a `CONDEMN`, but at a very different number.

`computed_result` is then the band the index falls into: `PASS` when `severity_index` is less than or equal to the model's `pass_si_max`, otherwise `REPAIR` when it is less than or equal to the model's `repair_si_max`, and otherwise `CONDEMN`. Both band edges are inclusive, and the bands are evaluated in that order.

### 3.6. Determination, Repair Orders and Independent Verification

A machine result is not a decision. A `CertifyingInspector` signs the determination for a session that has reached `COMPLETED` status, supplying a `determination` of `PASS`, `REPAIR` or `CONDEMN` and, where the signed verdict differs from the machine's, an `override_reason` explaining the departure. Signing a session that is not in `COMPLETED` is rejected with `ERR_SHELL_TEST_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The relationship between the signed `determination` and the stored `computed_result` is governed by two rules that must be read together. If the two differ and no `override_reason` accompanies the request, the signature is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). If an `override_reason` is present but the signed determination is *less* severe than `computed_result` under the severity ordering in which `PASS` is least severe, `REPAIR` more severe, and `CONDEMN` most severe, the signature is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_UPGRADE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422). An inspector may always be stricter than the rig; never more lenient. And when the unit's `condemn_required` is true, the only determination the inspector may sign is `CONDEMN` — anything else is refused with `ERR_CONDEMN_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

Signing writes `determination` and `override_reason` onto the session, records `determined_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances the session `COMPLETED → DETERMINED`, advances its `version` by one, and drives the unit out of `TESTED` along one of three branches, each of which requires the unit to be in `TESTED` to begin with. A `PASS` determination advances the unit `TESTED → TEST_PASSED`, and is only defined while the unit's `condemn_required` is false. A `REPAIR` determination advances the unit `TESTED → REPAIR_PENDING`, and is likewise only defined while `condemn_required` is false. A `CONDEMN` determination advances the unit `TESTED → CONDEMNED` and carries no `condemn_required` precondition at all — condemnation is always available. All three branches advance the unit's `version` by one. The condemnation branch additionally sets the unit's `condemn_reason` and runs the four-hop cascade described in §3.8; the reason is chosen as the first applicable of `LIFE_EXPIRED` when the unit's `life_expired` is true, then `COMPONENT_FAIL` when its `inspection_verdict_summary` is `FAIL`, and otherwise `SI_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`.

A `REPAIR_PENDING` unit gets a repair order. A `Technician` opens one by supplying the `equipment_unit_id` and the mandatory `expected_equipment_unit_version` described in §2.5; a mismatch against the unit's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Opening an order against a unit whose model has `reconditionable` set to false is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RECONDITIONABLE` (HTTP 422), and against a unit that is not in `REPAIR_PENDING` with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A new order begins in `DRAFT` status with an empty `part_lines` array and `reserved_parts_cents`, `installed_parts_cents`, `labor_cents` and `verification_failure_count` all at zero, with `warranty_claim_ref`, `installed_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id` and `cancel_reason` all null. `opened_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the unit's `version` advances by one.

Replacement components are appended to a `DRAFT` order one line at a time. The `Technician` supplies the `approved_part_id` of the cage part, a `quantity` of at least one, the `lot_code` of the physical stock being drawn, and optionally an `oem_cert_ref` documenting the manufacturer's certificate for that lot. Two suitability rules guard the append: a part whose `model_spec_id` differs from the unit's `model_spec_id` is refused with `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and a part whose `oem_approved` is false is refused with `ERR_PART_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422) — the plant installs only components the manufacturer has blessed for reconditioning. Appending to an order that is not in `DRAFT` yields `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Each accepted append adds one value object to `part_lines` carrying a `line_index` that starts at 1 for the first line and is one greater than the highest existing index for each line after it, the `approved_part_id`, the part's `part_number` and `component_code`, the requested `quantity`, the supplied `lot_code` and `oem_cert_ref`, a `unit_price_cents_snapshot` copied from the part's `unit_price_cents` at that instant, a `line_total_cents` equal to the quantity multiplied by that snapshotted unit price, and a line-level `status` of `DRAFT`. Snapshotting the price is deliberate: a later price change in the cage must not re-price work already quoted. The order's `version` advances by one per appended line.

Submitting the order commits stock. A `Technician` submits a `DRAFT` order carrying at least one line; an order with no lines cannot be submitted and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every line is checked against its part's `on_hand_qty` first, across all lines, before any bucket is touched: if any line's `quantity` exceeds its part's `on_hand_qty`, the whole submission is refused with `ERR_PART_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409) and every stock bucket in the plant is left exactly as it was. On success each line's quantity moves out of its part's `on_hand_qty` and into that part's `reserved_qty`, each line's `status` moves `DRAFT → RESERVED`, the order's `reserved_parts_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `line_total_cents` over every line whose `status` is `RESERVED`, the order advances `DRAFT → PARTS_PENDING`, and its `version` advances by one.

Installation consumes what was reserved. A `Technician` records installation on a `PARTS_PENDING` order, supplying a `warranty_claim_ref` where one is needed. The warranty rule is unconditional in one direction: when the unit's `warranty_coverage` is `IN_WARRANTY`, an installation with no `warranty_claim_ref` is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), because an in-warranty repair must be attributed to a manufacturer claim *before* parts are consumed. On success, every line still in `RESERVED` has its quantity moved out of its part's `reserved_qty` and into that part's `consumed_qty`, and its `status` moves `RESERVED → INSTALLED`. The order recomputes `reserved_parts_cents` and `installed_parts_cents` — the latter being the sum of `line_total_cents` over lines whose `status` is `INSTALLED` — records `installed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances `PARTS_PENDING → INSTALLED`, and advances its `version` by one.

Verification is where segregation of duties bites, and where the order's cycle either closes or collapses. A `Technician` verifies an `INSTALLED` order by supplying an `outcome` of `PASSED` or `FAILED` and optionally a `note`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `installed_by_user_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A `PASSED` outcome records `verified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, sets `labor_cents` to the flat repair labour charge of 4,500 cents — forty-five dollars per closed repair — advances the order `INSTALLED → CLOSED`, and advances the unit `REPAIR_PENDING → REPAIRED`, with both `version` counters advancing by one. A `FAILED` outcome takes one of two branches according to how many attempts the order has already burned, and the plant allows at most two failed verification attempts. When the order's `verification_failure_count` plus one is less than or equal to 2, the counter is incremented and the order cycles back `INSTALLED → PARTS_PENDING`; lines already in `INSTALLED` status stay there, because the parts have been physically consumed and are not returned to stock. When `verification_failure_count` plus one exceeds 2, the counter is still incremented — so it records every failed attempt including the exhausting one — the order's `cancel_reason` is set to `verification attempts exhausted`, the order advances `INSTALLED → CANCELLED`, and the unit advances `REPAIR_PENDING → CONDEMNED` with `condemn_reason` `REPAIR_UNSUCCESSFUL` and the full four-hop cascade of §3.8. Verifying an order that is not `INSTALLED` yields `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

An order can be abandoned before it is installed. Either a `Technician` or a `PlantManager` cancels an order in `DRAFT` or `PARTS_PENDING` status, supplying a `cancel_reason`; cancelling a `CLOSED` or already `CANCELLED` order yields `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Cancelling from `PARTS_PENDING` compensates the reservation exactly: every line still in `RESERVED` has its quantity moved out of its part's `reserved_qty` and back into that part's `on_hand_qty`, its `status` reverts `RESERVED → DRAFT`, and the order's `reserved_parts_cents` is recomputed to 0. Cancelling from `DRAFT`, where nothing was ever reserved, touches no stock at all. Either way the order advances `DRAFT → CANCELLED` or `PARTS_PENDING → CANCELLED`, `cancel_reason` is recorded from the request, and the order's `version` advances by one.

A unit that reaches `REPAIRED` is not finished — it must be retested. Booking a fresh session for a `REPAIRED` unit and recording its impacts advances it `REPAIRED → TESTED`, closing the recertification cycle `TESTED → REPAIR_PENDING → REPAIRED → TESTED`, and the new session is adjudicated exactly like the first. The model's `max_retest_count` is what bounds how many times that loop may run.

### 3.7. Recertification Labelling, Label Voiding, Staging and Release

A shell that passes leaves the plant wearing a label, and that label is the plant's permanent traceability claim. A `CertifyingInspector` issues one by supplying the `equipment_unit_id` and the mandatory `expected_equipment_unit_version` of §2.5. The unit must be in `TEST_PASSED` status — from anything else the issue is refused with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — and its `condemn_required` must be false, failing which the issue is refused with `ERR_CONDEMN_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The owning program's account `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a `BILLING_HOLD` account blocks the issue with `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 402). There is also a freshness window on the passing result: if the logical clock is more than 14 days past the instant at which the unit's most recent `DETERMINED` session was signed — that session's `updated_at` — the pass is considered stale and the issue is refused with `ERR_LABEL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). A two-week-old pass is not a current certification. Finally, a mismatched `expected_equipment_unit_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The label's traceability code is composed at issuance and never recomputed. `certified_on` is the date portion of the logical clock. `standard_code_snapshot` is a copy of the model's `standard_code`, taken at issue time so that a later change to the model specification cannot alter a label already affixed to a shell. `daily_sequence` is a **plant-wide** running number within a single certification date: it is one greater than the count of all certification labels — across every unit, every shipment and every account — whose `certified_on` equals the `certified_on` now being assigned and whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `VOIDED`, considered in `created_at` order. Voided labels still consume their sequence number, so codes are never reused. The `label_code` itself is assembled as the fixed prefix `RCP`, then a hyphen, then `standard_code_snapshot`, then a hyphen, then `certified_on` rendered as four-digit year followed by two-digit month followed by two-digit day with no separators, then a hyphen, then `daily_sequence` rendered as a decimal number zero-padded to three digits. The first label issued on 1 August 2026 for a model whose `standard_code` is `ND002` therefore reads `RCP-ND002-20260801-001`; a second label the same day for a different model whose `standard_code` is `ND010` reads `RCP-ND010-20260801-002`; and if the first of those is later voided, the next label issued that day is still numbered 003.

Issuance is a cascade, not a single write. The new label is created in `ACTIVE` status with `issued_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and with `void_reason`, `voided_at` and `voided_by_user_id` all null. The unit advances `TEST_PASSED → CERTIFIED`, has `certified_at` stamped from the logical clock, and has `record_retention_until` set to `certified_at` advanced by the model's `record_retention_years`. On the parent shipment, `units_in_process` falls by one and `units_certified` rises by one; if that brings `units_in_process` to 0, the shipment advances `IN_PROCESS → READY_FOR_RETURN`, which in turn decrements the account's `open_shipment_count` and, when that reaches 0, sets the account's `loaner_recall_due_at` to the clock plus 14 days. The unit's `version` advances by one.

Voiding a label is the mirror image of issuing one, and it is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` precisely because it unwinds another role's signature. The officer supplies a `void_reason`. The label must still be `ACTIVE`; a second void on an already-`VOIDED` label is refused with `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_VOIDED` (HTTP 409). The unit must still be in the plant's or the loading dock's hands, meaning its `custody_status` is `AT_PLANT` or `STAGED_FOR_RETURN`, and its `recon_status` must be `CERTIFIED`; a unit already `RETURNED` to the program or already `DISPOSED` cannot have its label voided, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The parent shipment must not have been settled: when its `settlement_locked` is true the void is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409).

A permitted void runs four hops. First, the label's `status` moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, with `voided_at` stamped from the logical clock, `voided_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `void_reason` recorded from the request. Second, the unit reverts `CERTIFIED → REPAIR_PENDING`, and both its `certified_at` and its `record_retention_until` are cleared to null — the retention clock never started, because the certification never happened. Third, on the parent shipment `units_certified` falls by one and `units_in_process` rises by one, keeping the disposition ledger of §2.8 balanced. Fourth, if the shipment had already reached `READY_FOR_RETURN`, it reverts `READY_FOR_RETURN → IN_PROCESS`, which clears `final_total_cents` to null, sets `settlement_locked` back to false, increments the account's `open_shipment_count` by one and clears the account's `loaner_recall_due_at` to null. Both the label's `version` and the unit's `version` advance by one. The voided unit is now back in `REPAIR_PENDING` and must travel the repair-retest-determine-certify path again from the beginning.

Physical hand-back is tracked on the custody axis, which advances under its own requests and never touches `recon_status`. An `IntakeClerk` stages a unit for return, moving its `custody_status` `AT_PLANT → STAGED_FOR_RETURN`; the unit's `recon_status` must be `CERTIFIED` for staging to be defined, and the unit's `version` advances by one. The clerk then releases the staged unit into the program's custody, supplying the `released_to` printed name of the program agent who physically took it. Release requires `custody_status` `STAGED_FOR_RETURN` and `recon_status` `CERTIFIED`, and it requires an `ON_FILE` waiver posture on the owning account — a release to a program with a `MISSING` or `STALE` waiver is refused with `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING` (HTTP 409). Release moves `custody_status` `STAGED_FOR_RETURN → RETURNED`, records `released_to`, moves one unit on the parent shipment out of `units_certified` and into `units_released`, and advances the unit's `version` by one. Note that `units_certified` counts units whose `recon_status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `custody_status` is not yet `RETURNED`, which is exactly why release moves the unit between those two buckets rather than creating a new one.

### 3.8. Condemnation, Destruction Verification and Record Retention

Not every shell can be saved, and some are decided before they ever reach a rig. A `CertifyingInspector` condemns a unit whose `recon_status` is `IDENTIFIED`, `DISASSEMBLED` or `INSPECTED`, supplying a `condemn_reason` with the request; the operation is only defined while the unit's `condemn_required` is true, and an attempt against a unit that is neither in one of those three statuses nor flagged for condemnation is refused with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The `condemn_reason` the server persists is derived rather than trusted: it is the first applicable of `LIFE_EXPIRED` when the unit's `life_expired` is true, then `COMPONENT_FAIL` when its `inspection_verdict_summary` is `FAIL`, and otherwise `NOT_RECONDITIONABLE`, which is what remains when the model itself has `reconditionable` set to false. The unit advances to `CONDEMNED`, its `version` advances by one, and the four-hop cascade below runs.

That **condemnation cascade** is identical wherever condemnation originates — from a pre-test condemnation, from a `CONDEMN` determination on a completed session, or from a repair order whose verification attempts are exhausted — and all four hops must land. Hop one moves the unit's `custody_status` `AT_PLANT → PENDING_DISPOSAL`, advancing the unit's `version`. Hop two creates a destruction record in `PENDING` status referencing the unit through its `equipment_unit_id`, with `condemn_reason_snapshot` copied from the unit's `condemn_reason` at that instant and with `method`, `witness_name`, `witnessed_by_user_id`, `witnessed_at`, `verification_ref`, `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at` all null. Hop three decrements the parent shipment's `units_in_process` by one and increments its `units_condemned` by one. Hop four checks whether that brought `units_in_process` to 0 and, if so, advances the shipment `IN_PROCESS → READY_FOR_RETURN`, which itself decrements the account's `open_shipment_count` by one and, when that reaches 0, sets the account's `loaner_recall_due_at` to the logical clock plus 14 days. The condemn reason recorded by the determination path differs only in its precedence list, which prefers `SI_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` where the pre-test path prefers `NOT_RECONDITIONABLE`, and the repair-exhaustion path always records `REPAIR_UNSUCCESSFUL`.

Destruction is a two-person control, and the two persons must be different. A `ComplianceOfficer` first records witnessed destruction against a record in `PENDING` status whose unit's `custody_status` is `PENDING_DISPOSAL`, supplying the `method` used — `SHELL_CRUSH`, `BAND_SAW_CUT` or `SHREDDING` — and the printed `witness_name`. Witnessing stores the method and the name, records `witnessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `witnessed_at` from the logical clock, advances the record `PENDING → WITNESSED`, and advances its `version` by one. Witnessing a record that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_DESTRUCTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

A second `ComplianceOfficer` then verifies the evidence, supplying the `verification_ref` — a photo or scale-ticket reference proving the shell cannot re-enter resale. The record must be in `WITNESSED` status, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_DESTRUCTION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the record's `witnessed_by_user_id`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Verification stores `verification_ref`, records `verified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, and advances the record `WITNESSED → VERIFIED`. It also completes the unit's custody journey: the unit moves `custody_status` `PENDING_DISPOSAL → DISPOSED` and has its `record_retention_until` set to the verification instant advanced by the model's `record_retention_years`. Both the record's and the unit's `version` advance by one.

Record retention closes the loop on both outcomes. A unit's `record_retention_until` is derived from whichever terminal event it reached: `certified_at` advanced by the model's `record_retention_years` for a certified unit, or the destruction verification instant advanced by the same term for a destroyed one; while neither has happened it stays null, and a label void resets it to null. Once that deadline has passed, a `ComplianceOfficer` may purge the unit's retained reconditioning records, which stamps `records_purged_at` from the logical clock and advances the unit's `version` by one. The purge is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) whenever `record_retention_until` is null — meaning the retention clock never started — or is greater than or equal to the logical clock, meaning the term has not yet fully elapsed.

### 3.9. Turnaround Commitments, Loaner Substitution and Commercial Settlement

Turnaround is a promise made at acceptance, and it is bounded by the program's season rather than by the plant's convenience. Three inputs decide it: the shipment's `service_tier`, its `counted_unit_count`, and its `season_start_date`, all read against `accepted_at`. Batch capacity is expressed in units per shop day: the plant processes 25 units per batch day, so the batch allowance is the counted unit count divided by 25 and rounded *up* to a whole number of days. The tier contributes a base allowance of 30 days for both `STANDARD` and `SEASONAL_CONTRACT`, and only 10 days for `PRIORITY_RUSH`. `raw_due_at` is `accepted_at` advanced by the sum of the tier base allowance and the batch allowance, in whole days. Independently, the season deadline is `season_start_date` pulled back by a 7-day pre-season buffer, so the plant aims to have equipment back a full week before the first competition. `committed_due_at` is then the earlier of `raw_due_at` and that season deadline. `loaner_required` is set to true exactly when `raw_due_at` falls strictly later than `committed_due_at` — that is, when the season deadline is what clipped the commitment, meaning the plant has promised a date it cannot actually meet on its own throughput and must cover the gap with loaner equipment.

A concrete case makes the clipping visible. Take a shipment accepted on 1 August 2026 with 12 counted units and a season starting 25 August 2026, giving a season deadline of 18 August 2026. Under `PRIORITY_RUSH` the batch allowance is one day, so `raw_due_at` is 11 days after acceptance, namely 12 August 2026; that is earlier than the season deadline, so `committed_due_at` is also 12 August 2026 and `loaner_required` stays false. Under `STANDARD` the same shipment has a `raw_due_at` 31 days after acceptance, namely 1 September 2026; the season deadline is earlier, so `committed_due_at` becomes 18 August 2026 and `loaner_required` becomes true. The batch boundary is sharp: under `PRIORITY_RUSH`, 25 counted units still yield 12 August 2026 while 26 units push the raw date to 13 August 2026.

When a program needs cover, an `IntakeClerk` books a loaner. The booking names the borrowing `customer_account_id`, the `requested_model_spec_id` — loaners are only substitutable within a model — and the loan window as an inclusive `window_start` and an **exclusive** `window_end` that must fall strictly after the start. The clerk may name a specific `loaner_unit_id`, in which case `selected_by` is recorded as `EXPLICIT`; omitting it delegates the choice to the plant's selector policy and records `selected_by` as `SERVER_SELECTED`. The assignment's initial `status` depends on the clock at creation: `OUT_ON_LOAN` when `window_start` is less than or equal to the logical clock, and `RESERVED` when `window_start` is strictly later. `returned_at` begins null. Booking drives the chosen loaner `AVAILABLE → RESERVED` or `AVAILABLE → OUT_ON_LOAN` to match, increments that loaner's `assignment_count` by one, and advances the loaner's `version` by one. Two account gates apply: a program whose `liability_waiver_status` is not `ON_FILE` is refused with `ERR_LIABILITY_WAIVER_MISSING` (HTTP 409), and one whose account `status` is `BILLING_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 402).

When the clerk names a loaner explicitly, two dedicated checks guard that choice. If that loaner already carries an assignment in `RESERVED` or `OUT_ON_LOAN` status whose window intersects the requested window — treating both windows as half-open, so a booking that ends exactly when another begins is not an overlap — the request is refused with `ERR_LOANER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409). If that loaner's own `certification_valid_until` falls before the requested `window_end`, the request is refused with `ERR_LOANER_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422); the plant will not lend equipment whose certification lapses mid-loan.

When the clerk omits the loaner, the **selector policy** decides. A loaner is eligible only if all four of the following hold: its `model_spec_id` equals the requested `requested_model_spec_id`; its `status` is `AVAILABLE`; its `certification_valid_until` is greater than or equal to the requested `window_end`; and it carries no assignment in `RESERVED` or `OUT_ON_LOAN` status whose half-open window intersects the requested half-open window. Among the eligible pool, the winner is chosen by three ranking keys applied in order: first `condition_grade` ascending in the order `A`, then `B`, then `C`, so the best-conditioned loaner goes out first; then `assignment_count` ascending, so the least-worked loaner of a given grade is preferred; and finally `created_at` ascending as a deterministic last tie-break. The first row under that total ordering is selected and written to the assignment's `loaner_unit_id`. An empty eligible pool is an error rather than a null selection: it is refused with `ERR_NO_LOANER_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). To make the ordering concrete, suppose a request for a window running from 5 August 2026 to 12 August 2026 against some model, and four candidate loaners: one of grade `B` with an `assignment_count` of 0; one of grade `A` with an `assignment_count` of 7; a second of grade `A` with an `assignment_count` of 7 created later than the first; and one of grade `A` with an `assignment_count` of 0 but a `certification_valid_until` of 11 August 2026. The last of these is ineligible because its certification lapses before the window ends. Grade `A` outranks grade `B`, so the grade-`B` loaner loses despite having no prior loans, and between the two remaining grade-`A` loaners with equal counts, the earlier-created one wins.

Loaners come back through a single request. An `IntakeClerk` returns an assignment whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `OUT_ON_LOAN`, optionally supplying a `return_condition_note`. The return stamps `returned_at` from the logical clock, advances the assignment `RESERVED → RETURNED` or `OUT_ON_LOAN → RETURNED`, drives the loaner back `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` or `OUT_ON_LOAN → AVAILABLE` as appropriate, and advances both the assignment's and the loaner's `version` by one. Returning an assignment that is already `RETURNED` is refused with `ERR_LOANER_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Commercial settlement closes the shipment. A `PlantManager` settles a shipment whose `status` is `READY_FOR_RETURN` and whose `settlement_locked` is false; the request carries no body. A settle attempted while `units_in_process` is anything other than 0 is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409) — and because that rung precedes the generic state rung of §2.7, that is the error a caller settling too early actually receives. A settle on a shipment whose `settlement_locked` is already true is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), and one on a program whose account `status` is `BILLING_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_ON_HOLD` (HTTP 402). A successful settle computes the final total into `final_total_cents`, sets `settlement_locked` to true, advances the shipment `READY_FOR_RETURN → CLOSED`, and advances its `version` by one.

The settlement figure is computed by one rule with **two variants that differ only in where rounding happens** — and that difference is deliberately observable. The billable set is every equipment unit of the shipment whose `recon_status` is `CERTIFIED` or `CONDEMNED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order; units in any other reconditioning status are excluded from billing entirely. The tier multiplier is 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 1.00 for `SEASONAL_CONTRACT`, and 1.35 for `PRIORITY_RUSH`. For each unit in the billable set, the exact base amount is the flat base service charge of 8,996 cents — 89.96 dollars per unit — multiplied by that tier multiplier, without rounding. Each unit then attracts one extra: a `CONDEMNED` unit adds a condemned-handling charge of 2,500 cents, while a `CERTIFIED` unit adds the sum of `labor_cents` across that unit's repair orders whose `status` is `CLOSED`. The exact line amount is the exact base plus that extra.

The `PROVISIONAL` variant runs at acceptance and writes `provisional_total_cents`; it sums the exact line amounts across the set and rounds *once*, half up, at the end. Because no unit has reached `CERTIFIED` or `CONDEMNED` at acceptance time, the billable set is empty then, so the provisional run instead prices `counted_unit_count` synthetic lines, each being the exact base amount of 8,996 cents multiplied by the tier multiplier with no extras. The `FINAL` variant runs at settlement and writes `final_total_cents`; it rounds *each* line's exact amount half up to whole cents first and then sums the rounded lines, and it operates on the real billable set, so it also picks up the condemned-handling charge and the closed-repair labour. From that gross figure both variants continue identically: the discount rate is 0.000 when the gross is below 10,000 cents (100 dollars), 0.125 when the gross is below 100,000 cents (1,000 dollars), and 0.150 otherwise; the discount amount is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents; the net is the gross minus the discount; a compliance surcharge of 2 percent of the net is computed and rounded half up to whole cents; and the total is the net plus that surcharge.

The one-cent divergence between the two rounding orders is real and must be preserved. Take a `PRIORITY_RUSH` shipment whose billable set is two units in creation order: one `CERTIFIED` unit with a single `CLOSED` repair order contributing 4,500 cents of labour, and one `CONDEMNED` unit. Their exact line amounts are 8,996 times 1.35, which is 12,144.60, plus 4,500, giving 16,644.60; and 12,144.60 plus 2,500, giving 14,644.60. Summing first gives 31,289.20, which rounds to a gross of 31,289; a 0.125 discount of 3,911.125 rounds to 3,911, leaving a net of 27,378, on which a 2 percent surcharge of 547.56 rounds to 548, for a total of 27,926. Rounding each line first gives 16,645 plus 14,645, a gross of 31,290; the discount of 3,911.25 rounds to 3,911, leaving a net of 27,379, on which a surcharge of 547.58 rounds to 548, for a total of 27,927. Same units, same constants, and a one-cent difference carried all the way through the tier discount and the surcharge.
