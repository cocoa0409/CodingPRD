# Wind Farm Operations & Turbine Maintenance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A wind farm operator earns revenue only while its turbines turn, and it loses that revenue in three quite different ways: a turbine trips on a SCADA fault and nobody notices for hours, a gearbox fails and the crane that could lift it is three weeks out, or the grid operator instructs a curtailment that the site never reconciled against the landowner's royalty statement. Today those three loss channels are tracked in three unrelated spreadsheets, and the availability guarantee that the commercial team signed — the one with liquidated damages attached — is reconciled by hand at the end of each measurement period, usually after the numbers have stopped being actionable. This document specifies the backend service that closes that gap: a single system of record for supervisory control triage, maintenance planning, major-component replacement, blade inspection, curtailment, availability settlement and landowner payments.

The service is deliberately built as an operational ledger rather than a reporting tool. Every downtime hour, every lost kilowatt-hour, every cent of crane cost and every cent of landowner royalty is posted to a named counter at the moment the operational event happens, and those counters must reconcile at all times — not overnight. Two consequences follow, and they shape most of the rules below. First, the system carries a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time, because rolling reset windows, weather-window expiry, work-order ageing and measurement periods must be reproducible. Second, availability is computed **two ways from the same period data** — a time-based figure and an energy-based figure — because service agreements in this market are written on one basis or the other, and the two figures do not agree. The basis the agreement names is the one that determines liquidated damages; the other figure is still computed and still published, so the commercial team can see what the alternative basis would have cost.

The system is mounted under the `/wind-ops` prefix. Six operator personas share it under strict segregation of duties: the person who submits a work order may not be the person who signs off its completion, and the inspector who records a blade finding may not be the person who accepts that finding into the repair programme.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. Both headers must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of the six roles below. A request that omits either header, sends either as an empty value, or names a role outside the six is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing happens. A request whose headers are well-formed but whose role is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `SiteAdmin`, `ControlRoomOperator`, `MaintenancePlanner`, `ReliabilityEngineer`, `CrewLead` and `CommercialManager`.

- A **`SiteAdmin`** is the operator's site administrator. It registers wind farms, turbine models, turbines, technician crews, fault code rules and spare-part lines, books goods receipts against stock, decommissions turbines, and drives the logical clock. It also shares work-order cancellation with `MaintenancePlanner`, and it may read every collection in the system, including the commercially restricted service-agreement and landowner-settlement reads.
- A **`ControlRoomOperator`** works the supervisory control desk. It posts SCADA telemetry, raises fault codes against turbines, records triage dispositions, attempts remote resets, and issues, activates and releases curtailment instructions. It may read every collection except the commercially restricted service-agreement and landowner-settlement reads.
- A **`MaintenancePlanner`** owns the planning side. It records and confirms weather windows, raises and schedules work orders, requests and demobilizes cranes, and plans, branches and cancels major component replacements. Its reads exclude the commercially restricted service-agreement and landowner-settlement collections.
- A **`ReliabilityEngineer`** owns engineering judgement. It closes fault events, requests work-order revisions, waives low-consequence blade findings, and shares work-order raising, submission, starting, completion, blade-finding recording, blade-finding prioritization and replacement execution with other roles. It may read the commercially restricted collections.
- A **`CrewLead`** is the technician crew lead on site. It starts and completes the work orders assigned to its own crew, records blade findings, prioritizes findings recorded by somebody else, and executes component replacements. Its reads exclude the commercially restricted collections.
- A **`CommercialManager`** owns the money. It opens service agreements, settles and finalizes measurement periods, drafts landowner settlements, and approves or voids them. It may read the commercially restricted collections.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` differently across the six. When `X-User-Role` is `CrewLead`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Crew` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Crew` compare that resource's crew foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a `CrewLead` starts or completes a work order, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal that work order's `crew_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `SiteAdmin`, `ControlRoomOperator`, `MaintenancePlanner`, `ReliabilityEngineer` or `CommercialManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit key — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a work order at submission time, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a blade finding at recording time — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two segregation-of-duties rules run on top of role authorisation, and both are checked with the acting `X-User-ID` rather than the role. Completing a work order requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from that order's `submitted_by_user_id`. Prioritizing a blade finding requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from that finding's `recorded_by_user_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that these two rules are evaluated after basic role authorisation but before the crew-assignment ownership check: on work-order completion the order of evaluation is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, then `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise for a specific collection. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads omit those four fields from `meta`.

Filters named on a list endpoint combine conjunctively — a request naming both a farm filter and a state filter returns only the rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recording when the row was inserted. Timestamps are ISO 8601 in UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Clients never supply `id` and never supply `created_at`.

Every entity in this system carries the full common-field set: alongside `id` and `created_at`, each row also exposes an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that reach the row by cascade from an operation that named a different resource. A read never advances `version`.

The server maintains a **logical clock** and must never fall back to real wall-clock time for any business decision. Before any client advances it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `current_utc`, and every time-dependent rule in this document — rolling remote-reset windows, weather-window expiry, measurement-period closure, work-order ageing — reads that clock rather than the host's time. Advancing the clock has observable consequences in its own right: every weather window still in the forecast or confirmed stage whose end bound is at or before the new clock value moves to `EXPIRED`; the day count that feeds a work order's ageing term is recomputed the next time that order is scored; and a turbine whose rolling reset window opened more than the window length before the new clock value has its 24-hour reset counter zeroed on its next remote-reset attempt.

### 2.5. Interval Conventions

Every interval in this system is **half-open**: the start bound is inclusive and the end bound is exclusive. This applies to weather windows (`start_utc` to `end_utc`), planned work-order execution intervals (`planned_start_utc` to `planned_end_utc`), crane mobilization intervals (`mobilization_start_utc` to `demobilization_end_utc`), curtailment intervals (`start_utc` to `end_utc`), service-agreement measurement periods (`period_start_utc` to `period_end_utc`) and landowner settlement periods (`period_start_utc` to `period_end_utc`). On every one of these pairs the end bound must be strictly after the start bound; a request violating that is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two intervals **overlap** when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the first ends strictly after the second starts. Under this definition exact adjacency — one interval starting at the very instant another ends — does **not** overlap. Adjacency is therefore permitted for weather windows on the same farm and for crew occupancy across work orders. There is exactly one deliberate exception, described in the crane subsection: because a crane mobilization is padded on both sides before comparison, two mobilizations that are merely adjacent do collide once the pads are applied.

Where an interval is clipped against another, the overlap duration in hours is the amount of time common to both half-open intervals, and it is zero when they do not intersect at all. The clipped duration is computed by taking the later of the two start bounds and the earlier of the two end bounds, subtracting the former from the latter, treating any negative result as zero, and expressing the remainder in hours.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Money

All money in this system is held as an integer number of cents. Wherever a monetary amount is derived from a multiplication or a percentage, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value exactly at the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero. Field names ending in `_cents` are always integers and are never returned as strings.

Energy figures, hour figures and score figures are decimal numbers. Lost-energy and curtailed-energy amounts are rounded half up to two decimal places. Priority scores and blade severity scores are rounded half up to two decimal places. Availability percentages are rounded half up to **four** decimal places, and every downstream comparison against a guaranteed availability percentage is made against the already-rounded four-decimal figure rather than against the raw quotient. Operating-hour and crew-hour figures carry one decimal place. Where a calculation specifies more than one rounding step, the order of those steps is part of the specification and is stated explicitly in the subsection that defines the calculation — two of the scoring calculations round in deliberately opposite orders and produce different results as a consequence.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Farm and Turbine Writes

Two creation operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both of them make a decision that depends on the current state of a parent row the client read earlier.

Drafting a landowner settlement — the request handled by `POST /wind-ops/farms/{id}/landowner-settlements` — requires a non-optional `expected_wind_farm_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `WindFarm`. If that value does not equal the farm's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FARM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The guard exists because a new settlement legitimately supersedes an earlier draft settlement for the same parcel and period, and because it consumes headroom under the farm's `landowner_budget_cap_cents`; a writer working from a stale view of the farm's ledger would silently overcommit that cap, so the version mismatch is surfaced instead. A successful draft creation increments the farm's `version` by 1, which means a client drafting two settlements in sequence must re-read the farm between them.

Issuing a curtailment instruction — the request handled by `POST /wind-ops/turbines/{id}/curtailment-orders` — requires a non-optional `expected_turbine_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Turbine`. If that value does not equal the turbine's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TURBINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a newer grid or environmental instruction legitimately supersedes an earlier pending order for the same reason, and because the curtailment stack limit is evaluated against the turbine's live count of active orders; a writer holding a stale turbine view would stack an order on top of state it never observed. A successful creation increments the turbine's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and the presence of a version field elsewhere carries no meaning.

### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error belonging to the earliest rung of the ladder below. This ordering is normative: it is not enough to reject the request, the correct error identifier must be chosen. Reading the ladder from the top:

1. **Authentication.** Header presence, non-emptiness and role membership, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role must appear in the target endpoint's authorised roles, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Segregation of duties**, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to exactly two operations: work-order completion (against `submitted_by_user_id`) and blade-finding prioritization (against `recorded_by_user_id`).
4. **Crew ownership**, yielding `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Applies to exactly two operations: starting a work order and completing a work order, and only when the acting role is `CrewLead`.
5. **Request shape.** The body must parse as JSON; every required input field must be present and non-null; every enum-typed field must carry a declared member; every numeric field must fall inside its declared range; every timestamp must be a valid ISO-8601 instant with a `Z` offset; and on every interval field pair the end bound must be strictly after the start bound. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Path resolution**, left to right. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier; see §2.9 for which identifier maps to which error.
7. **Body reference resolution**, yielding `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that accepts a foreign-key identifier in its body.
8. **Farm stale-version check**, yielding `ERR_STALE_FARM_VERSION`, on landowner settlement drafting only.
9. **Turbine stale-version check**, yielding `ERR_STALE_TURBINE_VERSION`, on curtailment order issuance only.
10. **Declared-transition checks.** The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Work-order transitions — submit, request-revision, schedule, start, complete and cancel — yield `ERR_WORK_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Fault-event transitions — triage, remote-reset and close — yield `ERR_FAULT_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. Every other action endpoint, covering crane mobilizations, component replacements, weather windows, curtailment orders, service agreements, blade findings and landowner settlements, yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Immediately after this rung, executing a component replacement additionally requires that its parent major-component work order is `IN_PROGRESS`, and that particular failure yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
11. **Turbine decommissioning**, yielding `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED`. Applies to five operations: raising a fault event on a turbine, recording a blade finding on a turbine, issuing a curtailment order on a turbine, posting SCADA telemetry for a turbine, and raising a work order against a turbine.
12. **Settlement freeze**, yielding `ERR_AGREEMENT_IN_SETTLEMENT`. Applies to two operations: drafting a landowner settlement on the farm, and posting SCADA telemetry for one of the farm's turbines.
13. **Weather-window confirmation**, yielding `ERR_WEATHER_WINDOW_NOT_CONFIRMED`, on work-order scheduling only.
14. **Safety gates.** In order: the crane workable-day gate (`ERR_LIFT_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on crane reservation), the crew wind limit (`ERR_CREW_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on scheduling), the crew certification requirement (`ERR_CREW_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`, on scheduling), and the force-stop interlock (`ERR_TURBINE_FORCE_STOPPED`, which applies to two operations: attempting a remote reset and starting a work order).
15. **Temporal conflicts.** In order: landowner settlement period overlap (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), weather-window overlap (`ERR_WEATHER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), crew occupancy conflict (`ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), crane buffered-slot conflict (`ERR_CRANE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`), and planned-interval containment inside the named weather window (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`).
16. **Curtailment stack limit**, yielding `ERR_CURTAILMENT_STACK_LIMIT`, on curtailment activation only.
17. **Referential consistency.** In order: crew-to-farm match (`ERR_CREW_FARM_MISMATCH`, on scheduling), crane-to-farm match (`ERR_CRANE_FARM_MISMATCH`, on crane reservation), spare-part component-type match (`ERR_SPARE_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH`, on replacement planning), and up-tower eligibility (`ERR_UPTOWER_NOT_PERMITTED`, on taking the up-tower branch).
18. **Quota, capacity and fiscal gates.** In order: reset eligibility (`ERR_RESET_NOT_PERMITTED`, which applies both to a triage disposition of `REMOTE_RESET` and to a remote-reset attempt), reset quota (`ERR_RESET_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`, on a remote-reset attempt), crew daily capacity (`ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on scheduling), spare-part availability (`ERR_SPARE_PART_UNAVAILABLE`, on replacement planning), landowner budget headroom (`ERR_MINIMUM_PAYMENT_ABOVE_CAP`, on settlement drafting), service-due eligibility (`ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DUE`, on raising a scheduled-service work order), the revision bound (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on requesting a revision), and finally the non-empty availability denominator (`ERR_NO_TURBINES_IN_SERVICE`, on settling a service agreement).

Because the crane workable-day gate sits on rung 14 and the crane-to-farm match sits on rung 17, a crane reservation that both names a crane on the wrong farm and fails the workable-day gate is reported as `ERR_LIFT_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, not as `ERR_CRANE_FARM_MISMATCH`.

### 2.9. Path and Reference Resolution

Path identifiers resolve strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Four collections have dedicated not-found identifiers: a farm identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_FARM_NOT_FOUND`, a turbine identifier yields `ERR_TURBINE_NOT_FOUND`, a work-order identifier yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, and a fault-event identifier yields `ERR_FAULT_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. Every other single-resource path segment — turbine models, fault code rules, crews, weather windows, crane mobilizations, spare parts, component replacements, blade findings, service agreements, curtailment orders and landowner settlements — yields the generic `ERR_PATH_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All five carry HTTP 404. Left-to-right resolution matters on nested paths: a request naming a non-existent farm and, under it, a non-existent turbine reports the farm error, because the farm segment is resolved first.

Foreign-key identifiers supplied inside a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and a body identifier that names no existing row yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). This covers `turbine_id`, `turbine_model_id`, `fault_code_rule_id`, `crew_id`, `weather_window_id`, `spare_part_id`, `crane_mobilization_id`, `blade_damage_id`, `fault_event_id` and `parent_work_order_id`.

### 2.10. Two Conserved Ledgers

The system maintains two invariants that must hold after **every** request, successful or rejected, and that the acceptance tests will read directly.

The first is the **site opex ledger**. Each `WindFarm` carries four accumulating buckets — `landowner_payable_cents`, `road_maintenance_reserve_cents`, `community_fund_balance_cents` and `maintenance_spend_cents` — plus a conserved total, `opex_accrued_cents`, which must always equal the sum of those four. Nothing may be posted to `opex_accrued_cents` that is not simultaneously posted to one of the four buckets, and nothing may be posted to a bucket without the same amount reaching the total. The buckets are fed by exactly three kinds of event: an approved landowner settlement credits the first three, a completed work order's labour cost credits `maintenance_spend_cents`, and a crane or up-tower charge arising from an executed component replacement — or a crane cancellation fee — likewise credits `maintenance_spend_cents`. Voiding an approved settlement is a compensating action, decrementing each bucket by exactly the amount the approval credited, so that the invariant holds exactly and no rounding drift accumulates.

The second is the **spare-part quantity ledger**. Each `SparePart` line carries `quantity_on_hand`, `quantity_reserved` and `quantity_consumed`, plus a conserved total, `total_units_accounted`, which must always equal their sum. `quantity_on_hand` is supplied as the opening balance when the line is registered, and thereafter changes only through goods receipts, reservations and reservation cancellations. Only a goods receipt raises `total_units_accounted`; every reservation, execution and cancellation merely moves units between the three buckets and leaves the total untouched.

### 2.11. A Turbine Has Two Independent State Dimensions

A `Turbine` carries two enumerated state fields on the same row, and they evolve independently. `availability_state` is the **availability dimension** and takes one of `ONLINE`, `FAULTED`, `DOWN_DISPATCH`, `DOWN_SERVICE`, `DOWN_MAJOR` or `DECOMMISSIONED`; it begins at `ONLINE` when the turbine is commissioned, and `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal. `curtailment_state` is the **curtailment dimension** and takes one of `UNCONSTRAINED`, `CURTAILED` or `FORCED_STOP`; it begins at `UNCONSTRAINED`.

A transition on one dimension never writes the other. Raising a fault moves the availability dimension and leaves the curtailment dimension exactly as it was; activating a curtailment order moves the curtailment dimension and leaves the availability dimension exactly as it was. The two therefore combine freely: a turbine may legitimately be `FORCED_STOP` on the curtailment dimension while simultaneously `FAULTED`, `DOWN_SERVICE` or `ONLINE` on the availability dimension. The one place where the dimensions interact is a pair of interlocks, not a state write: while `curtailment_state` is `FORCED_STOP`, a remote reset and a work-order start are both refused with `ERR_TURBINE_FORCE_STOPPED`.

Decommissioning is the single exception where one operation touches both dimensions, and it does so because the turbine is leaving service entirely: it sets `availability_state` to `DECOMMISSIONED` and returns `curtailment_state` to `UNCONSTRAINED`.

### 2.12. Cascade Discipline and Recomputation

Several operations in this domain reach far beyond the resource named in the request. Where a subsection below enumerates the hops of a cascade, every hop is part of the specification: the hops run inside a single request, in the order given, and either all of them land or none of them do. A rejected request writes nothing at all — no counter moves, no `version` advances, no partial cascade survives.

Cascades cross aggregate boundaries freely. Closing a fault event changes a turbine counter, which changes a service agreement's projection, which changes the priority score of work orders that the request never named. This is intentional and observable: a client that reads a work order it did not touch after closing an unrelated fault event on the same farm must see the new score. Every row a cascade mutates has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, including rows reached only indirectly.

Derived fields are **recomputed, not patched**. A turbine's service-due flags, a spare part's stock status, a crane's day counts and cost breakdown, a work order's priority score and a service agreement's projection are all recomputed in full from their current inputs whenever a trigger fires, rather than adjusted by a delta.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Site: Farms, Models, Turbines and Crews

Before anything operational can happen, a `SiteAdmin` registers the site itself. A wind farm is created with a `name` between 1 and 120 characters that must not be blank, a `region_code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters used to look up noise and wildlife rules for the jurisdiction, and a `grid_operator_code` naming the grid operator that will issue curtailment instructions. Two levy percentages are recorded at the same time: `road_levy_pct`, the percentage of each approved landowner payment that accrues to the road-maintenance reserve, and `community_levy_pct`, the percentage that accrues to the community fund. Both are optional on the creation request; when `road_levy_pct` is omitted it defaults to 2.5, and when `community_levy_pct` is omitted it defaults to 1.5. Either value, whether supplied or defaulted, must be at least 0 and at most 20. The final input is `landowner_budget_cap_cents`, which must be at least 0 and acts as a hard ceiling on the sum of `minimum_payment_cents` across the farm's draft and approved settlements.

A newly registered farm starts every derived and accumulating figure at zero: `turbine_count` and `installed_capacity_kw` at 0, and each of `landowner_payable_cents`, `road_maintenance_reserve_cents`, `community_fund_balance_cents`, `maintenance_spend_cents` and `opex_accrued_cents` at 0. `turbine_count` is the count of the farm's turbines whose `availability_state` is anything other than `DECOMMISSIONED`, and `installed_capacity_kw` is the sum of `rated_power_kw` over that same set of turbines — both recomputed whenever a turbine joins or leaves service.

A turbine model is registered independently of any farm and pins the engineering parameters that later drive both scheduling and procurement. Its `model_code` must be unique across all models and between 1 and 40 characters; `rated_power_kw` is the nameplate rating in kilowatts and must be greater than 0; `hub_height_m` and `rotor_diameter_m` are the hub height and rotor diameter in metres and must each be greater than 0. Maintenance intervals live here too: `service_interval_hours` is the scheduled-maintenance interval expressed in turbine operating hours and must be greater than 0, while `service_interval_tolerance_hours` is the number of operating hours past the due point that are still tolerated before the interval counts as breached and must be at least 0. Finally the model records three procurement lead times in days, each at least 0: `gearbox_lead_time_days`, `blade_lead_time_days` and `generator_lead_time_days`, used by the spare-parts strategy to reason about long-lead components on this model.

Commissioning a turbine places it on a farm — the farm is taken from the creation path, and the turbine's `wind_farm_id` is set from that segment rather than from the body. The body names a `turbine_model_id`, which pins the service interval and lead times for the life of the turbine; an `asset_tag`, which must be unique within the farm; a `commissioning_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; and `expected_kwh_per_hour`, the contractual expected production rate that must be greater than 0 and that every lost-energy and deemed-energy calculation reads. Two counters may optionally be seeded at commissioning: `operating_hours`, the cumulative operating hours which otherwise starts at 0 and thereafter is advanced only by SCADA telemetry, and `last_service_operating_hours`, the operating-hours reading recorded at the most recent completed scheduled service, which otherwise also starts at 0. Commissioning sets `availability_state` to `ONLINE` and `curtailment_state` to `UNCONSTRAINED`, sets `active_curtailment_count` to 0 and `effective_power_limit_kw` to the model's `rated_power_kw`, computes the three service-due figures described in the next subsection, increments the farm's `turbine_count` by 1 and adds the model's `rated_power_kw` to the farm's `installed_capacity_kw`. The turbine's remaining counters begin at their zero values: `active_fault_code` is null, `remote_reset_count_24h` is 0, `reset_window_start_utc` is null until the first remote reset, and `period_actual_energy_kwh`, `period_potential_energy_kwh`, `counted_downtime_hours_period` and `cumulative_lost_energy_kwh` are all 0.

Technician crews are rostered per site and never travel. A crew is registered with the `wind_farm_id` of the site it belongs to, a `crew_code` unique within that farm, a `technician_count` of at least 1, and a non-empty `certifications` array drawn from `GWO_BASIC`, `ROPE_ACCESS`, `HV_SWITCHING`, `CRANE_BANKSMAN` and `BLADE_COMPOSITE`. Its safety and capacity envelope is set by `max_work_wind_speed_ms`, the highest forecast wind speed at which the crew may work at height, which must be greater than 0, and `daily_hours_capacity`, the hours the crew may commit on a single UTC calendar date, also greater than 0. `hourly_rate_cents` is the fully burdened crew rate per hour in cents and must be at least 0. The crew's `status` may optionally be supplied at registration and defaults to `AVAILABLE`; only `AVAILABLE` and `OFF_ROTATION` are accepted as input values. The third member of the enumeration, `ON_SHIFT`, is reachable only by cascade — the system sets it while one of the crew's work orders is in progress and returns the crew to `AVAILABLE` when that order finishes.

Retiring a turbine is a `SiteAdmin` action that carries a `decommission_reason` and drives the availability dimension `ONLINE → DECOMMISSIONED`. It is reachable only from `ONLINE`, and it is refused while any work order on the turbine sits in `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Decommissioning cancels every curtailment order on the turbine still in `PENDING` — such an order can never be activated afterwards — releases every curtailment order still `ACTIVE`, sets `active_curtailment_count` to 0 and returns `curtailment_state` to `UNCONSTRAINED`. It then decrements the farm's `turbine_count` by 1 and subtracts the model's `rated_power_kw` from the farm's `installed_capacity_kw`, and from that moment the turbine is excluded from every availability denominator and from every landowner metered-energy sum.

### 3.2. The Control Room: Telemetry, Fault Triage and Remote Reset

Supervisory control feeds the system through a telemetry increment posted by a `ControlRoomOperator` against one turbine. The payload is purely additive: `operating_hours_delta` is added to the turbine's `operating_hours`, `actual_energy_kwh` is added to `period_actual_energy_kwh`, and `potential_energy_kwh` is added to `period_potential_energy_kwh`. `period_actual_energy_kwh` is the metered production the turbine has recorded since it was commissioned; `period_potential_energy_kwh` is the loss-free potential production over the same span, and the two together are the entire input to the energy-based availability figure. Telemetry is the only path by which `operating_hours` advances. Posting telemetry is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED` for a retired turbine, and refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_IN_SETTLEMENT` while any service agreement on the turbine's farm sits in `IN_SETTLEMENT`, because settlement freezes the farm's period figures.

Each telemetry post re-derives the turbine's three service figures from its model. `service_due_operating_hours` is the turbine's `last_service_operating_hours` plus the model's `service_interval_hours`. `service_due_flag` is true when the turbine's `operating_hours` has reached or passed `service_due_operating_hours`, using an at-or-above comparison. `service_interval_breached` is true only when `operating_hours` has passed `service_due_operating_hours` plus the model's `service_interval_tolerance_hours` **strictly** — landing exactly on the tolerated boundary is not yet a breach. These figures depend on a single turbine and its model and on nothing else. The same arithmetic supplies the eligibility gate for raising a scheduled-service work order, described in §3.3, which opens 200 operating hours before the due point. Telemetry also refreshes the live availability projection on every `ACTIVE` service agreement of the farm, as described in §3.9.

Fault handling starts from a catalogue. A `SiteAdmin` registers one rule per SCADA fault code: `fault_code` is unique across the catalogue and between 1 and 24 characters, `severity_class` is the triage class and is one of `ADVISORY`, `RESETTABLE` or `LOCKOUT`, `max_remote_resets_per_24h` is the number of remote resets permitted per turbine per rolling 24-hour window for this code and must be at least 0, `requires_dispatch` marks a code that must always go to a technician, and `contractual_exclusion` marks a code whose downtime does **not** count against the availability guarantee. A rule may later be amended, and the amendable subset is exactly `max_remote_resets_per_24h`, `requires_dispatch` and `contractual_exclusion` — all three optional on the amendment request. Amending a rule increments that rule's own `version` by 1 and changes nothing on any fault event already raised.

When supervisory control reports a trip, a `ControlRoomOperator` raises a fault event against the turbine, supplying the `fault_code` and the `raised_at` instant at which supervisory control saw it. The submitted code must match a registered rule; the resolved rule is recorded as the event's `fault_code_rule_id`, and its parameters are **pinned** onto the event at that moment — `severity_class_pinned`, `max_remote_resets_pinned`, `requires_dispatch_pinned` and `contractual_exclusion_pinned` copy the rule's severity class, reset quota, dispatch requirement and exclusion flag respectively, and `fault_code_rule_version_pinned` records the rule's `version` as observed at raise time. A later amendment of the rule never rewrites any pinned value; the event is judged for the rest of its life by the snapshot it was born with. Raising a fault sets the event's `triage_state` to `RAISED` and its `reset_attempt_count` to 0, sets the turbine's `active_fault_code` to the submitted code, and drives the availability dimension `ONLINE → FAULTED` while leaving the curtailment dimension untouched. It is refused unless the turbine's `availability_state` is `ONLINE`, and refused with `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED` for a retired turbine. The event's `cleared_at`, `downtime_hours` and `lost_energy_kwh` all start null.

Triage is the control room's disposition decision, submitted as a `disposition` of either `REMOTE_RESET` or `DISPATCH`. A `disposition` of `REMOTE_RESET` moves the event `RAISED → RESET_PENDING`; it is permitted only when the event's `severity_class_pinned` is `RESETTABLE` and its `requires_dispatch_pinned` is false, and any other combination — `ADVISORY`, `LOCKOUT`, or a code the rule marks dispatch-only — is refused with `ERR_RESET_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `disposition` of `DISPATCH` moves the event `RAISED → DISPATCH_REQUIRED`, and it may equally be applied to an event already sitting in `RESET_PENDING`, moving it `RESET_PENDING → DISPATCH_REQUIRED`; that escalation consumes no reset attempt and simply takes the event off the reset track. Neither disposition writes either turbine state dimension — the turbine stays `FAULTED` throughout triage.

A remote reset attempt reports the SCADA outcome as `reset_outcome`, either `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED`, and it may only be made from `RESET_PENDING`. Before the quota is examined, the rolling window is brought up to date: if the turbine's `reset_window_start_utc` is null, or if the logical clock has reached or passed that instant plus 24 hours, then `reset_window_start_utc` is set to the current logical clock and `remote_reset_count_24h` is reset to 0. Only then is eligibility tested, and it requires three things simultaneously: the event's `severity_class_pinned` is `RESETTABLE`, its `reset_attempt_count` is **strictly** below its `max_remote_resets_pinned`, and the turbine's `curtailment_state` is not `FORCED_STOP`. A count exactly equal to the pinned maximum is not eligible and is refused with `ERR_RESET_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`; an ineligible severity class or a dispatch-only code is refused with `ERR_RESET_NOT_PERMITTED`; and a force-stopped turbine is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_FORCE_STOPPED`, because a turbine held down for icing or wildlife protection must not be spun back up from a desk.

An eligible attempt always increments both counters — the event's `reset_attempt_count` by 1 and the turbine's `remote_reset_count_24h` by 1 — and then branches on the outcome with equal weight on each branch. A `reset_outcome` of `SUCCEEDED` moves the event `RESET_PENDING → CLEARED`, stamps `cleared_at` with the current logical clock, clears the turbine's `active_fault_code` to null, and drives the availability dimension `FAULTED → ONLINE`. A `reset_outcome` of `FAILED` leaves the event in `RESET_PENDING` and the turbine in `FAULTED`, deliberately so: any remaining quota can still be spent on a further attempt from the same state, and escalation to `DISPATCH_REQUIRED` is a separate triage call rather than an automatic consequence of failure. Crucially, a successful reset accrues nothing yet — `downtime_hours`, `lost_energy_kwh` and every downstream availability effect are deferred to the closure step, so a cleared event that is never closed contributes nothing to availability or to lost energy.

Closure is a `ReliabilityEngineer` decision that admits the event's downtime into the availability accounts, moving it `CLEARED → CLOSED`. At that moment `downtime_hours` is computed as the elapsed time from `raised_at` to `cleared_at` expressed in hours, and `lost_energy_kwh` is that figure multiplied by the turbine's `expected_kwh_per_hour`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Three further hops follow inside the same request. First, the turbine's `cumulative_lost_energy_kwh` rises by the event's `lost_energy_kwh` unconditionally, while its `counted_downtime_hours_period` rises by `downtime_hours` only when the event's `contractual_exclusion_pinned` is false — an excluded code adds nothing to counted downtime, though it still adds its lost energy. Second, because the turbine's counted downtime changed, every service agreement on that turbine's farm still in `ACTIVE` recomputes its `projected_shortfall_pct` and `at_risk_flag`. Third, because the at-risk picture may have flipped, every work order on any of that farm's turbines sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` recomputes its `priority_score` in full at the current logical clock — so a score change is observable on orders this request never named.

One further path reaches `CLEARED`, and it is a cascade rather than a control-room action: when a fault-dispatch work order is completed, the fault event it references moves `DISPATCHED → CLEARED` with `cleared_at` stamped from the logical clock, and then continues `CLEARED → CLOSED` in the same request, running the identical three hops above. The reverse edge also exists: cancelling the fault-dispatch work order that dispatched an event returns that event `DISPATCHED → DISPATCH_REQUIRED`, leaving `cleared_at` null and `downtime_hours` and `lost_energy_kwh` unaccrued, so the event becomes eligible to back a fresh dispatch order.

### 3.3. Forecast Windows and the Work Order Loop

Nothing at height happens outside a forecast working window. A `MaintenancePlanner` records a window against a farm with `wind_farm_id`, an inclusive `start_utc`, an exclusive `end_utc` that must be strictly after `start_utc`, a `forecast_wind_speed_ms` of at least 0 giving the forecast mean wind speed over the window, a `forecast_gust_ms` that must be at least as large as `forecast_wind_speed_ms`, and an `icing_risk` flag that is true when the forecast carries an icing risk. A new window is created in `state` `FORECAST` with `active_work_order_count` at 0. Its half-open interval must not overlap any window on the same farm still in `FORECAST` or `CONFIRMED`; an overlap is refused with `ERR_WEATHER_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, while exact adjacency — a new window starting at the instant an existing one ends — is permitted.

Confirming a window drives it `FORECAST → CONFIRMED` and is permitted only while its `end_utc` is still strictly after the current logical clock; a window that has already lapsed cannot be confirmed. Confirmation is what makes a window usable in two distinct ways: it becomes eligible to back a work-order schedule, and it becomes eligible to count as a crane standby day whenever its `forecast_wind_speed_ms` exceeds a mobilization's `max_lift_wind_speed_ms`. A confirmed window is **shared**, not exclusive: several crews may be scheduled into the same confirmed window at once, and crew contention is policed by the crew rules of §3.4 rather than by window exclusivity. A window's onward lifecycle is entirely cascade-driven: the first work order to start in it moves it `CONFIRMED → CONSUMED`, at which point it stops backing new schedules and stops counting toward crane standby days, though `active_work_order_count` is not itself changed by starting. When the last order occupying a consumed window finishes or is cancelled and `active_work_order_count` reaches 0, the window moves `CONSUMED → EXPIRED`. Independently of any work order, advancing the logical clock moves every window still in `FORECAST` or `CONFIRMED` whose `end_utc` is at or before the new clock value straight to `EXPIRED`.

A work order is raised against a turbine by a `MaintenancePlanner` or a `ReliabilityEngineer`. The body names the `turbine_id`, a `work_type` of `SCHEDULED_SERVICE`, `FAULT_DISPATCH`, `BLADE_REPAIR` or `MAJOR_COMPONENT`, the `required_certification` the assigned crew must hold — one of `GWO_BASIC`, `ROPE_ACCESS`, `HV_SWITCHING`, `CRANE_BANKSMAN` or `BLADE_COMPOSITE` — and `estimated_hours`, the planned crew hours, which must be greater than 0 and which drives both crew capacity and projected lost energy. Three optional links may accompany it. `fault_event_id` is **required** when `work_type` is `FAULT_DISPATCH` and must be omitted for every other work type; the named event must be sitting in `DISPATCH_REQUIRED`, and raising the order moves it `DISPATCH_REQUIRED → DISPATCHED` while changing no other field on the event. `blade_damage_id` is **required** when `work_type` is `BLADE_REPAIR` and must be omitted for every other work type; the named finding must be sitting in `PRIORITIZED`. `parent_work_order_id` is always optional and links this order to an earlier one as follow-up work — a major-component order raised out of an earlier inspection typically carries it. A new order starts in `state` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `priority_score` at 0 and `labor_cost_cents` at 0, and with `crew_id`, `weather_window_id`, `planned_start_utc`, `planned_end_utc`, `actual_hours` and `submitted_by_user_id` all null. Raising an order against a retired turbine is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED`.

A scheduled-service order carries one extra gate. It may be raised only once the turbine's `operating_hours` has reached the point 200 operating hours before its `service_due_operating_hours` — that is, at or above `service_due_operating_hours` minus 200. The comparison is inclusive: landing exactly on that earliest-creation point is allowed. Below it the request is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_DUE`, which stops planners from burning a service window on a turbine that is nowhere near due.

Submission moves an order `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and it is also the way an order re-enters planning from `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Either way the turbine must not be `DECOMMISSIONED`, `submitted_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` — overwriting whatever it held on a previous submission — and the order's `priority_score` is computed. Re-submission is **never** gated by `revision_round`: only the revision back-edge is bounded, so an order that has exhausted its revisions is still re-submittable and still schedulable.

The priority score is a weighted repair-prioritization figure on a 0-to-100 scale, built from four terms. The **severity term** starts from a severity weight determined by the work type: a `MAJOR_COMPONENT` order weighs 1.00; a `FAULT_DISPATCH` order takes its weight from the linked fault event's `severity_class_pinned`, being 0.90 for `LOCKOUT`, 0.60 for `RESETTABLE` and 0.20 for `ADVISORY`; a `BLADE_REPAIR` order takes the linked finding's `damage_category` divided by 5; and a `SCHEDULED_SERVICE` order weighs 0.30. The **energy term** starts from the projected lost energy, which is the order's `estimated_hours` multiplied by the turbine's `expected_kwh_per_hour`; that figure is divided by a normalizer of 5,000 kWh and the quotient is capped at 1.0. The **liquidated-damages term** is 1.0 when at least one service agreement exists on the turbine's farm that is in `ACTIVE` with its `at_risk_flag` true, and 0.0 otherwise — agreements in `IN_SETTLEMENT` or `SETTLED` never contribute this term. The **age term** starts from the number of whole days elapsed from the order's `created_at` to the current logical clock, rounding that elapsed day count down to a whole number; that count is divided by a normalizer of 30 days and the quotient is capped at 1.0. The four terms are then weighted — 40 for severity, 30 for energy, 20 for liquidated-damages risk and 10 for age — added together at full precision, and the resulting **sum is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once**. No intermediate term is rounded; the single rounding is applied to the total.

Sending an order back for rework is a `ReliabilityEngineer` action carrying a `revision_note`, and it is reachable from two states: `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED`. In both cases the order's `revision_round` must be **strictly** below 3 before the request is accepted, and a fourth attempt on an order already at 3 is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. An accepted request increments `revision_round` by 1. From `SUBMITTED` that is the whole effect. From `SCHEDULED` the reservation the order held must additionally be unwound: `crew_id`, `weather_window_id`, `planned_start_utc` and `planned_end_utc` are all set to null; the named window's `active_work_order_count` is decremented by 1 while the window's own `state` is left exactly as it was; the crew returns to `AVAILABLE` provided it holds no other order in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; and when the order's `work_type` is `BLADE_REPAIR`, the linked finding returns `REPAIR_SCHEDULED → PRIORITIZED` so it can back a fresh repair order.

### 3.4. Committing a Crew to a Window

Scheduling is the tightest gate in the domain, and it belongs to the `MaintenancePlanner`. The request names a `crew_id`, a `weather_window_id`, and the proposed `planned_start_utc` and `planned_end_utc` bounding the half-open execution interval. It is reachable only from `SUBMITTED`, moving the order `SUBMITTED → SCHEDULED`.

Seven conditions are checked, and the ladder of §2.8 fixes which one is reported when several fail together. The named window must be in `CONFIRMED`; a window in `FORECAST`, `CONSUMED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_WEATHER_WINDOW_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The crew's `max_work_wind_speed_ms` must be at least as large as the window's `forecast_wind_speed_ms` — equality is acceptable — and a crew whose limit falls below the forecast is refused with `ERR_CREW_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The crew's `certifications` array must contain the order's `required_certification`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CREW_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. The crew must not be in `OFF_ROTATION`. The proposed interval must be **contained** inside the window's interval, meaning `planned_start_utc` is at or after the window's `start_utc` and `planned_end_utc` is at or before its `end_utc`; anything protruding is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. The crew and the window must both belong to the same farm as the order's turbine, and a crew rostered elsewhere is refused with `ERR_CREW_FARM_MISMATCH`. Finally the crew's occupancy and daily capacity are both tested.

Crew occupancy and crew capacity read the same population of orders and nothing else: the candidate crew's work orders in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Orders in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` never consume occupancy and never consume capacity. **Occupancy** asks whether any such order's planned interval overlaps the proposed one under the half-open rule of §2.5 — that is, whether an existing order starts strictly before the proposed end and ends strictly after the proposed start. Adjacency is explicitly allowed here: a crew may finish one order at exactly the instant it begins the next. An overlap is refused with `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **Capacity** is computed per UTC calendar date. The target date is the UTC calendar date of the proposed `planned_start_utc`; the crew's already-committed hours for that date are the sum of `estimated_hours` over its `SCHEDULED` and `IN_PROGRESS` orders whose own `planned_start_utc` falls on that same UTC date; and the request is refused with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that committed total plus this order's `estimated_hours` **strictly exceeds** the crew's `daily_hours_capacity`. Landing exactly on capacity is permitted.

A successful schedule stores `crew_id`, `weather_window_id`, `planned_start_utc` and `planned_end_utc` on the order, sets the crew's `status` to `ON_SHIFT`, and increments the window's `active_work_order_count` by 1 while **leaving the window in `CONFIRMED`** — which is precisely what allows a second crew to be scheduled into the same window. When the order's `work_type` is `BLADE_REPAIR`, the linked finding moves `PRIORITIZED → REPAIR_SCHEDULED`, and from then on no further repair order may reference that finding.

### 3.5. On Site: Starting, Completing and Abandoning Work

Starting an order moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and may be done by the assigned `CrewLead` or by a `ReliabilityEngineer`. When the acting role is `CrewLead`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `crew_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The turbine must be in the availability state the work type expects: `ONLINE` for a `SCHEDULED_SERVICE`, `BLADE_REPAIR` or `MAJOR_COMPONENT` order, and `FAULTED` for a `FAULT_DISPATCH` order. A turbine whose `curtailment_state` is `FORCED_STOP` cannot have work started on it at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_FORCE_STOPPED`.

The start writes the availability dimension according to work type, and each branch is a distinct declared transition: a `FAULT_DISPATCH` order drives `FAULTED → DOWN_DISPATCH`, a `SCHEDULED_SERVICE` or `BLADE_REPAIR` order drives `ONLINE → DOWN_SERVICE`, and a `MAJOR_COMPONENT` order drives `ONLINE → DOWN_MAJOR`. In every branch the curtailment dimension is left exactly as it was. The assigned crew's `status` is set to `ON_SHIFT`. If this is the first order to start inside the named window, that window moves `CONFIRMED → CONSUMED`, which closes it to new schedules; the window's `active_work_order_count` is **not** changed by starting.

Completion is the deepest cascade in the system, and it is the one operation that touches turbine state, fault accounting, availability projection, work-order scoring, crew rostering, window lifecycle, spare-part consumption closure and the site ledger in a single request. It carries `actual_hours`, the hours actually worked, which must be greater than 0 when supplied, and it may be performed by a `ReliabilityEngineer` or by the assigned `CrewLead` — subject in both cases to the segregation-of-duties rule that the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `submitted_by_user_id`. The order must be in `IN_PROGRESS`. The hops run in this order:

1. The order moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and its `labor_cost_cents` is set to `actual_hours` multiplied by the assigned crew's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent.
2. The turbine's availability dimension returns to `ONLINE` from whichever down state it held — `DOWN_DISPATCH → ONLINE`, `DOWN_SERVICE → ONLINE` or `DOWN_MAJOR → ONLINE` — with the curtailment dimension untouched. A `SCHEDULED_SERVICE` completion additionally sets the turbine's `last_service_operating_hours` to its current `operating_hours`, which in turn re-derives `service_due_operating_hours`, `service_due_flag` and `service_interval_breached`.
3. On a `FAULT_DISPATCH` order the linked fault event is carried from `DISPATCHED` through `CLEARED` to `CLOSED` inside this same request, with `cleared_at` stamped from the logical clock and `downtime_hours` and `lost_energy_kwh` computed exactly as §3.2 describes.
4. That closure admits the downtime: the turbine's `counted_downtime_hours_period` rises by `downtime_hours` when the event's `contractual_exclusion_pinned` is false and by nothing when it is true, while `cumulative_lost_energy_kwh` rises by `lost_energy_kwh` regardless.
5. Every service agreement on the farm still in `ACTIVE` recomputes `projected_shortfall_pct` and `at_risk_flag` under the live projection described in §3.9.
6. Every **other** work order on the farm's turbines sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` recomputes its `priority_score` in full, so a flip of `at_risk_flag` is observable on orders this request never named.

Alongside those hops, completion posts the labour cost to the site ledger, adding `labor_cost_cents` both to the farm's `maintenance_spend_cents` and to its `opex_accrued_cents`. It decrements the named window's `active_work_order_count` by 1, and when that count reaches 0 the window moves `CONSUMED → EXPIRED`. It returns the crew's `status` to `AVAILABLE`, provided the crew holds no other order in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. On a `MAJOR_COMPONENT` order it moves every linked component replacement `EXECUTED → CLOSED`, with no stock movement, because those units were already consumed at execution. On a `BLADE_REPAIR` order it moves the linked finding `REPAIR_SCHEDULED → REPAIRED`, which is terminal for that finding.

Abandoning an order is a `MaintenancePlanner` or `SiteAdmin` action carrying a `cancellation_reason`, and it is reachable from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `REVISION_REQUESTED` as well as from `SCHEDULED`. From the three pre-schedule states the effect is to move the order to `CANCELLED` and to cancel every component replacement it owns that still sits in `PLANNED`, `CRANE_RESERVED` or `UPTOWER_READY`, each of which releases its spare-part reservation under the conserved-quantity rule of §2.10. From `SCHEDULED` the same cancellation additionally sets `crew_id` to null, decrements the window's `active_work_order_count` by 1 while leaving the window's `state` unchanged, and returns the crew to `AVAILABLE` when it holds no other `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` order. In every case the two reverse cascades fire: a linked fault event returns `DISPATCHED → DISPATCH_REQUIRED`, and a linked finding returns `REPAIR_SCHEDULED → PRIORITIZED`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for a work order.

### 3.6. The Blade Inspection Programme

Blade findings enter the system from three kinds of inspection, recorded by a `ReliabilityEngineer` or a `CrewLead` against a turbine. The `inspection_method` says how the finding was observed and is one of `DRONE`, `ROPE_ACCESS` or `GROUND_OPTICAL`; `blade_position` says which of the three blades carries it and is one of `A`, `B` or `C`; and `inspected_at` is the instant of the inspection that produced the finding. The classification itself is four measurements: `damage_category`, an IEC-style category that must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 5; `damage_type`, the mechanism, one of `LEADING_EDGE_EROSION`, `CRACK_TRANSVERSE`, `LIGHTNING_STRIKE` or `DELAMINATION`; `chord_position_pct`, the chordwise position of the damage as a percent of chord, which must be at least 0 and at most 100; `damage_length_mm`, the longest dimension of the damage in millimetres, at least 0; and `exposed_area_cm2`, the exposed laminate area in square centimetres, also at least 0. Recording a finding sets its `state` to `OPEN`, stamps `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves `waiver_rationale` null. A finding may not be recorded against a retired turbine, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED`.

Classification immediately produces a `severity_score` on a 0-to-100 scale from four factors. The **category factor** is `damage_category` divided by 5. The **type factor** is read from the mechanism: 0.30 for `LEADING_EDGE_EROSION`, 1.00 for `CRACK_TRANSVERSE`, 0.70 for `LIGHTNING_STRIKE` and 0.85 for `DELAMINATION`. The **length factor** is `damage_length_mm` divided by a normalizer of three metres — 3,000 millimetres — with the quotient capped at 1.0. The **exposure factor** is `exposed_area_cm2` divided by a normalizer of 3,000 square centimetres, again capped at 1.0. Each factor is then weighted: 30 for category, 30 for type, 20 for length and 20 for exposure.

The rounding order here is pinned and it is deliberately the **opposite** of the work-order priority score. Each of the four weighted terms is rounded half up to two decimal places **first**; the four already-rounded terms are then added; and the resulting sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again. Summing at full precision and rounding once is the wrong order and yields a different figure.

From the score, two derived fields follow. `repair_priority_band` is `CRITICAL` when `severity_score` is at least 80, `HIGH` when it is at least 60 but below 80, `MEDIUM` when it is at least 40 but below 60, and `LOW` otherwise. `repair_due_days` follows directly from the band: 14 days for `CRITICAL`, 45 for `HIGH`, 120 for `MEDIUM` and 365 for `LOW`.

Accepting a finding into the repair programme moves it `OPEN → PRIORITIZED` and may be done by a `ReliabilityEngineer` or a `CrewLead`, but never by the inspector who recorded it — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `recorded_by_user_id`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Prioritization is what makes the finding eligible to back a blade-repair work order. From there the finding's remaining transitions are all cascades of the work order that references it, described in §3.3, §3.4 and §3.5: `PRIORITIZED → REPAIR_SCHEDULED` when that order is scheduled, `REPAIR_SCHEDULED → REPAIRED` when it completes, and `REPAIR_SCHEDULED → PRIORITIZED` when it is cancelled or sent back for revision. A finding that returns to `PRIORITIZED` keeps its `severity_score`, `repair_priority_band` and `repair_due_days` exactly as they were.

The alternative outcome is a waiver, available to a `ReliabilityEngineer` only, moving the finding `OPEN → WAIVED` and storing the engineering `waiver_rationale`. A waiver is permitted only when `repair_priority_band` is `MEDIUM` or `LOW`; a `CRITICAL` or `HIGH` finding cannot be waived and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both `REPAIRED` and `WAIVED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Major Components: Stock, Cranes and the Two Execution Branches

The spare-parts strategy exists because the components that matter here have lead times measured in months. A `SiteAdmin` registers one stock line per part with a `component_type` of `GEARBOX`, `BLADE` or `GENERATOR`, a `part_number` unique across all lines, an opening `quantity_on_hand` of at least 0, a `lead_time_days` of at least 0 giving the procurement lead time for the line, a `reorder_point` of at least 0, a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0, and an `uptower_repairable` flag that is true when the line supports an in-place repair path needing no crane. Registration sets `quantity_reserved` and `quantity_consumed` to 0 and `total_units_accounted` to the opening `quantity_on_hand`.

A line's `status` is always derived, never supplied: it is `OUT_OF_STOCK` when `quantity_on_hand` is exactly 0, `BELOW_REORDER_POINT` when `quantity_on_hand` is above 0 but strictly below `reorder_point`, and `IN_STOCK` when `quantity_on_hand` is at or above `reorder_point`. `procurement_flag` is true exactly when `status` is anything other than `IN_STOCK`. Both are re-derived every time `quantity_on_hand` moves. A goods receipt, also a `SiteAdmin` action, adds a `quantity_received` to `quantity_on_hand` and to `total_units_accounted`, and re-derives `status` and `procurement_flag` from the new balance — this is the only event in the system that raises the conserved total.

Cranes are scarce in a different way: a farm holds exactly **one** crane slot. A `MaintenancePlanner` requests a mobilization with `wind_farm_id`, a `crane_class` of `CRAWLER_600T`, `TOWER_ASSIST` or `SELF_ERECTING`, a `mobilization_start_utc` giving the instant the crane arrives, a `demobilization_end_utc` strictly after it giving the instant the crane leaves, a `day_rate_cents` and a `mobilization_fee_cents` each at least 0, and a `max_lift_wind_speed_ms` greater than 0 giving the highest forecast wind speed at which this crane may lift. A mobilization begins in `state` `REQUESTED` with `reserved_replacement_count` at 0 and every cost figure — `lift_days`, `standby_days`, `billable_days`, `subtotal_cents`, `volume_credit_cents` and `total_crane_cost_cents` — at 0.

The single crane slot is enforced with a symmetric pad. Every mobilization reserves the slot for its own interval **plus two full days, 48 hours, on each side**, for haul-road access and assembly. A new mobilization is refused with `ERR_CRANE_BUFFER_CONFLICT` when its padded interval overlaps the equally padded interval of any mobilization on the same farm still in `REQUESTED` or `MOBILIZED`; mobilizations in `DEMOBILIZED` or `CANCELLED` have released the slot and are ignored. Unlike crew occupancy, exact adjacency **does** conflict here — a mobilization beginning the instant another ends sits 96 hours inside the combined pad. Demobilizing a `MOBILIZED` crane drives it `MOBILIZED → DEMOBILIZED` and frees the slot for a new buffered mobilization, but it is permitted only once every replacement holding that crane is in `EXECUTED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. Demobilization changes no money at all: `total_crane_cost_cents` and every per-replacement `allocated_crane_cost_cents` are left exactly as they stand, because the crane cost was already charged at execution time.

A replacement is planned by a `MaintenancePlanner` under a major-component work order. The body names the `work_order_id` of the `MAJOR_COMPONENT` order it executes under, the `spare_part_id` of the stock line the unit is drawn from, the `component_type` being replaced — `GEARBOX`, `BLADE` or `GENERATOR` — an `estimated_lift_hours` of at least 0 giving the crane hours the lift is expected to occupy, and `uptower_labor_hours`, the additional crew hours booked if the up-tower branch is later taken, which must be greater than 0 when supplied. The named part's `component_type` must equal the replacement's `component_type`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SPARE_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH`, and the part must have at least one unit of `quantity_on_hand` available, failing which it is refused with `ERR_SPARE_PART_UNAVAILABLE`. Planning creates the replacement in `state` `PLANNED` with `execution_path` null, `crane_mobilization_id` null and `allocated_crane_cost_cents` at 0, and moves exactly one unit from the part's `quantity_on_hand` into its `quantity_reserved` — leaving `total_units_accounted` unchanged — then re-derives the part's `status` and `procurement_flag` from the reduced on-hand balance.

From `PLANNED` the replacement forks, and the two branches are equally specified. The **crane-lift branch** binds the replacement to a mobilization, driving `PLANNED → CRANE_RESERVED`, and takes a `crane_mobilization_id` naming a mobilization in `REQUESTED` or `MOBILIZED`. The mobilization's `wind_farm_id` must equal the farm of the replacement's work order's turbine, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CRANE_FARM_MISMATCH`. A `GEARBOX` or a `BLADE` may be lifted only by a `CRAWLER_600T`. A successful reservation sets `execution_path` to `CRANE_LIFT`, stores the `crane_mobilization_id`, moves the mobilization `REQUESTED → MOBILIZED` if it was still `REQUESTED`, increments its `reserved_replacement_count` by 1, and reprices the whole mobilization. The **up-tower branch** drives `PLANNED → UPTOWER_READY` and takes no body at all. It is permitted only when the replacement's `component_type` is `GENERATOR` **and** the reserved part's `uptower_repairable` is true; a part that is not up-tower repairable is refused with `ERR_UPTOWER_NOT_PERMITTED`. Taking it sets `execution_path` to `UPTOWER_REPAIR`, leaves `crane_mobilization_id` null, sets `estimated_lift_hours` to 0 and `allocated_crane_cost_cents` to 0, and adds the stored `uptower_labor_hours` to the parent work order's `estimated_hours`.

Repricing a mobilization runs in eight steps over its **reserved set** — the replacements whose `crane_mobilization_id` is this crane and whose `state` is `CRANE_RESERVED`, `EXECUTED` or `CLOSED`.

1. `lift_days` is the sum of `estimated_lift_hours` across the reserved set divided by a crane shift of 10 hours, with the quotient rounded **up** to a whole day.
2. Each whole UTC calendar date inside the half-open span from `mobilization_start_utc` to `demobilization_end_utc` is classified. A date is a **standby** date when some weather window on the same farm is in `CONFIRMED`, its interval fully contains that date's own midnight-to-midnight UTC span, and its `forecast_wind_speed_ms` is **strictly above** the crane's `max_lift_wind_speed_ms`. Every other date is **workable**, including a date with no covering confirmed window at all. `standby_days` is the count of standby dates, and the workable-day count is the total number of dates in the span minus `standby_days`.
3. The reservation gate is applied at this point: `lift_days` must not exceed the workable-day count. Equality is permitted; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_LIFT_WIND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
4. `billable_days` is `lift_days` plus `standby_days` — the operator pays for the days it lifts and for the days the wind kept the crane idle on site.
5. `subtotal_cents` is `mobilization_fee_cents` plus `day_rate_cents` multiplied by `billable_days`.
6. A volume credit percentage is read from `subtotal_cents` and from nothing else: 0 percent when `subtotal_cents` is below 5,000,000 cents, 3 percent when it is at or above 5,000,000 cents but below 15,000,000 cents, and 6 percent at or above 15,000,000 cents. The tier is keyed on the step-5 subtotal, never on the post-credit total — keying it on the total is a circular reading and produces a different credit. `volume_credit_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that percentage divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent.
7. `total_crane_cost_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `volume_credit_cents`.
8. The total is then shared out across the reserved set as a waterfall, taking the members in order of `created_at` ascending. When the reserved set's total lift hours are above 0, each member's provisional share is `total_crane_cost_cents` multiplied by that member's `estimated_lift_hours`, divided by the reserved set's total lift hours, with the result rounded **down** to a whole cent. When the reserved set's total lift hours are exactly 0, each member's provisional share is instead `total_crane_cost_cents` divided by the number of reserved members, again rounded down. The remainder — `total_crane_cost_cents` minus the sum of the provisional shares — is assigned **in its entirety** to the reserved member with the earliest `created_at`, and every other member's `allocated_crane_cost_cents` is its provisional share unchanged.

Execution is where the two branches rejoin, and they rejoin with **divergent** side effects. Either a `CrewLead` or a `ReliabilityEngineer` may execute, from `CRANE_RESERVED → EXECUTED` or from `UPTOWER_READY → EXECUTED`, and in both cases the parent major-component work order must be in `IN_PROGRESS`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Both branches move exactly one unit from the part's `quantity_reserved` into its `quantity_consumed`, leaving `total_units_accounted` unchanged. From there they part company. On the **crane-lift branch**, the replacement's `allocated_crane_cost_cents` is added to the farm's `maintenance_spend_cents` and the same amount to its `opex_accrued_cents`. On the **up-tower branch** no crane cost is posted at all and `allocated_crane_cost_cents` stays 0; instead the farm's `maintenance_spend_cents` and `opex_accrued_cents` each rise by the replacement's `uptower_labor_hours` multiplied by an up-tower labour rate of 950,000 cents per hour, rounded half up to a whole cent. The join closes only when the parent order completes, moving every `EXECUTED` replacement of that order `EXECUTED → CLOSED` with no stock movement.

Cancelling a replacement is a `MaintenancePlanner` action carrying a `cancellation_reason`, reachable from `PLANNED`, `CRANE_RESERVED` and `UPTOWER_READY`, and it runs the same four hops from each of those states.

1. The replacement moves to `CANCELLED`, its `allocated_crane_cost_cents` is set to 0 and its `execution_path` is cleared.
2. One unit returns from the part's `quantity_reserved` to its `quantity_on_hand`, leaving `total_units_accounted` unchanged, and the part's `status` and `procurement_flag` are re-derived from the restored balance.
3. The mobilization's `reserved_replacement_count` is decremented. When it reaches 0 the mobilization moves `MOBILIZED → CANCELLED`, its `billable_days` is set to 0, and its `total_crane_cost_cents` is rewritten to 40 percent of `mobilization_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent — a cancellation fee that is itself charged to the farm's `maintenance_spend_cents` and `opex_accrued_cents`. When survivors remain, the mobilization is simply repriced and the waterfall re-run over them.
4. Whenever the parent work order is in `SCHEDULED`, it returns `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED` with `revision_round` incremented by 1, `crew_id` and `weather_window_id` set to null, its window's `active_work_order_count` decremented by 1 with the window's own `state` unchanged, and its crew returned to `AVAILABLE` when it holds no other `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` order. When the parent is in any other state this hop does nothing. This hop does **not** depend on whether the crane itself cancelled.

### 3.8. Curtailment: Grid, Noise, Icing and Wildlife

Curtailment instructions arrive from four quite different authorities, and the system separates them because only one of them is compensated. A `ControlRoomOperator` issues an order against a turbine with a `reason` of `GRID`, `NOISE`, `ICING` or `WILDLIFE`, an inclusive `start_utc`, an exclusive `end_utc` strictly after it, a `power_limit_kw` of at least 0 giving the power cap while the order is in force, and a `deemed_kwh_per_hour` of at least 0 giving the deemed generation rate used for landowner royalty. A `power_limit_kw` of exactly 0 means a **forced stop** rather than a cap. The `compensable` flag is derived, never supplied: it is true exactly when `reason` is `GRID`, and false for `NOISE`, `ICING` and `WILDLIFE`. Compensable curtailment is excluded from counted downtime and earns deemed energy for landowners; the other three reasons count as downtime and earn nothing. Issuance requires `expected_turbine_version` per §2.7, creates the order in `state` `PENDING` with `curtailed_hours` and `lost_energy_kwh` at 0, and increments the turbine's `version` by 1. It is refused with `ERR_TURBINE_DECOMMISSIONED` for a retired turbine.

Bringing an order into force drives it `PENDING → ACTIVE` and writes only the curtailment dimension. The turbine must not be `DECOMMISSIONED`, and it must currently hold fewer than **two** active orders; a third concurrent order is refused with `ERR_CURTAILMENT_STACK_LIMIT`. Activation increments the turbine's `active_curtailment_count` by 1 and sets its `effective_power_limit_kw` to the smallest `power_limit_kw` across all of that turbine's active orders — the most restrictive instruction wins. The curtailment dimension follows from that minimum: it becomes `FORCED_STOP` when the minimum is 0 and `CURTAILED` otherwise, which in transition terms means `UNCONSTRAINED → CURTAILED` when the activated order caps power above 0, `UNCONSTRAINED → FORCED_STOP` when it caps power at 0, and `CURTAILED → FORCED_STOP` when a zero-limit order is stacked on top of an existing cap. A turbine already in `FORCED_STOP` stays there. The availability dimension is not written at all.

Releasing an order drives it `ACTIVE → RELEASED` and carries a `released_at` instant. This is the one place where accrual is measured from what actually happened rather than from what was planned: `curtailed_hours` is the elapsed time from `start_utc` to `released_at` expressed in hours, **not** from `start_utc` to the order's `end_utc`, so an early release accrues strictly less than the order asked for. `lost_energy_kwh` is `curtailed_hours` multiplied by `deemed_kwh_per_hour`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The turbine's `active_curtailment_count` is decremented by 1, and `effective_power_limit_kw` is recomputed as the smallest `power_limit_kw` across the orders that remain active, or as the model's `rated_power_kw` — the uncurtailed nameplate ceiling — when none remain. The curtailment dimension is likewise re-derived: `UNCONSTRAINED` when no active order remains, which covers both `FORCED_STOP → UNCONSTRAINED` and `CURTAILED → UNCONSTRAINED`; `FORCED_STOP` when any remaining active order still caps power at 0; and `CURTAILED` otherwise.

Release is also an availability event when the curtailment was not compensable. When `compensable` is false, the released `curtailed_hours` is added to the turbine's `counted_downtime_hours_period`, and nothing is added when it is true. Because that counter moved, every service agreement on the farm still in `ACTIVE` recomputes `projected_shortfall_pct` and `at_risk_flag`, and then every work order on the farm's turbines in `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` recomputes its `priority_score` — the same two-hop propagation that fault closure triggers.

There is no direct way to cancel a pending order. `PENDING → CANCELLED` is reachable only by cascade from turbine decommissioning, and a cancelled order can never be activated afterwards. `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.9. Availability Guarantees, Liquidated Damages and Landowner Payments

A `CommercialManager` opens an availability guarantee over a farm with `wind_farm_id`, a `guaranteed_availability_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 carried to four decimal places, an `availability_basis` of either `TIME_BASED` or `ENERGY_BASED` naming which of the two computed figures the assessment is taken from, an `lda_rate_cents_per_pct_point` of at least 0 giving the liquidated damages charged per whole percentage point of shortfall, an `lda_cap_cents` of at least 0 capping the assessment for the period, and the measurement period's `period_start_utc` and `period_end_utc`. A new agreement is created in `state` `ACTIVE` with `at_risk_flag` false and with `projected_shortfall_pct`, `time_based_availability_pct`, `energy_based_availability_pct` and `assessed_lda_cents` all at 0.

Settling the period drives the agreement `ACTIVE → IN_SETTLEMENT`, and it is permitted only once the logical clock has reached or passed `period_end_utc` and only while the farm has at least one turbine whose `availability_state` is not `DECOMMISSIONED`. A farm with no turbine in service would produce a zero denominator, and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_TURBINES_IN_SERVICE` before any division is attempted. Settlement computes **both** availability figures from the same period data and then applies only the one the agreement names.

The turbine set for a settlement is every turbine on the agreement's farm whose `availability_state` is not `DECOMMISSIONED`. Counted downtime over the period is the sum of two contributions, each clipped to the half-open measurement period as described in §2.5. The first is, over every fault event on one of those turbines whose `triage_state` is `CLOSED` and whose `contractual_exclusion_pinned` is false, the hours of overlap between that event's span from `raised_at` to `cleared_at` and the measurement period. The second is, over every curtailment order on one of those turbines whose `state` is `RELEASED` and whose `compensable` is false, the hours of overlap between that order's span from `start_utc` to `end_utc` and the measurement period. **No union or merge is performed.** An hour covered both by a counted fault event and by a counted curtailment order accrues downtime under both sources and is therefore counted twice; because the aggregation is pure addition, the result is independent of the order in which rows are visited and needs no tie-break.

The denominator is the period length in hours — the elapsed time from `period_start_utc` to `period_end_utc` expressed in hours — multiplied by the number of turbines in the turbine set. `time_based_availability_pct` is then 100 multiplied by the quantity one minus counted downtime divided by that denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. `energy_based_availability_pct` reads only telemetry: it is 100 multiplied by the sum of `period_actual_energy_kwh` across the turbine set divided by the sum of `period_potential_energy_kwh` across the same set, rounded half up to four decimal places — except that when the potential sum is exactly 0 the figure is `100.0000` by definition rather than a division. The two figures are computed from identical period data and, in general, they do not agree.

The assessment then reads exactly one of them. Under a `TIME_BASED` agreement the basis figure is `time_based_availability_pct`, which ignores telemetry entirely and depends only on counted downtime hours and the turbine count. Under an `ENERGY_BASED` agreement the basis figure is `energy_based_availability_pct`, which ignores downtime intervals entirely and depends only on metered against potential energy. Because the two figures differ for identical period data, the resulting liquidated damages differ too — that divergence is the whole point of the basis choice. The shortfall is `guaranteed_availability_pct` minus the basis figure, treated as 0 whenever the basis figure meets or exceeds the guarantee, rounded half up to four decimal places; and it is taken from the **already-rounded** four-decimal basis figure, never from the raw quotient. `assessed_lda_cents` is that shortfall multiplied by `lda_rate_cents_per_pct_point`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and then capped at `lda_cap_cents` — the assessment is the lesser of the two.

While an agreement is `ACTIVE`, a lighter version of the same arithmetic runs continuously as a **live projection**. Any cascade that changes a turbine's `counted_downtime_hours_period` or its telemetry counters — a fault closure, a non-compensable curtailment release, a telemetry post, a work-order completion — recomputes `projected_shortfall_pct` on every `ACTIVE` agreement of that farm using the same shortfall arithmetic, with one substitution: counted downtime is read as the sum of the live `counted_downtime_hours_period` counter across the turbine set rather than by re-clipping interval rows to the period. The projection writes `projected_shortfall_pct` and `at_risk_flag` and **never** writes `assessed_lda_cents`. `at_risk_flag` is true exactly when `projected_shortfall_pct` is greater than 0, and while it is true it adds the liquidated-damages term to the priority score of every open work order on the farm, as §3.3 describes.

Settlement also freezes the farm's telemetry: while any agreement on the farm sits in `IN_SETTLEMENT`, a telemetry post on any of the farm's turbines is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_IN_SETTLEMENT`, and so is drafting a new landowner settlement for that farm. Finalizing drives the agreement `IN_SETTLEMENT → SETTLED`, clears `at_risk_flag`, releases the telemetry freeze, and leaves `assessed_lda_cents`, `time_based_availability_pct` and `energy_based_availability_pct` frozen at their settled values. A `SETTLED` agreement is terminal, its figures are immutable, and turbine cascades stop recomputing its `projected_shortfall_pct` and `at_risk_flag`.

Landowner royalties run on their own periods and their own basis. A `CommercialManager` drafts a settlement against a farm with a `parcel_code` of 1 to 40 characters naming the parcel being paid, a `royalty_basis` of `METERED_ONLY` or `DEEMED_INCLUSIVE` reflecting what the lease for that parcel says, the settlement's `period_start_utc` and `period_end_utc`, a `royalty_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 applied to the royalty base revenue, a `minimum_payment_cents` of at least 0 acting as a floor payment guaranteed to the landowner for the period, an `energy_price_cents_per_kwh` greater than 0, and a `road_damage_assessment_cents` of at least 0 recording assessed haul-road damage for the period for community-relations reporting. Drafting requires `expected_wind_farm_version` per §2.7. Three further gates apply: the settlement's half-open period must not overlap any non-void settlement for the same farm and the same `parcel_code`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; no agreement on the farm may be in `IN_SETTLEMENT`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_IN_SETTLEMENT`; and `minimum_payment_cents` must not exceed the farm's remaining headroom, which is `landowner_budget_cap_cents` minus the farm's current `landowner_payable_cents`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_MINIMUM_PAYMENT_ABOVE_CAP`.

Drafting computes the whole royalty chain immediately. `metered_kwh` is the sum of `period_actual_energy_kwh` across the farm's turbines whose `availability_state` is not `DECOMMISSIONED`. `deemed_kwh` depends on the basis and is where the two variants diverge: under `METERED_ONLY` it is forced to 0, so compensable grid curtailment earns the landowner nothing even though the operator was compensated for it, while under `DEEMED_INCLUSIVE` it is the sum, over every curtailment order on those turbines whose `state` is `RELEASED` and whose `compensable` is true, of the hours of overlap between that order's declared span from `start_utc` to `end_utc` — never from `start_utc` to `released_at`, so the settlement clips the full declared span even when the order was released early — and the half-open settlement period, multiplied by its `deemed_kwh_per_hour`. Because `deemed_kwh` is added to the base rather than substituted into it, a `DEEMED_INCLUSIVE` settlement produces a strictly larger royalty than a `METERED_ONLY` settlement for identical farm telemetry whenever any compensable curtailment falls in the period. The royalty base in kilowatt-hours is `metered_kwh` plus `deemed_kwh`; `royalty_cents` is that base multiplied by `energy_price_cents_per_kwh`, multiplied by `royalty_pct`, divided by 100, and rounded half up to a whole cent — the energy figures keep full precision until this single rounding. `payment_cents` is the larger of `minimum_payment_cents` and `royalty_cents`. `road_levy_cents` is `payment_cents` multiplied by the farm's `road_levy_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `community_levy_cents` is `payment_cents` multiplied by the farm's `community_levy_pct` divided by 100, likewise rounded half up.

A drafted settlement sits in `state` `DRAFT` with `void_reason` null, and it posts **nothing** to the farm ledger — a draft is not part of `opex_accrued_cents`. Approval drives it `DRAFT → APPROVED` and is refused while any agreement on the same farm is in `IN_SETTLEMENT`. Approval makes four postings in one transaction: `payment_cents` is added to the farm's `landowner_payable_cents`; `road_levy_cents` plus `road_damage_assessment_cents` is added to `road_maintenance_reserve_cents`; `community_levy_cents` is added to `community_fund_balance_cents`; and the total of those three postings is added to `opex_accrued_cents`, preserving the invariant of §2.10 exactly.

Voiding an approved settlement drives it `APPROVED → VOID`, stores the `void_reason`, and is a pure compensating action: it subtracts `payment_cents` from `landowner_payable_cents`, `road_levy_cents` plus `road_damage_assessment_cents` from `road_maintenance_reserve_cents`, `community_levy_cents` from `community_fund_balance_cents`, and the same total from `opex_accrued_cents`. The settlement's own computed amounts are left intact for audit, and the ledger invariant holds exactly after the reversal with no rounding drift. `VOID` is terminal, and a voided settlement no longer blocks a fresh settlement over the same parcel and period.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted beneath the `/wind-ops` prefix and obeys the envelope, pagination, identity and rejection rules of Section 2. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer with HTTP 201; every other successful call — state transitions, telemetry posts, amendments, retrievals and list reads — answers with HTTP 200. Successful responses carry the created or updated resource in `data` unless the description says otherwise, and list endpoints carry an array in `data` together with the pagination fields in `meta`. The Authorized Role(s) column is exhaustive: a caller presenting any other role in `X-User-Role` is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and a caller whose role is listed may still be rejected by the segregation-of-duties and crew-identity rules of §2.1.
