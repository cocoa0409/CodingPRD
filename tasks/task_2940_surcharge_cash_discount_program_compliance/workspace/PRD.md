# Surcharge & Cash Discount Program Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Card-acceptance pricing has become one of the most legally exposed surfaces a merchant touches. Whether a merchant may add a fee to a credit-card sale at all, how large that fee may be, whether the fee must be disclosed at the door and again at the register, whether it must be printed as its own line on the receipt, and whether the merchant must instead post a dual-price list and describe the difference as a discount for cash — every one of these answers changes at a state line, and several of them change again when a legislature or an attorney general moves. Card networks add a second, independent rulebook: a surcharging merchant must register with each network it intends to surcharge, must wait out a notice period before the registration takes effect, must price consistently either across a whole brand or across named credit product tiers, must never surcharge a debit or prepaid card, and must never collect more than the merchant-discount cost it can document. When any of these conditions lapses, the merchant is expected to stop surcharging at the terminal — not at the end of the month.

This document specifies the backend service that holds that entire compliance posture as data. The service records each state's statutory profile, each merchant's documented cost of acceptance, each program's pinned cost evidence and rate table, each network registration and its notice window, each disclosure artifact and its attested effectivity window, each point-of-sale terminal and what it is currently authorised to enforce, and each sale the terminals capture with the exact surcharge or cash discount the server computed for it. It also records the enforcement side of the story: complaints filed by networks, by attorney-general offices, and by cardholders, the investigation and remediation tracks those complaints follow, the exposure score they accumulate against a program, and the cardholder refunds a merchant issues when a remediation plan is accepted.

The service is deliberately unforgiving at the moment of capture. A terminal may only book a sale while it is actively enforcing a live program whose disclosures are attested and in-window, whose registration is effective, and whose rate table still sits under the cap. Everything else in the system — the law-change cascade, the quarantine cascade, the remediation cascade, the segregation-of-duties checks — exists so that a merchant cannot keep collecting a surcharge that has quietly become unlawful. Readers should treat the compliance rules in this specification as hard preconditions, not as advisory warnings: a program that has lost a precondition is expected to be visibly blocked in the data, and its terminals are expected to stop.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Users, Roles, and Access Control

Every request to this service carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of the six declared roles. A request that omits either header, sends either header empty, or names a role outside the declared six is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-formed request whose role is simply not listed as authorised for the target operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); the authorised roles per operation are tabulated in §4.

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `MerchantOperator`, `NetworkAuditor`, `StateRegulator`, and `TerminalAgent`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is a platform operator: it seeds jurisdictions and merchants and it drives the service's logical clock. A `ComplianceOfficer` is a payment-compliance professional who files cost documentation, builds programs and their rate tables, submits and effectuates network registrations, attests disclosure artifacts, activates and suspends and resumes and withdraws programs, opens investigations, submits remediation plans, and creates, issues and voids refunds. A `MerchantOperator` is the merchant itself acting through its portal: it provisions and configures its own terminals, drives its own terminals between enforcement and quarantine, and files draft disclosure artifacts for its programs. A `NetworkAuditor` is a card-network compliance auditor: it decides registrations, files complaints whose `source` is `NETWORK` or `CARDHOLDER`, and rules on those complaints' investigation and remediation. A `StateRegulator` is a state attorney-general office: it applies statutory law changes to a jurisdiction, files complaints whose `source` is `ATTORNEY_GENERAL`, and rules on those complaints' investigation and remediation. A `TerminalAgent` is the point-of-sale terminal itself, authenticating as its own terminal identity in order to capture sales.

Two of the six roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `MerchantOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Merchant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Merchant` compare the resource's merchant foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a `MerchantOperator` may only provision terminals under its own merchant record, only configure and drive terminals whose `merchant_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and only draft disclosure artifacts for programs whose `merchant_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_MERCHANT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `TerminalAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Terminal`; a capture whose body `terminal_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

The other four roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `NetworkAuditor`, or `StateRegulator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition attributes an action to its actor — as `attested_by_user_id` on `CostDocumentation` and on `DisclosureArtifact`, as `activated_by_user_id` on `SurchargeProgram`, as `created_by_user_id` on `BrandRateRule` and on `RemediationRefund`, as `issued_by_user_id` on `RemediationRefund`, as `decided_by_user_id` on `NetworkRegistration`, and as `filed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on `ComplaintCase` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. Because these identifiers are opaque and free, the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.10 compare them only to one another.

Several operations accept an `action` or a `decision` discriminator in the body, and role authority is finer-grained than the endpoint. Where the acting role is authorised for the endpoint but not for the specific value requested, the rejection is `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403) rather than `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Concretely: on program status actions, `SUSPEND` is open to both `ComplianceOfficer` and `NetworkAuditor` while `RESUME` and `WITHDRAW` are open to `ComplianceOfficer` only; on terminal state actions, `ENFORCE` and `RESTORE` are open to `MerchantOperator` only while `QUARANTINE` is open to `MerchantOperator` and `ComplianceOfficer`; on complaint investigation actions, `REVIEW` is open to `ComplianceOfficer` only while `SUBSTANTIATE` and `DISMISS` are open to `NetworkAuditor` and `StateRegulator` only; on complaint remediation actions, `SUBMIT_PLAN` is open to `ComplianceOfficer` only while `ACCEPT_PLAN` and `CLOSE` are open to `NetworkAuditor` and `StateRegulator` only.

Complaint channels are owned. A `NetworkAuditor` may file and decide cases whose `source` is `NETWORK` or `CARDHOLDER`; a `StateRegulator` may file and decide cases whose `source` is `ATTORNEY_GENERAL`. A role reaching across that boundary — a `NetworkAuditor` acting on an `ATTORNEY_GENERAL` case, or a `StateRegulator` acting on a `NETWORK` or `CARDHOLDER` case — is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_SOURCE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), and the check applies to complaint filing, to investigation actions, and to remediation actions alike.

Read access is broad. Every retrieval and list operation is available to all six roles, with two exceptions: the per-program exposure summary and the per-program settlement summary are available to `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `NetworkAuditor`, and `StateRegulator`, and exclude `MerchantOperator` and `TerminalAgent`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and the `offset` parameter defaults to 0 with a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape defect and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Every list result is ordered by `created_at` ascending — the oldest row first — and pagination is applied to that ordering. List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"`, and their `meta` object includes `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more` which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

Beyond pagination, each collection accepts a small set of equality filters. A filter is always named exactly for the resource field it constrains, is always optional, and combines with every other supplied filter conjunctively — a request that supplies two filters returns only rows matching both. The filters available on each collection are called out in the workflow narratives of §3, alongside the fields they select on.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Version Counters

Every resource this service persists is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied identifier in a create body is ignored. Foreign-key fields — a program's `merchant_id`, a rate rule's `program_id`, a refund's `transaction_id`, and so on — always carry the plain UUID of the referenced row.

Most resources also carry three further common properties. The `created_at` field is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. The `updated_at` field is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent mutation, in the same format, and equals `created_at` for a row that has never been mutated. The `version` field is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; it is the anchor for the optimistic-concurrency rules of §2.7. `Jurisdiction`, `Merchant`, `CostDocumentation`, `SurchargeProgram`, `BrandRateRule`, `NetworkRegistration`, `DisclosureArtifact`, `Terminal`, `ComplaintCase`, and `RemediationRefund` all carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

`Transaction` is the single exception. A captured sale is an immutable event, not a mutable resource: a `Transaction` carries `id` and `created_at` only, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation entirely. A `Transaction`'s derived `refunded_surcharge_cents` is recomputed at read time from the refunds that reference it rather than being treated as a versioned mutation of the sale.

Whenever a rule in this document says that an operation "bumps" or "increments" a row's version, it means both that `version` increases by 1 and that `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock. Cascades follow the same discipline: a cascade that changes a child row bumps the child's `version`, and a cascade that changes a parent's derived fields bumps the parent's `version`, so a client can always detect that something it was holding has moved.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this service is done against a server-held **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. Notice periods, effectivity windows, disclosure expiry, complaint response deadlines, transaction capture instants, and every `*_at` field are evaluated and stamped from that clock. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until it is moved, every temporal rule in the system is evaluated against that fixed instant.

A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting clock as `current_utc`. Moving the clock changes no entity state and triggers no cascade — it only changes the instant against which subsequent requests are evaluated. This means an artifact whose window has lapsed does not become `EXPIRED` merely because the clock moved past `expires_at`; it simply stops satisfying a disclosure requirement, because readiness is evaluated against the clock at the moment of evaluation. Day arithmetic anywhere in this specification is exact multiples of 24 hours: "30 days after" an instant means that instant plus 2,592,000 seconds.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Existence

Identifiers in a request path are resolved left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the rejection. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTIFACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_REFUND_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved with the same codes, and a body foreign key that does not resolve is likewise a 404. Body-level resolution happens after request-shape validation and before any business rule: a create that names a non-existent merchant is a 404, not a 422, even when the body would also have failed a later compliance check.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent record's `version`, because both are decisions the client makes on the basis of derived state it read a moment earlier.

Filing a `BrandRateRule` under a program requires `expected_program_version` in the body as a mandatory field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SurchargeProgram`. Every rate rule is admitted or refused against the program's pinned `effective_cap_pct` and its `consistency_scope`, and both of those move whenever another writer files a rule, re-pins the cost documentation, or drives the program through a status change. If `expected_program_version` does not equal the program's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the client validated its rate against a cap that no longer holds. The same field and the same rejection apply to the partial update that re-pins a program's cost documentation or changes its consistency scope.

Filing a `CostDocumentation` under a merchant requires `expected_merchant_version` in the body as a mandatory field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Merchant`. A new document legitimately supersedes the merchant's currently `ACTIVE` document, so the write would otherwise succeed on its own merits; the version check exists to catch the case where the merchant's surcharge ledger or active document has already moved on and the filer's superseding decision was made against an obsolete view. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MERCHANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other operation in this service accepts a version field. Activation, status actions, terminal actions, attestation, registration decisions, effectuation, complaint actions, refund issuance and voiding, sale capture, and every read take no `expected_*_version` input at all, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Percentages, Cents, and Rounding Discipline

Money is always integer cents. There are no fractional-cent fields anywhere in this specification, and no currency code: every `*_cents` field is a whole number of cents.

Percentages are decimal numbers carried to **four decimal places**, rounded **half up**. This covers `base_mdr_pct`, `assessment_pct`, `documented_cost_pct`, `surcharge_cap_pct`, `effective_cap_pct`, `rate_pct`, and `applied_rate_pct`. Wherever a percentage is derived from other percentages, each rounding step named in §3 is applied in the order stated and no additional rounding is inserted.

Any conversion from a percentage to cents rounds **half up to the nearest whole cent**, and that rounding happens exactly once per transaction line. Aggregate figures never re-round an amount that was already rounded at capture time; where an aggregate deliberately reports what a pooled rounding would have produced, it is published as a separate, clearly named reference figure and is never the settled number.

Exposure scores are decimal numbers carried to **two decimal places**, rounded **half up**, and the score ceiling described in §3.10 is applied *after* that rounding rather than before it.

### 2.9. The Order of Rejection

A single request can fail many ways at once, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first failing check determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status. Later checks are not evaluated once an earlier one has failed.

1. **Header validity** — both auth headers present, non-empty, and naming one of the six roles: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every operation.
2. **Endpoint authorisation** — the acting role is listed for the operation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403. Applies to every operation.
3. **Action-level authorisation** — the acting role is permitted for the specific `action` or `decision` requested: `ERR_ACTION_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`, 403. Applies to the four multi-action operations: program status actions, terminal state actions, complaint investigation actions, and complaint remediation actions.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right: the 404 family of §2.6. Applies to every operation with a path identifier.
5. **Merchant ownership** — a `MerchantOperator`'s `X-User-ID` equals the target's merchant: `ERR_MERCHANT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, 403. Applies to exactly four operations: provisioning a terminal under a merchant, configuring a terminal, driving a terminal's state action, and drafting a disclosure artifact for a program (where the comparison is against that program's `merchant_id`).
6. **Terminal identity** — a `TerminalAgent`'s `X-User-ID` equals the body `terminal_id`: `ERR_TERMINAL_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, 403. Applies to sale capture only.
7. **Complaint channel ownership** — the acting role owns the case's `source` channel: `ERR_COMPLAINT_SOURCE_ROLE_MISMATCH`, 403. Applies to exactly three operations: filing a complaint, investigation actions, and remediation actions.
8. **Request shape** — required fields present, enum members recognised, numbers inside their declared ranges, ISO 8601 instants parseable, `base_amount_cents` at least 1, `refund_amount_cents` at least 1, `requested_surcharge_cents` at least 0, a disclosure artifact's `expires_at` strictly after its `effective_from`, a rate rule's `effective_to` strictly after its `effective_from`, and `product_tier` equal to `NOT_APPLICABLE` exactly when `card_funding` is not `CREDIT`: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every operation.
9. **Jurisdiction cap coherence** — a jurisdiction whose `surcharge_permitted` is true carries a non-null `surcharge_cap_pct`, and one whose `surcharge_permitted` is false carries `surcharge_cap_pct` null: `ERR_JURISDICTION_CAP_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to jurisdiction registration and to law changes.
10. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves: the 404 family. Applies to merchant onboarding, program creation, the program partial update, terminal configuration, sale capture, complaint filing, and refund creation.
11. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_STATE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERMINAL_SERIAL`, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_REGISTRATION`, all 409. Applies to jurisdiction registration, terminal provisioning, and registration submission respectively.
12. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_MERCHANT_VERSION`, 412. Applies to rate-rule filing, the program partial update, and cost-documentation filing.
13. **Declared transition available from the current state** — `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ARTIFACT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TERMINAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`, or `ERR_INVALID_REFUND_STATE`, all 409. Applies to every state-changing operation.
14. **Capturing terminal is enforcing** — a sale may only be booked from a terminal in state `ENFORCING`; a `PROVISIONED`, `CONFIGURED`, `QUARANTINED`, or `DISABLED` terminal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINAL_STATE`, 409. Applies to sale capture.
15. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to exactly two operations: program activation and refund issuance.
16. **Surcharge permitted in the jurisdiction** — `ERR_SURCHARGE_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`, 422. Applies to four operations: program creation, program activation, program status actions, and sale capture.
17. **Cash discount permitted in the jurisdiction** — `ERR_CASH_DISCOUNT_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`, 422. Applies to three operations: program creation, program activation, and program status actions.
18. **Credit-only surcharging at the terminal** — `requested_surcharge_cents` is 0 whenever `card_funding` is `DEBIT` or `PREPAID`: `ERR_DEBIT_SURCHARGE_FORBIDDEN`, 422. Applies to sale capture.
19. **Debit exclusion loaded** — a terminal reaching enforcement under a `SURCHARGE` program has `debit_exclusion_enabled` true: `ERR_DEBIT_EXCLUSION_NOT_ENABLED`, 422. Applies to terminal state actions.
20. **Program is not prohibited or withdrawn** — a sale may not be booked against a program in status `DISABLED` or `WITHDRAWN`: `ERR_PROGRAM_DISABLED_BY_PROHIBITION`, 422. Applies to sale capture.
21. **Terminal-program agreement** — the terminal's `program_id` equals the body `program_id`: `ERR_TERMINAL_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to sale capture and to terminal configuration.
22. **Jurisdiction scope agreement** — the program's `jurisdiction_id` equals its merchant's `jurisdiction_id`: `ERR_JURISDICTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to program creation.
23. **Cost-documentation ownership** — the pinned document's `merchant_id` equals the program's `merchant_id`: `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_MERCHANT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to program creation and the program partial update.
24. **Refund transaction scope** — the refunded transaction belongs to the merchant that owns the complaint case's program: `ERR_REFUND_TRANSACTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to refund creation.
25. **Pinned documentation is current** — the program's pinned `CostDocumentation` is in state `ACTIVE`: `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`, 409. Applies to rate-rule filing and program activation.
26. **Rate-window separation** — `ERR_RATE_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, 409. Applies to rate-rule filing.
27. **Disclosure-window separation** — `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to artifact attestation.
28. **Notice period elapsed** — `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, 422. Applies to registration effectuation.
29. **Product-tier scope agreement** — `ERR_PRODUCT_TIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 422. Applies to rate-rule filing.
30. **Brand coverage** — `ERR_BRAND_CONSISTENCY_VIOLATION`, 422. Applies to program activation.
31. **Registration effectiveness** — `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_EFFECTIVE`, 409. Applies to program activation and to program status actions.
32. **Disclosure readiness** — `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`, 422. Applies to three operations: program activation, program status actions, and terminal state actions.
33. **Posted price list** — `ERR_PRICE_POSTING_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to program activation.
34. **A rate rule is in effect** — `ERR_NO_RATE_RULE_IN_EFFECT`, 422. Applies to sale capture and to program activation.
35. **Receipt line item** — `ERR_RECEIPT_LINE_ITEM_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to sale capture.
36. **Rate under the cap** — `ERR_SURCHARGE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to rate-rule filing.
37. **Terminal-computed amount agrees with the server** — `ERR_SURCHARGE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to sale capture.
38. **Refund within the unrefunded surcharge** — `ERR_REFUND_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SURCHARGE`, 422. Applies to refund creation.
39. **One live refund per case and transaction** — `ERR_REFUND_DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION`, 409. Applies to refund creation.
40. **No pending refunds at closure** — `ERR_REMEDIATION_REFUNDS_PENDING`, 409. Applies to complaint remediation actions.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. First, because disclosure readiness sits at step 32 and the posted-price-list rule at step 33, a cash-discount program that has attested its signage but not its price list fails with `ERR_PRICE_POSTING_REQUIRED` and not with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` — the two conditions stay independently reachable, which is why the posted price list is deliberately excluded from the readiness computation described in §3.6. Second, because the credit-only check at step 18 precedes the rate-selection check at step 34, a terminal that reports a positive surcharge on a debit card is told that debit surcharging is forbidden rather than being told no rate rule was found.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this service must be made by two different people, and the server enforces that by comparing opaque `X-User-ID` values to one another.

A program may not be activated by an officer who attested any of that program's disclosure artifacts. At activation, the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against `attested_by_user_id` of every `DisclosureArtifact` belonging to the program; if it matches any of them, the activation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The comparison covers every artifact of the program regardless of the artifact's current state, so expiring an artifact does not launder the conflict.

A refund may not be issued by the officer who created it. At issuance, the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the refund's `created_by_user_id`, and equality is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Voiding a refund carries no such restriction: the officer who created a refund may void it.

### 2.11. Cascade Integrity and Conserved Quantities

This service maintains several derived aggregates, and the correctness bar is that they are consistent immediately after every single mutation — there is no deferred reconciliation, no eventual convergence, and no background sweep. A request either completes with every derived field it touches recomputed, or it fails and changes nothing.

Two invariants are load-bearing. The **money invariant**: for every merchant, the sum of `net_surcharge_collected_cents` and the total of that merchant's issued refunds equals `gross_surcharge_collected_cents`, and `gross_surcharge_collected_cents` never decreases for any reason — refunds move the net figure only. The **terminal census invariant**: a merchant's `enforcing_terminal_count` always equals the number of that merchant's terminals currently in state `ENFORCING`, which means a quarantine cascade followed by the matching restorations returns the count to its pre-cascade value, while terminals driven to `DISABLED` leave the census permanently smaller.

Cascades are described in §3 at the point where they originate, and each is expressed as an ordered sequence of steps because later steps read state that earlier steps wrote. A cascade never partially applies: if a law change disables three programs, all three programs, all of their affected terminals, and all of the affected merchants' census figures move together within that one request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Jurisdictional Law Variance and the Prohibition Cascade

Everything in this service ultimately hangs off a jurisdiction. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each state the platform operates in by supplying its `state_code` — exactly two uppercase letters — together with the seven law-variance flags that describe what that state permits and demands. `surcharge_permitted` says whether credit-card surcharging is lawful at all. `surcharge_cap_pct` carries the statutory ceiling as a percentage: it must be greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimal places, it must be supplied and non-null whenever `surcharge_permitted` is true, and it must be null whenever `surcharge_permitted` is false. Either half of that coherence rule being broken is rejected with `ERR_JURISDICTION_CAP_REQUIRED`. `cash_discount_permitted` says whether a cash-discount program structure is lawful. `price_posting_required` says whether the state demands a posted dual-price list from cash-discount merchants. `disclosure_signage_required` says whether entry and point-of-sale signage is mandated. `receipt_line_item_required` says whether the surcharge must be printed as its own line on the receipt. Finally `ag_enforcement_posture` records how aggressive the state's attorney general is, as one of `PERMISSIVE`, `MONITORED`, or `RESTRICTIVE`; that posture becomes a multiplier in the attorney-general exposure calculation of §3.10.

No two jurisdictions may share a `state_code`; a second registration for the same code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_STATE`. A freshly registered jurisdiction begins with `law_change_count` at 0 and `last_law_change_at` null. The jurisdiction collection may be filtered by `state_code` and by `surcharge_permitted`.

Legislatures move, and when they do a `StateRegulator` applies the change. A law change always restates `surcharge_permitted`, and may additionally restate any of `surcharge_cap_pct`, `cash_discount_permitted`, `price_posting_required`, `disclosure_signage_required`, `receipt_line_item_required`, and `ag_enforcement_posture`; flags not named in the request keep their current values. Applying a law change increments `law_change_count` by exactly 1, sets `last_law_change_at` to the current logical clock, and bumps the jurisdiction's `version`. The cap-coherence rule is re-evaluated against the post-change picture, so a change that turns surcharging on without supplying a cap, or turns it off while leaving a cap in place, is rejected with `ERR_JURISDICTION_CAP_REQUIRED` and nothing is applied.

A law change that flips `surcharge_permitted` from true to false triggers the **prohibition cascade**, evaluated in this order. First, every `SurchargeProgram` whose `jurisdiction_id` is the changed jurisdiction, whose `program_type` is `SURCHARGE`, and whose `status` is neither `DISABLED` nor `WITHDRAWN` moves to `DISABLED` with `disable_reason` set to `JURISDICTION_PROHIBITION` — that is, programs in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, and `REMEDIATION` are all swept. Second, for each such program, every `Terminal` whose `program_id` is that program and whose `state` is `CONFIGURED` or `ENFORCING` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `PROGRAM_DISABLED`; note that the prohibition sweep reaches configured-but-not-yet-enforcing terminals, which is wider than the suspension sweep described in §3.7. Third, each of those terminal transitions recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` on the terminal's merchant. Every cascaded program's `version` is bumped by 1, as is every cascaded terminal's. Programs whose `program_type` is `CASH_DISCOUNT` are untouched by a surcharge prohibition, even in the very same jurisdiction — the two program structures are governed by two independent flags.

A law change that flips `surcharge_permitted` from false to true triggers the **re-permission cascade**, which is deliberately narrower. Every program of that jurisdiction whose `status` is `DISABLED` and whose `disable_reason` is exactly `JURISDICTION_PROHIBITION` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `disable_reason` reset to `OPERATOR_SUSPENSION`, and its `effective_cap_pct` is recomputed against the jurisdiction's new `surcharge_cap_pct`. Its `version` is bumped. Re-permission never re-activates a program by itself and never restores a quarantined terminal: an officer must resume the program explicitly, which is why `DISABLED → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` is a three-step round trip rather than an automatic one. Programs that were disabled for any other reason stay `DISABLED`.

### 3.2. Merchant Onboarding and the Documented-Cost Chain

A `PlatformAdmin` onboards a merchant into a jurisdiction by supplying the governing `jurisdiction_id`, the merchant's `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters — and its four-digit `mcc`. A new merchant starts with `gross_surcharge_collected_cents`, `net_surcharge_collected_cents`, and `enforcing_terminal_count` all at 0, and with `active_cost_documentation_id` null. The merchant collection may be filtered by `jurisdiction_id` and by `mcc`.

Before a merchant may surcharge anything, its cost of card acceptance has to be documented, because the surcharge is capped at that documented cost. A `ComplianceOfficer` files a `CostDocumentation` record against the merchant, supplying `base_mdr_pct` (the acquirer's base merchant-discount rate, at least 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimal places), `assessment_pct` (the network assessment, in the same range and precision), `per_item_fee_cents` (the fixed per-transaction fee, at least 0 and at most 100000), `evidence_uri` (where the acquirer statement evidencing the cost lives, between 1 and 300 characters), and the `expected_merchant_version` demanded by §2.7. The server stamps `attested_by_user_id` with the filing officer's `X-User-ID` and `effective_from` with the logical clock, and the row is created in state `ACTIVE` with `superseded_at` null.

Filing runs a two-step derivation, and the order is pinned because the two numbers are read by everything downstream. **Step one** freezes an average ticket. The server collects every `Transaction` captured by a terminal belonging to this merchant whose `occurred_at` is at or before the current logical clock — every captured sale qualifies regardless of which of the three outcomes it carries, there is no lower time bound, and each sale is counted once. It then takes the arithmetic mean of those sales' `base_amount_cents`, rounds it half up to a whole number of cents, and stores the result as `average_ticket_cents` on the new document. If the merchant has captured no transactions at all, `average_ticket_cents` is instead the default average ticket of 5000 cents — fifty dollars — which is a fallback, never an error. Only `base_amount_cents` participates: surcharge and cash-discount amounts are deliberately excluded so that the ticket average cannot feed back into the very surcharge it helps to cap.

**Step two** turns the three cost components into a single percentage. The per-item component is `per_item_fee_cents` divided by `average_ticket_cents`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to four decimal places. `documented_cost_pct` is then `base_mdr_pct` plus `assessment_pct` plus that per-item component, with the sum rounded half up to four decimal places. The two rounding steps happen in exactly that order — the per-item component first, then the total — and no other document, program, or transaction participates in the arithmetic.

This chain looks circular and is cut on purpose. The merchant-discount cost depends on the average ticket, the average ticket depends on transactions, and transactions are priced by the very rate that this cost caps. The circle is broken by freezing both derived numbers onto the `CostDocumentation` row at filing time: `average_ticket_cents` and `documented_cost_pct` are never recomputed afterwards. A sale captured tomorrow does not retroactively change a document filed today; only a *new* document, filed later, picks up the newer average ticket.

Filing also supersedes. The merchant's existing document in state `ACTIVE`, if there is one, moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the logical clock and its own `version` bumped; the merchant's `active_cost_documentation_id` is repointed at the new document; and the merchant's `version` is bumped by 1. A merchant therefore has at most one `ACTIVE` document at any instant, and the sequence of superseded documents is the audit trail. The consequence for programs is sharp: any `SurchargeProgram` still pinned to the document that just became `SUPERSEDED` can no longer file rate rules or activate, failing with `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`, until an officer re-pins it. Cost-documentation records may be listed filtered by `merchant_id` and by `state`.

### 3.3. Program Construction: Pinning, Scope, and the Effective Cap

A `ComplianceOfficer` creates a program by naming the `merchant_id` that will operate it, the `jurisdiction_id` whose law governs it, the `cost_documentation_id` whose cost evidence it pins, the `program_type`, and the `consistency_scope`. The supplied `jurisdiction_id` must equal the merchant's own `jurisdiction_id`; a program cannot be governed by a state its merchant does not operate in, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_JURISDICTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The pinned document must belong to the same merchant, on pain of `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_MERCHANT_MISMATCH`, and must be in state `ACTIVE` at the moment of pinning. Creating a `SURCHARGE` program in a jurisdiction whose `surcharge_permitted` is false is rejected with `ERR_SURCHARGE_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`; creating a `CASH_DISCOUNT` program in a jurisdiction whose `cash_discount_permitted` is false is rejected with `ERR_CASH_DISCOUNT_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`.

`program_type` is the fork the whole domain turns on and is immutable once set. A `SURCHARGE` program adds a card-acceptance fee on top of the posted price. A `CASH_DISCOUNT` program posts the card price as the price and deducts a discount for customers who pay in cash. The two structures follow different activation preconditions, different pricing arithmetic, different disclosure requirements, and different caps.

`consistency_scope` governs how granular the rate table may be. `BRAND_LEVEL` means the merchant prices a whole card brand identically and every rate rule must carry `product_tier` of `ALL`. `PRODUCT_LEVEL` means the merchant prices named credit product tiers separately and every rate rule must name a specific tier rather than `ALL`.

A newly created program starts with `status` of `DRAFT`, `disable_reason` of `NONE`, `disclosure_ready` false, `network_exposure_score` 0, and `ag_exposure_score` 0, and with both `activated_at` and `activated_by_user_id` null until it is activated for the first time. Its `cost_documentation_pinned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `effective_cap_pct` is computed immediately.

The **effective cap** is the single most important derived number on a program, and it is recomputed on every mutation of the program: at creation, on the partial update described below, on activation, on a resume, and on the re-permission cascade of §3.1. For a `CASH_DISCOUNT` program the cap is simply the cash-discount ceiling of 10.0 percent. For a `SURCHARGE` program it is the smallest of three numbers: the frozen `documented_cost_pct` of the pinned cost documentation, the jurisdiction's statutory `surcharge_cap_pct`, and the network ceiling of 3.0 percent. Whichever of the three is smallest binds — a merchant whose documented cost is 2.19 percent is capped at 2.19 even in a state that permits four, while a merchant whose documented cost is 3.05 percent is capped at 3.0 by the network ceiling. Because all three inputs already carry four decimal places, taking the minimum introduces no further rounding. The cap is inclusive: a rate rule whose rate exactly equals the cap is admissible.

While a program has not yet reached `ACTIVE` — that is, while its `status` is `DRAFT` or `PENDING_REGISTRATION` — an officer may adjust it through a partial update that accepts an optional replacement `cost_documentation_id`, an optional replacement `consistency_scope`, and the mandatory `expected_program_version` of §2.7. Re-pinning to a fresh `ACTIVE` document is how a program recovers from `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`. The update recomputes `effective_cap_pct`, re-stamps `cost_documentation_pinned_at` from the logical clock only when `cost_documentation_id` actually changed, and bumps the program's `version` by 1. The replacement document must still belong to the program's merchant. Attempting the same update on a program in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REMEDIATION`, `DISABLED`, or `WITHDRAWN` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`. Programs may be listed filtered by `merchant_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `cost_documentation_id`, `status`, and `program_type`.

### 3.4. The Rate Table and Its Separation Rules

A program's prices live in its rate rules. Each rule names the `brand` it prices — one of `VISA`, `MASTERCARD`, `DISCOVER`, or `AMEX` — the `product_tier` it applies to, the `rate_pct` to apply, and a closed effectivity interval running from `effective_from` through `effective_to` inclusive. `rate_pct` must be greater than 0 and at most 100 with up to four decimal places, and `effective_to` must be strictly after `effective_from`. The filing officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as the rule's `created_by_user_id`, and filing a rule bumps the parent program's `version` by 1 in addition to creating the rule. Filing carries the `expected_program_version` guard of §2.7 and is refused on a program whose pinned documentation has been superseded, with `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`, or on a program that has been withdrawn, with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`.

`product_tier` is one of `ALL`, `CONSUMER_CREDIT`, `COMMERCIAL_CREDIT`, or `REWARDS_CREDIT`, and it must agree with the program's `consistency_scope` in both directions: a rule filed under a `BRAND_LEVEL` program must carry `ALL`, and a rule filed under a `PRODUCT_LEVEL` program must carry something other than `ALL`. Either direction being violated is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_TIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This is what "brand-level versus product-level consistency" means operationally: a merchant cannot quietly charge more on rewards cards while claiming to price the brand uniformly, and it cannot claim tier-level pricing while filing a single catch-all rate.

Rate changes must also be spaced out in time, because a merchant that re-prices every week cannot honestly disclose its rates. A candidate rule is compared against every existing rule of the *same* program, the *same* `brand`, and the *same* `product_tier`; rules of another brand or another tier never constrain it. For each such peer, the gap is measured between the two closed intervals: if the candidate's `effective_from` lies after the peer's `effective_to`, the gap is the number of whole days between those two instants; if the peer's `effective_from` lies after the candidate's `effective_to`, the gap is the number of whole days between those two instants; and if the two closed intervals overlap at any point, the gap is zero. A gap of fewer than 30 days against any peer is rejected with `ERR_RATE_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. Because the comparison is a strict "fewer than", exact adjacency fails: a candidate starting at the very instant a peer ended yields a gap of zero and is refused. This is the opposite convention from the disclosure windows of §3.6, where exact adjacency is accepted — the two boundary conventions are intentionally different and must not be unified.

Finally, the rate itself must sit under the program's cap. A `rate_pct` at most `effective_cap_pct` is admitted, with equality passing; a `rate_pct` strictly greater than `effective_cap_pct` is rejected with `ERR_SURCHARGE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The three rate-rule checks are evaluated in the order given here — the window-separation buffer first, then the product-tier scope agreement, then the cap — so a rule that both crowds a peer and exceeds the cap surfaces as a buffer violation. Rate rules may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `brand`, and `product_tier`.

### 3.5. Network Registration and the Notice Period

Card networks require advance notice before a merchant begins surcharging, and this service models that as a first-class record. A `ComplianceOfficer` submits a registration against a program by naming the `network` receiving it — one of `VISA`, `MASTERCARD`, `DISCOVER`, or `AMEX` — and the `registration_ref` string the network issued, between 1 and 64 characters. Submission is only available for a program whose `program_type` is `SURCHARGE`, whose `status` is `DRAFT`, and whose jurisdiction has `surcharge_permitted` true. A `CASH_DISCOUNT` program never enters `PENDING_REGISTRATION` at all — network registration simply does not apply to a cash-discount structure — and an attempt from a program in any state other than `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`.

Submission stamps the registration's `submitted_at` from the logical clock, copies the platform's notice period of 30 days onto the row as `notice_days`, and derives `earliest_effective_at` as `submitted_at` plus those 30 days. The registration is created in state `SUBMITTED`, with `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_note`, `effective_at`, and `revoked_at` all null. The first registration also drives the program from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, and the program's `version` is bumped by 1. A program may hold at most one registration per network that is still live — that is, in state `SUBMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `EFFECTIVE` — and a second submission for the same network while one of those is outstanding is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_REGISTRATION`. A registration that ended in `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` does not block a fresh submission for that network.

A `NetworkAuditor` then rules on the registration by supplying a `decision` of `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `REJECT`, or `REVOKE`, optionally with a `decision_note`. Every decision records the auditor's `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`, stores the supplied note as `decision_note`, and bumps the registration's `version` by 1. `ACKNOWLEDGE` moves `SUBMITTED → ACKNOWLEDGED` and does nothing else. `REJECT` moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, and its side-effect set diverges deliberately from the acknowledge arm: the owning program stays exactly where it is, in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, and not one program field changes — the program simply can never reach `ACTIVE` through this particular registration and must obtain another. `REVOKE` moves `EFFECTIVE → REVOKED`, stamps `revoked_at` from the logical clock, and carries a cross-entity consequence described below. A decision requested from the wrong state — acknowledging a registration that is not `SUBMITTED`, or revoking one that is not `EFFECTIVE` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. `REJECTED` and `REVOKED` are both terminal.

Between acknowledgement and effectiveness sits the notice period, and it is enforced at the moment of effectuation rather than at submission. A `ComplianceOfficer` effectuates an acknowledged registration, moving `ACKNOWLEDGED → EFFECTIVE` and stamping `effective_at` from the logical clock, but only if the clock has reached `earliest_effective_at`. The bound is inclusive: the clock being exactly at `earliest_effective_at` passes, and the clock being strictly before it is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Consider a registration submitted at the clock's startup default of `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`: its `earliest_effective_at` is `2026-08-31T00:00:00Z`, effectuating with the clock at `2026-08-30T23:59:59Z` fails, and effectuating with the clock at exactly `2026-08-31T00:00:00Z` succeeds. Each registration's window is its own — sibling registrations of the same program impose no notice constraint on one another, so a program can hold one effective registration and one still inside its notice period at the same time. Effectuation bumps the registration's `version` by 1. Registrations may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `network`, and `state`.

Revocation is where the network's authority reaches into an operating program. When a `NetworkAuditor` revokes a registration that was `EFFECTIVE`, and the owning program's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the program moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `disable_reason` set to `REGISTRATION_REVOKED`; every `Terminal` of that program in state `ENFORCING` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `PROGRAM_SUSPENDED`; and each of those terminal transitions recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` on the terminal's merchant. The program's `version` and each affected terminal's `version` are bumped. If the program was not `ACTIVE` at the time of revocation, only the registration changes.

### 3.6. Disclosure Artifacts and Program Readiness

Disclosure is the merchant's own obligation, so the merchant files the evidence. A `MerchantOperator` drafts a disclosure artifact against one of its own programs by naming the `artifact_type` — one of `ENTRY_SIGNAGE`, `POS_SIGNAGE`, `RECEIPT_TEMPLATE`, or `PRICE_LIST_POSTING` — and the `content_hash` of the filed signage or template, which must be exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. The artifact is created in state `DRAFT` with `effective_from`, `expires_at`, `attested_at`, and `attested_by_user_id` all null, and with `expired_reason` of `NONE`. Because the operator is entity-backed, the program named must belong to the acting merchant, or the request fails with `ERR_MERCHANT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then attests the draft for a definite window, supplying `effective_from` and `expires_at`, where `expires_at` must be strictly after `effective_from`. Attestation moves the artifact `DRAFT → ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_at` from the logical clock, and records the attesting officer's `X-User-ID` as `attested_by_user_id` — the value that the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10 will later compare against whoever tries to activate the program. Attesting an artifact that is not in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ARTIFACT_STATE`.

An attested window is **half-open**: it runs from `effective_from` inclusive up to `expires_at` exclusive. Two attested artifacts of the same program and the same `artifact_type` may not have intersecting windows, and an attestation whose requested window intersects an existing `ATTESTED` artifact's window is rejected with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the windows are half-open, exact adjacency is accepted: a new window beginning at the very instant an existing window ends does not intersect it and is admitted. This is the opposite convention from the rate-rule buffer of §3.4, where adjacency is refused — signage may be replaced seamlessly, prices may not.

Attestation recomputes the owning program's `disclosure_ready` flag, bumps that program's `version` by 1, and bumps the artifact's own `version` by 1.

**Readiness** is a set-containment test evaluated against the logical clock. First the server builds the *required* set from the program's `program_type` and its jurisdiction's flags. For a `SURCHARGE` program: if the jurisdiction's `disclosure_signage_required` is true, both `ENTRY_SIGNAGE` and `POS_SIGNAGE` are required; if its `receipt_line_item_required` is true, `RECEIPT_TEMPLATE` is required as well. For a `CASH_DISCOUNT` program: if `disclosure_signage_required` is true, only `POS_SIGNAGE` is required. `PRICE_LIST_POSTING` is deliberately never a member of the required set, because the posted dual-price list is policed by its own independent rule at step 33 of §2.9; a program that has attested its signage but not its price list must fail with `ERR_PRICE_POSTING_REQUIRED` rather than be masked by `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`.

Then the server builds the *satisfied* set: the `artifact_type` values of this program's artifacts that are in state `ATTESTED` and whose half-open window contains the current logical clock — that is, whose `effective_from` is at or before the clock and whose `expires_at` is strictly after the clock. Artifacts in `DRAFT` and artifacts in `EXPIRED` never satisfy a requirement, and neither does an `ATTESTED` artifact whose window the clock has left. `disclosure_ready` is true exactly when every member of the required set appears in the satisfied set. An empty required set — the case of a jurisdiction with every disclosure flag false — yields true vacuously. A program with no attested artifacts at all and a non-empty required set yields false, and activation in that condition fails with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`.

Readiness is recomputed at four moments: when an artifact is attested, whenever an artifact is forced to expire by a cascade, at program activation, and when a suspended program is resumed. It is not recomputed merely because the clock moved, but because it is evaluated against the clock, a lapsed window shows up the next time any of those four moments occurs — and, critically, terminal enforcement consults the stored flag, which is why the remediation cascade of §3.10 explicitly recomputes it before quarantining terminals.

Attested artifacts can be forced out of effect by two cascades. When a program is driven from `ACTIVE` into `REMEDIATION`, every artifact of that program in state `ATTESTED` moves to `EXPIRED` with `expired_reason` set to `REMEDIATION_ORDER`. When a program is withdrawn, every `ATTESTED` artifact moves to `EXPIRED` with `expired_reason` set to `PROGRAM_WITHDRAWN`. In both cases the program's `disclosure_ready` is recomputed and each expired artifact's `version` is bumped by 1. `EXPIRED` is terminal — an expired artifact is never revived, and recovery means drafting and attesting a fresh one. Artifacts may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `artifact_type`, and `state`.

### 3.7. The Program Lifecycle and Its Cascades

A program's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REMEDIATION`, `DISABLED`, and the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, and its `disable_reason` always explains why it is not `ACTIVE`: `NONE` while nothing is wrong, `JURISDICTION_PROHIBITION` after a law change, `REGISTRATION_REVOKED` after a network revocation, `REMEDIATION_ORDER` after a substantiated complaint, and `OPERATOR_SUSPENSION` after a deliberate suspension. Every transition back into `ACTIVE` resets `disable_reason` to `NONE`.

Activation is a fork, and the two arms are genuinely different specifications rather than variations on one. A **cash-discount program activates straight out of `DRAFT`**, moving `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. Its preconditions are: the jurisdiction's `cash_discount_permitted` is true, else `ERR_CASH_DISCOUNT_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`; the activating officer's `X-User-ID` differs from `attested_by_user_id` of every disclosure artifact of the program, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the pinned `CostDocumentation` is still in state `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`; readiness holds for the cash-discount required set, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`; when the jurisdiction's `price_posting_required` is true, a `PRICE_LIST_POSTING` artifact is `ATTESTED` with the clock inside its window, else `ERR_PRICE_POSTING_REQUIRED`; and at least one rate rule of the program has a closed effectivity interval containing the logical clock, else `ERR_NO_RATE_RULE_IN_EFFECT`. No registration is consulted on this arm and no brand-coverage check runs.

A **surcharge program activates out of `PENDING_REGISTRATION`**, moving `PENDING_REGISTRATION → ACTIVE`. Its preconditions are: the jurisdiction's `surcharge_permitted` is true, else `ERR_SURCHARGE_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`; the activating officer's `X-User-ID` differs from `attested_by_user_id` of every disclosure artifact of the program, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the pinned `CostDocumentation` is still in state `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_COST_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`; at least one registration of the program is in state `EFFECTIVE`, else `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_EFFECTIVE`; every network that holds an `EFFECTIVE` registration has a rate rule for that same brand whose closed interval contains the logical clock, else `ERR_BRAND_CONSISTENCY_VIOLATION`; readiness holds for the surcharge required set, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`; and at least one rate rule of the program is in effect at the clock, else `ERR_NO_RATE_RULE_IN_EFFECT`. Registration effectiveness and brand coverage are both required here and both absent from the cash-discount arm.

Both arms share the same side effects. `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `activated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`; `disable_reason` is set to `NONE`; `effective_cap_pct` is recomputed through the arm of the cap calculation matching the program's `program_type`; `disclosure_ready` is recomputed; and the program's `version` is bumped by 1. Requesting activation from a state that declares no activation transition — an already-`ACTIVE` program, a `SUSPENDED` program, a `SURCHARGE` program still in `DRAFT`, or a `WITHDRAWN` program — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`.

Three lifecycle actions are driven explicitly through a single action-bearing operation that accepts `action` and an optional `reason`. **`SUSPEND`**, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `NetworkAuditor`, requires `status` of `ACTIVE` and moves the program `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `disable_reason` set to `OPERATOR_SUSPENSION`. Its cascade runs in two steps: first, every `Terminal` of the program in state `ENFORCING` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `PROGRAM_SUSPENDED`; second, each such terminal transition recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` on that terminal's merchant. The program's `version` is bumped by 1.

**`RESUME`**, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` only, requires `status` of `SUSPENDED` and moves the program `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Its preconditions are: the jurisdiction still permits the program's structure — `surcharge_permitted` true for a `SURCHARGE` program, `cash_discount_permitted` true for a `CASH_DISCOUNT` program, failing with `ERR_SURCHARGE_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION` and `ERR_CASH_DISCOUNT_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION` respectively; for a `SURCHARGE` program, no registration of the program is in state `REVOKED`, else `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_EFFECTIVE`; and readiness holds, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`. On success `disable_reason` is set to `NONE`, `activated_at` and `activated_by_user_id` are re-stamped from the logical clock and the acting `X-User-ID`, `effective_cap_pct` and `disclosure_ready` are recomputed, and the program's `version` is bumped by 1. Resuming does **not** un-quarantine anything: terminals that were quarantined by the suspension stay quarantined until their operator restores them individually, which is what closes the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle at the program level and leaves the terminal cycle of §3.8 to be closed separately.

**`WITHDRAW`**, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` only, is permitted from any `status` other than `WITHDRAWN` and is irreversible. Its side effects are: every disclosure artifact of the program in state `ATTESTED` moves to `EXPIRED` with `expired_reason` set to `PROGRAM_WITHDRAWN`; every registration of the program in state `SUBMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `EFFECTIVE` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock; every `Terminal` of the program whose `state` is not already `DISABLED` moves to `DISABLED`; `enforcing_terminal_count` is recomputed for the program's merchant; and the program's `version` is bumped by 1. Because `DISABLED` is terminal for a terminal, withdrawal permanently shrinks that merchant's terminal census.

Two further program transitions are driven by the enforcement machinery rather than by an officer: `ACTIVE → REMEDIATION` when a complaint is substantiated past the exposure threshold, and `REMEDIATION → ACTIVE` when the last blocking case closes and readiness has been rebuilt. Both are specified in full in §3.10, at the point where they originate.

### 3.8. Terminals: Configuration, Enforcement, and the Quarantine Cycle

The terminal is where compliance either happens or does not, so its state is tracked separately from the program's. A `MerchantOperator` provisions a terminal under its own merchant by supplying only the manufacturer's `serial_number`, between 1 and 40 characters. Serial numbers are unique per merchant: a second terminal with the same `serial_number` under the same merchant is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERMINAL_SERIAL`, while two different merchants may legitimately hold the same serial. A provisioned terminal begins in state `PROVISIONED` with `program_id` null, `debit_exclusion_enabled` false, `bin_table_version` null, `quarantine_reason` of `NONE`, and `last_configured_at` null. It knows nothing yet and can do nothing yet.

Configuration is what binds a terminal to a program and loads its card-recognition tables. The operator supplies the `program_id` the terminal will enforce, the `debit_exclusion_enabled` flag asserting that the terminal's BIN table excludes debit and prepaid ranges from surcharging, and the `bin_table_version` tag identifying the table that was loaded. Configuration is permitted from `PROVISIONED`, from `CONFIGURED` (a re-configuration), and from `QUARANTINED` (a repair), and in all three cases the terminal lands in `CONFIGURED`; attempting it on a `DISABLED` terminal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINAL_STATE`. The named program must belong to the same merchant as the terminal, or the request is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`, and that program must not be `WITHDRAWN`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`. On success the server stores `program_id`, `debit_exclusion_enabled`, and `bin_table_version` from the request, stamps `last_configured_at` from the logical clock, resets `quarantine_reason` to `NONE`, recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` on the merchant if the terminal was leaving `ENFORCING`, and bumps the terminal's `version` by 1. Because a quarantined terminal may be re-configured back to `CONFIGURED`, the loop `CONFIGURED → ENFORCING → QUARANTINED → CONFIGURED` is a supported repair path and not a dead end.

Three further terminal actions are driven through a single action-bearing operation that accepts `action` and an optional `note`.

**`ENFORCE`**, available to a `MerchantOperator` only, requires the terminal to be in `CONFIGURED` and moves it to `ENFORCING`. The operator must own the terminal. Three cross-entity conditions are checked against the bound program: its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`; its `disclosure_ready` must be true, else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`; and if its `program_type` is `SURCHARGE`, the terminal's `debit_exclusion_enabled` must be true, else `ERR_DEBIT_EXCLUSION_NOT_ENABLED`. A cash-discount terminal is exempt from the debit-exclusion requirement, because a cash discount is never applied as a card fee in the first place. On success `quarantine_reason` is reset to `NONE`, `enforcing_terminal_count` is recomputed for the merchant, and the terminal's `version` is bumped by 1.

**`QUARANTINE`**, available to a `MerchantOperator` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, requires the terminal to be in `ENFORCING` and moves it to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `OPERATOR_ACTION`. It recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` for the merchant and bumps the terminal's `version` by 1. The same `ENFORCING → QUARANTINED` transition is also reached by three system cascades, which differ only in the reason they record: `PROGRAM_SUSPENDED` when the bound program leaves `ACTIVE` for `SUSPENDED`, `PROGRAM_DISABLED` when a jurisdictional prohibition disables the program, and `DISCLOSURE_EXPIRED` when a remediation order strips the program's attested disclosures.

**`RESTORE`**, available to a `MerchantOperator` only, requires the terminal to be in `QUARANTINED` and is the one place in this service where the same request from the same state lands in two different target states depending on the world around it. Both branches require the operator to own the terminal, both reset `quarantine_reason` to `NONE`, and both bump the terminal's `version` by 1.

- **Branch A — restore to enforcement.** When the bound program's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `disclosure_ready` is true, the terminal moves `QUARANTINED → ENFORCING`. If the program's `program_type` is `SURCHARGE`, the terminal's `debit_exclusion_enabled` must be true, else the restore is rejected with `ERR_DEBIT_EXCLUSION_NOT_ENABLED`. Because the terminal reaches `ENFORCING`, `enforcing_terminal_count` is recomputed for the merchant, which is precisely how a quarantine cascade is compensated and the census returned to its pre-cascade value.
- **Branch B — restore to configuration.** When the bound program's `status` is not `ACTIVE`, or its `disclosure_ready` is false, the terminal moves `QUARANTINED → CONFIGURED` instead. This branch additionally requires that the program's `status` is neither `DISABLED` nor `WITHDRAWN`, else the restore is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE` — a terminal bound to a prohibited or withdrawn program cannot be brought back at all. Because the terminal does not reach `ENFORCING`, `enforcing_terminal_count` is unchanged by this branch.

Any of the three actions requested from a state that does not declare it — enforcing a terminal that is not `CONFIGURED`, quarantining one that is not `ENFORCING`, restoring one that is not `QUARANTINED`, or any action at all on a `DISABLED` terminal — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINAL_STATE`.

A merchant's `enforcing_terminal_count` is always the number of that merchant's terminals whose `state` is exactly `ENFORCING`; terminals in `PROVISIONED`, `CONFIGURED`, `QUARANTINED`, and `DISABLED` are all excluded, and the count is a non-negative integer recomputed on every transition into or out of `ENFORCING`, including the quarantine cascades and configuration. Terminals may be listed filtered by `merchant_id`, `program_id`, and `state`.

### 3.9. Capturing a Sale: Rate Selection and Dual-Variant Pricing

A sale is booked by the terminal itself, acting as a `TerminalAgent`. The capture body carries the `terminal_id` the agent is authenticating as, the `program_id` the terminal believes it is enforcing, the `card_brand` presented, the `card_funding` resolved from the BIN table — one of `CREDIT`, `DEBIT`, or `PREPAID` — the `product_tier` resolved from the BIN table, the `base_amount_cents` posted price of the goods (at least 1 and at most 100000000), the `requested_surcharge_cents` the terminal computed locally (at least 0 and at most 100000000), and the `receipt_line_present` flag stating whether the printed receipt actually carried a separate surcharge line. `product_tier` is one of `CONSUMER_CREDIT`, `COMMERCIAL_CREDIT`, `REWARDS_CREDIT`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and it must be exactly `NOT_APPLICABLE` when `card_funding` is anything other than `CREDIT` and must be one of the three credit tiers when `card_funding` is `CREDIT`; violating that correspondence is a request-shape defect.

Before pricing begins, the capture must clear four gates. The capturing terminal must be in state `ENFORCING`; a sale from a `PROVISIONED`, `CONFIGURED`, `QUARANTINED`, or `DISABLED` terminal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERMINAL_STATE`. The terminal's stored `program_id` must equal the body's `program_id`, else `ERR_TERMINAL_PROGRAM_MISMATCH` — a terminal cannot book against a program it is not configured for, even if that program is perfectly healthy. The program must not be in status `DISABLED` or `WITHDRAWN`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_DISABLED_BY_PROHIBITION`. And for a `SURCHARGE` program, the governing jurisdiction's `surcharge_permitted` must still be true, else `ERR_SURCHARGE_PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`.

The credit-only rule then applies to what the terminal claims. If `card_funding` is `DEBIT` or `PREPAID`, `requested_surcharge_cents` must be 0; a positive requested surcharge on debit or prepaid funding is rejected with `ERR_DEBIT_SURCHARGE_FORBIDDEN`. This is the terminal-level enforcement of debit exclusion, and it is checked before rate selection so that a debit sale is never told that no rate rule exists.

**Rate selection.** The server then chooses which rate applies, and it begins with the credit-only guard: on a `SURCHARGE` program whose sale carries `card_funding` other than `CREDIT`, no rule is selected at all, `applied_rate_pct` is 0, and the absence of a rule is explicitly *not* an error. Otherwise — for a `SURCHARGE` program with `CREDIT` funding, and for every `CASH_DISCOUNT` capture regardless of funding — candidate rules are gathered from the program's rate table: rules whose `program_id` is the capture's program, whose `brand` equals the capture's `card_brand`, whose closed effectivity interval contains the logical clock (that is, `effective_from` at or before the clock and `effective_to` at or after the clock, both ends inclusive), and whose `product_tier` either equals the capture's `product_tier` or is `ALL`. Rules of another program, another brand, or a non-containing interval are excluded. If no candidate survives, the capture is rejected with `ERR_NO_RATE_RULE_IN_EFFECT`.

Among the candidates, a rule whose `product_tier` exactly equals the capture's tier ranks strictly above a rule whose `product_tier` is `ALL`, so a specific tier rate always beats a catch-all. Among candidates of equal rank, the rule with the latest `effective_from` wins; if two such rules share the same `effective_from`, the one with the earliest `created_at` wins. `created_at` is the only tie-break beyond `effective_from`, and a rule's `id` is never consulted. The winner's `rate_pct` becomes the capture's `applied_rate_pct`, carried at four decimal places with no rounding at selection time.

**Pricing.** With the rate in hand, the server resolves the money in exactly one of three arms, and the arm chosen is recorded as the capture's `outcome`.

- On a `SURCHARGE` program with `card_funding` of `CREDIT`: `surcharge_amount_cents` is `base_amount_cents` multiplied by the applied rate, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent; `cash_discount_amount_cents` is 0; `cash_price_cents` equals `base_amount_cents`; `total_amount_cents` is `base_amount_cents` plus `surcharge_amount_cents`; and `outcome` is `APPROVED_WITH_SURCHARGE`. The cardholder pays more than the posted price, and the merchant's collected surcharge grows.
- On a `SURCHARGE` program with `card_funding` of `DEBIT` or `PREPAID`: `surcharge_amount_cents` is 0, `cash_discount_amount_cents` is 0, `cash_price_cents` equals `base_amount_cents`, `total_amount_cents` equals `base_amount_cents`, and `outcome` is `APPROVED_NO_SURCHARGE`. The sale is accepted at the posted price, with nothing added.
- On a `CASH_DISCOUNT` program, whatever the funding: `surcharge_amount_cents` is 0; `cash_discount_amount_cents` is `base_amount_cents` multiplied by the applied rate, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent; `cash_price_cents` is `base_amount_cents` minus `cash_discount_amount_cents`; `total_amount_cents` equals `base_amount_cents`, because under a cash-discount structure the card price *is* the posted price; and `outcome` is `APPROVED_CASH_DISCOUNT`. The merchant's collected surcharge does not move at all.

The two structures diverge visibly on identical inputs, which is the whole point of the distinction. A posted price of 10000 cents priced at 2.19 percent yields, under a surcharge program, a `surcharge_amount_cents` of 219 and a `total_amount_cents` of 10219 with `cash_price_cents` of 10000; under a cash-discount program the same inputs yield a `cash_discount_amount_cents` of 219, a `cash_price_cents` of 9781, and a `total_amount_cents` of 10000. Rounding to the cent happens exactly once per capture line, and no later aggregate re-rounds it.

Two fiscal checks close out the capture. If the jurisdiction's `receipt_line_item_required` is true and the computed `surcharge_amount_cents` is greater than 0, then `receipt_line_present` must be true; a surcharged sale whose receipt carried no separate line is rejected with `ERR_RECEIPT_LINE_ITEM_REQUIRED`. And the terminal's own arithmetic must agree with the server's: `requested_surcharge_cents` must equal the computed `surcharge_amount_cents`, and any disagreement is rejected with `ERR_SURCHARGE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` — a terminal running a stale rate table is a compliance incident, not a rounding nuisance.

On success the sale is persisted with `occurred_at` stamped from the logical clock and `refunded_surcharge_cents` at 0, and the merchant's surcharge ledger moves. The merchant's `gross_surcharge_collected_cents` is the sum of `surcharge_amount_cents` over every sale captured by that merchant's terminals whose `outcome` is `APPROVED_WITH_SURCHARGE`, taken over all time with no window; it increases by this sale's surcharge and never decreases for any reason. The merchant's `net_surcharge_collected_cents` is that gross figure less the total of the merchant's refunds in state `ISSUED`, so it increases by the same amount here. Both are integer cents with no rounding, since every input is already an integer. Capturing a sale bumps the merchant's `version` by 1. Sales may be listed filtered by `terminal_id`, `program_id`, and `outcome`.

### 3.10. Complaints, Exposure Scoring, and the Remediation Order

Enforcement arrives as a complaint. A `NetworkAuditor` files cases whose `source` is `NETWORK` or `CARDHOLDER`; a `StateRegulator` files cases whose `source` is `ATTORNEY_GENERAL`. The filing body names the `program_id` the complaint targets, the `source`, the `allegation_type` — one of `UNDISCLOSED_SURCHARGE`, `DEBIT_SURCHARGED`, `CAP_EXCEEDED`, `MISSING_RECEIPT_LINE`, `PROHIBITED_JURISDICTION`, or `PRICE_POSTING_MISSING` — and the `narrative` as filed, between 1 and 2000 characters. The server stamps `received_at` from the logical clock, records the filer's `X-User-ID` as `filed_by_user_id`, and opens the case with `investigation_state` of `OPEN` and `remediation_state` of `NONE`. A newly filed case carries `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `plan_summary` null, and `refund_total_cents` 0.

The response deadline depends on who is asking. `response_due_at` is `received_at` plus the window for the case's `source`: 10 days for a `NETWORK` complaint, 20 days for an `ATTORNEY_GENERAL` complaint, and 30 days for a `CARDHOLDER` complaint, with day arithmetic in exact 24-hour multiples and the result rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Filing also recomputes the target program's `ag_exposure_score`, because a merely open attorney-general-style exposure already counts under that method.

**Two independent dimensions.** A complaint case carries two state dimensions on the same row. `investigation_state` moves through `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, and `DISMISSED` and is driven only by investigation actions. `remediation_state` moves through `NONE`, `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_SUBMITTED`, `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, and `CLOSED` and is driven only by remediation actions. The two dimensions never read each other except through one coupling: substantiating the investigation sets `remediation_state` to `PLAN_REQUIRED`. A case whose investigation ends in `DISMISSED` keeps `remediation_state` at `NONE` forever, and no remediation action ever becomes available on it. A case is finished either at `DISMISSED`, or at `SUBSTANTIATED` with `remediation_state` of `CLOSED`.

Three investigation actions are driven through a single action-bearing operation accepting `action` and an optional `note`. **`REVIEW`** is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` only, requires `investigation_state` of `OPEN`, and moves it to `UNDER_REVIEW`; it records the reviewer's `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`, leaves `remediation_state` untouched at `NONE`, recomputes the program's `ag_exposure_score` (an `UNDER_REVIEW` case still scores as unresolved), and bumps the case's `version` by 1. **`SUBSTANTIATE`** is available to a `NetworkAuditor` or a `StateRegulator` whose channel matches the case's `source`, requires `investigation_state` of `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, and moves it to `SUBSTANTIATED`; it sets `remediation_state` to `PLAN_REQUIRED`, recomputes both `network_exposure_score` and `ag_exposure_score`, may trigger the remediation cascade below, and bumps the case's `version` by 1. **`DISMISS`** is available to the same two roles under the same channel rule, requires `investigation_state` of `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, and moves it to `DISMISSED`; `remediation_state` stays `NONE`, both exposure scores are recomputed (a dismissed case contributes zero to both), and the case's `version` is bumped by 1. An investigation action requested from a terminal `investigation_state` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`.

**Exposure scoring.** Each program carries two exposure figures computed over that program's complaint cases by two deliberately different methods, both rounded half up to two decimal places, both then clamped to a ceiling of 100 applied after the rounding, and both ignoring cases belonging to other programs.

The **network method**, persisted as `network_exposure_score`, counts *only* cases whose `investigation_state` is `SUBSTANTIATED`. Each such case contributes a base of 20 points multiplied by the weight of its filing `source`: 1.5 for `NETWORK`, 2.0 for `ATTORNEY_GENERAL`, and 1.0 for `CARDHOLDER`. Those contributions are summed, the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the result is capped at 100. The jurisdiction's enforcement posture plays no part. One substantiated `NETWORK` case therefore scores 20 multiplied by 1.5, which is 30.00, while one substantiated `CARDHOLDER` case scores 20 multiplied by 1.0, which is 20.00.

The **attorney-general method**, persisted as `ag_exposure_score`, counts a wider set and ignores the source entirely. Every case whose `investigation_state` is `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `SUBSTANTIATED` contributes: 20 points if it is `SUBSTANTIATED`, and 8 points if it is merely `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`. That sum is then multiplied by the posture weight of the program's jurisdiction — 0.5 for `PERMISSIVE`, 1.0 for `MONITORED`, and 2.0 for `RESTRICTIVE` — the product is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the result is capped at 100. Dismissed cases contribute nothing here either. The two methods genuinely disagree: a program with one substantiated `NETWORK` case and one still-open `ATTORNEY_GENERAL` case, sitting in a `MONITORED` state, scores 30.00 under the network method and 1.0 multiplied by the sum of 20 and 8, which is 28.00, under the attorney-general method.

**The remediation cascade.** The network method is what gates enforcement, and the trigger threshold is 30 points. When a substantiation leaves the recomputed `network_exposure_score` at or above 30 and the program's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the following runs in order within the same request. Step one: the program moves `ACTIVE → REMEDIATION` with `disable_reason` set to `REMEDIATION_ORDER`. Step two: every disclosure artifact of that program in state `ATTESTED` moves to `EXPIRED` with `expired_reason` set to `REMEDIATION_ORDER`. Step three: because every attested artifact is now gone, the program's `disclosure_ready` is recomputed and becomes false, and consequently every `Terminal` of the program in state `ENFORCING` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to `DISCLOSURE_EXPIRED`. Step four: each of those terminal quarantines recomputes `enforcing_terminal_count` on the terminal's merchant. The program's `version` is bumped by 1, as is each expired artifact's and each quarantined terminal's. A single substantiated network complaint can therefore stop a merchant's entire estate of terminals, which is the intended serialisation point between enforcement and capture. If the recomputed score stays below 30, or the program was not `ACTIVE`, only the case's own fields move.

**The remediation track.** Three remediation actions are driven through a second action-bearing operation accepting `action` and an optional `plan_summary`. **`SUBMIT_PLAN`** is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` only, requires `remediation_state` of `PLAN_REQUIRED`, and moves it to `PLAN_SUBMITTED`; `plan_summary` must be present in the request, else the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the supplied text is stored on the case. `investigation_state` is untouched and stays `SUBSTANTIATED`. **`ACCEPT_PLAN`** is available to a `NetworkAuditor` or `StateRegulator` matching the case's `source`, requires `remediation_state` of `PLAN_SUBMITTED`, and moves it to `PLAN_ACCEPTED`; acceptance is what unlocks refund creation on the case. **`CLOSE`** is available to the same two roles under the same channel rule and requires `remediation_state` of `PLAN_ACCEPTED`; it additionally requires that no refund of the case is still in state `PENDING`, else the close is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_REFUNDS_PENDING`. Closing moves `remediation_state` to `CLOSED` and recomputes the case's `refund_total_cents`. Each of the three actions bumps the case's `version` by 1, and a remediation action requested from the wrong `remediation_state` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`.

**The bubble-up.** Closing a case is also how a program escapes remediation. When a case reaches `remediation_state` of `CLOSED`, and the owning program's `status` is `REMEDIATION`, and no other case of that program has `investigation_state` of `SUBSTANTIATED` with a `remediation_state` other than `CLOSED`, and readiness now yields true again because a fresh set of disclosure artifacts has been attested, then the program moves `REMEDIATION → ACTIVE` with `disable_reason` reset to `NONE`, its `disclosure_ready` is recomputed, and its `version` is bumped by 1. If any of those conditions fails — another substantiated case is still open on its remediation track, or the disclosures have not been rebuilt — the case still closes but the program stays in `REMEDIATION`. As with a resume, the program returning to `ACTIVE` does not un-quarantine any terminal; each must be restored individually. Complaint cases may be listed filtered by `program_id`, `source`, `investigation_state`, and `remediation_state`.

### 3.11. Remediation Refunds, Ledger Compensation, and the Reporting Surfaces

An accepted remediation plan usually means giving surcharges back. Under a case whose `remediation_state` is `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, a `ComplianceOfficer` creates a refund by naming the `transaction_id` whose surcharge is being returned and the `refund_amount_cents` to return. Creating a refund before the plan is accepted, or after the case has closed, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`. The named sale must have been captured by a terminal of the merchant that owns the case's program; a refund reaching across merchants is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_TRANSACTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The amount must be at least 1 cent and at most the sale's `surcharge_amount_cents` less its current `refunded_surcharge_cents` — the unrefunded remainder — and an amount beyond that remainder is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_SURCHARGE`. At most one refund that is not `VOIDED` may exist for a given pairing of case and sale; a second live refund for the same pairing is rejected with `ERR_REFUND_DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION`, while a previously voided one does not block a fresh attempt.

A created refund begins in state `PENDING`, with the creating officer's `X-User-ID` recorded as `created_by_user_id` and with `issued_by_user_id`, `issued_at`, and `voided_at` all null. Creation moves no ledger field whatsoever: a pending refund is an intention, not a payment.

Issuance is the money movement, and it requires a second pair of hands. A `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the refund's `created_by_user_id` issues the refund, moving it `PENDING → ISSUED`; equality is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The owning case's `remediation_state` must still be `PLAN_ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`, and issuing a refund that is not `PENDING` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REFUND_STATE`. On success the server stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock and `issued_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, decreases the merchant's `net_surcharge_collected_cents` by `refund_amount_cents` while leaving `gross_surcharge_collected_cents` completely unchanged, increases the sale's `refunded_surcharge_cents` by the same amount, recomputes the case's `refund_total_cents`, and bumps both the merchant's `version` and the refund's `version` by 1.

Voiding is the compensating action, and it has two arms that differ in exactly what they undo. Voiding a refund in state `ISSUED` moves it `ISSUED → VOIDED`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, increases the merchant's `net_surcharge_collected_cents` by `refund_amount_cents` — restoring it precisely to its pre-issuance value — decreases the sale's `refunded_surcharge_cents` by the same amount, recomputes the case's `refund_total_cents`, and bumps both the merchant's `version` and the refund's `version` by 1. Voiding an issued refund additionally requires that the owning case's `remediation_state` is not `CLOSED`, else it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`. Voiding a refund in state `PENDING` also moves it to `VOIDED` and stamps `voided_at`, but no ledger field moves at all, because the refund was never issued; only the refund's own `version` is bumped. Voiding may be done by any `ComplianceOfficer`, including the one who created the refund, and voiding an already-`VOIDED` refund is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REFUND_STATE`. A case's `refund_total_cents` is always the sum of `refund_amount_cents` over that case's refunds in state `ISSUED` — pending and voided refunds are excluded — and a sale's `refunded_surcharge_cents` is likewise the sum over the `ISSUED` refunds referencing it. Refunds may be listed filtered by `complaint_case_id`, `transaction_id`, and `state`.

**The exposure summary.** A computed read exposes both exposure methods side by side for one program, so that an officer can see why the network method has or has not tripped. It reports `program_id`, the persisted `network_exposure_score` and `ag_exposure_score`, `substantiated_case_count` as the number of that program's cases whose `investigation_state` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, `unresolved_case_count` as the number whose `investigation_state` is `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, `posture_multiplier` as the weight implied by the jurisdiction's `ag_enforcement_posture`, and `remediation_threshold` as the network-method trigger of 30.

**The settlement summary.** A second computed read reconciles a program's collected surcharges over an optional window, bounded by the `from_iso8601` and `to_iso8601` query parameters. A sale is in scope when its `occurred_at` is at or after `from_iso8601` — an inclusive lower bound — and strictly before `to_iso8601` — an exclusive upper bound; an omitted bound imposes no constraint on that side. Only sales of the named program participate.

The summary reports `program_id`; `transaction_count` as the number of in-scope sales, counting all three outcome arms; `base_amount_total_cents` as the sum of `base_amount_cents` over all in-scope sales, again across all three arms; and `per_line_rounded_surcharge_cents` as the sum of the `surcharge_amount_cents` already stored on those sales. That last figure is the settled number, and the rounding order behind it is pinned: each line was rounded to the cent when it was captured, and the lines are then summed — the summary never re-rounds and never pools first.

To make the divergence between the two possible orderings observable, the summary also publishes `pooled_rounding_reference_cents`. That reference takes the in-scope sales whose `outcome` is `APPROVED_WITH_SURCHARGE`, groups them by the `applied_rate_pct` they carry, and for each group multiplies the group's total `base_amount_cents` by that group's rate, divides by 100, rounds half up to a whole cent, and finally sums the per-group results. `rounding_divergence_cents` is then `per_line_rounded_surcharge_cents` minus `pooled_rounding_reference_cents`. Consider three surcharged sales of 1234, 5678, and 9012 cents all priced at 2.19 percent: the per-line surcharges are 27, 124, and 197, summing to 348, whereas pooling the bases to 15924 and applying the rate once gives 349. The settled figure is 348 and `rounding_divergence_cents` is -1. The pooled reference is published for transparency only and is never the settled amount.

Finally the summary reports `refunded_surcharge_cents` as the sum of `refund_amount_cents` over refunds in state `ISSUED` that reference an in-scope sale — pending and voided refunds are excluded — and `net_surcharge_cents` as `per_line_rounded_surcharge_cents` minus that refunded total.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Status codes follow one rule. An operation that brings a new resource into existence answers 201 Created — registering a jurisdiction, onboarding a merchant, filing cost documentation, creating a program, filing a rate rule, submitting a network registration, drafting a disclosure artifact, provisioning a terminal, capturing a sale, filing a complaint case, and creating a refund. Every other successful operation — every read, every list, every state change, every partial update, and the clock control — answers 200 OK. Error statuses are as catalogued in §5. Operations that create or modify a resource return that resource in `data`; the two computed summaries and the clock control return the computed object described in §3.
