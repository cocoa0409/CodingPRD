# Correspondent Banking Due Diligence API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Correspondent banking is the business of holding an account for another bank, and it is the single largest concentration of money-laundering and sanctions-evasion risk that a financial institutions group carries. When our institution opens a nostro/vostro relationship for a **respondent bank**, we inherit that respondent's customers, its downstream correspondents, its licensing standing and its home jurisdiction's regulatory posture — without ever meeting any of them. The team that owns this exposure today works out of spreadsheets: ownership look-through charts drawn by hand, Wolfsberg questionnaires tracked by email thread, corridor volume thresholds recalculated on request, and refresh deadlines that slip because nobody recomputes them when an adverse-media hit lands. This document specifies the system that replaces that apparatus.

The service is a single JSON/HTTP API mounted under `/correspondent-banking`. It carries the whole respondent lifecycle: onboarding a prospect, verifying its licence and its prudential regulator's standing, mapping its beneficial-ownership tree through up to four levels of look-through, collecting and independently assessing its compliance-programme questionnaire, discovering and adjudicating the nested and payable-through relationships hiding behind it, calibrating transaction-monitoring thresholds against declared corridor activity and corridor risk, triaging adverse media, chasing requests for information under severity-driven service levels, escalating unresponsive respondents, screening individual payment instructions against sanctions posture and hold thresholds, and — when the relationship can no longer be carried — planning and executing an orderly exit with a bounded wind-down and a fully settled obligation.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is **determinism**: every score, threshold, deadline and cascade is a pure function of stored data and a server-held logical clock, evaluated in a pinned order, so two runs over identical data produce byte-identical results. There is no background reconciliation — every cascade completes inside the request that triggered it, and the only "batch" behaviour is an explicit operator-driven sweep. The second is **segregation of duties**: the analyst who scores a respondent may never be the committee member who approves it, the manager who drafts an exit may never be the one who approves it, and the respondent itself may supply and answer but never assess or decide. Those constraints are enforced in code, not in policy documents, and they are the reason several of the rules below are stated as identity comparisons rather than as role checks.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including read endpoints and including the clock-setting capability — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it acts under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the six role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This is the very first check performed on any request, before path resolution and before body parsing.

The six roles are `RespondentContact`, `ComplianceAnalyst`, `SanctionsOfficer`, `FIGRelationshipManager`, `CommitteeApprover` and `PlatformAdmin`. Role names are case-sensitive and are compared literally. Once authenticated, the acting role must appear in the set of roles the addressed endpoint permits; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. The authoritative role set for each endpoint is the "Authorized Role(s)" column of the endpoint table in §4.

A `RespondentContact` is the respondent bank itself, acting on its own file. It completes the due-diligence questionnaire, declares its beneficial owners and its downstream correspondents, answers requests for information and submits payment instructions. When `X-User-Role` is `RespondentContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RespondentBank`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `RespondentBank` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. That comparison follows the chain: directly through a resource's `respondent_bank_id`, or transitively through the questionnaire, monitoring profile, alert or information request that the resource hangs from. A `RespondentContact` that addresses a resource belonging to any other respondent is rejected with `ERR_RESPONDENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. This scope check applies to every endpoint whose permitted roles include `RespondentContact`, reads included.

The remaining five roles are operator roles. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAnalyst`, `SanctionsOfficer`, `FIGRelationshipManager`, `CommitteeApprover` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it in audit fields where a transition says so — `assessed_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `calibrated_by_user_id`, `investigated_by_user_id`, `triaged_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `drafted_by_user_id` — and it compares recorded values to each other when enforcing segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

Role responsibilities divide as follows. A `ComplianceAnalyst` runs due diligence: it is the only role that may score a respondent, verify or reject a beneficial owner, record a questionnaire assessment decision, overlay an independent control rating, open a monitoring profile or calibrate one. A `SanctionsOfficer` owns the sanctions and screening surface and is the only role that may dispose of a payment instruction. A `FIGRelationshipManager` is the commercial owner of the relationship and is the only role that may allocate or reverse a settlement against a closure plan. A `CommitteeApprover` performs no assessment work at all; it is the only role that may record a committee decision on a respondent and the only role that may approve a closure plan — which is precisely what makes the segregation-of-duties comparisons meaningful. A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform: reference data, activity feeds, and exclusively the operator sweep and the logical clock. Beyond those exclusives, several operations are shared: licensing verification is open to `ComplianceAnalyst` and `PlatformAdmin`; restriction and un-restriction to `SanctionsOfficer` and `ComplianceAnalyst`; nested-relationship adjudication, alert disposition, information-request raising, dispatch and adjudication, and adverse-media logging and triage to `ComplianceAnalyst` and `SanctionsOfficer`; jurisdiction-profile writes to `PlatformAdmin` and `SanctionsOfficer`; respondent creation and amendment to `FIGRelationshipManager` and `PlatformAdmin`; observation posting and overdue marking to `ComplianceAnalyst` and `PlatformAdmin`; refresh scheduling and progression to `ComplianceAnalyst` and `PlatformAdmin`; closure drafting, submission and progression to `FIGRelationshipManager` and `ComplianceAnalyst`; payment submission to `FIGRelationshipManager`, `RespondentContact` and `PlatformAdmin`; questionnaire creation, control-assessment rows, beneficial-owner declaration and nested-relationship recording to `ComplianceAnalyst` alongside `RespondentContact`. Every operator role may read every business collection; a `RespondentContact` may read only rows scoped to its own respondent id.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when absent, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when absent and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. Both must parse as integers; a `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` (the effective limit applied, whether supplied or defaulted), `offset` (the effective offset), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the limit/offset window is applied) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 declares another order, every list endpoint returns rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Seven collections declare a different order and it is normative: questionnaires are returned `questionnaire_version` descending then `created_at` ascending; control-assessment rows are returned in the fixed control-domain weight order declared for the control score (`GOVERNANCE`, `KYC_ONBOARDING`, `TRANSACTION_MONITORING`, `SANCTIONS_SCREENING`, `PEP_HANDLING`, `TRAINING`, `AUDIT`, `NESTED_CONTROLS`); a respondent's expanded nested chain is returned `depth` ascending then `created_at` ascending; monitoring profiles are returned `effective_from` ascending then `created_at` ascending; activity observations are returned `period_month` ascending then `created_at` ascending; adverse-media items are returned `event_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; refresh cycles are returned `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending; closure plans are returned `created_at` descending. The operator sweep returns affected respondents in its own sweep order rather than in creation order.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every one of the fourteen persisted resource types — jurisdiction profiles, respondent banks, beneficial owners, questionnaires, control assessments, nested relationships, monitoring profiles, activity observations, monitoring alerts, information requests, adverse-media events, periodic refreshes, payment instructions and closure plans — carries the same four common properties, and all four appear in every response body for that resource.

`id` is a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits, and both are read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time; both are set to the logical-clock instant at which a row is inserted, and `updated_at` is refreshed — again from the logical clock — on every accepted mutation of that row, including mutations a row suffers as the passive target of a cascade. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row — again including cascade-driven mutations. A read never changes `version`, `updated_at` or either timestamp.

The `version` of a `RespondentBank` deserves separate emphasis because it is the concurrency anchor for the whole aggregate (see §2.7). It advances not only when the respondent row's own fields change, but on every accepted write that the specification below describes as touching the respondent: amendment, licensing verification, risk assessment, committee decision, restriction or lift, beneficial-owner verification, questionnaire creation, questionnaire assessment, nested-relationship creation and adjudication, monitoring-profile creation, refresh scheduling and progression, adverse-media triage, closure-plan drafting and progression, an overdue marking or adjudication that moves the strike ledger, an alert disposition that restricts the respondent, and each respondent the operator sweep actually changes. A single request that cascades across several rows increments each touched row's own `version` exactly once, no matter how many hops of the cascade reach it.

Alongside those common properties, many resources carry a dedicated audit stamp that records when a particular decision was taken, and every one of them is written from the logical clock rather than from wall-clock time and is null until that decision occurs: `license_verified_at`, `assessed_at`, `submitted_at`, `triaged_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `decided_at`, `dispatched_at`, `responded_at`, `escalated_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `lapsed_at`, `calibrated_at`, `notice_served_at` and `closed_at`. A refresh cycle additionally freezes `completed_control_score` and `completion_risk_rating` at completion so that the score credited to a cycle cannot drift afterwards. Free-text explanatory fields — `headline`, `evidence_note`, `response_note`, `clarification_note`, `decision_note`, `disposition_reason`, `rejection_reason` and `owner_name` among them — are stored exactly as supplied and are never interpreted by any rule.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Interval Semantics and Month Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every deadline, cadence, service level, effective interval and window is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before it is ever set, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin`-only capability replaces the clock value with a supplied `current_utc` instant; the clock may be moved forward or backward, and setting it performs no state transitions of its own. Nothing becomes overdue, lapses or closes merely because the clock moved — those outcomes still require their own explicit operations.

Two interval conventions coexist deliberately and must not be conflated. A transaction-monitoring profile governs a **closed** interval from `effective_from` to `effective_to` inclusive of both endpoints; consequently a new profile whose `effective_from` equals an existing profile's `effective_to` overlaps that profile, and adjacency is a conflict. A periodic refresh cycle governs a **half-open** window from `window_start` inclusive to `scheduled_due_date` exclusive; consequently a new cycle whose `window_start` equals an existing cycle's `scheduled_due_date` does not overlap it, and adjacency is permitted.

Month arithmetic appears in the refresh cadence. Adding a whole number of months to an instant preserves the day-of-month and the time-of-day; when the target month has fewer days than the source day-of-month, the day is clamped to the target month's last day. Adding zero months returns the instant unchanged. All other temporal arithmetic in this document is expressed in hours or in days-of-24-hours, and is exact.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Unless a rule below states otherwise, every monetary amount and every score is stored and returned with 2 decimal places and every rounding is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Three quantities carry 4 decimal places instead: `control_credit`, an observation's `breach_ratio` and an alert's `breach_ratio`. `domain_weight` is stored with 4 decimals. Integer quantities — counters, cent amounts, hop depths, whole-hour service windows, whole-day notice periods, and ownership chain depths — carry no fractional part at all.

The **point** at which rounding happens is part of the specification and is pinned per calculation, because several calculations in this domain deliberately round at different points over the same shape of data. Two contrasts are worth stating up front. The look-through ownership calculation multiplies the whole ancestor chain at full precision and rounds exactly once at the end. The settlement allocation rounds every individual line before summing them, and then absorbs the entire residue into a single line. Likewise, the adverse-media accumulator rounds each event's contribution before adding it and re-rounds the running total after each addition, whereas the control score sums weighted ratings at full precision and rounds once before applying its caps. Where a rule says "then", the order is normative; computing the same expression in a different order and arriving at a different last cent is a defect, not a rounding preference.

Comparisons carry their own precision. Every threshold comparison in this document is stated with explicit inclusivity, and the stated inclusivity is normative: "at least" and "no less than" mean the boundary value satisfies the test, while "strictly greater than" and "before" mean it does not.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Respondent Record

Two write operations rewrite the basis on which the respondent's own scores were computed, and both therefore require the writer to prove it has read the respondent recently. Both use the same mechanism: a non-optional request field named `expected_respondent_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RespondentBank`.

The first is `POST /correspondent-banking/respondents/{respondentId}/questionnaires`. Creating a questionnaire supersedes the respondent's earlier open questionnaire and rewrites the control-score basis, so a writer working from an outdated view of the respondent — its scores, its `dd_state`, or a questionnaire that has already been superseded — must re-read before creating. If `expected_respondent_version` does not equal the respondent's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESPONDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

The second is `POST /correspondent-banking/respondents/{respondentId}/monitoring-profiles`. A new profile supersedes any overlapping profile and recalibrates every corridor threshold from the respondent's current `risk_rating`, so a writer holding a stale version would silently calibrate against an outdated rating. If `expected_respondent_version` does not equal the respondent's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESPONDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

The stale-version test is evaluated **before** any state precondition on either endpoint, so a client whose view is out of date learns that fact rather than being told the respondent is in the wrong state. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a version mismatch.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Referential Scoping

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost segment that fails to resolve decides the error. A request against a beneficial owner under a respondent that does not exist reports the missing respondent, not the missing owner. The mapping from path variable to error is fixed: `respondentId` yields `ERR_RESPONDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `jurisdictionProfileId` yields `ERR_JURISDICTION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `beneficialOwnerId` yields `ERR_BENEFICIAL_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `questionnaireId` yields `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_FOUND`, `controlAssessmentId` yields `ERR_CONTROL_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `nestedRelationshipId` yields `ERR_NESTED_RELATIONSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `monitoringProfileId` yields `ERR_MONITORING_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `activityObservationId` yields `ERR_ACTIVITY_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `monitoringAlertId` yields `ERR_MONITORING_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, `informationRequestId` yields `ERR_INFORMATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `adverseMediaEventId` yields `ERR_ADVERSE_MEDIA_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `periodicRefreshId` yields `ERR_PERIODIC_REFRESH_NOT_FOUND`, `paymentInstructionId` yields `ERR_PAYMENT_INSTRUCTION_NOT_FOUND` and `closurePlanId` yields `ERR_CLOSURE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. All fourteen carry HTTP 404. The same identifiers supplied in a request body rather than a path resolve to the same errors: an unresolvable body-supplied `parent_owner_id` yields `ERR_BENEFICIAL_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `nested_relationship_id` yields `ERR_NESTED_RELATIONSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `activity_observation_id` yields `ERR_ACTIVITY_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `monitoring_alert_id` yields `ERR_MONITORING_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`.

Jurisdictions are referenced by natural key, never by UUID. Seven body fields carry a jurisdiction code — `home_jurisdiction_code`, `downstream_jurisdiction_code`, `ordering_jurisdiction_code`, `beneficiary_jurisdiction_code`, `corridor_jurisdiction_code`, `country_of_residence` and the `jurisdiction_code` inside each entry of `expected_corridors` — and every one of them must resolve to an existing jurisdiction profile whose `jurisdiction_code` matches exactly. A code that does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_JURISDICTION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404, on any create or amend that carries it.

A body-supplied parent identifier must additionally belong to the aggregate the path addresses. A `parent_owner_id` must belong to the same `respondent_bank_id` as the owner being created; a `monitoring_alert_id` must belong to the respondent the information request is being filed against; a `nested_relationship_id` must belong to the addressed respondent; an `activity_observation_id` must belong to a monitoring profile of that respondent. A resolvable identifier that belongs to a different respondent is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_RESPONDENT_REFERENCE` and HTTP 422 — a distinct outcome from "not found", and one that applies specifically to beneficial-owner creation, information-request creation, payment-instruction submission and officer-raised alert creation.

### 2.9. The Global Validation Precedence Ladder

A single request frequently fails more than one rule at once. When it does, the response carries the error of the **lowest priority number** in the ordered ladder below, and no later check contributes to the response. Implementations must evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient, because several pairs of rules are deliberately reachable together and the benchmark distinguishes them.

1. **Priority 1** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Priority 2** — the acting role appears in the addressed endpoint's role list. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Priority 3** — when the acting role is `RespondentContact`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `RespondentBank` that owns the addressed resource, whether directly or through the resource's `respondent_bank_id`, questionnaire, monitoring profile, alert or request chain. Otherwise `ERR_RESPONDENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403. Applies to every endpoint whose roles include `RespondentContact`.
4. **Priority 4** — every path identifier resolves, evaluated strictly left to right per §2.8, each with its own 404 error id. Applies to every endpoint that has path parameters.
5. **Priority 5** — every field the endpoint declares as required is present and has the declared JSON type; unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_BODY`, 400. Applies to every POST and PATCH endpoint.
6. **Priority 6** — every enum-typed field carries a value from its declared set, including the action discriminators (`VERIFY`/`REJECT`/`RESET`, `ASSESS`/`RETURN`, `CONFIRM`/`PROHIBIT`/`SUSPEND`/`REVERT`/`TERMINATE`, `ALLOCATE`/`REVERSE`, and every other `action` value named in §3). Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, 400. Applies to every POST and PATCH endpoint.
7. **Priority 7** — numeric fields sit inside their declared domains: `ownership_pct` greater than 0.00 and no more than 100.00; `self_rating` and `assessed_rating` between 1 and 5 inclusive; `domain_weight` greater than 0.00 and no more than 1.00; `tolerance_pct` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive; `basel_aml_index` between 0.00 and 10.00 inclusive; `corridor_risk_weight` between 0.50 and 2.50 inclusive; `amount_usd` greater than 0; `amount_cents` greater than 0; `expected_corridors` holding between 1 and 8 entries inclusive; `depth` between 1 and 4 inclusive. Otherwise `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 400. Applies to every POST and PATCH endpoint carrying numeric fields.
8. **Priority 8** — pagination parameters parse as integers with `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` at least 0, defaulting to 10 and 0. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 400. Applies to every list read.
9. **Priority 9** — date and month values parse: `event_date` as an ISO 8601 date, `period_month` as `YYYY-MM`, `effective_from`, `effective_to` and `current_utc` as ISO 8601 instants. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_FORMAT`, 400. Applies to adverse-media logging, monitoring-profile creation, observation posting and the clock capability.
10. **Priority 10** — `expected_respondent_version` equals the addressed respondent's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_RESPONDENT_VERSION`, 412. Applies to questionnaire creation and monitoring-profile creation, and is checked before any state precondition on those two endpoints.
11. **Priority 11** — the addressed respondent's `dd_state` permits the requested operation per the lifecycle rules in §3. For example, a committee decision requires `PENDING_COMMITTEE`; a licensing verification is refused once `dd_state` is `TERMINATED`; a risk assessment requires a `dd_state` other than `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE`, 409. Applies to every respondent-scoped write endpoint.
12. **Priority 12** — the addressed child resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Each child type has its own error id, all with HTTP 409: questionnaire `ERR_INVALID_QUESTIONNAIRE_STATE`, nested relationship `ERR_INVALID_NESTED_STATE`, monitoring profile `ERR_INVALID_MONITORING_PROFILE_STATE`, alert `ERR_INVALID_ALERT_STATE`, information request `ERR_INVALID_RFI_STATE`, adverse-media event `ERR_INVALID_MEDIA_STATE`, periodic refresh `ERR_INVALID_REFRESH_STATE`, payment instruction `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE`, closure plan `ERR_INVALID_CLOSURE_STATE`, beneficial owner `ERR_INVALID_OWNER_STATE`. Applies to every child-resource transition endpoint.
13. **Priority 13** — round budgets are not exhausted: `clarification_rounds` is below 3 before a questionnaire may be returned for clarification again, and `insufficiency_rounds` is below 2 before an information request may be pushed back from `RESPONDED` to `SENT` again. Otherwise `ERR_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 409. Applies to the questionnaire assessment decision and the information-request adjudication.
14. **Priority 14** — `payment_posture` is not `BLOCKED` when a payment instruction is submitted, and is neither `BLOCKED` nor `SCREEN_ONLY` when one is released. Otherwise `ERR_RESPONDENT_PAYMENT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, 403. Applies to payment submission and to the officer screening decision. **Precedence pair P1:** a release attempted against a respondent whose posture is `BLOCKED` and whose closure plan would also breach its wind-down cap returns this 403, never the fiscal `ERR_WIND_DOWN_CAP_EXCEEDED` from priority 41.
15. **Priority 20** — a respondent whose `dd_state` is `TERMINATED` accepts no write at all, only reads; a respondent whose `dd_state` is `EXITING` accepts only closure-plan progression, payment screening decisions and information-request handling. Otherwise `ERR_RESPONDENT_RELATIONSHIP_CLOSED`, 403. Applies to every respondent-scoped write endpoint.
16. **Priority 21** — releasing a payment instruction whose `screening_state` is `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` is forbidden to every role including `SanctionsOfficer`; the only permitted exit from that state is rejection. Otherwise `ERR_SANCTIONED_PAYMENT_RELEASE_FORBIDDEN`, 403. Applies to the officer screening decision. **Precedence pair P2, first part:** when the instruction is `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` and also references an undisclosed nested party, this 403 wins over the referential `ERR_NESTED_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` from priority 30.
17. **Priority 22** — approval requires `license_status` to be `ACTIVE` or `CONDITIONAL`, `regulator_standing` to be exactly `GOOD_STANDING`, and `license_verified_at` to be set. Otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`, 422. Applies to the committee decision.
18. **Priority 23** — approval requires `ownership_verified` to be true, meaning `verified_ownership_pct` is at least 75.00 and `control_chain_complete` is true. Otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE`, 422. Applies to the committee decision.
19. **Priority 24** — approval requires the respondent's rating to be no worse than its home jurisdiction's `max_permitted_risk_rating`, ranking `LOW` below `MEDIUM` below `HIGH` below `PROHIBITED`; a rating of `PROHIBITED` or `UNRATED` is never approvable. Otherwise `ERR_RATING_EXCEEDS_JURISDICTION_CEILING`, 422. Applies to the committee decision.
20. **Priority 25** — a nested relationship may not be confirmed while the prohibition ladder yields a reason. The ladder's paired error is returned, and the ladder order is `ERR_NESTED_CYCLE_DETECTED`, then `ERR_NESTED_SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`, then `ERR_NESTED_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITS_NESTING`, then `ERR_NESTED_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_PAYABLE_THROUGH_NOT_PERMITTED`, all 422. Applies to nested-relationship creation and to nested-relationship adjudication.
21. **Priority 26** — a respondent whose `undisclosed_nested_count` is greater than 0 can neither be approved nor have its `payment_posture` widened to `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_NESTING_OUTSTANDING`, 422. Applies to the committee decision and to respondent amendment.
22. **Priority 27** — a respondent whose home jurisdiction is `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED` cannot be created in any state past `PROSPECT`, cannot be amended past `PROSPECT`, and cannot be approved; its `payment_posture` is forced to `BLOCKED`. Otherwise `ERR_HOME_JURISDICTION_SANCTIONED`, 422. Applies to respondent creation, respondent amendment and the committee decision.
23. **Priority 30** — when `involves_nested_party` is true, `nested_relationship_id` must be present, must belong to the same respondent, and must reference a relationship in state `DISCLOSED` or `CONFIRMED`. Otherwise `ERR_NESTED_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`, 422. Applies to payment submission and to the officer screening decision. **Precedence pair P2, second part:** one instruction can both carry an undisclosed nested party and sit in a sanctioned corridor, and in that case the safety check at priority 21 wins.
24. **Priority 31** — every jurisdiction code in the payload resolves to an existing jurisdiction profile, per §2.8. Otherwise `ERR_JURISDICTION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, 404. Applies to every create or amend carrying a jurisdiction code.
25. **Priority 32** — a body-supplied parent identifier belongs to the addressed aggregate, per §2.8. Otherwise `ERR_CROSS_RESPONDENT_REFERENCE`, 422.
26. **Priority 33** — a questionnaire may only be submitted when all eight control domains have a row, and may only be assessed under the refresh-final variant when every row carries an `assessed_rating`. Otherwise `ERR_CONTROL_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE`, 422. Applies to questionnaire submission and to the questionnaire assessment decision.
27. **Priority 34** — an observation may only be posted against a monitoring profile whose `calibration_state` is not `SUPERSEDED`. Otherwise `ERR_MONITORING_PROFILE_SUPERSEDED`, 422. Applies to observation posting.
28. **Priority 35** — the ownership graph stays acyclic and shallow and its percentages stay within 100. A `parent_owner_id` whose own ancestor set already contains the node yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CYCLE_DETECTED`; a resulting `chain_depth` above 4 yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`; sibling `ownership_pct` values sharing one `parent_owner_id`, or the direct owners taken together, summing above 100.00 yields `ERR_OWNERSHIP_PCT_EXCEEDS_100`. All three are 422 and are evaluated in exactly that order. Applies to beneficial-owner creation.
29. **Priority 36** — uniqueness holds: `swift_bic` across respondents; `jurisdiction_code` across jurisdiction profiles; the pair of `questionnaire_id` and `control_domain` across control assessments; `jurisdiction_code` within one profile's `expected_corridors`; the triple of `monitoring_profile_id`, `period_month` and `corridor_jurisdiction_code` across observations; at most one questionnaire per respondent in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION`; at most one closure plan per respondent in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `WIND_DOWN`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, 409 — except that the closure-plan case reports `ERR_CLOSURE_PLAN_ALREADY_OPEN`, also 409. Applies to respondent creation, jurisdiction-profile creation, control-assessment creation, monitoring-profile creation, observation posting, questionnaire creation and closure-plan drafting.
30. **Priority 37** — monitoring-profile effective intervals are closed per respondent, so adjacency is an overlap and at most one profile may be `CALIBRATED` over any instant. Otherwise `ERR_MONITORING_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to monitoring-profile creation. **Precedence pair P3:** a create whose interval overlaps and whose corridor totals would also exceed the aggregate ceiling returns this 409, not the fiscal error at priority 42.
31. **Priority 38** — refresh windows are half-open per respondent, so adjacency is allowed but any true interior overlap with a cycle in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is rejected with `ERR_REFRESH_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to refresh scheduling. **Precedence pair P4:** an overlapping window that also violates the post-completion buffer returns this 409, not the temporal `ERR_REFRESH_BUFFER_VIOLATION` at priority 40.
32. **Priority 39** — segregation of duties holds: the `X-User-ID` approving a respondent differs from its `assessed_by_user_id`; the `X-User-ID` performing an `ASSESS` on a questionnaire — that action alone, never a `RETURN` — differs from the `RespondentContact` id that submitted it; the `X-User-ID` approving a closure plan differs from its `drafted_by_user_id`; the `X-User-ID` disposing of an alert differs from the `X-User-ID` that adjudicated the information request raised from that alert. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 409. Applies to the respondent committee decision, the questionnaire assessment decision, the closure-plan approval and the alert disposition.
33. **Priority 40** — temporal admissibility against the logical clock. A new refresh cycle whose `window_start` falls within 30 days after a `COMPLETED` cycle's `scheduled_due_date` yields `ERR_REFRESH_BUFFER_VIOLATION`; an `effective_to` at or before `effective_from` yields `ERR_EFFECTIVE_INTERVAL_INVALID`; an adverse-media `event_date` after the clock yields `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`; an observation `period_month` beyond the clock's calendar month yields `ERR_OBSERVATION_PERIOD_IN_FUTURE`; marking an information request overdue while the clock is still before `due_at`, or escalating before the next 72-hour window has elapsed, yields `ERR_RFI_NOT_YET_OVERDUE`; closing a plan while the clock is still before `wind_down_end_at` yields `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. All six are 422. Applies to refresh scheduling, monitoring-profile creation, adverse-media logging, observation posting, overdue marking and closure-plan progression.
34. **Priority 41** — releasing a payment while `payment_posture` is `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` requires `wind_down_released_usd` plus `amount_usd` to be no greater than the live plan's `wind_down_cap_usd`. Otherwise `ERR_WIND_DOWN_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to the officer screening decision, and loses to priority 14 as noted in P1.
35. **Priority 42** — calibration requires the summed rounded corridor thresholds, after the aggregate buffer, to stay at or below 500000000.00. Otherwise `ERR_AGGREGATE_THRESHOLD_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to profile calibration, and loses to priority 37 as noted in P3.
36. **Priority 43** — settlement arithmetic preserves the obligation invariant. An allocation whose `amount_cents` exceeds `outstanding_obligation_cents` yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`; a reversal while `allocation_round` is 0 yields `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_TO_REVERSE`; a reversal whose `amount_cents` differs from `last_allocated_cents` yields `ERR_REVERSAL_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; a chronological-actual wind-down cap requested for a respondent with no observation history yields `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACTIVITY_HISTORY`. All four are 422. Applies to settlement allocation and to closure-plan approval.

Two further errors sit outside the numbered ladder because they guard a single operation each: `ERR_REFRESH_NOT_REASSESSED` (422) when a refresh cycle is completed without a refresh-final risk assessment taken at or after that cycle's `started_at`, and `ERR_OBLIGATION_OUTSTANDING` (422) when a closure plan is closed while `outstanding_obligation_cents` is greater than 0. Both are evaluated with the state and fiscal checks of the operation they guard.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Determinism

Every consequence of a write completes inside the request that caused it. There is no eventual consistency, no queue and no reconciliation job; the only deferred work in the entire system is the operator-driven sweep, which is an explicit epoch-advancing operation rather than a background process. When a request cascades — and several requests in this domain cascade five hops deep — the response is emitted only after every hop has been applied and every rollup recomputed.

Cascades that touch a set of sibling rows process them in `created_at` ascending order, always, so that counters and running sums are deterministic. Where the specification names a "most recent" row it also names the tie-break: the most recent lapsed refresh cycle is chosen by `scheduled_due_date` descending with `created_at` descending as tie-break; the latest assessed questionnaire is chosen by `created_at` descending.

A cascade hop fires only when its own precondition holds. A chain described as "the alert escalates, which raises a request, which narrows the posture, which holds cleared payments, which forces recalibration" stops at the first hop whose precondition fails, and the hops beyond it simply do not happen — they are not retried and they do not error. Each hop that does fire refreshes the affected row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` exactly once per request.

## 3. Business Logic & Domain Requirements

This section is the authoritative statement of behaviour. Where it describes an operation it names it by what the operation does — "the licensing-verification capability", "the committee decision", "the officer screening decision" — and §4 maps each of those names onto a concrete method and path. Where it names a field it uses that field's exact stored name.

### 3.1. Jurisdiction Reference Data and the Corridor Risk Ledger

Every risk judgement in this system eventually bottoms out in a jurisdiction profile, so the reference data is described first. A jurisdiction profile is created by a platform administrator or a sanctions officer and carries `jurisdiction_code`, a two-character code of uppercase ASCII letters that is unique across all profiles, and `name`, a string of 1 to 120 characters. A second create carrying an existing `jurisdiction_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` and HTTP 409.

Four fields describe the jurisdiction's standing and all four have defaults, so a create may omit them. `sanctions_status` is one of `PERMITTED`, `RESTRICTED` or `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED` and defaults to `PERMITTED`. `fatf_listing` is one of `NONE`, `INCREASED_MONITORING` or `CALL_FOR_ACTION` and defaults to `NONE`. `secrecy_flag` is a boolean defaulting to false. `nested_correspondent_prohibited` is a boolean defaulting to false. Two numeric fields are required on create: `basel_aml_index`, a value from 0.00 to 10.00 inclusive, and `corridor_risk_weight`, a value from 0.50 to 2.50 inclusive. A fifth optional field, `max_permitted_risk_rating`, is one of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `PROHIBITED` and defaults to `HIGH`; it is the ceiling the committee may not approve past.

The profile's `jurisdiction_risk_points` is computed on every create and on every amendment, and is never supplied by a client. It is built from four additive contributions. The sanctions contribution is 0 when `sanctions_status` is `PERMITTED`, 8 when it is `RESTRICTED` and 20 when it is `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`. The listing contribution is 0 for `NONE`, 5 for `INCREASED_MONITORING` and 12 for `CALL_FOR_ACTION`. The secrecy contribution is 3 when `secrecy_flag` is true and 0 otherwise. The index contribution is `basel_aml_index` multiplied by 2.0. Add the four together, and if that total is greater than 40.00 use 40.00 instead. Round the result to 2 decimal places, half up, and store it in `jurisdiction_risk_points`. A jurisdiction with `basel_aml_index` 8.75, `sanctions_status` `RESTRICTED`, `fatf_listing` `INCREASED_MONITORING` and `secrecy_flag` true therefore accumulates 17.50 plus 8 plus 5 plus 3, which is 33.50, below the ceiling. The same jurisdiction with `fatf_listing` `CALL_FOR_ACTION` accumulates 43.50 and is recorded as 40.00.

Amending a profile accepts any subset of `sanctions_status`, `fatf_listing`, `basel_aml_index`, `secrecy_flag`, `corridor_risk_weight`, `nested_correspondent_prohibited`, `max_permitted_risk_rating` and `name`, and recomputes `jurisdiction_risk_points` from the post-amendment values. One amendment carries a cross-entity cascade: moving `sanctions_status` to `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`. When that happens, every respondent bank whose `home_jurisdiction_code` equals this profile's `jurisdiction_code` and whose `dd_state` is not `TERMINATED` moves to `dd_state` `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `SANCTIONS_EXPOSURE` and `payment_posture` `BLOCKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Every nested relationship whose `downstream_jurisdiction_code` equals this code and whose state is `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `PROHIBITED` with `prohibition_reason` `SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`, also in `created_at` ascending order. Because those relationships changed state, the nested exposure rollup described in §3.5 is recomputed for every respondent the cascade touched. And because the posture moved to `BLOCKED` for a sanctions reason, every payment instruction of each affected respondent whose `screening_state` is `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` moves to `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` with `hold_reason` cleared to null, in `created_at` ascending order. That last hop fires only for a jurisdiction sanctions change; a posture that reaches `BLOCKED` for any other reason leaves existing instructions where they are.

Reading jurisdiction profiles is open to all five operator roles. The list read accepts the filters `sanctions_status`, `fatf_listing` and `nested_correspondent_prohibited` alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Respondent Onboarding, Licensing Standing and the Commercial Profile

A respondent bank enters the system through the onboarding capability, available to a relationship manager or a platform administrator. The create requires `legal_name` (1 to 160 characters), `swift_bic` (either 8 or 11 uppercase alphanumeric characters, unique across respondents), `home_jurisdiction_code`, `relationship_manager_user_id`, `expected_monthly_volume_usd` and `expected_monthly_count`. It optionally accepts `license_status`, `regulator_name`, `regulator_standing` and `nested_activity_permitted`. A duplicate `swift_bic` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` and HTTP 409; an unresolvable `home_jurisdiction_code` with `ERR_JURISDICTION_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404; and a `home_jurisdiction_code` whose profile carries `sanctions_status` `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED` with `ERR_HOME_JURISDICTION_SANCTIONED` and HTTP 422 — the relationship cannot be opened at all.

A newly created respondent is initialised deterministically: `dd_state` `PROSPECT`, `payment_posture` `BLOCKED`, `risk_rating` `UNRATED`, `risk_assessment_variant` `NONE`, `approved_risk_rating` `UNRATED`, `license_status` `UNVERIFIED` unless supplied, `regulator_standing` `UNVERIFIED` unless supplied, `nested_activity_permitted` false unless supplied, `version` 1, and the whole battery of computed and counter fields at zero or null: `verified_ownership_pct` 0.00, `control_chain_complete` false, `ownership_verified` false, `disclosed_nested_count` 0, `undisclosed_nested_count` 0, `max_nested_depth` 0, `nested_exposure_units` 0, `inherent_risk_score` 0.00, `control_score` 0.00, `control_credit` 0.0000, `adverse_media_uplift` 0.00, `residual_risk_score` 0.00, `unresponsive_strikes` 0, `escalation_units` 0, and `license_verified_at`, `assessed_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `last_approved_at`, `refresh_due_date`, `restriction_reason` and `last_risk_assessed_at` all null.

The amendment capability accepts `legal_name`, `expected_monthly_volume_usd`, `expected_monthly_count`, `nested_activity_permitted`, `relationship_manager_user_id` and `payment_posture`, each optional. Two rules govern it. Widening `payment_posture` to `OPEN` requires `dd_state` to be `APPROVED` and `undisclosed_nested_count` to be exactly 0; otherwise the amendment is refused with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_NESTING_OUTSTANDING` and HTTP 422. And changing either `expected_monthly_volume_usd` or `expected_monthly_count` while the respondent has a monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` sets that profile to `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` with `recalibration_reason` `OFFICER_ACTION`, because the expectations the thresholds were derived from have moved. A respondent whose home jurisdiction is comprehensively sanctioned cannot be amended out of `PROSPECT`; such an amendment is refused with `ERR_HOME_JURISDICTION_SANCTIONED`.

Licensing verification is recorded by a compliance analyst or a platform administrator. The body requires `license_status` and `regulator_standing` and optionally carries `regulator_name`; all three are written onto the respondent, and `license_verified_at` is stamped with the logical clock. `license_status` is one of `UNVERIFIED`, `ACTIVE`, `CONDITIONAL`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`; `regulator_standing` is one of `UNVERIFIED`, `GOOD_STANDING`, `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` or `DEREGISTERED`.

The outcome of a verification depends on where the respondent stands. From `dd_state` `PROSPECT`, a verification recording `license_status` in the set `ACTIVE` or `CONDITIONAL` together with `regulator_standing` in the set `GOOD_STANDING` or `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`, against a home jurisdiction that is not comprehensively sanctioned, moves `dd_state` from `PROSPECT` to `DD_IN_PROGRESS`. From `PROSPECT`, a verification recording `license_status` `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, or `regulator_standing` `DEREGISTERED`, is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` and HTTP 422 — an unlicensed bank never enters due diligence. The identical payload against a respondent already in the approved family (`APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE`) has the opposite effect: it drives `dd_state` to `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `LICENSE_LAPSE` and narrows `payment_posture` to `SCREEN_ONLY`. A verification against a respondent whose `dd_state` is `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_RELATIONSHIP_CLOSED` and HTTP 403.

The restriction capability is the officer lever that sits outside the committee flow, and it is available to a sanctions officer or a compliance analyst. Its `action` is one of `RESTRICT`, `LIFT` or `CLEAR_SCREENING`, and it optionally carries `restriction_reason` and `payment_posture`.

`RESTRICT` requires `dd_state` to be `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE` and requires `restriction_reason` to be supplied. It sets `dd_state` to `RESTRICTED`, writes the supplied `restriction_reason` — one of `UNRESPONSIVE_STRIKES`, `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, `SANCTIONS_EXPOSURE`, `PROHIBITED_NESTING`, `REFRESH_LAPSED`, `LICENSE_LAPSE` or `OFFICER_ACTION`, and `OFFICER_ACTION` for a bare officer restriction — and narrows `payment_posture` to `BLOCKED`, or to the supplied `payment_posture` when that is `SCREEN_ONLY` or `BLOCKED`. Three further hops follow. Every nested relationship of the respondent in state `CONFIRMED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, processed `created_at` ascending; the state weight of `SUSPENDED` equals that of `CONFIRMED`, so `nested_exposure_units` does not move. If at least one relationship was suspended, the respondent's monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` moves to `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` with `recalibration_reason` `OFFICER_ACTION`. A `RESTRICT` from any other `dd_state` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE` and HTTP 409.

`LIFT` is the compensating action and it is deliberately hard to satisfy. It requires `dd_state` to be `RESTRICTED`, `unresponsive_strikes` to be exactly 0, no adverse-media event of the respondent to be in `adjudication_state` `NEW`, no nested relationship of the respondent to be in state `PROHIBITED` or `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `license_status` to be `ACTIVE` or `CONDITIONAL`, `regulator_standing` to be `GOOD_STANDING` or `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`, the home jurisdiction not to be comprehensively sanctioned, and `last_approved_at` to be non-null. When all of those hold, `dd_state` returns to `APPROVED`, `restriction_reason` is cleared to null, `payment_posture` moves from `BLOCKED` to `SCREEN_ONLY`, and every nested relationship in state `SUSPENDED` moves back to `CONFIRMED` in `created_at` ascending order, provided the prohibition ladder of §3.5 yields no reason for that row; `nested_exposure_units` is recomputed and is unchanged by the suspension round trip. A lift whose strike count is still above 0 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE`; a lift while nesting is still undisclosed is refused with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_NESTING_OUTSTANDING`. Restriction and lift may alternate any number of times over a relationship's life.

`CLEAR_SCREENING` touches only the posture dimension. It requires `dd_state` to be `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE` or `UNDER_REFRESH`, requires that no information request of the respondent with `severity` `CRITICAL` is in state `SENT`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, and requires that no periodic refresh of the respondent is in state `LAPSED` with a `scheduled_due_date` at or after `last_approved_at`. It then moves `payment_posture` from `SCREEN_ONLY` to `OPEN`. Payment instructions already sitting in `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` are **not** released by this move; each still needs an officer decision.

The single respondent read returns every stored field including the computed scores, the counters and the current `version` — the value an optimistic-concurrency writer must echo back. The list read accepts the filters `dd_state`, `payment_posture`, `risk_rating`, `home_jurisdiction_code` and `license_status`, plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. Ownership Transparency and the Four-Level Look-Through

A beneficial owner is declared against a respondent by a compliance analyst or by the respondent itself. The body requires `owner_name` (1 to 160 characters), `owner_type` (one of `NATURAL_PERSON`, `LEGAL_ENTITY` or `STATE`), `ownership_pct` (greater than 0.00 and no more than 100.00, 2 decimals) and `country_of_residence` (a resolvable jurisdiction code). It optionally carries `parent_owner_id`, naming an existing owner of the **same** respondent under which this owner sits, and `is_pep`, a boolean defaulting to false. Every owner is created with `verification_status` `PENDING`, `verified_by_user_id` null and `rejection_reason` null.

`chain_depth` is derived, never supplied. An owner with no `parent_owner_id` has `chain_depth` 1. Otherwise `chain_depth` is one more than the parent's `chain_depth`. The maximum is 4. Three graph rules are checked, in this order, and each has its own error: if the supplied `parent_owner_id` resolves to an owner whose own ancestor chain already contains the node being created, the create is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CYCLE_DETECTED`; if the resulting `chain_depth` would exceed 4, it is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`; and if the `ownership_pct` values of the siblings sharing this `parent_owner_id` — or, for a direct owner, of all owners with a null `parent_owner_id` — would sum above 100.00 once this row is added, it is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_PCT_EXCEEDS_100`. All three carry HTTP 422. A `parent_owner_id` that belongs to a different respondent is refused with `ERR_CROSS_RESPONDENT_REFERENCE`; one that resolves to nothing at all is refused with `ERR_BENEFICIAL_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`.

`effective_ownership_pct` is the look-through figure and it is computed on create and again whenever the owner is verified. Walk from this owner up through `parent_owner_id` to the root, collecting the node and every ancestor; the number of nodes collected equals this owner's `chain_depth`. Multiply all of their `ownership_pct` values together, keeping full precision throughout with no intermediate rounding. Then divide by 100 raised to the power of one less than the number of nodes. Round that single quotient to 2 decimal places, half up, and store it. A direct owner holding 60.00 therefore has `effective_ownership_pct` 60.00. A second-level owner holding 40.00 under that owner has 60.00 multiplied by 40.00, which is 2400.00, divided by 100, giving 24.00. A third-level owner holding 25.00 under that one has 60.00 times 40.00 times 25.00, which is 60000.00, divided by 10000, giving 6.00. The verification status of an ancestor is irrelevant to this arithmetic; verification gates the rollup, not the look-through.

The verification capability is exclusive to a compliance analyst. Its `action` is one of `VERIFY`, `REJECT` or `RESET`, and it optionally carries `rejection_reason`.

`VERIFY` requires the owner to be in `verification_status` `PENDING`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be neither `DECLINED` nor `TERMINATED`, requires that when `parent_owner_id` is set the parent owner is already `VERIFIED`, and requires that the jurisdiction named by `country_of_residence` is not comprehensively sanctioned. It sets `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, writes `verified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, clears `rejection_reason`, recomputes `effective_ownership_pct` for this node, and reruns the respondent-level rollup. `REJECT` requires the owner to be `PENDING` and requires `rejection_reason` — one of `DOCUMENT_MISSING`, `IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, `SANCTIONS_HIT` or `REFUSED_DISCLOSURE`. It sets `verification_status` to `REJECTED`, writes the reason and `verified_by_user_id`, reruns the rollup, and reverts every descendant owner currently in `VERIFIED` back to `PENDING`, processed `created_at` ascending, because a rejected ancestor invalidates everything hanging beneath it. `RESET` requires the owner to be `REJECTED` and requires `rejection_reason` to be anything other than `SANCTIONS_HIT`; a sanctions hit is never reset and such a request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OWNER_STATE` and HTTP 409. A successful `RESET` returns the owner to `PENDING` and clears both `rejection_reason` and `verified_by_user_id`. Any other from-state for any action is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_OWNER_STATE`.

There is one system-driven move on this dimension: an owner in `VERIFIED` returns to `PENDING`, with `verified_by_user_id` cleared, when an adverse-media item of the same respondent is confirmed under the conditions in §3.8. Ownership can be re-verified afterwards, restoring the rollup.

The respondent-level rollup runs after every verification action and after that cascade, and it writes three respondent fields. Take the respondent's **direct** owners, meaning exactly those whose `parent_owner_id` is null, and among them the ones whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`. `verified_ownership_pct` is the sum of their `ownership_pct` values rounded to 2 decimal places, half up. `control_chain_complete` starts as true and becomes false if any of those verified direct owners has `owner_type` `LEGAL_ENTITY` and `ownership_pct` of at least 25.00 while having no child owner — that is, no owner whose `parent_owner_id` is this owner's `id` — in `verification_status` `VERIFIED`; a verified corporate holder of a controlling stake must expose at least one verified layer beneath it. `ownership_verified` is true only when `verified_ownership_pct` is at least 75.00 **and** `control_chain_complete` is true. The 75.00 comparison is inclusive: exactly 75.00 verifies, 74.99 does not.

Reads on this graph are open to every role. The list read filters on `respondent_bank_id`, `parent_owner_id` (to walk one level down a chain), `verification_status` and `owner_type`, with `limit` and `offset`. The single read returns `chain_depth` and `effective_ownership_pct` alongside the rest.

### 3.4. Questionnaire Collection and Independent Control Assessment

A due-diligence questionnaire is the respondent's declaration of its own compliance programme, and the analyst's independent overlay on it. Creating one is the first of the two optimistic-concurrency writes: the body requires `questionnaire_version` (1 to 40 characters, for example `WOLFSBERG_V1_4`) and `expected_respondent_version`, and optionally carries `declared_nested_activity` (default false) and `declared_downstream_count` (default 0). A version mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_RESPONDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before any state check, exactly as §2.7 requires. A respondent may hold at most one questionnaire in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION` at a time; a create while one of those is open supersedes it rather than being refused, moving that earlier row to `SUPERSEDED`. A questionnaire already in `ASSESSED` is also moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the create, with its `computed_control_score` and `control_score_variant` frozen for audit; the respondent's own `control_score` is left untouched until the new questionnaire reaches `ASSESSED`. The new row is created in `DRAFT` with `clarification_rounds` 0, `submitted_at`, `assessed_at`, `assessed_by_user_id` and `computed_control_score` null, and `control_score_variant` `NONE`.

Control-assessment rows are added to a questionnaire one control domain at a time by the respondent or by an analyst. The body requires `control_domain` and `self_rating`, and optionally carries `evidence_note` (0 to 500 characters, defaulting to the empty string). `control_domain` is one of eight values — `GOVERNANCE`, `KYC_ONBOARDING`, `TRANSACTION_MONITORING`, `SANCTIONS_SCREENING`, `PEP_HANDLING`, `TRAINING`, `AUDIT`, `NESTED_CONTROLS` — and at most one row per domain may exist per questionnaire; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. `self_rating` is an integer from 1 to 5 inclusive. The row is created with `assessed_rating` null, `gap_severity` `NONE`, `remediation_required` false, and `domain_weight` stamped at creation from the fixed weight table below. Rows may only be added while the parent questionnaire is in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_QUESTIONNAIRE_STATE` and HTTP 409. One exception exists for the analyst's own first row: adding a row to a questionnaire in `SUBMITTED` moves that questionnaire to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`.

The analyst's overlay is applied by amending a control-assessment row, and is exclusive to a compliance analyst. It accepts `assessed_rating` (an integer from 1 to 5 inclusive), `gap_severity`, `remediation_required` and `evidence_note`, all optional. It requires the parent questionnaire to be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION`, and the first such overlay on a questionnaire in `SUBMITTED` moves it to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`. `gap_severity` is one of `NONE`, `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and setting it to `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` forces `remediation_required` to true regardless of what the body supplied; setting it to `NONE` or `MINOR` leaves `remediation_required` false unless the body explicitly sets it.

Submission is exclusive to the respondent contact. It requires the questionnaire to be in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be one of `PROSPECT`, `DD_IN_PROGRESS`, `PENDING_COMMITTEE`, `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH`, `REFRESH_OVERDUE` or `RESTRICTED`, and requires internal consistency of the declaration: either `declared_downstream_count` is 0 or `declared_nested_activity` is true. It also requires a control-assessment row for all eight control domains; a missing domain is refused with `ERR_CONTROL_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE` and HTTP 422. On success the state becomes `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, overwriting any earlier value when the questionnaire is being resubmitted after clarification. Existing control-assessment rows survive a resubmission so the analyst can revise `assessed_rating`.

The assessment decision is exclusive to a compliance analyst and carries `action`, optionally `variant` and optionally `clarification_note`. `action` is either `ASSESS` or `RETURN`.

`ASSESS` requires the questionnaire to be in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, requires a `variant`, requires a row for all eight control domains, requires — under the `REFRESH_FINAL` variant only, exactly as priority 33 of §2.9 scopes it — that no row has a null `assessed_rating`, and — when `declared_nested_activity` is true, whichever variant is supplied — requires the `NESTED_CONTROLS` row to carry an `assessed_rating` of at least 2. Under `ONBOARDING_PROVISIONAL` a null `assessed_rating` is no obstacle to assessing, because that variant credits the row's `self_rating` instead. A missing domain, or a null `assessed_rating` where one of the two clauses above requires it, is refused with `ERR_CONTROL_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE`. It then computes the control score, writes `computed_control_score` and `control_score_variant` on the questionnaire, stamps `assessed_at` with the logical clock and `assessed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, mirrors the computed value onto the respondent's `control_score`, and moves the questionnaire to `ASSESSED`. Because the analyst must be independent of the respondent, an `ASSESS` whose `X-User-ID` equals the respondent's `id` — the identity a respondent contact acts under — is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409.

`RETURN` requires the questionnaire to be in `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, requires `clarification_rounds` to be no greater than 2 before the increment, and requires a substantive reason to return: at least one control-assessment row of this questionnaire must have `gap_severity` `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, or a null `assessed_rating`. It increments `clarification_rounds` by 1 and sets the state to `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION`. A fourth return — one attempted while `clarification_rounds` is already 3 — is refused with `ERR_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 409. The cycle `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` to `RETURNED_FOR_CLARIFICATION` to `SUBMITTED` may therefore run at most three times.

The control score is a **dual-variant** calculation: identical stored rows produce different numbers under the two variants, and which variant is permitted depends on the respondent's `dd_state`. The eight domains carry fixed weights that sum to exactly 1.00 and are always evaluated in this order: `GOVERNANCE` 0.10, `KYC_ONBOARDING` 0.18, `TRANSACTION_MONITORING` 0.20, `SANCTIONS_SCREENING` 0.22, `PEP_HANDLING` 0.10, `TRAINING` 0.05, `AUDIT` 0.08, `NESTED_CONTROLS` 0.07. For each domain in that order, determine the rating to credit, multiply it by the domain's weight, and add the product to a running total kept at full precision with no intermediate rounding. Multiply the total by 20.0 and round the product to 2 decimal places, half up. That is the raw score, and it lies between 20.00 and 100.00.

Only then are the gap caps applied, and the first matching clause wins. Count the rows of this questionnaire whose `gap_severity` is `CRITICAL` and the rows whose `gap_severity` is `MAJOR`. If the critical count is at least 1, the stored score is the smaller of the raw score and 40.00. Otherwise, if the major count is at least 2, it is the smaller of the raw score and 60.00. Otherwise, if the major count is exactly 1, it is the smaller of the raw score and 75.00. Otherwise it is the raw score unchanged. Rounding before capping is normative; capping a full-precision total and rounding afterwards can differ by a cent and is wrong. Only the rows belonging to the questionnaire being assessed participate; rows of any other questionnaire, including a superseded one, are excluded.

The two variants differ solely in which rating each domain contributes. Under `ONBOARDING_PROVISIONAL` — the only variant accepted while the respondent's `dd_state` is `PROSPECT`, `DD_IN_PROGRESS` or `PENDING_COMMITTEE` — a domain credits its `assessed_rating` when that is present and its `self_rating` otherwise, and a domain with no row at all contributes the neutral rating 3. The respondent's own self-assessment is therefore credited. Under `REFRESH_FINAL` — the only variant accepted while `dd_state` is `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH`, `REFRESH_OVERDUE` or `RESTRICTED` — a domain credits its `assessed_rating` when present and the rating 1 otherwise; `self_rating` is ignored entirely and a missing domain row also contributes 1. Identical rows therefore score strictly lower under `REFRESH_FINAL` whenever any `assessed_rating` is null or any domain row is absent. Supplying the wrong variant for the respondent's current `dd_state` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE` and HTTP 409.

A worked example makes the ordering concrete. Suppose all eight rows carry `assessed_rating` 4 except `SANCTIONS_SCREENING`, which carries 2 with `gap_severity` `MAJOR`, and `AUDIT`, which carries 3. The weighted total is 0.10 times 4 plus 0.18 times 4 plus 0.20 times 4 plus 0.22 times 2 plus 0.10 times 4 plus 0.05 times 4 plus 0.08 times 3 plus 0.07 times 4, which is 0.40 plus 0.72 plus 0.80 plus 0.44 plus 0.40 plus 0.20 plus 0.24 plus 0.28, giving 3.48. Multiplied by 20.0 that is 69.60, which rounds to 69.60. Exactly one row is `MAJOR` and none is `CRITICAL`, so the cap is 75.00 and the stored `computed_control_score` stays 69.60. Had a second row been `MAJOR`, the cap would be 60.00 and the stored value 60.00.

Questionnaire reads are open to every role. The list read filters on `respondent_bank_id` and `state` and returns rows `questionnaire_version` descending then `created_at` ascending. Control-assessment reads filter on `questionnaire_id`, `control_domain` and `gap_severity`, and return rows in the fixed domain weight order above rather than in creation order.

### 3.5. Nested Correspondents: Discovery, Depth and the Prohibition Ladder

Behind every respondent sit institutions we never contracted with. A nested relationship records one such edge. Creating one requires `downstream_institution_name` (1 to 160 characters), `downstream_jurisdiction_code`, `relationship_type` and `discovery_source`, and optionally carries `downstream_respondent_bank_id` when the downstream institution is itself a respondent already known to this system. `relationship_type` is one of `DOWNSTREAM_CORRESPONDENT`, `PAYABLE_THROUGH` or `NESTED_PSP`. `discovery_source` is one of `QUESTIONNAIRE`, `TRANSACTION_MONITORING`, `RFI` or `ADVERSE_MEDIA`.

The `discovery_source` decides the initial state, and this is the pivot of the whole subsection. A row created with `discovery_source` `QUESTIONNAIRE` starts in state `DISCLOSED`: the respondent told us. A row created with any other `discovery_source` starts in `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`: we found it ourselves. A respondent contact may only create rows with `discovery_source` `QUESTIONNAIRE`; any other source supplied under that role is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. A create is also refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` when this respondent already has a row with the same `downstream_institution_name` and `downstream_jurisdiction_code` pair in a state other than `PROHIBITED` or `TERMINATED`.

`depth` is computed at creation and recomputed at every adjudication, and is never supplied. A row's depth starts at 1 — the respondent's own downstream institution. When that row's `downstream_respondent_bank_id` is set, the chain extends transitively: look at the downstream respondent's own rows in states `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED`, order them by `depth` descending and then `created_at` ascending, take the first, add 1 to the depth, and repeat from that row. Stop when the current row has no `downstream_respondent_bank_id`, or when the downstream respondent has no live row to continue with. If the walk arrives at a respondent already visited on this chain — the originating respondent counts as visited from the start — the configuration is a cycle, and that verdict feeds the prohibition ladder below as its first clause, whose reason is `CYCLE` and whose paired error is `ERR_NESTED_CYCLE_DETECTED`. A cycle is therefore not a create-time short circuit of its own: it is the ladder, together with the creation rule stated below for a non-questionnaire `discovery_source`, that decides whether the request is refused with `ERR_NESTED_CYCLE_DETECTED` or the row is recorded in `PROHIBITED` with `prohibition_reason` `CYCLE`. Rows in `PROHIBITED` or `TERMINATED` never extend a chain. The ceiling is 4: a computed depth above 4 is rejected with `ERR_NESTED_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` rather than clamped to 4, and that ceiling is tested only when the walk found no cycle — a cyclic configuration is settled by the ladder instead.

Exactly one `prohibition_reason` is ever recorded for a row, and it comes from a first-match ladder evaluated against the row's downstream jurisdiction profile, its computed depth, its `relationship_type` and the parent respondent's `nested_activity_permitted` flag. In order: if the chain revisits a respondent already on the chain, the reason is `CYCLE` and the paired error is `ERR_NESTED_CYCLE_DETECTED`. Otherwise, if the downstream jurisdiction's `sanctions_status` is `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`, the reason is `SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION` and the error is `ERR_NESTED_SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`. Otherwise, if that jurisdiction's `nested_correspondent_prohibited` is true, the reason is `JURISDICTION_PROHIBITS_NESTING` and the error is `ERR_NESTED_JURISDICTION_PROHIBITS_NESTING`. Otherwise, if `depth` is at least 3, the reason is `DEPTH_EXCEEDED` and the error is `ERR_NESTED_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` — depth 3 is prohibited, depth 2 is not. Otherwise, if `relationship_type` is `PAYABLE_THROUGH` and the respondent's `nested_activity_permitted` is false, the reason is `PAYABLE_THROUGH_NOT_PERMITTED` and the error is `ERR_PAYABLE_THROUGH_NOT_PERMITTED`. Otherwise there is no reason and the relationship may be confirmed. All five errors carry HTTP 422. Later clauses are not evaluated once one matches, so a payable-through edge into a comprehensively sanctioned jurisdiction reports `ERR_NESTED_SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`, never the payable-through error.

The ladder is consulted at creation too, and the outcome depends on who is creating. When the ladder yields a reason and the `discovery_source` is not `QUESTIONNAIRE`, the row is still created, in state `PROHIBITED`, carrying the ladder's `prohibition_reason` — we record what we found even though it cannot be permitted. When a respondent contact declares the row, that is, `discovery_source` is `QUESTIONNAIRE`, a ladder reason instead rejects the create outright with the ladder's paired error.

Every creation and every adjudication reruns the respondent-level exposure rollup, which uses a state weight table: `DISCLOSED` weighs 1, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED` weighs 5, `CONFIRMED` weighs 3, `SUSPENDED` weighs 3, and both `PROHIBITED` and `TERMINATED` weigh 0. `nested_exposure_units` is the sum, over every nested relationship of the respondent including prohibited and terminated ones, of that row's state weight multiplied by its `depth`. `disclosed_nested_count` counts the rows in `DISCLOSED` or `CONFIRMED`. `undisclosed_nested_count` counts the rows in `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`. `max_nested_depth` is the largest `depth` among rows in `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED`, and 0 when there are none. `CONFIRMED` and `SUSPENDED` share weight 3 deliberately, so suspending a confirmed relationship and later reverting the suspension leaves `nested_exposure_units` untouched.

Adjudication is available to a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer. Its `action` is one of `CONFIRM`, `PROHIBIT`, `SUSPEND`, `REVERT` or `TERMINATE`, and it optionally carries `prohibition_reason`. Every accepted action writes `adjudicated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock, and reruns the exposure rollup. A from-state that has no declared transition for the requested action is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NESTED_STATE` and HTTP 409.

`CONFIRM` is permitted from `DISCLOSED` and from `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`. It requires the respondent's `nested_activity_permitted` to be true, requires the prohibition ladder to yield no reason for this row — otherwise the ladder's paired error is returned — and requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be none of `DECLINED`, `EXITING` or `TERMINATED`. From `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED` it additionally requires that an information request of this respondent with `request_topic` `NESTED_DISCLOSURE` has reached state `ACCEPTED`: we do not simply absorb something we discovered ourselves without the respondent explaining it. Confirming from `DISCLOSED` moves the row's weight from 1 to 3, so `nested_exposure_units` increases by twice the row's `depth`, and `disclosed_nested_count` is unchanged. Confirming from `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED` moves the weight from 5 to 3, so `nested_exposure_units` decreases by twice the depth, `undisclosed_nested_count` falls by 1 and `disclosed_nested_count` rises by 1. Either way, if the confirmation raises `max_nested_depth`, the respondent's monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` moves to `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` with `recalibration_reason` `NESTED_DISCOVERY`.

`REVERT` is the exact compensating action for a confirmation from `DISCLOSED`. It is permitted from `CONFIRMED`, requires that no payment instruction references this row through `nested_relationship_id` while in `screening_state` `RELEASED` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be neither `EXITING` nor `TERMINATED`. It returns the row to `DISCLOSED`, moving the weight from 3 back to 1 and decreasing `nested_exposure_units` by twice the depth, restoring `nested_exposure_units`, `disclosed_nested_count` and `max_nested_depth` to their pre-confirmation values.

`PROHIBIT` is permitted from `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED` and `CONFIRMED`, and requires the prohibition ladder to yield a non-null reason for the row; it writes that single winning reason into `prohibition_reason` and moves the row to `PROHIBITED`, dropping its weight to 0. Two hops follow. When the respondent's `dd_state` is `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE`, it moves to `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `PROHIBITED_NESTING` and `payment_posture` `BLOCKED`, which in turn suspends every `CONFIRMED` relationship of that respondent. And when the winning `prohibition_reason` is `SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`, every payment instruction referencing this row whose `screening_state` is `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` moves to `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS`, processed `created_at` ascending. A prohibition also closes an open undisclosed-nesting alert: any monitoring alert of this respondent with `alert_type` `NESTED_ACTIVITY_UNDISCLOSED` still in state `OPEN` moves to `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` with `disposition_reason` `PROHIBITED_NESTING`.

`TERMINATE` is permitted from `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` and `SUSPENDED`. From `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` it requires that no payment instruction referencing this row is in `screening_state` `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`. It drops the row's weight to 0 and recomputes all four rollup fields. The same move is driven as a cascade when a closure plan reaches `NOTICE_SERVED` and when a respondent reaches `DECLINED`.

The suspension edge is not an operator action at all: a row in `CONFIRMED` moves to `SUSPENDED` only as a cascade from its respondent entering `RESTRICTED`, and returns from `SUSPENDED` to `CONFIRMED` only as a cascade from a successful lift, and only when the prohibition ladder still yields no reason for that row. `PROHIBITED` and `TERMINATED` are terminal: no action leaves them.

Reads are open to every role. The list read filters from either end of the edge, on `respondent_bank_id` or on `downstream_respondent_bank_id`, and also on `state`, `relationship_type` and `discovery_source`. A separate computed read expands a respondent's whole nested chain recursively through `downstream_respondent_bank_id`, returning rows `depth` ascending then `created_at` ascending, including rows reached transitively through another respondent's relationships with their recomputed depth, and accepting a `state` filter. The `depth` reported against a row is that row's own depth recomputed by the rule above from the respondent that owns it, not the number of expansion hops taken to reach it — so a row owned by a downstream respondent reports whatever the rule above yields for it, commonly 1, and the returned rows are ordered on those recomputed values. That read recomputes depth without persisting anything, and a cycle encountered during expansion truncates the walk rather than raising an error.

### 3.6. Risk Scoring, the Committee Decision and the Payment Posture

Scoring a respondent is exclusive to a compliance analyst. The body carries a single field, `variant`, which is either `ONBOARDING_PROVISIONAL` or `REFRESH_FINAL`. A scoring run against a respondent whose `dd_state` is `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE`. Every run executes the same six steps in this pinned order: the ownership rollup, the inherent score, the control score under the matching variant, the adverse-media uplift, the residual score, and the rating assignment. It then writes `risk_assessment_variant` from the payload, `assessed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `last_risk_assessed_at` from the logical clock.

The inherent score is the sum of four components, capped and rounded once at the end. The jurisdiction component is simply the home jurisdiction profile's `jurisdiction_risk_points`, a value from 0 to 40.

The ownership component considers only the respondent's owners whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`; unverified and rejected owners contribute nothing. Start at 0. Add 10 if any verified owner has `is_pep` true. Add 8 if any verified owner has `owner_type` `STATE` and `effective_ownership_pct` of at least 10.00. Add 6 if the respondent's `verified_ownership_pct` is strictly below 75.00. Then count the distinct `country_of_residence` values among the verified owners whose jurisdiction profile has a `sanctions_status` other than `PERMITTED`, multiply that count by 4, and add the smaller of 12 and that product. Finally, if the running total exceeds 24.0, use 24.0 instead.

The nested component counts the respondent's rows in states `CONFIRMED` or `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, multiplies that count by 5, adds 8 when `max_nested_depth` is at least 2, and uses 20.0 instead if the result exceeds 20.0. Rows in `DISCLOSED` and `SUSPENDED` are live for the depth and rollup purposes of §3.5 but do not enter this count; rows in `PROHIBITED` or `TERMINATED` are excluded throughout.

The activity component is tiered on `expected_monthly_volume_usd`, with every boundary inclusive at the lower edge: at least 100000000.00 scores 14; otherwise at least 10000000.00 scores 10; otherwise at least 1000000.00 scores 6; otherwise 2. Exactly 1000000.00 therefore scores 6, not 2. Add 2 when `nested_activity_permitted` is true, then use 16.0 instead if the result exceeds 16.0.

Add the four components. If the sum exceeds 100.00, use 100.00. Round to 2 decimal places, half up, and store in `inherent_risk_score`.

The adverse-media uplift accumulates over a **half-open** window and rounds twice over. Only the respondent's adverse-media events whose `adjudication_state` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `event_date` falls at or after the window start and strictly before the window end participate; events in `NEW` or `DISMISSED` never contribute. Each event's own contribution is its tier points multiplied by its reliability multiplier, rounded to 2 decimal places, half up, and stored on the event as `uplift_points`: tier points are 12.0 for `severity_tier` `TIER_1`, 7.0 for `TIER_2` and 3.0 for `TIER_3`, and the multiplier is 1.00 for `source_reliability` `A`, 0.75 for `B` and 0.50 for `C`. Process the qualifying events sorted by `event_date` and then by `created_at`, adding each rounded contribution to a running total and re-rounding the running total to 2 decimal places after each addition. Finally, if the total exceeds 20.00, use 20.00, and store the result in `adverse_media_uplift`. Summing at full precision and rounding once at the end is wrong. A `TIER_2` event from source `B` contributes 5.25; three of them accumulate 5.25, then 10.50, then 15.75.

The window itself has two variants. Under `ONBOARDING_LOOKBACK`, used when the scoring variant is `ONBOARDING_PROVISIONAL` or whenever `last_approved_at` is null, the window runs from the logical clock minus 730 days of 24 hours, inclusive, to the logical clock, exclusive. Under `SINCE_LAST_APPROVAL`, used when the scoring variant is `REFRESH_FINAL` and `last_approved_at` is non-null, the window runs from `last_approved_at`, inclusive, to the earlier of the logical clock and the base cadence window end described in §3.8, exclusive — so an event published after the base cadence window closed cannot inflate the uplift that would accelerate that same window.

The residual score is derived in a pinned order, and the credit depends only on the control score. If `control_score` is no greater than 20.00, `control_credit` is 0.0000. Otherwise `control_credit` is `control_score` minus 20.00, divided by 80.00, multiplied by 0.4500, rounded to 4 decimal places, half up. Then multiply `inherent_risk_score` by one minus `control_credit`, round that product to 2 decimal places, add `adverse_media_uplift`, and round the sum to 2 decimal places again; store it in `residual_risk_score`. The intermediate rounding before the uplift is added is normative. With `control_score` 69.60, the credit is 49.60 divided by 80.00 times 0.4500, which is 0.279, recorded as 0.2790; with `inherent_risk_score` 58.00 the credited figure is 58.00 times 0.7210, which is 41.818, rounded to 41.82; adding an uplift of 5.25 gives 47.07.

The rating follows immediately, and one override outranks the thresholds. If the home jurisdiction's `sanctions_status` is `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`, `risk_rating` is `PROHIBITED` regardless of the score. Otherwise, a `residual_risk_score` of at least 70.00 is `PROHIBITED`; at least 50.00 is `HIGH`; at least 30.00 is `MEDIUM`; anything below 30.00 is `LOW`. Every comparison is inclusive, so exactly 50.00 is `HIGH` while 49.99 is `MEDIUM`. The example above, 47.07, is `MEDIUM`.

A scoring run under `ONBOARDING_PROVISIONAL` also advances the lifecycle when the respondent is ready. From `dd_state` `DD_IN_PROGRESS`, the respondent moves to `PENDING_COMMITTEE` when `ownership_verified` is true, at least one questionnaire of the respondent is in state `ASSESSED`, `license_verified_at` is non-null with `license_status` `ACTIVE` or `CONDITIONAL`, and no nested relationship of the respondent is in state `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`. When any of those fails the scores are still written but the state stays `DD_IN_PROGRESS`.

The committee decision is exclusive to a committee approver, requires `dd_state` to be `PENDING_COMMITTEE`, and carries `action` — one of `APPROVE`, `DECLINE` or `RETURN` — plus an optional `decision_note` and an optional `override_risk_rating`. In every case the approver must not be the analyst who scored the respondent: an `X-User-ID` equal to `assessed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409. The rating the committee acts on is the respondent's `risk_rating`, improved by exactly one notch when `override_risk_rating` is supplied — `PROHIBITED` becomes `HIGH`, `HIGH` becomes `MEDIUM`, `MEDIUM` becomes `LOW`, and `LOW` stays `LOW`.

`APPROVE` carries five substantive gates, each with its own error and all evaluated after the segregation check in the ladder order of §2.9. `license_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `CONDITIONAL`, `regulator_standing` must be exactly `GOOD_STANDING` and `license_verified_at` must be set, else `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. `ownership_verified` must be true, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE`. The rating acted on must not be `PROHIBITED` or `UNRATED`, and must rank no worse than the home jurisdiction's `max_permitted_risk_rating` under the ranking `LOW` below `MEDIUM` below `HIGH` below `PROHIBITED`, else `ERR_RATING_EXCEEDS_JURISDICTION_CEILING`. `undisclosed_nested_count` must be 0 and no nested relationship may be in state `PROHIBITED`, else `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_NESTING_OUTSTANDING`. No information request of the respondent may be in state `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE`. And the home jurisdiction must not be comprehensively sanctioned, else `ERR_HOME_JURISDICTION_SANCTIONED`.

A successful approval sets `dd_state` to `APPROVED`, writes `approved_risk_rating` from the rating acted on, `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `last_approved_at` from the logical clock, computes `refresh_due_date` under the `AT_APPROVAL` variant of §3.8, and moves `payment_posture` from `BLOCKED` to `OPEN` when the rating acted on is `LOW` or `MEDIUM`, or from `BLOCKED` to `SCREEN_ONLY` when it is `HIGH`. Note that `approved_risk_rating` is frozen at this instant: later scoring runs move `risk_rating` without touching `approved_risk_rating`, and the refresh cadence in §3.8 is deliberately driven by the frozen value.

`DECLINE` sets `dd_state` to `DECLINED`, a terminal state, and leaves `payment_posture` at `BLOCKED`. Two cascades follow, each processed `created_at` ascending: every nested relationship of the respondent in `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` and all four exposure rollup fields are recomputed; and every information request in `DRAFT`, `SENT`, `RESPONDED`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED` moves to `ABANDONED` with `escalation_units` recomputed. `RETURN` sends the respondent back to `dd_state` `DD_IN_PROGRESS` with every score and `risk_rating` retained unchanged and `approved_risk_rating` and `last_approved_at` untouched; the respondent may be re-scored and re-submitted to committee any number of times.

The payment posture is a second, independent state dimension on the same respondent. Its initial value is `BLOCKED` and it has no terminal value. It reaches `OPEN` or `SCREEN_ONLY` at approval as described above, and `SCREEN_ONLY` from `BLOCKED` on a successful lift. It narrows from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY` in three situations, all system-driven: an information request of this respondent reaching `SENT` with `severity` `CRITICAL`, a periodic refresh lapsing during the operator sweep, and an information request reaching `ESCALATED` during that sweep. Each requires `dd_state` to be `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE`, and each carries the same hop: because the posture left `OPEN`, every payment instruction of the respondent in `screening_state` `SCREENED_CLEAR` moves to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `POSTURE_SCREEN_ONLY`, processed `created_at` ascending. It widens from `SCREEN_ONLY` back to `OPEN` only through the clear-screening action of §3.2 or the amendment of §3.2, and this dimension is independent of `dd_state`: a respondent may sit in `dd_state` `APPROVED` with `payment_posture` `SCREEN_ONLY` indefinitely. It reaches `BLOCKED` from either `OPEN` or `SCREEN_ONLY` when `dd_state` enters `RESTRICTED` in the same request, or when the home jurisdiction becomes comprehensively sanctioned. It reaches `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` from `OPEN` or `SCREEN_ONLY` when a closure plan is approved, and a move from `OPEN` carries the same held-payment hop; it returns from `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` to `SCREEN_ONLY` when an approved plan is aborted, and moves from `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` to `BLOCKED` when the relationship closes.

### 3.7. Monitoring Calibration, Observed Activity and Alert Generation

A transaction-monitoring profile pins what we expect to see from a respondent over a bounded period, and the thresholds that turn an observation into an alert. Creating one is the second optimistic-concurrency write and is exclusive to a compliance analyst. The body requires `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `expected_corridors` and `expected_respondent_version`, and optionally carries `tolerance_pct` (from 0.00 to 0.50 inclusive, defaulting to 0.15) and `supersede`, a boolean. `expected_corridors` is an array of 1 to 8 entries, each entry carrying `jurisdiction_code`, `expected_monthly_usd` and `expected_monthly_count`; every `jurisdiction_code` must resolve to a jurisdiction profile and must be unique within the array, a repeat being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

The interval is **closed** at both ends. An `effective_to` at or before `effective_from` is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_INTERVAL_INVALID` and HTTP 422. A new interval that touches or overlaps any existing profile of this respondent is refused with `ERR_MONITORING_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409 — because the interval is closed, a new `effective_from` exactly equal to an existing `effective_to` is a conflict, not an adjacency. The one way to place an overlapping interval is to supply `supersede` true, which moves each overlapped profile of that respondent to `calibration_state` `SUPERSEDED` from whichever of `PROVISIONAL`, `CALIBRATED` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` it held, freezing its `corridor_thresholds` for audit. A stale `expected_respondent_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_RESPONDENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 before the interval check runs. The new row is created in `PROVISIONAL` with `corridor_thresholds` an empty array and `aggregate_usd_threshold`, `hold_threshold_usd`, `calibrated_at`, `calibrated_by_user_id` and `recalibration_reason` all null.

The calibration capability is exclusive to a compliance analyst and carries `action` — one of `CALIBRATE`, `REQUIRE_RECALIBRATION` or `SUPERSEDE` — plus an optional `recalibration_reason`. `CALIBRATE` requires `calibration_state` to be `PROVISIONAL` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, requires the respondent's `risk_rating` to be anything other than `UNRATED`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be none of `PROSPECT`, `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED` (and, from `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, none of `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED`), requires every `jurisdiction_code` in `expected_corridors` to resolve, and requires that no other profile of this respondent in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` has a closed interval overlapping this one. Those gates are evaluated in a pinned order: the `dd_state` admissibility first, then the `calibration_state` from-state check, then the `UNRATED` refusal — which carries `ERR_INVALID_RESPONDENT_STATE` and HTTP 409 — then the overlap check, and the corridor arithmetic last. So a `CALIBRATE` addressed to a profile whose `calibration_state` is not `PROVISIONAL` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` reports `ERR_INVALID_MONITORING_PROFILE_STATE` even when the respondent's `risk_rating` is still `UNRATED`. It writes `corridor_thresholds`, `aggregate_usd_threshold` and `hold_threshold_usd`, stamps `calibrated_at` with the logical clock and `calibrated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, clears `recalibration_reason` to null, and moves the state to `CALIBRATED`. Recalibrating uses the respondent's rating **as it stands now**, so a rating that worsened since the previous calibration tightens every threshold. `REQUIRE_RECALIBRATION` requires `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` and requires `recalibration_reason`; `SUPERSEDE` moves the profile to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`. Any other from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MONITORING_PROFILE_STATE` and HTTP 409.

Calibration arithmetic rounds per corridor before it aggregates, and that order is normative. First take the rating factor from the respondent's `risk_rating`: 1.30 for `UNRATED`, 1.30 for `LOW`, 1.15 for `MEDIUM`, 1.00 for `HIGH` and 0.85 for `PROHIBITED` — a worse rating yields a smaller factor and therefore a tighter threshold. Then, for each entry of `expected_corridors` in payload order, look up that jurisdiction's `corridor_risk_weight`. The unrounded dollar figure is `expected_monthly_usd` multiplied by one plus `tolerance_pct`, multiplied by the rating factor, divided by the corridor risk weight. Clamp that figure into the range 10000.00 to 500000000.00 inclusive — anything below the floor becomes 10000.00 and anything above the ceiling becomes 500000000.00 — then round to 2 decimal places, half up, and record it as that entry's `usd_threshold`. The count figure is `expected_monthly_count` multiplied by one plus `tolerance_pct`, multiplied by the rating factor, divided by the corridor risk weight; take the smallest integer that is not less than that figure — always rounding up, never to nearest — and then use 5 instead if the result is below 5. Record it as `count_threshold`. Each entry of `corridor_thresholds` therefore carries `jurisdiction_code`, `usd_threshold` and `count_threshold`, in the same order as the corridors were supplied.

Then aggregate: sum the **rounded** `usd_threshold` values, multiply the sum by 1.10, round to 2 decimal places, half up, and store as `aggregate_usd_threshold`. Divide that stored figure by 10.0, round to 2 decimal places, and store as `hold_threshold_usd`. Summing unrounded corridor figures before applying the buffer is wrong. If the buffered aggregate exceeds 500000000.00 the calibration is refused with `ERR_AGGREGATE_THRESHOLD_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. As a worked example, a single corridor expecting 2000000.00 per month and 400 transactions, with `tolerance_pct` 0.15, a `MEDIUM` rating and a `corridor_risk_weight` of 1.25, yields a dollar figure of 2000000.00 times 1.15 times 1.15 divided by 1.25, which is 2116000.00, inside the clamp, so `usd_threshold` is 2116000.00; the count figure is 400 times 1.15 times 1.15 divided by 1.25, which is 423.2, rounded up to 424. The aggregate is 2116000.00 times 1.10, which is 2327600.00, and `hold_threshold_usd` is 232760.00.

An activity observation posts one month of observed corridor flow against a profile and is the alert-generating write, available to a platform administrator or a compliance analyst. The body requires `period_month` (formatted `YYYY-MM`), `corridor_jurisdiction_code`, `aggregate_usd` and `txn_count`, and optionally carries `distinct_downstream_count` (defaulting to 0). The triple of `monitoring_profile_id`, `period_month` and `corridor_jurisdiction_code` is unique; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` and HTTP 409. A `period_month` later than the logical clock's own calendar month is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_PERIOD_IN_FUTURE` and HTTP 422; the clock's own month is accepted. A profile in `calibration_state` `SUPERSEDED` accepts no observation and is refused with `ERR_MONITORING_PROFILE_SUPERSEDED` and HTTP 422.

Alerts are then created in a pinned order — `CORRIDOR_UNEXPECTED` first, then `VOLUME_BREACH`, then `COUNT_BREACH`, then `NESTED_ACTIVITY_UNDISCLOSED` — so that both their `created_at` ordering and the observation's `alerts_created_count` are deterministic. Between zero and four alerts may be created by one observation, and `alerts_created_count` records exactly how many were.

Look for an entry of the profile's `expected_corridors` whose `jurisdiction_code` equals the observation's `corridor_jurisdiction_code`. If there is none, this is an unexpected corridor. Create one alert with `alert_type` `CORRIDOR_UNEXPECTED` and `breach_ratio` null, whose `severity` comes from that jurisdiction's `sanctions_status`: `CRITICAL` when the status is `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`, `HIGH` when it is `RESTRICTED`, and `MEDIUM` otherwise. Record `usd_threshold_applied` 0.00, `count_threshold_applied` 0 and `breach_ratio` 0.0000 on the observation, and evaluate no volume or count breach at all — there was no threshold to breach.

If the corridor is expected, take its entry in `corridor_thresholds` and record `usd_threshold_applied` from `usd_threshold` and `count_threshold_applied` from `count_threshold` on the observation, then set the observation's `breach_ratio` to `aggregate_usd` divided by that `usd_threshold`, rounded to 4 decimal places, half up. Volume and count alerts are suppressed unless the profile's `calibration_state` is exactly `CALIBRATED`; a `PROVISIONAL` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` profile still records the observation and still raises the unexpected-corridor and undisclosed-nesting alerts, but never a volume or count breach. When the profile is `CALIBRATED`: if `aggregate_usd` is at least the corridor's `usd_threshold`, create an alert with `alert_type` `VOLUME_BREACH` carrying the observation's `breach_ratio`, whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` when `breach_ratio` is at least 3.0000, otherwise `HIGH` at 2.0000 or more, otherwise `MEDIUM` at 1.5000 or more, otherwise `LOW`. And if `txn_count` is at least the corridor's `count_threshold`, create an alert with `alert_type` `COUNT_BREACH` and `breach_ratio` null, whose `severity` is `HIGH` when `txn_count` is at least twice the `count_threshold` and `MEDIUM` otherwise. Both breach tests are inclusive: an `aggregate_usd` exactly equal to the threshold breaches.

Last, count the respondent's nested relationships in states `DISCLOSED` or `CONFIRMED`. If `distinct_downstream_count` is **strictly greater** than that count, create an alert with `alert_type` `NESTED_ACTIVITY_UNDISCLOSED` and `breach_ratio` null, whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` when the respondent's `nested_activity_permitted` is false and `HIGH` when it is true. This is the one test in the calculation that is strict rather than inclusive: a `distinct_downstream_count` exactly equal to the known count does not alert. This last check runs whether or not the corridor was expected and whatever the profile's calibration state.

An officer may also raise an alert directly, with no originating observation, and that create is available to a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer. The body requires `alert_type` and `severity`, both supplied by the officer rather than derived, and optionally `activity_observation_id`. The row is created in state `OPEN` with `activity_observation_id` null when omitted. An `activity_observation_id` belonging to another respondent's profile is refused with `ERR_CROSS_RESPONDENT_REFERENCE` and HTTP 422.

The disposition capability is available to a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer and carries `action` — one of `INVESTIGATE`, `ESCALATE`, `CLOSE_FALSE_POSITIVE` or `CLOSE_CONFIRMED` — plus an optional `disposition_reason`. Any from-state without a declared transition for the requested action is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ALERT_STATE` and HTTP 409. Across all actions, the acting `X-User-ID` must not be the identity that adjudicated the information request raised from this alert; such a request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409.

`INVESTIGATE` moves the alert from `OPEN` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be neither `DECLINED` nor `TERMINATED`, and writes `investigated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. `CLOSE_FALSE_POSITIVE` moves it from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to the terminal `CLOSED_FALSE_POSITIVE`, requires `disposition_reason` to be one of `DATA_QUALITY`, `EXPECTED_SEASONALITY` or `RESPONDENT_EXPLAINED`, and is forbidden when `alert_type` is `NESTED_ACTIVITY_UNDISCLOSED` — an undisclosed-nesting alert can never be dismissed as a false positive, and such a request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ALERT_STATE`. `CLOSE_CONFIRMED` moves it to the terminal `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` and requires a `disposition_reason`; when that reason is `UNRESOLVED_RISK` the respondent is restricted with `restriction_reason` `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, and when it is `PROHIBITED_NESTING` the respondent is restricted with `restriction_reason` `PROHIBITED_NESTING`, each carrying the full restriction cascade of §3.2.

`ESCALATE` is the deepest cascade in the domain and runs exactly five hops. It requires the alert to be in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, requires that no information request of this respondent linked to this alert through `monitoring_alert_id` is in state `SENT`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, and requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be none of `DECLINED`, `EXITING` or `TERMINATED`. Hop one moves the alert to `ESCALATED` and stamps `escalated_at` with the logical clock. Hop two creates a new information request for the respondent with `monitoring_alert_id` set to this alert, `severity` copied from this alert's `severity`, and `request_topic` `ACTIVITY_EXPLANATION` when `alert_type` is `VOLUME_BREACH`, `COUNT_BREACH` or `CORRIDOR_UNEXPECTED` and `NESTED_DISCLOSURE` otherwise; that request is dispatched immediately, so it is created in state `SENT` with `dispatched_at` at the logical clock and `sla_hours` and `due_at` from the service-level rule of §3.8. Hop three fires only when that request's `severity` is `CRITICAL`: the respondent's `payment_posture` moves from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY`. Hop four fires only when hop three fired: every payment instruction of the respondent in `screening_state` `SCREENED_CLEAR` moves to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `POSTURE_SCREEN_ONLY`, processed `created_at` ascending. Hop five fires only when hop four actually held at least one instruction: the respondent's monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` moves to `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` with `recalibration_reason` `ALERT_ESCALATION`. In every case `escalation_units` is recomputed. A hop whose own precondition fails simply does not happen, and the hops beyond it do not happen either; the escalation still succeeds.

Two closures are cascade-driven rather than operator-driven. An alert in `ESCALATED` moves to `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` when the information request linked to it through `monitoring_alert_id` reaches `ACCEPTED`, with `disposition_reason` `RESPONDENT_EXPLAINED`, or reaches `ABANDONED`, with `disposition_reason` `UNRESOLVED_RISK`. An alert in `OPEN` with `alert_type` `NESTED_ACTIVITY_UNDISCLOSED` moves to `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` with `disposition_reason` `PROHIBITED_NESTING` when a nested relationship of the same respondent is prohibited.

Profile reads filter on `respondent_bank_id` and `calibration_state`. Observation reads filter on `monitoring_profile_id`, `period_month` and `corridor_jurisdiction_code`, and return rows `period_month` ascending then `created_at` ascending — the exact order the chronological wind-down basis of §3.9 consumes them in. Alert reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `activity_observation_id`, `state`, `severity` and `alert_type`. Profile and observation reads are open to every operator role, with profile reads additionally open to the respondent contact within its own scope.

### 3.8. Chasing the Respondent: Information Requests, Adverse Media, Refresh Cadence and the Sweep

An information request is raised against a respondent by a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer. The body requires `request_topic` — one of `OWNERSHIP_EVIDENCE`, `LICENSING_EVIDENCE`, `NESTED_DISCLOSURE`, `ACTIVITY_EXPLANATION` or `CONTROL_REMEDIATION` — and `severity`, one of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`. It optionally carries `monitoring_alert_id` naming the alert that motivated it; when supplied, that alert must belong to the same respondent, else `ERR_CROSS_RESPONDENT_REFERENCE`, and the request's `severity` must equal the linked alert's `severity`. The row is created in state `DRAFT` with `escalation_level` 0, `reminder_count` 0, `insufficiency_rounds` 0, `retro_credited` false, `response_note` the empty string, and `dispatched_at`, `sla_hours`, `due_at`, `responded_at` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` null. The service-level clock does not start at creation.

Dispatch starts it. Dispatch is available to a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer, requires the request to be in `DRAFT` or in `ESCALATED` (a re-issue), requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be none of `PROSPECT`, `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED`, and — when `monitoring_alert_id` is set — requires that alert to be in state `ESCALATED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. Re-issuing from `ESCALATED` additionally requires `reminder_count` to be no greater than 2. Dispatch stamps `dispatched_at` with the logical clock, computes and writes `sla_hours` and `due_at`, sets the state to `SENT`, raises `escalation_level` to at least 1, and recomputes the respondent's `escalation_units`. When the request's `severity` is `CRITICAL`, the dispatch also narrows the respondent's `payment_posture` from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY`, carrying the held-payment hop of §3.6. Any other from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RFI_STATE` and HTTP 409.

The service level is computed from three inputs as they stand at the moment of dispatch, and the two compressions multiply rather than compete. The base is 24 hours for `severity` `CRITICAL`, 72 for `HIGH`, 168 for `MEDIUM` and 336 for `LOW`. Halve it when the respondent's `risk_rating` is `HIGH` or `PROHIBITED`. Halve it again when the respondent's `unresponsive_strikes` is at least 2. Take the smallest integer that is not less than the result, then use 12 instead if that integer is below 12, and store it in `sla_hours`. `due_at` is `dispatched_at` plus `sla_hours` hours. A `HIGH`-severity request against a `HIGH`-rated respondent with two strikes therefore gets 72 times 0.5 times 0.5, which is 18 hours; a `CRITICAL` request in the same circumstances gets 24 times 0.25, which is 6, floored up to the minimum of 12 hours. The request becomes eligible to be marked overdue once the logical clock reaches `due_at`; the comparison is inclusive, so a clock exactly equal to `due_at` is overdue.

The respondent answers through the response capability, exclusive to the respondent contact acting on its own file. It requires `response_note` (1 to 1000 characters), requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be other than `TERMINATED`, and is permitted from `SENT`, `OVERDUE` and `ESCALATED` — a late answer is accepted rather than refused. It moves the request to `RESPONDED`, stamps `responded_at` with the logical clock and overwrites `response_note`. Strikes are not credited back here; acceptance decides that.

Overdue maintenance is operator-driven and available to a compliance analyst or a platform administrator, with `action` one of `REMIND`, `MARK_OVERDUE` or `ESCALATE`. `REMIND` requires state `SENT` or `OVERDUE` and increments `reminder_count` by 1. `MARK_OVERDUE` requires state `SENT` and requires the logical clock to have reached `due_at`; a clock still before `due_at` is refused with `ERR_RFI_NOT_YET_OVERDUE` and HTTP 422. It sets the state to `OVERDUE`, increments `reminder_count` by 1 and `escalation_level` by 1 capped at 3, and adds one unresponsive strike. `ESCALATE` requires state `OVERDUE` and requires the logical clock to be at least 72 hours past `due_at` for each level being advanced; too early is likewise `ERR_RFI_NOT_YET_OVERDUE`. It advances `escalation_level` by one level, up to a maximum of 3, and sets the state to `ESCALATED`.

The strike ledger is the respondent-level consequence, and it moves in exactly four places. A request moving from `SENT` to `OVERDUE` adds 1 to `unresponsive_strikes`. A request moving from `RESPONDED` to `ACCEPTED` with `retro_credit` true sets `unresponsive_strikes` to the larger of 0 and its current value minus that request's `reminder_count`, so the credit never drives the counter negative. A request moving from `RESPONDED` back to `SENT` on an insufficiency leaves the counter unchanged. A request moving from `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED` to `ABANDONED` adds 1. Separately, `escalation_units` is always the sum of `escalation_level` across every information request of that respondent, recomputed after each of these moves. And whenever `unresponsive_strikes` reaches 3 or more while the respondent's `dd_state` is neither `EXITING` nor `TERMINATED`, the respondent moves to `dd_state` `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `UNRESPONSIVE_STRIKES`, and `payment_posture` narrows from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY` while a posture already `SCREEN_ONLY`, `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` or `BLOCKED` is left untouched. The threshold is inclusive at 3.

Adjudication closes the loop and is available to a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer, with `action` one of `ACCEPT`, `INSUFFICIENT` or `ABANDON` and an optional `retro_credit` boolean. `ACCEPT` requires state `RESPONDED` and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the respondent's `id`. It moves the request to the terminal `ACCEPTED`, writes `adjudicated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, resets `escalation_level` to 0 and recomputes `escalation_units`. When `retro_credit` is true and `reminder_count` is greater than 0 it also sets `retro_credited` true and credits the strike ledger as described. And when `monitoring_alert_id` is set and that alert is in `ESCALATED`, the alert moves to `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` with `disposition_reason` `RESPONDENT_EXPLAINED`.

`INSUFFICIENT` requires state `RESPONDED` and requires `insufficiency_rounds` to be no greater than 1 before the increment; a third insufficiency is refused with `ERR_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 409. It increments `insufficiency_rounds` by 1 and `escalation_level` by 1 capped at 3, returns the request to `SENT`, re-stamps `dispatched_at` with the logical clock, and recomputes `sla_hours` and `due_at` using the respondent's **current** `unresponsive_strikes` — so a re-issued request usually carries a shorter window than the original. `ABANDON` requires state `ESCALATED` with `escalation_level` exactly 3, or state `OVERDUE`; it moves the request to the terminal `ABANDONED`, adds one strike, resets `escalation_level` to 0 and recomputes `escalation_units` while `unresponsive_strikes` is retained. When `monitoring_alert_id` is set and that alert is in `ESCALATED`, the alert moves to `CLOSED_CONFIRMED` with `disposition_reason` `UNRESOLVED_RISK`.

Adverse-media items are logged against a respondent by a compliance analyst or a sanctions officer. The body requires `headline` (1 to 200 characters), `category` — one of `SANCTIONS_EVASION`, `MONEY_LAUNDERING`, `FRAUD`, `REGULATORY_ACTION`, `LITIGATION` or `OTHER` — `severity_tier` (`TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`), `source_reliability` (`A`, `B` or `C`) and `event_date`. An `event_date` after the logical clock is refused with `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE` and HTTP 422. The row is created in `adjudication_state` `NEW` with `uplift_points` 0.00 and `triaged_by_user_id` and `triaged_at` null; points are only earned at triage.

Triage carries `action`, either `CONFIRM` or `DISMISS`, requires `adjudication_state` `NEW` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MEDIA_STATE` and HTTP 409), and writes `adjudication_state`, `triaged_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `triaged_at` from the logical clock. `DISMISS` additionally requires that the item is not a high-confidence severe one: it is permitted when `severity_tier` is other than `TIER_1`, or when `source_reliability` is `C`. A `TIER_1` item from source `A` or `B` therefore cannot be dismissed and such a request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEDIA_STATE`. A dismissed item keeps `uplift_points` 0.00 and never enters any scoring window.

`CONFIRM` requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be neither `DECLINED` nor `TERMINATED` and requires `event_date` to be at or before the logical clock. It then runs the second five-hop cascade of the domain. Hop one moves the item to the terminal `CONFIRMED` and writes its own `uplift_points` from the per-event formula of §3.6. Hop two recomputes the respondent's `adverse_media_uplift`, `residual_risk_score` and `risk_rating`. Hop three recomputes `refresh_due_date` under the `ON_ADVERSE_MEDIA` variant below; when the recomputed `refresh_due_date` is strictly earlier than the `scheduled_due_date` of the respondent's cycle in state `SCHEDULED`, that cycle moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_ACCELERATION` and a fresh cycle is created with `trigger_reason` `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, `window_start` at the logical clock and `scheduled_due_date` at the new `refresh_due_date`. Hop four fires only when `category` is `SANCTIONS_EVASION` or `severity_tier` is `TIER_1`: every beneficial owner of the respondent in `verification_status` `VERIFIED` reverts to `PENDING`, processed `created_at` ascending, and the ownership rollup is rerun, which drops `verified_ownership_pct` and can flip `ownership_verified` to false. Hop five fires only when hop four left `ownership_verified` false while `dd_state` is `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE`, `UNDER_REFRESH` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE`: the respondent moves to `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `ADVERSE_MEDIA` and `payment_posture` `BLOCKED`, which suspends every `CONFIRMED` nested relationship. When only `severity_tier` `TIER_1` holds and `category` is not `SANCTIONS_EVASION`, a `TIER_1` confirmation still restricts the respondent with `restriction_reason` `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, narrowing `payment_posture` to `SCREEN_ONLY`, and schedules a cycle with `trigger_reason` `ADVERSE_MEDIA` when none of the respondent's cycles is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.

The next refresh date is where this domain's circularity is broken. Stating the rule naively is circular — the cadence depends on the rating, the rating depends on the media uplift, and the uplift window depends on the cadence — so the evaluation runs in exactly five steps and implementations must not iterate towards a fixed point. The cadence table maps a rating to whole months: `LOW` 36, `MEDIUM` 24, `HIGH` 12 and `PROHIBITED` 0.

Step one computes the base window end by adding the cadence of the respondent's **frozen** `approved_risk_rating` to `last_approved_at`, using the month arithmetic of §2.5. Step two computes an uplift over the half-open window from `last_approved_at`, inclusive, to the earlier of the logical clock and that base window end, exclusive — this is the `SINCE_LAST_APPROVAL` variant of §3.6, never the `ONBOARDING_LOOKBACK` one. Step three derives a live residual score and a live rating from that uplift, using the same residual and rating rules of §3.6. Step four computes an accelerated candidate by adding the cadence of the **live** rating to `last_approved_at`; the live rating from step three must never be fed back into steps one or two. Then, if any adverse-media item of the respondent is in `adjudication_state` `CONFIRMED` with `severity_tier` `TIER_1` and an `event_date` inside that same half-open window, take the earliest `triaged_at` among them, add 30 days of 24 hours, and use that instant instead of the accelerated candidate whenever it is earlier. Step five stores the final value: take the later of `last_approved_at` plus 30 days of 24 hours and the earlier of the base window end and the accelerated candidate. The floor guarantees a refresh is never instantly overdue.

Two variants use this same five-step procedure with different inputs. The at-approval variant, `AT_APPROVAL`, runs from a committee approval or a refresh completion, where `last_approved_at` and `approved_risk_rating` are the values just written in that request; steps one and four therefore agree initially and `refresh_due_date` equals the base window end unless a `TIER_1` item already sits inside the window. The on-adverse-media variant, `ON_ADVERSE_MEDIA`, runs from a confirmation, where `last_approved_at` and `approved_risk_rating` are unchanged historical values; the live rating of step three can be worse than `approved_risk_rating`, so `refresh_due_date` moves strictly earlier.

A refresh cycle is scheduled by a compliance analyst or a platform administrator. The body requires `trigger_reason` — one of `CADENCE`, `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, `ALERT_ESCALATION`, `NESTED_DISCOVERY` or `REGULATOR_ACTION` — plus `window_start` and `scheduled_due_date`. The window is **half-open**, running from `window_start` inclusive to `scheduled_due_date` exclusive, so a new cycle whose `window_start` equals an existing cycle's `scheduled_due_date` is legal. Any true interior overlap with a cycle in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_REFRESH_WINDOW_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409. A `window_start` falling within 30 days after a `COMPLETED` cycle's `scheduled_due_date` is refused with `ERR_REFRESH_BUFFER_VIOLATION` and HTTP 422, and the overlap check is evaluated first when both hold. The row is created in `SCHEDULED` with `rating_at_schedule` frozen from the respondent's `approved_risk_rating` and `cadence_months_applied` frozen from the cadence of that same rating, with `started_at`, `completed_at`, `lapsed_at`, `completed_control_score`, `completion_risk_rating` and `cancelled_reason` all null — the two frozen completion figures start null rather than at 0.00 or `UNRATED`, exactly like the audit stamps of §2.4, and every cycle however it comes into being, this capability included, is created that way — and the respondent moves from `dd_state` `APPROVED` to `REFRESH_DUE`. A create whose `trigger_reason` is `ADVERSE_MEDIA`, `ALERT_ESCALATION` or `NESTED_DISCOVERY` also cancels every other cycle of that respondent in `SCHEDULED`, with `cancelled_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_ACCELERATION`, processed `created_at` ascending.

Progression carries `action` — one of `START`, `COMPLETE`, `LAPSE` or `CANCEL` — plus an optional `cancelled_reason`, and is available to a compliance analyst or a platform administrator. Any from-state without a declared transition for the action is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFRESH_STATE` and HTTP 409.

`START` requires state `SCHEDULED`, requires the logical clock to have reached `window_start`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be `APPROVED`, `REFRESH_DUE` or `REFRESH_OVERDUE`, and requires that no other cycle of that respondent is `IN_PROGRESS`. It sets the state to `IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `started_at` with the logical clock, and moves the respondent to `dd_state` `UNDER_REFRESH`.

`COMPLETE` requires state `IN_PROGRESS` and five respondent-side conditions: a questionnaire of the respondent in state `ASSESSED` with `assessed_at` at or after this cycle's `started_at`; `risk_assessment_variant` exactly `REFRESH_FINAL` with `last_risk_assessed_at` at or after `started_at`, else `ERR_REFRESH_NOT_REASSESSED` and HTTP 422; `ownership_verified` true; and `risk_rating` other than `PROHIBITED`. It writes `completed_at` from the logical clock, `completed_control_score` from the respondent's `control_score` and `completion_risk_rating` from its `risk_rating`, sets the state to the terminal `COMPLETED`, moves the respondent back to `dd_state` `APPROVED` with `last_approved_at` re-stamped and `approved_risk_rating` refreshed to the live `risk_rating`, recomputes `refresh_due_date` under the `AT_APPROVAL` variant, and moves the respondent's monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` to `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` with `recalibration_reason` `REFRESH_COMPLETION`. The cycle `APPROVED` to `REFRESH_DUE` to `UNDER_REFRESH` to `APPROVED` repeats for the life of the relationship.

`LAPSE` is permitted from `SCHEDULED` once the logical clock has reached `scheduled_due_date`, and from `IN_PROGRESS` once the clock has reached `scheduled_due_date` plus 90 days of 24 hours. It stamps `lapsed_at` with the logical clock, sets the state to the terminal `LAPSED`, and moves the respondent to `dd_state` `REFRESH_OVERDUE`; from a lapse out of `SCHEDULED` the respondent's `payment_posture` also narrows from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY`, leaving a posture already `SCREEN_ONLY`, `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` or `BLOCKED` untouched. Then, once the clock stands at or past that cycle's `scheduled_due_date` plus 30 days of 24 hours, the respondent moves from `REFRESH_OVERDUE` to `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `REFRESH_LAPSED`, `payment_posture` `BLOCKED` and every `CONFIRMED` nested relationship suspended in `created_at` ascending order; the cycle chosen for that comparison is the respondent's most recent `LAPSED` cycle by `scheduled_due_date` descending with `created_at` descending as tie-break. `CANCEL` requires state `SCHEDULED` and requires `cancelled_reason`, either `SUPERSEDED_BY_ACCELERATION` or `RELATIONSHIP_EXITING`. A cycle in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is also cancelled with `cancelled_reason` `RELATIONSHIP_EXITING` as a cascade when the respondent enters `EXITING`.

The operator sweep is the system's only epoch-advancing operation and is exclusive to a platform administrator. It accepts one optional field, `dry_run`. Its order is fully pinned: respondents are processed by `refresh_due_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and within one respondent refresh cycles are processed before information requests. For each respondent it performs, in that order, the same transitions the explicit capabilities perform: a respondent in `dd_state` `APPROVED` whose `last_approved_at` is set and whose `refresh_due_date` the clock has reached moves to `REFRESH_DUE`, creating a cycle with `trigger_reason` `CADENCE`, `window_start` at `refresh_due_date` and `scheduled_due_date` at `refresh_due_date` plus 90 days of 24 hours, with `rating_at_schedule` from the live `risk_rating` and `cadence_months_applied` from the cadence of `approved_risk_rating`, but only when no cycle of that respondent is already `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; every eligible cycle is lapsed exactly as `LAPSE` would; every request in `SENT` whose `due_at` the clock has reached is marked overdue exactly as `MARK_OVERDUE` would, adding its strike; and every request standing in `OVERDUE` whose `due_at` plus 72 hours the clock has reached — including one this same sweep has just marked overdue, because the overdue pass runs to completion over that respondent's requests before the escalation pass begins — is escalated to `escalation_level` 3 and state `ESCALATED`, narrowing `payment_posture` from `OPEN` to `SCREEN_ONLY` when it is still `OPEN`. Every restriction those moves imply is applied, including the move to `RESTRICTED` at three strikes and the move from `REFRESH_OVERDUE` to `RESTRICTED` after the 30-day restriction grace. With `dry_run` true nothing at all is written and the response lists the respondents that would have changed, in sweep order. Otherwise the response returns the affected respondent rows in sweep order, each with its `version` incremented once.

Reads on all three collections are open to the operator roles, with information-request and refresh reads additionally open to the respondent contact in its own scope. Request reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `monitoring_alert_id`, `state` and `request_topic`; media reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `adjudication_state`, `severity_tier` and `category` and return rows `event_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; refresh reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `state` and `trigger_reason` and return rows `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

### 3.9. Payment Screening, the Wind-Down Cap and Orderly Closure

A payment instruction is submitted against a respondent by a relationship manager, by the respondent contact itself or by a platform administrator. The body requires `ordering_jurisdiction_code`, `beneficiary_jurisdiction_code`, `amount_usd` (strictly greater than 0) and `purpose_code` — one of `TRADE_SETTLEMENT`, `REMITTANCE`, `INTERBANK_PLACEMENT`, `RETURN_OF_FUNDS` or `SETTLEMENT`. It optionally carries `involves_nested_party` (defaulting to false) and `nested_relationship_id`. A submission against a respondent whose `payment_posture` is `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_PAYMENT_POSTURE_BLOCKED` and HTTP 403, evaluated before the screening ladder runs, so that outcome never reaches a screening branch. When `involves_nested_party` is true, `nested_relationship_id` must be present, must belong to this respondent and must reference a relationship in state `DISCLOSED` or `CONFIRMED`; otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_NESTED_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` and HTTP 422.

Screening then runs synchronously inside the creating request and produces exactly one outcome from a first-match ladder. Resolve the jurisdiction profiles of both `ordering_jurisdiction_code` and `beneficiary_jurisdiction_code`. If either has `sanctions_status` `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`, `screening_state` is `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` with `hold_reason` null — a blocked instruction is never a hold. Otherwise, if the respondent has no monitoring profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED` whose closed interval from `effective_from` to `effective_to` contains the logical clock, `screening_state` stays `RECEIVED` with `hold_reason` null: there is no threshold to screen against yet. Otherwise, working against that covering profile: if either jurisdiction has `sanctions_status` `RESTRICTED`, the outcome is `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `SANCTIONS_RESTRICTED_JURISDICTION`. Otherwise, if the respondent's `payment_posture` is `SCREEN_ONLY` or `WIND_DOWN_ONLY`, the outcome is `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `POSTURE_SCREEN_ONLY`. Otherwise, if `involves_nested_party` is true while `nested_relationship_id` is null or references a relationship in a state other than `DISCLOSED` or `CONFIRMED`, the outcome is `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `UNDISCLOSED_NESTED_PARTY`. Otherwise, if `amount_usd` is at least the profile's `hold_threshold_usd`, the outcome is `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `AMOUNT_THRESHOLD` and `hold_threshold_applied` written from that `hold_threshold_usd`; the comparison is inclusive, so an amount exactly equal to the threshold is held. Otherwise the outcome is `SCREENED_CLEAR` with `hold_reason` null, and `hold_threshold_applied` is written from the covering profile's `hold_threshold_usd`. Those last two branches are the only ones that write a threshold: every other outcome of a ladder run — `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS`, a `RECEIVED` left unscreened for want of a covering profile, and a `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` held for `SANCTIONS_RESTRICTED_JURISDICTION`, `POSTURE_SCREEN_ONLY` or `UNDISCLOSED_NESTED_PARTY` — leaves `hold_threshold_applied` at 0.00, which is also the value the field carries on a freshly inserted instruction. A cascade that later moves an instruction between screening states does not rewrite `hold_threshold_applied`.

Beyond that ladder, an instruction changes state only through the officer decision or through a cascade. The cascades are these: an instruction in `SCREENED_CLEAR` moves to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` with `hold_reason` `POSTURE_SCREEN_ONLY` when the respondent's posture leaves `OPEN` for `SCREEN_ONLY` or `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` in the same request; and an instruction in `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` moves to `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` with `hold_reason` cleared when one of its jurisdictions becomes comprehensively sanctioned or when the nested relationship it references becomes `PROHIBITED` with `prohibition_reason` `SANCTIONED_JURISDICTION`.

The screening decision is exclusive to a sanctions officer and requires `action` and `decision_reason`. `action` is one of `RELEASE`, `HOLD`, `RESCREEN` or `REJECT`. `decision_reason` is one of `CLEARED_AFTER_REVIEW`, `WIND_DOWN_PERMITTED`, `SANCTIONS_MATCH`, `UNRESOLVED_NESTED_RISK` or `RESPONDENT_RESTRICTED`. Every accepted decision writes `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the logical clock and `decision_reason`. A from-state with no declared transition for the action is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_STATE` and HTTP 409.

`RELEASE` is permitted from `SCREENED_CLEAR` and from `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and never from `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS`: a release attempted against a blocked instruction is refused with `ERR_SANCTIONED_PAYMENT_RELEASE_FORBIDDEN` and HTTP 403 for every role, the sanctions officer included, and rejection is the only exit from that state. A release requires the respondent's `payment_posture` to be `OPEN`, `SCREEN_ONLY` or `WIND_DOWN_ONLY`; a posture of `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_PAYMENT_POSTURE_BLOCKED` and HTTP 403. Releasing from `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` while `hold_reason` is `UNDISCLOSED_NESTED_PARTY` additionally requires the referenced relationship to have reached `CONFIRMED` in the meantime. When the posture is `WIND_DOWN_ONLY`, three further conditions hold: `purpose_code` must be `RETURN_OF_FUNDS` or `SETTLEMENT`; the logical clock must be no later than the live plan's `wind_down_end_at`; and that plan's `wind_down_released_usd` plus this instruction's `amount_usd` must be no greater than its `wind_down_cap_usd`, else the release is refused with `ERR_WIND_DOWN_CAP_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. A successful release moves the instruction to the terminal `RELEASED`, clears `hold_reason`, and — when the respondent has a plan in state `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `WIND_DOWN` — increases that plan's `wind_down_released_usd` by `amount_usd`.

`HOLD` moves an instruction parked in `RECEIVED` to `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`. `RESCREEN` re-runs the whole screening ladder from `RECEIVED` or from `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` and rewrites `screening_state`, `hold_reason` and `hold_threshold_applied` from its outcome. `REJECT` is terminal and is permitted from every non-terminal `screening_state`: from `HELD_FOR_REVIEW` it requires `decision_reason` to be `SANCTIONS_MATCH`, `UNRESOLVED_NESTED_RISK` or `RESPONDENT_RESTRICTED`; from `RECEIVED` the same three; and from `BLOCKED_SANCTIONS` it requires `decision_reason` to be exactly `SANCTIONS_MATCH`.

A closure plan is drafted by a relationship manager or a compliance analyst. The body requires `termination_reason` — one of `UNRESPONSIVE`, `SANCTIONS_EXPOSURE`, `PROHIBITED_NESTING`, `RISK_APPETITE`, `COMMERCIAL` or `REGULATORY_ACTION` — plus `cap_basis` and `original_obligation_cents`. `cap_basis` is `NONE`, `FORWARD_PROJECTED` or `CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL`. A respondent may hold at most one plan in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `WIND_DOWN`; a second is refused with `ERR_CLOSURE_PLAN_ALREADY_OPEN` and HTTP 409. The row is created in `DRAFT` with `drafted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, `outstanding_obligation_cents` equal to `original_obligation_cents`, `settled_cents` 0, `last_allocated_cents` 0, `allocation_round` 0, `allocation_lines` an empty array, `return_rounds` 0, `wind_down_released_usd` 0.00, and `notice_period_days` computed immediately. Drafting also moves the respondent to `dd_state` `EXITING` and `payment_posture` `WIND_DOWN_ONLY`.

The notice period is derived, never supplied, and its hard override is applied last. The base is 30 days for `termination_reason` `UNRESPONSIVE`, 0 for `SANCTIONS_EXPOSURE`, 15 for `PROHIBITED_NESTING`, 15 for `REGULATORY_ACTION`, 60 for `RISK_APPETITE` and 90 for `COMMERCIAL`. Add 15 days when the plan's `original_obligation_cents` is greater than 0 — the extension is decided from the obligation the plan opens with, not from the running balance. Subtract 15 days when the respondent's `risk_rating` is `PROHIBITED`. If the running figure is below 0, use 0. Then, and only then, if the respondent's home jurisdiction has `sanctions_status` `COMPREHENSIVELY_SANCTIONED`, set the figure to 0 regardless of everything above. Store the result in `notice_period_days`. A commercial exit with an obligation and a prohibited rating therefore carries 90 plus 15 minus 15, which is 90 days; the same exit from a comprehensively sanctioned home jurisdiction carries 0.

Submission moves the plan from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_APPROVAL`. It requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `drafted_by_user_id`, requires the respondent's `dd_state` to be none of `PROSPECT`, `DECLINED` or `TERMINATED`, and requires the obligation invariant to hold, namely `original_obligation_cents` equal to `outstanding_obligation_cents` plus `settled_cents`. Any other from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLOSURE_STATE` and HTTP 409.

Approval is exclusive to a committee approver and carries `action`, either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`; a `RETURN` action is also accepted from `PENDING_APPROVAL` and sends the plan back to `DRAFT`, incrementing `return_rounds` by 1 and requiring `return_rounds` to be no greater than 1 beforehand, so at most two returns are possible. Approval requires state `PENDING_APPROVAL`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `drafted_by_user_id` and from the respondent's `assessed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409, and requires `cap_basis` to be `FORWARD_PROJECTED` or `CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL`. `APPROVE` recomputes `notice_period_days`, computes and writes `wind_down_cap_usd` under the plan's `cap_basis`, writes `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and moves the plan to `APPROVED`; it also drives the respondent to `dd_state` `EXITING` with `payment_posture` `WIND_DOWN_ONLY` and cancels every cycle of that respondent in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` with `cancelled_reason` `RELATIONSHIP_EXITING`, processed `created_at` ascending. `REJECT` moves the plan to the terminal `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `COMMERCIAL_DECISION` and returns the respondent to its pre-exit `dd_state` with `payment_posture` `SCREEN_ONLY`.

The wind-down cap is a **dual-basis** calculation: the two bases can differ by cents on identical data. In both, first convert the notice period into months by dividing `notice_period_days` by 30.0, keeping full precision. Then take the base monthly figure according to `cap_basis`. Under `FORWARD_PROJECTED` the base is simply the respondent's declared `expected_monthly_volume_usd`, ignoring what actually flowed; this is the only basis permitted when the respondent has no activity observations at all. Under `CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL`, walk the respondent's activity observations across all of its monitoring profiles ordered `period_month` ascending then `created_at` ascending, take the last 3 distinct `period_month` values, and for each of those periods sum `aggregate_usd` across corridors and then cap that period total at 1.25 multiplied by the `aggregate_usd_threshold` of the profile that owns the period's rows, rounded to 2 decimal places; the base is the mean of the capped period totals, rounded to 2 decimal places, half up. A `CHRONOLOGICAL_ACTUAL` approval for a respondent with no observation rows at all is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACTIVITY_HISTORY` and HTTP 422 rather than falling back to the declared volume. Finally, multiply the base by the months and by 0.60, round to 2 decimal places, half up, and store as `wind_down_cap_usd`. With `notice_period_days` 90 and a base of 2000000.00, the cap is 2000000.00 times 3.0 times 0.60, which is 3600000.00.

Progression carries `action` — `SERVE_NOTICE`, `BEGIN_WIND_DOWN`, `CLOSE` or `ABORT` — plus an optional `abort_reason`. Implementations must also accept `START_WIND_DOWN` as an equivalent spelling of `BEGIN_WIND_DOWN`. `SERVE_NOTICE` requires state `APPROVED` and a non-null `notice_period_days`, requires that no information request of the respondent sits in `RESPONDED` awaiting adjudication, stamps `notice_served_at` with the logical clock and sets `wind_down_end_at` to `notice_served_at` plus `notice_period_days` days of 24 hours, and moves the plan to `NOTICE_SERVED`. Serving notice also terminates the relationship graph: every nested relationship of the respondent in `DISCLOSED`, `UNDISCLOSED_DETECTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED`, processed `created_at` ascending, so `nested_exposure_units` falls to 0.

`BEGIN_WIND_DOWN` requires state `NOTICE_SERVED`, requires the logical clock to be at or after `notice_served_at`, and requires every nested relationship of the respondent to be in `TERMINATED` or `PROHIBITED`; it moves the plan to `WIND_DOWN` and sets the respondent's `payment_posture` to `WIND_DOWN_ONLY`. `CLOSE` requires state `WIND_DOWN`, requires the logical clock to have reached `wind_down_end_at`, else `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` and HTTP 422, requires `outstanding_obligation_cents` to be exactly 0, else `ERR_OBLIGATION_OUTSTANDING` and HTTP 422, and requires that no payment instruction of the respondent sits in `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`. It stamps `closed_at`, moves the plan to the terminal `CLOSED`, moves the respondent to `dd_state` `TERMINATED` with `payment_posture` `BLOCKED`, and abandons every information request of the respondent in `DRAFT`, `SENT`, `RESPONDED`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, processed `created_at` ascending, with `escalation_units` recomputed to 0.

`ABORT` requires `abort_reason` — one of `RISK_RESOLVED`, `COMMERCIAL_DECISION` or `SUPERSEDED_PLAN` — and is permitted from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` and `APPROVED` but never once `NOTICE_SERVED` has been reached; after notice the relationship must run to `CLOSED`. From `APPROVED` it additionally requires that no payment instruction of the respondent sits in `HELD_FOR_REVIEW`, and it returns the respondent to `dd_state` `APPROVED`, or to `RESTRICTED` when the respondent was restricted before the exit began, with `payment_posture` `SCREEN_ONLY`. From `DRAFT` the respondent's `dd_state` is left untouched because it never entered `EXITING` through that path. A relationship may re-enter `EXITING` later with a fresh plan.

The settlement allocation capability is exclusive to a relationship manager, requires the plan to be in `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `WIND_DOWN`, and carries `action`, either `ALLOCATE` or `REVERSE`, plus `amount_cents`, an integer greater than 0.

`ALLOCATE` distributes `amount_cents` across the respondent's expected corridors, largest expected volume first. Take the `expected_corridors` of the respondent's profile in `calibration_state` `CALIBRATED`, or of its most recent profile when none is calibrated, and sort them by `expected_monthly_usd` descending with `jurisdiction_code` ascending as tie-break. Let the total expected be the sum of their `expected_monthly_usd`. For each corridor in that sorted order, the line amount is `amount_cents` multiplied by that corridor's `expected_monthly_usd`, divided by the total expected, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Round every line first; then compute the residue as `amount_cents` minus the sum of the rounded lines, and add that entire residue — which may be negative — to the **first** line in sort order. Distributing the residue across several lines is wrong. Write the resulting array into `allocation_lines`, each entry carrying `jurisdiction_code` and `line_cents`. Then add `amount_cents` to `settled_cents`, subtract it from `outstanding_obligation_cents`, set `last_allocated_cents` to `amount_cents` and increment `allocation_round` by 1. An `amount_cents` greater than `outstanding_obligation_cents` is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` and HTTP 422.

`REVERSE` is the exact inverse. It requires `allocation_round` to be at least 1, else `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_TO_REVERSE` and HTTP 422, and requires `amount_cents` to equal `last_allocated_cents` exactly, else `ERR_REVERSAL_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. It subtracts `amount_cents` from `settled_cents`, adds it back to `outstanding_obligation_cents`, decrements `allocation_round` by 1, sets `last_allocated_cents` to 0, and clears `allocation_lines` to an empty array when `allocation_round` reaches 0. Because `last_allocated_cents` is 0 afterwards, a second `REVERSE` immediately following the first is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` when it carries a positive amount and with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_TO_REVERSE` when no rounds remain. After every allocation and every reversal two invariants hold: `outstanding_obligation_cents` plus `settled_cents` equals `original_obligation_cents`, and `outstanding_obligation_cents` lies between 0 and `original_obligation_cents` inclusive.

As a worked example, allocating 100000 cents across corridors expecting 3000000.00, 2000000.00 and 1000000.00 — a total of 6000000.00 — gives raw lines of 50000, 33333.33 and 16666.67, which round to 50000, 33333 and 16667, summing to 100000, so the residue is 0 and the first line stays 50000. Allocating 100001 cents instead gives 50000.5, 33333.67 and 16666.83, which round to 50001, 33334 and 16667, summing to 100002, so the residue is −1 and the first line becomes 50000.

Payment-instruction reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `nested_relationship_id`, `screening_state`, `hold_reason` and `purpose_code` and are open to every role. Closure-plan reads filter on `respondent_bank_id`, `state` and `termination_reason`, are limited to the operator roles, and return rows `created_at` descending.
