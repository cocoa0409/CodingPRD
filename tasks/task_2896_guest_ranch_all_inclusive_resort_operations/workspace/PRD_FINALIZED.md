# Guest Ranch All-Inclusive Resort Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A western guest ranch does not sell rooms; it sells a week. Guests arrive on one fixed weekday, sleep in one cabin for the whole stay, eat every meal at a single seating in one dining room, and spend their days in the saddle, on the river, on the trail or in the children's programme. Everything is bundled into one all-inclusive price, and almost every operational decision — how many wranglers ride out, which horse carries which guest, whether the kitchen can absorb one more nut allergy, whether a cabin can be closed for a new roof — is a capacity decision made against a fixed physical plant. The ranch currently runs this on paper, and the paper does not reconcile: mounts get double-booked across back-to-back rides, riders whose ability was never re-assessed end up on steep terrain, seasonal staff leave in August and nobody knows what their retention bonus should be, and the folio at check-out never matches the quote taken at deposit.

This document specifies the API that replaces that paper. The system is the single record of the operating season and everything that hangs off it: the cabin inventory and its off-season capital projects, the guest register with its liability waivers and statutory equine-activity acknowledgements, rider ability assessments, the herd and its health history, activity slots with their guide-staffing ratios and mount requirements, the weekly package booking with its deposit and cancellation terms, the seasonal staff contracts with their on-site housing and end-of-season bonuses, and the incident reports that the ranch's insurer and the state equine activity statute both require.

Two properties matter more than any individual feature. The first is **conservation**: money and capacity that are committed must be released in exactly the amount committed. A cancelled seat returns precisely the surcharge it charged; a released mount returns precisely the scheduled hours it consumed; a refunded deposit plus the forfeited remainder equal the deposit that was collected, to the cent, always. The second is **safety propagation**: a single safety event — a lame horse, a revoked waiver, a guide who quits, an incident that requires remediation — must ripple outward through every seat, mount, slot and staffing ratio it touches in the same request, so that no ride ever departs against a stale picture of who is riding what. Most of the specification below exists to make those two properties true.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every rule in this section applies to every endpoint unless the endpoint's own narrative in §3 says otherwise. The API is mounted flat: each collection is a top-level path segment with no shared prefix.

### 2.1. Ranch Personnel, Guests and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is absent, empty, or contains only whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles the addressed endpoint permits, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `RanchManager`, `ReservationsAgent`, `HorseProgramDirector`, `ActivityGuide`, `Guest` and `SafetyOfficer`. Their remits are as follows. The `RanchManager` is the ranch's general manager and owns the season lifecycle, the cabin inventory, activity slot scheduling, guide staffing, seasonal hiring, staff housing and off-season capital projects. The `ReservationsAgent` staffs the reservations desk: guest records, waivers, package bookings, deposits and folios, activity seats, and front-desk check-in and check-out. The `HorseProgramDirector` is the head wrangler and owns the herd registry, herd health, rider ability assessment, and every mount assignment or release. An `ActivityGuide` is the seasonal guide actually working a slot: they run and complete the activity, check riders in at the trailhead, and file incident reports. A `Guest` acts for themself only. The `SafetyOfficer` is the safety and compliance officer who dispositions and closes incident reports and may restrict or suspend a guest.

Three of the six roles are backed by no persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `RanchManager`, `ReservationsAgent`, `HorseProgramDirector` or `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any stored entity; the server may record it — it stores the disposing officer's identifier in `disposed_by_user_id` and the closing officer's in `closed_by_user_id` on an incident report — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. The one place an opaque identifier is compared to anything is the segregation-of-duties rule: the `X-User-ID` that closes an incident report must differ from the `disposed_by_user_id` already recorded on it, and an attempt by the same identifier to do both is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ActivityGuide`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`; ownership checks compare that identifier to the `staff_member_id` of a guide assignment on the addressed slot, and the filing guide's identifier is copied into `reported_by_staff_member_id` when an incident report is created. A guide may only act on a slot for which they personally hold a guide assignment whose `assignment_status` is `SCHEDULED`; running a slot, completing a slot, checking a rider in at the trailhead, and filing an incident report against a seat on that slot are all refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_GUIDE` (HTTP 403) otherwise. When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guest`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's guest reference to that identifier. A guest reading their own guest record, signing their own waiver, or reading one of their own activity seats is permitted; addressing another guest's record, another guest's seat, or a seat whose `guest_id` is not their own is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The `Guest` role holds no exclusive write capability beyond signing its own waiver, and may read only a season, a booking, an activity slot, an activity seat, and its own guest record.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result set; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a field-value violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

Unless a list endpoint's own narrative states otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row appears first and the ordering is stable across pages. List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"`, and their `meta` object additionally contains `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective skip), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

List endpoints also accept domain filters, described alongside the workflows they serve in §3. Filters combine conjunctively: a row must match every supplied filter to appear in the result.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource the API creates carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers are opaque and carry no type prefix, no ranch code and no sequence number; clients must never construct one or infer meaning from its shape. Alongside `id`, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. A mutation for this purpose includes any change the server makes to a computed field: when a booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents` moves because one of its seats was cancelled, or when a slot's `confirmed_rider_count` moves because a seat was created, that is a mutation of the booking and of the slot respectively, and both `updated_at` and `version` advance. This applies to `Season`, `Cabin`, `Guest`, `RiderAssessment`, `Booking`, `Horse`, `ActivitySlot`, `GuideAssignment`, `ActivityReservation`, `StaffMember` and `IncidentReport`.

The one exception is the herd-health record. A `HorseHealthEvent` is an immutable care record: once filed it is never edited, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its response payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Ranch Clock

Every date and deadline rule in this specification reads a server-held logical UTC clock rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this is the mandated startup default and the server never falls back to the machine's real time. An operator sets the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and from that moment on every piece of date arithmetic in the system — a guest's `age_years`, the number of days out from an arrival date, a staff member's `days_worked`, waiver expiry, the gate that decides whether a slot may start, the calendar date stamped onto a horse's duty-day run — reads the new instant. All five operational roles may set the clock; a `Guest` may not.

Wherever this document says a field is "set from the clock", the value written is the logical clock's current instant, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Wherever it says "the clock date", the value used is the UTC calendar date of that instant. Date-only fields — birth dates, arrival and departure dates, contract dates, observation dates, waiver signature and expiry dates — are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form.

### 2.6. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in integer cents, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents and no decimal money fields; a request carrying a non-integer or negative amount where a cents field is expected is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. Percentages and fractions — `deposit_pct`, `buyout_premium_pct`, `child_discount_pct`, `cancellation_refund_pct` — are decimal numbers between 0 and 1 inclusive, and a value outside that band is likewise an invalid field value.

Wherever a calculation in §3 says an amount is rounded, the method is **round half up**: the fractional part is examined and a remainder of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Rounding is applied at the points the individual calculation names and at no others. This matters because several ranch calculations round more than once: the package price rounds each party member's line separately and then sums the rounded lines, which is not the same number as summing unrounded lines and rounding once at the end. Where a calculation names the rounding points, that order is normative.

Durations are expressed in hours as decimal numbers. A slot's planned `duration_hours` and its measured `actual_duration_hours` are rounded half up to two decimal places. A horse's accumulated `scheduled_load_hours` and `realized_load_hours` are rounded half up to one decimal place. Guide, ability-rank and guide-count arithmetic produces whole numbers: a required guide count is a whole number of guides, and where a count is derived from a fractional ratio it is rounded up to the next whole guide.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Contended Writes

Four writes in the system decide against a parent row whose counters move underneath them. Each of those four requires the client to pin the version of that parent row it last observed, and each rejects a pin that no longer matches with HTTP 412. Version pinning is **not optional on these four endpoints**: the pin field is a required input, and a request that omits it is rejected as a missing required field. No other endpoint in the system takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

- **Booking a seat on a slot.** A `POST /activity-slots/{slotId}/reservations` request must carry `expected_slot_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ActivitySlot`. Every accepted seat moves that slot's `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `required_guide_count`, `seats_available` and `readiness_posture`, so a client holding a stale view of the slot is deciding against an obsolete seat count and staffing ratio. If `expected_slot_version` does not equal the slot's stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SLOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the seat itself would otherwise have been a legitimate booking, and the stale pin is the only signal that the client's view is out of date.
- **Opening a rider assessment.** A `POST /guests/{guestId}/rider-assessments` request must carry `expected_guest_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Guest`. A new assessment supersedes the prior one and rewrites `effective_ability_rank`, so an assessment authored against a stale guest view would silently overwrite a rank that has already moved — for example by an incident remediation cap. A mismatch with the guest's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Assigning a named mount.** A `POST /activity-reservations/{reservationId}/mount-assignments` request must carry `expected_horse_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Horse` named in the request. Every accepted assignment or release moves `scheduled_load_hours` and may change `horse_status`, so a client with a stale view of the animal is allocating against an obsolete daily load. A mismatch with the horse's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HORSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Posting a folio adjustment.** A `POST /bookings/{bookingId}/folio-adjustments` request must carry `expected_booking_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Booking`. Adjustments are additive money entries that a second author may legitimately post as well, so nothing in the booking's state forbids the write; only a stale pin reveals that the author priced the adjustment against an obsolete folio. A mismatch with the booking's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

In all four cases the check compares the supplied pin against the *parent* row named by the rule — the slot, the guest, the horse, the booking — and not against the row being created.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing decides the response with HTTP 404. A request addressing an activity slot under a season, for instance, fails with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND` before the slot is even looked for. The eleven not-found codes map one-to-one onto the resource families: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CABIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HORSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUIDE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The same codes serve request bodies. Any identifier supplied inside a payload must resolve to a stored row before the request proceeds: `season_id`, `cabin_id`, `lead_guest_id`, each `guest_id` inside `party_members`, a seat's `booking_id` and `guest_id`, `horse_id` on a mount assignment or an incident report, and `staff_member_id` on a guide assignment. An unresolvable body reference produces the matching not-found code with HTTP 404 — a cabin that does not exist yields `ERR_CABIN_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown party member yields `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown mount yields `ERR_HORSE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown guide yields `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`.

Shape checks follow existence. Every field an endpoint declares as required must be present, or the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` (HTTP 400). Every field that is present must satisfy its declared type, its enum membership and its numeric range — dates in ISO 8601 form, cents as non-negative integers, percentages between 0 and 1 — or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (HTTP 422). Only after both of those pass does the system reach the cross-entity referential rule that a seat's booking must belong to the same season as its slot and that the named guest must appear in that booking's party roster; a violation there is `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

### 2.9. The Order in Which the Ranch Says No

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The response is decided by the **first** rule violated in the strict order below, and never by a later one. The ordering is normative: it is what makes a mid-flight safety change surface as a safety error rather than being masked by a capacity error, and it is what makes a wrong-state request report the state rather than the money. The list reads from highest precedence (evaluated first) to lowest.

1. `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty; on every request.
2. `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` — `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles; on every request.
3. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — the acting role is listed on the addressed endpoint; on every request.
4. `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — a `Guest` caller addresses only their own resources; on reading a guest record, signing a waiver, and reading an activity seat.
5. `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_GUIDE` — an `ActivityGuide` holds a `SCHEDULED` guide assignment on the addressed slot; on running a slot, completing a slot, checking a rider in, and filing an incident report.
6. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the closing `X-User-ID` differs from `disposed_by_user_id`; on closing an incident report.
7. The eleven not-found codes — every templated path identifier resolves, left to right; on every endpoint that has path parameters.
8. The body not-found codes — every foreign key in the payload resolves; on creating a booking, creating a guide assignment, assigning a named mount, and filing an incident report.
9. `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` — every declared required body field is present; on every write endpoint.
10. `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` — every supplied field satisfies its type, enum and range; on every write endpoint.
11. `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH` — the seat's booking shares the slot's season and the named guest is in that booking's party; on creating a seat.
12. `ERR_STALE_SLOT_VERSION` — `expected_slot_version` equals the slot's stored `version`; on creating a seat.
13. `ERR_STALE_GUEST_VERSION` — `expected_guest_version` equals the guest's stored `version`; on opening a rider assessment.
14. `ERR_STALE_HORSE_VERSION` — `expected_horse_version` equals the horse's stored `version`; on assigning a named mount.
15. `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` — `expected_booking_version` equals the booking's stored `version`; on posting a folio adjustment.
16. `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION` — the requested season step is one of the four legal steps; on advancing a season.
17. `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` — the governing season permits the write; on creating a booking, confirming a booking, checking a booking in, scheduling an activity slot, opening a slot, and running a slot.
18. `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID` — the booking is in the state the operation requires; on confirming, cancelling, checking in, checking out, and posting a folio adjustment. This state gate is deliberately evaluated **before** any money check on the same request.
19. `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID` — the slot is in the state the operation requires; on opening, running, completing, cancelling a slot, and on creating a seat.
20. `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID` — the seat's `reservation_status` and `mount_status` permit the operation; on rider check-in, seat cancellation, named mount assignment, mount auto-assignment, and mount release.
21. `ERR_CABIN_NOT_READY` — the cabin is `READY`; on booking check-in and on placing a maintenance hold.
22. `ERR_STAFF_STATE_INVALID` — the staff member's `employment_status` permits the operation and is not already terminal; on onboarding, separating, and completing a season.
23. `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID` — the assessment's `assessment_status` permits the operation; on evaluating and on flagging for re-evaluation.
24. `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID` — the report's `incident_status` permits the operation; on disposing and on closing an incident report.
25. `ERR_HORSE_NOT_IN_SERVICE` — the horse is `IN_SERVICE`; on named mount assignment and on mount auto-assignment.
26. `ERR_GUEST_SUSPENDED` — the guest's `guest_standing` is not `SUSPENDED`; on creating a seat and on rider check-in.
27. `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED` — the guest's waiver resolves to `SIGNED`; on creating a seat and on rider check-in.
28. `ERR_EQUINE_STATUTE_ACK_REQUIRED` — `equine_statute_ack` is true before the guest may hold a seat on a mounted slot; on creating a seat and on rider check-in.
29. `ERR_GUARDIAN_SIGNATURE_REQUIRED` — a minor's waiver names an adult guardian guest with a live waiver; on recording a waiver signature.
30. `ERR_RIDER_ABILITY_INSUFFICIENT` — the guest's `effective_ability_rank` reaches the slot's `min_ability_rank`; on creating a seat. This safety gate is deliberately evaluated **before** any capacity or duplicate check on the same request.
31. `ERR_RIDER_WEIGHT_EXCEEDS_MOUNT` — the rider's `weight_lb` is within the horse's `weight_limit_lb`; on named mount assignment.
32. `ERR_MOUNT_ABILITY_MISMATCH` — the rider's rank lies inside the horse's ability band; on named mount assignment.
33. `ERR_MOUNT_DUTY_LIMIT` — the horse's `consecutive_duty_days` is below 5; on named mount assignment.
34. `ERR_MOUNT_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED` — the horse's scheduled hours for that calendar date stay within the daily ceiling; on named mount assignment.
35. `ERR_MOUNT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` — the horse's other held seats respect the recovery buffer; on named mount assignment.
36. `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOUNT` — the eligible set is non-empty; on mount auto-assignment.
37. `ERR_GUIDE_RATIO_UNMET` — `assigned_guide_count` reaches `required_guide_count`; on opening and on running a slot.
38. `ERR_MOUNTS_INCOMPLETE` — a mounted slot has a mount for every confirmed rider; on running a slot.
39. `ERR_DIETARY_BRIEFING_REQUIRED` — `dietary_briefing_ack` is true when the party declares a restriction; on booking check-in.
40. `ERR_STATUTE_NOTICE_REQUIRED` — the statutory equine notice acknowledgement is present; on filing an incident report and again on disposing it.
41. `ERR_ARRIVAL_DAY_INVALID` — `arrival_date` falls on the season's `arrival_weekday`; on creating a booking.
42. `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_INVALID` — the stay length lies inside the season's stay bounds; on creating a booking.
43. `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON` — the stay window lies inside the season's operating dates; on creating a booking.
44. `ERR_CABIN_DOUBLE_BOOKED` — no other live booking of the same cabin overlaps the stay interval; on creating a booking.
45. `ERR_BUYOUT_CONFLICT` — buyout exclusivity holds in both directions; on creating a booking.
46. `ERR_GUIDE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` — the guide has no overlapping duty and stays inside the daily hours ceiling; on creating a guide assignment.
47. `ERR_GUEST_SLOT_CONFLICT` — the guest holds no other live seat on an overlapping slot; on creating a seat.
48. `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_SEASON` — the slot interval lies inside the season window and ends after it starts; on scheduling an activity slot.
49. `ERR_SLOT_NOT_STARTED` — the clock has reached the slot's `starts_at`; on running a slot.
50. `ERR_CONTRACT_DATE_INVALID` — the clock date sits on the correct side of `hired_on` or `contract_end_date`; on onboarding, separating, and completing a season.
51. `ERR_MEMBER_TYPE_MISMATCH` — each roster entry's `member_type` agrees with the referenced guest's age; on creating a booking.
52. `ERR_LEAD_GUEST_NOT_IN_PARTY` — `lead_guest_id` appears in the roster and the roster holds at least one adult; on creating a booking.
53. `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTY_MEMBER` — roster guest identifiers are unique; on creating a booking.
54. `ERR_PACKAGE_TIER_UNKNOWN` — `package_tier_code` names a tier the season publishes; on creating a booking.
55. `ERR_CABIN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the party fits the cabin's `bed_capacity`; on creating a booking.
56. `ERR_SLOT_ON_HOLD` — the slot is not on `HOLD`; on creating a seat.
57. `ERR_SLOT_FULL` — the slot has a seat left; on creating a seat. This aggregate capacity check is evaluated **only after every safety gate above has passed**.
58. `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESERVATION` — the guest holds no live seat on this exact slot already; on creating a seat.
59. `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` — the guide is `ACTIVE`, belongs to the slot's season, and holds the certification the activity demands; on creating a guide assignment.
60. `ERR_HOUSING_BUNK_TAKEN` — the requested bunk is free; on onboarding a staff member.
61. `ERR_GUIDE_ASSIGNMENTS_OPEN` — no guide assignment of the staff member is still `SCHEDULED`; on completing a season for a staff member.
62. `ERR_DINING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the party fits the remaining single-seating dining capacity; on booking check-in.
63. `ERR_OPEN_ACTIVITY_RESERVATIONS` — no seat of the booking is still live on a live slot; on booking check-out.
64. `ERR_SEASON_HAS_LIVE_BOOKINGS` — no booking of the season is still `CHECKED_IN`; on advancing a season.
65. `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT` — the payment amount and kind are valid; on posting a payment.
66. `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` — the deposit requirement is met; on confirming a booking. This money gate sits deliberately **below** the booking state gate at rule 18, so a booking that is not `HELD` reports its state and not its deposit.
67. `ERR_FOLIO_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` — the folio settles to exactly zero; on booking check-out.
68. `ERR_CAPITAL_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` — the capital project fits inside the unspent reserve; on placing a maintenance hold.

The conditions summarised above are specified in full, with their business context, in §3. Where a rule appears in more than one operation, it applies with the same error identifier in every one of them.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and the Conservation Rule

Many ranch operations touch more than one row. A cascade is not an eventual, background reconciliation: every effect described in §3 for a given operation lands inside the same request, before the response is written, and the response reflects the fully settled state. If any part of the cascade cannot be applied, nothing is applied.

Cascades in this system are **conserving**. When a committed quantity is reversed, the amount reversed is exactly the amount committed, not a recomputed approximation. A cancelled activity seat writes its own `surcharge_cents` into `surcharge_reversed_cents` and subtracts that same figure from the parent booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents`, returning it to the value it held before the seat existed. A released mount subtracts from the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` exactly the `duration_hours` its slot contributed, restoring the pre-assignment figure. A cabin returned from an off-season project releases from the season's `capital_committed_cents` exactly the `maintenance_capital_cost_cents` that project committed. A cancelled deposit splits into a refunded part and a forfeited part whose sum equals the deposit collected, to the cent.

Cascades propagate along a fixed chain. A safety event on a guest or a horse cancels or releases seats; each cancelled or released seat forces its parent activity slot to recompute `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `assigned_guide_count`, `required_guide_count`, `seats_available` and `readiness_posture`; and any slot that was `OPEN` and whose recomputed posture is no longer clear moves to `HOLD` with a `hold_reason` naming the defect. Each row touched in the chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, including parent rows whose only change was a recomputed counter.

### 2.11. Season Gating

The `Season` is the fiscal and operational envelope for everything the ranch does. Its `season_status` moves through `PLANNING`, `OPEN`, `IN_SEASON`, `CLOSED` and `OFF_SEASON`, and that status gates writes elsewhere in the system. While a season is in `PLANNING`, no booking may be held against it and no activity slot may be opened. Holding a booking, confirming a booking, scheduling an activity slot and opening a slot all require the governing season to be `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON`. Checking a party into a cabin, running a slot, completing a slot and checking a rider in at the trailhead all require the season to be `IN_SEASON` specifically. Once the season is `CLOSED`, new bookings, new seats and slot openings for that season are refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. Cabin maintenance holds and capital project commitments become permitted only once the season reaches `CLOSED` or `OFF_SEASON`.

The same envelope reaches into the readiness of individual activity slots: whenever the governing season is in any status other than `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON`, every slot of that season reports a `readiness_posture` of `FROZEN`, which outranks every other posture the slot might otherwise report.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Operating Season and Its Package Terms

Everything begins with a season. A `RanchManager` opens the ranch's books for a year by registering a season with a human label in `name`, the single weekday on which every package stay may begin in `arrival_weekday` (one of `MONDAY`, `TUESDAY`, `WEDNESDAY`, `THURSDAY`, `FRIDAY`, `SATURDAY`, `SUNDAY`), and the calendar window guests may occupy cabins, given as `season_start_date` and `season_end_date`. Stay length is bounded by `min_stay_nights`, which defaults to 7 and must be at least 1, and `max_stay_nights`, which defaults to 21 and must be greater than or equal to `min_stay_nights`. The commercial terms follow: `deposit_pct` is the fraction of the quoted package price a party must pay before its booking can be confirmed and defaults to 0.30, and must be greater than 0 and no greater than 1; `buyout_premium_pct` is the premium added when a party takes the whole ranch and defaults to 0.15; `no_show_penalty_cents` is the flat charge billed at check-out for each activity seat the party failed to show up for and defaults to 4500; `retention_bonus_cents` is the full end-of-season bonus payable to one seasonal staff member who sees their contract out and defaults to 120000. Finally, `dining_seat_capacity` is the number of seats in the dining room and must be at least 1 — because the ranch serves one seating, that number is a hard ceiling on how many guests may be in house at once.

The all-inclusive price list lives in `package_tiers`, an array of at least one element in which each element carries a `code` that must be unique within the season, an `adult_nightly_rate_cents`, a `child_discount_pct` giving the fraction taken off the adult rate for a child in the party, and an `included_activity_credit_cents` giving the value of activity surcharges the tier absorbs at check-out. A season is created in `PLANNING` with every rollup counter — `gross_package_revenue_cents`, `retention_bonus_liability_cents`, `net_surplus_cents`, `capital_reserve_cents`, `capital_committed_cents`, `bed_nights_sold` and `in_house_guest_count` — initialised to 0. Seasons can be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `season_status`.

The season lifecycle advances one step at a time. A `RanchManager` names the next status in `target_status`, and only four steps exist: `PLANNING → OPEN`, `OPEN → IN_SEASON`, `IN_SEASON → CLOSED` and `CLOSED → OFF_SEASON`. Any other value, any step out of order, and any repeat of a step already applied is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. The move to `OPEN` additionally requires the season to publish at least one package tier, and once it lands, bookings and slot openings become permitted. The move to `IN_SEASON` opens the operational gates: booking check-in, slot running and slot completion all become available. The move to `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_SEASON_HAS_LIVE_BOOKINGS` while any booking of the season is still `CHECKED_IN` — in-house guests cannot be closed out from under. The move to `OFF_SEASON` is available only once every staff member of the season has reached a terminal employment status, either `COMPLETED_SEASON` or `DEPARTED_EARLY`; while any contract is still `HIRED` or `ACTIVE` the step is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEASON_TRANSITION`. Reaching `OFF_SEASON` is what unlocks cabin maintenance holds and capital project commitments. Each accepted step increments the season's `version` by 1.

The `IN_SEASON → CLOSED` step is also the ranch's annual accounting event, and it computes four figures in order. `gross_package_revenue_cents` becomes the sum of `final_folio_total_cents` over every booking of this season whose `booking_status` is `CHECKED_OUT`; bookings that are `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. `retention_bonus_liability_cents` becomes the sum of `retention_bonus_settled_cents` over every staff member of this season whose `employment_status` is `COMPLETED_SEASON` or `DEPARTED_EARLY`. `net_surplus_cents` becomes the gross package revenue less the retention bonus liability less the season's `capital_committed_cents`, and it may be negative. `capital_reserve_cents` becomes one quarter of the net surplus, rounded half up to the nearest cent, where a negative net surplus is first floored at zero so the reserve is never negative. The reserve computed here is the entire budget the ranch has to spend on off-season capital work.

### 3.2. Cabins, Housekeeping Turnover and Off-Season Capital Projects

Cabins outlive seasons and are shared across them, so they are registered once by a `RanchManager` with a `name` that must be unique across the ranch, compared case-sensitively, and a `bed_capacity` of at least 1 that caps the party size the cabin can sleep. A new cabin starts `READY`, holds no project — `maintenance_project_code` is null and `maintenance_capital_cost_cents` is 0 — and has never had a turnover, so `last_turnover_completed_at` is null. Cabins may be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `cabin_status`.

Occupancy is not something a housekeeper sets directly; it follows the booking. When a party checks in, the cabin named on their booking moves `READY → OCCUPIED` and the season's `in_house_guest_count` rises by the party's size. When the party checks out, the cabin moves `OCCUPIED → TURNOVER` and the season's `in_house_guest_count` falls by the same size. A cabin in `TURNOVER` is not available to the next party until housekeeping reports the turnover finished, which moves it `TURNOVER → READY` and stamps `last_turnover_completed_at` with the logical clock instant. Attempting to check a party into a cabin that is not `READY` — because it is still `OCCUPIED`, awaiting turnover, or out of service — is refused with `ERR_CABIN_NOT_READY`.

Off-season capital work is the ranch's other use for a cabin, and it is deliberately gated on money that has actually been earned. A `RanchManager` places a maintenance hold by naming the funding season in `season_id`, the project in `maintenance_project_code`, and its cost in `maintenance_capital_cost_cents`. Four conditions must hold. The cabin must be `READY`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CABIN_NOT_READY`. The named season must have reached `CLOSED` or `OFF_SEASON` — capital work cannot start while guests are still arriving — and a season still in `PLANNING`, `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON` yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. The project's cost must fit inside what the season has left: the requested cost may be no greater than the season's `capital_reserve_cents` less its `capital_committed_cents`, and a project that would push the committed total past the reserve is refused with `ERR_CAPITAL_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`. And no booking on this cabin whose `booking_status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` may have an `arrival_date` on or after the funding season's `season_start_date`, since a cabin under construction cannot receive next year's guests; such a conflict is refused with `ERR_CABIN_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. A hold that passes all four moves the cabin `READY → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, records the project code and its cost on the cabin, adds that cost to the season's `capital_committed_cents`, and increments both the cabin's `version` and the season's `version` by 1.

Returning a cabin to service is the exact reverse. The capability accepts a cabin that is either in `TURNOVER` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and names the funding season in `season_id`. For a cabin coming back from a capital project, the season's `capital_committed_cents` is reduced by exactly the `maintenance_capital_cost_cents` this hold committed — not by a recomputed figure — the `maintenance_project_code` is cleared, `maintenance_capital_cost_cents` is reset to 0, the cabin moves `OUT_OF_SERVICE → READY`, `last_turnover_completed_at` is stamped from the clock, and both the cabin's `version` and the season's `version` increment by 1.

### 3.3. Guest Records, Liability Waivers and Guest Standing

A guest record is deliberately thin: a `full_name`, a `birth_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and a `weight_lb` in pounds that must be greater than zero. Both of the latter two are safety inputs rather than trivia. From `birth_date` the system derives `age_years`, the whole number of years between the birth date and the current clock date with any part-year discarded, and that number decides whether the guest is a minor for waiver purposes, whether they may be listed as an adult on a booking roster, and whether their presence on a ride raises the guide requirement. From `weight_lb` the system decides which horses can carry them. A new guest starts with `guest_standing` of `GOOD`, a `waiver_status` of `NONE`, an `equine_statute_ack` of false, no guardian in `waiver_signed_by_guardian_guest_id`, and both `current_ability_rank` and `effective_ability_rank` at `UNASSESSED`. Guests may be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `guest_standing` or by `waiver_signed_by_guardian_guest_id` to find every minor a given guardian signed for.

**Waiver posture is derived, never stored directly.** Three stored fields feed it — `waiver_signed_on`, `waiver_expires_on` and `waiver_revoked_at` — and `waiver_status` is resolved from them on every read of the guest and again at every safety check, using the first matching clause of the following ordered test: the status is `NONE` when `waiver_signed_on` is null; otherwise `REVOKED` when `waiver_revoked_at` is not null; otherwise `EXPIRED` when the current clock date is strictly later than `waiver_expires_on`; otherwise `SIGNED`. Because the expiry test is strict, a clock date falling exactly on `waiver_expires_on` still resolves to `SIGNED` — the waiver lapses at the end of that day. Expiry is a pure derivation: the day the clock crosses the expiry date the guest's posture moves `SIGNED → EXPIRED` with no stored field changing at all.

Recording a signature takes the date of signature in `signed_on` and the statutory acknowledgement in `equine_statute_ack`, and may name a guardian in `waiver_signed_by_guardian_guest_id`. The acknowledgement is the ranch's compliance with the state equine activity statute and must be supplied as true; a signature that does not acknowledge the statute is refused with `ERR_EQUINE_STATUTE_ACK_REQUIRED`. From the supplied signature date the system computes `waiver_expires_on` as that date plus 365 days, sets `waiver_signed_on`, records `equine_statute_ack`, clears any `waiver_revoked_at`, and increments the guest's `version` by 1. Because the signature capability always writes a fresh signature-and-expiry pair and clears the revocation stamp, it is the single door back to `SIGNED` from `NONE`, from `EXPIRED` and from `REVOKED` alike. A guest whose `age_years` is below 18 may not sign for themself: the request must name a guardian in `waiver_signed_by_guardian_guest_id` that references another guest whose own `age_years` is at least 18 and whose own waiver resolves to `SIGNED`, and a minor's signature that names no such guardian is refused with `ERR_GUARDIAN_SIGNATURE_REQUIRED`. Either a `ReservationsAgent` or the guest themself may record a signature; a guest acting for another guest is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Revocation is the ranch's emergency brake, and it is loud. A `RanchManager` revokes a waiver with a `reason`; `waiver_revoked_at` is stamped from the logical clock, so the guest's posture resolves to `REVOKED` from that instant. The revocation then cascades in three further steps inside the same request. First, every activity seat of that guest whose `reservation_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose slot has not yet started — that is, whose slot `starts_at` is strictly later than the current clock instant — becomes `CANCELLED`, with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to that seat's own `surcharge_cents`. Second, each of those cancelled seats that was holding a mount moves its `mount_status` from `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED`, clears `assigned_horse_id`, and the affected horse's `scheduled_load_hours` is recomputed. Third, each affected slot recomputes `confirmed_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `required_guide_count` and `readiness_posture`, and any slot that was `OPEN` and whose recomputed posture is now `MOUNT_SHORT` or `UNDERSTAFFED` moves to `HOLD`. The guest's `version` increments by 1. Rides that have already departed are untouched.

**Standing is the second, independent dimension on a guest.** `guest_standing` is one of `GOOD`, `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED`, and it evolves without reference to the waiver dimension: a guest may hold a perfectly live waiver and still be barred from riding. A `RanchManager` or a `SafetyOfficer` may move a guest out of `GOOD` directly by naming the destination in `target_standing` together with a `reason`; the same two destinations are also reached automatically by incident handling, described in §3.10. Whenever a guest leaves `GOOD`, three things follow. The guest's `effective_ability_rank` is recomputed — collapsing to `UNASSESSED` for a `SUSPENDED` guest and capped at `BEGINNER` for a `RESTRICTED` one. Every future activity seat of the guest that the new standing no longer supports becomes `CANCELLED` with its surcharge reversed: for a suspension, every seat of theirs in `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` on a slot that has not yet started; for a restriction, every future seat whose slot demands an ability above `BEGINNER`. And each such seat releases any mount it held, so that every parent slot recomputes its counters, its `required_guide_count` and its `readiness_posture`, with an `OPEN` slot whose posture degraded moving to `HOLD`. The guest's `version` increments by 1.

Reinstatement, performed by a `RanchManager` with a `reason`, returns a `SUSPENDED` or `RESTRICTED` guest to `GOOD`, lifts the rank cap the prior standing imposed so that `effective_ability_rank` is recomputed from the assessment record alone, and increments the guest's `version` by 1. Reinstatement is forward-looking only: seats that the suspension or restriction cancelled are **not** restored, and the party must book them again.

### 3.4. Rider Ability Assessment and the Rank Ladder

No guest rides above their ability. Ability is expressed on a five-step ladder — `UNASSESSED`, then `BEGINNER`, `NOVICE`, `INTERMEDIATE` and `ADVANCED` in increasing order — and every comparison the ranch makes about ability is a comparison of positions on that ladder, with `UNASSESSED` occupying the lowest position, below `BEGINNER`.

The `HorseProgramDirector` opens an assessment for a guest by recording the level the guest claims in `requested_ability`, which must be one of `BEGINNER`, `NOVICE`, `INTERMEDIATE` or `ADVANCED`, together with the `expected_guest_version` pin described in §2.7. The new assessment is created `PENDING` with `assessed_ability`, `assessed_by_note`, `evaluated_at` and `re_evaluation_reason` all null, and it takes over as the guest's only non-terminal assessment: every prior assessment of that guest whose `assessment_status` is `EVALUATED` or `RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Opening an assessment increments the guest's `version` by 1. Assessments may be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `guest_id` or by `assessment_status`.

Out in the arena the director evaluates the rider and records the verdict as `assessed_ability`, an evaluator note in `assessed_by_note`, and the instant of the evaluation in `evaluated_at`. Evaluation is accepted on an assessment that is `PENDING` — moving it `PENDING → EVALUATED` — and on one that is `RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED`, moving it `RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED → EVALUATED`, in which case the newly supplied verdict, note and instant overwrite the previous ones and `re_evaluation_reason` is cleared. A `SUPERSEDED` assessment accepts no transition at all, and any evaluation attempt against an assessment in a status other than those two is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Every accepted evaluation rewrites the guest's `current_ability_rank` and `effective_ability_rank` and increments the guest's `version` by 1.

If a guide watches a rider in the saddle and concludes the paperwork is optimistic, either the `HorseProgramDirector` or an `ActivityGuide` may flag the assessment for re-evaluation with a `re_evaluation_reason`. This is accepted only on an assessment that is `EVALUATED`, moving it `EVALUATED → RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED`; any other starting status is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Flagging caps the guest's effective ability at `NOVICE` and then, inside the same request, cancels every seat that cap has invalidated: each seat of the guest whose `reservation_status` is `CONFIRMED`, whose slot has not yet started, and whose slot demands an ability above `NOVICE` becomes `CANCELLED` with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to that seat's `surcharge_cents`, each such seat releases any mount it was holding, and each parent slot recomputes its rider counts, its `required_guide_count` and its `readiness_posture`. The guest's `version` increments by 1. The cycle `EVALUATED → RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED → EVALUATED` may repeat without limit, and each return to `EVALUATED` lifts the `NOVICE` cap again.

**How the two rank fields are derived.** Both are recomputed whenever an assessment is opened or evaluated, whenever incident handling changes a guest's standing, and on every read of the guest. The starting point is the guest's most recent verdict: among the guest's assessments whose `assessment_status` is `EVALUATED`, the one with the greatest `evaluated_at` is selected, ties being broken by the earlier `created_at`; its `assessed_ability` becomes `current_ability_rank`. If the guest has no `EVALUATED` assessment at all, `current_ability_rank` is `UNASSESSED`. `effective_ability_rank` then starts from that value and has up to three caps applied in this exact order, each of which can only ever lower the rank and never raise it. First, if the guest holds any assessment whose `assessment_status` is `RE_EVALUATION_REQUESTED`, the rank is lowered to `NOVICE` if it stands above `NOVICE`. Second, if the guest's `guest_standing` is `RESTRICTED`, the rank is lowered to `BEGINNER` if it stands above `BEGINNER`. Third, if the guest's `guest_standing` is `SUSPENDED`, the rank collapses to `UNASSESSED` outright. Assessments that are `SUPERSEDED` are ignored when choosing the most recent verdict, and — importantly — a `SUPERSEDED` assessment no longer imposes the re-evaluation cap either, so opening a fresh assessment for a flagged guest lifts that cap even before the new assessment is evaluated.

### 3.5. Holding, Pricing and Confirming a Weekly Package

A `ReservationsAgent` holds a stay by naming the funding season in `season_id`, the cabin in `cabin_id`, the billing contact in `lead_guest_id`, the price list in `package_tier_code`, the stay window in `arrival_date` and `departure_date`, and the travelling party in `party_members`. A party may optionally take the entire ranch by setting `is_buyout` to true; when the flag is omitted it defaults to false. The `party_members` array is the family group roster and must hold between 1 and 12 elements; each element carries a `guest_id` referencing a registered guest, a `member_type` of either `ADULT` or `CHILD`, and a `dietary_restrictions` array whose members are drawn from `NONE`, `VEGETARIAN`, `GLUTEN_FREE`, `DAIRY_FREE` and `NUT_ALLERGY`. From the roster the system computes `party_size` as the number of elements, `adult_count` as the number of elements whose `member_type` is `ADULT`, and `child_count` as the number whose `member_type` is `CHILD`; from the window it computes `nights` as the whole number of days from `arrival_date` to `departure_date`. A new booking is created with `booking_status` of `HELD`, `payment_status` of `AWAITING_DEPOSIT`, `dietary_briefing_ack` false, `no_show_count` and every money counter at 0, `realized_nights` at 0, and `checked_in_at`, `checked_out_at` and `cancelled_at` all null. Bookings may be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `season_id`, `cabin_id`, `lead_guest_id` or `booking_status`.

The weekly cadence is enforced strictly. The `arrival_date` must fall on the weekday the season names in `arrival_weekday`, and an arrival on any other weekday is refused with `ERR_ARRIVAL_DAY_INVALID`. The computed `nights` must be at least the season's `min_stay_nights` and at most its `max_stay_nights`, and a stay outside that band is refused with `ERR_STAY_LENGTH_INVALID`. The `arrival_date` must be on or after the season's `season_start_date` and the `departure_date` on or before its `season_end_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STAY_OUTSIDE_SEASON`.

**Cabin occupancy is a half-open interval.** A booking occupies its cabin from `arrival_date` inclusive up to but not including `departure_date`. Two bookings on the same cabin conflict exactly when the candidate's arrival falls strictly before the existing booking's departure *and* the existing booking's arrival falls strictly before the candidate's departure; consequently one party departing on the very day another arrives is not a conflict, while a single overlapping night is. The bookings that hold an interval are those whose `booking_status` is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN`; a `CANCELLED` or `CHECKED_OUT` booking has released its interval and never blocks a new one. A conflict is refused with `ERR_CABIN_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Buyout exclusivity is tested over the same half-open interval but against **every** cabin in the season rather than the requested one: a booking with `is_buyout` true is refused if any live booking touches any cabin over its interval, and any ordinary booking is refused if its interval falls inside a live buyout's interval. Either direction is `ERR_BUYOUT_CONFLICT`.

The roster is then checked against the guest register and the cabin. Each element's `member_type` must agree with the referenced guest's `age_years`: an `ADULT` element requires the guest to be at least 18 years old and a `CHILD` element requires the guest to be under 18, and a disagreement is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The `lead_guest_id` must itself appear among the roster's `guest_id` values and the roster must hold at least one `ADULT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LEAD_GUEST_NOT_IN_PARTY`. No `guest_id` may appear twice in the roster; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PARTY_MEMBER`. The `package_tier_code` must name a tier the season publishes, or the request is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_TIER_UNKNOWN`. And the computed `party_size` may not exceed the cabin's `bed_capacity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CABIN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Pricing the hold.** A booking that survives all of the above is priced immediately, and the result is written to `provisional_package_total_cents`. The tier row named by `package_tier_code` supplies the rates. The length-of-stay discount band is chosen from the same `nights` figure used everywhere else in the calculation: a stay of seven nights or fewer earns no discount, a stay of eight through thirteen nights earns a 5 percent discount, and a stay of fourteen nights or more earns an 8 percent discount. Each party member is then priced on their own line. An `ADULT` member's nightly figure is the tier's `adult_nightly_rate_cents` exactly. A `CHILD` member's nightly figure is the tier's `adult_nightly_rate_cents` multiplied by one minus the tier's `child_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. That member's line is then their nightly figure multiplied by `nights` and by one minus the length-of-stay discount fraction, again rounded half up to whole cents. **The per-member rounding, and the fact that it happens before any summation, is normative**: the subtotal is the sum of the already-rounded member lines, and summing unrounded lines and rounding once at the end produces a different and incorrect figure. When `is_buyout` is true, a buyout premium equal to the subtotal multiplied by the season's `buyout_premium_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, is added to the subtotal; when it is false the premium is zero. The package total is the subtotal plus that premium, and it is written to `provisional_package_total_cents`. Finally `deposit_required_cents` is set to the package total multiplied by the season's `deposit_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. At hold time no gratuity, activity credit, surcharge or no-show component exists yet — the stay has not happened.

Whenever the roster changes, the hold is re-priced by the same procedure, so `provisional_package_total_cents` and `deposit_required_cents` always reflect the party currently on the booking.

**Taking the money.** A `ReservationsAgent` posts money against the folio by supplying a `kind` and an `amount_cents`. The `amount_cents` must be a positive integer and the `kind` must be either `DEPOSIT` or `SETTLEMENT`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT`. A `DEPOSIT` payment accumulates into `deposit_paid_cents`; a `SETTLEMENT` payment accumulates into `payments_applied_cents`. Payments are accepted while the booking is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`. The money dimension of a booking, `payment_status`, is entirely independent of its occupancy dimension: it starts at `AWAITING_DEPOSIT`, and the moment `deposit_paid_cents` reaches or passes `deposit_required_cents` it moves `AWAITING_DEPOSIT → DEPOSIT_PAID`. Each accepted payment increments the booking's `version` by 1.

Confirmation is the reservations desk's commitment. It requires no payload. The booking must be `HELD`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`; the governing season must be `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`; and `deposit_paid_cents` must have reached `deposit_required_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402). Because the state gate outranks the money gate, a booking that has already been cancelled or checked in reports its state and not its deposit. A successful confirmation moves `booking_status` from `HELD` to `CONFIRMED`, adds the booking's bed-nights — its `party_size` multiplied by its `nights` — to the season's `bed_nights_sold`, and increments both the booking's `version` and the season's `version` by 1. The season's `bed_nights_sold` counts every booking whose `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `CHECKED_OUT`, so a cancellation after confirmation removes the same bed-night figure it added.

### 3.6. Arrival, the Single Seating, and Settling the Folio

Because the ranch serves one seating in one dining room, arrival is a ranch-wide capacity event rather than a cabin-level one. A `ReservationsAgent` checks a party in by supplying `dietary_briefing_ack`, the kitchen's confirmation that it has been briefed on the party's requirements. Five conditions gate the arrival. The booking must be `CONFIRMED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. The season must be `IN_SEASON`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. The cabin must be `READY`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CABIN_NOT_READY`. The season's current `in_house_guest_count` plus this booking's `party_size` may not exceed the season's `dining_seat_capacity`, and a party that would overfill the seating is refused with `ERR_DINING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — this is a hard ranch-wide aggregate, not a per-cabin one. Finally, whenever any element of the party roster declares a `dietary_restrictions` value other than `NONE`, `dietary_briefing_ack` must be supplied as true, since a single seating cannot absorb an unannounced allergy; an unacknowledged briefing is refused with `ERR_DIETARY_BRIEFING_REQUIRED`. A successful arrival moves `booking_status` from `CONFIRMED` to `CHECKED_IN`, stamps `checked_in_at` from the logical clock, records the `dietary_briefing_ack` flag on the booking, moves the cabin `READY → OCCUPIED`, raises the season's `in_house_guest_count` by the party's size, and increments the booking's, the cabin's and the season's `version` by 1 each.

Departure is the ranch's single most consequential transaction, because it re-prices the entire stay. A `ReservationsAgent` checks the party out by supplying `settle_payment_cents`, the amount the party is paying at the desk. Two gates precede the money. The booking must be `CHECKED_IN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. And no activity seat belonging to this booking may still be live on a live slot — that is, no seat of the booking may have a `reservation_status` of `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` while its slot's `slot_status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `HOLD` or `RUNNING` — or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ACTIVITY_RESERVATIONS`.

**Re-pricing at departure.** The stay is priced a second time with exactly the procedure of §3.5, with one substitution: instead of the contracted nights, the calculation uses `realized_nights`, the whole number of days from the booking's `arrival_date` to the check-out clock date. That figure is used exactly as measured, with **no** floor at the season's `min_stay_nights`, so a party that leaves early genuinely re-bands the length-of-stay discount — a fourteen-night booking abandoned after six nights is priced at six nights with no discount at all. Every other step is unchanged: per-member nightly figures, per-member line rounding before summation, and the buyout premium if `is_buyout` is true. The result is written to `realized_subtotal_cents`, which will normally differ from `provisional_package_total_cents`.

The folio is then settled in ten steps, and the order is normative — reordering the middle steps changes the answer.

1. `realized_subtotal_cents` is taken from the re-priced stay above.
2. `activity_surcharge_total_cents` becomes the sum of `surcharge_cents` over every activity seat of this booking whose `reservation_status` is `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, less the sum of `surcharge_reversed_cents` over its `CANCELLED` seats.
3. The activity credit ceiling is the tier's `included_activity_credit_cents`, plus a further 5000 cents when `realized_subtotal_cents` has reached 1200000 cents; below that threshold the ceiling is the tier's figure alone.
4. `activity_credit_applied_cents` becomes the smaller of that ceiling and `activity_surcharge_total_cents`, so the credit never pays out more than the party actually incurred.
5. `no_show_penalty_total_cents` becomes the booking's `no_show_count` multiplied by the season's `no_show_penalty_cents`.
6. The gratuity base is `realized_subtotal_cents` alone. The base deliberately **excludes** activity surcharges, the applied credit, no-show penalties and manual adjustments.
7. `gratuity_cents` becomes 18 percent of that base, rounded half up to whole cents. This is the only rounding step in the settlement; every other step is exact integer arithmetic.
8. `folio_adjustment_total_cents` is the signed sum of every manual adjustment posted against the booking.
9. `final_folio_total_cents` becomes `realized_subtotal_cents` plus `activity_surcharge_total_cents`, minus `activity_credit_applied_cents`, plus `no_show_penalty_total_cents`, plus `gratuity_cents`, plus `folio_adjustment_total_cents`.
10. `folio_balance_cents` becomes `final_folio_total_cents` less `deposit_paid_cents` less `payments_applied_cents`.

The `settle_payment_cents` supplied with the request is applied as a settlement payment before that last step is judged. If the resulting `folio_balance_cents` is not exactly zero — whether the party has underpaid or overpaid — the check-out is refused with `ERR_FOLIO_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 402) and nothing is written. When the balance does resolve to zero, `payments_applied_cents` rises by the settled amount, `payment_status` moves `DEPOSIT_PAID → PAID_IN_FULL`, `booking_status` moves `CHECKED_IN → CHECKED_OUT`, `checked_out_at` is stamped from the clock, the cabin moves `OCCUPIED → TURNOVER`, the season's `in_house_guest_count` falls by the party's size, the season's `gross_package_revenue_cents` rises by `final_folio_total_cents`, and the booking's, the cabin's and the season's `version` each increment by 1. `PAID_IN_FULL` is reachable only through check-out.

**Manual adjustments.** A courtesy credit or a damage charge is posted by a `RanchManager` or a `ReservationsAgent` as a signed `amount_cents` together with a `memo` explaining it and the `expected_booking_version` pin of §2.7. Adjustment amounts are the one money field in the system that may be negative: a negative amount is a credit to the guest and a positive amount is a charge. Adjustments are accepted only while the booking is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `CHECKED_OUT`, and any other state is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted adjustment adds its amount to `folio_adjustment_total_cents` and increments the booking's `version` by 1; when the booking has already reached `CHECKED_OUT`, `final_folio_total_cents` and `folio_balance_cents` are recomputed on the spot so the closed folio stays arithmetically true.

**Cancelling a stay.** A `ReservationsAgent` or a `RanchManager` cancels with a `reason`, and cancellation is accepted while the booking is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; any other state is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. The refund is decided by how far out the cancellation falls. The system computes the whole number of days from the current clock date forward to the booking's `arrival_date` — a figure that is negative once the arrival date has passed — and selects the refund fraction from the first matching band: 1.00 when that figure is 90 days or more, 0.50 when it is 60 days or more, 0.25 when it is 30 days or more, and 0.00 otherwise. The selected fraction is stored in `cancellation_refund_pct`. `refunded_cents` becomes `deposit_paid_cents` multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up to whole cents, and `forfeited_cents` takes the remainder as `deposit_paid_cents` less `refunded_cents`. Because the forfeit is defined as the remainder rather than computed independently, `refunded_cents` and `forfeited_cents` always sum to exactly `deposit_paid_cents`, at every band, without exception. No other money field on the booking participates in the split.

The money dimension then lands on its terminal value, and one case is decided ahead of the bands. A booking cancelled while nothing had yet been collected — `deposit_paid_cents` at 0 **and** `payment_status` still `AWAITING_DEPOSIT` — moves to `FORFEITED` with both `refunded_cents` and `forfeited_cents` at 0, since the conserved total is itself 0; that outcome holds whichever band the arrival date selected, and `cancellation_refund_pct` still records that band's fraction. Every other cancellation takes its terminal value from the band, whatever `payment_status` the booking was carrying: a fraction of 1.00 moves `payment_status` to `REFUNDED`; a fraction strictly between 0 and 1 moves it to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`; a fraction of 0.00 moves it to `FORFEITED`. Occupancy-wise, `booking_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, `cancelled_at` is stamped from the clock, the cabin's occupancy interval is released for re-booking, and the booking's `version` increments by 1. A booking cancelled from `CONFIRMED` also removes its bed-nights — `party_size` multiplied by `nights` — from the season's `bed_nights_sold`.

Cancelling a stay then unwinds the party's activity programme in three further linked steps. Because the booking is `CANCELLED`, every activity seat of the booking whose `reservation_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` becomes `CANCELLED` with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to that seat's own `surcharge_cents`, which returns the booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents` to the value it held before those seats existed. Because those seats are cancelled, each one whose `mount_status` was `ASSIGNED` moves to `RELEASED`, `assigned_horse_id` is cleared, `mount_release_count` rises by 1, and the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` is recomputed. And because those seats have left `CONFIRMED`, each parent slot recomputes `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `required_guide_count`, `seats_available` and `readiness_posture`, with any slot that was `OPEN` and whose posture has degraded to `UNDERSTAFFED` or `MOUNT_SHORT` moving to `HOLD` and recording the matching `hold_reason`.

### 3.7. Scheduling Activities and Staffing Guides

The ranch's day is built out of **activity slots**. A `RanchManager` schedules one inside a season by naming the programme in `activity_type` — one of `TRAIL_RIDE`, `ARENA_LESSON`, `FLY_FISHING`, `HIKE` or `KIDS_PROGRAM` — the interval in `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, the ground it covers in `terrain_grade` (one of `MEADOW`, `ROLLING`, `STEEP` or `RIVER_CROSSING`), the ability floor in `min_ability_rank` (one of `UNASSESSED`, `BEGINNER`, `NOVICE`, `INTERMEDIATE` or `ADVANCED`), the seat count in `capacity`, which must be at least 1, the per-rider premium in `rider_surcharge_cents`, which defaults to 0, and `mount_required`. The `ends_at` instant must be strictly later than `starts_at` and must fall on the same calendar date, and the whole interval must lie inside the season's operating window; a violation of any of those is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_SEASON`. From the interval the system computes `duration_hours` as the elapsed hours between the two instants, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `mount_required` flag is not free-form: it is true exactly when `activity_type` is `TRAIL_RIDE` or `ARENA_LESSON`, and false for `FLY_FISHING`, `HIKE` and `KIDS_PROGRAM`. A new slot is `DRAFT` with `hold_reason` null, every counter — `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `assigned_guide_count` and `required_guide_count` — at 0, `seats_available` equal to `capacity`, and `actual_started_at`, `actual_ended_at` and `actual_duration_hours` all null. Slots may be read individually and listed, with the list narrowable by `season_id`, `activity_type` or `slot_status`.

**Guide staffing is a ratio, not a headcount.** The number of guides a slot needs, `required_guide_count`, is recomputed on every change to its confirmed seat mix and again whenever the slot is opened or run, and it is the largest of three independently computed requirements. The first is the ability-mix requirement: the confirmed riders on the slot are bucketed by the `rider_ability_at_booking` frozen onto each seat, and the buckets are divided by their per-guide allowances — one guide serves 4 `BEGINNER` riders, 6 `NOVICE` riders, 8 `INTERMEDIATE` riders or 10 `ADVANCED` riders. The four quotients are added together as real numbers and the **total** is rounded up to the next whole guide; the rounding-up happens once, on the sum, and never per bucket. The second is the terrain requirement: a slot whose `terrain_grade` is `STEEP` or `RIVER_CROSSING` demands at least 2 guides, and any other terrain demands at least 1. The third is the minors requirement: a slot carrying at least one rider under 12 years of age demands at least 2 guides, and a slot with no such rider adds nothing on this account. `required_guide_count` is the greatest of those three — except that a slot with no confirmed riders at all requires exactly 0 guides, not the terrain floor. The riders counted for all of this are the seats whose `reservation_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN`, and the count of those riders whose guest is under 12 years old is published as `minor_rider_count`.

**Readiness posture is the slot's single derived gate.** `readiness_posture` is recomputed on every seat, mount or guide change touching the slot, and it takes the first matching value in this precedence order: `FROZEN` whenever the governing season is in any status other than `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON`; otherwise `MOUNT_SHORT` when the slot requires mounts and `mount_assigned_count` is below `confirmed_rider_count`; otherwise `UNDERSTAFFED` when `assigned_guide_count` is below `required_guide_count`; otherwise `STAFFED_READY`. The precedence is deliberate: a slot missing both a mount and a guide reports `MOUNT_SHORT`, because the mount is the harder problem to solve. Alongside the posture, `seats_available` is maintained as `capacity` less `confirmed_rider_count`.

Guides are attached to a slot by a `RanchManager`, who names the person in `staff_member_id` and the part they play in `guide_role` — one of `LEAD`, `ASSISTANT` or `WRANGLER`. Three conditions apply. The named staff member must be `ACTIVE`, must belong to the slot's own season, and must hold the certification the activity demands: `EQUINE_HANDLER` for a `TRAIL_RIDE` or an `ARENA_LESSON`, `FLY_CASTING` for `FLY_FISHING`, `CHILD_PROGRAM` for a `KIDS_PROGRAM`, and `WILDERNESS_FIRST_AID` for a `HIKE`. Any of those three failing is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. The guide must also be free: they may hold no other `SCHEDULED` assignment whose slot interval overlaps this slot's half-open interval from `starts_at` inclusive to `ends_at` exclusive, and their total scheduled hours on that calendar date must stay within 10.0 hours; either failure is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. An accepted assignment is created `SCHEDULED` with `credited_hours` at 0, raises the slot's `assigned_guide_count` by 1, triggers a recomputation of the slot's `readiness_posture`, adds the slot's `duration_hours` to the guide's `scheduled_guide_hours`, and increments the slot's `version` by 1. Guide assignments may be read individually and listed, with the list narrowable by `slot_id`, `staff_member_id` or `assignment_status` — and because a cascade can release an assignment without the client ever asking, that list is the only way to discover such releases.

Releasing a guide is accepted while the assignment is `SCHEDULED` and its slot is still `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `HOLD`; a slot that is already running or finished refuses the release with `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`. A release takes a `reason`, moves `assignment_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `RELEASED`, lowers the slot's `assigned_guide_count` by 1, subtracts the slot's `duration_hours` from the guide's `scheduled_guide_hours`, recomputes the slot's `readiness_posture`, and increments the slot's `version` by 1. If the slot was `OPEN` and the release leaves it `UNDERSTAFFED`, the slot moves to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` of `UNDERSTAFFED`.

**The slot lifecycle.** A slot is opened for booking by a `RanchManager`, which moves it `DRAFT → OPEN`. Opening requires the governing season to be `OPEN` or `IN_SEASON`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`; it requires at least one guide already assigned; and it requires `assigned_guide_count` to have reached `required_guide_count`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_RATIO_UNMET`. The same capability re-opens a slot from `HOLD → OPEN`, but only once the defect has genuinely cleared: the slot's `readiness_posture` must read `STAFFED_READY`, and re-opening clears `hold_reason`. A slot in any other status refuses the request with `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted open increments the slot's `version` by 1.

A slot leaves `OPEN` for `HOLD` on its own, without anyone asking. Any event that leaves the recomputed posture at `UNDERSTAFFED` or `MOUNT_SHORT` — a new seat that raises `required_guide_count` or leaves a rider unmounted, a guide release, a staff separation, a horse taken out of service, a waiver revocation — pushes an `OPEN` slot onto `HOLD` in the same request. The recorded `hold_reason` is `UNDERSTAFFED` when `assigned_guide_count` is below `required_guide_count`, and `MOUNT_SHORT` otherwise. While a slot sits on `HOLD` no new seat may be created on it, and the cycle `OPEN → HOLD → OPEN` may repeat any number of times over the slot's life.

An `ActivityGuide` who holds a `SCHEDULED` assignment on the slot starts the activity, moving it `OPEN → RUNNING` and stamping `actual_started_at` from the logical clock. Four conditions gate the start: the slot must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`; the season must be `IN_SEASON`, or `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`; the logical clock must have reached the slot's `starts_at`, or `ERR_SLOT_NOT_STARTED`; and the posture must read `STAFFED_READY` — a shortfall of guides reports `ERR_GUIDE_RATIO_UNMET` and a mounted slot with fewer assigned mounts than confirmed riders reports `ERR_MOUNTS_INCOMPLETE`. Riders still sitting in `CONFIRMED` when the slot starts are not cancelled; they stay `CONFIRMED` and become candidates for a no-show at completion.

Completion is described from the rider's and the horse's side in §3.8, but its own contract is simple: the slot must be `RUNNING`, the request must supply `actual_duration_hours` — the hours the activity really took, from which `actual_ended_at` is recorded as that many hours after `actual_started_at` — and the slot moves `RUNNING → COMPLETED` with its `version` incremented by 1.

Cancelling a slot is a `RanchManager` action taking a `reason`, accepted while the slot is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `HOLD` and refused with `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID` otherwise. A `DRAFT` slot holds no seats, so nothing is reversed. From `OPEN` and from `HOLD` the reversal is identical and complete: every `CONFIRMED` seat becomes `CANCELLED` with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to its own `surcharge_cents`, returning each affected booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents` to its pre-reservation value; every `ASSIGNED` mount becomes `RELEASED` with each horse's `scheduled_load_hours` restored exactly; and every `SCHEDULED` guide assignment becomes `RELEASED`. The slot's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Seats, Mounts and Riding the Programme

A seat on a slot is an **activity reservation**, created by a `ReservationsAgent` who names the stay it bills to in `booking_id`, the rider in `guest_id`, and the slot version they last observed in `expected_slot_version`. Because it is the point where four entities meet, seat creation is the most heavily gated write in the system, and the order in which its gates fire is normative — the safety gates run first, and only after every one of them has passed does the system look at capacity. In priority order the seat is refused when the booking and the slot belong to different seasons or the named guest does not appear in the booking's `party_members` (`ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`); when `expected_slot_version` does not match (`ERR_STALE_SLOT_VERSION`); when the slot is not `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `HOLD` (`ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`); when the guest's `guest_standing` is `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_GUEST_SUSPENDED`); when the guest's resolved `waiver_status` is not `SIGNED` (`ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED`); when the slot requires a mount and the guest's `equine_statute_ack` is not true (`ERR_EQUINE_STATUTE_ACK_REQUIRED`); when the guest's `effective_ability_rank` sits below the slot's `min_ability_rank` (`ERR_RIDER_ABILITY_INSUFFICIENT`); when the guest already holds a `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` seat on another slot whose half-open interval overlaps this one (`ERR_GUEST_SLOT_CONFLICT`); when the slot is on `HOLD` (`ERR_SLOT_ON_HOLD`); when `seats_available` has fallen below 1 (`ERR_SLOT_FULL`); and finally when the guest already holds a `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` seat on this very slot (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESERVATION`). A full slot that a rider is too inexperienced to ride therefore answers with the ability failure, never with the capacity failure.

An accepted seat is created `CONFIRMED`. Its `mount_status` starts at `PENDING` on a slot that requires a mount and at `NOT_REQUIRED` on one that does not — and a `NOT_REQUIRED` seat keeps `assigned_horse_id` null for its whole life. The slot's `rider_surcharge_cents` is copied into the seat's `surcharge_cents`, `surcharge_reversed_cents` starts at 0, and the booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents` rises by the copied amount, which is what eventually lands on the folio. The guest's `effective_ability_rank` at that instant is frozen into `rider_ability_at_booking`, and it is that frozen rank — not the guest's live rank — that the guide-staffing ratio buckets, so a later assessment does not retroactively restaff a slot. `mount_release_count` starts at 0 and `checked_in_at`, `completed_at` and `cancelled_at` all start null. The slot then recomputes `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `required_guide_count`, `seats_available` and `readiness_posture`, an `OPEN` slot whose posture degraded moves to `HOLD` with the matching `hold_reason`, and the slot's `version` increments by 1. Seats may be read individually and listed, with the list narrowable by `slot_id`, `booking_id`, `guest_id`, `assigned_horse_id`, `reservation_status` or `mount_status`; because cascades cancel seats and release mounts without the client asking, that list is the only channel through which such changes can be discovered.

**Naming a mount.** A `HorseProgramDirector` attaches a specific animal to a seat by supplying `horse_id` and `expected_horse_version`, and the request is subject to the fullest safety screen in the system, in this order: the version pin must match (`ERR_STALE_HORSE_VERSION`); the seat's `mount_status` must be `PENDING` or `RELEASED` (`ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`); the horse's `horse_status` must be `IN_SERVICE` (`ERR_HORSE_NOT_IN_SERVICE`); the guest's `weight_lb` must not exceed the horse's `weight_limit_lb` (`ERR_RIDER_WEIGHT_EXCEEDS_MOUNT`); the rider's `effective_ability_rank` must fall within the band from the horse's `min_rider_ability` to its `max_rider_ability` inclusive (`ERR_MOUNT_ABILITY_MISMATCH`); the horse's `consecutive_duty_days` must be below 5 (`ERR_MOUNT_DUTY_LIMIT`); the horse's already-scheduled hours on the slot's calendar date plus this slot's `duration_hours` must not exceed 4.0 hours (`ERR_MOUNT_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`); and the animal must clear the recovery-buffer test against each of its other `ASSIGNED` seats (`ERR_MOUNT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`). That buffer test is deliberately strict on both sides: two rides conflict when the candidate slot starts before the other slot's `ends_at` plus 60 minutes **and** the other slot starts before the candidate's `ends_at` plus 60 minutes. A gap of exactly 60 minutes between two rides is therefore permitted; a gap of 59 minutes is not.

An accepted assignment moves the seat's `mount_status` from `PENDING` or `RELEASED` to `ASSIGNED`, sets `assigned_horse_id`, adds the slot's `duration_hours` to the horse's `scheduled_load_hours`, raises the slot's `mount_assigned_count` by 1, recomputes the slot's `readiness_posture` — which can lift a `HOLD` whose only defect was `MOUNT_SHORT` back to a re-openable state — and increments both the horse's `version` and the slot's `version` by 1 each.

**Letting the ranch choose.** Rather than naming an animal, the director may ask for the fairest available mount, which takes no request body and applies a two-step selector. Step one filters the herd to the eligible animals: the horse must be `IN_SERVICE`; the rider's rank must be at or above `min_rider_ability` and at or below `max_rider_ability`; the guest's `weight_lb` must not exceed `weight_limit_lb`; the horse must clear the same strict 60-minute buffer test against every other `ASSIGNED` seat it holds; its scheduled hours on the slot's date plus the slot's `duration_hours` must stay within 4.0 hours; and its `consecutive_duty_days` must be below 5. A horse failing any single clause is discarded. Step two orders the survivors by `scheduled_load_hours` ascending, then by `consecutive_duty_days` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and takes the first — so the least-worked animal wins, the freshest breaks the tie, and the earliest-registered breaks the tie after that. Ties are never broken by identifier. When the eligible set is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MOUNT`. Once a horse is selected the effects are identical to a named assignment.

**Releasing a mount.** The director may hand a horse back with a `reason` while the seat's `mount_status` is `ASSIGNED` and the slot has not yet completed. The release moves `mount_status` to `RELEASED`, clears `assigned_horse_id`, raises `mount_release_count` by 1, subtracts the slot's `duration_hours` from the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` — returning that figure to exactly the value it held before the assignment, never an approximation of it — lowers the slot's `mount_assigned_count` by 1, recomputes `readiness_posture` so that an `OPEN` slot which became `MOUNT_SHORT` drops to `HOLD`, and increments both the horse's and the slot's `version` by 1 each. A released seat may be mounted again, possibly on a different animal, and the cycle from `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED` and back may repeat any number of times while the slot is `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `HOLD`.

**Checking a rider in.** An `ActivityGuide` assigned to the slot checks a rider in at the corral. The seat must be `CONFIRMED`, its slot must be `RUNNING`, the guest's resolved `waiver_status` must still be `SIGNED` with `equine_statute_ack` true, the guest's `guest_standing` must not be `SUSPENDED`, and the seat's `mount_status` must be `NOT_REQUIRED` or `ASSIGNED` — a rider whose horse was released by a health cascade cannot mount up. Check-in stamps `checked_in_at` from the logical clock and increments the seat's `version` by 1. A waiver that lapsed between booking and riding blocks the check-in with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED` even though the seat itself is perfectly valid.

**Completing the slot.** When the guide completes a `RUNNING` slot with its `actual_duration_hours`, the slot fans that single fact out across every record it touches. Each `CHECKED_IN` seat becomes `COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped from the clock. Each seat still sitting in `CONFIRMED` becomes `NO_SHOW`: the parent booking's `no_show_count` rises by 1 and its `no_show_penalty_total_cents` is rewritten as `no_show_count` multiplied by the season's `no_show_penalty_cents`, a charge the folio collects at check-out. On the mount dimension, a completed rider's `ASSIGNED` mount becomes `COMPLETED` and can never be released again, while a no-show's `ASSIGNED` mount becomes `RELEASED`. Every `SCHEDULED` guide assignment on the slot becomes `COMPLETED` with `credited_hours` set to the slot's `actual_duration_hours`. And every horse that carried a completed rider has its `realized_load_hours` increased by `actual_duration_hours` and its duty-day run recomputed. The two load figures are therefore measures of different things and will legitimately disagree for the same seat: `scheduled_load_hours` sums the planned `duration_hours` over seats currently `ASSIGNED`, while `realized_load_hours` sums the `actual_duration_hours` over seats that reached `COMPLETED`. A ride that was planned for 3.0 hours and ran for 3.5 contributes 3.0 to the plan and 3.5 to the record. Seats that were `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` contribute to neither. Hour figures are carried to one decimal place, rounded half up.

**Cancelling a seat.** A `ReservationsAgent` or a `RanchManager` cancels a seat with a `reason`. A `CONFIRMED` seat may be cancelled while its slot is not `RUNNING`; a `CHECKED_IN` seat may be cancelled while its slot is `RUNNING`, and that late cancellation is not treated as a no-show. Either way the reversal is identical: `reservation_status` becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped from the clock, `surcharge_reversed_cents` is set to the seat's own `surcharge_cents` and the booking's `activity_surcharge_total_cents` drops by that amount, an `ASSIGNED` mount becomes `RELEASED` with the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` restored exactly, the slot recomputes `confirmed_rider_count`, `minor_rider_count`, `required_guide_count`, `seats_available` and `readiness_posture`, and both the seat's and the slot's `version` increment by 1 each. A seat in any other state is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. The Herd, Its Health Record and Its Duty Days

The `HorseProgramDirector` owns the herd. A horse is added with its barn `name`, which must be unique across every horse on the ranch and is compared case-sensitively so that `Dakota` and `dakota` are two different animals, the rider band it may carry as `min_rider_ability` and `max_rider_ability` — each one of `BEGINNER`, `NOVICE`, `INTERMEDIATE` or `ADVANCED`, with `max_rider_ability` at or above `min_rider_ability` on the rank ladder — and its `weight_limit_lb`, which must be greater than zero. A new horse is `IN_SERVICE` with `scheduled_load_hours`, `realized_load_hours` and `consecutive_duty_days` all at 0, and `last_duty_date` and `last_rest_started_on` both null. Horses may be read individually and listed, and the list may be narrowed by `horse_status`.

Everything that happens to an animal's health is recorded as an immutable **horse health event**, filed by the director against the horse in `horse_id` with an `event_type` — one of `LAMENESS`, `COLIC`, `REST_START`, `REHAB_START`, `VET_CLEARANCE`, `FARRIER` or `RETIREMENT` — the `observed_on` ISO 8601 date, and an optional free-text `note`. The event records the outcome it produced in `resulting_horse_status`, which is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `REST`, `LAME`, `REHAB` or `RETIRED`; a `FARRIER` visit changes nothing, so it echoes the status the animal already held. Health events are the one record type on the ranch that carries no version counter and no modification timestamp — only an identifier and a creation instant — precisely because nothing may ever change them. They may be read individually and listed by `horse_id` or `event_type`, and that list is the only way to discover events the system filed on its own account.

The events drive the animal's `horse_status` along a fixed set of paths. A `LAMENESS` or a `COLIC` event on an `IN_SERVICE` horse makes it `LAME`. A `REHAB_START` event on a `LAME` horse makes it `REHAB`. A `VET_CLEARANCE` event returns a horse from `REHAB` or from `REST` to `IN_SERVICE`, closing both the lameness cycle and the rest cycle so that the same animal may go in and out of service repeatedly across a season. A `REST_START` event on an `IN_SERVICE` horse makes it `REST` and records `last_rest_started_on` from the event's `observed_on`. A `RETIREMENT` event is accepted from `IN_SERVICE`, `REST`, `LAME`, `REHAB` or `REVIEW_HOLD` and makes the animal `RETIRED`, which is final: a retired horse can never re-enter the mount pool by any route. `REVIEW_HOLD` is not reachable by a health event at all — it is entered only when an incident report names the animal, and left only when that report is disposed, as §3.10 describes. Every accepted health event increments the horse's `version` by 1.

**Taking an animal out of service is a four-link cascade.** A `LAMENESS`, `COLIC`, `REST_START` or `RETIREMENT` event runs it in full inside the same request. First the horse's `horse_status` moves and `resulting_horse_status` is written onto the immutable event. Second, because the animal is no longer available, every activity seat that holds it — every reservation whose `assigned_horse_id` is this horse, whose `mount_status` is `ASSIGNED`, and whose slot has not yet started according to the logical clock — moves `mount_status` to `RELEASED`, clears `assigned_horse_id` and raises `mount_release_count` by 1. Rides that have already started are left alone; the ranch does not un-mount a rider who is already on the trail. Third, because those seats lost their mounts, each parent slot recomputes `mount_assigned_count` and `readiness_posture`, and a slot that requires mounts and now has fewer assigned mounts than confirmed riders reads `MOUNT_SHORT`. Fourth, because the posture degraded, each such slot that was `OPEN` transitions to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` of `MOUNT_SHORT`. Finally the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` is recomputed over its remaining `ASSIGNED` seats. A `REST_START` or `VET_CLEARANCE` event additionally resets `consecutive_duty_days` to 0, because a rested or newly cleared animal begins a fresh run.

**The duty-day run.** `consecutive_duty_days` counts the unbroken run of calendar days on which a horse has actually worked, and it is recomputed whenever a slot completes with that animal's mount reaching `COMPLETED`. Taking the slot's UTC calendar date as the duty date: when the horse's `last_duty_date` is the immediately preceding day, `consecutive_duty_days` increases by 1; when `last_duty_date` is that same date already — a second ride on a day the animal has already worked — `consecutive_duty_days` is left exactly as it is, because a duty day is a day and not a ride; and in every other case, including a first-ever duty and any gap of one or more idle days, `consecutive_duty_days` is set to 1, the idle gap having reset the run. In all three cases `last_duty_date` is then set to the duty date. Duty days are whole days on the UTC calendar. Because the mount eligibility screen refuses any animal whose `consecutive_duty_days` has reached 5, a horse that has worked five days running is out of the pool until a `REST_START` or `VET_CLEARANCE` event clears its run.

### 3.10. Seasonal Contracts, Bunkhouse Space and the Retention Bonus

Guides, wranglers, cooks and housekeepers are hired per season. A `RanchManager` creates a staff record inside a season with the person's `full_name`, their `role_type` — one of `WRANGLER`, `GUIDE`, `KITCHEN` or `HOUSEKEEPING` — their `certifications`, an array drawn from `WILDERNESS_FIRST_AID`, `EQUINE_HANDLER`, `FLY_CASTING` and `CHILD_PROGRAM` which defaults to empty, and the contract dates `hired_on` and `contract_end_date`, where `contract_end_date` must be strictly later than `hired_on`. The record is created `HIRED` with `season_id` fixed, `housing_unit_code` and `housing_bunk_number` null, `separated_on` null and `scheduled_guide_hours` at 0. Two derived figures come with it: `contract_days`, the whole number of days between `hired_on` and `contract_end_date`, and `days_worked`, which is the whole days between `hired_on` and the current logical clock date, floored at 0 before the contract begins and capped at `contract_days` once it has run its course. Staff may be read individually and listed, with the list narrowable by `season_id`, `role_type` or `employment_status`.

**Onboarding allots a bunk.** A `HIRED` staff member is onboarded with a `housing_unit_code` and a `housing_bunk_number`, which moves `employment_status` to `ACTIVE` and records both values. Two conditions apply: the logical clock date must have reached `hired_on`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_DATE_INVALID`, and no other staff member of the same season whose `employment_status` is `HIRED` or `ACTIVE` may already hold that same combination of `housing_unit_code` and `housing_bunk_number`, or the request is refused with `ERR_HOUSING_BUNK_TAKEN`. Bunk exclusivity is scoped to the pair, so two people may share a unit code in different bunks. A staff member who is not `HIRED` is refused with `ERR_STAFF_STATE_INVALID`. Only an `ACTIVE` staff member may be assigned as a guide, and onboarding increments the record's `version` by 1.

**The retention bonus is computed two different ways from the same numbers.** The pro-rated figure is the season's `retention_bonus_cents` multiplied by `days_worked` and divided by `contract_days`, rounded half up to whole cents. The **projected** variant, recomputed on every read of a `HIRED` or `ACTIVE` staff member and published as `retention_bonus_projected_cents`, is simply that pro-rated figure with no cliff applied, so it reports a positive accrual from the first day worked. The **settled** variant, computed only when the contract ends and published as `retention_bonus_settled_cents`, applies the ranch's actual policy: when the staff member reaches `COMPLETED_SEASON` the payout is the season's `retention_bonus_cents` in full, regardless of the pro-rate; otherwise, when twice `days_worked` is less than `contract_days` — that is, when the person worked less than half their contract — the payout collapses to 0; and otherwise the payout is the pro-rated figure. The two variants therefore disagree for the same person on the same day: someone who leaves after a quarter of their contract is projected a quarter of the bonus and settled at nothing, and someone who runs to the end is projected slightly under the full target and settled at the whole of it. `retention_bonus_projected_cents` reads 0 once the person is `DEPARTED_EARLY`.

**Completing a contract** is available to a `RanchManager` for an `ACTIVE` staff member once the logical clock date has reached `contract_end_date`, and it is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_DATE_INVALID` before that date and with `ERR_GUIDE_ASSIGNMENTS_OPEN` while any guide assignment of theirs is still `SCHEDULED`. It moves `employment_status` to `COMPLETED_SEASON`, writes the full `retention_bonus_cents` into `retention_bonus_settled_cents`, adds that amount to the season's `retention_bonus_liability_cents`, releases the `housing_unit_code` and `housing_bunk_number` for reuse by another arrival, and increments both the staff member's and the season's `version` by 1 each.

**Separating early is a three-link cascade.** A `RanchManager` separates a staff member with a `reason`, which is accepted from `HIRED` at any time and from `ACTIVE` only while the clock date is strictly before `contract_end_date`; on or after that date the correct action is completion, and separation is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_DATE_INVALID`. First, `separated_on` is set from the clock date, `days_worked` is frozen at its value on that date, the settled retention bonus is computed with the vesting cliff applied, and the season's `retention_bonus_liability_cents` rises by the amount actually settled — which is 0 for anyone below the cliff and for anyone separated straight out of `HIRED`, whose `days_worked` is 0. Second, because the person has left, every guide assignment of theirs still in `SCHEDULED` becomes `RELEASED` and their `scheduled_guide_hours` drops to 0. Third, because those guides were released, each affected slot recomputes `assigned_guide_count` and `readiness_posture`, and an `OPEN` slot whose posture became `UNDERSTAFFED` transitions to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` of `UNDERSTAFFED`. A separation out of `HIRED` cascades nothing beyond the first link, since a `HIRED` person can hold no guide assignments. Both the staff member's and the season's `version` increment by 1 each.

### 3.11. Incidents: Filing, Disposition and Closure

An equine-activity **incident report** is filed by the `ActivityGuide` who was working the slot, against the seat the incident happened on. The report carries a `severity` of `MINOR`, `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL`, the boolean `equine_activity_statute_notice_given` recording whether the statutory equine-activity notice was posted and read to the rider, a factual `narrative`, the `occurred_at` instant, and optionally the `horse_id` of the animal involved, which is omitted for a non-equine incident such as a fall on a hike. Filing requires the acting guide to be the guide assigned to the slot. The report copies `guest_id` from the seat and takes `reported_by_staff_member_id` from the calling user's identity, so neither is client-supplied. A report naming a horse must have `equine_activity_statute_notice_given` set to true, or it is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_NOTICE_REQUIRED`. The report is created `FILED` with `disposition_note`, `disposed_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` all null and `remediation_completed` false. Reports may be read individually and listed, with the list narrowable by `reservation_id`, `guest_id`, `horse_id`, `reported_by_staff_member_id` or `incident_status`.

Filing has immediate consequences. When the report names a horse, that animal moves `horse_status` from `IN_SERVICE` to `REVIEW_HOLD` and is withheld from the mount pool until the report is disposed, with its `version` incremented by 1. When `severity` is `CRITICAL`, a three-link cascade runs against the rider: the guest's `guest_standing` moves from `GOOD` to `SUSPENDED`; then every seat of that guest whose `reservation_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` and whose slot has not yet started by the logical clock becomes `CANCELLED` with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to its `surcharge_cents`; then each of those seats releases its mount, moving `mount_status` from `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED`, clearing `assigned_horse_id`, recomputing the horse's `scheduled_load_hours` and forcing the parent slot to recompute `confirmed_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `required_guide_count` and `readiness_posture`. A `MINOR` or `SERIOUS` report leaves the guest's standing untouched.

**Disposition is a fork, and its two branches do very different amounts of work.** A `SafetyOfficer` disposes a `FILED` report by supplying a `disposition` of either `CLEARED` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` together with a `disposition_note`; a report in any other status is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`, and a report whose `equine_activity_statute_notice_given` is not true is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_NOTICE_REQUIRED` — the notice is checked again here, at disposition, and not only at filing. Either branch records the `disposition_note` and sets `disposed_by_user_id` from the acting user's identity.

The `CLEARED` branch touches only the animal: a horse sitting in `REVIEW_HOLD` returns to `IN_SERVICE` and re-enters the mount pool immediately, and nothing at all happens to the guest.

The `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` branch runs a three-level cascade against the guest and routes the animal differently. First `incident_status` becomes `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. Second, because remediation is required, the involved guest's `guest_standing` moves from `GOOD` to `RESTRICTED` and their `effective_ability_rank` is thereby capped at `BEGINNER`. Third, because the guest is restricted, every future seat of theirs on a slot whose `min_ability_rank` sits above `BEGINNER` becomes `CANCELLED` with `surcharge_reversed_cents` set to its `surcharge_cents`, each such seat releases its mount from `ASSIGNED` to `RELEASED`, and each parent slot recomputes `confirmed_rider_count`, `mount_assigned_count`, `required_guide_count` and `readiness_posture`. On the animal's side, a horse in `REVIEW_HOLD` is routed to `REHAB` rather than back to service, and stays out of the pool until a `VET_CLEARANCE` health event returns it. The `version` of the report increments by 1, as do the versions of the horse and the guest wherever they were touched.

**Closure is where the two branches join, under segregation of duties.** A `SafetyOfficer` closes a report that is `CLEARED` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, supplying `remediation_completed`. The acting user identity must differ from the `disposed_by_user_id` recorded at disposition — the officer who judged an incident may not also sign it off — and a self-closure is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The two branches then diverge in one respect only: closing a report that came through `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` additionally demands `remediation_completed` to be true, whereas a report that came through `CLEARED` closes regardless. Closure sets `closed_by_user_id`, records `remediation_completed`, moves `incident_status` to `CLOSED`, and makes the report immutable; a report in `FILED` or already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Closure with `remediation_completed` true also restores the rider. A guest in `RESTRICTED` returns to `GOOD` and the `BEGINNER` cap on their `effective_ability_rank` lifts, so their assessed rank governs again. A guest in `SUSPENDED` returns to `GOOD` and becomes eligible for new seats, and this is the only route out of suspension — it works from either branch, since a critical incident may perfectly well be cleared. In neither case are the seats that the earlier cascade cancelled restored: reinstatement returns a guest's eligibility to book, never the bookings they lost. The guest's `version` increments by 1.
