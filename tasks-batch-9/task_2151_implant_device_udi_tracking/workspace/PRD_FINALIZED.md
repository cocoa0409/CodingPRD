# Implant Device UDI Tracking API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A surgical implant is the only item in a hospital's supply chain that is simultaneously a piece of inventory, a piece of a patient, a regulated article subject to manufacturer recall, and a high-value line on a bill that somebody eventually has to defend. Today those four identities live in four disconnected systems: the dock scans a carton into a materials package, the operating room writes a serial number onto a paper count sheet, regulatory affairs keeps recall correspondence in a shared mailbox, and accounts payable reconciles consignment invoices against whatever the vendor's representative asserts. When a manufacturer publishes a Class I field action, nobody can answer the only question that matters — *which patients are carrying this device, and where is the rest of the affected stock sitting right now* — without a multi-day manual chart review.

This document specifies the Implant Device UDI Tracking service, a single system of record that follows a serialized device from the receiving dock through quarantine release, reservation, implantation, patient linkage, registry reporting, charge capture, recall remediation, explantation, vendor return and final consignment settlement. Every device is tracked at the serial level under its Unique Device Identifier: the device-identifier half (UDI-DI) names the model, and the production-identifier half (UDI-PI) names the lot and the individual serial. Because the same physical unit is at once a custody object and a financial object, the service tracks two independent state dimensions on every serialized unit, and it enforces a conserved-quantity discipline on both: no lot may lose or gain units across any operation, and no vendor's open consignment liability may drift across a forward action and its compensating reversal.

The service is deliberately opinionated about who may do what. Three named separations of duty are enforced on the acting user identity rather than on the role: whoever received a lot may not release it from quarantine, whoever documented an implant may not verify it, and whoever matched a vendor invoice may not approve it. All business resources are mounted beneath the `/udi` prefix. Because expiry sweeps, registry deadlines, recall outreach windows and return-credit tiers all depend on the passage of time, the service runs on an explicitly settable logical clock rather than on wall-clock time, so that every temporal rule in this document is reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent, empty, or consists only of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six role names defined below.

The six roles are `ReceivingTech`, `InventoryLead`, `CircClinician`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` and `VendorRep`.

- A `ReceivingTech` is the dock technician who books lots and serialized units into quarantine.
- An `InventoryLead` owns master data, quarantine release, reservations, rotation and the operational checkpoint.
- A `CircClinician` is the circulating nurse who owns patients, cases and implant documentation.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` owns field actions, recall outreach and registry reporting, and provides the second signature on invoice approval.
- A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` owns charge-capture accuracy and the reconciliation half of the payables separation.
- A `VendorRep` is the manufacturer's representative who submits and resubmits consignment invoices and records return credits.

Five of those six roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ReceivingTech`, `InventoryLead`, `CircClinician`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; any non-empty string is accepted. The server records that string verbatim wherever a transition captures the acting user — into `received_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on a lot, into `documented_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on an implant record, and into `matched_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a vendor invoice — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The sixth role is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor`; if it does not resolve to an existing vendor, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) exactly as a missing header would be. Ownership checks on any resource that references a vendor compare that resource's vendor foreign key to `X-User-ID`: on invoice submission and resubmission the check is direct, against the invoice's `vendor_id`; on a return credit the check walks from the explant record to its implant record, to the device unit, to that unit's lot, to that lot's catalog item, and compares the catalog item's `vendor_id`. A `VendorRep` acting on another vendor's row is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Authorization proper is table-driven: if the role named in `X-User-Role` does not appear in the authorized-roles list for the endpoint being called (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Two roles are additionally walled off from protected health information — `VendorRep` may not read patients at all, and `ReceivingTech` may read neither patients, charge captures, registry submissions, vendor invoices, explant records, recall notifications, nor the recall exposure view. Beyond role membership, three operations enforce a per-user separation described in §2.8; a violation there is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when absent, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when absent and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on it. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads carry no pagination fields in `meta`.

Each list endpoint also exposes its own filter parameters, and a filter that is supplied is applied conjunctively with every other supplied filter. Vendors may be filtered by `standing`. Consignment agreements accept `vendor_id` and `status`. Catalog items accept `vendor_id` and `category`. Lots accept `catalog_item_id` and `status`. Serialized units expose every one of their foreign keys as a filter — `lot_id`, `consignment_agreement_id`, `vendor_invoice_id`, `reserved_case_id` and the self-reference `replaced_unit_id` — plus `custody_state` and `billing_state`. Patients accept `registry_consent`. Surgical cases accept `patient_id`, `status` and `or_room_code`. Implant records accept `surgical_case_id`, `device_unit_id`, `patient_id` and `status`; this is the recall lookup surface across implanted patients, where filtering by patient answers "what is in this patient" and filtering by device unit answers "where did this serial go". Charge captures accept `implant_record_id` and `status`. Registry submissions accept `implant_record_id` and `status`. Field actions accept `vendor_id`, `catalog_item_id` and `status`. Recall notifications accept `field_action_id`, `implant_record_id`, `patient_id` and `status`. Vendor invoices accept `vendor_id`, `consignment_agreement_id` and `status`. Explant records accept `implant_record_id`, `replacement_device_unit_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Shape of a Stored Record

Every one of the fourteen persisted resource types — vendors, consignment agreements, catalog items, lots, device units, patients, surgical cases, implant records, charge captures, registry submissions, field actions, recall notifications, vendor invoices and explant records — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 timestamps in UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or arrived as a cascade from another resource's transition. No resource type in this service omits `updated_at` or `version`; all four common properties are present on every record and in every response payload.

A mutation always refreshes `updated_at` on the row it mutates and always advances that row's `version`. This applies to derived counters as much as to state fields: when a device unit's custody change forces a recount of its lot's on-hand quantity, the lot has been mutated, and its `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly.

Timestamps that record *when something happened in the business* are distinct from `created_at`/`updated_at` and are stamped from the logical clock described in §2.5: `received_at_utc` on a lot, `implanted_at_utc` and `expired_at_utc` on a device unit, `documented_at_utc` and `verified_at_utc` on an implant record, `submitted_at_utc` and `adjudicated_at_utc` on a registry submission, `published_at_utc` on a field action, and `explanted_at_utc` on an explant record.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for any business rule. It maintains a single logical clock whose value at process start is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time before or after that clock has been set. Any of the six roles may move the clock by posting to the clock endpoint at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a body carrying `to_iso8601`, the ISO 8601 instant the clock should now read; the response body is an object carrying `current_utc` with the newly set value. Setting the clock performs no sweeps and mutates no business record — it only moves the reference instant. Sweeping is the exclusive job of the operational checkpoint described in §3.10.

Every expiry test, deadline computation, turnaround measurement and elapsed-days tier in this document reads the logical clock and nothing else.

### 2.6. Nested Paths and the Validation Precedence Ladder

Resource paths nest: a serialized unit is created beneath its lot at `lot_id`, a reservation and an implant record beneath their case at `case_id`, a charge line and an explant beneath their implant record at `implant_record_id`. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost segment that fails to resolve determines the error. The full set of path parameters is `vendor_id`, `agreement_id`, `catalog_item_id`, `lot_id`, `device_unit_id`, `patient_id`, `case_id`, `implant_record_id`, `charge_id`, `submission_id`, `field_action_id`, `notification_id`, `invoice_id` and `explant_record_id`; each maps to its own not-found code as catalogued in §5.

Every request runs through one ordered ladder of checks. **The first check that fails determines the entire response; no later check runs, and no side effect belonging to a later stage is applied.** The order is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and non-blank, the role one of the six declared names, and for a `VendorRep` an `X-User-ID` that resolves to an existing vendor. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorization — the role appears in the endpoint's authorized-roles list. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Request shape — every required input field present and non-null, every enum field carrying a declared member, every integer field an integer inside its declared range, `scheduled_end_utc` strictly after `scheduled_start_utc`, exactly one of `catalog_item_id` and `device_unit_id` on a reservation request, `lot_numbers` present only when `scope_type` is `LOT`, `serial_low` and `serial_high` present only when `scope_type` is `SERIAL_RANGE`, and pagination inside its declared bounds. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. Path-parameter existence, left to right. Failure: the matching not-found code (404).
5. Body foreign-key existence, checked in the order the fields are declared in the endpoint's input list, and for the `device_unit_ids` array in array order. Failure: the matching not-found code (404).
6. Vendor ownership — a `VendorRep` may act only on rows whose vendor matches `X-User-ID`, on invoice submission, invoice resubmission and return credit. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
7. Optimistic concurrency — the `expected_case_version`, `expected_lot_version` or `expected_agreement_version` in the body matches the parent's current `version`. Failure: `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412).
8. Path-target state — the resource named by the path is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Cross-referenced entities named in the *body* are not judged here; they are judged at steps 15 through 18. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE` (409).
9. Separation of duties on `X-User-ID`, for lot release, implant verification and invoice approval. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. Recall safety — the referenced device unit is not in `RECALL_HOLD` and its lot is not `RECALLED`. Applies to reservation and to implant documentation. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_RECALLED` (422).
11. Expiry safety — the referenced unit's lot expiry instant is strictly after the logical clock, and for a reservation strictly after the case's `scheduled_end_utc`. Applies to the pinned-serial form of reservation and to implant documentation. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_EXPIRED` (422).
12. UDI transcription — `udi_di` equals the unit's `udi_di_snapshot` and `udi_pi_serial` equals the unit's `udi_serial`. Applies to implant documentation. Failure: `ERR_UDI_INCOMPLETE` (422).
13. Patient linkage — a supplied `patient_id` equals the case's `patient_id`. Applies to implant documentation. Failure: `ERR_PATIENT_LINK_MISMATCH` (422).
14. Registry completeness — the submission's `completeness_score` is at least 80. Applies to registry submission. Failure: `ERR_REGISTRY_SUBMISSION_INCOMPLETE` (422).
15. Lot release status — the referenced unit's lot is `AVAILABLE`. Applies to reservation and implant documentation. This deliberately runs *before* the unit-custody check at step 16, because a unit sitting in a still-quarantined lot always also fails that later check, and the operator needs the lot-level reason rather than the unit-level one. Failure: `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` (409).
16. Referenced unit custody — the device unit named in the body is in an allowed custody state: `AVAILABLE` for a reservation, `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED` for implant documentation, `IMPLANTED` for an explant. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE` (409).
17. Referenced agreement status — the consignment agreement is `ACTIVE`. Applies to unit receipt, invoice submission and invoice approval. Failure: `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
18. Referenced vendor standing — the vendor's `standing` is `ACTIVE`. Applies to invoice submission and resubmission. Failure: `ERR_VENDOR_ON_HOLD` (409).
19. Uniqueness, each identifier judged inside its own declared scope: a device unit's `udi_serial` must be unique among the units of the same catalog item, resolved through the unit's lot, and a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_UDI_SERIAL`; a `lot_number` must be unique among the lots of the same catalog item, and a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER`; and `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` covers all the remaining scopes — `vendor_code` globally, `agreement_code` per vendor, `device_identifier` globally, `mrn` globally, `case_number` globally, `action_code` globally, and `invoice_number` per vendor. All three codes are 409.
20. Invoice exclusivity — every unit in `device_unit_ids` has a null `vendor_invoice_id`. Applies to invoice submission and resubmission. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_INVOICED` (409).
21. Operating-room occupancy, using the unbuffered half-open geometry of §3.3. Failure: `ERR_OR_ROOM_CONFLICT` (409).
22. Patient recovery occupancy, using the 120-minute buffered closed-interval geometry of §3.3. Failure: `ERR_PATIENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409).
23. Non-empty selection pool for a catalog-driven reservation. Failure: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNIT` (409).
24. Case readiness is neither `BLOCKED_RESERVATION` nor `BLOCKED_DOCUMENTATION`, on case close. Failure: `ERR_CASE_READINESS_UNMET` (409).
25. Case readiness is not `BLOCKED_REGISTRY`, on case close. Failure: `ERR_REGISTRY_OUTSTANDING` (409).
26. Field-action remediation complete — `notification_open_count` is 0, on field-action close. Failure: `ERR_RECALL_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE` (409).
27. Every unit in `device_unit_ids` is in billing state `BILLABLE`, on invoice submission and resubmission. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_NOT_BILLABLE` (409).
28. An invoice held on variance may be approved only with a non-empty `override_reason`. Failure: `ERR_INVOICE_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED` (422).
29. `acknowledged_variance_cents` equals the charge's `variance_cents` exactly, on charge correction. Failure: `ERR_VARIANCE_ACK_MISMATCH` (422).
30. Case readiness is not `BLOCKED_CHARGE`, on case close. Failure: `ERR_CHARGE_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED` (409).
31. `credit_issued_cents` is less than or equal to the explant record's `credit_expected_cents`. Failure: `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED` (422).

Two consequences of that ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. A recalled unit reports `ERR_UNIT_RECALLED` even when it is also expired, because recall safety sits above expiry safety. And on case close, the three readiness codes are tried in exactly the same top-to-bottom order as the readiness computation itself: a reservation or documentation block surfaces as `ERR_CASE_READINESS_UNMET`, an outstanding registry obligation as `ERR_REGISTRY_OUTSTANDING`, and only then an unfinished charge as `ERR_CHARGE_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations mutate a child collection whose meaning depends on the parent's current shape, and all three are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional** input field, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is a rejection, never a silent overwrite.

A request to `POST /udi/surgical-cases/{case_id}/implant-records` must carry `expected_case_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that surgical case. A case legitimately accepts any number of implant records, so no state precondition stops a second documentation request; the stale-version check is the only guard that stops a writer from documenting against a case whose implant set, reservation set or readiness posture it no longer knows. If the value differs from the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A request to `POST /udi/lots/{lot_id}/device-units` must carry `expected_lot_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that lot. A lot legitimately accepts repeated unit additions while it sits in quarantine, so no state precondition stops a later request; the stale-version check is the only guard that stops a receiver from posting serials against a `received_qty` it no longer knows. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A request to `POST /udi/vendor-invoices` must carry `expected_agreement_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the consignment agreement named in the body. An agreement legitimately accepts many invoices, so nothing in the invoice lifecycle blocks a later submission; the stale-version check is what stops a vendor from billing under a price-list revision it has not yet seen. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none may be added. In particular, invoice resubmission, charge operations, registry operations, reservations and explant operations take no expected-version input at all.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of operations must be performed by two different people. The check is on `X-User-ID`, not on `X-User-Role`, which has two consequences: two different users holding the same role satisfy the separation, and one user cannot satisfy it by switching roles between the two halves.

Releasing a lot from quarantine requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the lot's `received_by_user_id`. Verifying an implant record requires that `X-User-ID` differ from that record's `documented_by_user_id`. Approving a vendor invoice requires that `X-User-ID` differ from that invoice's `matched_by_user_id`. Any of the three attempted by the same user is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and because that check sits at step 9 of the ladder it fires only after the target resource has already been confirmed to be in a legal state for the transition.

### 2.9. Money, Rounding and the Cents Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integers denominated in cents; there are no decimal money fields anywhere in the API. Where a computation produces a fractional cent, it is rounded half up — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero — and the result is a whole cent.

Rounding **order** is pinned everywhere it could plausibly be reordered, and reordering produces different, wrong answers. On a charge line, the per-unit amount is rounded to a whole cent first and only then multiplied by the quantity; multiplying first and rounding once at the end is incorrect. At the case level, each charge line is already a whole-cent integer, and the case total is the plain integer sum of those already-rounded lines; summing unrounded per-line products and rounding once at the end is incorrect. In period-based invoice reconciliation, the per-catalog-item average is rounded to a whole cent before it is multiplied by the invoiced count. In the settlement waterfall, only the credit cap is rounded.

Counts, quantities, basis points and completeness scores are likewise integers. Basis points are computed against a scale of 10,000.

### 2.10. Two Independent State Dimensions on Every Serialized Unit

A `DeviceUnit` is the only resource in the service carrying two state machines on one row, and they evolve independently of each other. `custody_state` is the physical dimension and takes one of `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED`, `IMPLANTED`, `EXPLANTED`, `RECALL_HOLD`, `EXPIRED`, `WASTED`, `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR` or `DISPOSED`; it starts at `QUARANTINED`, and `EXPIRED`, `WASTED`, `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR` and `DISPOSED` are terminal. `billing_state` is the financial dimension and takes one of `NOT_BILLABLE`, `BILLABLE`, `INVOICED`, `RECONCILED`, `CREDIT_PENDING` or `CREDITED`; it starts at `NOT_BILLABLE`, and `RECONCILED` and `CREDITED` are terminal. An `IMPLANTED` unit may be `NOT_BILLABLE`, `BILLABLE`, `INVOICED` or `RECONCILED`; a `CREDIT_PENDING` unit may be `EXPLANTED` or `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR`. Neither dimension constrains the other except where a transition below explicitly says so.

A third, non-transitioning classifier sits alongside them. `ownership` is set once, at unit receipt: it is `CONSIGNED` when a `consignment_agreement_id` was supplied and `OWNED` when it was not, and it never changes afterwards. An `OWNED` unit never becomes billable and therefore never contributes to any vendor liability or invoice.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this service are conserved across forward actions and their compensating reversals, and drift in either is a defect.

The first is the unit count on a lot. `received_qty` is the count of every device unit ever created on the lot and never decreases. `on_hand_qty` is the count of that lot's units whose `custody_state` is `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED` or `RECALL_HOLD`. `consumed_qty` is the count of those whose `custody_state` is `IMPLANTED`, `EXPLANTED`, `WASTED`, `EXPIRED`, `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR` or `DISPOSED`. The ten custody states partition into exactly those two buckets with none omitted and none double-counted, so `on_hand_qty` plus `consumed_qty` always equals `received_qty`. The canonical compensating pair is the reserve-then-unreserve round trip: both `AVAILABLE` and `RESERVED` sit in the on-hand bucket, so reserving a unit and then releasing that reservation leaves all three counters numerically identical to where they started. These three counters are recomputed whenever any unit's `custody_state` changes and whenever a unit is added to the lot. That recomputation carries one automatic transition with it: whenever the lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, its `received_qty` is at least 1 and the freshly recomputed `on_hand_qty` is 0, the lot moves `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED` — a purely compound transition driven by aggregate child custody and never by any request, wherever in this document a recomputation of `on_hand_qty` occurs.

The second is a vendor's open consignment liability. `open_consignment_liability_cents` on a vendor is the sum of `contract_price_cents` over every device unit whose lot's catalog item belongs to that vendor and whose `billing_state` is `BILLABLE` or `INVOICED`. Units in `NOT_BILLABLE`, `CREDIT_PENDING`, `CREDITED` or `RECONCILED` contribute nothing, and `OWNED` units, which never reach `BILLABLE`, contribute nothing ever. It is recomputed on every change to any unit's `billing_state` — that is, on lot release under on-receipt terms, on implant documentation under on-implant terms, on invoice submission, on invoice dispute, on invoice approval, on explantation, on explant disposition and on explant credit. The conserved-quantity guarantee is monetary: implanting a consigned unit moves it from `NOT_BILLABLE` to `BILLABLE` and adds its frozen contract price to the sum, explanting it moves it to `CREDIT_PENDING` and removes exactly that amount, and neither shipping the return nor recording the credit touches the sum again — so the liability after a full explant-and-return cycle equals the liability before the implant.

### 2.12. Derived Counters and Cascade Semantics

A number of fields are never written by a client and are instead re-derived from their children after every relevant mutation. `on_hand_available_qty` on a catalog item counts that item's units whose `custody_state` is `AVAILABLE`. `implant_count` on a patient counts that patient's implant records whose `status` is `DOCUMENTED`, `VERIFIED`, `AMENDED` or `EXPLANTED`. `implant_record_count` on a case counts that case's implant records whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`. `recall_open_count` on a vendor counts that vendor's field actions whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `IN_REMEDIATION`. `notification_open_count` on a field action counts that action's recall notifications whose `status` is `PENDING`. `total_posted_charge_cents` on a case is described in §3.6, and `readiness_status` in §3.10.

Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggered them, and the effects are visible in that same response. A cascade that is described below as running "in turn" is not optional and is not deferred: when a device unit's custody changes, its lot's counters are re-derived, and that re-derivation in turn re-derives the catalog item's availability counter, and so on to the end of the chain. Every row touched anywhere along such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Vendors, Consignment Terms and the Device Catalog

Nothing enters the building until the manufacturer behind it exists in the system. An `InventoryLead` registers a vendor with a legal `name` and a short `vendor_code`, which must be non-empty and unique across all vendors; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`. A freshly registered vendor opens with `standing` set to `ACTIVE`, `open_consignment_liability_cents` at 0 and `recall_open_count` at 0. The `standing` field takes only `ACTIVE` or `HOLD`, and it is never set by a client — the recall workflow of §3.8 is the only thing that moves it.

Consigned stock is stock the hospital holds but does not yet own, and the terms of that arrangement live on a consignment agreement. Creating one requires the owning `vendor_id`, an `agreement_code` that must be unique among that vendor's agreements, a `billing_trigger`, a `reconciliation_method`, and the two ends of the true-up window, `period_start_utc` and `period_end_utc`, both of which are treated as inclusive bounds. Two optional terms carry defaults: `invoice_tolerance_bps`, the absolute variance tolerance in basis points, defaults to 150 and must be at least 0 and at most 10000; `replenishment_days`, the contractual replenishment service level in days, defaults to 14 and is recorded for reporting only — no rule in this service enforces it. The agreement opens in `DRAFT` with `price_list_version` at 1.

`billing_trigger` decides *when* a consigned unit becomes a liability: `ON_IMPLANT` means the hospital owes for the unit at the moment it goes into a patient, and `ON_RECEIPT` means it owes from the moment the lot clears quarantine. `reconciliation_method` decides *how* an invoice under that agreement is checked, and takes either `UNIT_MATCH` or `PERIOD_TRUEUP`; the two produce materially different expected totals, as §3.10 sets out.

An agreement moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` when the `InventoryLead` activates it, which advances the agreement's `version` by one; attempting to activate an agreement that is not in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. Recording a vendor price-list revision against an `ACTIVE` agreement increments `price_list_version` by exactly one and likewise advances the agreement's `version` by one — that second increment is what makes an in-flight vendor invoice stale, and it is deliberate. An agreement that is not `ACTIVE` cannot take a price-list revision. The only remaining transition is `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, which no user performs; it happens during the operational checkpoint of §3.11 once the agreement's `period_end_utc` has passed.

The device catalog holds one row per device model. Registering one requires the manufacturing `vendor_id`, the `device_identifier` — the GS1 GTIN-14 that forms the UDI-DI half of the identifier and which must be globally unique and non-empty — a human-readable `description`, a `category` of `HIGH_COST_IMPLANT`, `STANDARD_IMPLANT` or `ACCESSORY`, a `list_charge_cents` chargemaster price that must be strictly greater than zero, the `charge_code` that will be stamped onto every charge line for this model, and `shelf_life_days`, a nominal shelf life recorded for information and read by no rule. One optional flag, `registry_required`, defaults to false; when it is true, verifying an implant of this model creates a national-registry obligation. A new catalog item opens with `on_hand_available_qty` at 0.

### 3.2. Receiving: Lots, Serialized Units and the Quarantine Gate

Physical stock arrives as a lot. A `ReceivingTech` books it in with the `catalog_item_id` of the model it contains, the manufacturer's `lot_number` — the UDI-PI lot, which must be unique among that catalog item's lots, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_NUMBER` — and an `expiration_date` written as a plain calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. That calendar date is not itself an instant, and the distinction matters for every expiry comparison in this document: **the expiry instant of a lot is its `expiration_date` at 23:59:59.999Z**, so a lot dated for today is still good for the whole of today. Booking a lot sets its `status` to `QUARANTINE`, stamps `received_at_utc` from the logical clock, records the acting user in `received_by_user_id`, and starts `received_qty` at 0. A lot's `status` takes one of `QUARANTINE`, `AVAILABLE`, `RECALLED`, `EXPIRED` or `DEPLETED`, and the last three are terminal.

Serials are then added to the quarantined lot one at a time. Each addition supplies the `udi_serial` that forms the UDI-PI serial half of the identifier, the `acquisition_cost_cents` negotiated for *this individual serial* — which must be strictly greater than zero — and the `expected_lot_version` demanded by §2.7. Two optional fields ride along: `consignment_agreement_id`, which when supplied puts the unit on consignment terms and when omitted leaves it hospital-owned outright, and `replaced_unit_id`, the self-reference used when this serial is going in to replace a previously explanted one. A serial must be unique among the units of the same catalog item, resolved through the unit's lot; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UDI_SERIAL`. Units may only be added while the lot is still in `QUARANTINE` — a later attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE` — and when a consignment agreement is named, that agreement must be `ACTIVE` or the request fails with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Each accepted serial opens with `custody_state` at `QUARANTINED`, `billing_state` at `NOT_BILLABLE`, `ownership` at `CONSIGNED` when an agreement was named and `OWNED` otherwise, and a `udi_di_snapshot` copied verbatim from the catalog item's `device_identifier` at this moment — a frozen copy, so that later catalog edits can never retroactively invalidate a transcription check. Its `vendor_invoice_id`, `reserved_case_id`, `contract_price_cents`, `price_list_version_at_billing`, `implanted_at_utc` and `expired_at_utc` all start null. Adding the unit re-derives the lot's `received_qty` and `on_hand_qty` and advances the lot's `version` by one, which is precisely why the next serial in the same carton needs a refreshed `expected_lot_version`.

Quarantine release is the incoming-inspection gate, and it is the first of the three separated duties. Either a `ReceivingTech` or an `InventoryLead` may release, but the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the lot's `received_by_user_id`, and a lot with a `received_qty` of zero cannot be released at all — an empty lot has nothing to inspect, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. Release moves the lot `QUARANTINE → AVAILABLE`, stores the acting user in `released_by_user_id`, and cascades: every unit on the lot sitting in `QUARANTINED` moves to `AVAILABLE`, the lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` are re-derived, and the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty` is re-derived in turn.

Release is also the first place money can appear. For any released unit that is `CONSIGNED`, whose agreement is `ACTIVE`, and whose agreement's `billing_trigger` is `ON_RECEIPT`, the unit's `billing_state` moves `NOT_BILLABLE → BILLABLE`; at that instant, and only at that instant, `contract_price_cents` is frozen as a copy of that unit's `acquisition_cost_cents` and `price_list_version_at_billing` is frozen as a copy of the agreement's current `price_list_version`. That billing change in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`. Units on `ON_IMPLANT` terms, and all `OWNED` units, stay `NOT_BILLABLE` through release.

Two ways exist to take a unit out of circulation short of implanting it. Wasting a unit — the case where the package was opened but the device never went in — is a `CircClinician` action, permitted when the unit's `custody_state` is either `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`, and it moves that unit to the terminal `WASTED` state while clearing `reserved_case_id`. Both source states share one side-effect set: the lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` and the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty` are re-derived, and when the lot is `AVAILABLE` and its recomputed `on_hand_qty` reaches 0 while `received_qty` is at least 1, the lot auto-transitions `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED`. Wasting a unit that was `RESERVED` does everything the `AVAILABLE` branch does and additionally re-derives the readiness posture of the case it was detached from. Attempting to waste a unit in any other custody state — an `IMPLANTED` one, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.

### 3.3. Patients and the Two Geometries of the Operating-Room Schedule

A `CircClinician` registers a patient with an `mrn` that must be globally unique and non-empty and a `display_name`, plus an optional `registry_consent` that defaults to `UNKNOWN` and otherwise takes `GRANTED` or `DECLINED`. A new patient's `implant_count` starts at 0. Consent is not frozen: a demographic update, available to a `CircClinician` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, may revise `registry_consent` and `display_name`, and doing so advances the patient's `version` by one. Vendors never see patient records at all.

Scheduling a case requires the `patient_id` it operates on, a globally unique `case_number`, the `or_room_code` naming the theatre, the booking window `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc`, and the `surgeon_code` of the operating surgeon. The end must be strictly after the start, and a request that violates that is malformed rather than conflicting. A new case opens in `SCHEDULED` with `readiness_status` at `READY` and `blocked_reservation_count` at 0. Case `status` takes one of `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` or `ABORTED`; the last three are terminal.

Two occupancy rules guard scheduling, and they use **deliberately different interval geometry**. The room rule is half-open and unbuffered: the requested window is treated as including its start instant and excluding its end instant, and it is compared against the identically-treated window of every other case in the same `or_room_code` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Two windows that merely touch — one case's end instant equal to another's start instant — do **not** intersect and are accepted, which is what lets a room be turned over back to back. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_OR_ROOM_CONFLICT`.

The patient rule is closed and buffered, because a human body needs recovery time that a room does not. Take the requested window and every existing window of the same `patient_id` whose case `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and expand each by a recovery buffer of 120 minutes — two hours — on *each* side — that is, from two hours before its start to two hours after its end — then compare the expanded windows as closed intervals in which both endpoints belong to the interval. Under closed comparison, touching endpoints **do** intersect and the request is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The room check is evaluated before the patient check, so a request that violates both reports the room conflict.

Starting a case moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and advances the case's `version` by one; a case not in `SCHEDULED` cannot be started and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The same cancel action serves two very different purposes depending on where the case is, and the two branches have deliberately different side-effect sets. From `SCHEDULED` the case moves to `CANCELLED`: every unit still `RESERVED` to it returns to `AVAILABLE` with `reserved_case_id` cleared, and `blocked_reservation_count` is reset to 0. From `IN_PROGRESS` the case moves to `ABORTED`, which does all of that *and* runs a three-hop void cascade — every implant record of the case still in `DOCUMENTED` moves to `VOIDED`; each record moving to `VOIDED` in turn moves every charge line of that record in `DRAFT`, `POSTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` to `VOIDED`; each charge moving to `VOIDED` in turn re-derives the case's `total_posted_charge_cents`; and each record moving to `VOIDED` in turn re-derives that patient's `implant_count`. Records already `VERIFIED`, `AMENDED` or `EXPLANTED` are untouched by the abort. A case in any terminal state cannot be cancelled again.

### 3.4. Reserving Stock: First-Expiring-First-Out and the Reservation Round Trip

Reserving a device against a scheduled case is available to both a `CircClinician` and an `InventoryLead`, and the request takes exactly one of two mutually exclusive forms. Supplying `catalog_item_id` asks the server to pick a serial; supplying `device_unit_id` pins a specific one. Supplying both, or neither, is malformed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either way the operation succeeds with a 201 and returns the reserved device unit, not the case.

A reservation is only legal against a case that is still `SCHEDULED`; against a case in any other status it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The pinned-serial form then walks the safety checks in the ladder order of §2.6: a unit in `RECALL_HOLD`, or one whose lot is `RECALLED`, is refused with `ERR_UNIT_RECALLED`; a unit whose lot expiry instant falls on or before the logical clock, or on or before the case's `scheduled_end_utc`, is refused with `ERR_UNIT_EXPIRED` — the lot must survive strictly past the end of the booking, because a device that expires mid-operation is useless; a unit whose lot is still in `QUARANTINE` is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`, deliberately ahead of the unit-level check so the operator is told the actual reason; and only then is the unit's own `custody_state` judged, which must be `AVAILABLE` with a null `reserved_case_id` or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.

The catalog-driven form runs a first-expiring-first-out selection instead, and it never sees the safety checks above because it only ever considers units that already pass them. The eligible pool is every device unit whose `custody_state` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `reserved_case_id` is null, whose lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose lot's `catalog_item_id` equals the requested one, and whose lot expiry instant is strictly after the case's `scheduled_end_utc`. Units in `QUARANTINED`, `RESERVED`, `RECALL_HOLD`, `IMPLANTED` or any terminal custody state are excluded outright, as are units whose lot expires on or before the case's scheduled end. The pool is then ordered by the lot's `expiration_date` ascending, so that the stock closest to expiring leaves the shelf first; ties on that date are broken by the lot's `received_at_utc` ascending, and any remaining tie by the device unit's own `created_at` ascending. No part of this selection ever reads a resource `id`. The first candidate in that order is the one reserved. If the pool is empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNIT`.

Whichever form was used, reserving moves the chosen unit `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`, sets its `reserved_case_id` to the case, decrements the case's `blocked_reservation_count` by one but never below zero, re-derives the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty`, and re-derives the case's `readiness_status`. That floored decrement is the repair path for the recall and expiry cascades of §3.8 and §3.11, which are the only things that ever raise the counter.

The round trip back is the reservation release, permitted to an `InventoryLead` or a `CircClinician`. It requires a unit whose `reserved_case_id` is non-null — a unit that is not reserved is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE` — and a case whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. It moves the unit `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`, clears `reserved_case_id`, re-derives the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty` and the case's `readiness_status`, and — as §2.11 promised — leaves the lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` numerically unchanged, because both `AVAILABLE` and `RESERVED` sit in the same conservation bucket. It does not raise `blocked_reservation_count`; a voluntary release is not a blockage.

### 3.5. Documenting the Implant, Verifying It, and Amending It

Documentation is the moment the supply chain and the medical record meet. A `CircClinician` records an implant beneath the case with the `device_unit_id` of the serial that went in, the `udi_di` and `udi_pi_serial` transcribed at the point of care, the `anatomic_site`, a `laterality` of `LEFT`, `RIGHT`, `BILATERAL` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and the `expected_case_version` required by §2.7. An optional `patient_id` may be supplied for belt-and-braces confirmation; when it is omitted the patient is copied from the case, and when it is supplied it must equal the case's `patient_id` or the request is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_LINK_MISMATCH`. The transcription is checked against the physical unit: `udi_di` must equal the unit's frozen `udi_di_snapshot` and `udi_pi_serial` must equal its `udi_serial`, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UDI_INCOMPLETE` — this is the guard against a nurse reading the wrong package.

Documentation requires the case to be `IN_PROGRESS`; a case still `SCHEDULED`, or already closed, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The unit itself may arrive from either of two custody states, and **the branch is chosen by the unit's current `custody_state`, never by anything in the request body**. The ordinary path is `RESERVED → IMPLANTED`, and it requires that the unit's `reserved_case_id` is exactly this case. The emergency path is `AVAILABLE → IMPLANTED`, covering unreserved stock grabbed off the shelf mid-procedure, and it requires that `reserved_case_id` is null. A unit that is `RESERVED` to some *other* case satisfies neither branch and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. Both branches additionally require the lot to be `AVAILABLE` — a still-quarantined lot reports `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` first — and the lot's expiry instant to be strictly after the logical clock, failing which the answer is `ERR_UNIT_EXPIRED`; a unit under a field action reports `ERR_UNIT_RECALLED` ahead of both.

The two branches then run an identical side-effect set, and it is long. A new implant record is created in `DOCUMENTED` carrying `surgical_case_id`, `device_unit_id` and `patient_id`, with `documented_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `documented_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, `amendment_count` at 0, and `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at_utc` null. The device unit moves to `IMPLANTED`, its `reserved_case_id` is cleared, and its `implanted_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock. When that unit's `ownership` is `CONSIGNED` and its agreement is `ACTIVE` with a `billing_trigger` of `ON_IMPLANT`, its `billing_state` moves `NOT_BILLABLE → BILLABLE`, `contract_price_cents` is frozen from `acquisition_cost_cents` and `price_list_version_at_billing` from the agreement's current `price_list_version`; that billing change in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`. A unit already made billable at receipt is not made billable a second time and its frozen price is not refreshed. Finally the lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty`, the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty`, the patient's `implant_count`, the case's `implant_record_count` and the case's `readiness_status` are all re-derived, and the case's `version` is advanced by one — which means a client documenting a second implant on the same case must first re-read the case to obtain a fresh `expected_case_version`. The response is a 201 carrying the new implant record.

Verification is the second separated duty. Either a `CircClinician` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may verify, from `DOCUMENTED → VERIFIED` or from `AMENDED → VERIFIED`, and in both cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the record's `documented_by_user_id` or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A record in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE`. Both source states write `verified_by_user_id` from the acting user and `verified_at_utc` from the logical clock, and both re-derive the case's `readiness_status`. They differ in exactly one respect, and only because of what already exists: coming from `DOCUMENTED` there is normally no registry submission yet, so one is created under the rules below; coming from `AMENDED` a submission generally already exists and is left exactly as it is. The controlling condition in both cases is the same — a submission is created only when the record has none.

When the implanted model's `registry_required` is true and no registry submission yet exists for this record, verification creates one, and which state it opens in depends on the patient. If the patient's `registry_consent` is anything other than `DECLINED` — that is, `GRANTED` or `UNKNOWN` — the submission is created in `REQUIRED` with `deadline_utc` set to this verification's `verified_at_utc` plus 30 days. If the patient's `registry_consent` is `DECLINED`, the submission is created directly in the terminal `EXEMPT` state, recording that the obligation was recognised and lawfully waived. When `registry_required` is false, verification creates nothing at all.

Amendment reopens a verified record. A `CircClinician` may move a record `VERIFIED → AMENDED`; from any other status the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE`. Amending increments `amendment_count` by one, moves any registry submission for this record that is sitting in `SUBMITTED` back to `REQUIRED` — because the registry was told something that has now changed — and re-derives the case's `readiness_status`. The `VERIFIED → AMENDED → VERIFIED` cycle is unbounded and every lap is counted in `amendment_count`.

### 3.6. Charge Capture Accuracy for High-Cost Implants

Hospitals lose money on implants in two directions: they under-bill when the chargemaster is stale, and they over-bill when the negotiated serial price came in lower than list. This service therefore prices every implant **twice** — once optimistically at the point of care and once definitively after the acquisition price is known — and forces a human acknowledgement whenever the two disagree materially on an expensive device.

A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` opens a charge line beneath an implant record. The only input is an optional `quantity`, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. The line is created in `DRAFT` with its `charge_code` copied from the catalog item reached through the implanted unit and its lot, with `capture_variant` set to `PROVISIONAL`, with `final_charge_cents` and `variance_cents` null, and with `provisional_charge_cents` computed immediately. Charge `status` takes one of `DRAFT`, `POSTED`, `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED`, the last two terminal. Throughout, `effective_charge_cents` is a derived view of the line: it is `final_charge_cents` whenever that is non-null, and `provisional_charge_cents` otherwise.

Both pricings run the same four-step ladder and differ only in **which base they start from**. The provisional pricing starts from the catalog item's `list_charge_cents` — the chargemaster list. The final pricing starts from the device unit's `acquisition_cost_cents` — what this exact serial cost. From that base, a markup percentage is selected from a tier ladder whose lower bounds are inclusive and whose upper bounds are exclusive: a base below 50,000 cents takes 60 percent; a base of at least 50,000 cents and below 250,000 cents takes 45 percent; a base of at least 250,000 cents and below 1,000,000 cents takes 32 percent; a base of 1,000,000 cents or more takes 22 percent. Because the ladder is keyed on the base itself, a list price and an acquisition cost that land in different tiers also draw different markup percentages, so the two pricings can diverge by considerably more than the difference between the two bases — that divergence is the entire point of the exercise.

The arithmetic order is pinned. First the per-unit amount is computed as the base multiplied by one plus the markup percentage, and that per-unit amount is rounded half up to a whole cent **before** anything else happens. Only then is the rounded per-unit amount multiplied by the line's `quantity` to give the line amount. Finally, and only for a catalog item whose `category` is `HIGH_COST_IMPLANT`, the line amount is capped: it becomes the lesser of the computed line amount and the base plus 750,000 cents. The cap is applied *after* the quantity multiply, not before it. Lines for `STANDARD_IMPLANT` and `ACCESSORY` items are never capped.

Posting a draft line moves it `DRAFT → POSTED`, which requires that the parent implant record is `VERIFIED` or `AMENDED` — posting against an unverified record is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE`, and posting a line that is not in `DRAFT` with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. Posting re-derives the case's `total_posted_charge_cents` and `readiness_status`.

Finalizing a posted line reruns the pricing under the final variant, writes `final_charge_cents`, writes `variance_cents` as the line's `provisional_charge_cents` minus its `final_charge_cents` — provisional first, final second, so an over-priced provisional yields a positive variance — and sets `capture_variant` to `FINAL`. It then **forks on the computed numbers alone, never on any request field**: when the catalog item's `category` is `HIGH_COST_IMPLANT` and the absolute value of `variance_cents` is at least 25,000 cents, the line moves `POSTED → CORRECTION_REQUIRED`; in every other case — a smaller absolute variance, or any non-high-cost category regardless of variance — it moves `POSTED → FINALIZED`. Both branches write the same three fields and both re-derive the case's `total_posted_charge_cents` and `readiness_status`; they differ only in the state reached. Finalizing a line that is not `POSTED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`.

A line held for correction is released by an explicit acknowledgement. The analyst supplies `acknowledged_variance_cents`, which must equal the line's stored `variance_cents` exactly — same sign, same magnitude — or the request is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_ACK_MISMATCH`. A correct acknowledgement moves the line `CORRECTION_REQUIRED → FINALIZED` and re-derives the case's `readiness_status`. The correction endpoint changes no monetary field; the acknowledgement is a signature, not a repricing.

Voiding a line is the compensating action and is permitted from `DRAFT`, `POSTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, all three moving to the terminal `VOIDED`; all three re-derive the case's `total_posted_charge_cents` and `readiness_status`. Because the case total is defined as the sum of `effective_charge_cents` over exactly those charge lines of the case's records whose `status` is `POSTED`, `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` or `FINALIZED` — `DRAFT` and `VOIDED` lines contribute nothing — voiding a posted line returns the case total precisely to its pre-posting value. That summation is itself an ordering rule: each line is already a whole-cent integer, and the case total is the plain integer sum of those already-rounded lines, never a sum of unrounded products rounded once at the end.

### 3.7. Registry Submission and Adjudication

Registry submissions are never created by a client; they appear only as a consequence of verifying an implant of a model whose `registry_required` flag is set, as §3.5 describes, and each one carries the `implant_record_id` of the record it reports. A submission's `status` takes one of `REQUIRED`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `EXEMPT`, with `ACCEPTED` and `EXEMPT` terminal. Its `registry_code`, `submitted_at_utc`, `adjudicated_at_utc` and `rejection_reason` all start null, and `resubmission_count` starts at 0.

Whether a submission is fit to send is decided by a `completeness_score` between 0 and 100 which is **not stored once and forgotten**: it is recomputed from live data every time a submission is read individually, every time it appears in a list, and on every submission attempt. The score starts at zero and accumulates five weighted components drawn from the implant record named by the submission, that record's patient, its device unit and that unit's lot — no other record participates. Thirty points are awarded when both the record's `udi_di` and its `udi_pi_serial` are non-empty; if exactly one of the two is non-empty, a partial fifteen points are awarded instead; if neither is, nothing is awarded. Twenty-five points are awarded when the patient's `registry_consent` is `GRANTED`, and nothing when it is `DECLINED` or `UNKNOWN`. Twenty points are awarded when the record's `anatomic_site` is non-empty **and** its `laterality` is anything other than `NOT_APPLICABLE`; both conditions must hold. Fifteen points are awarded when the verification turnaround was prompt — take the interval from the record's `documented_at_utc` to its `verified_at_utc`, express it in whole hours discarding any fraction, and award the points when that number of hours is at most 72, which is three days. Ten points are awarded when the lot carries an `expiration_date` at all. From that running total, a penalty of 20 points is subtracted when the device's lot is in `RECALLED` status and nothing is subtracted otherwise. The final `completeness_score` is that difference clamped into the range 0 to 100: anything below 0 becomes 0 and anything above 100 becomes 100.

A `ComplianceOfficer` submits to the national registry, supplying the external `registry_code`. Submission is legal from `REQUIRED` and from `REJECTED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. The underlying implant record must be `VERIFIED` or `EXPLANTED` — a record voided or reopened for amendment cannot be reported, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE`. Only after those state checks pass is the freshly recomputed `completeness_score` compared against the threshold: a score of at least 80 may be submitted, and a score of 79 or less is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRY_SUBMISSION_INCOMPLETE`. A successful submission moves the record to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at_utc` from the logical clock, stores the supplied `registry_code`, and re-derives the case's `readiness_status`. The two source states differ in exactly one side effect: coming from `REJECTED` the submission also increments `resubmission_count` by one, counting the laps of the `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` cycle; coming from `REQUIRED` the counter is untouched.

Adjudication records what the registry said back. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies an `outcome` of either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, plus an optional `rejection_reason`. Adjudication is legal only from `SUBMITTED`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. An `ACCEPTED` outcome moves the submission to the terminal `ACCEPTED`, stamps `adjudicated_at_utc` from the logical clock and re-derives the case's `readiness_status`. A `REJECTED` outcome does exactly the same three things and additionally stores the supplied `rejection_reason` — which for that outcome is not optional at all: an adjudication of `REJECTED` without a present, non-empty `rejection_reason` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

One further transition is driven from elsewhere: amending the underlying implant record drags any submission sitting in `SUBMITTED` back to `REQUIRED`, as §3.5 sets out, and that reversal re-derives the case's `readiness_status` too. A submission that has already been `ACCEPTED` is terminal and is not dragged back.

Every submission also carries a `deadline_utc`, fixed at creation to the verification instant plus 30 days. Nothing enforces that deadline as a hard gate; the operational checkpoint of §3.11 merely counts breaches.

### 3.8. Field Actions: Exposure Lookup, Quarantine Cascade and Patient Outreach

When a manufacturer issues a recall or other field action, the hospital has two obligations that pull in opposite directions: freeze every affected unit still on the shelf, and reach every patient already carrying one. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts the action with the issuing `vendor_id`, the `catalog_item_id` of the model in scope, a globally unique `action_code`, an `action_class` of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`, a `scope_type` of `CATALOG`, `LOT` or `SERIAL_RANGE`, and the regulatory `effective_from_utc` from which every outreach clock is measured. Scope-dependent fields must match the declared scope exactly: `lot_numbers` is required when `scope_type` is `LOT` and must be omitted otherwise, and `serial_low` and `serial_high` are required when `scope_type` is `SERIAL_RANGE` and must be omitted otherwise; getting that wrong is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A drafted action opens in `DRAFT` with `published_at_utc` null and `implanted_cohort_size`, `on_hand_cohort_size` and `notification_open_count` all 0. Its `status` takes `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `IN_REMEDIATION` or `CLOSED`, the last terminal.

The **exposure cohort** is the heart of the feature. Every candidate device unit is resolved through its lot, and the base predicate is that the lot's `catalog_item_id` equals the action's `catalog_item_id`. Under `CATALOG` scope, the base predicate alone decides. Under `LOT` scope, the unit additionally qualifies only when its lot's `lot_number` is one of the values listed in `lot_numbers`. Under `SERIAL_RANGE` scope, the unit additionally qualifies only when its `udi_serial` compares as at or after `serial_low` and at or before `serial_high` under a plain lexicographic byte comparison — **both ends of the range are inclusive**. From the qualifying set, the on-hand cohort is those units whose `custody_state` is `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`, and its size is `on_hand_cohort_size`. The implanted side takes the qualifying units whose `custody_state` is `IMPLANTED` or `EXPLANTED`, then collects the distinct implant records pointing at those units whose own `status` is `DOCUMENTED`, `VERIFIED`, `AMENDED` or `EXPLANTED`; the count of those distinct records is `implanted_cohort_size`, and a `VOIDED` record never enters it. Units already in `WASTED`, `EXPIRED`, `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR` or `DISPOSED` enter neither cohort.

Publishing runs that cohort for real and is legal only from `DRAFT`. It stamps `published_at_utc` from the logical clock, writes both cohort sizes, and then executes a four-hop cascade. Every matched lot whose `status` is `QUARANTINE` or `AVAILABLE` moves to the terminal `RECALLED`. Each lot moving to `RECALLED` in turn moves each of its units in `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED` to `RECALL_HOLD`, re-deriving that lot's `on_hand_qty` and the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty`. Each `RESERVED` unit moving to `RECALL_HOLD` in turn has its `reserved_case_id` cleared and increments the detached case's `blocked_reservation_count` by one, which in turn flips that case's `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED_RESERVATION` — that is the serialization point between the recall and any case still trying to close. Separately, one recall notification is created in `PENDING` for each distinct implant record in the implanted cohort, in ascending `created_at` order of those records, each carrying the originating `field_action_id`, its `implant_record_id`, the `patient_id` denormalized from the record so that recall lookup never needs a join, an `escalation_level` of 0 and a null `outcome_note`. Finally, an `action_class` of `CLASS_I` sets the vendor's `standing` to `HOLD`, freezing that vendor's invoicing; `CLASS_II` and `CLASS_III` leave `standing` alone. In every case the vendor's `recall_open_count` is re-derived.

Each notification's `due_utc` is measured from the action's `effective_from_utc`, not from publication, and the window depends on severity: a `CLASS_I` action allows 7 days, a `CLASS_II` action 30 days, and a `CLASS_III` action 90 days. Notification `status` takes `PENDING`, `NOTIFIED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `UNREACHABLE` or `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal.

Outreach is recorded by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying an `outcome` of `NOTIFIED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `UNREACHABLE` and an optional `outcome_note`. All three outcomes are legal only from `PENDING`; a notification already resolved is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LIFECYCLE_STATE`. All three branches carry an identical side-effect set: the outcome and the note are stored, the action's `notification_open_count` is re-derived, and — on the first resolution against a still-`PUBLISHED` action — the action itself bubbles up `PUBLISHED → IN_REMEDIATION`. An action already in `IN_REMEDIATION` stays there.

Closing an action requires `notification_open_count` to be exactly 0; while any notification is still `PENDING` the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RECALL_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE`. Closing is legal from `IN_REMEDIATION` and also directly from `PUBLISHED` — the latter is reachable precisely when the implanted cohort was empty, so that zero notifications were ever created and the open count never left 0. The `ComplianceOfficer` supplies `on_hand_disposition`, which takes `RETURN_TO_VENDOR` or `DISPOSE` and decides the fate of the frozen stock. Closing moves every notification of this action sitting in `NOTIFIED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `UNREACHABLE` to `CLOSED`; moves every device unit in `RECALL_HOLD` attributable to this action to the terminal `RETURNED_TO_VENDOR` under a `RETURN_TO_VENDOR` disposition or to the terminal `DISPOSED` under a `DISPOSE` disposition, re-deriving each affected lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` either way; and re-derives the vendor's `recall_open_count`, restoring that vendor's `standing` to `ACTIVE` at the moment the count reaches 0. A vendor with another open action stays on `HOLD`.

Alongside all of that sits a pure read used for recall lookup across implanted patients: it recomputes the exposure cohort from scratch and answers with `implanted_cohort_size`, `on_hand_cohort_size`, the list of affected `patient_ids`, the list of affected `implant_record_ids`, and the current `notification_open_count`. It mutates nothing — it is safe to call against a `DRAFT` action to preview the blast radius before publishing.

### 3.9. Explantation, Return and Vendor Credit

An explant reverses an implant, and a `CircClinician` records it beneath the implant record with a clinical `reason` of `DEVICE_FAILURE`, `INFECTION`, `RECALL`, `UPGRADE` or `OTHER`, plus an optional `replacement_device_unit_id` naming the serial that went in instead during a revision. The record must be `VERIFIED` — an unverified or already-explanted record is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECORD_STATE` — and the device unit must be `IMPLANTED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. The response is a 201 carrying a new explant record in `RECORDED` with `explanted_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, `return_authorization_no` and `credit_issued_cents` null, and `credit_expected_cents` computed on the spot. Explant `status` takes `RECORDED`, `RETURN_AUTHORIZED`, `SHIPPED`, `CREDIT_ISSUED`, `CREDIT_DENIED` or `DISPOSED_LOCALLY`, with the last three terminal.

The expected credit degrades with time in the body. Measure the elapsed interval from the unit's `implanted_at_utc` to the logical clock, express it in whole days of 86,400 seconds each and discard any remainder. A unit implanted for at most 30 whole days earns 100 percent of its basis; a unit implanted for at least 31 and at most 180 whole days earns 75 percent; a unit implanted for 181 whole days or more earns 50 percent. The basis is the unit's frozen `contract_price_cents` when that is non-null, and zero when it is null — so a hospital-owned unit that never became billable produces a `credit_expected_cents` of 0. The basis multiplied by the tier percentage is rounded half up to a whole cent.

Recording the explant sets off a chain. The implant record moves `VERIFIED → EXPLANTED`. The device unit moves `IMPLANTED → EXPLANTED`, and when its `billing_state` is `BILLABLE` or `INVOICED` it moves to `CREDIT_PENDING`; that billing change in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`, removing exactly the amount the implant had added. A unit that is `NOT_BILLABLE` stays `NOT_BILLABLE`. The lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` are re-derived — both `IMPLANTED` and `EXPLANTED` sit in the consumed bucket, so those counters do not actually move, but the derivation runs. Every charge line of that record sitting in `POSTED` moves to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, because a device that came back out cannot be billed at the price of a device that stayed in; that charge change in turn re-derives the case's `total_posted_charge_cents` and `readiness_status`.

Disposition is the fork that decides whether the vendor ever sees the device again, and the two branches have deliberately different side-effect sets. An `InventoryLead` supplies a `disposition` of either `RETURN_TO_VENDOR` or `DISPOSE_LOCALLY`, legal only from `RECORDED`. The return branch requires a present, non-empty `return_authorization_no` — the vendor's RA number — or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it stores that number and moves the explant `RECORDED → RETURN_AUTHORIZED`, touching neither the unit's custody nor its billing, so the credit stays alive. The local-disposal branch takes no RA number and moves the explant `RECORDED → DISPOSED_LOCALLY`, moves the device unit `EXPLANTED → DISPOSED`, and moves its `billing_state` `CREDIT_PENDING → BILLABLE`, which in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents` — the hospital keeps the liability precisely because nothing is going back.

Shipping an authorized return is an `InventoryLead` action legal only from `RETURN_AUTHORIZED`. It moves the explant to `SHIPPED` and the device unit `EXPLANTED → RETURNED_TO_VENDOR`, re-deriving the lot's `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty`. It does not touch the vendor liability — as §2.11 promised, the liability already left the sum at explant time and neither shipping nor crediting touches it again.

The vendor closes the loop. A `VendorRep`, acting only on their own vendor's devices, records `credit_issued_cents` against a `SHIPPED` explant. That amount must not exceed the record's `credit_expected_cents`; a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED`. A strictly positive amount moves the explant to the terminal `CREDIT_ISSUED` and the device unit's `billing_state` `CREDIT_PENDING → CREDITED`. An amount of exactly zero is a denial: the explant moves to the terminal `CREDIT_DENIED` and the device unit's `billing_state` moves `CREDIT_PENDING → BILLABLE`, putting the liability back onto the hospital's open balance — the compensating branch for a return the vendor refused to honour. Both branches store the supplied figure and re-derive the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`.

### 3.10. Consignment Reconciliation and Vendor Billing

The vendor bills the hospital for consumed consignment stock, and the hospital checks that bill against its own record of what it consumed. A `VendorRep` submits an invoice carrying the `vendor_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, since a vendor may only bill for itself — the `consignment_agreement_id` being billed under, an `invoice_number` unique among that vendor's invoices, the `submitted_total_cents` being asked for, which must be strictly greater than zero, a non-empty `device_unit_ids` array naming the serials being billed, and the `expected_agreement_version` required by §2.7. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the vendor's `standing` must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_VENDOR_ON_HOLD` — a vendor with an open `CLASS_I` field action cannot invoice at all. Each named serial must be free of an existing invoice, checked first: a unit whose `vendor_invoice_id` is already set is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_INVOICED`. Only after that does the billing check run: every named unit must be in `billing_state` `BILLABLE`, and one that is not is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_BILLABLE`. A successful submission creates the invoice in `SUBMITTED`, moves every listed unit `BILLABLE → INVOICED` with `vendor_invoice_id` pointing at the new invoice, and advances the agreement's `version` by one. Invoice `status` takes `SUBMITTED`, `MATCHED`, `VARIANCE_HOLD`, `DISPUTED` or `APPROVED`, with `APPROVED` terminal.

Matching is a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` action legal only from `SUBMITTED`. It computes an `expected_total_cents`, and **which of two algorithms runs is decided entirely by the agreement's `reconciliation_method`, never by anything on the invoice**; whichever ran is recorded in `reconciliation_variant`.

Under `UNIT_MATCH` the check is serial-level and never looks outside the invoice: `expected_total_cents` is the exact sum of `contract_price_cents` over the device units whose `vendor_invoice_id` equals this invoice at match time.

Under `PERIOD_TRUEUP` the check is period-level, and it deliberately widens the denominator beyond the invoice. Take each catalog item represented among the invoice's units and process them in ascending `created_at` order of the catalog items themselves. For each such item, assemble a pool consisting of every device unit whose `consignment_agreement_id` equals this invoice's agreement, whose lot points at that catalog item, whose `billing_state` is `BILLABLE`, `INVOICED` or `RECONCILED`, and whose `implanted_at_utc` falls inside the agreement's period treating both `period_start_utc` and `period_end_utc` as inclusive bounds — including units that are not on this invoice at all. That widening is the entire source of divergence between the two variants: when the price list moved mid-period, two byte-identical invoices reconcile to different expected totals. Sum the pool's `contract_price_cents`, divide by the number of units in the pool, and round that per-item average half up to a whole cent **before** multiplying — then multiply the rounded average by the number of *this invoice's* units belonging to that catalog item and add the product into the running total. The running total across all represented catalog items is `expected_total_cents`.

Whichever variant ran, `variance_cents` is then the invoice's `submitted_total_cents` minus its `expected_total_cents` — submitted first, expected second, so an over-billing vendor produces a positive variance — and `variance_bps` is the absolute value of that variance multiplied by 10,000, divided by `expected_total_cents`, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. Matching also records `matched_by_user_id` from the acting user. The fork is then on the computed number against the agreement's `invoice_tolerance_bps`: a `variance_bps` **strictly greater** than the tolerance sends the invoice `SUBMITTED → VARIANCE_HOLD`, and a `variance_bps` less than or exactly equal to the tolerance sends it `SUBMITTED → MATCHED`. Equality passes. Both branches write exactly the same four computed fields and the same `matched_by_user_id`; they differ only in the state reached.

Approval is the third separated duty and is open to both a `ComplianceOfficer` and a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`. It is legal from `MATCHED` and from `VARIANCE_HOLD`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the invoice's `matched_by_user_id` or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the agreement must still be `ACTIVE`. Approving from `VARIANCE_HOLD` additionally requires a present, non-empty `override_reason` — approving a held invoice without one is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED`. Approving from `MATCHED` needs no reason and ignores one if supplied. Both branches then run the identical settlement side-effect set: `credit_applied_cents` and `net_payable_cents` are computed and written, `approved_by_user_id` is recorded, the invoice moves to `APPROVED`, every device unit on the invoice moves `INVOICED → RECONCILED`, and that billing change in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`, since reconciled units leave the liability base.

The settlement arithmetic looks circular and is not, because its evaluation order is pinned. Begin with the gross amount due, which is the invoice's already-written `expected_total_cents` — never its `submitted_total_cents`. Next assemble the credit pool: the sum of `contract_price_cents` over every device unit whose `consignment_agreement_id` equals this invoice's agreement and whose `billing_state` is `CREDIT_PENDING`, regardless of which invoice, if any, those units were originally billed on. Next select a credit-cap percentage from a tier ladder **keyed on the gross amount due and on nothing else**: a gross below 500,000 cents caps credit at 20 percent; a gross of at least 500,000 cents and below 2,500,000 cents caps it at 35 percent; a gross of 2,500,000 cents or more caps it at 50 percent. The tier is never re-selected using the net payable — re-keying it that way is the wrong reading and produces a different, unstable answer. Then `credit_applied_cents` is the lesser of the credit pool and the gross amount due multiplied by the cap percentage rounded half up to a whole cent. Finally `net_payable_cents` is the gross amount due minus `credit_applied_cents`.

Not every invoice survives. A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` may dispute an invoice, legal only from `VARIANCE_HOLD`; a `MATCHED` or `SUBMITTED` invoice cannot be disputed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`. Disputing moves the invoice to `DISPUTED` and releases its stock: every device unit on it moves `INVOICED → BILLABLE` and has its `vendor_invoice_id` cleared, and that billing change in turn re-derives the vendor's `open_consignment_liability_cents`. The vendor then gets one more attempt: resubmission is a `VendorRep` action legal only from `DISPUTED`, taking a corrected `submitted_total_cents` and a corrected `device_unit_ids` list which replace the stored values outright. It moves the invoice back to `SUBMITTED`, moves every newly listed unit `BILLABLE → INVOICED` with `vendor_invoice_id` set, and resets `expected_total_cents`, `variance_cents`, `variance_bps`, `reconciliation_variant` and `matched_by_user_id` all back to null so that the invoice must be matched afresh by someone who is then free to approve it — the separation resets with the match. Resubmission repeats the vendor-standing, already-invoiced and billable checks that governed the original submission, but it carries no expected-version field of any kind.

### 3.11. Case Closure Readiness and the Operational Checkpoint

A case may not close while anything about it is unfinished, and "unfinished" is summarised in a single `readiness_status` that is re-derived after every mutation touching the case's reservations, implant records, registry submissions or charges. It takes one of `READY`, `BLOCKED_RESERVATION`, `BLOCKED_DOCUMENTATION`, `BLOCKED_REGISTRY` or `BLOCKED_CHARGE`, and it is evaluated **strictly top to bottom with the first match winning**. If the case's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and its `blocked_reservation_count` is greater than zero, the posture is `BLOCKED_RESERVATION`. Otherwise, if any implant record of this case has a `status` of `DOCUMENTED` or `AMENDED`, the posture is `BLOCKED_DOCUMENTATION`. Otherwise, if any registry submission belonging to this case's records has a `status` of `REQUIRED` or `REJECTED`, the posture is `BLOCKED_REGISTRY`. Otherwise, if any charge line belonging to this case's records has a `status` of `DRAFT`, `POSTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, the posture is `BLOCKED_CHARGE`. Otherwise the posture is `READY`. The records considered are this case's implant records in any status other than `VOIDED` and `EXPLANTED`, together with the registry submissions and charge lines reached through those records; a case with no implant records and no charges is `READY`.

A dedicated read exposes that posture together with the four counters behind it: `readiness_status`, the case's `blocked_reservation_count`, an `unverified_record_count` giving the number of this case's implant records in `DOCUMENTED` or `AMENDED`, an `outstanding_registry_count` giving the number of registry submissions of those records in `REQUIRED` or `REJECTED`, an `unfinalized_charge_count` giving the number of charge lines of those records in `DRAFT`, `POSTED` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, and the case's `total_posted_charge_cents`.

Closing is a `CircClinician` action legal only from `IN_PROGRESS`; a `SCHEDULED` case must be started first and a terminal case cannot be closed again, both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The close gate then maps each blocked posture to its own error, tried in exactly the same top-to-bottom order as the derivation above: `BLOCKED_RESERVATION` and `BLOCKED_DOCUMENTATION` both surface as `ERR_CASE_READINESS_UNMET`, `BLOCKED_REGISTRY` as `ERR_REGISTRY_OUTSTANDING`, and `BLOCKED_CHARGE` as `ERR_CHARGE_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED`. A `READY` case moves `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED`, and every device unit still `RESERVED` to it is released back to `AVAILABLE` with `reserved_case_id` cleared and the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty` re-derived.

Time itself does not pass in this system without being asked to. Advancing the operational epoch is an `InventoryLead` action that executes five rules **in this exact order**, with later rules observing everything earlier rules did, and with entities processed inside each rule in ascending `created_at` order and never in `id` order.

The first rule expires stale contracts: every consignment agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `period_end_utc` is strictly before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, and the number moved is reported as `expired_agreement_count`.

The second rule sweeps expired stock in a three-deep cascade. Every lot whose `status` is `QUARANTINE` or `AVAILABLE` and whose expiry instant — its `expiration_date` at 23:59:59.999Z — is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED` and adds one to `expired_lot_count`. For each unit on such a lot, a unit in `QUARANTINED` or `AVAILABLE` moves to `EXPIRED` with `expired_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock and adds one to `expired_unit_count`. A unit in `RESERVED` is treated differently but is **not** spared: it is first detached from its case by clearing `reserved_case_id`, then moved to `EXPIRED` with `expired_at_utc` stamped and counted in `expired_unit_count` too, and the detachment adds one to `detached_reservation_count` and increments that case's `blocked_reservation_count` by one, which in turn re-derives that case's `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED_RESERVATION`. Each swept lot then has its `on_hand_qty` and `consumed_qty` re-derived, which in turn re-derives the catalog item's `on_hand_available_qty`.

The third rule is a rotation alert and mutates nothing: `rotation_alert_count` is the number of device units whose `custody_state` is `AVAILABLE` and whose lot's expiry instant falls at or after the logical clock and at or before the logical clock plus 45 days — the stock that ought to be moved to the front of the shelf before it is written off.

The fourth rule escalates overdue outreach. Every recall notification whose `status` is `PENDING` and whose `due_utc` is strictly before the logical clock has its `escalation_level` incremented by one and is counted in `escalated_notification_count`. When a notification's `escalation_level` reaches 2 or more, it moves to `UNREACHABLE`, which re-derives its field action's `notification_open_count` and, when that action is still `PUBLISHED`, bubbles it up to `IN_REMEDIATION` — exactly as a manual resolution would. Two checkpoints past the due date are therefore enough to close a patient out as unreachable.

The fifth rule is a compliance report and mutates nothing: `overdue_submission_count` is the number of registry submissions whose `status` is `REQUIRED` or `REJECTED` and whose `deadline_utc` is strictly before the logical clock.

The response reports all eight figures — `ops_run_index`, `expired_agreement_count`, `expired_lot_count`, `expired_unit_count`, `detached_reservation_count`, `rotation_alert_count`, `escalated_notification_count` and `overdue_submission_count` — where `ops_run_index` is the number of checkpoints executed so far in this server's life, so that the very first one answers 1.
