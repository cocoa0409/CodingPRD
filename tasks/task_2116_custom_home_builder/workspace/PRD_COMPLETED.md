# Custom Home Builder API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A custom home is built once, for one family, against one contract, and almost every dollar that moves through the job is contested by somebody. The homeowner wants to know why the tile they picked cost more than the tile the builder budgeted. The construction lender wants to know that the money it advances this month is backed by approved invoices and by signed releases from the trades who earned them. The estimator wants a change order priced the same way every time. The superintendent wants the cabinet crew to show up the week the cabinets actually land on site, not the week the bar chart says they should. Today these four conversations happen in four different systems — a spreadsheet, an email thread, a shared drive of photographs, and a filing cabinet of paper waivers — and they disagree with one another constantly.

This document specifies the Custom Home Builder API, a single system of record for the financial and operational life of a residential build. Its spine is a ledger: every allowance line, every approved change order, every posted cost, and every funded draw contributes to one derived contract position that is recomputed the instant any of its inputs move. The same underlying data deliberately produces two different owner-billable answers depending on whether the job is written as a fixed-price contract or a cost-plus contract, and the same allowance line deliberately produces two different variance figures — one estimated at selection time and one settled against actual invoices. Neither pair is a contradiction; both are required, and the system must be able to state both without ambiguity.

Layered on top of the ledger is a negotiation system. A change order shuttles between an estimator who prices it, a project manager who submits it, and a homeowner who approves it, rejects it, or sends it back for a revision — and the same builder employee may never both price and submit the same change order. A draw request shuttles between a controller who assembles it, a lender who reviews it inside a contractual window, and the subcontractors whose lien waivers must be collected and verified before any money is released. Because these two axes of a draw — the lender's funding decision and the trades' waiver compliance — advance independently, the system tracks them separately and only releases funds when both agree. Everything downstream of substantial completion — the punch list, the final walkthrough, the warranty period, and the callback service that follows it — is specified here as well, because for the homeowner the job is not over when the last coat of paint dries.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header carrying nothing but whitespace counts as empty, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both values before either is matched or compared. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven roles below, the request is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the headers are accepted, the role must appear on the authorization list of the endpoint being called; when it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `PlatformAdmin`, `ProjectManager`, `Estimator`, `Controller`, `Client`, `Lender`, and `Subcontractor`.

**`PlatformAdmin`** is the back-office operator who seeds reference data and repositions the logical clock; it owns no business decision. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id` and never performs an ownership check against it.

**`ProjectManager`**, **`Estimator`**, and **`Controller`** are the three builder-side roles, and all three are backed by the same entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectManager`, `Estimator`, or `Controller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`. For these three roles the server additionally requires that the `StaffMember` so identified carries a `staff_function` matching the acting role — `ProjectManager` requires `PROJECT_MANAGER`, `Estimator` requires `ESTIMATOR`, and `Controller` requires `CONTROLLER`. A `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a `StaffMember` at all, or that resolves to one whose `staff_function` names a different function than the acting role, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Staff identity is not merely checked; it is recorded, because the segregation-of-duties rules described later read it back out of `priced_by_staff_id`, `submitted_by_staff_id`, and `approved_by_staff_id`.

**`Client`** is the homeowner. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Client` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which in practice means that the `client_id` of the project owning the target row must equal `X-User-ID`.

**`Lender`** is the construction lender. When `X-User-Role` is `Lender`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Lender` record; ownership checks compare the `lender_id` of the project owning the target draw request to `X-User-ID`.

**`Subcontractor`** is the trade contractor. When `X-User-Role` is `Subcontractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Subcontractor` record; ownership checks compare the target row's own trade reference to `X-User-ID` — the `subcontractor_id` of a schedule task, the `subcontractor_id` of a lien waiver, or the `responsible_subcontractor_id` of a defect ticket, depending on which resource is being acted upon.

Whenever a `Client`, `Lender`, or `Subcontractor` principal acts on a resource that these comparisons say it does not own, the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty random uppercase-alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time, so it moves only when the clock is repositioned.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls how many rows come back; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a list endpoint's description says otherwise, rows come back sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first. Because the logical clock stands still between repositionings, many rows share a `created_at`; ties are broken by insertion order, so the row written first comes first. The one deliberate exception is the listing of the selections proposed against a single allowance line, which is presented newest first, that is, that same total order reversed end to end — `created_at` descending with the later-written row first among ties — because the most recent proposal is the one the reader almost always wants.

Filter parameters are matched for exact equality against the stored field, and one supplied empty — or holding only whitespace — is treated as though it had not been supplied at all. List responses report their paging state in the `meta` object, which carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before paging is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps, and the Logical Jobsite Clock

Every resource in this system carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is first written and increases by one on every mutation of that row, with one deliberate exception: recomputing a project's derived ledger fields as a side effect of some row beneath it changing state refreshes that project's `updated_at` but leaves its `version` untouched, so a project's `version` moves only on the handful of operations this document names explicitly as incrementing it. Every entity described in this document — homeowners, lenders, staff members, subcontractors, projects, allowance items, selections, change orders, schedule tasks, cost entries, draw requests, lien waivers, defect tickets, and photo documents alike — carries all four; there is no entity in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server keeps a **logical clock** whose value is the "now" used to stamp every system-assigned and computed timestamp, to evaluate every deadline, and to decide every window comparison described in this document. Before the first repositioning call in a server process, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may reposition it by supplying a mandatory `to_iso8601` instant, and the 200 response's `data` carries the single field `current_utc` reporting the resulting clock reading. Everything that follows — a selection deadline, a lender's review window, a warranty coverage boundary, a defect response clock — is measured against this logical clock and against nothing else.

### 2.5. Money, Whole Days, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are decimal numbers carried to exactly **2 decimal places**, and every rounding operation in this document uses the **round-half-up** convention: a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero. Where a calculation applies rounding at more than one step, this document states the steps in the order they must be evaluated, and the order is binding — rounding an intermediate figure at a different point produces a different number of cents and is not an acceptable substitute.

A money value supplied on a request must be non-negative and must carry no more than two decimal places; a value that is negative where the field forbids it, or that carries a third decimal place, is a malformed request. Durations described as days are whole days; a fractional day is never stored and never added. Where this document says that a number of whole days is derived from the difference between two instants, that difference is truncated toward zero. Timestamps themselves are compared at millisecond resolution.

### 2.6. The Project Ledger and Its Derived Fields

Nine fields on a project are never written directly by a client; they are recomputed by the server from the rows beneath the project every time one of those rows changes state. Together they are the project ledger, and the correctness of this API is largely the correctness of this recomputation.

`approved_change_order_total` is the sum of `priced_amount` across the project's change orders whose status is `APPROVED`. Change orders in `DRAFT`, `PRICED`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, or `VOIDED` contribute nothing.

`allowance_variance_total` is a sum across the project's allowance items, where each item's contribution depends on its own status: an item in `RECONCILED` contributes its `final_variance_amount`; an item in `SELECTED` or `LOCKED` contributes its `provisional_variance_amount`; an item in `OPEN` or `FROZEN` contributes zero.

`contract_sum_current` is `base_contract_amount` plus `approved_change_order_total` plus `allowance_variance_total`, rounded half up to two decimal places. At project creation, before any change order or allowance line exists, it is initialised to `base_contract_amount`.

`cost_to_date` is the sum of `amount` across the project's cost entries whose status is `APPROVED`, `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW`, or `PAID`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `PENDING` and `REJECTED` entries never contribute. `eligible_cost_pool` is the narrower sum over cost entries whose status is `APPROVED` or `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW` only — it is the pool of money not yet paid out, and it is the quantity the draw machinery conserves.

`pending_draw_amount` is the sum of `requested_amount` across the project's draw requests whose status is `LENDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`. `funded_to_date` is the sum of `net_funding_amount` across the project's draw requests whose status is `FUNDED`, and `retainage_held` is the sum of `retainage_amount` across those same `FUNDED` draws.

`budget_posture` is a derived verdict recomputed after every mutation that moves `contract_sum_current`, `allowance_variance_total`, or `cost_to_date`. It is evaluated as a strictly ordered test. First: if the project carries a `gmp_cap` and `contract_sum_current` is **strictly greater than** that cap, the posture is `OVER_GMP`; a `contract_sum_current` exactly equal to `gmp_cap` is *not* over the cap. Otherwise, if `allowance_variance_total` is strictly greater than two percent of `base_contract_amount` — that is, `base_contract_amount` multiplied by a factor of 0.02 and rounded half up to two decimal places — the posture is `ALLOWANCE_OVERRUN`. Otherwise the posture is `WITHIN_BUDGET`. A newly created project starts at `WITHIN_BUDGET`.

The posture is not merely a label. When a recomputation moves the posture *into* `OVER_GMP`, every allowance item on that project that is currently `OPEN` cascades to `FROZEN`, has its `frozen_at` stamped with the logical clock now, and has its `version` incremented. When a later recomputation moves the posture *out of* `OVER_GMP`, every allowance item on that project that is currently `FROZEN` cascades back to `OPEN`, has its `frozen_at` cleared to null, and has its `version` incremented again. This freeze-and-thaw pair is the mechanism by which a budget breach stops the homeowner from committing further allowance dollars, and the mechanism by which descoping a change order releases them again.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties on the Builder's Books

Two operations in this system require that two different builder employees have touched the record, and both are enforced with the same rejection, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The first governs change orders: the `StaffMember` submitting a priced change order to the homeowner must not be the same `StaffMember` recorded in `priced_by_staff_id`. Because a `ProjectManager` is permitted to price change orders as well as submit them, this rule is genuinely reachable — a project manager who prices a change order themselves cannot then submit it, and must hand it to a colleague.

The second governs draws: the `StaffMember` submitting a draw request to the lender must not equal the `approved_by_staff_id` of any cost entry that the submission would pull into the draw. A controller who approved even one of the eligible invoices is barred from submitting the draw that bills for it.

### 2.8. Stale-Read Protection on Allowance Lines and Draws

Two write operations in this system are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because both of them legitimately destroy work that another writer may have just created.

Proposing a new selection against an allowance line requires the request to carry `expected_allowance_item_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `AllowanceItem` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the allowance item's current `version`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALLOWANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because creating a proposal supersedes whatever proposal was previously outstanding on the same line; a client working from a stale view of the allowance line would silently discard a proposal it never saw.

Opening a draw request against a project requires the request to carry `expected_project_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Project` as the controller last observed it. If that value does not equal the project's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because opening a draw supersedes any existing draft draw on the same project; a controller working from a stale project view would silently throw away a draft another writer had just assembled.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Where an endpoint is not named above, the client neither supplies nor is asked for an expected version.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Resources addressed through a nested path are resolved from left to right, and the first segment that names a row which does not exist decides the rejection. Each resource family has its own not-found code: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRAW_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIEN_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFECT_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_PHOTO_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

References supplied inside a request body are resolved on the same principle but at a later point in the ladder, once the body itself has been found well-formed. A reference to a homeowner that does not resolve yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`; a lender reference yields `ERR_LENDER_NOT_FOUND`; a staff reference yields `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`; a trade reference yields `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`; and project, allowance-item, and schedule-task references reuse `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_SCHEDULE_TASK_NOT_FOUND` respectively. This body-level resolution applies to every creation endpoint in the system and additionally to the triage of a defect ticket, where the responsible trade is named in the body rather than the path. Where a payload names more than one reference they are resolved in a fixed order, and the first that fails to resolve decides the rejection: on a project `client_id` then `lender_id`; on a schedule task `project_id`, then `subcontractor_id`, then `predecessor_task_id`, then `driving_allowance_item_id`; on a cost entry `project_id`, then `subcontractor_id`, then `source_allowance_item_id`; on a change order `project_id` then `source_allowance_item_id`; and on a selection, an allowance item, a draw request, a photo document or a defect ticket the single parent reference each carries.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Many requests in this system are capable of failing more than one way at once, and the benchmark for correctness is not merely *that* such a request is refused but *which* refusal it earns. Checks are evaluated in the following strict order, and the first check that fails ends the request; no later check may mask an earlier one.

1. **Headers present.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must both be present and non-empty on every request, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` must name one of the seven declared roles, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Role permitted.** The acting role must appear on the endpoint's authorization list, or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). This applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path resources exist.** Every row named by a path segment must exist, validated left to right, or the matching 404 from §2.9. This applies to every endpoint that takes a path parameter.
5. **Request body well-formed.** Every required field must be present, every enum-typed value must be a declared member of its enumeration, every money field must be non-negative with at most two decimals, a schedule task's `planned_finish` must be strictly later than its `planned_start`, a draw's `period_end` must be strictly later than its `period_start`, `limit` must fall within 1 through 100, and `offset` must not be negative. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This applies to every endpoint, with one positional caveat: on a list endpoint the `limit`/`offset` check is applied at the moment the page is cut, which places it after step 7 rather than before it, so a builder-side principal whose staff identity does not match its role earns `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` even when its paging parameters are also out of range.
6. **Body references resolve.** Every foreign key supplied in the body must name an existing row, or the matching 404 from §2.9. This applies to every creation endpoint and to defect-ticket triage.
7. **Staff identity matches the role.** For `ProjectManager`, `Estimator`, and `Controller`, `X-User-ID` must resolve to a `StaffMember` whose `staff_function` matches the acting role, or `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403). This applies to every endpoint whose authorization list includes any of those three roles.
8. **Entity-backed ownership.** A `Client`, `Lender`, or `Subcontractor` principal must own the target as defined in §2.1, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This applies to every endpoint that writes — a creation, a transition, or a decision — acted on by one of those three roles. It deliberately does not apply to any read: single reads and list endpoints alike perform no ownership comparison, so a homeowner, a lender or a trade authorised for a read endpoint may read any row of that family, and the only narrowing a reader ever sees is the client-portal filter §3.8 places on photo-document listing.
9. **Contract terms coherent.** At project creation only, a cost-plus contract must supply a builder fee and a guaranteed maximum price, when supplied, must not sit below the base contract amount, or `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMS_INVALID` (422).
10. **Target state admits the transition.** The target row must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, or the family-specific conflict code — `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ALLOWANCE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_COST_ENTRY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DRAW_NOT_IN_DRAFT`, `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEFECT_INVALID_STATE`, or `ERR_PHOTO_ALREADY_PUBLISHED` — each with HTTP 409. This applies to every transition endpoint.
11. **Parent project state admits the operation.** Proposing a selection, pricing a change order, approving a submitted change order, voiding an approved change order, settling an allowance item, and releasing a schedule task all require the parent project to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, failing with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` (409); publishing a photo document requires only that the project has left `PLANNING`, failing with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACCEPTING_PHOTOS` (409).
12. **Allowance line accepting proposals.** A selection may be proposed only against an allowance item whose status is `OPEN` and whose project's `budget_posture` is anything other than `OVER_GMP`; otherwise `ERR_ALLOWANCE_FROZEN` (409).
13. **Expected parent version matches.** The supplied `expected_allowance_item_version` or `expected_project_version` must equal the parent's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_ALLOWANCE_VERSION` / `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412) as applicable.
14. **Project ready to start.** Starting construction requires at least one allowance item and at least one `PLANNED` schedule task on the project, or `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_READY_TO_START` (409).
15. **Project ready for substantial completion.** Declaring substantial completion requires every schedule task on the project to be `COMPLETE` and no change order on the project to sit in `DRAFT`, `PRICED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. The task check runs first: a project that fails both earns `ERR_PROJECT_TASKS_INCOMPLETE` (409), and only a project whose tasks are all complete can earn `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING` (409).
16. **No blocking punch items.** At the final walkthrough, no punch-phase defect of severity `FUNCTIONAL` or `LIFE_SAFETY` may remain in `OPEN`, `ASSIGNED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED`, or `ERR_BLOCKING_PUNCH_ITEMS` (409).
17. **Cosmetic acknowledgement exact.** At the final walkthrough, `acknowledged_open_cosmetic_count` must equal the count of punch-phase defects of severity `COSMETIC` on the project sitting in `OPEN`, `ASSIGNED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED`, or `ERR_PUNCH_ACK_COUNT_MISMATCH` (422).
18. **Warranty coverage before assignment.** A warranty-phase defect may be assigned to a trade only when its `coverage_result` is `COVERED`, or `ERR_DEFECT_NOT_COVERED` (422).
19. **Defect decision legal for the phase.** A denial at triage is legal only for a warranty-phase ticket whose `coverage_result` is `OUT_OF_COVERAGE` or `EXCLUDED`, and a waiver at client review is legal only for a punch-phase ticket of severity `COSMETIC`; any other use is `ERR_DEFECT_PHASE_MISMATCH` (422).
20. **Segregation of duties.** The change-order submitter must differ from `priced_by_staff_id`, and the draw submitter must differ from the `approved_by_staff_id` of every cost entry the draw would include, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
21. **Predecessor in the same project.** A supplied `predecessor_task_id` must name a schedule task belonging to the same project as the task being created, or `ERR_PREDECESSOR_CROSS_PROJECT` (422).
22. **Allowance reference in the same project.** A supplied `driving_allowance_item_id` on a new schedule task, and a supplied `source_allowance_item_id` on a new cost entry or a new change order, must each name an allowance item belonging to the same project as the row being created, or `ERR_ALLOWANCE_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (422).
23. **Waiver form matches.** The signed `waiver_type` must equal the `required_waiver_type` the draw demands of that trade, or `ERR_WAIVER_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422).
24. **Release gates clear.** Releasing a schedule task requires the driving allowance item to be `LOCKED` or `RECONCILED` (`ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_LOCKED`), the computed `material_ready_at` to be no later than `planned_start` (`ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_READY`), and the predecessor task to be `COMPLETE` (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_INCOMPLETE`), each with HTTP 409.
25. **Actual cost present before reconciliation.** An allowance item may be reconciled only once at least one allowance-actual cost entry in `APPROVED`, `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW`, or `PAID` points at it, or `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ACTUAL_MISSING` (409).
26. **Crew capacity.** A new schedule task must not exceed the trade's crew capacity as defined in §3.4, or `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` (409).
27. **Jobsite exclusivity.** A new exclusive-use schedule task must not collide with another exclusive-use task as defined in §3.4, or `ERR_JOBSITE_EXCLUSIVE_CONFLICT` (409).
28. **Selection deadline.** A selection may be proposed only while the logical clock now is at or before the allowance item's `selection_deadline`, or `ERR_SELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (422).
29. **Lender decision window.** A lender decision must arrive while the logical clock now is at or before the draw's `lender_decision_due_at`, or `ERR_LENDER_DECISION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409).
30. **Revision rounds.** A revision request and a submission are both bounded by the revision limit described in §3.3, or `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
31. **Draw has eligible cost.** A draw submission must pull in at least one eligible cost entry, or `ERR_DRAW_NO_ELIGIBLE_COSTS` (422).
32. **Waiver amount sufficient.** The signed `amount_covered` must be at least the waiver's `required_amount`, or `ERR_WAIVER_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
33. **Lender cumulative cap.** Cumulative draw exposure after this submission must not exceed the lender's cap as defined in §3.6, or `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_LENDER_CAP` (422).
34. **Lender exists at all.** A project whose `lender_id` is null can neither open nor submit a draw request, and both attempts earn `ERR_PROJECT_HAS_NO_LENDER` (422).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Job: Parties, Contract Terms, and Breaking Ground

Before a single foundation is poured, four kinds of reference record must exist, and all four are seeded by a `PlatformAdmin`. A homeowner record carries the `full_name` that appears on the construction contract, the `email` used for portal notifications, and a `portal_enabled` switch that defaults to true; when a homeowner's `portal_enabled` is set to false, the client portal hides published photo documentation from that homeowner even though the documents remain published. A construction lender record carries its institution `name`, a `retainage_pct` giving the fraction of each approved cost line the lender withholds on every draw — it must sit between zero and 0.20 with both ends allowed, and it is accepted to four decimal places — a `max_cumulative_draw_pct` ceiling that must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1 and is likewise accepted to four decimal places, and a `review_window_days` count of whole days, which must be at least 1. A builder-side employee record carries a `full_name` and a `staff_function` naming which capacity that employee may act in: `PROJECT_MANAGER`, `ESTIMATOR`, or `CONTROLLER`. A trade contractor record carries its `company_name`, its `trade` — one of `SITEWORK`, `CONCRETE`, `FRAMING`, `PLUMBING`, `ELECTRICAL`, `HVAC`, `DRYWALL`, `FINISH_CARPENTRY`, `PAINT`, or `LANDSCAPE` — a `lead_time_days` count of whole days it needs between mobilization notice and its first day on site (zero is permitted), a `crew_capacity` of at least 1 describing how many schedule intervals its crews can occupy simultaneously, and a `waiver_required` flag defaulting to true that determines whether draws containing this trade's costs generate lien-waiver paperwork.

Opening a build is likewise a `PlatformAdmin` action. The project payload names the homeowner through `client_id`, optionally names the construction lender through `lender_id` (which defaults to null and stays null for an owner-financed build, permanently foreclosing the draw workflow), records the jobsite `address`, declares the accounting basis in `contract_type` as either `FIXED_PRICE` or `COST_PLUS`, states the signed `base_contract_amount` which must be strictly greater than zero and carry at most two decimals, and supplies the contractual `target_substantial_completion_date`. Two optional terms complete the picture: `builder_fee_pct`, the builder's fee expressed as a fraction of actual cost, which defaults to null, is accepted to four decimal places, and must fall between zero and 0.50 inclusive when present; and `gmp_cap`, a guaranteed maximum price ceiling which defaults to null and, when present, must be a non-negative money value of at most two decimals that is at least the `base_contract_amount`. Coherence between these terms is checked at creation and nowhere else: a `COST_PLUS` project submitted without a `builder_fee_pct`, or any project whose `gmp_cap` sits below its `base_contract_amount`, is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMS_INVALID`. A `FIXED_PRICE` project may omit `builder_fee_pct` entirely — the field is simply ignored for that accounting basis, though a value supplied there is still validated, stored on the project and echoed back on every read. A newly opened project sits in `PLANNING`, with `contract_sum_current` initialised to `base_contract_amount` and `budget_posture` initialised to `WITHIN_BUDGET`; its `approved_change_order_total`, `allowance_variance_total`, `cost_to_date`, `eligible_cost_pool`, `pending_draw_amount`, `funded_to_date`, and `retainage_held` all start at zero, and `construction_started_at`, `substantial_completion_at`, `final_accepted_at`, `warranty_starts_at`, and `warranty_expires_at` all start null.

A `ProjectManager` drives the project forward one step at a time by supplying an `action`. Breaking ground — `START_CONSTRUCTION` — moves a project `PLANNING → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, and it is only permitted once the job actually has a book and a schedule: at least one allowance item must exist on the project and at least one schedule task on the project must sit in `PLANNED`. A project missing either is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_READY_TO_START`. On success the logical clock now is written into `construction_started_at`, `contract_sum_current` and `budget_posture` are recomputed, and the project's `version` is incremented by one.

Declaring substantial completion — `DECLARE_SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLETION` — moves a project `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLETION`, and it demands that the field work and the paperwork have both landed. Every schedule task on the project must be `COMPLETE`, and no change order on the project may remain in `DRAFT`, `PRICED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. These two checks are ordered: the incomplete-task check runs first and yields `ERR_PROJECT_TASKS_INCOMPLETE`, and only a project whose tasks are all complete can go on to earn `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_PENDING` for an in-flight change order. On success the logical clock now is written into `substantial_completion_at`; `warranty_starts_at` is set equal to `substantial_completion_at`; `warranty_expires_at` is set to `warranty_starts_at` advanced by a full year of 365 days; every draw submitted from this moment onward demands final unconditional lien releases rather than conditional progress releases; and the project's `version` is incremented by one. Requesting a lifecycle step from a status that does not declare it — asking to break ground on a project already under construction, for instance — is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`.

Projects can be read individually, exposing every derived ledger field described in §2.6, and listed with optional filters on `client_id`, `lender_id`, and `status`. Both reads are open to all seven roles, because everyone from the lender to the drywall crew has a legitimate reason to know where the job stands.

### 3.2. The Allowance Book: Budget Lines, Selections, Buy-Out, and Reconciliation

An allowance is a promise to spend a stated number of dollars on a category of finish the homeowner has not yet chosen. A `ProjectManager` adds one to a project by naming the `project_id`, the `category` — one of `FLOORING`, `CABINETRY`, `APPLIANCES`, `PLUMBING_FIXTURES`, `LIGHTING`, `COUNTERTOPS`, or `LANDSCAPE` — the `allowance_amount` carried in the contract, which must be strictly greater than zero with at most two decimals, and a `selection_deadline` giving the last logical-clock instant at which the homeowner may still propose against the line. A new line opens in `OPEN` with `selected_selection_id`, `final_variance_amount`, `reconciled_amount`, `material_lead_time_days`, `locked_at`, `frozen_at`, and `reconciled_at` all null, and `provisional_variance_amount` at zero. Allowance items can be read individually and listed with filters on `project_id`, `status`, and `selected_selection_id`.

The homeowner proposes against a line by naming the `allowance_item_id`, the `vendor_name` they picked, the `vendor_quote_amount` for the material (which must be at least zero and carry at most two decimals), optional `freight_amount` and `install_amount` figures that each default to zero and may not be negative, a `lead_time_days` count of whole days from buy-out to material on site which may be zero but not negative, and the mandatory `expected_allowance_item_version` described in §2.8. The server computes `selection_total` as the vendor quote plus freight plus installation, rounded half up to two decimals, and files the proposal in `PROPOSED` with `decided_at` and `superseded_at` null. Two temporal and postural gates guard the proposal: the logical clock now must be at or before the line's `selection_deadline` — an instant exactly equal to the deadline is still in time, while anything later earns `ERR_SELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` — and the line must be `OPEN` on a project whose `budget_posture` is not `OVER_GMP`, failing which the proposal earns `ERR_ALLOWANCE_FROZEN`. Filing a new proposal against a line that already carries an outstanding `PROPOSED` proposal drives that older proposal `PROPOSED → SUPERSEDED` and stamps its `superseded_at` with the logical clock now, incrementing that superseded proposal's own `version` too; the allowance item's `version` is incremented as part of the same write — on every accepted proposal, the very first one on a fresh line included, not only on the ones that supersede something — which is precisely why the stale-version guard exists.

A `ProjectManager` rules on the proposal by supplying a `decision`. Approving it advances the proposal `PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`, and requires that the proposal still be `PROPOSED`, that its parent line still be `OPEN`, and that the project's `budget_posture` not be `OVER_GMP`; a decision aimed at a proposal in any other status is refused with `ERR_SELECTION_NOT_PENDING`, and an approval aimed at a still-`PROPOSED` proposal whose line has left `OPEN` or whose project has gone `OVER_GMP` is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_FROZEN` instead. Neither branch of the decision looks at the parent project's lifecycle status at all, and the rejecting branch is gated on nothing beyond the proposal's own `PROPOSED` status — not on the line's status, not on the posture, and not on the selection deadline. Either branch answers with the selection row itself rather than with the allowance line. Acceptance stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock now and cascades the parent line `OPEN → SELECTED`: `selected_selection_id` is pointed at the accepted proposal, `provisional_variance_amount` is computed as the accepted proposal's `selection_total` minus the line's `allowance_amount`, rounded half up to two decimals, and that figure immediately enters `allowance_variance_total`, which forces `contract_sum_current` and then `budget_posture` to be recomputed. The allowance item's `version` is incremented. Rejecting a proposal advances it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, stamps `decided_at`, and deliberately leaves the parent line in `OPEN` so the homeowner can come back with a different vendor.

Estimated variance is not settled variance, and the `Controller` closes the gap in two steps by supplying an `action`. Locking the buy-out price advances the line `SELECTED → LOCKED` and requires the parent project to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`. The lock stamps `locked_at` with the logical clock now and copies the accepted proposal's `lead_time_days` into the line's `material_lead_time_days`. It also reaches sideways into the schedule: for every schedule task whose `driving_allowance_item_id` names this line — whatever status that task currently holds, `PLANNED`, `RELEASED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE` alike — the server computes `material_ready_at` as the line's `locked_at` advanced by the accepted proposal's `lead_time_days` plus a further three days of fabrication buffer, and increments that task's `version`. Should the line somehow carry no accepted proposal, a lead time of zero whole days stands in. The trade's own `lead_time_days` plays no part in this computation whatsoever — only the accepted proposal's does. Finally, if the line locked with a `provisional_variance_amount` strictly greater than zero, the server automatically opens a change order in `DRAFT` with `origin` of `ALLOWANCE_OVERAGE`, `source_allowance_item_id` pointing back at this line, `direct_cost` equal to the `provisional_variance_amount`, `schedule_impact_days` of zero, and a server-generated `scope_description` naming the line's `category` and its `id` — the overrun becomes a priced, client-approvable document rather than a silent budget leak. The generated document is in every other respect a fresh draft, with `revision_count` at zero and every pricing, submission and decision field null, and because a `DRAFT` change order contributes nothing to `approved_change_order_total` its creation moves no project figure and no project `version`. The line's `version` is incremented.

Reconciling advances the line `LOCKED → RECONCILED`, and it is legal only once at least one cost entry of type `ALLOWANCE_ACTUAL` in status `APPROVED`, `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW`, or `PAID` points at the line; without one, the attempt earns `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ACTUAL_MISSING`. Reconciliation sets `reconciled_amount` to the sum of those qualifying allowance-actual cost entry `amount` values rounded half up to two decimals — entries still `PENDING` and entries `REJECTED` are excluded — and sets `final_variance_amount` to that reconciled figure minus the line's `allowance_amount`, again rounded half up to two decimals. `reconciled_at` is stamped with the logical clock now. The project ledger is then recomputed with the line's `final_variance_amount` standing in place of its `provisional_variance_amount` inside `allowance_variance_total`, after which `contract_sum_current` is recomputed and `budget_posture` is re-evaluated, in that order. The line's `version` is incremented. A settle action requested from a status that does not declare it — reconciling a line still in `SELECTED`, for example — is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_INVALID_STATE`.

The freeze cycle described in §2.6 is what makes `FROZEN` more than bookkeeping. While a line is `FROZEN` with `frozen_at` stamped, no new proposal may be filed against it; when the posture recovers and the line thaws back to `OPEN` with `frozen_at` cleared, proposals resume. Both directions of the cycle increment the line's `version`, so a client holding an allowance-line version from before a freeze will find its next proposal rejected on the stale-version check rather than silently landing.

### 3.3. Change Orders: Pricing, Two Signatures, and the Client Negotiation Cycle

A `ProjectManager` opens a change order in `DRAFT` by naming the `project_id`, the `origin` — `CLIENT_REQUEST`, `FIELD_CONDITION`, or `ALLOWANCE_OVERAGE` — a narrative `scope_description`, the `direct_cost` of the changed scope before any markup (at least zero, at most two decimals), and optionally the `schedule_impact_days` count of whole calendar days the change pushes the schedule, which defaults to zero and may not be negative. When the origin is `ALLOWANCE_OVERAGE` the payload also carries `source_allowance_item_id` identifying the overrun line; that reference must belong to the same project as the change order. The field is nevertheless optional on every origin — the server never demands it because the origin reads `ALLOWANCE_OVERAGE`, and never refuses it because the origin reads something else — but wherever it is supplied it must resolve to an existing line and must sit on the same project. A fresh change order carries `revision_count` of zero and null `markup_rate_applied`, `contingency_rate_applied`, `priced_amount`, `priced_by_staff_id`, `submitted_by_staff_id`, `revision_note`, `void_reason`, `priced_at`, `submitted_at`, `client_response_due_at`, and `decided_at`. Change orders can be read individually — the read exposes which markup and contingency tiers were applied — and listed with filters on `project_id`, `source_allowance_item_id`, `priced_by_staff_id`, `submitted_by_staff_id`, and `status`.

Pricing is the estimator's craft, and both an `Estimator` and a `ProjectManager` may perform it. Pricing advances a change order `DRAFT → PRICED`, and the same operation also closes the negotiation loop by advancing `REVISION_REQUESTED → PRICED`; both edges require the parent project to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`. The pricing request may optionally restate `direct_cost` and `schedule_impact_days`, and when it does, those supplied values overwrite what the change order carried. The computation proceeds in five pinned steps, and its only inputs are the change order's own `direct_cost` and `schedule_impact_days` — no sibling change order and no project ledger field takes part.

First, a markup tier is selected from the raw `direct_cost` alone: a direct cost below 5,000.00 dollars earns a markup rate of 0.20; a direct cost from 5,000.00 dollars up to but not including 25,000.00 dollars earns 0.15; and a direct cost of 25,000.00 dollars or more earns 0.10. The markup itself is the `direct_cost` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals. Second, general conditions are priced for the schedule impact: the `schedule_impact_days` count is multiplied by a general-conditions day rate of 485.00 dollars per calendar day, rounded half up to two decimals. Third, a pre-contingency subtotal is formed as the `direct_cost` plus the markup plus the general-conditions figure. Fourth — and this is the step implementations most often get wrong — a contingency tier is selected **from that pre-contingency subtotal and never from the final price**: a subtotal of 10,000.00 dollars or less earns a contingency rate of 0.00; a subtotal above 10,000.00 dollars and up to and including 50,000.00 dollars earns 0.03; and a subtotal strictly above 50,000.00 dollars earns 0.05. The contingency amount is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals. There is no fixed point to solve here: the tier must not be re-derived from the priced result, so a change order whose subtotal lands at 48,850.00 dollars takes the 0.03 tier even though its final price crosses 50,000.00 dollars. Fifth, `priced_amount` is the subtotal plus the contingency, rounded half up to two decimals, and the two rates that were used are recorded in `markup_rate_applied` and `contingency_rate_applied`.

Every pricing pass stamps `priced_by_staff_id` with the acting employee's identifier and `priced_at` with the logical clock now. When the pass is a repricing off the back of a client revision request, it additionally increments `revision_count` by one before the recomputation is filed, using whatever `direct_cost` and `schedule_impact_days` now stand on the record.

Submission is a `ProjectManager` action that advances a change order `PRICED → SUBMITTED`, and it carries no payload fields of its own. It is barred to whoever priced the document, as §2.7 describes, and it is also bounded by the revision budget: a change order may be submitted while its `revision_count` is at most 3, and once `revision_count` has passed that ceiling the submission is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A successful submission stamps `submitted_by_staff_id`, records `submitted_at` as the logical clock now, and computes `client_response_due_at` as `submitted_at` advanced by a two-week client response window of 14 days.

The homeowner then rules on the document with a `decision` that can go three ways, and only from `SUBMITTED`; a decision aimed at a change order in any other status earns `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. **Approval** advances `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, requires the project to be `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, stamps `decided_at`, and sets off a four-link cascade: the change order's `priced_amount` joins `approved_change_order_total`, which forces `contract_sum_current` to be recomputed; because `contract_sum_current` moved, `budget_posture` is re-evaluated and may become `OVER_GMP`; because the posture became `OVER_GMP`, every allowance item on the project currently in `OPEN` cascades `OPEN → FROZEN`; and the project's `version` is incremented by one. If the approved change order carries a `schedule_impact_days` greater than zero, every schedule task on the project still sitting in `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` has both its `planned_start` and its `planned_finish` pushed later by exactly that many days, processed in order of `planned_start` ascending and, where two tasks tie, `created_at` ascending with insertion order settling any remaining tie; each shifted task has its `version` incremented, and a shift never recomputes `float_days` or `material_ready_at`. **Requesting a revision** advances `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, requires that `revision_count` still be strictly below 3, stores the homeowner's `revision_note` — an optional field recorded as null when the payload omits it — leaves `decided_at` untouched, and deliberately leaves `priced_amount` at its last value until the estimator reprices; a revision requested once the count has reached the ceiling earns `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. **Rejection** advances `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps `decided_at`, and leaves `approved_change_order_total` and `contract_sum_current` exactly where they were; `REJECTED` is terminal. Only the approving branch asks anything of the project's lifecycle status: a revision request and a rejection are both accepted whatever status the project holds.

An approved change order can still be descoped. The homeowner voids it, advancing `APPROVED → VOIDED` while the project is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, supplying a `void_reason` that is stored alongside a fresh `decided_at`. The void is the exact compensating mirror of the approval: the change order's `priced_amount` is removed from `approved_change_order_total`, which forces `contract_sum_current` to be recomputed; `budget_posture` is re-evaluated and may leave `OVER_GMP`; if it did leave `OVER_GMP`, every allowance item on the project currently in `FROZEN` cascades `FROZEN → OPEN`; and the project's `version` is incremented by one. If the voided change order carried a `schedule_impact_days` greater than zero, every schedule task on the project still in `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` has its `planned_start` and `planned_finish` pulled earlier by exactly that many days, processed in the same `planned_start` ascending, `created_at` ascending order and incrementing each shifted task's `version`. `VOIDED`, like `REJECTED`, is terminal — the pair exists so that the contract sum can always be walked back to what it would have been.

### 3.4. Sequencing the Trades: Crew Capacity, Material Readiness, and Delay Propagation

A schedule task is one trade's occupancy of one interval of the jobsite. A `ProjectManager` adds one by naming the `project_id`, the `subcontractor_id` of the crew that will occupy the interval, a `task_name` label, and the interval bounds `planned_start` (inclusive) and `planned_finish`, which must be strictly later than `planned_start`. Three optional references shape the task's dependencies: `predecessor_task_id`, which is null for a schedule root and otherwise names the task that must finish first and must belong to the same project (a cross-project reference is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_CROSS_PROJECT`); `driving_allowance_item_id`, which is null when the activity consumes no allowance material and otherwise names the allowance line whose buy-out date governs material readiness, and which must likewise belong to the same project; and `lag_days`, a non-negative whole-day count defaulting to zero that describes required waiting time after the predecessor's actual finish. A fourth optional input, `exclusive_use`, defaults to false and declares that the activity takes over the whole jobsite. A new task lands in `PLANNED` with `material_ready_at`, `actual_start`, `actual_finish`, `float_days`, and `released_at` null and `delay_days` at zero.

Two overlap rules police the calendar at creation time, and they use deliberately different interval conventions. The **crew-capacity rule** treats every interval as half-open — `planned_start` included, `planned_finish` excluded — so that a crew finishing one task exactly when it starts the next is not double-booked; touching intervals do not intersect. The server counts the existing schedule tasks for the same `subcontractor_id` — across every project that trade is booked on, not merely the project being scheduled — whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED`, or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose half-open interval intersects the new task's half-open interval, and that count must be strictly less than the trade's `crew_capacity`; otherwise the creation is refused with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`. The **jobsite exclusive-use rule** applies only when the new task's `exclusive_use` is true, and it is deliberately stricter: the occupied interval runs from `planned_start` through `planned_finish` extended by a one-day jobsite cure buffer, and both ends of that buffered interval are closed, so that touching endpoints *do* intersect. If that closed buffered interval intersects the equally buffered interval of any existing `exclusive_use` task on the same project whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED`, or `IN_PROGRESS`, the creation is refused with `ERR_JOBSITE_EXCLUSIVE_CONFLICT`. Tasks can be read individually — the read surfaces material readiness and float — and listed with filters on `project_id`, `subcontractor_id`, `predecessor_task_id`, `driving_allowance_item_id`, and `status`.

Releasing a task to the field is the `ProjectManager`'s statement that the crew can be called, and it advances the task `PLANNED → RELEASED` only when the project is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and three cross-entity gates are simultaneously clear. If the task names a `driving_allowance_item_id`, that allowance line must be `LOCKED` or `RECONCILED`, and a line still `OPEN`, `FROZEN`, or `SELECTED` earns `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_LOCKED`. If the task carries a computed `material_ready_at`, that instant must be no later than the task's `planned_start`; material landing after the crew is due on site earns `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_READY`. If the task names a `predecessor_task_id`, that predecessor must be `COMPLETE`, and anything else earns `ERR_PREDECESSOR_INCOMPLETE`. A task with no driving allowance line, no material readiness date, or no predecessor simply skips the corresponding gate. The release carries no payload fields of its own. A successful release stamps `released_at` with the logical clock now and, when a predecessor exists, computes `float_days` as the number of whole days between this task's `planned_start` and the predecessor's `actual_finish` advanced by this task's `lag_days`; while the predecessor is not yet `COMPLETE`, `float_days` remains null.

Field progress belongs to the trade itself, and a `Subcontractor` supplies an `action` to move its own task. Starting advances `RELEASED → IN_PROGRESS`; a task carrying a `material_ready_at` may not be started before that instant — a start at exactly `material_ready_at` is in time — and an early start earns `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_READY`. Neither progress edge asks anything of the parent project's lifecycle status. A successful start stamps `actual_start` with the logical clock now. Completing advances `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE`, stamps `actual_finish` with the logical clock now, and computes `delay_days` as the number of whole days between `planned_finish` and `actual_finish`, floored at zero — a crew that finishes early or on time records a delay of zero, never a negative number. A progress action requested from a status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`.

When a completed task records a `delay_days` greater than zero, the delay propagates exactly one hop. Every direct successor — every task whose `predecessor_task_id` names the completed task — that is still in `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` has both its `planned_start` and its `planned_finish` pushed later by that many days, processed in order of `planned_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and insertion order settling any remaining tie. Successors already `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE` are never shifted, and the propagation does not continue to the successors' own successors. Each shifted successor then has its `float_days` recomputed from its new `planned_start` against the completed task's `actual_finish` advanced by that successor's `lag_days`, and has its `version` incremented.

### 3.5. Capturing Cost and Reading the Contract Position

Every dollar the job spends enters as a cost entry, posted by a `ProjectManager` and landing in `PENDING`. The payload names the `project_id`, the `entry_type` — `SUBCONTRACT_INVOICE`, `MATERIAL`, `LABOR`, `ALLOWANCE_ACTUAL`, or `CHANGE_ORDER_WORK` — the job `cost_code` the money is charged to, the `amount`, which must be strictly greater than zero with at most two decimals, and the `incurred_at` instant, which decides both draw-period eligibility and the per-line retainage ordering. Two optional references complete it: `subcontractor_id`, which is null for an owner-direct material purchase carrying no lien exposure, and `source_allowance_item_id`, which is required whenever `entry_type` is `ALLOWANCE_ACTUAL` — its absence there is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and which is otherwise merely optional rather than forbidden, so the server accepts and stores it on any entry type; wherever it is supplied it must resolve and must point at an allowance line on the same project. A new entry carries null `draw_request_id`, `approved_by_staff_id`, `approved_at`, and `paid_at`. Cost entries can be read individually and listed with filters on `project_id`, `subcontractor_id`, `source_allowance_item_id`, `draw_request_id`, `approved_by_staff_id`, and `status`.

A `Controller` rules on a posted cost with a `decision`, and the review is not gated on the project's lifecycle status at all — it is accepted whether the project is still `PLANNING` or has already reached `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLETION` or `FINAL_ACCEPTED`. Approving advances the entry `PENDING → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_staff_id` with the acting controller's identifier and `approved_at` with the logical clock now, and recomputes the project's `cost_to_date` and `eligible_cost_pool` followed by `budget_posture`. Rejecting advances the entry `PENDING → REJECTED`, leaves `approved_by_staff_id` and `approved_at` null because no approval ever happened, and leaves `cost_to_date` and `eligible_cost_pool` untouched, because a rejected invoice never counted toward either. A review aimed at an entry whose status is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_COST_ENTRY_INVALID_STATE`.

Beyond approval, a cost entry's status is moved only by the draw machinery, and it moves along a conserving path. When a draw is submitted, each eligible entry advances `APPROVED → INCLUDED_IN_DRAW` and has its `draw_request_id` pointed at that draw; `eligible_cost_pool` does not move, because both statuses count toward it. When a lender returns the draw or a controller withdraws it, each of those entries reverts `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW → APPROVED` with `draw_request_id` cleared back to null, and again neither `eligible_cost_pool` nor `cost_to_date` moves. Only when the draw actually funds does an entry advance `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW → PAID`, stamping `paid_at` with the logical clock now; that is the single moment the amount leaves `eligible_cost_pool`, while `cost_to_date` is deliberately left unchanged because the money was spent regardless of who has reimbursed it.

The contract position is a computed read, available to `PlatformAdmin`, `ProjectManager`, `Controller`, `Client`, and `Lender`, and it is where the fixed-price and cost-plus accounting bases visibly diverge on identical data. The read reports the project's `contract_type`, a `variant_applied` label naming which basis was used, and the four ledger inputs `contract_sum_current`, `approved_change_order_total`, `allowance_variance_total`, and `cost_to_date`, all computed as §2.6 defines. It also reports `gmp_headroom`, which is the `gmp_cap` minus `contract_sum_current` rounded half up to two decimals when a cap exists, and null when the project carries no cap.

The two remaining figures depend entirely on the accounting basis, and both variants are specified here with equal force. Under a `FIXED_PRICE` contract, `variant_applied` reads `FIXED_PRICE`, `owner_billable_amount` is simply `contract_sum_current` — the owner owes the contract sum regardless of what the build actually cost — and `builder_margin_amount` is `contract_sum_current` minus `cost_to_date`, rounded half up to two decimals. A `gmp_cap` on a fixed-price job does not clamp the billable figure at all; it only feeds `budget_posture`. Under a `COST_PLUS` contract, `variant_applied` reads `COST_PLUS`, and the billable figure is built from actual cost instead: the server first computes `cost_to_date` multiplied by one plus the project's `builder_fee_pct`, rounded half up to two decimals; `owner_billable_amount` is then that figure, except that when the project carries a `gmp_cap` the billable figure is the lesser of the two — the guaranteed maximum price is a genuine ceiling on a cost-plus job. `builder_margin_amount` is then `owner_billable_amount` minus `cost_to_date`, rounded half up to two decimals. Note that `contract_sum_current` is still reported for a cost-plus job, but it is not the billable figure; consider a job with a base contract of 900,000.00 dollars, no approved change orders, no allowance variance, and a `cost_to_date` of 45,000.30 dollars — the owner owes 900,000.00 dollars under a fixed-price contract, and a far smaller sum under a cost-plus contract with an eighteen-percent builder fee.

### 3.6. Draw Requests: Assembling the Bill, the Lender Window, and the Cumulative Cap

A draw request bills the construction lender for a billing period. Only a `Controller` may open one, and only on a project that actually has a lender — a project whose `lender_id` is null refuses both the opening and the submission of a draw with `ERR_PROJECT_HAS_NO_LENDER`. Opening a draw names the `project_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and the inclusive `period_end` (which must be strictly later than `period_start`), and the mandatory `expected_project_version` described in §2.8; the project's own lifecycle status is not a gate on opening, and the stale-version check is evaluated before the missing-lender check. The new draw lands in `DRAFT` on its funding axis and `PENDING` on its waiver axis, with `requested_amount`, `retainage_amount`, `net_funding_amount`, `included_cost_entry_count`, `required_waiver_count`, and `verified_waiver_count` all at zero and `submitted_by_staff_id`, `lender_decision_due_at`, `condition_note`, `void_reason`, `submitted_at`, `conditionally_approved_at`, `returned_at`, `funded_at`, and `voided_at` all null. Opening a draw supersedes any draw already sitting in `DRAFT` on the same project: that older draft advances `DRAFT → VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped and `void_reason` set to the literal `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_DRAFT`, and because a draft draw holds no cost entries, no cost disposition changes. The project's `version` is incremented by one. Draws can be read individually — the read exposes both state axes and the full funding arithmetic — and listed with filters on `project_id`, `submitted_by_staff_id`, and `status`.

Submitting the draw is where the arithmetic happens, it carries no payload fields of its own, and it advances the funding axis `DRAFT → LENDER_REVIEW`. The draw's own status is the only state gate the submission applies — the project's lifecycle status is read to choose the required waiver form but is never itself a bar, so a draw may be submitted from a project in any status — and a submission from a draw whose status is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_DRAW_NOT_IN_DRAFT`. The eligible set is assembled first: every cost entry on the project whose status is `APPROVED` and whose `incurred_at` falls inside the billing period with **both ends inclusive**, ordered by `incurred_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Entries that are `PENDING`, `REJECTED`, already `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW`, or already `PAID` are excluded. If the eligible set is empty, the submission is refused with `ERR_DRAW_NO_ELIGIBLE_COSTS`.

Three money figures come out of that set, and the order in which rounding is applied is binding. `requested_amount` is the sum of the eligible entries' `amount` values, rounded half up to two decimals. `retainage_amount` is **not** computed by applying the lender's rate to that sum; instead, each eligible entry's `amount` is multiplied by the lender's `retainage_pct` and rounded half up to two decimals **on that line**, and the already-rounded per-line figures are then added together. Summing the amounts first and applying the rate once produces a different number of cents and is explicitly not the rule. `net_funding_amount` is `requested_amount` minus `retainage_amount`, rounded half up to two decimals. `included_cost_entry_count` records how many entries the draw pulled in.

Two gates then decide whether the submission survives. The segregation-of-duties gate from §2.7 rejects a submission whose acting controller approved any of the eligible entries. The cumulative-exposure gate compares the project's total draw exposure after this submission against the lender's ceiling: the ceiling is the lender's `max_cumulative_draw_pct` multiplied by the project's `contract_sum_current`, rounded half up to two decimals, and the exposure is the project's `funded_to_date` plus its `pending_draw_amount` plus this draw's freshly computed `requested_amount`, rounded half up to two decimals. The submission is refused with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_LENDER_CAP` only when the exposure is **strictly greater than** the ceiling; an exposure exactly equal to the ceiling is allowed through.

A surviving submission does five things. Every eligible cost entry advances `APPROVED → INCLUDED_IN_DRAW`. For every distinct `subcontractor_id` among the included entries whose trade record has `waiver_required` set to true — taken in the order each such trade first appears in that `incurred_at`-ordered eligible set, and skipping altogether the entries that carry no `subcontractor_id` — the server creates one lien waiver in `REQUESTED`, with its `required_waiver_type` set to `CONDITIONAL_PROGRESS` when the project is `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` and to `UNCONDITIONAL_FINAL` when the project has reached `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLETION`, and its `required_amount` set to that trade's included total; `required_waiver_count` records how many were created. The draw's `waiver_compliance` is set to `COMPLETE` immediately when `required_waiver_count` is zero — a draw made entirely of owner-direct purchases needs no releases — and otherwise to `PENDING`. `submitted_by_staff_id` and `submitted_at` are stamped, and `lender_decision_due_at` is computed as `submitted_at` advanced by the lender's `review_window_days` whole days. Finally the project's `pending_draw_amount` is recomputed.

The lender then rules with a `decision`, and may attach a `condition_note`; the note is optional on both outcomes and is stored as null when the payload omits it. Both outcomes require the acting lender to own the project and require the logical clock now to be at or before `lender_decision_due_at`; a decision arriving strictly after that instant is refused with `ERR_LENDER_DECISION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, while a decision arriving exactly at it is still in time. **Conditionally approving** advances `LENDER_REVIEW → CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, stamps `conditionally_approved_at` and stores the `condition_note`; and if the draw's `waiver_compliance` already reads `COMPLETE` at that moment, the draw immediately auto-advances `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED → FUNDED` in the same request. **Returning** advances `LENDER_REVIEW → RETURNED`, stamps `returned_at` and stores the `condition_note`, sends every cost entry the draw was holding back from `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW` to `APPROVED`, voids every lien waiver on the draw sitting in `REQUESTED`, `RECEIVED`, or `REJECTED`, and recomputes the project's `pending_draw_amount`.

Funding is never a direct client action; it is always the automatic consequence of the two axes agreeing. When a draw reaches `FUNDED`, `funded_at` is stamped with the logical clock now, the project's `funded_to_date` and `retainage_held` are recomputed, every cost entry the draw holds advances `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW → PAID`, and the project's `pending_draw_amount` and `eligible_cost_pool` are recomputed. `FUNDED` is terminal on the funding axis.

A `Controller` may withdraw a draw at three points, always supplying a `void_reason` and always stamping `voided_at`. Withdrawing a `DRAFT` draw advances `DRAFT → VOIDED` and changes no cost entry, because a draft holds none. Withdrawing a draw in `LENDER_REVIEW` advances `LENDER_REVIEW → VOIDED` and performs the same compensation a lender return does: every `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW` cost entry goes back to `APPROVED`, every lien waiver in `REQUESTED`, `RECEIVED`, or `REJECTED` is voided, and `pending_draw_amount` is restored to its pre-submission value. Withdrawing a draw the lender has already returned advances `RETURNED → VOIDED` and needs no further compensation, because the return already performed it. `VOIDED` is terminal. A lender decision or a withdrawal requested from a status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.7. Lien Waivers: The Second Axis, and What Actually Releases the Money

A draw request carries two independent state dimensions. The funding axis — the draw's `status` — tracks what the lender has decided. The waiver axis — the draw's `waiver_compliance` — tracks whether the trades have signed their releases, and it evolves entirely without regard to the funding axis. A draw can be `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` with `waiver_compliance` still `PENDING`, and it can sit in `LENDER_REVIEW` with `waiver_compliance` already `COMPLETE`. Money moves only when both axes agree.

Waiver rows are never created by a client; they are generated by the draw submission described in §3.6, each carrying a `draw_request_id` naming the draw whose funding it conditions and a `subcontractor_id` naming the trade that must sign, along with the computed `required_waiver_type` and `required_amount`. A waiver form may be any of `CONDITIONAL_PROGRESS`, `UNCONDITIONAL_PROGRESS`, `CONDITIONAL_FINAL`, or `UNCONDITIONAL_FINAL`. A new row lands in `REQUESTED` with `resubmit_count` at zero and `waiver_type`, `through_date`, `amount_covered`, `signed_document_ref`, `received_at`, `verified_at`, `rejected_at`, and `voided_at` all null. Waivers can be read individually — the read exposes the required form and required amount — and listed with filters on `draw_request_id`, `subcontractor_id`, and `status`.

The trade returns its signed release, supplying the `waiver_type` it actually signed, the `through_date` through which the release runs, the `amount_covered` in dollars, and optionally an opaque `signed_document_ref` pointing at the scan. This advances the waiver `REQUESTED → RECEIVED` and stamps `received_at` with the logical clock now. The same operation is how a trade answers a rejection: it also advances `REJECTED → RECEIVED`, overwriting `waiver_type`, `through_date`, `amount_covered`, and `signed_document_ref` with the new submission — an omitted `signed_document_ref` overwrites the stored one to null — re-stamping `received_at`, and incrementing `resubmit_count` by one. Both edges require the acting trade to own the waiver and require the parent draw to be in `LENDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` — a release cannot be filed against a draw that is still a draft, has been returned, has funded, or has been voided, and such a filing is refused with the draw-family code `ERR_DRAW_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than with a waiver-family one, after the waiver's own status has already been found acceptable. A receive or review action requested from a waiver status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`.

A `Controller` then rules on the signed release with a `decision`. Verifying advances `RECEIVED → VERIFIED` and demands two things of the paperwork: the signed `waiver_type` must equal the `required_waiver_type` the draw demanded of that trade, failing which the verification is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_TYPE_MISMATCH`; and the signed `amount_covered` must be at least the waiver's `required_amount`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_AMOUNT_INSUFFICIENT`. A successful verification stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock now and sets off a four-link cascade that is the single most consequential chain in the system: because this waiver became `VERIFIED`, the parent draw recomputes `verified_waiver_count` and advances its `waiver_compliance`; because `waiver_compliance` reached `COMPLETE` while the draw's `status` was `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, the draw auto-advances to `FUNDED` and the project's `funded_to_date`, `retainage_held`, and `pending_draw_amount` are recomputed; and because the draw reached `FUNDED`, every cost entry it holds advances `INCLUDED_IN_DRAW → PAID` and leaves the project's `eligible_cost_pool`. Rejecting instead advances `RECEIVED → REJECTED`, stamps `rejected_at` with the logical clock now, and deliberately leaves the parent draw's `waiver_compliance` exactly where it was. Verification asks nothing of the parent draw's own status, and either decision answers with the waiver row itself rather than with the draw the cascade may just have funded.

The waiver axis itself moves in three declared ways, and in every case `verified_waiver_count` is refreshed first. The edge `PENDING → PARTIAL` fires when the count of verified waivers is strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `required_waiver_count`; this edge sets `verified_waiver_count` and pointedly does not touch the draw's `status`. The edge `PARTIAL → COMPLETE` fires when `verified_waiver_count` equals `required_waiver_count`, and this edge, when the draw's `status` is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, triggers the funding auto-advance. The edge `PENDING → COMPLETE` fires directly when `verified_waiver_count` equals `required_waiver_count` — either because a single verification satisfied the only requirement, or because the draw was submitted with no required waivers at all — and this edge triggers the same funding auto-advance under the same condition. `COMPLETE` is terminal on the waiver axis.

Waivers are killed en masse when the draw beneath them fails: whenever the parent draw reaches `RETURNED` or `VOIDED`, every one of its waivers sitting in `REQUESTED`, `RECEIVED`, or `REJECTED` advances to `VOIDED` — `REQUESTED → VOIDED`, `RECEIVED → VOIDED`, `REJECTED → VOIDED` alike — with `voided_at` stamped at the logical clock now. Both `VERIFIED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

### 3.8. Jobsite Photo Documentation and the Client Portal

A `ProjectManager` uploads a photo record against a project, supplying the `captured_at` instant the photo was taken and a `caption` field note, both of them mandatory; the upload itself is accepted whatever status the project holds, `PLANNING` included, because only publication is gated. The record lands with `visibility` of `INTERNAL` and a null `published_at`, which means the homeowner cannot see it. Publication is a separate, deliberate act, also reserved to a `ProjectManager`: it sets `visibility` to `CLIENT_PORTAL` and stamps `published_at` with the logical clock now. Publishing requires that the project has left `PLANNING` — there is nothing to show a homeowner on a job that has not broken ground — and an attempt on a project still in `PLANNING` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_ACCEPTING_PHOTOS`. Publishing a document whose `visibility` already reads `CLIENT_PORTAL` is refused with `ERR_PHOTO_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`.

Photo documents for a project are listed with an optional `visibility` filter. Two portal rules narrow what a homeowner sees. First, a reader acting as `Client` only ever receives rows whose `visibility` is `CLIENT_PORTAL`, regardless of what filter was requested. Second, a homeowner whose `portal_enabled` flag is false sees no published photo documentation at all — the switch is the homeowner's own opt-out and it overrides publication.

### 3.9. Punch List, Final Walkthrough, Warranty Coverage, and Callback Service

A defect ticket serves two eras of the same house. A ticket whose `phase` is `PUNCH` is a pre-acceptance walkthrough defect; a ticket whose `phase` is `WARRANTY` is a post-acceptance callback. Either a `Client` or a `ProjectManager` may open one, naming the `project_id`, the `phase`, the `room` where the defect is, a narrative `description`, a `severity` of `COSMETIC`, `FUNCTIONAL`, or `LIFE_SAFETY`, a `category` classifying the cause as `WORKMANSHIP`, `MATERIAL_DEFECT`, `SYSTEMS`, `HOMEOWNER_DAMAGE`, or `NORMAL_WEAR`, and optionally a `source_schedule_task_id` naming the activity that produced the defect, which must resolve to an existing schedule task but is deliberately not required to belong to the ticket's own project. Filing is accepted whatever status the project holds. The ticket lands in `OPEN` with `reported_at` stamped at the logical clock now, `rework_count` at zero, and `responsible_subcontractor_id`, `response_due_at`, `completion_note`, `assigned_at`, and `resolved_at` all null. Tickets can be read individually and listed with filters on `project_id`, `responsible_subcontractor_id`, `source_schedule_task_id`, `phase`, and `status`.

Warranty coverage is decided once, at creation, and written into three fields. For a `PUNCH` ticket the answer is trivial: `coverage_anchor` is `NONE`, `coverage_end_at` is null, and `coverage_result` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`; no further evaluation happens. For a `WARRANTY` ticket the evaluation runs in a pinned order, and the **exclusion test comes first**: if the ticket's `category` is `HOMEOWNER_DAMAGE` or `NORMAL_WEAR`, the ticket is excluded outright — `coverage_anchor` is `NONE`, `coverage_end_at` is null, and `coverage_result` is `EXCLUDED` — and no anchor is selected and no date arithmetic is performed, no matter how recently the house was accepted.

Only a non-excluded warranty ticket reaches anchor selection, and the two anchors give materially different answers on the same claim date. When the ticket carries no `source_schedule_task_id`, the **whole-house anchor** applies: `coverage_anchor` is `WHOLE_HOUSE` and `coverage_end_at` is the project's `warranty_starts_at` advanced by a full year of 365 days, regardless of which trade caused the defect. When the ticket does name a `source_schedule_task_id`, the **trade anchor** applies: `coverage_anchor` is `TRADE`, and the window runs from that source task's `actual_finish` for a term that depends on the task's trade — the mechanical systems trades `PLUMBING`, `ELECTRICAL`, and `HVAC` carry an extended two-year term of 730 days, while every other trade carries the same 365-day term as the whole house. `coverage_end_at` is that source task's `actual_finish` advanced by the applicable term. Because the trade anchor starts at the task's own finish rather than at substantial completion, a claim the whole-house anchor would call out of coverage can legitimately be covered under the trade anchor. Finally, `coverage_result` is `COVERED` when the ticket's `reported_at` is at or before `coverage_end_at` — the upper boundary is inclusive, so a claim reported at exactly the coverage end instant is covered — and `OUT_OF_COVERAGE` when it falls after. Where the selected anchor has no instant to measure from — a whole-house claim on a project whose `warranty_starts_at` is still null, or a trade claim whose source task has not yet recorded an `actual_finish` — the anchor label is still written, but `coverage_end_at` stays null and `coverage_result` is `OUT_OF_COVERAGE`.

Triage is a `ProjectManager` action driven by a `decision`, and it branches two ways out of `OPEN` with deliberately different consequences. **Assigning** advances `OPEN → ASSIGNED` and requires the request to supply a `responsible_subcontractor_id`, whose absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — except on the rework edge below, where the field may be omitted and the trade already recorded on the ticket then stands; wherever the field is supplied it must resolve to an existing trade and it replaces whatever was recorded before; it is legal for any `PUNCH` ticket, and for a `WARRANTY` ticket only when its `coverage_result` reads `COVERED` — a warranty ticket that is `OUT_OF_COVERAGE` or `EXCLUDED` cannot be handed to a trade, and the attempt earns `ERR_DEFECT_NOT_COVERED`. Assignment stamps `assigned_at` with the logical clock now and sets `response_due_at` to `assigned_at` advanced by a severity-driven number of whole days: a `LIFE_SAFETY` defect must be answered within a single day, a `FUNCTIONAL` defect within one week of 7 days, and a `COSMETIC` defect within 30 days. **Denying** advances `OPEN → DENIED` and is legal only for a `WARRANTY` ticket whose `coverage_result` is `OUT_OF_COVERAGE` or `EXCLUDED`; it stamps `resolved_at` with the logical clock now and, unlike assignment, leaves both `responsible_subcontractor_id` and `response_due_at` null, because no trade was engaged and no response clock ever started. A denial aimed at a punch ticket is refused with `ERR_DEFECT_PHASE_MISMATCH`. Triage also serves the rework cycle: it advances `REJECTED → ASSIGNED`, incrementing `rework_count` by one, re-stamping `assigned_at` with the logical clock now, and resetting `response_due_at` to that same instant advanced by the same severity-driven day count — the clock restarts from the moment of reassignment, not from the original assignment.

The assigned trade hands its remediation back by advancing the ticket `ASSIGNED → READY_FOR_REVIEW`, optionally attaching a `completion_note` describing what it did; the field is written on every hand-back, so omitting it clears whatever note an earlier rework cycle left behind. Only the trade named in `responsible_subcontractor_id` may do so. The homeowner then rules with a `decision` of its own. Verifying advances `READY_FOR_REVIEW → VERIFIED` and stamps `resolved_at`. Rejecting advances `READY_FOR_REVIEW → REJECTED`, leaves `resolved_at` null, and keeps the ticket blocking for `FUNCTIONAL` and `LIFE_SAFETY` severities — which is exactly why the rework cycle `ASSIGNED → READY_FOR_REVIEW → REJECTED → ASSIGNED` exists. Waiving is a third branch out of `OPEN` entirely, advancing `OPEN → WAIVED` with `resolved_at` stamped, and it is reachable only for a `PUNCH` ticket of severity `COSMETIC`; a waiver aimed at a warranty ticket or at a non-cosmetic punch item is refused with `ERR_DEFECT_PHASE_MISMATCH`. `VERIFIED`, `DENIED`, and `WAIVED` are all terminal. Any triage, hand-back, or homeowner review requested from a status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_DEFECT_INVALID_STATE`.

The final walkthrough is the homeowner's own act and the last transition the project row ever makes, advancing it `SUBSTANTIAL_COMPLETION → FINAL_ACCEPTED`. Three conditions guard it. No punch-phase defect of severity `FUNCTIONAL` or `LIFE_SAFETY` may remain in `OPEN`, `ASSIGNED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED`; an unresolved one earns `ERR_BLOCKING_PUNCH_ITEMS`. The request must carry `acknowledged_open_cosmetic_count`, and that number must equal exactly the count of punch-phase defects of severity `COSMETIC` on the project sitting in `OPEN`, `ASSIGNED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED` — the homeowner is signing that they know precisely how much cosmetic work they are accepting, and a mismatch earns `ERR_PUNCH_ACK_COUNT_MISMATCH`. And no draw request on the project may still be in `LENDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, because the job cannot be closed while the lender is still deciding; a walkthrough attempted with money in flight is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`. Although that code is the same one §2.10 step 10 uses for the ordinary state check, this draw test is evaluated last of the three — after the blocking-punch-item test and after the cosmetic acknowledgement — so a project that would fail a punch test as well earns the punch code instead.

Acceptance stamps `final_accepted_at` with the logical clock now and increments the project's `version` by one. It also performs one narrowly-scoped cascade: every punch-phase `COSMETIC` defect still sitting in `OPEN` advances to `WAIVED` with `resolved_at` stamped. Cosmetic tickets that had progressed to `ASSIGNED`, `READY_FOR_REVIEW`, or `REJECTED` are deliberately left untouched — work already under way is not silently written off. `FINAL_ACCEPTED` is terminal for the project row itself, but it is not the end of the relationship: warranty callbacks continue indefinitely afterward as new defect tickets with `phase` of `WARRANTY`, evaluated against the coverage rules above.
