# Affiliate Homeownership Sweat Equity API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Habitat-style homeownership affiliate runs one of the most heavily documented pipelines in the nonprofit housing sector. A family is chosen on three published criteria — need, ability to pay, and willingness to partner — by a volunteer committee whose scores must be traceable to individual reviewers. The same family then works hundreds of hours of sweat equity alongside community volunteers on a live construction site, finishes a homebuyer education curriculum, and finally closes on a zero-interest first mortgage paired with a silent second lien that carries the affiliate's subsidy. Every one of those steps is auditable, and every step is also a place where a well-meaning operator can quietly break the affiliate's compliance posture: by scoring a family and then underwriting its loan, by crediting hours the family never worked, by staffing a shift on a site that is under a safety hold, or by committing subsidy the pool no longer holds.

This service is the affiliate's system of record for that pipeline. It is mounted under `/homeownership`, and it exists to make the compliance posture mechanical rather than cultural: the selection committee's quorum is counted, not asserted; sweat equity hours pass through a claim–verify–credit ledger whose reversals net to exactly zero; the subsidy pool is a conserved quantity whose committed, disbursed, and available buckets always sum back to the pool; and the four operational custodies — family services, committee scoring, mortgage origination, and construction — are separated by identity checks that the server enforces on every write.

The design priority is determinism over convenience. All date and time reasoning reads a logical UTC clock that an administrator advances explicitly, so expiries, ages, forgiveness schedules, and reporting periods are reproducible. All money is integer cents. All state changes are recorded as transitions between named states, several entities carry two independent state dimensions that evolve on their own schedules, and shared parent records are guarded by optimistic concurrency so that a client cannot commit an arithmetic decision built on a record it has not observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, the acting identity, and `X-User-Role`, the role that identity is acting in. Both must be present and non-empty; a request that omits either — on any endpoint other than the logical-clock endpoint — is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). A header value that is not one of the seven role names below is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). A recognised role that is not listed among the addressed endpoint's authorized roles is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `FamilyServicesCoordinator`, `SelectionCommitteeMember`, `MortgageOfficer`, `ConstructionManager`, `Applicant`, and `Volunteer`.

`ProgramAdmin` is the executive director's seat: it owns the program envelope, the subsidy pool, land acquisition, grant reporting, the waitlist selector, the selection decision the committee's scores feed, and the logical clock. `FamilyServicesCoordinator` owns applicant intake, income and housing documentation, homebuyer education delivery, and the sweat equity hour ledger. `SelectionCommitteeMember` scores applicants and may retract a review, and holds no write access to hours, sites, or mortgages. `MortgageOfficer` structures the affordability-based payment, sizes and commits the silent second, closes the loan, and acts on the lien afterwards. `ConstructionManager` owns lot readiness, the site safety hold, shift scheduling, skilled-trade coordination, attendance, and cost capture. `Applicant` is the partner family acting on its own file. `Volunteer` is a community volunteer or tradesperson taking seats on shifts; it holds no exclusive operation, because every write it performs is one a `ConstructionManager` may also perform on its behalf — and notably it cannot check itself in, which is what makes the no-show strike ladder trustworthy.

Five of the seven roles have opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `FamilyServicesCoordinator`, `SelectionCommitteeMember`, `MortgageOfficer`, or `ConstructionManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `reviewer_user_id` on a selection review, as `claimed_by_user_id` or `verified_by_user_id` on a sweat equity entry, as `originating_officer_user_id` on a mortgage application, as `crew_lead_user_id` on a shift — and compares it to previously recorded operator identifiers for segregation-of-duties tests, but never resolves it against any entity's `id`.

Two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting applicant household; ownership checks on any resource that references a household compare the resource's household field to `X-User-ID`, resolving through a member's `household_id` where a member or a partner-family seat is addressed. When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting volunteer profile; ownership checks compare an assignment's `volunteer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and — when a volunteer profile itself is the addressed row — that profile's own `id` to it. For both entity-backed roles, an `X-User-ID` that matches no row of the backing entity is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 403), and an identity that resolves but addresses a row belonging to somebody else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and like every other instant this service emits it is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from the wall clock — two requests issued between clock moves therefore carry the same timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a payload-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422); so is a `limit` or `offset` whose value is not an integer literal, while a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as if it had been omitted and takes its default.

Unless a specific report states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by the order in which the rows were created — never by `id`. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). For the two entity-backed roles, ownership on a collection endpoint acts as a filter rather than as a rejection: rows the caller does not own are silently omitted and `total_count` counts only the rows that survive that scoping. An `Applicant` listing households, household members, sweat equity entries, education enrollments, mortgage applications, or shift assignments therefore sees only its own household's rows, and a `Volunteer` listing volunteer profiles or shift assignments sees only its own; listing construction shifts is not scoped, so a `Volunteer` sees every shift.

Collection endpoints also accept equality filters that make every foreign key reverse-reachable, so a caller never needs a nested URL to find children of a parent. Programs filter on `service_area_code` and `status`. Households filter on `program_id`, `selection_status`, `partnership_readiness`, and `score_basis`. Household members filter on `household_id`, `relationship`, and `status`. Selection reviews filter on `household_id`, `dimension`, `status`, and `reviewer_user_id`. Sweat equity entries filter on `household_id`, `member_id`, `shift_assignment_id`, the self-referential `reversal_of_entry_id`, `category`, and `status`. Build sites filter on `program_id`, `assigned_household_id`, `readiness_status`, and `safety_posture`. Cost entries filter on `build_site_id`, `category`, `is_in_kind`, and `status`. Construction shifts filter on `build_site_id`, `status`, `required_trade`, and the two window bounds `starts_after` and `starts_before`, which select shifts whose `start_at` falls at or after and at or before the supplied instants respectively. Volunteer profiles filter on `program_id`, `status`, and `trade_certification`, the last selecting profiles holding a certification for the named trade. Shift assignments filter on `shift_id`, `volunteer_id`, `household_member_id`, `role`, and `status`. Education enrollments filter on `household_id`, `curriculum_code`, and `status`. Mortgage applications filter on `household_id`, `build_site_id`, the self-referential `superseded_application_id`, `status`, and `lien_status`. Grant awards filter on `program_id`, `status`, and `award_code`. Every filter is optional; omitting it — or supplying it as an empty string — means "no restriction on that column". A filter drawn from a fixed vocabulary rejects a value outside that vocabulary with `ERR_VALIDATION`, as does an `is_in_kind` that is not one of `true`, `True`, `1`, `false`, `False`, or `0` and a window bound that is not a parseable instant; an identifier filter naming a row that does not exist is not an error but simply matches nothing. A query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored outright rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and Path Resolution

Each resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply one. Most resources also carry `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits; `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row, including mutations that arrive as cascades from another entity's transition. A row is versioned at most once per request: however many hops of a single request touch the same row, its `version` rises by exactly 1, and a row created by the request itself stays at `version` 1 even when a later hop of that same request writes to it. Whenever a mutation bumps `version`, it also refreshes `updated_at` to the logical clock instant of the request; a second hop that finds the row already bumped still refreshes `updated_at`.

The construction cost ledger is the one exception. A cost entry is append-only: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Its `id` is never used as a tie-break or ordering key in any business decision; `created_at` is the ordering key for cost reporting. Because a cost entry has no `version`, it is never guarded by optimistic concurrency. A cost-entry identifier that resolves to no row reports `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, the same identifier the sweat equity ledger uses.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right in path order, and the first identifier that resolves to no row decides the response with HTTP 404 and the error naming the missing entity: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved the same way and with the same error identifiers, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields; a payload naming both a missing household and a missing site reports the one that appears first in the endpoint's input list.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads the wall clock. A fresh service starts with its logical clock at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every date comparison, expiry test, age derivation, forgiveness accrual, and reporting-period boundary reads that value. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it, though the role is checked only when it is offered: an `X-User-Role` header supplied on this endpoint must name one of the seven roles and must be `ProgramAdmin`, while a request that supplies no role header at all is accepted and `X-User-ID` is never examined here. The instant is moved by supplying `current_utc` — the spellings `to_iso8601`, `current_utc_iso8601`, and `now` are accepted as aliases for the same field, the first one present winning — and the value must be a parseable ISO 8601 instant or the request is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The clock may only move forward, so a `current_utc` that is not strictly after the present value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE` (HTTP 422). The endpoint returns the clock state as an object carrying the single field `current_utc`, the new instant formatted with three fractional digits.

Advancing the clock is not a passive act. Any homebuyer-education enrollment sitting in `COMPLETED` whose currency window has run out becomes `EXPIRED`, whatever its curriculum; an expiry of a `HOMEBUYER_CORE` enrollment then cascades into the household's readiness dimension and from there into that household's cleared-to-close mortgages, as §3.5 describes, while an expiring `BUDGET_COACHING` or `HOME_MAINTENANCE` enrollment moves nothing but its own row. Every volunteer profile's minor flag is re-derived from the volunteer's date of birth against the new instant. Every closed application whose lien is still live re-derives its forgiveness accrual. Every row the clock touches has its `version` incremented by 1.

### 2.6. Money, Hours, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer cents; there is no decimal money anywhere in the API, and no rounding is applied to a sum of cents. Where a monetary value is produced by division or by multiplication with a rate, the result is rounded half-up to a whole number of cents at the step where it is produced, and later steps consume the rounded value — never an unrounded intermediate. Two quantities deliberately use truncation instead: monthly gross income and the maximum total housing payment are floored to whole cents, never rounded.

Hours are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places, rounded half-up. A supplied hour figure, or any other supplied decimal such as a review `score`, that carries more than two decimal places is a payload-shape failure rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`; the server never silently rounds an input to fit. The crediting rule in §3.4 pins the rounding order explicitly: each entry's weighted hours are rounded to two decimal places before that entry is compared against any allowance and before it is added to any ledger. Rounding per entry and then summing is the specification; summing raw products and rounding the total afterwards produces different ledger balances and is wrong. Ratios — cost burden, debt-to-income, area-median-income percentage, and shift fill — are rounded half-up to four decimal places before any threshold comparison. Selection scores are whole numbers by construction; the composite selection score and the willingness score round half-up to two decimal places.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Four write endpoints size their arithmetic against a parent record that other writers also move. Each of them requires a non-optional version field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent; it must be an integer of at least 1, and anything else is a payload-shape failure. If the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with HTTP 412 and the parent-specific error identifier. No other endpoint accepts a version field.

Approving a mortgage commits silent-second subsidy out of the shared program pool, so an approval must carry `expected_program_version` matching the governing program's current `version`. If an earlier accepted write — another approval, a pool adjustment, or a closing — has already moved the pool since the client last read the program, the approval is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the pool arithmetic this request was built on no longer holds. A subsidy-pool adjustment carries `expected_program_version` for the same reason and is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) when it was sized against a balance an earlier write has already changed.

Reserving a seat on a shift requires `expected_shift_version` matching the parent shift's current `version`; a mismatch means the shift's confirmed counts, lock state, or slot inventory changed after the client computed that a seat was free, and the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHIFT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Originating a mortgage application requires `expected_household_version` matching the household's current `version`, because a create legitimately supersedes a prior draft for the same household; a mismatch means the scores, verified income, or readiness the officer underwrote against have since moved, and the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

A request that violates several rules returns exactly one error, and which one is fixed rather than incidental: checks are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the first failing check decides the response. This ordering is observable and must be implemented literally.

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. The role is authorized for the addressed endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. For the entity-backed roles `Applicant` and `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing household or volunteer profile respectively — `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). This applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `Applicant` or `Volunteer`.
5. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — the matching not-found error (404).
6. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in the endpoint's declared input order — the matching not-found error (404). This applies to every creating endpoint that carries foreign keys.
7. Ownership: an `Applicant` may address only its own household and that household's members, entries, enrollments, applications, and partner seats; a `Volunteer` may address only its own profile and assignments whose `volunteer_id` is its own identity — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This too applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `Applicant` or `Volunteer`. Reserving a seat is the one endpoint where this check follows position 8 instead of preceding it, because the ownership question there is asked of the `volunteer_id` or `household_member_id` the body names, and those fields are shape-checked first.
8. Payload shape: required fields present, enum values in range, numbers inside their declared bounds, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, timestamps valid ISO 8601 — `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). A request body that is not a JSON object, or that cannot be parsed as JSON at all, is the same failure and is likewise rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).
9. Optimistic concurrency, on the four endpoints of §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SHIFT_VERSION`, or `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (412).
10. The dimension being moved is not already terminal — `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). The terminal values across the system are `DENIED`, `CLOSED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, `NO_SHOW`, `SUPERSEDED`, `SATISFIED`, `RECAPTURED`, `REVERSED`, `FAILED`, and `EXPIRED`. Two dimensions read that list narrowly: on an education enrollment only `FAILED` and `EXPIRED` are treated as terminal, so a module outcome recorded against a `COMPLETED` enrollment falls through to position 11, and on a selection review neither of its two values is terminal.
11. The addressed row is in a state the requested transition declares as a legal origin for the dimension being moved — `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` (409). Because the terminal test runs first, a row parked in a terminal value that is also not a legal origin reports `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` rather than this error.
12. The governing program is not frozen — `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` (409). This guards household intake, household submission, screening, committee referral, return-to-screening and the selection decision, selection reviews and their retraction, sweat equity entries and all their sub-actions, construction shifts and all their sub-actions, shift assignments and all their sub-actions, mortgage applications and all their sub-actions, education module outcomes, and cost entries and their voiding. It does not guard adding a household member, recording a land acquisition, advancing lot readiness, setting the safety posture, registering a volunteer, opening an education enrollment, or any read. On six writes — reserving, confirming, checking in, and releasing a seat, voiding a cost line, and acting on a lien — this posture check is evaluated ahead of positions 10 and 11 rather than after them, so a frozen program answers `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` even when the addressed row is also in the wrong state; on the seat reservation it precedes position 9 too, so a frozen program outranks a stale `expected_shift_version`. Every other guarded write evaluates it in the position listed here.
13. Documentation completeness on the committee referral, which requires the household's documentation to be verified — `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (409).
14. The area-median-income band on the committee referral — `ERR_INCOME_OUT_OF_BAND` (422).
15. Committee quorum on the selection decision — `ERR_COMMITTEE_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409).
16. One submitted review per reviewer per household per dimension — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW` (409).
17. A willingness veto carries its documented reason — `ERR_VETO_REASON_REQUIRED` (422).
18. Segregation of duties between committee scoring and mortgage work — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This guards mortgage origination, submission to underwriting, and approval.
19. Segregation of duties between claiming and verifying hours — `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN` (403), on the verification decision.
20. The member named by a sweat equity entry belongs to the entry's household and is active — `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on both claiming and crediting.
21. The work date sits inside its legal range — `ERR_WORK_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422), on claiming and on resubmission.
22. Member age and minor category eligibility at crediting — `ERR_MEMBER_INELIGIBLE_FOR_CREDIT` or `ERR_MINOR_CATEGORY_NOT_ALLOWED` (422).
23. The per-category hour allowance at crediting — `ERR_CATEGORY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED` (422).
24. The family-member hour allowance at crediting — `ERR_FAMILY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED` (422).
25. The site is not under a safety hold — `ERR_SITE_UNDER_SAFETY_HOLD` (409). This guards six operations: scheduling a shift, opening a shift, locking a shift, reserving an assignment, confirming an assignment, and checking in.
26. The site is ready to build before a shift may be opened — `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY_TO_BUILD` (409).
27. Shift seat declaration — `ERR_SHIFT_HAS_NO_SLOTS` or `ERR_REQUIRED_TRADE_MISSING` (422), on scheduling and on opening.
28. Volunteer eligibility — `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_WAIVER_EXPIRED`, or `ERR_TRADE_CERTIFICATION_INVALID` (422), on reserving and on confirming.
29. Interval conflicts — `ERR_SITE_SHIFT_BUFFER_CONFLICT` on scheduling a shift, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` on reserving an assignment (409).
30. Seat inventory — `ERR_SHIFT_ROLE_FULL` or `ERR_PARTNER_MEMBER_NOT_ON_SITE` (409), on reserving an assignment.
31. The weekly volunteer hour ceiling — `ERR_VOLUNTEER_WEEKLY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422), on reserving an assignment.
32. Timing windows — `ERR_CHECK_IN_WINDOW_CLOSED` on check-in, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ENDED` on shift completion (409).
33. Lock readiness — `ERR_TRADE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` or `ERR_SHIFT_FILL_INSUFFICIENT` (409), on locking.
34. Education gating — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` on enrollment, `ERR_EDUCATION_NOT_COMPLETE` or `ERR_EDUCATION_EXPIRED` on closing (409).
35. Underwriting prerequisites — `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_SELECTED` or `ERR_SITE_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409), on origination and on submission to underwriting.
36. Affordability — `ERR_NO_AFFORDABLE_STRUCTURE` (422), on submission to underwriting.
37. Closing prerequisites — `ERR_SITE_NOT_COMPLETED` or `ERR_SWEAT_EQUITY_INCOMPLETE` (409), on closing.
38. Subsidy capacity — `ERR_SUBSIDY_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (409), on approval.
39. Pool adjustments may not fall below outstanding obligations — `ERR_POOL_BELOW_OBLIGATIONS` (409).
40. In-kind bookkeeping — `ERR_IN_KIND_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), on appending a cost entry.
41. Lien action preconditions — `ERR_FORBEARANCE_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_LIEN_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING`, or `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OPEN` (409).
42. Reporting period closed before an impact report — `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OPEN` (409).
43. Waitlist promotion feasibility — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WAITLIST_HOUSEHOLD` or `ERR_NO_READY_SITE_CAPACITY` (409).
44. The logical clock advances strictly forward — `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE` (422).

Three collisions are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. An approval on a suspended program whose remaining subsidy is *also* too small fails both the frozen-program check at position 12 and the subsidy-capacity check at position 38; the answer is `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`, never `ERR_SUBSIDY_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. A reservation for a volunteer whose waiver expires before the shift *and* who is already booked on an overlapping shift fails both position 28 and position 29; the answer is `ERR_WAIVER_EXPIRED`, never `ERR_VOLUNTEER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Crediting an entry whose member has been removed from the household while the family allowance is *also* exhausted fails both position 20 and position 24; the answer is `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`, never `ERR_FAMILY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED`.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

The affiliate's compliance posture splits the pipeline into four custodies that no single operator may combine: family services intake and hour crediting, committee scoring, mortgage origination, and construction custody. Two checks make that split real.

The first protects the selection process from the lender. An identity that has authored a submitted selection review on a household may not originate, submit to underwriting, or approve that household's mortgage; any of those three writes whose `X-User-ID` appears as `reviewer_user_id` on a submitted review of the same household is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Retracted reviews do not bind — only reviews still in `SUBMITTED` status do. The second protects the hour ledger from itself: the identity recording a verification decision on a sweat equity entry may not be the identity that claimed it, so a verification whose `X-User-ID` equals the entry's `claimed_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Note the deliberate asymmetry of who may do what: a `ConstructionManager` may verify construction hours it witnessed but may not credit them, because crediting is the `FamilyServicesCoordinator`'s custody; a `ProgramAdmin` issues the selection decision but may never author a review, so the scores it acts on are always somebody else's; and a `MortgageOfficer` can neither touch the household's verified income nor its hour ledger, both of which its structure depends on.

### 2.10. The Subsidy Pool as a Conserved Quantity

Subsidy capital, measured in cents, is conserved. A program's pool is always partitioned into exactly three buckets: `subsidy_committed_cents` is the sum of the silent-second amounts of that program's applications in `APPROVED` or `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` status; `subsidy_disbursed_cents` is the sum over applications that are `CLOSED` and whose lien is `ACTIVE`, `FORBEARANCE`, or `TRANSFER_PENDING`; and `subsidy_available_cents` is `subsidy_pool_cents` less committed less disbursed. That identity — pool equals available plus committed plus disbursed — holds after every accepted write, with no rounding anywhere, because the ledger is pure integer cents. Applications in `DRAFT`, `UNDERWRITING`, `DENIED`, or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing to either bucket, and a closed application whose lien has reached `SATISFIED` or `RECAPTURED` has left the disbursed bucket.

`program_posture` is derived, never supplied: it is `FROZEN` whenever the program's `status` is `SUSPENDED`; otherwise `SUBSIDY_RESTRICTED` when the program is `ACTIVE` and `subsidy_available_cents` is exactly zero; otherwise `OPEN`. It is recomputed on every write that moves a bucket or the program's status, and a program in `FROZEN` posture refuses the writes enumerated at position 12 of §2.8.

### 2.11. Two-Dimensional State and Cascade Discipline

Two entities carry two independent state dimensions. An applicant household has a `selection_status` that walks the selection pipeline and a `partnership_readiness` that tracks education and sweat equity; a mortgage application has a `status` that walks origination and a `lien_status` that tracks the silent second after closing. The two dimensions of an entity move on their own schedules: the readiness regression cycle in §3.5, for example, runs to completion without `selection_status` moving at all. A transition request always names one dimension, and the state checks at positions 10 and 11 of §2.8 are evaluated against that dimension only.

Cascades are synchronous and complete inside the request that triggers them. When a transition's side effects reach into other entities — crediting an hour entry touching a member, a household, a readiness dimension, and a mortgage; a safety hold cancelling shifts, then assignments, then volunteer commitments — every hop lands before the response is written, and each touched row's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes. The response body carries the addressed resource in its post-cascade state.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Affiliate Program and Stewarding the Subsidy Pool

Everything in this service hangs off an affiliate program, and a `ProgramAdmin` opens one by declaring the affiliate's `affiliate_name` (1 to 120 characters, and unique across programs — a repeat of a name already on file is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`), the `service_area_code` of the county it builds in (2 to 10 uppercase characters), the `ami_median_income_cents` that will serve as the denominator of every income-band test (at least 1,000,000 cents — a thousand dollars a month of area median income is the floor the service accepts), and the `subsidy_pool_cents` of silent-second capital the affiliate is prepared to commit this program cycle (zero or more). Two policy dials are optional. `sweat_equity_base_hours_required` is the hour requirement placed on a partner household with a single adult occupant; it defaults to 300 hours and may be anywhere from 0 through 500 hours carried to two decimal places. `committee_quorum` is the number of distinct committee reviewers required *per scoring dimension* before a selection decision may be issued; it defaults to 3 and may be set anywhere from 2 through 9.

A freshly created program opens in `ACTIVE` status with an `OPEN` posture, and its derived counters start at their identity values: `subsidy_committed_cents` and `subsidy_disbursed_cents` at zero, `subsidy_available_cents` equal to the whole pool, `donated_material_fmv_cents` at zero, and `households_served_count` at zero. The last two are pure aggregates and never accept input. `donated_material_fmv_cents` is the sum of the documented fair market values of every in-kind cost line still in `RECORDED` status across all of the program's build sites; `households_served_count` is the number of the program's households whose `selection_status` has reached `CLOSED`. Reading a program returns the live ledger and posture.

Pool stewardship happens through a single administrative action that takes an `action`, an optional `delta_cents`, a mandatory `expected_program_version`, and an optional `memo`. With `action` of `TOP_UP` the pool grows by `delta_cents`; with `REDUCE` it shrinks by the same figure. `delta_cents`, when supplied, must be zero or greater, and when it is omitted the movement is taken as zero, so a `TOP_UP` or `REDUCE` without it is accepted and leaves the pool where it stood while still versioning the program; the field is ignored outright by the two status actions. A reduction that would leave `subsidy_pool_cents` below the sum of `subsidy_committed_cents` and `subsidy_disbursed_cents` breaks the conservation identity of §2.10 and is refused with `ERR_POOL_BELOW_OBLIGATIONS`. With `action` of `SUSPEND_PROGRAM` the program's `status` flips to `SUSPENDED`, which immediately re-derives `program_posture` to `FROZEN` and stops intake, hour crediting, construction scheduling, and every money movement across the program; `RESUME_PROGRAM` flips `status` back to `ACTIVE` and re-derives the posture from the available balance. Each of those two is a state transition and is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` when the program is not standing where it expects: `SUSPEND_PROGRAM` requires `ACTIVE` and `RESUME_PROGRAM` requires `SUSPENDED`, so neither is idempotent. Both are checked after the version comparison of §2.7, so a stale `expected_program_version` is reported first. In all four cases `subsidy_available_cents` and `program_posture` are recomputed and the program's `version` increments by 1. Note that pool adjustment is the one program-level write that a frozen program still accepts — otherwise a suspension could never be lifted.

### 3.2. Applicant Intake, Household Composition, and Documented Screening

A `FamilyServicesCoordinator` opens an applicant file by recording the household against its `program_id`: the `household_name` (1 to 120 characters), the `household_size` counting every person who will occupy the home (at least 1, at most 12), the `current_bedroom_count` of the dwelling the family lives in today (0 through 10), the `habitability_deficiency_count` of documented substandard-housing findings in that dwelling (0 through 10), and the family's own statement of its finances: `self_reported_annual_income_cents`, `self_reported_housing_cost_cents` (a monthly figure), `monthly_debt_payment_cents` of recurring non-housing debt service, the tri-merge middle `credit_score` (300 through 850), and `on_time_housing_payment_months` of consecutive on-time housing payments evidenced in the file (0 through 120). All four money fields must be zero or greater. The new file opens with `selection_status` of `DRAFT`, `partnership_readiness` of `NOT_READY`, `documentation_status` of `INCOMPLETE`, and `adult_occupant_count` of 1. Everything computed is empty at this point: `verified_annual_income_cents` and `verified_housing_cost_cents` are null, `need_score`, `ability_to_pay_score`, `willingness_score`, `composite_selection_score`, `score_basis`, and `sweat_equity_required_hours` are null, `sweat_equity_credited_hours` and `family_member_credited_hours` are 0, `sweat_equity_satisfied` is false, and `closing_date` and `denial_reason_code` are null. The `id` the response returns is the value the family will use as its `X-User-ID` when acting in the `Applicant` role.

Household composition is recorded member by member, by the coordinator or by the family itself. Each member carries its `household_id`, its `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), its `relationship` — one of `APPLICANT`, `CO_APPLICANT`, `DEPENDENT`, `EXTENDED_FAMILY`, or `FRIEND_OF_FAMILY` — its `birth_date` as a UTC-midnight instant, and an optional `is_adult_occupant` flag that defaults to false and marks a member who is at least 18 and will occupy the home. A member begins with `credited_hours_to_date` of 0 and `status` of `ACTIVE`, and adding one recomputes the household's `adult_occupant_count` and bumps the household's `version`. Adding a member is one of the few writes that carries no posture or state gate at all: it is accepted while the program is frozen and against a household in any `selection_status`, terminal ones included, so composition can be corrected on a file that has already been denied or closed. Members are never deleted; when a household is denied or withdraws, every `ACTIVE` member of that household flips to `REMOVED`.

Submission moves the file from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and may be performed by the family or by the coordinator on its behalf, carrying an `attestation_signed` acknowledgement. Two conditions guard it, each refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` and each evaluated after the origin-state and posture gates: the household must have at least one `ACTIVE` member whose `relationship` is `APPLICANT`, and the declared `household_size` must be at least as large as the count of `ACTIVE` members on file. Submission sets `documentation_status` to `SUBMITTED` and recomputes `adult_occupant_count` as the number of `ACTIVE` members flagged as adult occupants, with a floor of 1 — a household always counts as having at least one adult occupant even before anyone is flagged. That count is not a stored tally kept in step by hand but a figure re-derived from the live membership on every read of the household, so removing an adult occupant lowers it again on the next read without any write against the household itself.

Screening is the coordinator's documented verification pass and moves the file from `SUBMITTED` to `SCREENING`. It carries `income_documents_received` as a boolean, optionally `verified_annual_income_cents` and `verified_housing_cost_cents` — these two fields are writable *only* here — and optionally `screening_notes`. The program must not be frozen. Whichever verified figures are supplied are written; `documentation_status` advances to `VERIFIED` only when both verified figures are supplied *and* `income_documents_received` is true, and otherwise remains at `SUBMITTED` unchanged. Screening also produces the household's first scores, computed on the **preliminary basis**, which reads the family's self-reported figures and ignores the verified ones even when they have just been written in the same request. `score_basis` is set to `PRELIMINARY`, `need_score`, `ability_to_pay_score`, and `ami_pct` are written, and `willingness_score` stays null — which means `composite_selection_score` stays null too, because the composite cannot be formed without a willingness figure.

The scoring rule itself is a weighted three-part instrument, and it runs identically on both bases; only its two income inputs differ. Take the income basis for the current basis and floor it to a whole number of cents divided by twelve to obtain monthly gross income. The **cost burden ratio** is the housing-cost basis divided by that monthly gross, rounded half-up to four decimal places; if monthly gross is zero the ratio is taken as zero. Cost burden contributes 40 points when the ratio is at least 0.5000, 30 points when it is at least 0.4000, 20 points when it is at least 0.3000, and nothing below that. **Overcrowding** is `household_size` divided by the greater of 1 and `current_bedroom_count`, contributing 30 points at two or more persons per bedroom, 20 points at one and a half or more, and nothing below. **Substandard housing** contributes 30 points when `habitability_deficiency_count` is 2 or more, 15 points when it is exactly 1, and nothing when it is zero. `need_score` is the sum of those three contributions and therefore ranges from 0 to 100.

Ability to pay is built the same way. The **debt-to-income ratio** is `monthly_debt_payment_cents` divided by monthly gross income, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and taken as zero when monthly gross is zero; it contributes 50 points at 0.1300 or below, 35 points at 0.2500 or below, 20 points at 0.3600 or below, and nothing above that. **Credit** contributes 25 points at a `credit_score` of 620 or more, 15 points at 580 or more, and nothing below 580. **Payment history** contributes 25 points when `on_time_housing_payment_months` is 12 or more and nothing otherwise. `ability_to_pay_score` is the sum, again 0 through 100. Separately, `ami_pct` is the income basis divided by the program's `ami_median_income_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. When a willingness figure exists, `composite_selection_score` is 0.40 times `need_score` plus 0.35 times `ability_to_pay_score` plus 0.25 times `willingness_score`, rounded half-up to two decimal places; while `willingness_score` is null the composite is null.

The **final basis** is the same instrument fed from `verified_annual_income_cents` and `verified_housing_cost_cents` instead of the self-reported pair. Because verified figures routinely differ from what a family stated, the same row yields a different `need_score`, a different `ability_to_pay_score`, a different `ami_pct`, and often a different income-band verdict than the preliminary pass produced minutes earlier. Stored preliminary values are overwritten wholesale, never blended. The final basis is used in exactly two places: the income-band gate at committee referral, and the recomputation at the selection decision. Only the selection decision *writes* a final-basis figure. The referral computes the final-basis `ami_pct` solely in order to run its gate and stores no score field of its own: the household's stored `ami_pct`, `need_score`, `ability_to_pay_score`, and `score_basis` are left untouched by the referral and continue to carry whatever the last preliminary pass wrote — a household that has just been referred still reads back `score_basis` of `PRELIMINARY` and the preliminary `ami_pct` — until the selection decision recomputes them on the final basis.

Referring a screened household to the committee moves it from `SCREENING` to `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, carries an optional `referral_memo`, and is the point where two gates and one freeze land. The household's `documentation_status` must be `VERIFIED`, or the referral is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. Its final-basis `ami_pct` must sit inside the affiliate's income band — at least 0.3000 and at most 0.8000, both bounds inclusive — or the referral is refused with `ERR_INCOME_OUT_OF_BAND`; a household at exactly 30% or exactly 80% of area median income passes. A successful referral freezes `sweat_equity_required_hours` for the remainder of the household's life: it is the program's `sweat_equity_base_hours_required` plus 50 hours for each adult occupant beyond the first (never a negative adjustment when `adult_occupant_count` is 1), capped at 500 hours. With the default 300-hour base, a two-adult household owes 350 hours and a three-adult household 400; a household large enough to push past 500 owes 500 and no more. Later changes to the program's base requirement or to the household's adult count do not move a frozen requirement.

### 3.3. Committee Scoring, the Selection Decision, and Waitlist Promotion

A `SelectionCommitteeMember` files one review per scoring dimension on a household under committee review, naming the `household_id`, the `dimension` — `NEED`, `ABILITY`, or `WILLINGNESS` — a `score` from 0 through 100 carried to two decimal places, a `recommendation` of `ADVANCE`, `ABSTAIN`, or `VETO_WILLINGNESS`, and optionally a `documented_reason` of 10 to 500 characters — those bounds are enforced whenever the field is present, so a reason of nine characters is a payload-shape failure rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` before any of the review's own rules are reached. The server stamps `reviewer_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and opens the review in `SUBMITTED` status. The household must be standing in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` or the filing is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A reviewer may hold at most one `SUBMITTED` review per household per dimension; filing a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW`, and the existing one must be retracted first. A `VETO_WILLINGNESS` recommendation is only meaningful on the willingness dimension and only with its rationale on the record: because the length bounds are already enforced as payload shape, `ERR_VETO_REASON_REQUIRED` is what answers a veto whose `documented_reason` was omitted altogether, or a veto filed on the `NEED` or `ABILITY` dimension. Retraction moves a review from `SUBMITTED` to `RETRACTED`, carries an optional `retraction_reason`, and removes the row from every quorum count, every score aggregate, and the veto test. Any `SelectionCommitteeMember` may retract any review — the service does not require that the retracting identity be the one that filed it — and neither of the review's two statuses is treated as terminal, so a second retraction of the same row answers `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.

Quorum and willingness are recomputed whenever a review is filed or retracted, and again when the decision is issued. Filing and retracting both write the recomputed `willingness_score` and `composite_selection_score` straight onto the household and bump its `version`, so a committee member's action versions two rows at once; the composite they write is formed from the household's *stored* `need_score` and `ability_to_pay_score`, which at that moment are still the preliminary ones, and a null stored score is read as zero for this purpose. Neither action touches `score_basis`, which stays at `PRELIMINARY` while the committee works, and neither recomputes `need_score`, `ability_to_pay_score`, or `ami_pct`. Only reviews in `SUBMITTED` status of the target household are candidates; retracted rows are invisible to all three computations. Within a dimension, a reviewer contributes exactly one effective review — the one with the greatest `created_at` — and a tie is structurally impossible because of the one-submitted-review-per-dimension rule. Quorum is met only when, for each of the three dimensions `NEED`, `ABILITY`, and `WILLINGNESS` independently, the number of distinct `reviewer_user_id` values among that dimension's effective reviews is at least the program's `committee_quorum`. `willingness_score` is the mean of the `score` values of the effective willingness reviews, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and is null when there are no effective willingness reviews at all — which is what makes the composite null immediately after a retraction removes the last one. A veto is present when any effective willingness review carries `recommendation` of `VETO_WILLINGNESS`.

The decision itself belongs to the `ProgramAdmin`, carries an optional `decision_minutes` that documents the committee's meeting, and is a three-way fork out of `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`. Quorum must be met on all three dimensions or the decision is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, and that test comes first: a decision refused for want of quorum writes nothing at all, leaving `score_basis` at `PRELIMINARY` and the household's `version` where it stood. Once quorum is established and before branching, the household's scores are recomputed on the final basis and `score_basis` is set to `FINAL`, and `willingness_score` and `composite_selection_score` are written. With quorum met, a final-basis `composite_selection_score` of 70.00 or more and no veto present sends the household to `SELECTED`. A composite of at least 55.00 but strictly below 70.00, again with no veto, sends it to `WAITLISTED`; this branch writes exactly the same score fields and the same `FINAL` basis as the selected branch does, and differs from it in nothing but the resulting state. A composite strictly below 55.00, *or* the presence of any effective willingness veto regardless of score, sends the household to `DENIED`.

Denial is the branch with teeth. `denial_reason_code` is written as `WILLINGNESS_VETO` when a veto was the cause and as `SCORE_BELOW_WAITLIST` when the composite simply fell short — the reason code vocabulary also includes `INCOME_BAND` and `DOCUMENTATION` for denials recorded on those grounds. Denial then cascades across the household's other dimension and its children: `partnership_readiness` moves to `WITHDRAWN` no matter which readiness state it held, every `ACTIVE` member of the household flips to `REMOVED`, and every education enrollment of that household still in `ENROLLED` or `IN_PROGRESS` flips to `FAILED`. `DENIED` and `CLOSED` are the two terminal values of the selection dimension; `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for readiness and is reachable from every non-terminal readiness state.

Waitlisted households are not forgotten. A `ProgramAdmin` runs the promotion selector against a program with a `promote_count` from 1 through 10, and the selector is fully deterministic. The eligible pool is every household of that program whose `selection_status` is `WAITLISTED`, whose `partnership_readiness` is not `WITHDRAWN`, and whose `composite_selection_score` is 55.00 or more. That pool is ranked by `composite_selection_score` from highest to lowest, ties broken by `need_score` from highest to lowest, and any remaining ties broken by `created_at` from oldest to newest — the row `id` is never a tie-break. Capacity is the number of the program's build sites whose `readiness_status` is `READY_TO_BUILD` and whose `assigned_household_id` is still null; sites under a safety hold or already under construction do not count. The number actually promoted is the smallest of the requested `promote_count`, the capacity, and the size of the ranked pool, and the promoted households are the first that many rows of the ranking. Each promoted household transitions `WAITLISTED → SELECTED` with its `score_basis` left at `FINAL`, and its `version` increments by 1; the response is the array of promoted households, carried as a bare array in `data` with no `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, or `has_more` alongside it — this is a write, not a listing. An empty eligible pool is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_WAITLIST_HOUSEHOLD`, and a non-empty pool with zero ready-and-unassigned site capacity is refused with `ERR_NO_READY_SITE_CAPACITY`.

When the committee's work needs another documentation pass, the coordinator can send the file backwards from `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` to `SCREENING` with an optional `reason`. That regression clears `composite_selection_score` and `willingness_score` to null and resets `score_basis` to `PRELIMINARY`, but it deliberately leaves every `SUBMITTED` review untouched, so quorum survives the loop and the committee does not have to re-score a family whose paperwork was merely incomplete. The `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → SCREENING → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` cycle may repeat without bound.

### 3.4. The Sweat Equity Hour Ledger

Sweat equity is the partnership's currency, and it moves through a four-step ledger — claimed, verified, credited, and occasionally reversed — precisely so that no single identity can put hours on a household's record alone. A claim is opened by the family or by its coordinator and names the `household_id`, the `member_id` of the person who did the work, the `category` of work, the `work_date` as the UTC instant the hours were worked, and the `hours_claimed` — at least 0.25 hours, at most 12, carried to two decimal places. The five categories are `CONSTRUCTION`, `RESTORE_STORE`, `HOMEOWNER_EDUCATION`, `OTHER_AFFILIATE`, and `ADVOCACY`. A new claim opens in `CLAIMED` status with `claimed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, with `credit_weight` and `hours_credited` still null because nothing has reached the ledger yet, with `capped_hours` at 0, and with `shift_assignment_id`, `reversal_of_entry_id`, and `verified_by_user_id` all null. Two checks guard the claim: the named member must belong to the same household as the entry and must still be `ACTIVE`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the `work_date` must be at or before the logical clock's present instant and at or after the household record's own `created_at`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_WORK_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Verification is the segregation-of-duties gate and is recorded by a `FamilyServicesCoordinator` or a `ConstructionManager` with a `decision` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` and, for a rejection, a `rejection_reason`. Either way the acting identity is stamped onto `verified_by_user_id`, and either way the acting identity must differ from `claimed_by_user_id`. A `VERIFIED` decision additionally requires that the member is still `ACTIVE` and that the `work_date` has not drifted past the logical clock, and moves the entry `CLAIMED → VERIFIED`. A `REJECTED` decision requires its `rejection_reason` to be present — an omitted reason is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, and because the field is optional as far as payload shape is concerned that refusal lands only after the origin-state, posture, and self-verification gates have all passed — moves the entry `CLAIMED → REJECTED`, and sets `hours_credited` to 0 rather than leaving it null. A rejected claim is not the end of the story: the family — and only the family, acting as `Applicant` on its own household — may correct and resubmit it, overwriting `hours_claimed` and `work_date` from the new payload, clearing `verified_by_user_id` back to null, and returning the entry `REJECTED → CLAIMED`. Both of those fields are mandatory on a resubmission, carry the same bounds the original claim did, and the replacement `work_date` is re-tested against the clock and the household's `created_at` in the same way, so a correction may not smuggle in an out-of-range date. That correction is only available while the household's `selection_status` is `SELECTED` or `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE` — any other selection state, the household having vanished included, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — and the `CLAIMED → REJECTED → CLAIMED` cycle may repeat without bound.

Crediting is where hours actually land, and it belongs exclusively to the `FamilyServicesCoordinator`. The entry must be `VERIFIED` and the household must be `SELECTED` or `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE`, any other selection state being refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The named member must also still belong to the household and still be `ACTIVE`, or the credit is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` before the waterfall begins. The allocation then runs as a waterfall in a fixed order. First, the member's age at the work date is computed as the whole number of years between `birth_date` and `work_date`, taking 365.25 days to the year and discarding the fraction. A member under 16 at that date cannot earn credit at all and the request is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_INELIGIBLE_FOR_CREDIT`; a member who is 16 or 17 counts as a minor, and a minor may only earn credit in the `RESTORE_STORE` or `ADVOCACY` categories — site safety keeps under-18 partners off `CONSTRUCTION` and the homebuyer curriculum is adult-only — so any other category from a minor is refused with `ERR_MINOR_CATEGORY_NOT_ALLOWED`.

Second, the entry's `credit_weight` is derived. Work in `CONSTRUCTION`, `RESTORE_STORE`, or `HOMEOWNER_EDUCATION` carries full weight of 1.00; `OTHER_AFFILIATE` work carries 0.75; `ADVOCACY` carries 0.50. A minor's weight is then halved. The product is rounded half-up to two decimal places, so an adult on advocacy work earns a weight of 0.50 and a 17-year-old on the same work earns 0.25. Third — and this is the step whose ordering matters most — the entry's gross weighted hours are `hours_claimed` multiplied by `credit_weight` and **rounded half-up to two decimal places immediately**, before any allowance comparison and before any ledger addition. Three separate 1.75-hour `OTHER_AFFILIATE` entries therefore credit 1.31 hours each for a household total of 3.93, not the 3.94 that summing the raw products and rounding once at the end would produce; per-entry rounding is the specification and sum-first rounding is wrong.

Fourth comes the per-category allowance, which caps the *sum* of already-credited hours in that category for that household. Homebuyer-education hours cap at 25.00 hours per household and advocacy hours cap at 10.00; the other three categories are uncapped. Category usage is the sum of `hours_credited` over the household's entries in `CREDITED` or `REVERSED` status whose `category` matches this entry's, and the remaining category room is that cap less the usage, floored at zero and rounded to two decimal places — an uncapped category always has room. Fifth comes the family-member allowance, which exists because the partnership is supposed to be worked by the partners: any member whose `relationship` is something other than `APPLICANT` or `CO_APPLICANT` draws against an aggregate allowance of half the household's frozen `sweat_equity_required_hours`, truncated to a whole number of hours — 150 hours against a 300-hour requirement, 175 against 350. The remaining family room is that allowance less the household's current `family_member_credited_hours`, floored at zero and rounded to two decimal places; members who are the applicant or co-applicant are exempt from this allowance entirely. A family-scoped entry arriving with zero family room left is refused outright with `ERR_FAMILY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED`.

Sixth, `hours_credited` is the smallest of the gross weighted hours, the remaining category room, and the remaining family room, and `capped_hours` records the difference between the gross figure and what was actually credited — the portion refused because an allowance was exhausted. Partial credit is normal and expected: an entry that overshoots an allowance credits what fits and books the rest as capped. An entry that would credit exactly zero hours is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED`. Only entries in `CREDITED` or `REVERSED` status consume allowance; entries sitting in `CLAIMED`, `VERIFIED`, or `REJECTED` consume nothing.

A successful credit then cascades four hops, all inside the same request. The member's `credited_hours_to_date` rises by the credited hours. The household's `sweat_equity_credited_hours` rises by the same amount, its `family_member_credited_hours` is recomputed, and `sweat_equity_satisfied` is re-derived as whether `sweat_equity_credited_hours` has reached `sweat_equity_required_hours` — equality satisfies the requirement. If the household's `selection_status` is `SELECTED` and its credited hours are now above zero, it advances `SELECTED → PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE`; the first credited hour is what turns a selected family into an active partner. If the household's `partnership_readiness` is `EDUCATION_COMPLETE` and the requirement has just been satisfied, readiness advances `EDUCATION_COMPLETE → CLOSING_READY`, and that in turn drives every `APPROVED` mortgage application of the household to `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE`. Each row touched along the way — entry, member, household, and any application — has its `version` incremented by 1.

Reversal exists because verified hours are sometimes discovered to be wrong after they were posted, and the affiliate's ledger is append-only rather than editable. A coordinator reverses a `CREDITED` entry with an optional `reversal_reason`. The original entry must have non-zero credited hours, must not itself be a mirror — an entry whose `reversal_of_entry_id` is set may never be reversed — and the household must not already be `CLOSED`; all three of those refusals are `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and all three are evaluated after the origin-state and posture gates. The reversal moves the original `CREDITED → REVERSED` and creates a **new** entry in `CREDITED` status whose `reversal_of_entry_id` points at the original, whose `hours_claimed` is 0, whose `credit_weight` equals the original's, and whose `hours_credited` is the exact negative of the original's. The mirror copies the original's `member_id`, `category`, and `work_date` verbatim, carries `capped_hours` of 0 and a null `shift_assignment_id` regardless of what the original held, and stamps *both* `claimed_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` with the reversing coordinator's identity — the segregation-of-duties rule governs claims and verifications, not the bookkeeping row a reversal writes. The mirror is created at `version` 1 and stays there for the rest of the request; the reversal's response body is the **original** entry, now reading `REVERSED`, and the mirror's `id` is discoverable only by listing the ledger with a `reversal_of_entry_id` filter. Because the reversed original stays in the ledger set at its positive figure and the mirror sits in the ledger set at the matching negative figure, the pair nets to exactly 0.00: the household's `sweat_equity_credited_hours`, its `family_member_credited_hours`, and the member's `credited_hours_to_date` all return to precisely their pre-credit values, with no drift and no allowance leakage. Formally, at all times the household's `sweat_equity_credited_hours` equals the two-decimal half-up sum of `hours_credited` over its entries in `CREDITED` or `REVERSED` status, and `family_member_credited_hours` equals the same sum restricted to entries whose member's `relationship` is neither `APPLICANT` nor `CO_APPLICANT`. A mirror row keeps its `CREDITED` status forever.

A reversal can also unwind readiness. Once `sweat_equity_satisfied` falls back to false because credited hours have dropped below the requirement, a household sitting at `CLOSING_READY` regresses `CLOSING_READY → EDUCATION_COMPLETE`, and every `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` mortgage of that household regresses to `APPROVED`. The `EDUCATION_COMPLETE ↔ CLOSING_READY` cycle is entirely independent of the selection dimension, which does not move during it.

Hours also arrive without anyone claiming them. When a shift is closed out, each partner-family seat that actually attended mints an entry on the family's behalf: its `hours_claimed` is forced to the shift's `duration_hours`, its `category` is forced to `CONSTRUCTION`, it opens directly in `VERIFIED` status rather than `CLAIMED` because the crew lead's attendance record *is* the verification, both its `claimed_by_user_id` and its `verified_by_user_id` are the shift's `crew_lead_user_id`, its `work_date` is forced to the shift's `start_at`, its `capped_hours` opens at 0 with `credit_weight` and `hours_credited` still null, and its `shift_assignment_id` records the seat it came from. The minted row is created at `version` 1 and is not returned anywhere in the close-out response, which carries only the shift; it is found by listing the ledger, optionally filtered by `shift_assignment_id`. Such an entry still has to be credited by a coordinator, and it passes through exactly the same waterfall as a manual claim — including the age and minor-category tests, which are applied against the shift's `start_at` as the work date. Minting is silent about failure: a seat whose member or household can no longer be resolved simply mints nothing and the close-out proceeds.

### 3.5. Homebuyer Education and the Road to Closing Readiness

The readiness dimension of a household starts at `NOT_READY` and exists to answer one question: may this family close? Its non-terminal values are `NOT_READY`, `EDUCATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `EDUCATION_COMPLETE`, and `CLOSING_READY`, and its single terminal value is `WITHDRAWN`.

A `FamilyServicesCoordinator` enrolls a selected household in a curriculum by naming the `household_id`, the `curriculum_code` — one of `HOMEBUYER_CORE`, `BUDGET_COACHING`, or `HOME_MAINTENANCE` — and the `required_module_count` of modules that must be passed, from 1 through 12. Enrollment opens in `ENROLLED` status with `passed_module_count` and `missed_session_count` at 0 and `completed_on` and `expires_on` null. The household must be `SELECTED` or `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE`, or the enrollment is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_SELECTED`, and a household may hold only one enrollment per curriculum in `ENROLLED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `COMPLETED` status — a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. Only `HOMEBUYER_CORE` gates closing; the other two curricula are recorded for the affiliate's own programme reporting and gate nothing. Enrolling a household in `HOMEBUYER_CORE` while its readiness is `NOT_READY` moves readiness `NOT_READY → EDUCATION_IN_PROGRESS`.

Module outcomes are recorded one at a time with an `outcome` of either `PASSED` or `MISSED` and the `module_code` of the session, which is mandatory and runs 1 to 40 characters. The first accepted record moves the enrollment `ENROLLED → IN_PROGRESS`, whichever outcome it carried. A `PASSED` outcome raises `passed_module_count`; only distinct modules count toward it, and the set of codes already passed is bookkeeping the service keeps to itself — it is never echoed on the enrollment, so `passed_module_count` is the only visible evidence of it. Re-recording a code that has already passed is accepted, leaves the count where it stood, and still increments the enrollment's `version`. A `MISSED` outcome raises `missed_session_count`, and the same session code may be missed repeatedly, each time counting again. The record that brings `passed_module_count` up to `required_module_count` — that is, the record arriving when exactly one module remained and no more than two sessions had been missed — completes the enrollment: `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, with `completed_on` set to the logical clock instant and `expires_on` set to 730 days after it. Homebuyer education is current for two years and no longer. The record that brings `missed_session_count` to 3 — the third miss, arriving when two had already been recorded — fails the enrollment: `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED`.

Completion of the core curriculum cascades. The household's readiness moves `EDUCATION_IN_PROGRESS → EDUCATION_COMPLETE`, and if `sweat_equity_satisfied` is already true at that instant the household continues in the same request to `CLOSING_READY`, which in turn drives every `APPROVED` mortgage of that household to `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE`. Failure of the core curriculum cascades the other way: readiness falls `EDUCATION_IN_PROGRESS → NOT_READY`, and any `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` mortgage of that household regresses to `APPROVED`. Both cascades are conditioned on readiness standing exactly at `EDUCATION_IN_PROGRESS` when the outcome lands: a core enrollment that completes or fails while the household's readiness sits anywhere else — because a second core enrollment already carried it forward, or because a denial withdrew it — moves the enrollment row alone and leaves readiness and every mortgage untouched. Reaching `CLOSING_READY` is thus the meeting point of the two partnership obligations — the hour ledger and the classroom — and it requires both: readiness may only advance `EDUCATION_COMPLETE → CLOSING_READY` when `sweat_equity_satisfied` is true and the household's `HOMEBUYER_CORE` enrollment is `COMPLETED` with an `expires_on` strictly later than the logical clock's present instant. Where a household has accumulated more than one core enrollment — which the duplicate rule permits once an earlier one has failed or expired — exactly one of them governs that test and every other test that speaks of "the household's core enrollment": the most recently created among those still in `ENROLLED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `COMPLETED` status, or, when none of them is in one of those three, the most recently created of all of them. Creation order is the tie-break, never the row `id`.

Currency lapses are enforced by the clock rather than by a background job. When an administrator advances the logical clock to or past a completed `HOMEBUYER_CORE` enrollment's `expires_on` — equality expires — the enrollment moves `COMPLETED → EXPIRED`, and the same request walks two more hops: the household's readiness falls from `CLOSING_READY` or from `EDUCATION_COMPLETE` to `NOT_READY`, and every `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` mortgage of that household regresses to `APPROVED`. `FAILED` and `EXPIRED` are the enrollment dimension's terminal values. A closing attempted while the household has no completed core enrollment is refused with `ERR_EDUCATION_NOT_COMPLETE`; one attempted while that enrollment has expired — either already moved to `EXPIRED`, or `COMPLETED` with an `expires_on` no later than the clock — is refused with `ERR_EDUCATION_EXPIRED`.

### 3.6. Land, Lot Readiness, Site Safety, and Construction Cost Capture

Land acquisition is recorded as a build site under a program: the `parcel_number` as the county assigns it (1 to 40 characters, and unique within the program — a repeat within the same program is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, while the same parcel number under a different program is accepted), the `street_address` (1 to 200 characters), the `acquisition_cost_cents` the affiliate paid for the lot (zero or more, since donated land is recorded at nil), and the `appraised_value_cents` the home is expected to be worth as completed, which must be at least 1,000,000 cents. A new site opens in `RAW_LAND` readiness with a `CLEAR` safety posture, with `title_cleared`, `environmental_cleared`, and `utilities_cleared` all false, with `assigned_household_id` and `pre_hold_readiness_status` null, and with both cost bases — `cash_cost_cents` and `total_development_cost_cents` — seeded at the acquisition price, since land is part of the cash basis from the first day even though it is not a ledger line.

Readiness advances one documented step at a time, and every step carries a `document_reference`; a step requested without its evidence is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, and because the field is optional as far as payload shape is concerned that refusal lands only once the step's own origin state has been confirmed. A `step` of `TITLE` moves the site `RAW_LAND → TITLE_CLEAR` and sets `title_cleared`, and is only available while `title_cleared` is still false; a repeat is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A `step` of `ENVIRONMENTAL` moves it `TITLE_CLEAR → ENV_CLEARED` and sets `environmental_cleared`, and adds no precondition of its own beyond that origin. A `step` of `UTILITIES` moves it `ENV_CLEARED → READY_TO_BUILD` and sets `utilities_cleared`, and requires that both `title_cleared` and `environmental_cleared` are already true, failing with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` otherwise. The site then leaves administrative hands: `READY_TO_BUILD → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` is not an operator action at all but a consequence of the first shift on that site locking its roster. The final `step` of `FINAL_INSPECTION` closes the build, moving the site `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION → COMPLETED`; it requires that no shift of the site is still in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `LOCKED` status and that `assigned_household_id` is set — each of those two, again, `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, the open-shift test evaluated first — and it recomputes both cost bases. `COMPLETED` is the readiness dimension's terminal value. Neither readiness advance nor the safety posture is guarded by program posture: land can be documented forward and an incident can be recorded against a site even while the program is suspended.

Safety is the `ConstructionManager`'s override and the most destructive cascade in the system. Placing a hold — a `posture` of `HOLD`, with an optional `incident_note` — is available from `READY_TO_BUILD` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` while `safety_posture` is `CLEAR`. The transition captures the outgoing readiness value into `pre_hold_readiness_status`, moves `readiness_status` to `SAFETY_HOLD`, and sets `safety_posture` to `HOLD`. It then cascades three hops: every shift of that site in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `LOCKED` status is cancelled; every assignment of each of those shifts in `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN` status is cancelled; and each cancelled assignment lowers its volunteer's `assigned_hours_committed` by that shift's `duration_hours`. No strikes are recorded — a safety hold is the affiliate's fault, not the volunteer's. The `version` of the site and of every cascaded shift, assignment, and volunteer profile increments by 1.

Releasing the hold with a `posture` of `CLEAR` restores `readiness_status` from `pre_hold_readiness_status` — `SAFETY_HOLD → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` when the hold was placed on a site already under construction, `SAFETY_HOLD → READY_TO_BUILD` when it was placed on a site that had not yet started, and `READY_TO_BUILD` as the fallback should the captured value somehow be null — then clears `pre_hold_readiness_status` to null and sets `safety_posture` back to `CLEAR`. Cancelled shifts are **not** restored; the crew must schedule new ones. The `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION ↔ SAFETY_HOLD` cycle may repeat as often as incidents require. While a site is on hold, its `safety_posture` of `HOLD` blocks six construction operations — scheduling a shift, opening one, locking one, reserving a seat, confirming a seat, and checking a seat in — each with `ERR_SITE_UNDER_SAFETY_HOLD`.

Construction cost capture is an append-only ledger owned jointly by the `ConstructionManager` and the `ProgramAdmin`. Each line names its `build_site_id` and a `category` of `SITE_WORK`, `MATERIALS`, `LABOR`, `PERMITS`, or `OTHER`, and declares through `is_in_kind` — false by default — whether it is cash out the door or a donation. A cash line must carry a positive `amount_cents` and a `fair_market_value_cents` of 0; an in-kind line must carry an `amount_cents` of 0, a positive `fair_market_value_cents` documenting the donation, and a `donor_name` of record (1 to 120 characters). Any other combination is refused with `ERR_IN_KIND_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, a test that runs after the program-posture check. Both money fields are optional and default to 0, and `donor_name` and `is_in_kind` are optional too, so an otherwise empty cash line fails the coherence test rather than the payload one. A line may additionally carry a `grant_award_code` of 1 to 40 characters, copied from the `award_code` of the restricted funding source the cost is charged against; the service records the string as given and never resolves it against a grant award, so a code naming no award is accepted. Lines open in `RECORDED` status, and the only mutation available is voiding: an optional `void_reason` moves the line `RECORDED → VOIDED`, and the row itself is never removed. Voiding an already-`VOIDED` line is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — a cost line has no terminal value for this purpose — and, having no `version` of its own, the voided line is the one mutated row in the system whose own record shows no sign of the write; only the site's and possibly the program's `version` move.

Both cost bases are recomputed on every append and every void, and again at final inspection. The **cash basis** is the site's `acquisition_cost_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` over that site's `RECORDED` lines that are not in-kind. Donated value is the sum of `fair_market_value_cents` over that site's `RECORDED` in-kind lines, and the **full fair-market basis** — stored as `total_development_cost_cents` — is the cash basis plus that donated value. Voided lines leave both bases entirely. At the program level, `donated_material_fmv_cents` is the donated value summed across every site of the program, so appending or voiding an in-kind line bumps both the site's `version` and the program's. Because the two bases are computed from the same set of lines, the full fair-market basis is strictly larger than the cash basis whenever any recorded in-kind line exists — which is exactly why grant reporting has to declare which basis it is using: the cash basis makes the affiliate look cheaper per home by rendering donated materials invisible.

### 3.7. Volunteer Scheduling, Roster Readiness, and Attendance

Community volunteers are registered by a `ConstructionManager` or a `ProgramAdmin` against a `program_id` with a `full_name`, a `birth_date`, a `waiver_expires_on` instant recording when the liability waiver lapses, and an optional `trade_certifications` array that defaults to empty. Each certification entry is an object naming a `trade` of `ELECTRICAL`, `PLUMBING`, `HVAC`, or `ROOFING` and an `expires_on` instant; an entry that is not an object, names a trade outside those four, or carries an `expires_on` that is absent or unparseable is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Accepted entries are stored normalised to exactly those two keys, so any further key a client sends inside a certification is discarded and never echoed back. A new profile opens `ACTIVE` with `no_show_strikes` at 0 and `assigned_hours_committed` at 0.00, and its `is_minor` flag is derived — never supplied — as whether the volunteer's age against the logical clock's present instant is under 18. `is_minor` is re-derived every time the clock advances, so a volunteer who turns 18 between two clock moves stops being a minor without anyone editing the profile.

A shift is scheduled on a site with a `crew_lead_user_id` naming the `ConstructionManager` accountable for it, a `start_at` and an `end_at` where the end is strictly after the start and the span is at most 10 hours — either failure is a payload-shape refusal with `ERR_VALIDATION`, evaluated before the program-posture, safety, and slot-declaration gates — a mandatory `volunteer_slots` count of general capacity from 0 through 40, an optional `required_trade_slots` count of skilled positions from 0 through 10 that defaults to 0, and an optional `required_trade` naming the certification a skilled seat must carry. `duration_hours` is derived as the elapsed seconds between `start_at` and `end_at` divided by 3600, rounded half-up to two decimal places. A shift opens in `SCHEDULED` status with `safety_briefing_completed` false, `confirmed_general_count` and `confirmed_trade_count` at 0, and `fill_ratio` at 0. Two declaration rules apply at scheduling and again at opening: the shift must offer at least one seat in total across general and trade capacity, and `required_trade_slots` may not exceed `volunteer_slots` — either failure is `ERR_SHIFT_HAS_NO_SLOTS`. And the trade declaration must be coherent: one or more trade slots with no `required_trade` named, or a `required_trade` named alongside zero trade slots, is `ERR_REQUIRED_TRADE_MISSING`.

A build site is occupied exclusively. Each shift holds its site for its own window extended by a 60-minute cleanup buffer on each end, and the interval is closed on both sides. A candidate window collides with an existing shift when the candidate's start is strictly before that shift's end plus 60 minutes *and* that shift's start is strictly before the candidate's end plus 60 minutes; a collision is refused with `ERR_SITE_SHIFT_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Because both comparisons are strict, a candidate beginning exactly 60 minutes after an existing shift ends is accepted, while one beginning 59 minutes after it is rejected. Only shifts in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, `LOCKED`, or `COMPLETED` status occupy the site; a cancelled shift releases its window immediately and never collides. Exclusivity holds regardless of seat counts — two shifts may not share a site window even if both are half empty.

Opening a shift moves it `SCHEDULED → OPEN` and makes it visible for sign-ups. The site must be in `READY_TO_BUILD` or `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` readiness, or the request is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY_TO_BUILD`; the site's safety posture must be `CLEAR`; the shift must offer at least one seat; and `start_at` must still be strictly in the future against the logical clock, a shift that has already started being refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The same action also reopens a locked shift — `LOCKED → OPEN` — but only before the shift begins and only while no seat has been checked in; a seat already in `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED` status refuses the reopening with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and reopening otherwise resets `safety_briefing_completed` to false. The `OPEN ↔ LOCKED` cycle exists to let a crew lead add capacity after locking too early.

Locking the roster moves the shift `OPEN → LOCKED` and carries a `safety_briefing_reference`, the documentary evidence that the briefing happened; a lock without it is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. That reference is a request field only: it is checked for presence and then discarded, never stored on the shift and never echoed in any shift response. The shift's briefing state is carried by the boolean `safety_briefing_completed` alone. Three readiness gates apply. `confirmed_trade_count` must be at least `required_trade_slots`, or the lock is refused with `ERR_TRADE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` — a shift that needs an electrician does not proceed without one. `confirmed_general_count` must reach at least sixty percent of `volunteer_slots`, rounded **up** to a whole volunteer, or the lock is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_FILL_INSUFFICIENT`; on a five-slot shift the minimum is 3 confirmed general volunteers, so three confirmations lock and two do not. The site's safety posture must be `CLEAR`, and `start_at` must still be strictly in the future — a lock attempted at or after the start instant is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and that test comes last of the four, after the coverage and fill gates. Locking sets `safety_briefing_completed` to true, releases every seat still in `RESERVED` status by cancelling it and lowering that volunteer's `assigned_hours_committed` by the shift's `duration_hours`, and cascades the site `READY_TO_BUILD → UNDER_CONSTRUCTION` when the site had not yet started. Confirmed counts and `fill_ratio` are maintained continuously: `confirmed_general_count` and `confirmed_trade_count` count that shift's seats in `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED` status with the matching `role`, excluding `RESERVED`, `CANCELLED`, and `NO_SHOW` seats entirely, and `fill_ratio` is `confirmed_general_count` divided by `volunteer_slots` rounded half-up to four decimal places, or exactly 0 when the shift offers no general slots at all.

Seats are reserved against a shift with the shift's identifier, the `expected_shift_version` of §2.7, a `role` of `GENERAL_VOLUNTEER`, `SKILLED_TRADE`, or `HOMEOWNER_PARTNER`, and then either a `volunteer_id` for a community seat or a `household_member_id` for a partner-family seat. Those two fields are mutually exclusive and each is mandatory for its own kind of seat: a `HOMEOWNER_PARTNER` seat must name a `household_member_id` and must not name a `volunteer_id`, a `GENERAL_VOLUNTEER` or `SKILLED_TRADE` seat must name a `volunteer_id` and must not name a `household_member_id`, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — this coherence test is what runs ahead of the ownership check, as §2.8 notes. The parent shift must be standing in `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN`, a shift in any other non-terminal status being refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A seat opens in `RESERVED` status with `checked_in_at` null, and a community seat immediately raises the volunteer's `assigned_hours_committed` by the shift's `duration_hours`. Community seats face a battery of checks. A profile whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` may hold no new reservation and is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED`. A profile whose `waiver_expires_on` is not strictly later than the shift's `start_at` is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_EXPIRED`. A `SKILLED_TRADE` seat requires the volunteer to hold a certification whose `trade` matches the shift's `required_trade` and whose `expires_on` is strictly later than the shift's `end_at`, or it is refused with `ERR_TRADE_CERTIFICATION_INVALID`.

Volunteer calendars use the opposite interval geometry from sites: a volunteer occupies a shift's window as a half-open interval from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`, with **no** buffer, so back-to-back shifts that merely touch at an endpoint are explicitly allowed. A candidate overlaps a held seat when the candidate shift's start is strictly before the held shift's end and the held shift's start is strictly before the candidate shift's end; the volunteer's held seats for this purpose are those in `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED` status, and an overlap is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Aggregate capacity is measured over a calendar-anchored week, not a floating window: the week runs from the Monday at 00:00:00.000Z at or before the candidate shift's `start_at` through the same instant seven days later, the later bound excluded. The volunteer's hours already held inside that week are summed from the `duration_hours` of the shifts of their held seats whose `start_at` falls in the window, the candidate's own duration is added, the total is rounded half-up to two decimal places, and it may not exceed 40.00 hours for an adult or 16.00 hours for a minor — landing exactly on the ceiling is accepted, one hundredth of an hour past it is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_WEEKLY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Cancelled and no-show seats neither block a window nor consume weekly hours. Partner-family seats, which carry no `volunteer_id`, are exempt from both the overlap test and the weekly ceiling.

Seat inventory is the last gate. General seats may not exceed `volunteer_slots` and skilled-trade seats may not exceed `required_trade_slots` plus two — the affiliate deliberately over-books trades by two because a plumber who cancels on the morning of a rough-in stops the build — counting seats in `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED` status for the requested role; exceeding either allowance is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_ROLE_FULL`. Partner-family seats are unlimited in number but must name a `household_member_id` whose household is the `assigned_household_id` of the shift's build site, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_MEMBER_NOT_ON_SITE`; the named member must additionally still be `ACTIVE`, a removed member being refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` immediately after the on-site test.

Confirmation moves a seat `RESERVED → CONFIRMED` and is what makes it count toward trade coverage and `fill_ratio`; it may be performed by the volunteer, by the family for a partner seat, or by the crew's manager. The parent shift must be `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` — any other status, including a shift that has since been locked, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — the site's posture `CLEAR`, and for a community seat the profile must be `ACTIVE` with an unexpired waiver against the shift's `start_at` and, for a skilled seat, a certification valid past the shift's `end_at`. A profile that is not `ACTIVE` at confirmation time, or that can no longer be resolved at all, is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED` — the same identifier a suspended profile draws at reservation. Confirmation increments the matching confirmed count on the shift, recomputes `fill_ratio`, and bumps both the assignment's and the shift's `version`.

Check-in is the `ConstructionManager`'s exclusive act — a volunteer cannot check itself in, which is what makes the strike ladder trustworthy — and moves a seat `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN`, stamping `checked_in_at` with the logical clock instant. The parent shift must be `LOCKED` with `safety_briefing_completed` true, either failing being refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`; the site's posture must be `CLEAR`; and the clock must sit at or after 30 minutes before `start_at` and strictly before `end_at`; outside that window the check-in is refused with `ERR_CHECK_IN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A successful check-in bumps both the seat's and the parent shift's `version`, even though no visible count on the shift moves — `CHECKED_IN` already counted as confirmed.

Closing out a shift moves it `LOCKED → COMPLETED` with an optional `completion_note`, and requires the logical clock to have reached `end_at` — equality completes, and anything earlier is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ENDED`. Completion forks, and both branches run. Every seat in `CHECKED_IN` status becomes `COMPLETED`, and each such seat whose `role` is `HOMEOWNER_PARTNER` mints the shift-synthesized sweat equity entry described in §3.4. Every seat still in `CONFIRMED` status — confirmed but never checked in — becomes `NO_SHOW` and raises its volunteer's `no_show_strikes` by 1; a profile reaching 3 strikes flips to `SUSPENDED`, and that suspension cancels every one of that volunteer's `RESERVED` seats on shifts whose `start_at` is still in the future. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, and `NO_SHOW` are the assignment dimension's terminal values.

Cancelling a shift outright — from `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `LOCKED`, with a `cancellation_reason` — cancels every one of its seats in `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN` status, lowering each affected volunteer's `assigned_hours_committed` by the shift's `duration_hours`, and records no strike against anybody. A volunteer or the manager may also release a single seat with a `cancellation_reason` from `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`; if the seat was `CONFIRMED`, the shift's matching confirmed count is decremented and `fill_ratio` recomputed, and for a community seat the volunteer's `assigned_hours_committed` falls by the shift's `duration_hours`. The `cancellation_reason` of either cancellation is likewise a request field only — validated and then discarded, never stored on the shift or on the assignment and never echoed in their responses. Committed volunteer hours are a conserved quantity: at all times a profile's `assigned_hours_committed` equals the two-decimal half-up sum of `duration_hours` over that volunteer's seats in `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED` status, so a reservation followed by any of its compensating exits — an explicit release, a lock-time release of an unconfirmed seat, a shift cancellation, a safety-hold cascade, or a suspension cascade — restores the figure to exactly its pre-reservation value. A no-show, recorded when the shift closes out, releases the hours the same way — `NO_SHOW` is not one of the four held statuses, so the seat leaves `assigned_hours_committed` exactly as a cancellation would — but unlike every compensating exit above it also leaves a permanent strike behind. The formula governs in every case: whatever moves a seat into or out of the four held statuses, the profile's `assigned_hours_committed` is always that two-decimal half-up sum and nothing else.

### 3.8. Mortgage Origination, Affordability Structuring, and Closing

A `MortgageOfficer` originates an application against a household and the build site that will be its home, supplying the `household_id`, the `build_site_id`, the `expected_household_version` of §2.7, a mandatory `term_months` of exactly 240, 300, or 360 — any other integer is a payload-shape failure — optionally an `escrow_monthly_cents` estimate, which is stored exactly as supplied and left null when it is omitted, and optionally a `closing_cost_credit_cents` figure recording the affiliate-funded closing cost credit, which defaults to 0 and may not exceed 500,000 cents. The requested term and escrow estimate are the officer's working assumptions only: the structuring pass overwrites both from the pinned snapshots, so neither survives underwriting unchanged. Creation stamps `originating_officer_user_id` from the acting identity, opens the file with `status` of `DRAFT` and `lien_status` of `NONE`, pins `appraised_value_cents` from the build site and `income_basis_annual_cents` from the household's `verified_annual_income_cents`, and leaves the computed structure — `affordable_pi_payment_cents`, `first_mortgage_principal_cents`, `silent_second_amount_cents` — null. `superseded_application_id`, `lien_recorded_on`, `right_of_first_refusal_expires_on`, and `denial_reason_code` are null; `forgiven_amount_cents`, `lien_outstanding_cents`, and `forbearance_counseling_sessions` are 0; `resale_restriction_months` carries its default of 240 months.

Three referential conditions guard origination. The household's `selection_status` must be `SELECTED` or `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE`, or the create is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_SELECTED`. The named build site must be either unassigned or already assigned to this same household; a site whose `assigned_household_id` names a different household is refused with `ERR_SITE_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. And the acting officer must not have authored a submitted selection review on this household, or the create is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful create writes `assigned_household_id` onto the site when the site was unassigned. It also supersedes history cleanly: *every* earlier application of the same household still sitting in `DRAFT` transitions `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, and the new application's `superseded_application_id` records the identifier of the last of them in creation order, so a single field names one predecessor even where several were retired at once. `CLOSED`, `DENIED`, and `SUPERSEDED` are the origination dimension's terminal values.

Submitting a draft to underwriting moves it `DRAFT → UNDERWRITING` and is where the structure is computed. The household must still be `SELECTED` or `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE` with `documentation_status` of `VERIFIED` and a non-null `verified_annual_income_cents` — all three of those refusals are `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_SELECTED`, tested in that order; the build site must be in `READY_TO_BUILD`, `UNDER_CONSTRUCTION`, or `COMPLETED` readiness, or the submission is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY_TO_BUILD`; and the segregation-of-duties test against submitted selection reviews applies here too, ahead of all four. Submission does not re-check whether the site has since been assigned to somebody else — that question is settled once, at origination. Submission re-pins `appraised_value_cents` and `income_basis_annual_cents` from the site's and household's current values, then runs the affordability structure.

That structure is a **single forward pass in a pinned order**, and the order is normative because the dependency looks circular — the term is chosen from a provisional principal while the final principal is a payment multiplied by the term. There is no iteration and no re-selection of the term after it is chosen. Step one: monthly gross income is the pinned `income_basis_annual_cents` divided by 12 and floored to whole cents. Step two: escrow is assembled from two separately rounded components. The monthly tax component is the pinned `appraised_value_cents` multiplied by an annual tax rate of 1.25 percent and divided by 12, rounded half-up to whole cents. The annual hazard insurance premium is the pinned appraised value multiplied by 0.35 percent, rounded half-up to whole cents, and then raised to a floor of 60,000 cents — every home carries at least a six-hundred-dollar annual premium; the monthly insurance component is that annual figure divided by 12, rounded half-up. `escrow_monthly_cents` is the sum of the monthly tax component and the monthly insurance component. Step three: the maximum total housing payment the household can carry is 30 percent of monthly gross income, floored to whole cents. Step four: `affordable_pi_payment_cents` is that maximum less `escrow_monthly_cents`; if the result is zero or negative the household has no structure at all and the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_AFFORDABLE_STRUCTURE`. That refusal is not clean: the re-pinning of `appraised_value_cents` and `income_basis_annual_cents` has already happened by the time the arithmetic is judged, so those two snapshots persist at their new values on a file that stays in `DRAFT` and whose `version` does not move. Because the note carries no interest, this figure is pure principal.

Step five: a provisional principal is formed as `affordable_pi_payment_cents` multiplied by 360, and it is used for nothing except picking the term tier. Step six: `term_months` is 240 when that provisional principal reaches at least 90 percent of the pinned appraised value (that percentage rounded half-up to whole cents), otherwise 300 when it reaches at least 70 percent of the appraised value on the same rounding, otherwise 360. A shorter term is chosen for the stronger household deliberately, so subsidy is not parked in a long note behind a family that can nearly self-fund the home. Step seven: `first_mortgage_principal_cents` is the lesser of `affordable_pi_payment_cents` multiplied by the chosen `term_months` and the appraised value — the term is *not* re-derived from this figure. Step eight: `silent_second_amount_cents` is the appraised value less the first mortgage principal, and if that difference is negative it is recorded as 0. The silent second is the subsidy the affiliate is being asked to commit.

Only the pinned snapshots feed that computation. Live edits to the household's verified income or to the site's appraisal after the snapshot never move an underwritten structure; the only way to re-price is to return the file to draft and submit it again, which re-pins from current values. Returning `UNDERWRITING → DRAFT` with a `return_reason` clears `appraised_value_cents`, `income_basis_annual_cents`, `escrow_monthly_cents`, `affordable_pi_payment_cents`, `term_months`, `first_mortgage_principal_cents`, and `silent_second_amount_cents` all back to null, unpinning everything. The `DRAFT ↔ UNDERWRITING` revision cycle may repeat, and a re-appraisal of the site between loops genuinely changes the structure. The same return action is also how a clearance is unwound by hand — `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE → APPROVED` — and on that path the computed structure is retained rather than cleared. For officers who want to see the arithmetic before committing to it, a read-only preview returns what the pinned snapshots would produce step by step — `monthly_gross_cents`, `tax_monthly_cents`, `insurance_annual_cents`, `insurance_monthly_cents`, `escrow_monthly_cents`, `max_total_payment_cents`, `affordable_pi_payment_cents`, `provisional_principal`, `term_months`, `first_mortgage_principal_cents`, and `silent_second_amount_cents` — without writing a single field. Those eleven figures are the whole of the preview's `data` object; it wraps them in no envelope of its own and echoes no identifier of the application. The preview is available against an application in any `status` and any `lien_status`, terminal ones included, since it writes nothing; and it reads a null snapshot as zero rather than refusing, so a draft that has never been submitted previews an appraised value of 0 and, in consequence, a silent second of 0.

Approval moves the application `UNDERWRITING → APPROVED` and is the moment subsidy is committed. It carries `expected_program_version` and the `resale_restriction_months` the affordability period will run for, which must be 120, 240, or 360 months. The program's posture must not be `FROZEN`; the acting officer must not have authored a submitted review on the household; and the program's `subsidy_available_cents` must be at least the application's `silent_second_amount_cents`, where equality approves — an approval that consumes the pool down to exactly zero succeeds and drives `program_posture` to `SUBSIDY_RESTRICTED`. A silent second strictly larger than the available balance is refused with `ERR_SUBSIDY_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. On success the program's `subsidy_committed_cents` rises by the silent second, `subsidy_available_cents` and `program_posture` are recomputed, and the program's `version` increments. If the household is already `CLOSING_READY`, the application continues immediately to `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` inside the same request; otherwise it waits in `APPROVED` for the readiness dimension to catch up, as §3.4 and §3.5 describe. The `APPROVED ↔ CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` cycle is driven entirely by the household's readiness dimension, and a regression out of `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE` back to `APPROVED` deliberately does **not** release the subsidy commitment — the commitment survives the regression.

Denial is available from `DRAFT`, `UNDERWRITING`, or `APPROVED` and carries a `denial_reason_code` of `AFFORDABILITY`, `SUBSIDY_CAPACITY`, `READINESS`, or `DOCUMENTATION`. Any denial clears the build site's `assigned_household_id` back to null when it named this household, bumping the site's `version`, and leaves the site's recorded cost lines exactly where they are — the cost ledger is append-only and a failed placement does not erase spending. A denial from `APPROVED` additionally performs the compensating fiscal action: `subsidy_committed_cents` falls by the silent second, restoring `subsidy_available_cents` to precisely its pre-approval value, `program_posture` is re-derived, and the program's `version` increments.

Closing moves the application `CLEARED_TO_CLOSE → CLOSED` and carries an optional `closing_date` of record for the settlement. That field is a request field only: it is parsed for shape and then discarded, and both the application's `lien_recorded_on` and the household's own `closing_date` are stamped from the logical clock instead, so a client-supplied settlement date never reaches storage and never appears in any response. Four conditions must hold, and they are tested in this order: the household's `HOMEBUYER_CORE` enrollment must be `COMPLETED` — nothing at all, or a status other than `COMPLETED` or `EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_EDUCATION_NOT_COMPLETE` — and its `expires_on` must be strictly later than the logical clock, an already-`EXPIRED` row or a lapsed window being refused with `ERR_EDUCATION_EXPIRED`; the build site's `readiness_status` must be `COMPLETED`, or the close is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_COMPLETED`; the household's `sweat_equity_satisfied` must be true, or the close is refused with `ERR_SWEAT_EQUITY_INCOMPLETE`; and the household's readiness must be `CLOSING_READY`, anything else being refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Closing does four things at once. The lien dimension moves `NONE → ACTIVE` — which requires a `silent_second_amount_cents` strictly above zero, a nil or absent silent second being the last refusal of the ladder with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — with `lien_recorded_on` stamped at the logical clock instant, `lien_outstanding_cents` set to the full silent second, and `forgiven_amount_cents` at 0. The program's `subsidy_committed_cents` falls by the silent second while `subsidy_disbursed_cents` rises by the same figure, so the pool identity is preserved with the capital simply moving buckets. The household transitions `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE → CLOSED`, its own `closing_date` field is stamped with the logical clock instant, and the program's `households_served_count` rises by 1. The `version` of the application, the household, the site, and the program each increment by 1.

### 3.9. Life of the Silent Second, and Reporting Back to Funders

After closing, the silent second lives on its own dimension whose values are `NONE`, `ACTIVE`, `FORBEARANCE`, `TRANSFER_PENDING`, and the two terminal outcomes `SATISFIED` and `RECAPTURED`. Every action on it carries an `action` and a mandatory `effective_date`, and — depending on the action — a `sale_price_cents` or a `counseling_session_reference`, both of which are optional as far as payload shape goes. Every action also requires the application's own `status` to be `CLOSED`: a lien action against a file that has not closed is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and that test runs after the program-posture check but ahead of the lien dimension's own origin and terminal gates. Five actions exist, and the service accepts either the formal or the operational spelling of each: `FORBEARANCE_GRANT` (also written `FORBEARANCE`), `REINSTATE` (also `CURE`), `TRANSFER_REQUEST` (also `TRANSFER_NOTICE`), `RECORD_TRANSFER` (also `RECAPTURE`), and `SATISFY` (also `PAYOFF`).

Forgiveness accrues on the logical clock, straight-line and slowly. Elapsed months are the whole number of 30-day months between `lien_recorded_on` and the clock's present instant, discarding the fraction. Nothing is forgiven before the 60th month; from the 60th month onward, `forgiven_amount_cents` is the silent second multiplied by the number of months beyond the 60th and divided by 180, rounded half-up to whole cents; from the 240th month onward the whole silent second is forgiven. `lien_outstanding_cents` is the silent second less the forgiven amount, floored at zero. These two figures are recomputed on every lien action and whenever the clock advances for any closed application whose lien is `ACTIVE`, `FORBEARANCE`, or `TRANSFER_PENDING`.

Foreclosure prevention runs through the forbearance cycle. A grant is available only on an `ACTIVE` lien of a `CLOSED` application, requires its `counseling_session_reference` as evidence that counseling actually happened — an omitted or empty reference is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, checked before the session count — moves the lien `ACTIVE → FORBEARANCE`, raises `forbearance_counseling_sessions` by 1, and recomputes the forgiveness figures. At most two grants are ever available: a grant requested when `forbearance_counseling_sessions` has already reached its limit is refused with `ERR_FORBEARANCE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Reinstatement moves the lien `FORBEARANCE → ACTIVE`, and the `ACTIVE ↔ FORBEARANCE` cycle can therefore run at most twice.

A resale inside the affordability period triggers the affiliate's right of first refusal. A transfer request is available on an `ACTIVE` lien, requires a `sale_price_cents` strictly greater than zero — a zero or missing figure is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` once the state gates have passed, and a negative one is a payload-shape failure with the same identifier — moves the lien `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING`, and sets `right_of_first_refusal_expires_on` to 30 days after the action's `effective_date` — operators record the notice with `effective_date` at the current logical instant, so the affiliate holds a 30-day window to buy the home back. The notice's sale price is retained internally for the recording that follows but is never echoed on the application. Requesting a transfer on a lien that is not `ACTIVE` is refused as any other illegal move on the lien dimension would be: `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` when the lien has already been satisfied or recaptured, and `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` otherwise. Recording the transfer moves the lien `TRANSFER_PENDING → RECAPTURED`, and it may reuse the price the notice recorded: `sale_price_cents` is optional on the recording, and when it is omitted the retained figure stands in. The recording may only happen once the window has lapsed: a recording attempted while the logical clock is still before `right_of_first_refusal_expires_on` is refused with `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_OPEN`. The recapture total the affiliate collects is `lien_outstanding_cents` plus a share of the home's appreciation, where appreciation is the `sale_price_cents` less the pinned `appraised_value_cents`, floored at zero, and the shared percentage steps down over time: 75 percent before the 60th month, 50 percent from the 60th through the 119th month, 25 percent from the 120th month up to but not including the `resale_restriction_months` mark, and nothing at all from that mark onward — a lien with no restriction figure of its own is read as 240 months for this purpose. The appreciation share is rounded half-up to whole cents before it is added. Both the recapture total and the appreciation share are internal working figures: neither is written to a visible field, so the amount collected is not readable from the application afterwards. Nor are `lien_outstanding_cents` and `forgiven_amount_cents` zeroed by the recording — they keep whatever the last accrual left them at, and stop moving because a `RECAPTURED` lien is no longer eligible for accrual. Fiscally, the program's `subsidy_disbursed_cents` falls by the outstanding balance and `subsidy_pool_cents` rises by the returned shared-appreciation portion, so recaptured capital flows back into `subsidy_available_cents` for the next family, and the program's `version` increments.

The gentler ending is satisfaction. Payoff is available on an `ACTIVE` lien and only once `lien_outstanding_cents` has been recomputed to exactly zero — that is, once full straight-line forgiveness has accrued at 240 elapsed months or more; a payoff requested while a balance remains is refused with `ERR_LIEN_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING`. It moves the lien `ACTIVE → SATISFIED` and lowers the program's `subsidy_disbursed_cents` by the silent second, the forgiven subsidy leaving the ledger as programme expense, and increments the program's `version` alongside the application's. Satisfaction and the recorded transfer are the only two lien actions that version the program at all: granting forbearance, reinstating, and lodging a transfer notice move no bucket and touch nothing but the application.

Funders are the last audience. A `ProgramAdmin` records an award under a program with its `award_code` (1 to 40 characters, and unique within the program — a repeat is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, while the same code under another program is accepted), the `donor_name` of the funder — stored as the award's `funder_name`, 1 to 120 characters, and the spelling `funder_name` is accepted on input as an alias for it — the `amount_cents` of the award, stored as `award_amount_cents` and required to be zero or greater, with `award_amount_cents` likewise accepted as an input alias, the inclusive reporting `period_start` at UTC midnight and the `period_end` which must be strictly after it or the award is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, the contractual `households_served_target` of 1 through 500, and an optional `restricted_to_in_kind_reporting` flag defaulting to false. Where both spellings of an aliased field arrive, the canonical one wins. An award opens in `OPEN` status.

The impact report may only be produced once the reporting period has closed: the logical clock must have reached `period_end`, and an earlier attempt — or a second attempt on an award already in `REPORTED` status — is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_OPEN`. The served set is every household of the award's program whose `selection_status` is `CLOSED` and whose `closing_date` falls inside the award period with **both** ends inclusive, the end bound extending through 23:59:59.999 of the `period_end` day. Households in `PARTNERSHIP_ACTIVE`, `SELECTED`, `WAITLISTED`, or `DENIED` are excluded even when their build is finished. From that set the report publishes `households_served` as the count, `persons_served` as the sum of `household_size`, and `sweat_equity_hours` as the sum of `sweat_equity_credited_hours` rounded half-up to two decimal places. It publishes the `basis` it valued homes on, spelled `FULL_FMV_BASIS` when the award's `restricted_to_in_kind_reporting` is true and `CASH_BASIS` otherwise. `cost_total_cents` is the sum, over the served households' closed applications, of that application's build site cost on the declared basis — `total_development_cost_cents` on the full fair-market basis, `cash_cost_cents` on the cash basis — and `subsidy_total_cents` is the sum of those applications' `silent_second_amount_cents`. `cost_per_household_cents` and `subsidy_per_household_cents` are those totals divided by `households_served`, each rounded half-up to whole cents, and `target_variance` is `households_served` less the award's `households_served_target`, so an award that fell two homes short reports a variance of negative two. The itemized `households` list is ordered by `closing_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, never by row identifier. When no household qualifies, every numeric field of the report is the integer 0 — never null, and never a division error — and the itemized list is empty. Alongside those figures the report identifies itself with `grant_award_id`, the award's `award_code`, and the `households_served_target` the variance was measured against; the whole thing is a single object in `data` and carries no pagination metadata even though it embeds a list. Accepting the report flips the award's `status` to `REPORTED` and increments its `version` by 1.
