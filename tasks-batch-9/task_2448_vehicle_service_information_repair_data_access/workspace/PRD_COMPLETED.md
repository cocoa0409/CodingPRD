# Vehicle Service Information Access API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Independent repair shops cannot fix modern vehicles without the manufacturer's own words. Repair procedures, technical service bulletins, immobilizer key routines, module flash files and advanced driver assistance system (ADAS) calibration documents all originate inside the OEM, and every one of them is licensed rather than given away. The platform specified here sits between the two sides of that transaction: it holds the manufacturer service-information licenses, resolves them into shop-level entitlements with either subscription or per-vehicle commercial terms, provisions the secure-gateway credentials a verified technician needs to talk to a vehicle's modules, and keeps the audit trail that makes such access defensible after the fact.

Two regulatory pressures shape almost every rule in this document. The first is **pricing parity** — a right-to-repair obligation that an OEM may not charge an independent shop materially more for the same data than it charges its own franchised dealers. The platform computes a parity verdict from the two published offers and refuses to activate a license whose OEM fails it. The second is **data availability** — the obligation to actually ship the procedure, which sometimes forces a document out of the door before the editorial team has finished reviewing it. That tension is why a publication version carries two independent status axes, and why the platform reports coverage completeness on two different bases: the strict one that only counts fully reviewed material, and the permissive one an OEM would quote to a regulator.

Around those two poles sit the operational mechanics: seat management across a many-to-many map of technicians and entitlements, diagnostic tool occupation windows, a data-credit ledger that must balance to the cent after every funding and every settlement, version pinning so that a module flash in flight is never silently re-based onto a recalled procedure, and a cascade discipline that unwinds seats, sessions and jobs in a defined order whenever a license is terminated or an identity is suspended. The remainder of this document specifies the global contract every endpoint honours (§2), the domain workflows and their arithmetic (§3), the endpoint surface (§4) and the error vocabulary (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Secure-Gateway Identity

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which the caller is acting. Exactly five roles exist, spelled as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `ContentPublisher`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ShopManager`, and `Technician`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds any string other than one of those five, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well-formed but the declared role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being addressed (see §4), the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Three of the five roles are **opaque operators**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it against any row's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `ContentPublisher` and when it is `ComplianceOfficer`. The platform does, however, *record* these identifiers where the workflow demands accountability: a `ComplianceOfficer` identifier is written to `verified_by_user_id` on the technician it verifies, a `ContentPublisher` identifier is written to `validated_by_user_id` when it validates a version and to `published_by_user_id` when it publishes one, and every audited action stores the acting identifier in `actor_user_id`.

The remaining two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `ShopManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Shop`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Shop` compare the resource's shop-identifying field to `X-User-ID`. A `ShopManager` may therefore address only its own shop, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_SHOP_ACCESS` (HTTP 403). That confinement is enforced on eleven operations: enrolling a technician, purchasing an entitlement, activating an entitlement, cancelling an entitlement, seating a technician, revoking a seat, registering a diagnostic tool, reading the shop record, listing the shop's technicians, listing the shop's diagnostic tools, and reading the shop's coverage map. For the four reads and for the three creates that carry an explicit `shop_id` in their payload (technician enrolment, entitlement purchase, diagnostic-tool registration), the compared value is that supplied identifier. For the seat and entitlement operations, the compared value is the `shop_id` recorded on the `ShopEntitlement` the request targets.

When `X-User-Role` is `Technician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks compare the seat holder recorded on the addressed seat to `X-User-ID`. A technician may act only through a seat assigned to itself, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER` (HTTP 403). This applies to seven operations: requesting a gateway session, activating one, closing one, queueing a programming job, starting a job, completing a job, and re-pinning a job. On session request the comparison uses the `technician_id` of the `SeatAssignment` named by the payload's `seat_assignment_id`; on every session action and on every job operation the platform walks from the job to its session, from the session to its seat, and compares that seat's `technician_id`.

One further identity rule cuts across the editorial roles. Publishing a version is refused when the acting `ContentPublisher` is the same operator who validated it: if the version's `validated_by_user_id` is not null and equals the publishing request's `X-User-ID`, the platform returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Moving a version through both validation and publication therefore requires two distinct `ContentPublisher` operator identifiers.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and it is the one timestamp in the system read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.9.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses report `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page. The default ordering for list endpoints is `created_at` ascending, with rows sharing a `created_at` — routine under the logical clock of §2.9, which never advances on its own — returned in the order they were inserted. Two lists override that default because their natural reading order is not insertion order: the versions of a single publication are returned in ascending `version_no`, and the audit trail is returned in ascending `occurred_at` with `created_at` ascending as the tie-breaker and insertion order as the final tie-breaker when both are equal, as they always are among the rows one request appends.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and Timestamps

Every resource the platform stores is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics — a shop identifier and a session identifier are indistinguishable by inspection, so the platform never infers a resource's kind from the shape of its identifier. Clients never supply an `id`; a payload attempting to do so is a malformed request.

Most resources also carry three bookkeeping properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was first written, read from the logical clock of §2.9 and rendered with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent mutation, from the same clock and in the same format, and equals `created_at` on a freshly created row. `version` is an integer revision counter that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. This applies to mutations the client requested directly *and* to mutations the row suffered as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource: when terminating a license lapses an entitlement, the entitlement's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed just as if the entitlement had been cancelled by its own shop. Instants a client supplies — the license effective bounds, the entitlement and session windows, a credential or attestation expiry — are stored and echoed back exactly as the client spelled them rather than re-rendered into the three-fractional-digit form.

Three record kinds deliberately omit the mutable half of that bookkeeping, because they are never mutated after creation. A vehicle model, a system area, and an audit event each carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and their absence from those payloads is part of the contract rather than an oversight. Vehicle models and system areas are immutable catalogue rows, and the audit trail is strictly append-only.

### 2.5. Stale-Revision Protection on Seat and Version Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the revision counter of their *parent* resource, because in both cases a second, strictly sequential request observing the same parent state would legitimately succeed and silently overwrite a picture the first client had already acted on.

Seating a technician on an entitlement requires a non-optional `expected_entitlement_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ShopEntitlement`. If that value does not equal the entitlement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENTITLEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard is necessary precisely because re-seating the same technician on the same entitlement is a legitimate operation that supersedes the prior seat, and because seating a different technician is legitimate whenever a seat remains free — so nothing in the state machine alone would stop a client from clobbering the seat map it based its decision on.

Minting a new version under a publication requires a non-optional `expected_publication_version` field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Publication`. A mismatch with the publication's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Technical service bulletin ingestion legitimately supersedes earlier material, so a second sequential ingest would happily mint the next version number and, once published, push the intervening one aside; only the stale-revision check protects an ingesting client from clobbering a version it never saw.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Any request that supplies `expected_entitlement_version` or `expected_publication_version` to an operation other than the two named above is a malformed request, and so is either field carrying a value below 1, since a revision counter starts at 1 and anything lower sits outside its declared bounds — such a value is refused on shape at rung 6 rather than as a stale revision at rung 9.

### 2.6. Reference Resolution and Path Traversal Order

Only one resource in this API is addressed through a nested path — the version collection beneath a publication. Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right: the publication named by the leading path segment must exist before the platform considers anything that follows it, and a publication that cannot be found yields `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) regardless of what the rest of the request contains. Every other relationship in the system is a peer reference carried in a payload field or resolved through a filtered list, never through a deeper URL.

Each entity type has its own not-found error, and the same error is used whether the missing identifier arrived in the path or in the request body: `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. Primary references are resolved in that order of entity precedence — manufacturer, shop, license, technician, entitlement, seat assignment, publication, publication version, session, job — so that when a single request names two missing resources the earlier entity in that sequence is the one reported.

A distinct error covers **secondary** references — identifiers that point at supporting catalogue rows rather than at the request's subject. Every element of `covered_system_area_ids` when a license is drafted, every such element again when that license is activated, the `system_area_id` and `vehicle_model_id` supplied when a publication is opened, the `manufacturer_id` axis check when a vehicle model is registered, the `diagnostic_tool_id` supplied when a session is requested, and the replacement version supplied on a re-pin that turns out to belong to a different publication: all of these resolve to `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) when they fail. This enumeration governs over the general path-or-body remark above wherever the two could be read against each other: registering a vehicle model whose `manufacturer_id` names no existing manufacturer answers `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND`, even though the identifier arrived in a request body — whereas a `manufacturer_id` carried in a license draft is not in this list, remains a primary reference, and still answers `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. The Order in Which Requests Are Refused

A single request can violate several rules at once, and the platform's error reporting is deterministic: checks are evaluated in a fixed priority ladder and the **first** failing check decides the response. Implementations must not report a later failure while an earlier one is outstanding. The ladder, from highest priority downward, is:

1. **Header authentication** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Shop scope confinement** for a `ShopManager` — `ERR_CROSS_SHOP_ACCESS`.
4. **Seat-holder confinement** for a `Technician` — `ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER`.
5. **Segregation of duties** on publication — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Primary reference existence**, in the entity precedence order of §2.6 — the ten not-found errors.
8. **Secondary reference existence** — `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
9. **Stale parent revision** — `ERR_STALE_ENTITLEMENT_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_PARENT_VERSION`.
10. **Declared state transition** — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, refined for one case into `ERR_JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`.
11. **Cross-entity state gates** — `ERR_PARITY_NON_COMPLIANT`, then `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_VERIFIED`, then `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`.
12. **Safety gates** — `ERR_SECURITY_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_TOOL_ATTESTATION_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_CALIBRATION_DOC_REQUIRED`.
13. **Referential staleness of a pinned procedure** — `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION`.
14. **Business constraints** — `ERR_TOOL_SHOP_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_NO_SEATS_AVAILABLE`.
15. **Temporal constraints** — `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_TOOL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_ENTITLEMENT`.
16. **Fiscal capacity** — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DATA_CREDIT`.

Two consequences of that ordering deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. First, the two scope confinements outrank request-shape validation and resource existence. A `ShopManager` supplying a `shop_id` that is neither its own nor an existing shop is told `ERR_CROSS_SHOP_ACCESS`, not `ERR_SHOP_NOT_FOUND`, because the scope rung compares identifier strings and does not need the row to exist. Where the scope check cannot be evaluated without first loading a stored row — the entitlement behind a seat operation, the seat behind a session, the session behind a job — the comparison necessarily happens after that row has been located, so a missing row yields its own not-found error instead. And where the field the scope check would compare is absent or malformed altogether, there is nothing to compare and the request-shape rung reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Wherever that deferral applies, the remainder of the request-shape pass and the resolution of every other reference are deferred with it: only the one field the scope rung must compare is validated eagerly, so a request that names a missing seat, entitlement, session or job *and* carries an unknown field or a second unresolvable reference elsewhere answers that located-first row's own not-found error. Segregation of duties is deferred in the same way and for the same reason, since it cannot be judged before the version row has been loaded.

Second, the state rung outranks every cross-entity gate. Attempting a transition the row's current state does not declare produces `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) even when the request would also have failed a parity, credential or window check. `ERR_INVALID_STATE` is available on every state-changing action endpoint in the system.

Request-shape validation at rung 6 covers, in one pass: that the body parses as JSON, that it contains no unknown fields, that every required field is present, that every enum-typed value is one of that field's declared members, that every integer sits inside its declared bounds, that every datetime is a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, that a supplied `vin` is exactly 17 characters, and that pagination parameters obey §2.3. Three further shapes are policed at this rung. A field the §3 narratives declare unique — a system area `code`, an OEM `oem_code`, a `model_code` within its manufacturer and model year, a diagnostic tool `serial_no` within its shop — is tested for collision once the request's own references have resolved, and a collision answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The seven action endpoints the §3 narratives give no payload at all — license activation, entitlement activation, version publication, session activation, session close, job start and job completion — accept no body field whatever, so any field supplied to them is an unknown field. And the free-text strings whose length the narratives leave open — every `reason`, plus `reviewer_notes`, `identity_document_ref` and `funding_ref` — are required to be non-empty and carry no upper length bound.

### 2.8. Money, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a stored row or a response. Wherever an intermediate calculation produces a fractional amount, the platform rounds to whole cents using **round half up** — the magnitude is increased when the discarded remainder is exactly one half. Intermediate quotients are held as exact rational values and are *not* pre-rounded; only the points this document explicitly identifies as rounding points perform a rounding.

Where a rounding point sits matters, and two calculations pin it deliberately. The provisional charge for a subscription entitlement rounds **each charge line** to whole cents and only then sums the lines; summing exact line values and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the specified behaviour and yields a different total. The gateway access fee rounds its discount and its security surcharge **independently**, and then combines the two already-rounded amounts with exact integer arithmetic. Everywhere else — the per-vehicle provisional charge, both settlement variants — a single rounding is applied to the final amount.

Two non-monetary quantities have their own precision. The pricing-parity index is carried to 4 decimal places using round half up, and it is the **rounded** index that is compared against the parity ceiling, so a raw ratio that rounds down to the ceiling is compliant. Coverage completeness is a percentage carried to 2 decimal places using round half up, and is exactly `0.00` when a shop has no coverage cells at all. The seat ledger and the data-credit ledger are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding of any kind.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host's wall clock, so that expiry and window behaviour is reproducible. Before any clock-setting call has been made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the platform must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability, which answers with the new `current_utc` echoed exactly as the request spelled it; the supplied instant may lie before the clock's present reading as freely as after it. Moving the clock mutates no entity and fires no automatic transition — nothing expires, lapses or settles merely because the clock advanced past a boundary. Expiry is observed only when an operation that checks it is attempted.

Whole-day arithmetic treats one day as 86,400 seconds and always truncates downward: a span of 30 days and 23 hours counts as 30 whole days. Interval overlap, by contrast, is not uniform across the system, and the difference is intentional. Entitlement coverage windows on the same shop-and-license pair are compared as **closed intervals with a one-day cooldown buffer**, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint conflict, and so does a gap of exactly 24 hours; only a gap strictly greater than 24 hours is accepted. Diagnostic tool occupation windows are compared as **half-open intervals with no buffer**, so a session starting at the exact instant a prior session ends is accepted.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation and the Audit Trail

Several operations in this system unwind a whole subtree of dependent state, and they do so **immediately and synchronously** within the originating request — there is no deferred worker and no eventual convergence. Cascades propagate in numbered hops, each hop caused by the one before it, and a hop only touches rows that are in the state that hop names. Hops mutate their targets exactly as an explicit call would: statuses change, reason fields are stamped, derived counters are recomputed, `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` advances by 1 on every row that carries one.

Alongside the state changes, cascading and direct operations alike append rows to an append-only audit trail, which exists because secure-gateway access is only defensible if it is logged. Each audit row records `actor_role`, which is the `X-User-Role` of the originating request or the literal `SYSTEM` when the row was produced by a cascade rather than by a direct call; `actor_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of that request, which is null for `SYSTEM` rows; a polymorphic subject pair consisting of `subject_entity` and `subject_id`; the `event_type`; an optional short machine-readable `detail` string; and `occurred_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the audited action was applied. The `detail` conventions are fixed rather than free: a `SESSION_CREDENTIAL_ISSUED` row carries `vin=` followed by the session's `vin`, a `JOB_STARTED` row carries `job_type=` followed by the job's `job_type`, the `LICENSE_TERMINATED`, `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`, `SEAT_REVOKED`, `SESSION_TERMINATED`, `JOB_ABORTED` and `VERSION_RECALLED` rows each carry `reason=` followed by the operative reason, and the `TECHNICIAN_VERIFIED`, `SEAT_ASSIGNED`, `SESSION_COMPLETED`, `JOB_COMPLETED`, `JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, `VERSION_PUBLISHED` and `ENTITLEMENT_SETTLED` rows carry a null `detail`.

The subject pair is deliberately *not* a declared foreign key — `subject_entity` addresses any of seven record kinds, spelled `Technician`, `SeatAssignment`, `GatewaySession`, `ModuleProgrammingJob`, `PublicationVersion`, `License` or `ShopEntitlement`, and `subject_id` holds the identifier of the audited row within whichever of those kinds applies. The `actor_role` vocabulary is the five role names plus `SYSTEM`. The `event_type` vocabulary is exactly fifteen values: `TECHNICIAN_VERIFIED`, `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`, `SEAT_ASSIGNED`, `SEAT_REVOKED`, `SESSION_CREDENTIAL_ISSUED`, `SESSION_COMPLETED`, `SESSION_TERMINATED`, `JOB_STARTED`, `JOB_COMPLETED`, `JOB_ABORTED`, `JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, `VERSION_PUBLISHED`, `VERSION_RECALLED`, `LICENSE_TERMINATED` and `ENTITLEMENT_SETTLED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The OEM Catalogue and the Pricing-Parity Verdict

Everything downstream is indexed by two catalogue axes, and a `PlatformAdmin` curates both. A **system area** is a functional region of a vehicle — powertrain, body electronics, driver assistance — registered with a `code` of 2 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase letters and the underscore and unique across the whole platform, and a human-readable `name` of 1 to 80 characters. Two boolean flags on the row drive obligations elsewhere and both default to false when omitted. `requires_calibration_doc` marks a system area whose coverage is only complete when an ADAS calibration document is published alongside the repair procedure; `requires_security_credential` marks a system area whose module work may only be performed under a secure-gateway credential token — a documentary marker the platform stores and reports but enforces through no gate of its own, since every activated gateway session already mints a credential. System areas are immutable catalogue rows once written.

Registering an OEM captures a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `oem_code` of 2 to 8 characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits and unique across the platform, a `region` — one of `NA`, `EU` or `APAC` — that fixes which regulator's right-to-repair obligation the OEM is judged under but is otherwise recorded for reporting only, since the parity ceiling is the same 1.15 in every region, and four pricing figures, each of which must be at least 1. `independent_list_monthly_cents` and `independent_covered_system_count` describe the offer the OEM makes to independent shops: what it charges per month, and how many vehicle systems that money buys. `oem_dealer_monthly_cents` and `oem_dealer_covered_system_count` describe the offer the OEM makes to its own franchised dealers for the same data. Registration immediately stamps the row's `parity_index` and `parity_status`, and `parity_status` reads `COMPLIANT` or `NON_COMPLIANT` — nothing else.

The parity verdict is a ratio measured against a ceiling of 1.15, meaning an independent offer priced no more than fifteen percent above the dealer offer passes. Two bases exist for computing it, and they can disagree sharply. Under the **list basis**, the index is the independent monthly price divided by the dealer monthly price, with the quotient rounded half up to four decimal places; how many systems each side gets for the money is ignored entirely. Under the **effective basis**, each offer is first normalised to cents per covered system per day: the independent figure is its monthly price divided by the product of its covered system count and 30 days, the dealer figure is its monthly price divided by the product of its covered system count and 30 days, and the index is the independent normalised figure divided by the dealer normalised figure, again rounded half up to four decimal places. Both normalised figures stay exact rationals until that single rounding. In either case the verdict is `COMPLIANT` when the rounded index is less than or equal to 1.15 and `NON_COMPLIANT` when it exceeds 1.15 — an index landing exactly on 1.1500 is compliant.

Only the list-basis verdict is ever persisted. The `parity_index` and `parity_status` stored on the OEM row are always the list-basis numbers, computed from the four pricing figures on that row alone; no license, entitlement or publication data enters the calculation. The effective-basis verdict is report-only and is never written to the row. A pricing restatement lets a `PlatformAdmin` supply any subset of the four pricing figures — `independent_list_monthly_cents`, `independent_covered_system_count`, `oem_dealer_monthly_cents`, `oem_dealer_covered_system_count`, each optional — and re-runs the list-basis computation, restamping both derived fields and advancing the OEM's `version` by 1 even when the supplied subset is empty and the verdict does not move. Restatement deliberately does **not** cascade to licenses already in force: an OEM that drifts into non-compliance keeps its active licenses, because the parity gate fires only at activation time.

The parity report is a computed read available to a `PlatformAdmin`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `ShopManager`. It accepts an optional `basis` query parameter whose accepted values are `LIST_BASIS` and `EFFECTIVE_BASIS`, defaulting to `LIST_BASIS`, and answers with `manufacturer_id`, the echoed `basis`, the `parity_index` and `parity_status` computed on that basis, the two normalised figures `independent_effective_cents_per_system_day` and `oem_effective_cents_per_system_day`, each carried to 4 decimal places under the same round-half-up rule and reported on either basis rather than only on the effective one, and the `threshold` against which the index was judged, which is 1.15. Consider an OEM charging 115,000 cents monthly to independents for 3 systems and 100,000 cents monthly to dealers for 4 systems: on the list basis its index rounds to 1.1500 and it is `COMPLIANT` by exactly the boundary, while on the effective basis the independent offer normalises to 115,000 divided by the product of 3 systems and 30 days and the dealer offer normalises to 100,000 divided by the product of 4 systems and 30 days, so the index rounds to 1.5333 and the same row reads `NON_COMPLIANT`. Restating the independent price to 115,100 cents pushes even the list-basis index to 1.1510 and flips the stored verdict to `NON_COMPLIANT`.

The second catalogue axis is the vehicle model. Registering one names its `manufacturer_id`, a `model_code` of 1 to 24 characters that must be unique within the combination of that manufacturer and model year, and a `model_year` between 1990 and 2035 inclusive. Vehicle models are immutable once registered. Both catalogue reads are filterable — a manufacturer's model list narrows by `model_year`, and a manufacturer's license list narrows by `status`.

### 3.2. Licensing Manufacturer Service Information

A license is the commercial instrument through which one OEM makes its service information available, and a `PlatformAdmin` drafts it. The draft payload names the granting `manufacturer_id`, the `license_model` offered to shops — `SUBSCRIPTION`, `PER_VEHICLE` or `EITHER` — a `monthly_fee_cents` of at least 1 charged for one subscription coverage window, a `per_vehicle_fee_cents` of at least 1 charged per VIN of quota, a `seat_cap` between 1 and 500 that ceilings how many seats any single entitlement under this license may buy, a `covered_system_area_ids` array holding between 1 and 12 distinct system-area identifiers, and the ISO 8601 UTC instants `effective_start` and `effective_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start. Both fee figures are supplied regardless of `license_model`, because a license offering `EITHER` term needs both. A freshly drafted license opens in `DRAFT` with its `suspension_reason` null.

The covered system areas are more than metadata: they are simultaneously the charge lines of the entitlement pricing calculation and the system axis of every coverage cell the license contributes. A license lives through four states — `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`, the last of which is terminal — and moves between them only through the transitions described below; any other attempt yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Activation drives `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and it is also the reinstatement path `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Every activation, first-time or reinstating, re-runs the right-to-repair parity gate: the granting OEM's stored `parity_status` must read `COMPLIANT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARITY_NON_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 409). Every activation also re-resolves every identifier in `covered_system_area_ids`, and an element that no longer names an existing system area yields `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Because the gate is re-run rather than remembered, a license suspended while its OEM was compliant cannot be reinstated after that OEM's pricing has drifted out of parity. A successful activation sets the status to `ACTIVE` and advances the license's `version` by 1; on the reinstatement path `suspension_reason` is deliberately left in place, preserving why the license was suspended for audit purposes.

Suspension drives `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and stamps the supplied `reason` into `suspension_reason`, advancing `version` by 1. Its effect is narrow and easy to misread: there is **no cascade** to entitlements. Every entitlement drawing on the suspended license stays exactly as it was, an `ACTIVE` entitlement continues to work, and technicians seated under it keep operating. What suspension blocks is new business — buying a new entitlement against this license, and activating a `PENDING` one, are both refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).

Termination is the destructive path, and it is reachable from `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and from `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`; both carry an identical cascade. The supplied `reason` is written to `suspension_reason`, the status becomes `TERMINATED`, `version` advances by 1, and one `LICENSE_TERMINATED` audit row is appended for the license itself. Then three hops run in order. **Hop 1** takes every entitlement drawing on this license that is currently `ACTIVE` through `ACTIVE → LAPSED`, running the final settlement arithmetic of §3.4 on each: `settled_cents` is stamped, `settlement_status` becomes `SETTLED`, the difference between `charged_cents` and `settled_cents` is credited back to the shop's `data_credit_balance_cents`, an `ENTITLEMENT_SETTLED` audit row is appended, and that shop's stored `coverage_completeness_pct` is recomputed. **Hop 2** is caused by hop 1: every seat on each just-lapsed entitlement that is still `ACTIVE` goes `ACTIVE → REVOKED` with its `revocation_reason` set to `LICENSE_TERMINATED`, that entitlement's `seats_assigned` is decremented, and a `SEAT_REVOKED` audit row is appended. **Hop 3** is caused by hop 2: every gateway session held on one of those just-revoked seats that is still `ACTIVE` goes `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with its `termination_reason` set to `SEAT_REVOKED`, the session's access fee is computed and stamped as described in §3.8, the resulting `total_access_fee_cents` is added to the shop's `gateway_fee_accrued_cents`, the session's diagnostic tool is released back to `IDLE` with its `occupied_by_session_id` cleared, and a `SESSION_TERMINATED` audit row is appended. Because every audit row in this cascade originates from the platform rather than from a shop operator, each carries `actor_role` of `SYSTEM` and a null `actor_user_id`.

### 3.3. Shops, Data Credit and Buying an Entitlement

An independent repair shop enters the platform with just two facts: a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `tier`, which is `BASIC`, `PRO` or `ENTERPRISE`. Tier is the shop's commercial standing and it discounts everything the shop buys. A newly registered shop starts with `funded_credit_total_cents` at 0, `data_credit_balance_cents` at 0, `gateway_fee_accrued_cents` at 0 and `coverage_completeness_pct` at `0.00`.

Funding is the only operation that raises a shop's lifetime credit. A `PlatformAdmin` supplies an `amount_cents` of at least 1 together with a non-empty `funding_ref` identifying the payment upstream, which the platform requires but stores on no row; the platform adds that amount to both `funded_credit_total_cents` and `data_credit_balance_cents` and advances the shop's `version` by 1. `funded_credit_total_cents` is a lifetime total and never decreases for any reason.

Those two fields, together with the charge fields on the shop's entitlements, obey a **data-credit conservation identity** that must hold after every funding, every entitlement purchase and every settlement. Taking the shop's `data_credit_balance_cents`, adding the `charged_cents` of every entitlement of that shop whose `settlement_status` is `UNSETTLED`, and adding the `settled_cents` of every entitlement whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`, must yield exactly `funded_credit_total_cents`. Entitlements in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` are always `UNSETTLED`; entitlements in `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED` are always `SETTLED`. The three mutations that maintain the identity are funding, which raises the funded total and the balance by the same amount; purchase, which lowers the balance by the new entitlement's `charged_cents`; and settlement, which raises the balance by the `charged_cents` minus `settled_cents` difference and flips `settlement_status` to `SETTLED`. Note carefully that `gateway_fee_accrued_cents` sits **outside** this identity: secure-gateway access fees accrue as a separate running total and are never netted against the data-credit balance. A worked run: fund 200,000 cents and the balance and funded total are both 200,000; buy a subscription entitlement charged 84,999 and the balance falls to 115,001, which plus the unsettled 84,999 is still 200,000; cancel that entitlement after 10 of its 31 days for a settled amount of 27,419 and a refund of 57,580, and the balance rises to 172,581, which plus the settled 27,419 is still 200,000.

Buying an entitlement is a `ShopManager` operation and the payload carries the buying `shop_id`, the `license_id` it draws coverage from, the `entitlement_model` chosen — `SUBSCRIPTION` or `PER_VEHICLE` — the `seats_total` purchased between 1 and 500, an optional `vin_quota` that defaults to 0, and the coverage window instants `window_start` and `window_end`. The license must be `ACTIVE` or the purchase is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The coverage window must open no earlier than the license's `effective_start`, must close no later than its `effective_end`, and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; a payload breaching any of those declared bounds is rejected as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The first two of those bounds can only be compared once the license row has been located, so a purchase naming no existing license answers `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND` even when its window also falls outside the license's interval, whereas a `window_end` at or before `window_start` is caught on shape alone.

Three commercial constraints are folded into a single rejection code. `seats_total` may not exceed the license's `seat_cap`; the chosen `entitlement_model` must be permitted by the license's `license_model`, where a license offering `SUBSCRIPTION` permits only `SUBSCRIPTION`, one offering `PER_VEHICLE` permits only `PER_VEHICLE`, and one offering `EITHER` permits both; and `vin_quota` must be at least 1 exactly when the model is `PER_VEHICLE` and must be 0 when the model is `SUBSCRIPTION`. Any of those three failures returns `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Windows on the same shop-and-license pair may not crowd each other. Using the closed-interval-plus-buffer rule of §2.9 with a cooldown of 24 hours, the purchase is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) if any sibling entitlement on the same shop and the same license, in status `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, satisfies both of these: the new window's start is at or before that sibling's end shifted 24 hours later, and that sibling's start is at or before the new window's end shifted 24 hours later. Concretely: windows that overlap conflict, windows that merely touch at an instant conflict, a gap of exactly 24 hours conflicts, and only a gap strictly wider than 24 hours is accepted. Siblings in `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored entirely.

The purchase then prices itself with the **provisional** variant of the entitlement charge. Three shared inputs feed both variants. The tier multiplier is 1.00 for a `BASIC` shop, 0.85 for a `PRO` shop and 0.70 for an `ENTERPRISE` shop, read from the shop's `tier` as it stands at the moment the variant runs. The seat factor is 1 plus five hundredths for each purchased seat beyond the first three, so an entitlement of three seats or fewer has a seat factor of exactly 1, four seats gives 1.05, and ten seats gives 1.35. The charge lines are exactly the elements of the license's `covered_system_area_ids` — every element, irrespective of whether the corresponding system area sets `requires_calibration_doc` or `requires_security_credential` — taken in ascending order of the system area's `code`, and the line count is how many there are, which the license constraint guarantees is at least 1.

For a `SUBSCRIPTION` entitlement the provisional charge divides the license's `monthly_fee_cents` by the line count to obtain a per-line base held as an exact rational, multiplies that base by the tier multiplier and then by the seat factor, and **rounds that per-line product half up to whole cents**; the resulting whole-cent line charge is then multiplied by the line count to give `charged_cents`. The rounding order is pinned and consequential: with a monthly fee of 100,000 cents across 3 lines for a `PRO` shop at three seats, the per-line base is 33,333 and a third cents, the per-line product is 28,333.3333 cents, the line charge rounds to 28,333, and `charged_cents` is 84,999 — whereas summing the exact line values and rounding once at the end would have produced 85,000, which is not the specified answer. For a `PER_VEHICLE` entitlement there is only one line and therefore no per-line rounding step: `charged_cents` is the license's `per_vehicle_fee_cents` multiplied by the purchased `vin_quota` and by the tier multiplier, rounded half up once; the seat factor plays no part in it, so `seats_total` does not move a per-vehicle charge at all. The same `PRO` shop buying 40 VINs at 2,500 cents each is charged 85,000 whatever its `seats_total`. `vin_quota` is a pricing input and nothing more: the platform never refuses a gateway session for exceeding it, and the final variant of §3.4 re-prices on the VINs actually consumed rather than on the quota bought.

Only after every other check has passed does the platform test whether the shop can pay: if `data_credit_balance_cents` is below the provisional `charged_cents` just computed, the purchase is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DATA_CREDIT` (HTTP 402). This is the last rung of the ladder in §2.7, so a shop with an empty balance that also breached its seat cap hears about the seat cap first. On success the platform stamps `charged_cents`, debits the shop's `data_credit_balance_cents` by that amount and advances the shop's `version` by 1, and the new entitlement opens with `status` of `PENDING`, `settlement_status` of `UNSETTLED`, `settled_cents` of 0, `seats_assigned` of 0 and `seats_available` equal to `seats_total`. Entitlements can be listed with narrowing by `shop_id`, by `license_id` and by `status`, and a single entitlement read exposes its charge, settlement and seat-ledger fields together.

### 3.4. Entitlement Activation, Settlement and the Two Exit Branches

An entitlement lives through `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, and then one of two terminal states, `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED`. Activation drives `PENDING → ACTIVE` and requires two things: that the entitlement really is `PENDING`, and that its license is still `ACTIVE` — a license that has since been suspended or terminated blocks activation with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation is otherwise unremarkable: the status flips, the entitlement's `version` advances by 1, and the owning shop's stored `coverage_completeness_pct` is recomputed, because it is only now that this entitlement's cells enter the coverage denominator.

The fork out of `ACTIVE` has two branches with the same settlement arithmetic and deliberately different side-effect sets. **Branch A is cancellation**, a shop-initiated exit driven by a `ShopManager` with a supplied non-empty `reason` that the platform requires but then records nowhere — it reaches neither the entitlement row nor the audit `detail` — reachable from `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` and also from `PENDING → CANCELLED`. **Branch B is lapse**, `ACTIVE → LAPSED`, which no operator can request: it happens only as hop 1 of the license-termination cascade of §3.2.

Both branches run the **final** variant of the entitlement charge. Two day counts frame it. The window length is the span from `window_start` to `window_end` truncated downward to whole days at 86,400 seconds per day; a window narrower than one whole day therefore has a length of zero whole days, and a `SUBSCRIPTION` entitlement on such a window settles at a `settled_cents` of 0 rather than provoking a division by zero. The elapsed length is the span from `window_start` to whichever is earlier of the current logical clock and `window_end`, likewise truncated downward to whole days, and then floored at zero so that a window that has not begun yields zero elapsed days rather than a negative count. For a `SUBSCRIPTION` entitlement, `settled_cents` is the already-stamped `charged_cents` multiplied by the elapsed day count and divided by the window day count, rounded half up once to whole cents — a straightforward proration of what was charged. Taking the earlier example of a subscription charged 84,999 over a window from 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z to 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z, which is 31 whole days, exited when the logical clock reads 2026-08-11T00:00:00Z, which is 10 elapsed days: `settled_cents` is 84,999 multiplied by 10, divided by 31, rounded half up, giving 27,419.

For a `PER_VEHICLE` entitlement the final variant does not prorate by time at all; it re-prices from scratch on consumption. The platform counts the **distinct** `vin` values across every gateway session whose seat belongs to this entitlement and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED` — sessions still sitting in `REQUESTED` are excluded, and VINs are de-duplicated by exact string equality. `settled_cents` is then the license's `per_vehicle_fee_cents` multiplied by that distinct-VIN count and by the tier multiplier of the shop's `tier` as it stands at settlement, rounded half up once. In both variants the refund credited back to the shop is `charged_cents` minus `settled_cents`; the same entitlement that was worth 84,999 provisionally is worth 27,419 finally, and the 57,580 difference returns to `data_credit_balance_cents`.

Cancellation's side-effect set, in full: the status becomes `CANCELLED`, the final variant runs and stamps `settled_cents`, `settlement_status` becomes `SETTLED`, the refund is added to the shop's `data_credit_balance_cents`, every seat on this entitlement still in `ACTIVE` goes to `REVOKED` with its `revocation_reason` set to `ENTITLEMENT_CANCELLED` and each such revocation terminates that seat's `ACTIVE` gateway session exactly as hop 3 of the license-termination cascade does — access fee stamped and accrued, tool released, residual jobs aborted — the shop's `coverage_completeness_pct` is recomputed, an `ENTITLEMENT_SETTLED` audit row is appended, and the entitlement's `version` advances by 1 for the settlement itself and by a further 1 for each seat the cascade revokes, because every seat-ledger recomputation is a mutation of the entitlement row in its own right. Cancelling from `PENDING` follows the identical path, and because a window that has not started yields zero elapsed days, `settled_cents` comes out at 0 and the shop receives a full refund.

Lapse's side-effect set is the mirror image with three specific divergences: the status becomes `LAPSED` rather than `CANCELLED`, the seat revocations carry `revocation_reason` of `LICENSE_TERMINATED` rather than `ENTITLEMENT_CANCELLED`, and because no shop operator initiated anything, the `ENTITLEMENT_SETTLED` audit row is written with `actor_role` of `SYSTEM` and no acting user identifier. Everything else matches: the same final arithmetic, the same `settled_cents` and `settlement_status` stamps, the same refund credit, the same seat and session cascade, the same coverage recomputation and the same pattern of `version` advances. Neither `LAPSED` nor `CANCELLED` admits any further transition, so a second exit attempt on an already-settled entitlement is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — which also protects the conservation identity of §3.3 from being credited twice.

### 3.5. Technician Identity, Seat Assignment and the Suspension Cascade

A `ShopManager` enrols a technician by supplying the employer `shop_id`, the technician's `full_name` as it appears on the identity document (1 to 120 characters), and a `credential_expires_at` instant at which the secure-gateway credential is expected to lapse. Enrolment does not confer access: a new technician opens in `identity_status` of `UNVERIFIED`, with `identity_document_ref` and `verified_by_user_id` both null. Identity moves through `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`, with `REVOKED` as its terminal state, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may move it. A shop's technician list narrows by `identity_status`.

Verification is the gate on credential provisioning, and it is reachable both from `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED` and, as a re-verification, from `SUSPENDED → VERIFIED`. The `ComplianceOfficer` supplies the `identity_document_ref` of the document it accepted and a `credential_expires_at` instant, which must lie strictly after the current logical clock; a verification offering an already-lapsed expiry is refused with `ERR_SECURITY_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). On success `identity_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, both `identity_document_ref` and `credential_expires_at` are re-stamped from the request — so every verification refreshes the credential lifetime — `verified_by_user_id` records the acting officer's opaque `X-User-ID`, a `TECHNICIAN_VERIFIED` audit row is appended, and the technician's `version` advances by 1. Re-verification restores nothing but the identity itself: seats that a prior suspension revoked stay `REVOKED`, and sessions that suspension terminated stay `TERMINATED`.

Suspension is where the deepest cascade in the system lives. It is reachable from `VERIFIED → SUSPENDED` and from `UNVERIFIED → SUSPENDED`, both carrying an identical effect set, and the officer supplies a `reason` which is carried into the audit trail's `detail` string rather than onto the technician row, which has no reason field. The status becomes `SUSPENDED`, a `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED` audit row is appended for the technician itself, and four hops run in order. **Hop 1** takes every seat held by this technician that is still `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` of `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`, decrements the owning entitlement's `seats_assigned`, and appends a `SEAT_REVOKED` row. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, takes every gateway session on one of those just-revoked seats that is still `ACTIVE` through `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` of `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`, computes and stamps its access fee, adds the resulting `total_access_fee_cents` to the shop's `gateway_fee_accrued_cents`, releases the occupied diagnostic tool, and appends a `SESSION_TERMINATED` row. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, takes every programming job on one of those just-terminated sessions whose status is `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` of `SESSION_TERMINATED` and appends a `JOB_ABORTED` row for each. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3, decrements by 1 the `active_pin_count` of the publication version each job that departed `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS` was pinned to, and leaves that count alone for a job aborted out of `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, which had already surrendered its pin on entering that state.

Revocation is the terminal step and it is reachable only from `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`; revoking a technician who is `UNVERIFIED` or `VERIFIED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The officer supplies a non-empty `reason`, which the platform requires but records nowhere, since revocation appends no audit row at all; the status becomes `REVOKED`, `credential_expires_at` is deliberately left untouched so the audit record of what the credential had been survives, and the technician's `version` advances by 1. No cascade runs, because the suspension that necessarily preceded revocation already revoked the seats and terminated the sessions.

A **seat assignment** is the junction that connects a technician to an entitlement; a technician with no seat can do nothing at the gateway, and a technician may hold seats on several entitlements at once. A `ShopManager` seats a technician by supplying the `entitlement_id`, the `technician_id`, and the `expected_entitlement_version` demanded by §2.5. The technician's employer shop must be the same shop that holds the entitlement; seating a technician from another shop is a cross-shop violation and is refused with `ERR_CROSS_SHOP_ACCESS`. Nothing else about either party is gated here: seating consults neither the entitlement's `status` — a `PENDING`, `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED` entitlement may be seated — nor the technician's `identity_status`, since the gates that matter are the ones session activation applies. Seats live in `ACTIVE`, with `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED` as the two terminal states, and a new seat opens in `ACTIVE` with a null `revocation_reason`. A successful seating recomputes the entitlement's seat ledger, advances the entitlement's `version` by 1, appends a `SEAT_ASSIGNED` audit row, and answers 201.

Seating the *same* technician again on the *same* entitlement is not an error — it is a **re-seat**, and it drives the prior row `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` while the new row opens `ACTIVE`. Because one active row simply replaces another, the entitlement's `seats_assigned` is unchanged by a re-seat, and the seat-availability check below does not apply to it. A re-seat does leave one sharp edge: a gateway session that was already requested against the superseded seat remains in `REQUESTED` forever, because activation requires an `ACTIVE` seat and the seat behind it is no longer active — such an activation attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Seating a technician who does **not** already hold an active seat on the entitlement consumes capacity, and requires at least one free seat: if the entitlement's `seats_available` is 0, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_SEATS_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).

The **seat ledger** is a derived pair maintained on the entitlement after every seat mutation. `seats_assigned` is the count of seat rows on that entitlement whose status is `ACTIVE` — rows in `REVOKED` and rows in `SUPERSEDED` are excluded — and `seats_available` is the purchased `seats_total` minus `seats_assigned`. Seat conservation is an invariant that must hold after every seat mutation without exception: `seats_total` always equals `seats_assigned` plus `seats_available`. Across a forward assignment followed by its compensating revocation, both derived numbers return to exactly the values they had before the assignment; across a re-seat, both are unchanged.

Revoking a seat explicitly is a `ShopManager` action carrying a `reason`, permitted only from `ACTIVE → REVOKED`. The supplied `reason` is written verbatim to `revocation_reason`, the entitlement's `seats_assigned` is decremented by 1 with `seats_available` recomputed, the entitlement's `version` advances by 1, every gateway session on that seat still in `ACTIVE` goes `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` of `SEAT_REVOKED` — stamping its access fee, accruing that fee on the shop, releasing its diagnostic tool, aborting its residual jobs in `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and decrementing the pinned `active_pin_count` of only those that departed `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — and a `SEAT_REVOKED` audit row is appended. Seat revocation also arrives by cascade, from a technician suspension, an entitlement cancellation or a license termination; the cascaded form does exactly what the explicit form does, except that `revocation_reason` is set by the platform to `TECHNICIAN_SUSPENDED`, `ENTITLEMENT_CANCELLED` or `LICENSE_TERMINATED` respectively and the `SEAT_REVOKED` audit row carries `actor_role` of `SYSTEM`. In both forms alike the session and job rows the revocation cascades into are audited with `actor_role` of `SYSTEM` and a null `actor_user_id`, because everything deeper than the seat is a cascade however the revocation itself was triggered. Seat lists narrow by `entitlement_id`, by `technician_id` and by `status`.

### 3.6. The Publication Pipeline: Ingestion, Validation and Distribution

Service information reaches shops as **publications**, and a publication is best understood as one cell of a matrix: a `ContentPublisher` opens it against exactly one `vehicle_model_id`, one `system_area_id` and one `publication_type`, where the type is `REPAIR_PROCEDURE`, `TSB` or `CALIBRATION_DOC`. That triple is not constrained to be unique — a second publication may be opened over the same model, system area and type, and the document sets of §3.9 simply pool the versions of every publication addressing a cell. A newly opened publication has `current_version_no` of 0 and `published_version_id` of null, and those two computed pointers are its whole content: `current_version_no` tracks the highest version number ever minted beneath it, and `published_version_id` names the single child version whose availability axis currently reads `PUBLISHED`, or null when none does. Publications can be listed with narrowing by `vehicle_model_id`, `system_area_id` and `publication_type`.

Ingesting a technical service bulletin mints a new version beneath a publication. The payload names the `source_tsb_ref` the material was ingested from (1 to 40 characters), an optional `calibration_procedure_ref` naming the ADAS calibration procedure, and the `expected_publication_version` that §2.5 requires. The calibration reference is mandatory when the parent publication's `publication_type` is `CALIBRATION_DOC`; omitting it there is a missing required field and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The minted row records the `publication_id` of the publication it was ingested beneath and takes a `version_no` of the publication's `current_version_no` plus 1 — so numbering starts at 1 and is monotonic within each publication — the publication's `current_version_no` is advanced to match, and the publication's own `version` advances by 1. The new version opens with `review_status` of `DRAFT`, `distribution_status` of `WITHHELD`, `validated_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` both null, and `active_pin_count` at 0.

Each version carries **two independent status axes**, and confusing them is the single most common way to get this domain wrong. The editorial axis is `review_status`, whose only values are `DRAFT` and `VALIDATED`. The availability axis is `distribution_status`, whose values are `WITHHELD`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `RECALLED`, the last two of which are terminal on that axis. Publishing does **not** require validation — a data-availability obligation can force material out of the door before review has finished, which is precisely why coverage reporting in §3.9 needs two bases. A version's `active_pin_count` is a third derived number: the count of programming jobs pinned to it whose status is `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Version lists beneath a publication narrow by `review_status` and by `distribution_status`, and are ordered by ascending `version_no`.

Validation advances the editorial axis `DRAFT → VALIDATED`. The acting `ContentPublisher` supplies `reviewer_notes`. Two preconditions apply: the version's `review_status` must currently be `DRAFT`, and its `distribution_status` must not be `RECALLED` — recalled material is not re-reviewed, and attempting it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success `review_status` becomes `VALIDATED`, `validated_by_user_id` records the acting publisher's `X-User-ID`, the version's `version` advances by 1, and the stored `coverage_completeness_pct` is recomputed for every shop that holds an `ACTIVE` entitlement whose license covers this version's model-and-system cell.

Publication advances the availability axis `WITHHELD → PUBLISHED`, and it is guarded by segregation of duties rather than by the editorial axis. The version's `review_status` is explicitly **not** checked; a `DRAFT` version can be published. What is checked is that if `validated_by_user_id` is not null, it must differ from the publishing request's `X-User-ID`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `distribution_status` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_by_user_id` records the acting publisher, the parent publication's `published_version_id` is repointed at this version, a `VERSION_PUBLISHED` audit row is appended, and the version's `version` advances by 1. Three hops then run. **Hop 1** takes the previously published sibling version of the same publication, if there was one, through `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`; the superseded row's `review_status` is left exactly as it was, since supersession says nothing about editorial quality. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, reaches into every programming job pinned to that superseded version: jobs in `IN_PROGRESS` go `IN_PROGRESS → REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and each appends a `JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` audit row, while jobs in `QUEUED` go straight to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` of `PINNED_VERSION_SUPERSEDED`. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, decrements the superseded version's `active_pin_count` once for every job that left the `QUEUED` or `IN_PROGRESS` set. Finally the stored coverage completeness is recomputed for every affected shop.

Recall drives `PUBLISHED → RECALLED` and is the withdrawal path for material found to be wrong. The publisher supplies a `reason`, `distribution_status` becomes `RECALLED`, and the parent publication's `published_version_id` is set back to null, leaving that cell with no published version at all. Jobs pinned to the recalled version behave as under supersession, with one difference in the recorded cause: `IN_PROGRESS` jobs move to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and append `JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, while `QUEUED` jobs move to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` of `PINNED_VERSION_RECALLED`. The version's `active_pin_count` is decremented once per departing job, coverage completeness is recomputed for every affected shop, a `VERSION_RECALLED` audit row is appended, and the version's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.7. Secure Gateway Sessions, Tool Occupation and Access Pricing

A shop's scan tools are registered by its `ShopManager` with the owning `shop_id`, a `serial_no` of 4 to 40 characters that must be unique within that shop, and an `oem_attestation_expires_at` instant at which the tool's OEM security attestation lapses. A tool's `status` is `IDLE` or `OCCUPIED`, it starts `IDLE`, and `occupied_by_session_id` names the session currently holding it or is null while the tool is idle. A shop's tool list narrows by `status`.

Requesting a gateway session is a `Technician` action and it issues no credential; it merely reserves a tool window. The payload names the `seat_assignment_id` the technician is acting through, the `diagnostic_tool_id` to be occupied, the `vin` the session is scoped to — exactly 17 characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits with the letters I, O and Q excluded, per the VIN standard — and the occupation window `window_start` and `window_end`. The entitlement behind the named seat must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409); the seat's own status is not examined at request time, so a session may be requested through a `SUPERSEDED` or `REVOKED` seat and will simply never become activatable. The requested tool must belong to the same shop as that entitlement; a tool from another shop is refused with `ERR_TOOL_SHOP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The session window must sit wholly inside the entitlement's coverage window — its start at or after the entitlement's `window_start` and its end at or before the entitlement's `window_end` — and its own end must be strictly after its own start; any of those failures returns `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_ENTITLEMENT` (HTTP 422). A successful request opens the session in `REQUESTED` with `credential_token` null, `termination_reason` null, and all four money fields — `base_subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `total_access_fee_cents` — at 0.

Activation is where the secure-gateway credential is actually provisioned, and it drives `REQUESTED → ACTIVE` through the densest gate in the system. Six conditions are checked beyond the state itself, and in this order: the seat behind the session must still be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the entitlement behind that seat must be `ACTIVE`, failing which `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the seated technician's `identity_status` must read `VERIFIED`, failing which `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_VERIFIED`; that technician's `credential_expires_at` must lie strictly after the current logical clock, failing which `ERR_SECURITY_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`; the tool's `oem_attestation_expires_at` must lie strictly after the current logical clock, so a session cannot be activated at or after the attestation instant, failing which `ERR_TOOL_ATTESTATION_EXPIRED`; and no sibling session on the same tool that is currently `ACTIVE` may overlap this session's window under the half-open arithmetic of §2.9 — that is, no sibling whose start is strictly before this window's end while this window's start is strictly before that sibling's end — failing which `ERR_TOOL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Back-to-back windows, where one starts at the exact instant another ends, are accepted; there is no buffer. The tool's own `status` is not a gate in its own right: two sessions whose windows are disjoint may both be activated on one tool and both sit in `ACTIVE`, in which case `occupied_by_session_id` names whichever was activated more recently, and a session leaving `ACTIVE` clears that field only while it is still the session named there.

On successful activation the session's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` and its `credential_token` is minted as the literal prefix `GW-` followed by the first eight hexadecimal characters of the session's own `id`, upper-cased. The occupied tool moves to `OCCUPIED` with `occupied_by_session_id` set to this session and its own `version` advanced by 1, a `SESSION_CREDENTIAL_ISSUED` audit row is appended whose `detail` is the literal text `vin=` followed by the session's `vin`, and the session's `version` advances by 1.

Closing a session is a single technician action that **branches on the clock**, and the two branches diverge in more than their resulting status. When the current logical clock is strictly before the session's `window_end`, the branch is `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, and it carries one extra precondition: no programming job on the session may be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the close is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Live module work therefore blocks an orderly close. When the current logical clock is at or after `window_end`, the branch is `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, and that precondition does not apply — an expired window is closed regardless of what is in flight. Both branches price the session identically, add the resulting `total_access_fee_cents` to the owning shop's `gateway_fee_accrued_cents`, release the diagnostic tool back to `IDLE` with `occupied_by_session_id` cleared, and advance the session's `version` by 1, and both leave `termination_reason` null, since neither branch stamps it and the audit row carries the cause instead. They differ in three places: the residual-job set they abort, the `abort_reason` they stamp, and the audit row they write. The completed branch aborts jobs in `QUEUED` and in `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` with `abort_reason` of `SESSION_COMPLETED`, decrementing `active_pin_count` for the `QUEUED` rows only — a `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` row already left the pin count when it entered that state — and appends a `SESSION_COMPLETED` audit row. The expired branch aborts jobs in `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` with `abort_reason` of `SESSION_EXPIRED`, decrementing `active_pin_count` for the `QUEUED` and `IN_PROGRESS` rows but not for the `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` rows, and appends a `SESSION_TERMINATED` audit row whose `detail` is the literal text `reason=SESSION_EXPIRED`.

The third exit from `ACTIVE` is `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, which no operator can request: it fires only as a cascade behind a seat revocation, whether that revocation was explicit or itself cascaded from a technician suspension, an entitlement cancellation or a license termination. `termination_reason` is stamped from the cascade origin, the session is priced exactly as the other two exits price it, the fee is accrued on the shop, the tool is released to `IDLE`, jobs in `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` are aborted with `abort_reason` of `SESSION_TERMINATED` with `active_pin_count` decremented for the `QUEUED` and `IN_PROGRESS` rows only, and a `SESSION_TERMINATED` audit row is written with `actor_role` of `SYSTEM`. Sessions listed by a client can be narrowed by `seat_assignment_id`, by `diagnostic_tool_id` and by `status`.

**Pricing a gateway session** happens on every exit from `ACTIVE` and never otherwise: a session that was requested but never activated is never priced and keeps all four money fields at 0. The calculation runs in five pinned steps.

Step one builds `base_subtotal_cents`. The billable job set is read at the instant the session leaves `ACTIVE` and **immediately before that exit's own job cascade is applied**, and it comprises exactly the jobs whose status at that read point is `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`. The consequences of that pinned read point are deliberate: a job the exit is about to abort out of `IN_PROGRESS` **is** billed, a job still sitting in `QUEUED` at the read point is **not** billed even though the same exit aborts it, and a job already aborted by some earlier event is **not** billed. To a flat session base of 2,500 cents and a flat VIN credential charge of 300 cents, the platform adds a per-job amount for each billable job according to its `job_type`: 4,000 cents for an `IMMOBILIZER_KEY` job, 3,000 cents for a `MODULE_FLASH` job, and 6,000 cents for an `ADAS_CALIBRATION` job. The sum is `base_subtotal_cents`.

Step two selects a volume discount rate, and it is keyed on `base_subtotal_cents` — **not** on the eventual total. A base subtotal below 5,000 cents earns no discount; a base subtotal from 5,000 cents up to but not including 10,000 cents earns five percent; a base subtotal of 10,000 cents or more earns ten percent. Keying the tier on the total would be circular, because the surcharge computed in step four is part of the total; the loop is resolved by fixing the tier's input to step one's output, and any implementation that keys the tier on a surcharge-inclusive figure will produce different, wrong answers. Step three computes `discount_cents` as `base_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that discount rate, rounded half up to whole cents.

Step four computes the security surcharge, likewise keyed on step one's output: the rate is twelve percent when at least one billable job has `job_type` of `IMMOBILIZER_KEY`, and four percent otherwise, and `surcharge_cents` is `base_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents independently of the discount rounding. Step five sets `total_access_fee_cents` to `base_subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`, in exact integer arithmetic over the two already-rounded parts. Worked through: a session with one `IMMOBILIZER_KEY` job and one `MODULE_FLASH` job, both billable, has a base subtotal of 2,500 plus 300 plus 4,000 plus 3,000, which is 9,800; 9,800 falls in the five-percent band, so `discount_cents` is 490; an immobilizer job is present, so `surcharge_cents` is twelve percent of 9,800, which is 1,176; and `total_access_fee_cents` is 9,800 minus 490 plus 1,176, which is 10,486.

### 3.8. Module Programming Jobs and Version Pinning

A programming job is the unit of module work performed under a live session, and queueing one is a `Technician` action naming the `session_id` it runs under, the `job_type` — `IMMOBILIZER_KEY`, `MODULE_FLASH` or `ADAS_CALIBRATION` — and the `pinned_publication_version_id` of the exact procedure version the work will follow. Pinning is what makes module work auditable: the technician commits to a specific document revision, and the platform undertakes to notice if that revision is withdrawn underneath them. At queue time the pinned version must be usable: a version still in `WITHHELD` was never available and is refused with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409), while a version in `SUPERSEDED` or `RECALLED` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 409) — the two failures are kept distinguishable on purpose. Queueing examines nothing beyond that pinned version: neither the session's own status — a job may be queued under a session still in `REQUESTED`, or under one already closed — nor the acting technician's credential expiry is gated here, both being left to the start-time re-run below. A successful queue opens the job in `QUEUED` with `repin_count` at 0 and `abort_reason` null, and increments the pinned version's `active_pin_count` by 1. Jobs are listed with narrowing by `session_id`, by `pinned_publication_version_id` and by `status`.

Starting a job drives `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS` and re-runs the gates that could have gone stale while the job sat in the queue. The session must still be `ACTIVE`, or the start is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The acting technician's `credential_expires_at` must still lie strictly after the current logical clock, or the start is refused with `ERR_SECURITY_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. The pinned version must still be `PUBLISHED`, with the same two-way distinction as at queue time: `WITHHELD` yields `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`, and `SUPERSEDED` or `RECALLED` yields `ERR_STALE_PUBLICATION_VERSION`. And one gate applies only to ADAS work: when the `job_type` is `ADAS_CALIBRATION`, there must exist, for the same model-and-system cell as the pinned version's own publication, a publication of type `CALIBRATION_DOC` carrying a version that is simultaneously `VALIDATED` on its editorial axis and `PUBLISHED` on its availability axis. If no such calibration document exists, the start is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_DOC_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A successful start sets `status` to `IN_PROGRESS`, appends a `JOB_STARTED` audit row whose `detail` is the literal text `job_type=` followed by the job's `job_type`, and advances the job's `version` by 1.

A job's status vocabulary is `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, of which the last two are terminal. The `IN_PROGRESS → REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` transition is never requested by anyone — it is the cascade described in §3.6, fired when the version a live job is pinned to leaves `PUBLISHED`, whether by being superseded by a newer sibling or by being recalled outright. On entering `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` the job's pinned version has its `active_pin_count` decremented by 1 and a `JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` audit row is appended. The debt this represents must be settled deliberately rather than papered over: asking a job that sits in `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` to complete is refused with `ERR_JOB_REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409), a refinement that exists so the technician learns *why* completion is barred instead of receiving a generic state error. Asking any other non-`IN_PROGRESS` job to complete yields plain `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Re-pinning closes the cycle `IN_PROGRESS → REVALIDATION_REQUIRED → IN_PROGRESS`, and a job may traverse it any number of times. The technician supplies a replacement `pinned_publication_version_id`. Four conditions hold: the job must currently be in `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the session must still be `ACTIVE`, or likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the replacement version's `distribution_status` must read `PUBLISHED`, and any other value on the replacement — `WITHHELD`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RECALLED` alike — is refused with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED`; and the replacement must belong to the same publication as the pin it displaces, since re-pinning is a move to a newer revision of the same document and not a change of subject, so a replacement under a different publication is refused with `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. On success the job's `pinned_publication_version_id` is replaced, `repin_count` is increased by 1, `status` returns to `IN_PROGRESS`, the newly pinned version's `active_pin_count` is increased by 1, and the job's `version` advances by 1.

Completing a job requires it to be `IN_PROGRESS` and its session to still be `ACTIVE`; either failure yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, except for the `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` refinement noted above. On success `status` becomes `COMPLETED`, the pinned version's `active_pin_count` is decremented by 1, a `JOB_COMPLETED` audit row is appended, and the job's `version` advances by 1. Abortion, by contrast, is always a cascade and never a request. A `QUEUED` job aborted by a cascade has its pinned version's `active_pin_count` decremented by 1; an `IN_PROGRESS` job aborted by session expiry or termination likewise has it decremented by 1; and a `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` job aborted by a session exit does **not** have it decremented again, because that job already left the pin count when it entered `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`. In every case `abort_reason` is stamped from the cascade origin and a `JOB_ABORTED` audit row is appended.

### 3.9. Coverage Completeness, Gap Identification and the Audit Trail

The coverage map is the answer to the question an independent shop actually cares about: for the vehicles I can be asked to fix, does the platform hold the documents I need? It is computed over a **cell universe** derived from the shop's live entitlements. Only entitlements in status `ACTIVE` contribute — `PENDING`, `LAPSED` and `CANCELLED` entitlements are excluded entirely, so a shop with no active entitlement has a cell universe of size zero. Each contributing entitlement brings in its license, and each such license contributes one cell for every pairing of a vehicle model built by that license's manufacturer with a system area named in that license's `covered_system_area_ids`. Vehicle models contribute irrespective of their `model_year`. Cells arising from more than one license are counted once — the universe is de-duplicated — and its size is reported as `total_cells`.

Each cell is then classified. Two document sets matter for a cell: the repair-procedure set, comprising every publication version whose publication addresses that cell's model and system area with `publication_type` of `REPAIR_PROCEDURE`, and the calibration set, comprising the same but with `publication_type` of `CALIBRATION_DOC`. A set satisfies the **strict** test when it contains at least one version whose `review_status` is `VALIDATED` *and* whose `distribution_status` is `PUBLISHED`. It satisfies the **loose** test when it contains at least one version whose `distribution_status` is `PUBLISHED`, with the editorial axis ignored. Versions in `WITHHELD`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RECALLED` satisfy neither test. Whether the calibration set is consulted at all depends on the system area's `requires_calibration_doc` flag.

A cell is `COVERED` when the repair-procedure set passes the strict test and, if the system area requires a calibration document, the calibration set also passes the strict test. A cell that is not `COVERED` is `DEGRADED` when the repair-procedure set passes the loose test and, if a calibration document is required, the calibration set also passes the loose test. Every remaining cell is a `GAP`. The three counts — `covered_cells`, `degraded_cells` and `gap_cells` — partition `total_cells` exactly.

Completeness is then a percentage over that partition, and the numerator depends on the requested basis. Under the **effective** basis, the numerator is `covered_cells` alone, so an unvalidated-but-published procedure earns nothing. Under the **declared** basis, the numerator is `covered_cells` plus `degraded_cells` — the number an OEM would quote to demonstrate that it has met a data-availability obligation. In both cases `completeness_pct` is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by `total_cells`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is exactly `0.00` when `total_cells` is zero. The divergence is real, not cosmetic: take a shop with one active entitlement on a license covering two system areas, one flagged `requires_calibration_doc` and one not, for a manufacturer with a single vehicle model, giving two cells. If the flagged cell has a validated and published repair procedure but no calibration document at all, it is a `GAP`, because the strict and loose calibration tests both fail. If the unflagged cell has a published repair procedure whose `review_status` is still `DRAFT`, it is `DEGRADED`. The same underlying data then reads `0.00` on the effective basis and `50.00` on the declared basis.

The coverage read accepts an optional `basis` query parameter whose accepted values are `EFFECTIVE` and `DECLARED`, defaulting to `EFFECTIVE`, and answers with `shop_id`, the echoed `basis`, `total_cells`, `covered_cells`, `degraded_cells`, `gap_cells`, `completeness_pct` and a `gaps` array. The `gaps` array lists every cell whose classification is not `COVERED` — degraded cells as well as outright gaps — each entry carrying `vehicle_model_id`, `model_code`, `system_area_id`, `system_area_code` and `cell_state`, where `cell_state` is `COVERED`, `DEGRADED` or `GAP`. Gap entries are ordered by ascending `model_code` and then by ascending system-area `code`; no identifier ever enters the ordering, so the report reads the way a human catalogue would.

The effective-basis value is additionally **stored** on the shop as `coverage_completeness_pct`, and that stored figure is recomputed at every point where the inputs could have moved: at every entitlement status change — activation, cancellation and the lapse cascade alike — and at every version validation, publication and recall touching a covered cell. The stored figure is always the effective-basis number; the declared-basis number is report-only and is never persisted.

Finally, the audit trail described in §2.10 is readable by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` and by no one else, since it is the compliance record rather than an operational feed. It narrows by `subject_entity`, by `subject_id`, by `actor_user_id` and by `event_type`, which together let a compliance review reconstruct either everything that happened to one vehicle-gateway session or everything one operator did across the platform.
