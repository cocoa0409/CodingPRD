# Imaging Facility Accreditation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Imaging providers live or die by their accreditation standing. A hospital MRI suite, a free-standing imaging center, or a mobile mammography coach cannot bill most payers unless a recognized accrediting body has certified that the facility's equipment performs to specification, that its images meet published acceptance criteria, and that the people operating and interpreting those images hold current credentials. Accreditation is therefore not a single event but a continuous compliance posture, renewed on a fixed cycle, monitored through periodic physics surveys and phantom testing, and subject to unannounced site audits.

This document specifies the API that an accrediting body exposes so that its constituent facilities can drive that posture programmatically. The API spans the full compliance journey: enrolling a facility and its modality units, commissioning and retiring equipment, credentialing interpreting physicians, technologists and medical physicists against continuing-education requirements, submitting clinical image sets for review, acquiring and scoring phantom images against spatial resolution, contrast and artifact criteria, filing medical physicist survey reports covering dose measurement and shielding, answering deficiencies through corrective action plans, reporting dose indices to a national registry for benchmark comparison, and finally renewing — or losing — accreditation for the next cycle.

Two programmatic guarantees underpin everything that follows. First, all deadlines, expiries and testing windows are evaluated against a logical clock that starts fixed at the instant `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`; the server never reads wall-clock time, so every temporal rule is deterministic and reproducible. Second, the entire resource graph is mounted under the `/accreditation` prefix, so facilities, units, equipment, image submissions, phantom tests, physicist surveys, personnel qualifications, corrective action plans, accreditation cycles and dose registry records are all addressable from a single namespace.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply uniformly to every resource and every operation in the API. Where a subsection pins down a contract — an identifier format, a rounding rule, a header name — implementations must treat it as binding, since downstream automated conformance checks assert these contracts directly.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operational capacity in which that principal acts. A request that omits either header, or that supplies a role value not among the declared roles, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` at HTTP 401. Header presence alone is never sufficient: when the acting role is not listed among the roles authorized for the target operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403. These two checks are the first gates every request passes, ahead of any resource lookup.

Five role names are recognized, spelled exactly as follows: `FacilityAdmin`, `MedicalPhysicist`, `AccreditationBody`, `Any`, and `Anonymous`.

`FacilityAdmin` is the identity of an accredited facility itself. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Facility` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. This is the role that enrolls units, commissions equipment, files clinical image submissions and phantom tests, stages dose registry records, responds to deficiencies with corrective action plans, and purchases registry credit. The API additionally tolerates an operator principal whose `X-User-ID` is not equal to any facility's `id` for cross-facility administrative plumbing such as commissioning equipment or staging registry records, but every facility-scoped lifecycle action is judged against the facility named in the request.

`MedicalPhysicist` acts as a credentialed individual rather than an organization. When `X-User-Role` is `MedicalPhysicist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PersonnelQualification`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `PersonnelQualification` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Because a survey is signed by the physicist who performed it, the survey's `physicist_qualification_id` must agree with `X-User-ID` for scheduling, submitting and resubmitting work to proceed under this role.

`AccreditationBody` is the accrediting organization acting as a reviewer. When `X-User-Role` is `AccreditationBody`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. Reviewers decide image review outcomes, accept or reject physicist surveys, void phantom acquisitions, credential and revoke personnel, adjudicate corrective action plans, open and activate accreditation cycles, reject submitted registry records, and drive the logical clock. Two further roles round out the matrix. `Any` denotes any authenticated caller of any role and exists only so that read operations, the facility summary, and the logical clock can be reached without pinning a specific role; like `AccreditationBody` its principal is opaque. `Anonymous` denotes a request that carries no usable `X-User-Role` header at all and appears only in negative scenarios that must surface `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.

### 2.1.1. Separation of Duties

The five roles are partitioned so that no single party can both produce evidence and pass judgment on it. A facility is never permitted to credential or revoke its own personnel, adjudicate its own corrective action plans, review its own clinical image submissions, accept or reject its own physicist surveys, void its own phantom acquisitions, open, activate or renew its own accreditation cycles, or reject its own submitted registry records. Conversely, the accrediting body is never permitted to enroll a unit, commission, retire or take equipment out of service, file a clinical image submission, record or score a phantom test, submit or resubmit a physicist survey, stage or submit a dose registry record, respond to a deficiency, or purchase registry credit on a facility's behalf. The sole cross-cutting authority is the mechanical one: purchasing registry credit and every read operation are reachable by both the facility that owns the data and the body that oversees it.

This division is enforced as a role-permission check, not as a data-visibility rule. Two consequences follow that are easy to get wrong. Because the accrediting body's principal is opaque, there is no facility whose `id` it could match against, so its latitude is defined entirely by the operations it is authorized for rather than by ownership of data. And because a `FacilityAdmin`'s principal does name a facility, the facility-scoped actions it may perform are those on the facility it represents; the API additionally tolerates an operator principal whose identifier matches no facility for cross-facility plumbing such as commissioning equipment or staging registry records, but the identity checks on facility-scoped lifecycle actions still resolve against the facility named in the request.

One boundary in this partition is worth calling out explicitly: a credential lapse is observed rather than asserted. A facility cannot cause its own accredited personnel to lapse, nor can it cause a lapsed credential to be re-verified; both movements of the credential lifecycle are the accrediting body's to perform.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List operations are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and it must fall within the inclusive range of 1 to 100. `offset` states how many leading items to skip and defaults to 0. Both parameters travel as ordinary query-string values.

Unless a resource has its own ordering rule, list results are returned sorted by creation timestamp ascending. The pagination block is reported back inside `meta`: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the total number of matching rows before paging), and `has_more` (true when further rows remain).

### 2.4. Accreditation Posture and Payer Standing

Three derived values on a facility summarize where that facility stands in the program, and they are recomputed as a consequence of the operations described in §3 rather than set by hand. None of them is ever accepted as a request input.

The `facility_status` lifecycle value is `PENDING` at creation, moves to `ENROLLED` when the facility registers, falls to `SUSPENDED` when the accrediting body suspends it, and returns to `PENDING` when it is reinstated onto the enrollment path; a facility whose renewal is rejected additionally falls to `DEFERRED`. `accreditation_currency` is a compound posture distilled from the facility's children and cycles; it reads exactly one of `PROVISIONAL`, `CURRENT`, `LAPSED`, or `SUSPENDED`. A freshly created facility and a newly registered facility are `PROVISIONAL`, and so is a facility whose cycle is activated, whose survey review lands as `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED`, or whose survey is rejected out of `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED`; a facility whose most recent physicist survey has been accepted is `CURRENT`; suspension forces `SUSPENDED`; and a rejected renewal forces `LAPSED`.

`payer_participation_status` reads exactly one of `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK`, or `REVOKED` and reflects whether payers will honor the facility's claims. It is `AT_RISK` at facility creation and again whenever a deficiency, a failure, a corrective action plan reaches its deadline and expires, a lapsed qualification or a failed phantom test puts the facility's compliance in question. It becomes `ACTIVE` when registration completes, and again when the last open deficiency across the whole facility is cleared. Revocation is reserved for suspension.

A fourth posture value, `roster_disposition`, summarizes the health of the facility's credentialed personnel and reads exactly one of `CLEAR`, `HOLD`, or `HALT`. It is derived by the roster deficiency aggregation described in §3.3 and is never supplied by a client. Three roll-up counters accompany these postures: `enrolled_unit_count` counts the facility's modality units whose `unit_status` is `ENROLLED`, `deferred_unit_count` counts those whose `unit_status` is `DEFERRED`, and `rejected_survey_count` counts the facility's physicist surveys in `REJECTED` status.

### 2.5. Hierarchical Consistency and Cascade Rules

The resource graph is deliberately flat in its addressing but deeply coupled in its semantics: units roll up to facilities, equipment, image submissions, phantom tests and dose registry records hang off units, and personnel qualifications, physicist surveys, corrective action plans and accreditation cycles hang off facilities. Whenever an operation changes the compliance posture of a node, the system propagates the consequence to that node's dependents without waiting for a separate client request.

Three propagation patterns recur throughout. Suspension is the broadest: deferring a facility defers its enrolled units, and each deferred unit pushes its equipment `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and voids any phantom acquisition that has not yet been scored. Withdrawal is terminal for a unit and retires its equipment. Lapse is terminal for a cycle and cascades across the entire facility — every unit defers, every item of equipment goes out of service, every unscored phantom acquisition is voided, every corrective action plan expires, and every still-scheduled physicist survey is rejected with `rejection_code` `DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. Deferral of a single unit is a narrower cascade carrying the same shape: it pushes that unit's `ACTIVE` equipment out of service, expires every corrective action plan of the owning facility, and voids the unit's still-`ACQUIRED` phantom acquisitions.

Every one of these cascades is a mutation of the parent or child resource it touches. Each affected resource has its `updated_at` refreshed, and each affected resource's `version` advances by exactly one per mutation, so a client holding a pre-cascade `version` will observe a mismatch on its next write. Counters such as `enrolled_unit_count`, `deferred_unit_count`, `deficiency_open_count`, `phantom_attempt_count`, `phantom_failure_count`, `rejected_survey_count`, `survey_count` and `resubmission_attempts` are always recomputed from their definition rather than incremented blindly, so replaying a recomputation never drifts.

### 2.6. Deadline and Window Governance

Accreditation is governed by dates, and the API treats them as first-class state. Each modality unit carries two computed deadlines: `clinical_deadline_at`, the instant by which an outstanding clinical image submission must be accepted, and `testing_deadline_at`, the instant by which outstanding phantom testing must pass. Neither is directly writable; both move only as side effects of reviews, failed phantom scores, and renewal-window extensions. The `clinical_deadline_at` moves not only when a review lands as `ACCEPT` but also when one lands as `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY`, where it is raised to whichever is later of its current value and the submission's new `deficiency_deadline_at`; the `testing_deadline_at` moves when a phantom score lands as `FAIL` and when the renewal window opens. A first submission received after the unit's `clinical_deadline_at`, or a phantom acquisition recorded after its `testing_deadline_at`, is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the comparison is strict, so an instant equal to the deadline is still inside the window, and the clinical check applies to any filing made through the creation path — including one that names a `resubmission_of_submission_id` — while the dedicated resubmission action does not re-apply it.

Corrective action plans carry their own trio of dates. `due_at` is the instant the facility's written response is due, `resubmission_deadline_at` is the instant by which the answering clinical image resubmission must arrive, and `deadline_status` classifies how close the plan is to its `due_at` instant, reading exactly one of `ON_TRACK`, `DUE_SOON`, or `OVERDUE`. The classification is produced by the corrective action deadline rule in §3.4 and is re-derived on every re-read of a plan, not only at creation.

A facility that has drifted out of date loses privileges. If any corrective action plan of the acting facility carries `deadline_status` `OVERDUE`, then submitting a plan response, creating an answering resubmission, opening the renewal window and completing a renewal are all refused with `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE`. Independently, if the facility still holds any corrective action plan in `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` status, then facility registration, renewal-window opening and equipment retirement are refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_PENDING`.

### 2.7. The Registry Credit Ledger

`registry_credit_cents` is a prepaid balance, denominated in whole cents, that a facility spends to interact with the national dose registry and to open renewal windows. It can never be negative. It is topped up through a business adjustment operation that adds the requested `amount_cents` to the stored balance, and it moves otherwise only as a consequence of the workflows in §3.7 and §3.6: submitting a registry record debits `2500`, starting a renewal window debits `75000`, and resubmitting a corrective-required physicist survey debits `100000`. Symmetrically, three events credit the balance back: a survey rejected by the accrediting body for a credential lapse credits `100000`, a rejected renewal credits `75000`, and a rejected registry record credits `2500`. Every operation that spends from the balance is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGISTRY_CREDIT` at HTTP 402 when the stored balance does not cover the debit in question; the three thresholds are therefore `2500` for a registry record submission, `75000` for a renewal-window opening, and `100000` for a survey resubmission. Individual registry records also expose a per-record `registry_fee_cents` value which is `0` while the record is staged, becomes `2500` once it is accepted into the registry, and returns to `0` if it is later rejected.

### 2.8. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in the API — `Facility`, `ModalityUnit`, `EquipmentItem`, `ClinicalImageSubmission`, `PhantomTest`, `PhysicistSurvey`, `PersonnelQualification`, `CorrectiveActionPlan`, `AccreditationCycle` and `DoseRegistryRecord` — carries a standard set of server-managed properties.

- `id` (string): the resource's unique identifier, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix or namespace embedded in it.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the resource's most recent mutation.
- `version` (integer): a server-managed counter that starts at 1 and advances by one on every mutation of the resource, including mutations produced by cascades rather than by direct client writes.

All ten of these entities are declared with full common fields, so `updated_at` and `version` are present on every one of them; there is no entity in this API that omits either.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Scores, deviations and benchmark values flow through the compliance decisions in this API, so their precision is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail.

Compliance decisions are made against stored values at two-decimal precision: the composite phantom score is resolved at scoring time and the rounded figure, not the full-precision mean, is what the pass/fail verdict compares; likewise a survey's dose deviation is rounded at submission time, and the acceptance gate, the rejection gate and the corrective-required classification all read back the rounded `dose_deviation_pct` rather than recomputing it, so a survey stored at a deviation of at most `10.0` is inside the band even where the raw arithmetic would fall outside it.

The composite phantom score is stored with exactly 2 decimal places, produced by rounding half up. The pass or fail verdict is decided by comparing the rounded value against its threshold, not the full-precision intermediate. Survey dose deviation percentages are likewise reported with 2 decimal places rounded half up, and band membership is judged against the rounded value. Registry benchmark ratios are computed at full precision and compared against their constants at that precision, while the `dose_index_value` and the `benchmark_comparison_value` they consume are each carried at 2 decimal places. The unit-and-category benchmark average is retained at full precision internally but reported with 6 significant digits. Whole-day quantities, such as the count of days remaining before a corrective action plan falls due, are integer counts with no rounding applied at the boundary.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this API are protected against clients acting on stale observations of their parent aggregate. Each is identified by the parent entity it guards, and each requires the client to echo back the parent's last observed `version` in a named field.

Enrolling a modality unit into a facility is guarded by the facility. The request that creates a new unit for a facility must carry `expected_facility_version`, being the client's last observed `version` of the target facility. Creating a clinical image submission — whether a first submission or the resubmission that answers a deficiency — is guarded by the unit. That request must carry `expected_unit_version`, being the client's last observed `version` of the target unit. These two writes are the operations on which the concurrency contract is anchored, and in both the version field is mandatory rather than optional.

The same stale-version rejection governs the wider set of facility-scoped and unit-scoped writes. Registration, suspension and reinstatement of a facility each carry `expected_facility_version` because each mutates the facility aggregate. Withdrawal of a unit from accreditation, and the three equipment lifecycle actions — taking an item out of service, returning it to service, and retiring it — each carry `expected_unit_version` because each mutates the owning unit. Voiding a phantom acquisition likewise accepts `expected_unit_version` against the unit the acquisition was recorded against, though on that path the field is optional; the four unit-scoped lifecycle actions named here require it. Purchasing registry credit carries `expected_facility_version` for the same reason. Whenever a supplied `expected_facility_version` or `expected_unit_version` does not equal the persisted `version` of the parent entity it names, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, protecting the client from overwriting a facility or unit aggregate it has not observed. Endpoints outside this set mutate only the resource they address and take no version-echo field at all.

### 2.11. Nested Resource Retrieval and Referential Integrity

Where a request path carries more than one resource identifier — for instance an accreditation cycle scoped to a facility, or a corrective action plan scoped to a facility — the identifiers are resolved left to right, and the request fails at the first one that does not resolve, with the not-found error dedicated to that identifier's type. A path that names a facility and then a cycle belonging to a different facility therefore reports the cycle as missing rather than the facility.

The same discipline applies to identifiers supplied in a body rather than a path. When a child resource is created against a parent named in its payload, the parent is resolved before any business rule is evaluated: a modality unit, physicist survey, personnel qualification or accreditation cycle that names a facility which does not exist fails with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`; an equipment item, clinical image submission, phantom test or dose registry record that names a unit which does not exist — or that belongs to a different facility than the one named — fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`. Optional cross-references are validated on the same footing: creating a clinical image submission whose `resubmission_of_submission_id` does not resolve fails with `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, a physicist survey whose `physicist_qualification_id` does not resolve fails with `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`, and an accreditation cycle whose `prior_cycle_id` does not resolve fails with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`.

Two request-shape rules round out the global contract. Any POST operation whose body carries an enum-typed field outside that field's declared member set, or which omits a required body field, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 422; a malformed JSON body is treated as an empty body, so it surfaces as the same missing-field refusal rather than as a distinct error. And every action endpoint refuses a target that is not in a state from which the requested transition is declared, with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409, or — when the target has already reached one of its terminal states — with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, also at HTTP 409.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

Many operations are guarded by several checks at once, and the order in which those checks fire is itself part of the contract: a caller whose request would fail two rules must observe the higher-priority error. The precedence ladder runs from broadest to narrowest, and an operation never evaluates a later rung until every earlier rung has been satisfied. Read in the other direction, the rung at which a rule sits bounds when its error can surface: an operation surfaces at most the errors declared for the rungs it reaches, so a check that would fire at a lower rung is not observable once a higher-rung check has already refused the request. Two checks that share a rung resolve to whichever of them the operation tests first, and likewise whenever a body field is missing, malformed or fixed by the shape of the request itself, that intrinsic refusal is what surfaces rather than a content-dependent one.

Authentication is evaluated first, always. A request missing `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role`, or naming an undeclared role, fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before anything else is examined; a request whose role is valid but not permitted on the target operation fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` next. Only once both pass does the system resolve the resources the request names.

Resource resolution occupies the next rung, and within it the not-found errors are terminal: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHANTOM_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` are all raised before any rule that depends on the resource's contents is considered. A request naming a nonexistent plan therefore reports the missing plan rather than complaining about the plan's status.

State rules come next. The system first confirms the target is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, raising `ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise, and confirms it has not already reached a terminal state, raising `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Only then are the contextual guards evaluated: `ERR_UNIT_DEFERRED` when the owning unit is not `ENROLLED`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` when the acting physicist's qualification has been revoked, `ERR_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED` when the owning facility's accreditation currency has lapsed or been suspended, and finally the stale-version rejection `ERR_STALE_VERSION` described in §2.10.

Business safety rules form the widest of the later rungs and are evaluated together as a group: `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE`, `ERR_DEFICIENCY_PENDING`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED`, `ERR_SURVEY_SHORTFALL`, `ERR_ROSTER_INELIGIBLE`, `ERR_CE_HOURS_SHORTFALL` and `ERR_SHIELDING_FAILED`. Structural business constraints — the uniqueness and windowing rules such as `ERR_UNIT_LABEL_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_PHANTOM_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_PHANTOM_TEST_CONFLICT`, `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_DOSE_RECORD_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_CYCLE_INDEX_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_INVALID_VIEW_COUNT` and `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — are checked after the safety group. Temporal rules follow, covering `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` and `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; then the referential rules `ERR_CAUSAL_ORDER`, `ERR_RESUBMISSION_CHAIN` and `ERR_PHANTOM_UNSCORED`; and finally the fiscal rule `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGISTRY_CREDIT`, which is evaluated last of all so that a caller is never told about a funding shortfall before being told their request was structurally invalid.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrollment: Facilities, Modality Units and Equipment

Everything in this API hangs off a facility. A facility is created with its legal `name`, the two-letter `state_code` in which it operates, and a `facility_type` of `HOSPITAL`, `IMAGING_CENTER` or `MOBILE_UNIT`. It comes into existence in `PENDING` status with `accreditation_currency` `PROVISIONAL` and `payer_participation_status` `AT_RISK` — a new facility has proven nothing yet. Its derived counters all start at zero: `registry_credit_cents`, `enrolled_unit_count`, `deferred_unit_count`, `current_cycle_index`, `rejected_survey_count`, and its `roster_disposition` is `CLEAR` because no one has been credentialed yet. Facilities can be listed with filters on `state_code`, `facility_type` and `facility_status`.

Units come next. A modality unit is enrolled against its owning facility by supplying the `facility_id`, a `modality_code` of `MRI`, `CT`, `MAMM`, `PET` or `US`, and an operator-facing `unit_label` that must be unique among that facility's units — a duplicate label is refused with `ERR_UNIT_LABEL_DUPLICATE`. Because enrolling a unit changes the facility aggregate, the request must carry `expected_facility_version`, and a facility version that has moved on is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. A new unit begins in `ENROLLED` status with `phantom_attempt_count`, `phantom_failure_count` and `deficiency_open_count` all at zero, and its two deadline fields `clinical_deadline_at` and `testing_deadline_at` unset until the workflows of §3.3 and §3.4 set them — opening a renewal window extends a deadline that exists but never materializes one that does not, so it leaves an unset field unset rather than carrying a null forward. Enrolling also recomputes the owning facility's `enrolled_unit_count`, so the facility's roll-up always reflects the units it actually holds. Units may be listed by `facility_id`, `modality_code` or `unit_status`.

Equipment is commissioned onto a unit rather than a facility, which is why the request names a `unit_id`. An item is described by its `manufacturer`, `model_number`, `serial_number`, `modality_code` and `installed_on` date. The `serial_number` must be unique among the facility's equipment, with `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE` raised on collision. Two fields of the equipment record — `equipment_status` and `performance_verdict` — are server-managed and are not client inputs. A caller may include them in a commissioning payload, but the values are ignored: a new item always starts with `equipment_status` `ACTIVE` and `performance_verdict` `PENDING`, and thereafter only the equipment lifecycle and accepted physicist surveys move them. Equipment may be listed by `unit_id`, `serial_number` or `equipment_status`.

Registration is the act that turns a pending facility into an enrolled one, and it is deliberately hard. It applies only to a facility still `PENDING`; a facility already `ENROLLED`, `DEFERRED` or `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Three conditions must all hold: at least one modality unit must exist for the facility, every one of that facility's units must be in `ENROLLED` status, and every equipment item of that facility must be `ACTIVE`. A facility that has no units, that has deferred a unit, or that has taken an item out of service is not ready, and each of those failures is likewise reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Registration is further refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_PENDING` when the facility still holds any corrective action plan in `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` status, and with `ERR_SURVEY_SHORTFALL` when the facility's roster aggregation is `HALT` or any of its qualifications is `REVOKED`. Because it mutates the facility aggregate it must carry `expected_facility_version`. A successful registration moves `facility_status` to `ENROLLED`, sets `accreditation_currency` to `PROVISIONAL`, promotes `payer_participation_status` to `ACTIVE` — the facility can now be reimbursed — recomputes `enrolled_unit_count`, and resets `rejected_survey_count` to `0`.

Suspension is the accrediting body's instrument for a facility that has fallen out of compliance. It applies only to a facility currently `ENROLLED`, and it requires only that at least one unit of the facility be in `ENROLLED` or `DEFERRED` status; a facility failing either gate draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It must carry `expected_facility_version`. Suspension moves `facility_status` to `SUSPENDED` and `accreditation_currency` to `SUSPENDED`, revokes payer participation by setting `payer_participation_status` to `REVOKED`, and defers every enrolled unit. Each deferred unit in turn pushes its equipment `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and voids any phantom acquisition that was still `ACQUIRED`; the facility's `enrolled_unit_count` and `deferred_unit_count` are recomputed afterwards. Reinstatement reverses the facility's status without undoing the deferrals: it applies only to a facility that is `SUSPENDED` or `DEFERRED`, requires that every corrective action plan of the facility be in `ACCEPTED` or `EXPIRED` status — an unresolved plan draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — moves `facility_status` back to `PENDING` — back onto the enrollment path, not straight to `ENROLLED` — sets `payer_participation_status` to `AT_RISK`, and resets `rejected_survey_count` to `0`. It too carries `expected_facility_version`.

Units leave the program in one of two ways. The system defers a unit on its own when the owning facility's `facility_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` or `DEFERRED`, or when an accreditation cycle of the facility is rejected — that deferral is not a client action and cannot be invoked directly. Each deferred unit pushes its equipment `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, expires every corrective action plan of its facility, voids the unit's still-`ACQUIRED` phantom acquisitions, and causes the facility's two unit counters to be recomputed. Withdrawal is the client-driven exit and is terminal. It applies only to a unit still `ENROLLED` — one already `WITHDRAWN` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and one `DEFERRED` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It is permitted only when no clinical image submission of the unit is sitting in `PENDING_REVIEW`, no phantom test of the unit is sitting in `ACQUIRED`, and no corrective action plan of the facility that references the unit is in `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` — a unit cannot be pulled out while unresolved work depends on it, and each of those three obstacles is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Withdrawal moves the unit to `WITHDRAWN`, retires every equipment item of that unit, recomputes the facility's `enrolled_unit_count`, and, because it mutates the unit aggregate, carries `expected_unit_version`.

Equipment transitions are narrower but equally rule-bound. Taking an item out of service applies only to one currently `ACTIVE`, and is permitted only while no phantom test of the owning unit is in `ACQUIRED` status — you may not strand an acquisition mid-window — and it moves the item to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` while deliberately leaving the owning unit's `clinical_deadline_at` and `testing_deadline_at` untouched, since taking a machine down does not by itself extend a testing window. Returning an out-of-service item to service is gated on evidence that the problem was investigated: the owning unit must already have at least one physicist survey in `ACCEPTED` status whose `unit_id` equals that unit. A successful return sets the item back to `ACTIVE` and resets its `performance_verdict` to `PENDING`, discarding the prior verdict because the equipment's condition is once again unproven. Retirement is permanent and is permitted only when the owning unit still has at least one other equipment item in `ACTIVE` status, so a unit can never be left with no serviceable equipment; retirement moves the item to `RETIRED` and freezes its `performance_verdict` at whatever value it held, preserving the last known verdict as a historical record. Retirement is additionally refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_PENDING` when the facility holds an `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` corrective action plan. All three transitions require the item to be in the status they act on: an already-`RETIRED` item draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any other status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A fourth transition is system-driven and is the deferral path described above: when a unit is deferred, each of its `ACTIVE` items follows to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, every phantom test of that unit still in `ACQUIRED` status is voided, and the unit's `phantom_attempt_count` is recomputed so the voided tests drop out. All three client-driven equipment actions mutate the owning unit and therefore carry `expected_unit_version`; commissioning an item, by contrast, leaves the unit serveable and takes no version-echo field.

### 3.2. Opening and Activating an Accreditation Cycle

Accreditation is administered in cycles, and cycles precede everything else a facility does — the very first cycle gives the facility something to be accredited against. A cycle is opened against a `facility_id` with a `cycle_index` that counts monotonically within that facility starting at `1`, a `cycle_started_at` instant, and an optional `prior_cycle_id` naming the cycle it renews. The `cycle_index` must be unique among the facility's cycles, with `ERR_CYCLE_INDEX_DUPLICATE` raised on a collision. A newly opened cycle sits in `PENDING` status with `cycle_expires_at` already computed as `cycle_started_at` plus `1095` days — three years — and with `audit_selection_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `audit_outcome` `PENDING` and `benchmark_submission_status` `NOT_STARTED`, none of which is meaningful until the cycle runs. Its `unit_count_at_start` is reported as `0` until activation freezes the real scope. The cycle's `cycle_index` is written onto the facility as `current_cycle_index` at the moment it opens, so the newest cycle opened — even while still `PENDING` — is the one survey windows are judged against. Cycles may be listed by `facility_id`, `prior_cycle_id` or `cycle_status`.

Activation is what puts a cycle into force. It requires the owning facility to be `ENROLLED`, and it cannot be applied to a cycle that has already reached one of its terminal states, which draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; a cycle not still `PENDING` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Three things happen at activation. The cycle's `unit_count_at_start` is frozen as the number of the facility's modality units then in `ENROLLED` status — a snapshot of scope taken at the moment accreditation begins, so later enrollment changes do not silently enlarge the cycle it governs. The cycle's `cycle_expires_at` is reasserted as `cycle_started_at` plus `1095` days. And the cycle moves to `ACTIVE`. Three facility- and sibling-level consequences follow: the cycle's `cycle_index` is written onto the facility as `current_cycle_index`, which is how the facility designates the cycle that physicist survey windows and renewal operations are judged against, the facility's `accreditation_currency` drops back to `PROVISIONAL` because a new cycle resets the facility to a posture it must re-earn, and every cycle of the facility with a lower `cycle_index` that has not already reached a terminal state is closed as `RENEWED`, so a successor cycle does not leave older cycles dangling. The window extensions, the random site audit draw, the renewal decision and the lapse cascade that a cycle ultimately produces are all described in §3.6.

### 3.3. Clinical Image Review and the Deficiency Cycle

Clinical image submissions are the facility's principal evidence that it can acquire diagnostic-quality studies, and they are filed against a specific unit. A submission carries the owning `unit_id`, an `image_type` of either `CLINICAL` or `PHANTOM_REFERENCE`, the `submitted_at` instant, a freeform `reviewer_note_code`, the `required_view_count` that the acceptance criteria demand, and the `accepted_view_count` that actually satisfy them. It begins life in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `deficiency_code` set to `NONE` and `resubmission_round` at `0`, and its `review_outcome_score`, `deficiency_issued_at`, `deficiency_deadline_at` and `corrective_action_plan_id` stay null until a review outcome lands. The `accepted_view_count` must fall within the closed range from `0` up to `required_view_count`, and a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_VIEW_COUNT` — a non-integral value supplied for either view count is instead refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Filing a submission is refused when the owning facility's `accreditation_currency` is `LAPSED` or `SUSPENDED`, with `ERR_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED`; when the owning unit is not `ENROLLED`, with `ERR_UNIT_DEFERRED`, which is tested first; and when the unit's `clinical_deadline_at` has already passed, with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because filing mutates the unit aggregate, it must carry `expected_unit_version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. A submission that has already reached one of its terminal states — `ACCEPTED`, `FAILED` or `RESUBMITTED` — cannot be reviewed again and draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; a submission that is not `PENDING_REVIEW` without being terminal draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Submissions can be listed by `unit_id`, `review_status`, or the `resubmission_of_submission_id` they answer.

A review request may also be refused outright, before any scoring, when it asks for an acceptance the evidence does not support: a request carrying `review_outcome` `ACCEPT` while `accepted_view_count` is below `required_view_count` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VIEW_COUNT`, the same code that guards an out-of-range view count at filing time.

The review outcome is computed by the clinical review outcome rule, which runs whenever a submission leaves `PENDING_REVIEW`. It first computes coverage, being the accepted view count divided by the required view count. It then selects the outcome in a fixed order of precedence. If the reviewer explicitly reported a `review_outcome` of `FAIL`, the outcome is `FAIL` with `deficiency_code` `IMAGE_QUALITY` and a `review_outcome_score` of `0.0`. Otherwise, if coverage is below `0.80`, the outcome is likewise `FAIL` with `IMAGE_QUALITY` and a score of `0.0`. Otherwise, if coverage is below `1.0` but not below `0.80`, the outcome is `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY`, the `deficiency_code` comes from the `reviewer_deficiency_category` the reviewer supplied, and the score is coverage multiplied by ten. Otherwise — coverage of exactly `1.0` or greater — the outcome is `ACCEPT` with `NONE` and the ceiling score of `10.0`. The score is stored to 2 decimal places rounded half up, while coverage itself is computed at full precision and only then compared against the `0.80` boundary.

This rule has two variants worth stating plainly. The reviewer-reported variant fires only when the request supplies `review_outcome` as `FAIL`, and it forces the `FAIL` triple regardless of how good coverage is; it is only reachable when `accepted_view_count` is `0`. The coverage-derived variant fires when the request omits `review_outcome` altogether, and it derives the outcome purely from coverage — which means an image set whose `accepted_view_count` equals `required_view_count` reviews as `ACCEPT` even if the reviewer privately intended a deficiency. A third input exists for callers that state the middle outcome outright: a request supplying `review_outcome` as `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY` takes the deficiency path directly, with the `deficiency_code` drawn from the reviewer's `reviewer_deficiency_category` and the score still the coverage score, whether or not coverage would independently have chosen that outcome. A `review_outcome` field that is present but outside those three member values draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as does a `reviewer_deficiency_category` outside the four deficiency codes.

An accepted review is gated by two safety conditions in addition to the coverage arithmetic. The facility must have no open corrective action plan whose `deadline_status` is `OVERDUE`, and every personnel qualification of the facility whose `personnel_role` is `INTERPRETING_PHYSICIAN` or `TECHNOLOGIST` must be `VERIFIED` — the applicable refusal being `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED`, which is tested ahead of the overdue-plan check. When the review lands, the submission's `review_outcome_score` is computed, its `deficiency_code` is set to `NONE` with `deficiency_issued_at` and `deficiency_deadline_at` cleared back to null, and the owning unit's `clinical_deadline_at` is pushed to `submitted_at` plus `547` days, i.e. eighteen months from the date the image set was submitted, replacing whatever value it held rather than extending it. Every corrective action plan of the facility sitting in `SUBMITTED` is accepted — those whose source submission belongs to that unit as well as those whose answering resubmission round already contains an accepted clinical image submission. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed, and if it falls to zero while every other unit of the facility is also at zero, the facility's `payer_participation_status` is restored to `ACTIVE`.

A review returning `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY` is a narrower gate. It requires that `accepted_view_count` be strictly below `required_view_count`, that it also be strictly above `0` — a submission with no acceptable views fails rather than drawing a deficiency — and that the facility hold at least one `MEDICAL_PHYSICIST` qualification in `VERIFIED` status; a facility without one draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When those hold, the submission moves to `DEFICIENCY`, its `deficiency_code` is taken from the reviewer's `reviewer_deficiency_category`, its `review_outcome_score` is the coverage score described above, and both `deficiency_issued_at` and `deficiency_deadline_at` are stamped — the issued instant is the submission's own `submitted_at`, and the deadline is that instant plus `45` days, rendered in the same precision as the instant it derives from. A corrective action plan is opened to answer the deficiency, in `OPEN` status, carrying the same `deficiency_code`, naming this submission as its `source_submission_id`, with `due_at` equal to the submission's `deficiency_deadline_at` and `resubmission_deadline_at` equal to that same `due_at`. The new plan's identifier is written back onto the submission as `corrective_action_plan_id`.

The deficiency outcome also has facility-wide reach. Every corrective action plan of the facility already in `SUBMITTED` with a null `resubmission_deadline_at` gains one, set to `submitted_at` plus `45` days. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed. The unit's `clinical_deadline_at` is raised to whichever is later of its current value and the submission's new `deficiency_deadline_at`, so the window never shortens — and a unit whose deadline had never been set takes the new one. Every corrective action plan of the facility has its `deadline_status` re-derived. And the facility's `payer_participation_status` drops to `AT_RISK`.

A review returning `FAIL` is the harshest outcome and is reachable when the reviewer explicitly reports it, and only then when `accepted_view_count` exceeds `0` — this is the reviewer-reported variant of the clinical review outcome rule being exercised against its coverage-derived sibling — or when the coverage-derived arithmetic falls below the `0.80` threshold. The submission moves to `FAILED` with `deficiency_code` `IMAGE_QUALITY`, an `review_outcome_score` of `0.0`, `deficiency_issued_at` set to the submission's `submitted_at` and `deficiency_deadline_at` set to that instant plus `45` days. A corrective action plan is created exactly as in the deficiency case but with `deficiency_code` fixed at `IMAGE_QUALITY`, and the submission's `corrective_action_plan_id` carries the plan's identifier. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed and the facility's `payer_participation_status` drops to `AT_RISK`.

Answering a deficiency means filing a resubmission, which is the same filing action performed against the deficient submission rather than the unit. The request supplies `submitted_at`, `reviewer_note_code` and `accepted_view_count`, and must carry `expected_unit_version`; it takes no `image_type`, which is fixed at `CLINICAL`, and it inherits its parent's `required_view_count`. The parent submission must be in `DEFICIENCY`; a corrective action plan answering it must exist — naming that submission as its source and still in `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` — and a request with no such plan draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as does a submission that is neither `DEFICIENCY` nor `FAILED`; and that plan's `deadline_status` must be `ON_TRACK` or `DUE_SOON`, which is what stops a facility from answering a deficiency whose window has already run out, and whose violation is likewise reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `FAILED` submission answers through exactly the same action and under exactly the same preconditions, exchanging `DEFICIENCY` for `FAILED` in the first of them. The answer may not extend the chain past round `2`: a submission already at round `2` draws `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The effect in both cases is identical: a fresh clinical image submission is created in `PENDING_REVIEW` whose `image_type` is `CLINICAL`, whose `resubmission_of_submission_id` names the submission being answered, and whose `resubmission_round` is exactly one greater than its parent's. The parent moves to the terminal `RESUBMITTED`. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed, and the unit's aggregate `version` advances via `expected_unit_version`. A submission that has already been answered — one sitting in `RESUBMITTED` — draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

The resubmission chain is bounded. A submission may not be created with a `resubmission_round` greater than `2`, and a request that would breach this is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The chain itself is validated: the referenced `resubmission_of_submission_id` must name a submission of the same unit and one whose `resubmission_round` is exactly one less than the new submission's round, with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_CHAIN` raised otherwise — the same-unit and one-less checks being the ones the creation path actually performs. Creating an answering resubmission is also refused with `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE` when the facility holds any plan whose `deadline_status` is `OVERDUE`, and with `ERR_INVALID_VIEW_COUNT` when the supplied `accepted_view_count` falls outside the range from `0` up to the parent's `required_view_count`.

### 3.4. Phantom Acquisition and Scoring

Phantom testing is the technical counterweight to clinical review. Where a clinical image set proves the facility can produce diagnostic pictures, a phantom test proves the machine itself resolves detail, distinguishes contrast and suppresses artifact. A phantom test is recorded against a `unit_id` with a `phantom_serial_code` identifying the physical phantom, an `acquired_at` instant marking the start of the acquisition window, and a `duration_minutes` giving that window's length. The test begins in `ACQUIRED` status with `phantom_total_score` null, as do its three sub-scores, and `scored_at` null. Recording a test is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` when the unit's `testing_deadline_at` has already passed — a window boundary is strict, so an instant equal to the deadline is still inside it — and with `ERR_UNIT_DEFERRED` when the unit is not `ENROLLED`, which is tested first.

The window is exclusive and the conflict is checked at booking time. The new window, running from `acquired_at` up to but not including `acquired_at` plus `duration_minutes`, must be disjoint from every other phantom test of the same unit that is still in `ACQUIRED` status. Two windows that merely touch — where one begins at the exact instant another ends — are permitted, so the boundary convention is half-open on the right. An overlap is refused with `ERR_PHANTOM_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Tests can be listed by `unit_id` or `result_status`.

Recording a phantom test does not by itself file a clinical image. Where a facility also wants the phantom reference set on the record for image review, it files a separate clinical image submission whose `image_type` is `PHANTOM_REFERENCE` — the same filing path §3.3 describes, distinguished only by that `image_type` value from the ordinary `CLINICAL` sets. The two are deliberately separate records: the phantom test carries the machine's measured performance, while the reference image submission carries the pictures a reviewer may examine.

Scoring is where the numbers arrive. The scoring request supplies `spatial_resolution_score`, `contrast_score`, `artifact_score` and a `scored_at` instant, each sub-score being an integer somewhere in the closed range from `0` to `10`. Scoring requires all three to be present and integral — a null, non-integral or out-of-range sub-score is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a missing `scored_at`. It also re-verifies the window: the test being scored must still own its acquisition window, and no other phantom test of the unit in `ACQUIRED` status may overlap it, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_PHANTOM_TEST_CONFLICT`. Because a phantom test is filed against a specific acquisition window, the record also carries its own `phantom_serial_code` naming the physical phantom used, and that identifier is what distinguishes a re-scan on a second phantom from a duplicate booking on the first. A test that has already reached a terminal state cannot be scored again and draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and a test outside the `ACQUIRED` state draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Scoring is also refused with `ERR_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED` when the owning facility's accreditation has lapsed or been suspended, and with `ERR_UNIT_DEFERRED` when the owning unit is not `ENROLLED`.

Reading a phantom test back exposes `phantom_total_score` and `scored_at`, which stay null until the test has been scored and are the only two fields that distinguish a test awaiting scoring from one that has been measured.

The composite score is produced by the phantom composite score rule, which runs when a test moves out of `ACQUIRED`. It sums the spatial resolution, contrast and artifact sub-scores and divides that total by three — the arithmetic mean of the three sub-scores — and stores the result as `phantom_total_score` at 2 decimal places rounded half up. The verdict is then decided by comparing that rounded value against the pass threshold of `7.0`: a test whose `phantom_total_score` reaches or exceeds `7.0` moves to `SCORED`, while one whose rounded total falls below `7.0` moves to `FAIL`. A raw mean of exactly seven thirds rounds to `2.33` and fails; the rounded-then-compared behaviour is the one in force, which is why the `unrounded-then-compared` variant neither diverges on any input the two share nor changes a verdict.

A successful score stamps `scored_at` from the request, sets `phantom_total_score` from the rule, recomputes the owning unit's `phantom_attempt_count` as the number of that unit's tests in `SCORED`, `PASS` or `FAIL` status, and recomputes `phantom_failure_count` as the number in `FAIL`. The unit's `last_phantom_score` is set to the test's `phantom_total_score` — the score of the most recently scored test on that unit. On the `SCORED` path, that is all that happens. On the `FAIL` path the consequences are heavier: the unit's `testing_deadline_at` is pushed to `scored_at` plus `30` days, giving the facility a month to produce a passing test, and the facility's `payer_participation_status` drops to `AT_RISK`. In both paths the owning unit's aggregate `version` advances, and `phantom_attempt_count` counts only tests that actually produced a score — a test still in `ACQUIRED` is not counted. A unit whose `testing_deadline_at` is re-derived on a re-read while acquisitions of its own are still `ACQUIRED` treats those acquisitions as past their window: once the deadline has passed they are voided, they drop out of `phantom_attempt_count`, and the unit is marked as mutated, though the deadline itself is left where it stood.

Not every acquisition is scored. The accrediting body can void an acquisition outright — and so can the owning facility, the operation being reachable by both roles — which is permitted only while `result_status` is `ACQUIRED` and transitions the test to the terminal `VOIDED`, leaving `phantom_total_score` null; an acquisition already `SCORED`, `PASS`, `FAIL` or `VOIDED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and one not `ACQUIRED` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding recomputes the unit's `phantom_attempt_count` so that the voided test drops out of consideration. Voiding is the mechanism by which a superseded or improperly acquired window is retired without pretending it was ever measured.

### 3.5. Medical Physicist Surveys and Dose Oversight

A physicist survey is the periodic physics inspection that keeps a facility's accreditation honest. It is scheduled by a `MedicalPhysicist` against a `facility_id`, naming the `physicist_qualification_id` of the credentialed physicist who will perform it, a `survey_type` of `ANNUAL`, `INITIAL` or `POST_REPAIR`, a `scheduled_at` instant, and a `reference_entrance_dose_mgy` giving the protocol's baseline entrance dose. An optional `unit_id` narrows the survey to one modality unit; when it is omitted the survey is facility-wide. A scheduled survey begins in `SCHEDULED` status with `rejection_code` `NONE`, and its `performed_at`, `measured_entrance_dose_mgy`, `shielding_verdict` and `dose_deviation_pct` stay null until the survey is actually performed. Because the survey is signed by an individual, scheduling is refused when the named `physicist_qualification_id` does not resolve, with `ERR_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`.

Surveys obey a windowing discipline of their own. The new survey's window runs from the instant it names for the acquisition — the `performed_at` where one has been recorded, and the `scheduled_at` otherwise — for one day, and it must be disjoint from every other physicist survey of the same facility that is not `REJECTED`; additionally, that instant must be at or after the corresponding instant of every such survey. A survey that overlaps a live one, or that predates one already on file, is refused with `ERR_SURVEY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the conflict test tolerates two windows that merely touch. Naming a `unit_id` that does not resolve, or that belongs to a different facility than the one named, fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`. Surveys can be listed by `facility_id`, `physicist_qualification_id`, `unit_id` or `survey_status`.

Submitting findings is the physicist's act and supplies `performed_at`, `measured_entrance_dose_mgy` and `shielding_verdict`, the last being `PASS` or `FAIL`. It acts only on a survey still `SCHEDULED` — one already `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any other status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and it is honoured only for the signatory named by the survey: a `MedicalPhysicist` whose own qualification is not the survey's `physicist_qualification_id` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. `measured_entrance_dose_mgy` must be present. `shielding_verdict` must be present. The `performed_at` instant must fall at or after the `cycle_started_at` of the facility's current accreditation cycle — the cycle whose `cycle_index` equals the facility's `current_cycle_index` — so a survey cannot be backdated into a cycle that has already closed; when the facility holds no cycle matching that index the check has nothing to judge against and does not bite. And `performed_at` must fall strictly before the acting qualification's `credential_expires_on`, so an expired credential cannot sign off on a survey. Each of those four refusals is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Submission is additionally refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` when the acting qualification's `credential_status` is `REVOKED`, and with `ERR_ROSTER_INELIGIBLE` when the facility's `roster_disposition` is `HALT`, the revoked check being tested first.

The dose deviation rule runs when a survey leaves `SCHEDULED`, and again when a corrective-required survey is resubmitted. It computes the deviation as the measured entrance dose minus the reference entrance dose, divided by the reference entrance dose, multiplied by one hundred — a signed percentage. `reference_entrance_dose_mgy` must be strictly positive and `measured_entrance_dose_mgy` must be present for the computation to exist at all. The result is stored as `dose_deviation_pct` at 2 decimal places rounded half up. The rule then classifies the survey's dose weight, a value used by consequential decisions, by the magnitude of the deviation: when the absolute deviation is at most `5.0` percent the dose weight is `1.0`; otherwise, when the absolute deviation is at most `10.0` percent, the weight is `0.5`; otherwise the weight is `0.0`. Band membership is judged against the rounded deviation, which is why `5.0` exactly earns full credit and `10.0` exactly earns half credit. The rule's second variant matters for the registry benchmark report described in §3.7: where the accreditation body consumes the single band weight, the benchmark report consumes the arithmetic mean of the applicable band weights, so a survey inside the `10` percent band reports `0.75` rather than `0.5`.

A catastrophic first submission short-circuits the whole flow. If, at the moment findings are submitted, the acting qualification's `credential_status` is `LAPSED`, the survey moves straight to `REJECTED` with `rejection_code` `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` rather than to `SUBMITTED`. In that event the facility's `registry_credit_cents` is credited by `100000`, `dose_deviation_pct` is still computed by the dose deviation rule, and `rejected_survey_count` is recomputed. A survey that survives the lapse check moves to `SUBMITTED`, which is the staging point for the accrediting body's verdict. `performed_at`, `measured_entrance_dose_mgy`, `shielding_verdict` and `dose_deviation_pct` are written onto the record on both paths, so a survey rejected for a lapsed credential still reports the findings that were submitted with it.

The verdict splits on shielding and dose. Acceptance requires `shielding_verdict` to be `PASS`, the absolute `dose_deviation_pct` to be at most `10.0` percent, and the named qualification to be `VERIFIED`; the qualification gate is tested first, so a facility holding a clinical-role qualification that is not `VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED` before the shielding verdict is consulted. When all three hold, the survey moves to `ACCEPTED`; the named qualification's `survey_count` is recomputed as the number of surveys naming it whose status is not `REJECTED`; if the survey names a `unit_id`, every equipment item of that unit has its `performance_verdict` set to `PASS` and that unit's `last_survey_at` is set to the survey's `performed_at`; if instead the survey is facility-wide, every modality unit of the facility has `last_survey_at` set to the survey's `performed_at`; the named qualification is promoted back to `VERIFIED` when the absolute deviation is at most `8.0` percent; the facility's `rejected_survey_count` is reset to `0`; and the facility's `accreditation_currency` becomes `CURRENT`.

When either shielding fails or the absolute deviation exceeds `10.0` percent, the survey does not fail outright but moves instead to `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED`, meaning the facility has a fixable problem. The `rejection_code` records what went wrong: `DOSE_OUT_OF_RANGE` when the absolute deviation exceeds `10.0` percent, and `SHIELDING_FAILED` otherwise. The facility's `accreditation_currency` is reasserted as `PROVISIONAL` — a corrective-required survey never promotes a facility, and the assertion overwrites whatever currency the facility held. The `ERR_SHIELDING_FAILED` refusal guards the acceptance path itself, so a survey carrying `shielding_verdict` `FAIL` can never be silently accepted. Acceptance is likewise refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED` when a personnel qualification of the facility holding one of the clinical roles is not `VERIFIED`.

Correcting the problem means resubmitting. The physicist supplies a fresh `measured_entrance_dose_mgy` and `shielding_verdict`; the survey must currently be `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED`; `shielding_verdict` must now be `PASS`; the recomputed absolute deviation must be at most `10.0` percent; and the facility's `registry_credit_cents` must hold at least `100000`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGISTRY_CREDIT`. A survey that has already been accepted or rejected draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any other non-`CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED` status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a `shielding_verdict` that is not `PASS`, or a recomputed deviation past the `10.0` percent limit, is refused with `ERR_SHIELDING_FAILED`. As on the submission path, an acting qualification whose `credential_status` is `REVOKED` draws `ERR_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` and a facility whose `roster_disposition` is `HALT` draws `ERR_ROSTER_INELIGIBLE`. A successful resubmission debits `100000` from the facility's `registry_credit_cents`, recomputes `dose_deviation_pct` by the dose deviation rule, resets `rejection_code` to `NONE`, and moves the survey to `RESUBMITTED`. A resubmitted survey can then be accepted under the same shielding, deviation and qualification conditions as a first submission, with the same equipment-verdict and `last_survey_at` consequences.

The accrediting body can also reject outright. A survey sitting in `SUBMITTED` may be rejected under no further condition than its status. A survey sitting in `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED` may be rejected only while shielding has failed or the absolute deviation still exceeds `10.0` percent, and once neither holds the survey is no longer rejectable and draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a survey in any other status draws the same refusal. In both cases the `rejection_code` is `DOSE_OUT_OF_RANGE` when the absolute deviation exceeds `10.0` percent and `SHIELDING_FAILED` otherwise; the facility's `registry_credit_cents` is credited by `100000`; and `rejected_survey_count` is recomputed. A rejection from `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED` additionally reasserts the facility's `accreditation_currency` as `PROVISIONAL`. Rejection is terminal.

### 3.6. Audit, Renewal and the Dose Registry

A physician survey tells the accrediting body how one facility performed on one day. The dose registry tells it how that facility compares to everyone else, month after month, and the accreditation cycle tells it when the facility must prove itself again. This subsection covers the reporting, the audit and the renewal machinery together, because they share a single trigger: the moment a cycle opens for renewal, both the benchmark report and the random audit draw become live obligations.

Dose registry records are staged against a unit. A record names the owning `unit_id`, an `exam_category_code` drawn from `CT_HEAD`, `CT_ABDOMEN`, `MAMM_SCREEN` and `PET_TORSO`, the `exam_month` being the first day of the month being reported, and the `dose_index_value` measured for that category and month. A record begins in `STAGED` status with `benchmark_status` `NOT_EVALUATED`, `registry_fee_cents` at `0`, and its `submitted_at`, `benchmark_comparison_value` and `dose_benchmark_average` null. A client may send a `benchmark_comparison_value` when staging, but the value is ignored — the field is computed by the classification rule and never accepted from the caller. The combination of unit, exam category and exam month must be unique among all dose registry records, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DOSE_RECORD_DUPLICATE`. Staging is refused with `ERR_UNIT_DEFERRED` when the owning unit is not `ENROLLED`. Records can be listed by `unit_id`, `exam_category_code` or `submission_status`.

Submitting a staged record is what puts it on the books, and it requires two things: the `dose_index_value` must be strictly greater than zero — a non-positive figure draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the facility's `registry_credit_cents` must hold at least `2500`, failing which the submission is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGISTRY_CREDIT`. A record already in a terminal state cannot be submitted again, drawing `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; one that is not `STAGED` without being terminal draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A successful submission stamps `submitted_at` from the current logical clock — which at the start of a session is the fixed scenario instant `2026-09-05T00:00:00Z`, the anchor the programmatic workflows stage their records against — sets `registry_fee_cents` to `2500`, debits `2500` from the facility's `registry_credit_cents`, and moves the record to `SUBMITTED`. It then evaluates the record twice: once through the classification rule to determine where the record sits against its benchmark, and once through the facility dose index benchmark rule to determine the unit-and-category average the record now belongs to. The classification output is stored as `benchmark_status`, and the comparison dose index the classification actually applied is stored as `benchmark_comparison_value`. The aggregation output is stored as `dose_benchmark_average`. Submitting a record also re-derives `dose_benchmark_average` for every other record of the same unit and category, so each reported figure reflects the set of submitted records as it now stands.

The classification rule decides the comparison value first and the verdict second. When no other record of the same unit and the same exam category is already `SUBMITTED`, the comparison value is the modality constant for the record's own `modality_code`: `2.0` for `MRI`, `2.3` for `CT`, `2.5` for `MAMM`, `3.0` for `PET` and `2.2` for `US`. When such records do exist, the comparison value is instead the maximum `benchmark_comparison_value` already on file among them. The rule then computes the ratio of the record's `dose_index_value` to that comparison value and classifies it: a ratio of at most `1.00` is `WITHIN_BENCHMARK`, a ratio above `1.00` but at most `1.35` is `ABOVE_BENCHMARK`, and a ratio above `1.35` is `CRITICAL`. Only submitted records of the same unit and category participate — staged records, rejected records, records of other units and records of other categories are all excluded. Ratios are computed at full precision and compared at that precision, while the dose index and the comparison value each carry 2 decimal places. Two variants deserve naming because they diverge on real inputs: the unit-local variant, where a `2.7` CT reading compares against the `2.3` already on file and yields a ratio of `1.1739`, hence `ABOVE_BENCHMARK`; and the category-fallback variant, where the same `2.7` compares against the CT constant of `2.3` with no facility-wide influence, while a `3.2` MRI reading against the MRI constant of `2.0` crosses into `CRITICAL`.

The facility dose index benchmark rule computes the unit-and-category average. It collects every dose registry record of the same unit and the same exam category whose `submission_status` is `SUBMITTED`, sums their `dose_index_value` values into a numerator, counts those records into a denominator, and divides the first by the second — the plain arithmetic mean of the collected `dose_index_value` figures. Staged and rejected records are excluded. The record being submitted is counted inside its own average — the included-self variant — which means a unit already holding one submitted `CT_HEAD` record at `2.3`, receiving a new `2.7`, yields an average of `2.50`; skipping the record under test would leave the same inputs at `2.30` over a different denominator. Both aggregates are order-insensitive sums, so no tie-break rule arises. The average is retained at full precision and reported with 6 significant digits. The rule also runs on every read of a facility's dose registry summary, not only at submission time.

Rejection reverses a submission cleanly. A record may be rejected only while it is `SUBMITTED` — one already `REJECTED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and one that is not `SUBMITTED` without being terminal draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The effect is to set `registry_fee_cents` back to `0`, clear `submitted_at` to null, return `benchmark_status` to `NOT_EVALUATED`, clear `dose_benchmark_average` to null, and credit `2500` back to the facility's `registry_credit_cents`, restoring the balance the submission had consumed. Rejection is terminal.

Accreditation cycles open the long-horizon view. A cycle is created against a `facility_id` with a `cycle_index` counting monotonically within the facility starting at `1`, a `cycle_started_at` instant, and an optional `prior_cycle_id` naming the cycle it renews. The `cycle_index` must be unique among the facility's cycles, with `ERR_CYCLE_INDEX_DUPLICATE` raised on a collision. A cycle is created in `PENDING` status with `audit_selection_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `audit_outcome` `PENDING`, `benchmark_submission_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and `cycle_expires_at` computed as `cycle_started_at` plus `1095` days — three years. The `benchmark_submission_status` field tracks how far the facility has got with its registry reporting for the cycle, reading `NOT_STARTED` before any record is filed, `PARTIAL` while reporting is under way, `COMPLETE` once it is finished, and `OVERDUE` once the reporting window has closed with work outstanding.

Activation requires the facility to be `ENROLLED` and does three things: it records `unit_count_at_start` as the number of the facility's units then in `ENROLLED` status, sets `cycle_expires_at` to `cycle_started_at` plus `1095` days, and marks the cycle `ACTIVE`. It also writes the cycle's `cycle_index` onto the facility as `current_cycle_index`, drops the facility's `accreditation_currency` back to `PROVISIONAL`, since a fresh cycle must be earned again, and closes as `RENEWED` every cycle of the facility with a lower `cycle_index` that has not already reached a terminal state. A cycle already in a terminal state cannot be activated, drawing `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; one not still `PENDING` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Opening the renewal window is a guarded act. The cycle must still be `ACTIVE` — a cycle already in a terminal state draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any other non-`ACTIVE` status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The facility must be `ENROLLED`; every physicist survey of the facility must be in `ACCEPTED` or `RESUBMITTED` status; and every equipment item of every unit of the facility must be `ACTIVE`. Additional refusals guard the same operation: `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE` when any plan of the facility carries `deadline_status` `OVERDUE`, `ERR_DEFICIENCY_PENDING` when any plan is still `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED`, `ERR_SURVEY_SHORTFALL` when the facility's roster aggregation is `HALT` or any qualification is `REVOKED`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED` when a clinical-role qualification is not `VERIFIED`, and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REGISTRY_CREDIT` when `registry_credit_cents` cannot cover the `75000` debit. A successful opening moves the cycle to `RENEWAL_DUE` and debits `75000` from the facility's `registry_credit_cents`. It then extends every deadline of the facility by `90` days: each modality unit's `clinical_deadline_at` and `testing_deadline_at`, and each corrective action plan's `due_at` and `resubmission_deadline_at`, all move forward by `90` days. Finally, it draws the audit, recording `audit_selection_status` and, when the draw is `SELECTED`, setting `audit_outcome` to `PENDING` so the inspection is owed.

The random site audit selection rule decides whether the facility must host an on-site inspection. Let the cycle index be the cycle's `cycle_index`; let the failing surveys be the count of the facility's physicist surveys whose `survey_status` is `REJECTED` or `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED`; and let the rejected count be the facility's `rejected_survey_count` read at evaluation time. The rule then selects in a fixed order of precedence. If the cycle index is an exact multiple of `5`, the audit is `SELECTED`. Otherwise, if the failing surveys count has reached `2`, the audit is `SELECTED`. Otherwise, if the rejected count is at least `1`, the audit is `SELECTED`. Otherwise the audit is `WAIVED`. Because the cycle index is one-based, cycle `5`, `10` and `15` are audited while a facility sitting at cycle `4` is waived. The three tests are applied in that order, so a cycle whose index is a multiple of five is audited even where no survey has failed and the facility's rejected count is zero. When the outcome is `SELECTED`, the cycle's `audit_selection_status` is recorded as `SELECTED` and its `audit_outcome` is set to `PENDING`, meaning the inspection is owed but not yet adjudicated.

Completing the renewal is permitted while the cycle's `audit_outcome` is `PASS` or `PENDING` — any other outcome, or a cycle not sitting in `RENEWAL_DUE`, draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — subject to the same deadline, deficiency, roster and qualification refusals described above. A successful renewal sets the cycle's `audit_outcome` to `PASS` and marks the cycle `RENEWED`, and additionally sets every cycle of the facility whose `cycle_index` is lower than this cycle's index and which is not already `RENEWED` to `RENEWED` as well — so a facility cannot leave old cycles dangling once it has moved on. A successor cycle activation triggers the same closure automatically.

Rejecting the renewal is the path to lapse. It applies only to a cycle sitting in `RENEWAL_DUE`; a cycle already terminal draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and any other status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It is permitted while the cycle's `audit_outcome` is `PENDING`, or when the acting role reports an `audit_outcome` of `FAIL` in the request — any other request-supplied `audit_outcome` being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and any other combination with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The consequences are the widest cascade in the system. The cycle's `audit_outcome` is recorded as `FAIL` and its status becomes `LAPSED`. The facility's `registry_credit_cents` is credited by `75000`, returning the renewal fee. The facility's `accreditation_currency` becomes `LAPSED`, and its `facility_status` drops to `DEFERRED`. Every modality unit of the facility becomes `DEFERRED`; every equipment item of the facility goes `OUT_OF_SERVICE`; every phantom test still in `ACQUIRED` status is voided; every corrective action plan of the facility expires; and every physicist survey still in `SCHEDULED` status is rejected with `rejection_code` `DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE` — a survey that was scheduled but never performed when the facility's accreditation collapsed. The facility's `enrolled_unit_count`, `deferred_unit_count` and `rejected_survey_count` are all recomputed.

Finally, two administrative capabilities round out the system. A `FacilityAdmin` can purchase registry credit for a facility by supplying an `amount_cents` figure, which is added to `registry_credit_cents`; the amount is a whole number of cents, and a missing one draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; because this mutates the facility aggregate it must carry `expected_facility_version`. And a test-driving capability available to the `AccreditationBody` advances the server's logical clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant. The clock starts fixed at `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and the server never reads real wall-clock time, so advancing it is what makes deadline, expiry and window behaviour observable; the call returns both the new `current_utc` and the `default_utc` it started from, and it re-evaluates every credential expiry, every corrective action plan's `deadline_status`, and every unit's `testing_deadline_at` threshold. Advancing the clock backwards is not refused — the endpoint simply asserts whatever instant it is given, and the temporal rules are re-derived against it.

### 3.7. Corrective Action Plans and Deficiency Resolution

A corrective action plan is never created by direct request. It comes into existence as a cascade of a clinical image submission whose review returned `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY` or `FAIL`, and it exists to make the facility answer that deficiency on the record. The category it carries is inherited rather than chosen: when the reviewer's `review_outcome` was `ACCEPT_WITH_DEFICIENCY`, the plan's `deficiency_code` is copied from the `reviewer_deficiency_category` the reviewer supplied, and when the reviewer's `review_outcome` was `FAIL` it is fixed at `IMAGE_QUALITY` because a failed image set is by definition a quality problem. A plan names the `facility_id` that owes the answer, the `source_submission_id` of the deficient or failed submission it answers, and that inherited `deficiency_code`, one of `IMAGE_QUALITY`, `POSITIONING`, `ARTIFACT` or `DOSE`. It opens in `OPEN` status with `due_at` and `resubmission_deadline_at` inherited from the creating submission's `deficiency_deadline_at`, `resubmission_attempts` at `0`, and `deadline_status` derived by the rule below. Plans can be listed by `facility_id`, `source_submission_id`, `plan_status` or `deadline_status`.

The corrective action deadline rule classifies how close a plan is to falling due, and it runs not only when a plan is created but on every re-read of the plan. It computes the days remaining as the number of whole days between the plan's `due_at` and the current logical clock — the elapsed seconds divided by the `86400` seconds in a day, truncated down to a whole number, and by construction never less than about `-0.99`. It then classifies: fewer than zero whole days remaining is `OVERDUE` — which, given that floor, means the clock has passed `due_at` at all; zero through `10` whole days remaining inclusive is `DUE_SOON`; more than `10` whole days remaining is `ON_TRACK`. The `10`-day window is what makes a plan due tomorrow and a plan due in ten days alike `DUE_SOON`. Because truncation is toward zero on a whole-day count, a plan due in exactly ten days and twenty-three hours still classifies as `DUE_SOON` — that is the whole-day variant. The rule considers every plan of the facility regardless of its `plan_status`, and a plan that has already expired keeps whatever value it last computed.

Submitting the facility's response is a `FacilityAdmin` act, performed against the plan, with no request body. It applies only to a plan sitting in `OPEN` — one `ACCEPTED` or `EXPIRED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and any other status draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Four preconditions apply. The source submission's `review_status` must be `DEFICIENCY`, with a mismatch drawing `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; when the source unit cannot be resolved at all the refusal is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`. The source unit must have at least one equipment item whose `equipment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and a unit with none draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The plan's `deadline_status` must not be `OVERDUE`, the refusal being `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE`. And the source unit must have at least one phantom test in `SCORED` or `PASS` status whose `scored_at` is at or after the source submission's `deficiency_issued_at`, so the plan cannot be answered with phantom evidence that predates the deficiency it is answering; the refusal `ERR_PHANTOM_UNSCORED` bites only when the unit actually holds at least one scored or passed phantom test and every such test carries a non-null `scored_at` earlier than the deficiency's `deficiency_issued_at` — a unit holding no scored phantom test at all passes this gate. Filing is additionally refused with `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE` when the facility holds any plan that is `OVERDUE`. A successful submission moves the plan to `SUBMITTED` and extends its `due_at` by exactly `1` day, a short administrative grace. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed — and because a `SUBMITTED` plan still counts, filing does not by itself reduce that figure.

Accepting the response is the accrediting body's act. It is permitted when the source submission's `review_status` is `ACCEPTED`, or when the plan's supporting resubmission round contains a clinical image submission in `ACCEPTED` status; a plan with neither draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A plan that is not `SUBMITTED` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and one already `ACCEPTED` or `EXPIRED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. A plan may also reach `ACCEPTED` automatically when the answering clinical image submission of its unit is reviewed as an acceptance, so the two paths converge. Acceptance moves the plan to `ACCEPTED`, recomputes the owning unit's `deficiency_open_count`, and — when that count reaches zero and every other unit of the facility is also at zero — restores the facility's `payer_participation_status` to `ACTIVE`. That restoration is the mirror image of the `AT_RISK` demotion each deficiency and failure imposed.

Rejecting the response sends the plan back for another attempt. It is permitted while the plan's supporting resubmission round contains at least one clinical image submission in `DEFICIENCY` or `FAILED` status, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise. Rejection increments `resubmission_attempts` by one, extends `due_at` by `30` days, and extends `resubmission_deadline_at` by `30` days, giving the facility a fresh month to answer properly. The unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed. Note that the plan returns to `OPEN` status once rejected, which is what allows it to be submitted again.

A plan that is never answered expires on its own. If the current logical clock passes the plan's `due_at`, a system transition moves an `OPEN` plan to `EXPIRED`. If the clock passes the plan's `resubmission_deadline_at`, a system transition moves a `SUBMITTED` plan to `EXPIRED`. In both cases the plan's `deadline_status` is forced to `OVERDUE`, the owning unit's `deficiency_open_count` is recomputed, and the facility's `payer_participation_status` drops to `AT_RISK`. Expiry is terminal, and it is the state that makes `ERR_DEADLINE_OVERDUE` bite on every subsequent submission, resubmission and renewal attempt at the facility.

Two further cancellations reach plans from outside their own lifecycle. Revoking a personnel qualification expires every `OPEN` plan of that facility, and a rejected accreditation renewal expires every plan of the facility without exception. Neither is a corrective action plan transition in its own right; both are cascades, and both leave the plan in its terminal `EXPIRED` state.

### 3.8. Personnel Credentialing

Accreditation is only as good as the people behind it, so the API maintains a roster of credentialed individuals. A `PersonnelQualification` is recorded against a `facility_id` with the person's `person_name`, a `personnel_role` of `INTERPRETING_PHYSICIAN`, `TECHNOLOGIST` or `MEDICAL_PHYSICIST`, a globally unique `credential_number`, the `ce_credit_hours` earned in the current cycle, the `required_ce_hours` the role demands, and a `credential_expires_on` expiry date. A new qualification begins in `PENDING` status with `qualification_verified_at` null and `survey_count` at `0`. The `credential_number` must be globally unique across all qualifications, with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` raised on collision. Recording a qualification immediately recomputes the facility's `roster_disposition`, since the roster composition has changed. Qualifications can be listed by `facility_id`, `personnel_role` or `credential_status`.

Verifying a person's credentials is the accrediting body's act, and it applies only to a qualification that is neither `VERIFIED` nor `REVOKED` — one already `VERIFIED` draws `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and one `REVOKED` draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. It requires `ce_credit_hours` to be at least `required_ce_hours` — a shortfall is refused with `ERR_CE_HOURS_SHORTFALL` — together with a `credential_expires_on` that falls strictly after the `verified_at` instant supplied with the request, a missing `verified_at` or an expiry that does not fall after it being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the qualification moves to `VERIFIED`, `qualification_verified_at` is stamped with the supplied `verified_at`, and the facility's `roster_disposition` is recomputed.

Lapse happens on its own clock. When the logical clock reaches a qualification's `credential_expires_on` — that is, when the expiry instant is no longer after the current instant — a system transition moves it from `VERIFIED` to `LAPSED`. Its immediate consequence is that every physicist survey naming that qualification and sitting in `SUBMITTED` status is rejected with `rejection_code` `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, and the facility's `rejected_survey_count` is recomputed. This is why advancing the logical clock is the observable way to exercise credential expiry.

Revocation is the draft act available to the accrediting body and takes effect from `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `LAPSED` — any status other than the terminal `REVOKED`, which draws `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Its cascade depends on which status the qualification is leaving. From `PENDING` or `LAPSED`, every physicist survey naming the qualification whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `SUBMITTED` or `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED` is rejected with `rejection_code` `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, and every `OPEN` corrective action plan of the facility expires. From `VERIFIED`, revocation requires that no physicist survey naming the qualification is sitting in `SUBMITTED` — a submitted survey must be adjudicated before its signatory can be revoked, the refusal being `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and every survey naming it that is `SCHEDULED` or `CORRECTIVE_REQUIRED` is rejected with `rejection_code` `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`. In every case the facility's `rejected_survey_count` and `roster_disposition` are recomputed. Revocation is terminal.

The roster deficiency aggregation distills the whole roster into a single posture value, and it runs whenever any qualification of the facility changes `credential_status` and again on the facility's re-read. It gathers the facility's qualifications whose status is `PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED`; because that set already includes lapsed medical physicists, the extra rule pulling in a `MEDICAL_PHYSICIST` row that is `LAPSED` is subsumed by it, so a lapsed physicist is always visible to the roster view. It then selects in a fixed order of precedence. If any gathered row is `REVOKED`, the disposition is `HALT` — the revoked-first variant, which halts even when a single row is revoked and every other row is verified. Otherwise, if two or more gathered rows are `LAPSED`, the disposition is `HALT`. Otherwise, if exactly one gathered row is `LAPSED`, the disposition is `HOLD`. Otherwise, if three or more gathered rows are `PENDING`, the disposition is `HOLD`. Otherwise the disposition is `CLEAR`. The precedence matters: because two lapsed rows already halt, the three-pending hold branch is unreachable whenever two rows have lapsed, which is the lapsed-then-pending variant of this rule. The counts are exact integers and no arithmetic beyond counting is involved.

The aggregated posture gates real operations. A disposition of `HALT` refuses a physicist's survey submission and survey resubmission with `ERR_ROSTER_INELIGIBLE`. Registration of the facility, opening its renewal window and completing its renewal are refused with `ERR_SURVEY_SHORTFALL` when the aggregation is `HALT` or when any qualification of the facility carries `credential_status` `REVOKED`, and are additionally refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_UNVERIFIED` when any qualification holding a clinical role is not `VERIFIED`. Finally, a physicist whose own qualification is `REVOKED` cannot submit or resubmit a survey at all, the refusal being `ERR_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create new resources — a facility, a modality unit, an equipment item, a clinical image submission, a phantom test, a physicist survey, a personnel qualification, an accreditation cycle or a dose registry record — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state-transition action and every resubmission, return `200 OK`. All paths are mounted under the `/accreditation` prefix except the logical-clock capability, which is mounted at `/_clock/set_current_utc` outside that prefix, and the two administrative capabilities — the registry-credit purchase and the logical-clock advance — follow the same status conventions as the rest.
