# Cert Training Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A local Community Emergency Response Team (CERT) program lives or dies on the currency of its people. Volunteers are recruited into cohorts, taught a fixed curriculum, examined on hands-on skills, credentialed with a photo ID that has a hard expiry, issued a backpack of equipment the program is fiscally answerable for, and — when a real incident opens — called out through a roster that must be assembled in minutes from whoever is genuinely current, genuinely free, and genuinely trained. Today that whole chain is run on spreadsheets that disagree with one another: the attendance sheet says a volunteer completed Search & Rescue, the credential binder says their card lapsed eight months ago, and the equipment cabinet log says a radio kit that was written off is still on issue. The service specified here replaces those spreadsheets with a single authoritative record in which every one of those facts is derived, not typed.

The system serves seven distinct constituencies with sharply different authority. Program coordinators run recruitment, scheduling, graduation review, and the credential lifecycle. Instructors deliver classroom sessions and record who attended. Skills evaluators — drawn from the same instructor roster, but acting under a different hat — sign the hands-on demonstrations, and they may never sign for a session they themselves taught. Logistics officers own the equipment inventory and the money tied up in it. Emergency managers on the agency side open activations, build call-out rosters, and run exercises. Trainees answer call-outs and may withdraw. Auditors read everything and change nothing. Two independent segregation-of-duties walls are enforced structurally rather than by policy memo, because in an after-incident review the question is never "was there a rule?" but "can you prove the rule held?".

Three properties dominate the design. First, **derived state is never client-supplied**: seat counts, committed equipment value, earned contact hours, readiness scores, credential expiry, and settlement amounts are all computed by the server from primary records, so no caller can assert a number the underlying evidence does not support. Second, **withdrawal of evidence propagates**: voiding a single attendance record can, through a chain of consequences, demote a graduate, suspend their credential, pull them off a live call-out roster, and re-open their cohort's graduation review. Third, **time is explicit**: every deadline, expiry, depreciation, and recency computation reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so a reviewer can replay any decision the system made and get the same answer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. The seven roles are `ProgramCoordinator`, `Instructor`, `SkillsEvaluator`, `LogisticsOfficer`, `EmergencyManager`, `Trainee`, and `Auditor`. Role names are case-sensitive and are never abbreviated.

Authorisation is per endpoint. Section 4 lists, for every endpoint, the roles permitted to invoke it; a syntactically valid request from a role that does not appear in that list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. Every listing and single-resource read in the system is open to all seven roles, which is what makes `Auditor` useful: it holds no write capability whatsoever and exists purely for oversight on behalf of the sponsoring agency.

Three roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` refers to a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `Instructor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Instructor`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Instructor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, an instructor may only mark delivered a class session whose `instructor_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SkillsEvaluator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Instructor` row, because evaluators are drawn from the same instructor roster; here the comparison runs the other way, since an evaluator is forbidden from signing a demonstration for a session whose `instructor_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Trainee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Trainee`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Trainee` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a trainee may answer only a call-out assignment whose `trainee_id` is their own, and may act on only their own trainee record. An entity-backed role acting on a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramCoordinator`, `LogisticsOfficer`, `EmergencyManager`, or `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `graduation_reviewer_user_id` on a cohort, as `issued_by_user_id` on a credential, or as `issued_by_user_id` on an equipment issuance, all of which are written from the acting `X-User-ID` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers are nevertheless load-bearing: the segregation-of-duties rules described in §2.7 compare one opaque coordinator identifier against another.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, so that repeated paging over an unchanging data set produces a stable, non-overlapping sequence. For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when the offset plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied identifier in a creation payload is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row was first written.

All fifteen entity types in this system — `Program`, `Cohort`, `CurriculumModule`, `Facility`, `Instructor`, `ClassSession`, `Trainee`, `ModuleCompletion`, `Credential`, `EquipmentAsset`, `EquipmentIssuance`, `Exercise`, `ExerciseParticipation`, `Activation`, and `RosterAssignment` — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row; at creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row, including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade originating on another resource.

Time in this system is a **logical clock** held by the server. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until the clock is explicitly moved, every read of "now" returns that instant — the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A coordinator-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and reports the new value as `current_utc`. Every response deadline, credential expiry, grace boundary, recency tier, depreciation month count, and delivery-time check in this specification reads that clock.

### 2.5. Nested Paths and Referential Resolution

Path parameters are validated strictly left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve determines the error. A request naming a cohort that does not exist fails with `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FOUND` before any deeper segment or any payload field is inspected. Each entity type has its own not-found code: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CURRICULUM_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLASS_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODULE_COMPLETION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ISSUANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXERCISE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_ROSTER_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

The same discipline governs foreign keys supplied inside a request body. Every reference in a creation payload must resolve to an existing row, and the references are checked in the order the fields appear in that endpoint's input list, so the first unresolvable reference decides which not-found code is returned. This body-level resolution applies to program, cohort, curriculum-module, facility, instructor, class-session, trainee, exercise, activation, and equipment-asset creation payloads alike — specifically to the creation of cohorts, class sessions, trainees, module completions, credentials, equipment assets, equipment issuances, exercises, exercise participations, activations, curriculum modules, and manually added roster assignments.

### 2.6. Order of Evaluation

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is itself part of the contract; the first failing check determines the response, and no later check may mask an earlier one. Checks run in this sequence:

1. Header presence and role validity (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401).
2. Role authorisation for the target endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403).
3. Existence of every resource named by a path segment, left to right (the entity-specific not-found code, 404).
4. Request-body shape: every required field present, every value of the declared type, every enumerated value a member of its declared set, every numeric bound honoured — `seat_capacity` at least 1, `requested_responders` at least 1, `unit_value_cents` at least 1, `attendance_minutes` at least 0, `contact_hours` strictly greater than 0, `demo_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive — every datetime a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC value, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, and `offset` at least 0 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400).
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the body, in input-list order (the entity-specific not-found code, 404).
6. Scoped uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPED_KEY`, 409).
7. Global uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_GLOBAL_KEY`, 409).
8. Ownership for entity-backed roles (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403).
9. Optimistic-concurrency version match (`ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACTIVATION_VERSION`, 412).
10. Legality of the requested state transition (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409).
11. Cross-entity state preconditions, in this relative order: cohort accepting enrollments (`ERR_COHORT_NOT_ACCEPTING`), cohort in graduation review (`ERR_COHORT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`), session delivered (`ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERED`), credential live (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ACTIVE`), activation rostered (`ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_ROSTERED`) — all 409.
12. Safety rules, in this relative order: segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403), instructor certification currency (`ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`, 409), instructor and facility activeness (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409), then demonstration attempts exhausted (`ERR_DEMO_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, 409).
13. Temporal rules, in this relative order: facility double-booking (`ERR_FACILITY_DOUBLE_BOOKED`), instructor double-booking (`ERR_INSTRUCTOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`), session outside the cohort window (`ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_COHORT_WINDOW`), and call-out response window closed (`ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`) — all 409.
14. Referential business rules, in this relative order: prerequisite not scheduled (`ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_SCHEDULED`), prerequisite not passed (`ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_PASSED`), curriculum cycle (`ERR_CURRICULUM_CYCLE`), equipment outstanding (`ERR_EQUIPMENT_OUTSTANDING`), and roster commitment outstanding (`ERR_ROSTER_COMMITMENT_OUTSTANDING`) — all 409.
15. Remaining business constraints, in this relative order: attendance insufficient (`ERR_ATTENDANCE_INSUFFICIENT`, 422), duplicate completion (`ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLETION`, 409), facility capacity insufficient (`ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`, 409), instructor daily load exceeded (`ERR_INSTRUCTOR_DAILY_LOAD_EXCEEDED`, 409), no eligible responders (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RESPONDERS`, 409), manually added trainee not eligible (`ERR_TRAINEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 409), no asset available (`ERR_NO_ASSET_AVAILABLE`, 409), trainee already holding that kit type (`ERR_TRAINEE_ALREADY_ISSUED_KIT`, 409), refresher hours insufficient (`ERR_REFRESHER_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`, 422), core modules incomplete (`ERR_CORE_MODULES_INCOMPLETE`, 422), credential already issued (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_ALREADY_ISSUED`, 409).
16. Fiscal ceilings last of all: the equipment budget ceiling (`ERR_EQUIPMENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, 409).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. A stale version is reported before the transition legality is examined, so a client working from an outdated read learns that fact rather than a misleading state error. And the equipment budget ceiling is evaluated after every selection, eligibility, and duplication rule, so a request that both exceeds the budget and names an already-kitted trainee reports the duplicate-kit condition, not the budget one.

### 2.7. The Two Segregation-of-Duties Walls

Independent verification is the backbone of a defensible training record, and this system enforces it in two places. The first wall separates teaching from examining: the user signing a skills demonstration must not be the instructor who delivered the class session that the demonstration belongs to. Concretely, the acting evaluator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `instructor_id` of the session recorded on the completion. The second wall separates graduation review from credential issuance: the coordinator who moved a cohort into graduation review — whose opaque identifier was stamped onto that cohort's `graduation_reviewer_user_id` — must not be the coordinator who mints credentials for that cohort's trainees. In both cases the violation is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

The second wall is deliberately re-armed by the revert path described in §2.10. When a cohort falls back out of graduation review, its `graduation_reviewer_user_id` is cleared to null, so the next coordinator to push it back into review becomes the newly barred party — the wall follows the person who actually performed the review, not the person who performed it first.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Program and Activation Ledgers

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both are legitimately repeatable — a second, sequential request would observe a state in which it, too, would succeed — and both are gated on an aggregate the client must have actually seen.

Issuing an equipment kit requires a non-optional `expected_program_version` field in the request payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Program` that owns the inventory. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no issuance row is created. Because the equipment budget ceiling is evaluated against `equipment_committed_cents`, a client whose view of that ledger predates another officer's issuance must re-read before it may commit more of the budget.

Manually adding a responder to an activation roster likewise requires a non-optional `expected_activation_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Activation`. A mismatch with the activation's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACTIVATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no assignment row is created — a client whose view of `rostered_count` and `fill_status` predates an earlier add is refused rather than allowed to over-fill the roster.

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version grounds.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding, and Conserved Ledgers

Money is held in integer cents throughout; there is no floating-point currency anywhere in the record. Where a monetary result is derived from a percentage, the multiplication is performed per line and each line is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up **before** the lines are summed. Summing unrounded products and rounding the total once is expressly forbidden: two lines that each land on a half-cent differ by a full cent between the two orders, and the per-line order is the contractual one.

Hours are held to one decimal place, rounded half-up, and the rounding is applied once to a completed total rather than to each contributing row. Ratios are held to four decimal places, rounded half-up. The readiness score is held to two decimal places, rounded half-up, with the rounding applied once at the very end of the computation — intermediate rounding of any partial result inside that computation is forbidden. Residual value percentages are held to four decimal places, rounded half-up. Credential term arithmetic is performed in whole days, while the hours compared against a refresher requirement are compared at one decimal place.

Several counters in this system are **conserved quantities** maintained by the server rather than reported by clients: a cohort's `seats_confirmed` and `seats_waitlisted`, a program's `equipment_committed_cents`, `active_member_count`, and `pending_graduation_count`, an activation's `rostered_count`, `accepted_count`, and `deployed_count`, a session's `recorded_completion_count`, an exercise's `accepted_participant_count`, a trainee's `earned_contact_hours`, `passed_core_count`, and `equipment_liability_cents`, and a cohort's `delivered_contact_hours`. Each is defined as an aggregate over primary rows, and every operation that changes those rows must leave the counter equal to that aggregate — which is what makes an increment on one path and a decrement on the reverse path mandatory rather than cosmetic.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and the Two Trainee State Dimensions

Withdrawal of evidence in this system propagates through chains of consequences, and each link in a chain is caused by the link before it rather than directly by the triggering request. Two chains matter most. **Chain A** begins when a passed completion is voided and runs six links deep: the completion's credited hours drop to zero; the trainee's hour and core-module tallies are reduced and, if the trainee has fallen below the graduation requirement while graduated, they are demoted back to enrolled; the demotion suspends the trainee's active credential and lapses their currency; the suspension withdraws their live call-out assignments; each affected activation recomputes its counts and fill status; and, separately caused by the same demotion, a cohort sitting in graduation review reverts to in-session and the program's pending-graduation tally is reduced. **Chain B** begins when a delivered class session is cancelled and runs five links: the cohort's delivered contact hours are reduced by the module's worth; every completion recorded against that session is voided; each voided completion forces its trainee's derived tallies and readiness score to be recomputed; if the cancellation leaves a core module without any delivered session on a cohort that is in graduation review, that cohort reverts to in-session and its reviewer identifier is cleared; and the program's pending-graduation tally is reduced.

Every row touched anywhere in such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been modified directly. A cascade is not a silent write.

A trainee carries **two parallel state dimensions on the same row**, and they advance independently. `enrollment_status` tracks the volunteer's relationship with a cohort and moves through `APPLIED`, `WAITLISTED`, `ENROLLED`, `GRADUATED`, and `WITHDRAWN`, with `APPLIED` as the initial value and `WITHDRAWN` as its only terminal value. `currency_status` tracks the standing of the volunteer's credential and moves through `NOT_CREDENTIALED`, `CURRENT`, `LAPSING`, `LAPSED`, and `REVOKED`, with `NOT_CREDENTIALED` as the initial value and `REVOKED` as its only terminal value. The currency dimension is driven entirely by the credential machine and is never advanced by an enrollment action; conversely, no currency transition changes the enrollment dimension. A graduated volunteer may therefore be lapsed, and a withdrawn volunteer may remain current until their credential is actually revoked. Note also that `GRADUATED` is **not** terminal: the demotion edge back to `ENROLLED` described above closes that loop.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program, a Curriculum, and the Teaching Resources

Everything in the system hangs off a **program**, the local CERT organisation itself. A `ProgramCoordinator` creates one by supplying its `name`, the `sponsor_agency` that backs it, a `jurisdiction_code` such as a county-and-district identifier, and `equipment_budget_cents` — the ceiling, in whole cents, on the total replacement value of equipment that may be on issue at any one moment. That budget figure must be an integer of at least 0. Three derived tallies open at 0 on a new program and are maintained thereafter by the server: `equipment_committed_cents`, the summed replacement value of every asset currently out on an active issuance; `active_member_count`, the number of trainees in the program whose enrollment standing is either enrolled or graduated; and `pending_graduation_count`, the number of the program's cohorts currently sitting in graduation review. A coordinator may later revise the program's `equipment_budget_cents` or its `sponsor_agency`; such a revision increments the program's `version`, which matters because that version is the concurrency token described in §2.8.

The **curriculum** is a program-independent catalogue of modules. Each module is defined with a `module_code` that must be unique across the entire catalogue, a `title`, a `contact_hours` value expressing the classroom time it is worth (strictly greater than zero and carrying at most one decimal place), and a `sequence_index` of at least 1 that hints at the intended teaching order. Four further attributes shape what the module means downstream, each with a default that applies when the attribute is omitted at creation. `is_core` defaults to true and marks the module as counting toward the graduation requirement. `requires_skills_demo` defaults to false; when true, a trainee's completion of that module cannot reach a passing state on attendance alone and needs an evaluator's signature. `refresher_eligible` defaults to false; when true, the hours a trainee earns from that module count toward keeping a credential current. `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `RETIRED`; retired modules drop out of the graduation requirement, which is always computed over modules that are both core and active.

Modules form a **prerequisite graph** through `prerequisite_module_id`, a self-referential reference that is null for entry-level modules and otherwise names the module that must come first. A coordinator may re-point that edge, rename a module through `title`, or retire it through `status` at any time; each such change increments the module's `version`. Whenever a prerequisite edge is supplied — at creation or on a later change — the graph is checked for cycles, and a value that would make a module reachable from itself, including a module naming itself directly, is rejected with `ERR_CURRICULUM_CYCLE`. Both the creation path and the amendment path perform this check; neither is exempt. Modules can be listed and filtered by `prerequisite_module_id`, by `is_core`, and by `status`, which makes it straightforward to walk the graph in either direction.

Classroom space is modelled as **facilities**. A facility is registered with a `name`, a globally unique `room_code`, a `capacity_seats` count of at least 1 describing how many bodies the room physically holds, and a `status` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `CLOSED`. **Instructors** are registered with a `full_name`, a `certification_expires_on` instant marking when their own teaching certification lapses, an optional `max_daily_contact_hours` cap on how many aggregate teaching hours they may be booked for within a single UTC calendar day (defaulting to 8 and required to be strictly greater than zero), and a `status` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `INACTIVE`. A coordinator may later revise an instructor's `certification_expires_on`, `max_daily_contact_hours`, or `status`, each revision incrementing the instructor's `version`. A single instructor row backs both the `Instructor` and the `SkillsEvaluator` roles; the distinction lives entirely in which hat the person is wearing on a given request.

Attempting to register a module whose `module_code` is already taken, a facility whose `room_code` is already taken, or an asset whose `asset_tag` is already taken is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GLOBAL_KEY`, since all three of those keys are unique across the whole system rather than merely within a program.

### 3.2. Cohort Recruitment, the Seat Ledger, and the Waitlist

A **cohort** is one intake of volunteers taught together. A coordinator opens it against a program by supplying `program_id`, a `cohort_code` that must be unique among that program's cohorts, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1, and the pair `session_window_start` and `session_window_end` that fence the period in which this cohort's classes may be held. A cohort whose `cohort_code` duplicates an existing one in the same program, a trainee whose `email` duplicates an existing one in the same program, or an activation whose `incident_number` duplicates an existing one in the same program are all rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPED_KEY`. A new cohort opens in `RECRUITING`, with `seats_confirmed`, `seats_waitlisted`, and `delivered_contact_hours` at 0, `next_waitlist_position` at 1, and `graduation_reviewer_user_id` null.

The cohort lifecycle runs `RECRUITING → SCHEDULED → IN_SESSION → GRADUATION_REVIEW → CLOSED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from either of the first two states, and with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` as the two terminal states. A coordinator drives each advance by naming the desired `to_status`. Moving `RECRUITING → SCHEDULED` requires that every curriculum module that is both core and active already has at least one class session on this cohort in `SCHEDULED` standing; on success the cohort's `seat_capacity` becomes immutable for the remainder of its life. Moving `SCHEDULED → IN_SESSION` requires the logical clock to have reached `session_window_start` — that is, the clock must be at or after that instant. Moving `IN_SESSION → GRADUATION_REVIEW` requires that every core active module now has at least one class session on this cohort in `DELIVERED` standing, and that `seats_confirmed` is at least 1; on success the acting coordinator's `X-User-ID` is stamped onto `graduation_reviewer_user_id` and the owning program's `pending_graduation_count` is increased by 1. Moving `GRADUATION_REVIEW → CLOSED` requires that no trainee of the cohort remains `ENROLLED` — each must be `GRADUATED` or `WITHDRAWN` — and that no completion belonging to any of the cohort's trainees is still awaiting a skills demonstration. A request naming a `to_status` that is not reachable from the cohort's present state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

There is also a reverse edge that no user ever requests: `GRADUATION_REVIEW → IN_SESSION`. The system takes it automatically whenever a voided completion or a cancelled session leaves a core active module without a delivered session on the cohort, or drops any graduated trainee of that cohort back to enrolled. On that revert, `graduation_reviewer_user_id` is cleared to null and the program's `pending_graduation_count` is decreased by 1. Cancelling a cohort from `RECRUITING` or from `SCHEDULED` transitions every one of its class sessions still in `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED` and every one of its trainees whose enrollment standing is `APPLIED`, `WAITLISTED`, or `ENROLLED` to `WITHDRAWN`.

Applicants are recorded against a cohort with `program_id`, `cohort_id`, `full_name`, `email`, and an optional `deployment_hold` flag defaulting to false, which is an administrative bar on ever being picked for a call-out. A new applicant starts with `enrollment_status` of `APPLIED` and `currency_status` of `NOT_CREDENTIALED`. Recording an applicant is only permitted while the cohort is `RECRUITING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `IN_SESSION`; against a cohort in any other state the attempt is rejected with `ERR_COHORT_NOT_ACCEPTING`.

Enrollment decisions are taken through a single enrollment action naming an `action` of `ENROLL`, `WITHDRAW`, or `GRADUATE`. A coordinator may take any of the three; a trainee acting on their own record may take only `WITHDRAW`, and a trainee attempting to act on someone else's record is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Enrolling requires the cohort to be `RECRUITING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `IN_SESSION`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_COHORT_NOT_ACCEPTING`. From `APPLIED`, the outcome depends on the seat ledger: when `seats_confirmed` is strictly below `seat_capacity` the applicant advances `APPLIED → ENROLLED`, `seats_confirmed` rises by 1, and the program's `active_member_count` rises by 1; when `seats_confirmed` has reached or exceeded `seat_capacity` the applicant advances `APPLIED → WAITLISTED` instead, their `waitlist_position` is stamped from the cohort's `next_waitlist_position`, that counter is then increased by 1, and `seats_waitlisted` rises by 1. The waitlist counter is monotone and is never decremented, so positions remain unique and comparable for the life of the cohort; a promoted trainee keeps the `waitlist_position` they were given, for audit.

Withdrawal is where the seat ledger is repaid. A trainee may be withdrawn from `APPLIED` with no ledger effect at all, from `WAITLISTED` with `seats_waitlisted` reduced by 1, or from `ENROLLED` or `GRADUATED`, in which case `seats_confirmed` falls by 1 and the program's `active_member_count` falls by 1. Withdrawal from `ENROLLED` or `GRADUATED` is barred while the volunteer still holds equipment or a live call-out commitment: if any of that trainee's equipment issuances is still on issue the request fails with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_OUTSTANDING`, and if any of their roster assignments stands at `NOTIFIED`, `ACCEPTED`, or `DEPLOYED` it fails with `ERR_ROSTER_COMMITMENT_OUTSTANDING`.

Freeing a confirmed seat immediately runs the **waitlist promotion selector**. Its candidate set is every trainee of that same cohort whose enrollment standing is `WAITLISTED`; they are ranked by `waitlist_position` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. Exactly one trainee is promoted per released seat, taking the `WAITLISTED → ENROLLED` edge, which lowers `seats_waitlisted` by 1, raises `seats_confirmed` by 1, and raises the program's `active_member_count` by 1. Promotion runs only while the released seat leaves `seats_confirmed` strictly below `seat_capacity`, and when the candidate set is empty no promotion occurs and the seat simply stays free. Cohort listings may be filtered by `program_id` and by `status`; trainee listings may be filtered by `program_id`, `cohort_id`, `enrollment_status`, and `currency_status`.

### 3.3. Scheduling and Delivering Class Sessions

A **class session** is the four-way join that makes teaching concrete: it names a `cohort_id`, the `module_id` being taught, the `facility_id` hosting it, the `instructor_id` teaching it, and a `starts_at` instant. The caller never supplies an end time. `ends_at` is derived by the server as `starts_at` advanced by the module's `contact_hours` converted to minutes — a module worth 2.5 contact hours produces a session 150 minutes long — and a client-supplied end time is ignored. New sessions open in `SCHEDULED`, with `delivered_at` null and `recorded_completion_count` at 0; that counter tracks how many completions have been recorded against the session and excludes any that have been voided. Sessions may be listed and filtered by `cohort_id`, `module_id`, `facility_id`, `instructor_id`, and `status`.

Scheduling is the most heavily guarded operation in the system, and its guards fire in the order §2.6 fixes. The teaching staff must be usable at all: an instructor whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`, or a facility whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`, is rejected with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The instructor's own certification must outlast the class: `certification_expires_on` must fall strictly after the computed `ends_at` of the candidate session, and a certification expiring exactly at the closing bell is not good enough — that failure is `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`.

The two overlap rules deliberately use **different interval geometry**, and the difference is the whole point. For the facility, a 30-minute turnover buffer is applied to both ends of the *candidate* session only; the candidate therefore occupies the room from 30 minutes before its `starts_at` until 30 minutes after its `ends_at`, and it conflicts with any existing session in the same facility standing at `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` whose own unbuffered half-open interval — start inclusive, end exclusive — intersects that buffered span. A consequence worth stating plainly: two sessions that are exactly back to back in the same room, where the candidate begins at the precise instant the existing one ends, **do** conflict, and the rejection is `ERR_FACILITY_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. For the instructor, no buffer is applied at either end; a candidate conflicts only when its own unbuffered half-open interval intersects the unbuffered half-open interval of another `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` session taught by the same instructor. Exact adjacency is therefore perfectly legal for an instructor, and only genuine overlap is rejected, with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

The session must sit inside its cohort's teaching window: `starts_at` must be at or after the cohort's `session_window_start` and the computed `ends_at` must be at or before the cohort's `session_window_end`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_COHORT_WINDOW`. Curriculum order is enforced too. When the module being scheduled carries a non-null `prerequisite_module_id`, the prerequisite must already have a session on this same cohort standing at `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` whose `ends_at` falls at or before the candidate's `starts_at`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

Two capacity rules close the set. The room must be able to hold the class: the facility's `capacity_seats` must be greater than or equal to the cohort's current `seats_confirmed`, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`. And an instructor's **daily teaching load** is capped in aggregate, separately from the overlap rule. For the UTC calendar date on which the candidate's `starts_at` falls, the server sums the `contact_hours` of every session already taught by that instructor whose `starts_at` falls on that same UTC calendar date and whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED`; cancelled sessions are excluded and give their hours back. That existing load plus the candidate module's own `contact_hours` must be less than or equal to the instructor's `max_daily_contact_hours` — a load landing exactly on the cap is permitted, and only a load strictly above it is refused, with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_DAILY_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. Hours in this comparison are handled to one decimal place. Note the interaction: an instructor with the default 8-hour cap can legally teach four unbuffered, back-to-back two-hour sessions in one UTC day, but a fifth two-hour session on that date is refused on aggregate grounds even if it overlaps nothing.

Delivery is the assigned instructor's own act. Moving a session `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED` requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the session's `instructor_id`, that the owning cohort is `IN_SESSION`, that the logical clock has reached or passed the session's `starts_at`, and that the instructor's `certification_expires_on` is strictly after the logical clock at that moment — an instructor whose certification lapsed between scheduling and teaching is stopped with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`. On success, `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the cohort's `delivered_contact_hours` is increased by the module's `contact_hours`.

Cancellation is a coordinator act, optionally carrying a free-text `reason`, and it behaves very differently depending on where the session stands. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` simply moves the session to `CANCELLED` and releases the facility and instructor intervals it was holding, so that time becomes available to both overlap checks again. Cancelling a session that has already been `DELIVERED` fires cascade chain B in full: first the cohort's `delivered_contact_hours` is reduced by that module's `contact_hours`; because the session's delivery is thereby invalidated, every completion recorded against it standing at `RECORDED`, `SKILLS_PENDING`, `PASSED`, or `FAILED` is moved to `VOIDED`; because those completions are gone, each affected trainee's `earned_contact_hours`, `passed_core_count`, `last_qualifying_activity_at`, and `readiness_score` are recomputed; because those recomputations may leave a core active module with no delivered session on a cohort sitting in graduation review, that cohort reverts to `IN_SESSION` with `graduation_reviewer_user_id` cleared to null; and because the cohort left review, the program's `pending_graduation_count` is reduced by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal for a session.

### 3.4. Attendance, the Skills Demonstration, and Voiding Evidence

Attendance is recorded by the delivering instructor as a **module completion**, supplying the `trainee_id`, the `class_session_id`, and `attendance_minutes` — the whole number of minutes the volunteer was actually present, which may be 0 but never negative. The `module_id` is not supplied by the caller; it is copied from the session at creation, along with the derived `attendance_ratio`, which is `attendance_minutes` divided by the module's `contact_hours` converted to minutes, rounded half-up to four decimal places. The evaluator fields `evaluator_id` and `demo_score` start null, `attempt_number` starts at 1, and `credited_contact_hours` starts at 0.

Four rules guard the recording. The referenced session must actually have been taught: a session whose status is not `DELIVERED` is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERED`. Curriculum order is enforced again, this time on the learner rather than the calendar: when the completed module carries a non-null `prerequisite_module_id`, the trainee must already hold a completion on that prerequisite standing at `PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_PASSED`. Attendance must clear the floor: `attendance_ratio` must be greater than or equal to 0.80 — a volunteer who sat exactly four fifths of the class is admitted, and anything strictly below that is refused with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_INSUFFICIENT` and HTTP 422. And a trainee may hold at most one non-voided completion per module: a second live completion for the same trainee and the same module is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLETION`, though a previously voided one does not block a fresh attempt.

A completion is born `RECORDED` and immediately resolves in one of two directions, decided by the module's `requires_skills_demo` flag. When the module needs no demonstration, the completion advances `RECORDED → PASSED` at once and the full passing ledger applies: `credited_contact_hours` is set to the module's `contact_hours`; the trainee's `earned_contact_hours` grows by that amount; the trainee's `passed_core_count` grows by 1 when the module is core; the trainee's `last_qualifying_activity_at` and `readiness_score` are recomputed; and, when the module is `refresher_eligible` and the trainee holds a credential standing at `ACTIVE`, `GRACE`, `EXPIRED`, or `SUSPENDED`, that credential's `refresher_hours_in_term` grows by the credited hours. When the module does require a demonstration, the completion advances `RECORDED → SKILLS_PENDING` instead and waits for a signature. Both automatic edges require the attendance floor to have been met.

Signing a demonstration is a `SkillsEvaluator` act carrying an `outcome` of `PASS` or `FAIL` together with a `demo_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive. The evaluator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the session's `instructor_id` on every path through this operation, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. From `SKILLS_PENDING`, an `outcome` of `PASS` accompanied by a `demo_score` of at least 70 stamps `evaluator_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `demo_score`, advances the completion `SKILLS_PENDING → PASSED`, and applies exactly the same passing ledger set described above. An `outcome` of `FAIL`, or an `outcome` of `PASS` with a `demo_score` strictly below 70, stamps `evaluator_id` and `demo_score` and advances the completion `SKILLS_PENDING → FAILED` instead, crediting no hours.

Failure is not final. Presenting a demonstration against a completion standing at `FAILED` performs a **bounded retake within the same request**: the completion is first reopened `FAILED → SKILLS_PENDING`, with `attempt_number` increased by 1 and both `evaluator_id` and `demo_score` cleared to null, and the freshly supplied outcome is then evaluated against the reopened completion exactly as above, landing it at `PASSED` or back at `FAILED`. The retake is permitted only while `attempt_number` is strictly less than 3; a completion that has already reached the third attempt is refused with `ERR_DEMO_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. The segregation-of-duties comparison applies to the retake path as well as to the first sitting.

Voiding is the coordinator's instrument for withdrawing evidence, optionally carrying a `reason`. It is permitted from `RECORDED`, `SKILLS_PENDING`, `FAILED`, and `PASSED`, in every case requiring only that the owning cohort is not already `CLOSED`; `VOIDED` is terminal. Voiding from `RECORDED`, `SKILLS_PENDING`, or `FAILED` has no ledger consequences, because none of those states ever credited anything. Voiding from `PASSED` fires cascade chain A, six links deep. The completion's `credited_contact_hours` drops to 0. Because of that, the trainee's `earned_contact_hours` falls by the hours just withdrawn, their `passed_core_count` falls by 1 when the module is core, their `readiness_score` is recomputed, and — when that leaves `passed_core_count` below the count of curriculum modules that are both core and active while the trainee stands at `GRADUATED` — the trainee is demoted `GRADUATED → ENROLLED`. Because of that demotion, the trainee's credential, if it stands at `ACTIVE`, moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and their `currency_status` moves `CURRENT → LAPSED`. Because of that suspension, every roster assignment of that trainee standing at `NOTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED` whose activation stands at `REQUESTED`, `ROSTERED`, or `ACTIVATED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Because of those withdrawals, each affected activation recomputes `rostered_count`, `accepted_count`, `deployed_count`, and `fill_status`, so an activation that had been `FILLED` may fall back to `PARTIAL` or even `UNFILLED`. And separately caused by the same demotion, a cohort sitting in `GRADUATION_REVIEW` reverts to `IN_SESSION` and the program's `pending_graduation_count` falls by 1. Completions may be listed and filtered by `trainee_id`, `module_id`, `class_session_id`, `evaluator_id`, and `status`.

### 3.5. Graduation Review and Minting the Credential

Graduation is deliberately a two-person job. Before any individual can graduate, a coordinator must have moved the whole cohort into `GRADUATION_REVIEW`, and it is at that moment that the acting coordinator's opaque `X-User-ID` is captured on `graduation_reviewer_user_id`. An individual graduation then advances the trainee `ENROLLED → GRADUATED` and requires three things: the cohort must be standing at `GRADUATION_REVIEW`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_COHORT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`; the trainee's `passed_core_count` must exactly equal the number of curriculum modules that are both core and active; and no completion belonging to that trainee may still be awaiting its demonstration. A trainee who is short a core module, or who still holds a `SKILLS_PENDING` completion, is refused with `ERR_CORE_MODULES_INCOMPLETE` and HTTP 422. On success, the trainee's `readiness_score` is recomputed using the certified variant described in §3.9.

Minting the **credential** — the plastic ID card the volunteer actually carries — is a separate act naming only the `trainee_id`. It is guarded by the same cohort-in-review and core-completeness rules, reported with the same `ERR_COHORT_NOT_IN_REVIEW` and `ERR_CORE_MODULES_INCOMPLETE` codes, plus two of its own. The trainee must already stand at `GRADUATED` and must not already have a credential row; a second mint for the same volunteer is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_ALREADY_ISSUED`. And the second segregation-of-duties wall applies here: the minting coordinator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `graduation_reviewer_user_id` recorded on that trainee's cohort, with a match refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The acting identifier is recorded on the credential as `issued_by_user_id`, which is an opaque operator identifier and never a reference to a persisted row.

A minted credential is stamped with a `credential_number` formed from the cohort's `cohort_code`, a hyphen, and a four-digit issuance sequence within that cohort ordered by issuance and starting at `0001` — the third credential minted in a cohort coded `CERT-2026A` therefore reads `CERT-2026A-0003`. Its `issued_on` is stamped from the logical clock and never changes again, not even across renewals, while `term_start_on` is also set to that instant but is reset on every later renewal or reinstatement. `reinstatement_count` opens at 0, `refresher_hours_in_term` opens at 0, `status` opens at `ACTIVE`, and the trainee's `currency_status` moves `NOT_CREDENTIALED → CURRENT`.

The term window itself is computed by a procedure whose steps look circular and must therefore be performed in exactly the order given, with no second pass. The base term is always 365 days — never the previously extended term length, however long the last term ran. The `renewal_tier` is resolved from `reinstatement_count` **as observed before this operation touches it**: a count of 0 yields `STANDARD`, a count of 1 or 2 yields `WATCH`, and a count of 3 or more yields `PROBATION`. From that tier comes `required_refresher_hours`: 8.0 hours for `STANDARD`, 12.0 hours for `WATCH`, and 16.0 hours for `PROBATION`. At issuance the count is necessarily 0, so a new credential is always `STANDARD` with 8.0 required hours, no surplus and no extension; `term_start_on` is the logical clock, `expires_on` is 365 days after `term_start_on`, and `grace_ends_on` is a further 60 days after `expires_on`. Under no circumstances is `required_refresher_hours` recomputed from the resulting term length, nor from an incremented `reinstatement_count`.

### 3.6. Keeping Current: Refresher Hours, Renewal, and the Credential Lifecycle

The credential machine is cyclic. `ACTIVE → GRACE → EXPIRED → ACTIVE` is one closed loop, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` is another, and `REVOKED` is the single terminal state, reachable from `ACTIVE`, `GRACE`, `EXPIRED`, and `SUSPENDED` alike. Coordinator-driven lifecycle actions name an `action` of `RENEW`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE`, or `REVOKE`, and before any of them is evaluated the credential's `refresher_hours_in_term` is refreshed from primary evidence.

That refresh is an aggregation over a **half-open term window** running from `term_start_on` inclusive to the current logical clock exclusive. Two sources contribute. The first is the sum of `hours_credited` over that trainee's exercise participations standing at `CREDITED` whose `updated_at` falls inside the window. The second is the sum of `credited_contact_hours` over that trainee's module completions standing at `PASSED` on modules whose `refresher_eligible` flag is true, again with `updated_at` inside the window. Rows in any other state contribute nothing — registered or voided participations, and recorded, pending, or failed completions, are all worth zero — and rows dated before `term_start_on` are excluded even if they were never counted in an earlier term. The two sums are added and the total is rounded half-up to one decimal place once, never per row.

**Renewal** is available from `GRACE`, from `EXPIRED`, and from `ACTIVE` (an early renewal), and all three edges behave identically apart from their effect on the currency dimension. Every renewal requires the trainee to stand at `GRADUATED` and requires `refresher_hours_in_term` to be greater than or equal to `required_refresher_hours` — hours landing exactly on the requirement qualify, and only a strictly smaller figure is refused, with `ERR_REFRESHER_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` and HTTP 422, in which case nothing further is executed. Once the gate is passed, the term is recomputed in the pinned order: the base term is again 365 days; `renewal_tier` is resolved from `reinstatement_count` as it stood **before** this renewal; `required_refresher_hours` follows from that tier; the surplus is the amount by which `refresher_hours_in_term` exceeds `required_refresher_hours`, floored at zero so that an exact match yields no surplus; the extension is the whole-number part of that surplus multiplied by 5 days per surplus hour, capped at 90 days however large the surplus grows; the new term length is 365 days plus that extension; `term_start_on` is set to the logical clock, `expires_on` to the new term length after `term_start_on`, and `grace_ends_on` to 60 days after `expires_on`; only then is `reinstatement_count` increased by 1; and finally `refresher_hours_in_term` is reset to 0.0 because the new term starts empty. A volunteer renewing with 14.6 hours against an 8.0-hour `STANDARD` requirement therefore banks a surplus of 6.6 hours, of which only the whole 6 hours count, buying 30 extra days for a 395-day term — and the tier that governed that renewal remains `STANDARD` even though the credential now carries a `reinstatement_count` of 1. Renewal from `GRACE` also moves the trainee's `currency_status` `LAPSING → CURRENT`; renewal from `EXPIRED` moves it `LAPSED → CURRENT`; renewal from `ACTIVE` leaves it `CURRENT`. All three recompute the trainee's `readiness_score`.

**Suspension** moves the credential `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, either because a coordinator asked for it or because the trainee was demoted out of graduation by a voided completion. It carries three consequences: the trainee's `currency_status` moves `CURRENT → LAPSED`; every roster assignment of that trainee standing at `NOTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED` whose activation stands at `REQUESTED`, `ROSTERED`, or `ACTIVATED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; and each affected activation recomputes `rostered_count`, `accepted_count`, and `fill_status`. **Reinstatement** closes that loop with `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, requiring the trainee to stand at `GRADUATED` and to hold a `passed_core_count` exactly equal to the number of core active curriculum modules; it moves the trainee's `currency_status` `LAPSED → CURRENT` and recomputes their `readiness_score`. Reinstatement pointedly does **not** increase `reinstatement_count` — only renewal does — so a volunteer who is suspended and reinstated repeatedly never drifts into a stricter tier, while one who lets the card lapse and renews repeatedly does. **Revocation** moves the credential to `REVOKED` from any live state, drives the trainee's `currency_status` to `REVOKED`, withdraws every roster assignment of that trainee standing at `NOTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED`, and has each affected activation recompute its counts and `fill_status`.

Expiry is not driven by any user. A coordinator-only operational sweep, scoped to a `program_id`, advances the passage of time for one program at the current logical clock and reports four figures: `swept_credential_count` for the credentials it examined, `graced_count` and `expired_count` for the credentials it moved, and `no_response_count` for the call-out notifications it timed out. A credential standing at `ACTIVE` whose `expires_on` the clock has reached or passed, while the clock is still strictly before `grace_ends_on`, moves `ACTIVE → GRACE` and mirrors the trainee's `currency_status` `CURRENT → LAPSING`. A credential standing at `GRACE` whose `grace_ends_on` the clock has reached or passed moves `GRACE → EXPIRED`, mirrors the trainee's `currency_status` `LAPSING → LAPSED`, withdraws every roster assignment of that trainee standing at `NOTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED` whose activation stands at `REQUESTED`, `ROSTERED`, or `ACTIVATED`, and has each affected activation recompute `rostered_count`, `accepted_count`, and `fill_status`. Credentials may be listed and filtered by `trainee_id` and `status`, and a single credential read exposes its term window, `renewal_tier`, `required_refresher_hours`, and `refresher_hours_in_term`.

### 3.7. The Equipment Cabinet: Issuance, Return, and Loss

Kit is tracked as individual **assets**. A `LogisticsOfficer` registers one against a program with `program_id`, a globally unique `asset_tag`, a `kit_type` of `BACKPACK_STANDARD`, `BACKPACK_ADVANCED`, or `RADIO_KIT`, and a `unit_value_cents` replacement value of at least 1 cent. A new asset opens with `status` `IN_STOCK`, `condition` `NEW`, and `issue_count` at 0. An asset's `status` thereafter is one of `IN_STOCK`, `ISSUED`, `RETIRED`, or `LOST`, and its `condition` is one of `NEW`, `SERVICEABLE`, `DAMAGED`, or `WRITTEN_OFF`. Assets may be listed and filtered by `program_id`, `kit_type`, and `status`.

Issuance is unusual in that **the caller never names the asset**. The request supplies only the `trainee_id`, the `kit_type` wanted, and the `expected_program_version` concurrency token described in §2.8; the server picks the physical item. The candidate pool is every asset belonging to the trainee's program whose `kit_type` matches the request, whose `status` is `IN_STOCK`, and whose `condition` is `NEW` or `SERVICEABLE`; assets that are issued, retired, or lost are excluded outright. Candidates are ranked by `issue_count` ascending, so the least-circulated item goes out first; ties are broken by `unit_value_cents` ascending, and any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending. The asset's `id` is never a sort input, not even as a last resort. An empty candidate pool is refused with `ERR_NO_ASSET_AVAILABLE`, in which case no issuance row is created and the program's `equipment_committed_cents` is untouched.

Two gates bracket that selection. The volunteer must hold a live credential — a trainee whose credential standing is not `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`, including a trainee with no credential at all, is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ACTIVE` — and a trainee may hold at most one outstanding issuance per `kit_type`, a second live one being refused with `ERR_TRAINEE_ALREADY_ISSUED_KIT`. The fiscal gate is evaluated **after** the asset has been chosen and **before** it is committed: the program's `equipment_committed_cents` plus the chosen asset's `unit_value_cents` must be less than or equal to the program's `equipment_budget_cents`. A total landing exactly on the ceiling is allowed; only a total strictly above it is refused, with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, and again no issuance row is created. Because the ranking prefers cheaper items on a tie, a program close to its ceiling may still be able to issue a low-value kit when a high-value one of the same type would have been refused. On commit, the chosen asset moves to `ISSUED`, its `issue_count` grows by 1, the program's `equipment_committed_cents` grows by the asset's `unit_value_cents`, and the program's `version` is incremented. The new issuance row records `asset_id` for the item chosen, `issued_on` from the logical clock, `issued_by_user_id` from the acting opaque identifier, `status` `ISSUED`, with `returned_on`, `lost_reported_on`, `months_held`, `residual_pct`, and `settlement_variant` all null and `settlement_cents` at 0. Issuances may be listed and filtered by `trainee_id`, `asset_id`, and `status`.

Returning a kit moves the issuance to `RETURNED`, along the edge `ISSUED → RETURNED`, and requires a `condition` of `SERVICEABLE` or `DAMAGED`. `returned_on` is stamped from the logical clock, the asset's `condition` is set to the supplied value, and the asset's `status` becomes `IN_STOCK` when the condition is `SERVICEABLE` — putting it back in the candidate pool — or `RETIRED` when the condition is `DAMAGED`, permanently removing it. Either way the program's `equipment_committed_cents` falls by the asset's `unit_value_cents` and the program's `version` is incremented.

Reporting a kit lost, optionally with a free-text `note`, moves the issuance `ISSUED → LOST_WRITTEN_OFF` and is the only place a settlement is computed. `lost_reported_on` is stamped from the logical clock. `months_held` is the number of whole days between `issued_on` and `lost_reported_on`, divided by 30 and rounded down to a whole number, so a kit held 89 days counts as 2 months and one held exactly 90 days counts as 3. `residual_pct` is 1.00 reduced by 0.015 for each such month, floored at 0.40 so that no kit ever settles below 40% of its value however long it was held, and the result is rounded half-up to four decimal places.

Which settlement applies depends on the volunteer's standing at the loss-report instant, and the two variants differ materially on identical facts. When the trainee's credential stands at `ACTIVE`, `GRACE`, or `EXPIRED`, `settlement_variant` is `DEPRECIATED_WRITEOFF` and `settlement_cents` is the asset's `unit_value_cents` multiplied by `residual_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents. When the trainee has no credential at all, or their credential stands at `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, `settlement_variant` is `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, depreciation is not applied at all, and `settlement_cents` is simply the asset's full `unit_value_cents`. The asset then moves to `status` `LOST` with `condition` `WRITTEN_OFF`, the program's `equipment_committed_cents` falls by the asset's `unit_value_cents`, and the program's `version` is incremented.

Finally the volunteer's `equipment_liability_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `settlement_cents` over every one of that trainee's issuances standing at `LOST_WRITTEN_OFF`, taken in `created_at` order for audit; returned and outstanding issuances contribute nothing. As §2.9 insists, each line is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded lines are then added — two lines that each fall on a half cent produce a total one cent different from the forbidden alternative of summing the raw products and rounding once.

### 3.8. Exercises, Participation, and After-Action Learning

Skills decay without practice, so the program runs drills. An `EmergencyManager` schedules an **exercise** against a program with `program_id`, a `title`, an `exercise_type` of `TABLETOP`, `FUNCTIONAL`, or `FULL_SCALE`, a `starts_at` and an `ends_at` that must fall strictly after `starts_at`, and a `base_credit_hours` figure strictly greater than zero and carrying at most one decimal place, which is what a baseline participant earns. A new exercise opens at `PLANNED` with `aar_summary` null and `accepted_participant_count` at 0. Exercises may be listed and filtered by `program_id` and `status`.

Volunteers are registered onto an exercise individually with `exercise_id`, `trainee_id`, and a `role_played` of `TEAM_LEADER`, `SEARCH_RESCUE`, `MEDICAL`, `LOGISTICS`, or `OBSERVER`. Registration resolves the row's `role_multiplier` from that role — 1.50 for `TEAM_LEADER`, 1.25 for `SEARCH_RESCUE`, 1.25 for `MEDICAL`, 1.00 for `LOGISTICS`, and 0.50 for `OBSERVER` — opens the row at `REGISTERED`, and leaves `hours_credited` at 0 until the after-action report has been accepted. Participations may be listed and filtered by `exercise_id`, `trainee_id`, and `status`.

The exercise lifecycle runs `PLANNED → RUNNING → COMPLETED → AAR_SUBMITTED → AAR_ACCEPTED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from `PLANNED` or `RUNNING`, and with `AAR_ACCEPTED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. Each advance names the desired `to_status`. Starting an exercise requires the logical clock to have reached or passed its `starts_at` and requires at least one participation standing at `REGISTERED`; completing it — the move to `COMPLETED` — requires the clock to have reached or passed its `ends_at`. Submitting the after-action report moves the exercise to `AAR_SUBMITTED` and supplies an `aar_summary` of at least 40 characters, which is written onto the exercise. The emergency manager drives every one of those advances and may also cancel; **accepting** the after-action report, however, is reserved to a `ProgramCoordinator`, which keeps the reviewer of the learning separate from the agency that ran the drill.

Acceptance is where credit is finally paid. Every participation of the exercise standing at `REGISTERED` moves to `CREDITED`, with `hours_credited` set to the exercise's `base_credit_hours` multiplied by that row's `role_multiplier`, rounded half-up to one decimal place, per row. A four-hour functional exercise therefore pays 6.0 hours to a team leader, 5.0 hours to a search-and-rescue or medical participant, 4.0 hours to a logistics participant, and 2.0 hours to an observer. Already-credited and voided rows are skipped. Each crediting adds the credited hours to that trainee's credential `refresher_hours_in_term` when the credential's term window contains the crediting instant, and recomputes that trainee's `last_qualifying_activity_at` and `readiness_score`. The exercise's `accepted_participant_count` is then recomputed as the number of its participations standing at `CREDITED`. Cancelling an exercise from `PLANNED` or `RUNNING` instead moves every participation standing at `REGISTERED` to `VOIDED`, and voided rows never contribute hours to anything.

### 3.9. Activation, the Call-Out Roster, and Responder Readiness

When an incident opens, emergency management raises an **activation** against the program: `program_id`, an `incident_number` unique within that program, the `requesting_agency`, a `severity` of `ROUTINE`, `URGENT`, or `CRITICAL`, and `requested_responders`, the headcount wanted, which must be at least 1. A new activation opens at `REQUESTED` with `fill_status` `UNFILLED`, all three of `rostered_count`, `accepted_count`, and `deployed_count` at 0, and `response_deadline_at` null. Its lifecycle runs `REQUESTED → ROSTERED → ACTIVATED → DEMOBILIZED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from `REQUESTED` or `ROSTERED`; `DEMOBILIZED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Activations may be listed and filtered by `program_id`, `status`, and `fill_status`.

The three counts are derived, not reported. `rostered_count` is the number of the activation's assignments standing at `NOTIFIED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DEPLOYED`, or `RELEASED`; `accepted_count` narrows that to `ACCEPTED`, `DEPLOYED`, or `RELEASED`; and `deployed_count` narrows it further to `DEPLOYED` or `RELEASED`. From the first of those comes `fill_status`: `UNFILLED` while `rostered_count` is 0, `PARTIAL` while it is above 0 but below `requested_responders`, and `FILLED` once it has reached or passed `requested_responders`.

Building the roster is the system's most opinionated selector, and it runs when emergency management moves the activation `REQUESTED → ROSTERED`. The **eligibility filter** admits a trainee only when every one of the following holds: the trainee belongs to the same program as the activation; their `enrollment_status` is `GRADUATED`; their `deployment_hold` flag is false; they hold a credential standing at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`; they hold no roster assignment standing at `NOTIFIED`, `ACCEPTED`, or `DEPLOYED` on any activation that is itself standing at `REQUESTED`, `ROSTERED`, or `ACTIVATED`; and — this clause applies only when the activation's `severity` is `CRITICAL` — their credential stands specifically at `ACTIVE`. In other words, a volunteer inside their grace period is a legitimate responder for a routine or urgent call-out but is excluded from a critical one.

Ranking is a **waterfall over two buckets**. The first bucket is the eligible volunteers whose credential stands at `ACTIVE`; the second is those whose credential stands at `GRACE`. The first bucket is exhausted entirely before any member of the second is selected, no matter how the two compare on any other measure. Within a bucket, candidates are ordered by `readiness_score` descending, then by `last_deployed_at` ascending with never-deployed volunteers placed first, then by `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break. The trainee's `id` is never a sort input, not even as a last resort. The number taken is the smaller of `requested_responders` and the size of the candidate set: an empty candidate set is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RESPONDERS`, leaving the activation at `REQUESTED` with `fill_status` still `UNFILLED`; a candidate set smaller than the request rosters everyone available, moves the activation to `ROSTERED`, and leaves `fill_status` at `PARTIAL`; and a candidate set at least as large as the request rosters exactly `requested_responders` volunteers and leaves `fill_status` at `FILLED`.

Each selected volunteer gets an assignment row opening at `NOTIFIED`, with `source` `AUTO_ROSTER`, `notified_at` stamped from the logical clock, `responded_at` null, and `selection_rank` set to their 1-based position in the ranking just described. The activation's `response_deadline_at` is stamped as the logical clock plus the window its `severity` earns: four hours for `ROUTINE`, two hours for `URGENT`, and one hour for `CRITICAL`. `rostered_count` and `fill_status` are then recomputed and the activation's `version` is incremented.

A responder may also be **added by hand** to an existing roster, supplying the `trainee_id` and the `expected_activation_version` token of §2.8. A manual addition must satisfy exactly the same eligibility filter as the automatic selector, including the critical-severity restriction, and a volunteer who fails it is refused with `ERR_TRAINEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The new row opens at `NOTIFIED` with `source` `MANUAL_ADD` and a `selection_rank` one greater than the highest rank already present on that activation; `rostered_count` and `fill_status` are recomputed and the activation's `version` is incremented.

The notified volunteer answers for themselves, supplying a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and may answer only their own assignment. Answering requires the activation to be standing at `ROSTERED`, failing which the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_ROSTERED`, and requires the logical clock to be strictly before `response_deadline_at`; a clock that has reached the deadline is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Accepting additionally requires the volunteer's credential to stand at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ACTIVE`. Accepting moves the assignment `NOTIFIED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes the activation's `accepted_count` and `fill_status`; declining moves it `NOTIFIED → DECLINED`, stamps `responded_at`, and recomputes `rostered_count` and `fill_status`. Both `DECLINED` and `NO_RESPONSE` are terminal for an assignment, as are `RELEASED` and `WITHDRAWN`.

Silence is handled by the same operational sweep that ages credentials: an assignment standing at `NOTIFIED` whose activation's `response_deadline_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves `NOTIFIED → NO_RESPONSE`, and the activation's `rostered_count` and `fill_status` are recomputed. Every such move is counted in the sweep's `no_response_count`.

Once at least one volunteer has accepted — `accepted_count` of at least 1 — emergency management may move the activation `ROSTERED → ACTIVATED`, after which accepted responders can be sent to work by naming a `to_status`. `ACCEPTED → DEPLOYED` requires the activation itself to be standing at `ACTIVATED`, stamps the trainee's `last_deployed_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes `deployed_count`. `DEPLOYED → RELEASED` closes the volunteer's tour: it recomputes the trainee's `last_qualifying_activity_at` and `readiness_score` — a completed deployment is a qualifying activity, an accepted-but-never-deployed assignment is not — and recomputes `deployed_count`. Demobilising the activation `ACTIVATED → DEMOBILIZED` requires that no assignment is still standing at `DEPLOYED`; it moves every assignment still at `NOTIFIED` to `NO_RESPONSE`, every assignment still at `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, and recomputes the activation's counts and `fill_status`. Cancelling an activation from `ROSTERED` moves every assignment standing at `NOTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN` and recomputes the counts and `fill_status`; cancelling from `REQUESTED` has no assignments to touch. Assignments may be listed and filtered by `activation_id`, `trainee_id`, and `status`.

**Readiness** is the number that decides who gets called first, and it is computed in nine pinned steps of which only the last rounds. First, the total core teaching load is the sum of `contact_hours` over every curriculum module that is both core and active. Second, the volunteer's earned core hours are accumulated in a variant-specific way described below. Third, the core ratio is 0.0 when the total core load is 0, and otherwise the earned core hours divided by that total, capped at 1.0 so that over-achievement cannot inflate the figure. Fourth, the demonstration average is 0.0 when the variant's demonstration set is empty, and otherwise the sum of the scores in that set divided by the number of scores in it. Fifth, the recency factor is derived from the whole number of days between the volunteer's `last_qualifying_activity_at` and the logical clock: 1.00 when that gap is 90 days or fewer, 0.90 when it is more than 90 but no more than 180, 0.75 when it is more than 180 but no more than 365, 0.50 when it exceeds 365, and 0.50 when `last_qualifying_activity_at` is null because the volunteer has never had a qualifying activity at all. Sixth, the exercise bonus is 2.5 points for each of that volunteer's exercise participations standing at `CREDITED`, capped at 10.0 points — four credited exercises reach the cap and a fifth adds nothing. Seventh, the base is 60.0 multiplied by the core ratio plus 40.0 multiplied by the demonstration average divided by 100.0. Eighth, the raw figure is the base multiplied by the recency factor, with the exercise bonus then added. Ninth and last, the score is that raw figure clamped to no less than 0.00 and no more than 100.00, and rounded half-up to two decimal places. Rounding any intermediate result — the core ratio, the demonstration average, the base, or the raw figure — is forbidden.

Two variants of that computation exist, differing only in which completions feed steps two and four. The **certified** variant counts, as earned core hours, only completions standing at `PASSED` on core modules, and its demonstration set is the `demo_score` of completions standing at `PASSED` whose module has `requires_skills_demo` true. This is the only variant ever written to the trainee's stored `readiness_score`, and it is the variant the roster selector ranks on. The **provisional** variant is never persisted: on top of everything the certified variant counts, it also credits completions standing at `SKILLS_PENDING` on core modules with **half** of their module's `contact_hours` toward earned core hours, and enters each of them into the demonstration set with an imputed score of 70. Identical stored data therefore yields a strictly higher provisional figure than certified figure whenever any pending completion exists, which is exactly what a coordinator wants to see on a graduation-review dashboard. In both variants, voided, recorded, and failed completions contribute nothing whatsoever. A dedicated computed read exposes the two side by side, returning `certified_readiness_score`, `provisional_readiness_score`, `core_hours_total`, `core_hours_earned_certified`, `core_hours_earned_provisional`, `core_ratio_certified`, `core_ratio_provisional`, `demo_avg_certified`, `demo_avg_provisional`, `recency_factor`, and `exercise_bonus`.

The stored `readiness_score` is refreshed on every event that could change it: any completion moving into or out of `PASSED`, any participation moving `REGISTERED → CREDITED`, a trainee moving `ENROLLED → GRADUATED`, a credential renewal or reinstatement, and an assignment moving `DEPLOYED → RELEASED`. `last_qualifying_activity_at` is itself derived, as the latest of the `updated_at` of any of that volunteer's passed completions, the `updated_at` of any of their credited participations, and the `updated_at` of any of their released assignments; it is null when the volunteer has none of the three.
