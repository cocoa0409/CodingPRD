# Emissions Monitoring & Allowance Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fossil fuel electric generating station lives under two clocks at once. One is the operational clock of the plant: boilers dispatch, scrubbers come out of service for maintenance, analysers drift and have to be zeroed every day, and a truckload of coal arrives that nobody has yet assayed for sulfur. The other is the regulatory clock: a calendar quarter closes, an electronic emissions file has to be validated, submitted and signed by a legally accountable representative, and at the end of the compliance period the station must hold enough allowances to cover every ton it actually emitted. When the two clocks disagree — when a monitor goes out of control halfway through a quarter that has already been electronically reported, or when a scrubber outage collides with a relative accuracy test audit — the station's compliance position changes retroactively, and every downstream number has to move with it.

This service is the system of record that keeps those two clocks reconciled. It tracks continuous emission monitoring systems and their daily calibrations, the quality-assurance audits that certify them, the fuel samples that support heat input determination by analysis, and the hour-by-hour operating and emissions record that all of this produces. It rolls those hours into quarterly reports, enforces the missing-data substitution procedures that price an unmonitored hour punitively rather than optimistically, evaluates source-specific permit limits on rolling averages, and manages allowance holdings across four programs through to a true-up at the compliance deadline. Because the same operating hour can be worth materially different emissions depending on which determination method was used, and because a single out-of-control monitor can invalidate hours, revert a validated report, and turn a permit exceedance into a non-event, the system is written around cascades: an operation is defined not only by what it changes but by everything that must change with it.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the person who submits an electronic report may not be the person who certifies it, and the trader who proposes an allowance movement may not be the party who records or settles it. Second, **allowances are a conserved quantity**: tonnage is never minted or destroyed, only moved between vintage buckets, surrendered, or encumbered against a future vintage — and every settlement records the exact per-bucket amounts it took so that a reversal restores the account without drift. All endpoints described below are mounted under the `/emissions` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including every read — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If `X-User-ID` is missing or blank, or if `X-User-Role` is missing or does not spell one of the six declared role names exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Note carefully that an *unrecognised* role string is an authentication failure, not an authorization failure. A recognised role that is simply not permitted on the requested endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and that check runs before any identifier in the path is resolved, so an unauthorized caller can never probe which resources exist.

The six roles are:

- **`RegulatoryReviewer`** — the permitting and enforcement authority. Registers facilities, units and permit limits, opens allowance accounts, dispositions quarterly reports and excess emission events, and runs the compliance-deadline true-up.
- **`FacilityOperator`** — plant operations staff for one facility. Records daily calibrations, operating hours, fuel samples, dispatch changes and control equipment outages.
- **`QAAnalyst`** — the quality assurance specialist. Owns monitor certification status, relative accuracy test audits and their retests, and fuel sample laboratory analysis.
- **`EmissionsReportPreparer`** — prepares, validates and electronically submits the quarterly report, applies missing data substitution, and files excess emission reports. Cannot certify what it submitted.
- **`DesignatedRepresentative`** — the legally accountable signatory for the source. Signs and revokes compliance certifications, and records, settles or reverses allowance transactions. Never proposes a transaction and never submits a report.
- **`AllowanceTrader`** — the market-facing role. Proposes allowance purchases, transfers and voluntary surrenders, and has no authority to record or settle what it proposed.

When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the identity of the acting facility: ownership checks compare the `operator_org` of the facility reached through the request path or body against `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). `FacilityOperator` is the only entity-backed role in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `RegulatoryReviewer`, `QAAnalyst`, `EmissionsReportPreparer`, `DesignatedRepresentative` or `AllowanceTrader`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands provenance — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a quarterly report when an `EmissionsReportPreparer` submits, and as `certifier_user_id` on a compliance certification when a `DesignatedRepresentative` signs — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those five roles are unscoped: they may address any facility.

Ten write operations are subject to the `FacilityOperator` scope check: adding a unit to a facility, installing a monitoring system on a unit, recording a daily calibration, appending a single operating hour, appending operating hours in bulk, running a missing-data substitution over a unit, recording a fuel sample, planning a control outage window, transitioning a control outage window, and changing a unit's dispatch position. On all ten the rejection code is `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filtering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when absent, and when supplied must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when absent, and when supplied must be an integer of at least 0. An explicit value outside those bounds is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific collection states otherwise, and the `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more`.

Collections also accept domain filters, all optional and combinable, each an exact-match filter on the named field:

- Facilities: `state_code`, `status`, `compliance_posture`, `operator_org`.
- Units: `facility_id`, `dispatch_state`, `control_status`, `dispatch_restricted`, `primary_fuel`.
- Monitoring systems: `unit_id`, `parameter`, `system_type`, `qa_status`, `cal_status`.
- Daily calibrations: `monitoring_system_id` and `result` on the cross-system collection, `result` alone on a single system's calibration history.
- Relative accuracy test audits: `monitoring_system_id`, `retest_of_rata_test_id`, `status`, `ra_basis`.
- Fuel samples: `unit_id`, `status`, `fuel_type`, `lot_identifier`.
- Quarterly reports: `unit_id`, `year`, `quarter`, `status`.
- Certifications: `quarterly_report_id`, `status`, `certifier_user_id` on the cross-report collection; `status` alone on a single report's certification history.
- Operating hours: `unit_id`, `quarterly_report_id`, `fuel_sample_id`, `data_quality`, `substitution_method`, `heat_input_method`, plus `from_hour_utc` and `to_hour_utc`, which bound `hour_begin_utc` as a half-open interval — inclusive of `from_hour_utc`, exclusive of `to_hour_utc`.
- Permit limits: `unit_id`, `pollutant`, `averaging_basis`, `evaluation_status`.
- Excess emission events: `permit_limit_id`, `unit_id`, `status`.
- Allowance accounts: `facility_id`, `program`, `compliance_year`, `status`.
- Allowance transactions: `allowance_account_id`, `counterparty_account_id`, `transaction_type`, `status`, `origin`, `vintage_year`.
- Control outage windows: `unit_id`, `status`, `outage_type`, `control_device`.

Two collections invert the default order because operators read them newest-first: a single monitoring system's calibration history, and a single report's certification history.

### 2.4. The Logical Compliance Clock

Because compliance is defined by deadlines, the server does not read the wall clock. It maintains a **logical current UTC instant**, which every server-assigned timestamp and every temporal comparison in this specification reads. Before any client sets it, the logical clock stands at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Any authenticated role may advance or reset it by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `expired_monitoring_system_ids`, the identifiers of the monitoring systems that the move expired.

Setting the clock is not inert. Every monitoring system whose `cal_status` is `CAL_CURRENT` and whose `last_calibration_utc` is more than 26 hours before the new instant moves to `CAL_EXPIRED` and has its `version` incremented by one. Daily calibration validity is therefore 26 hours, and a system that has not been calibrated inside that window stops producing measured data for hours created afterwards. Operating hours already persisted are **not** reclassified by the sweep; only hours created after it are classified `UNAVAILABLE` on account of the expiry.

### 2.5. Resource Identity and Audit Fields

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp — an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request against the row.

Two record types are append-only ledger entries and are the exception: a **daily calibration** and an **operating hour** carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, because neither has an update endpoint. To let a client chain a further version-guarded write without an intervening read, each of these records instead reports the parent version it left behind: a daily calibration exposes `monitoring_system_version_after`, and an operating hour exposes `quarterly_report_version_after`. Both are the parent's `version` observed *after* the write and after every cascade it triggered, and both are therefore always at least 2. An allowance transaction, which does carry `updated_at` and `version` of its own, likewise records `allowance_account_version_after` for the same purpose.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding

Every rounding operation in this specification uses the **round half up** convention. Precision is per-quantity and is load-bearing, because report totals are sums of already-rounded per-hour values rather than a single rounding of the raw products:

- Heat input in mmBtu — `heat_input_mmbtu`, `total_heat_input_mmbtu` — three decimal places.
- Pollutant mass in pounds — `so2_mass_lbs`, `nox_mass_lbs`, `total_so2_lbs`, `total_nox_lbs`, `certified_so2_lbs_ytd`, `certified_so2_lbs_snapshot` — one decimal place.
- Mass in tons — `co2_mass_tons`, `total_co2_tons`, `total_so2_tons` — three decimal places.
- Allowance tonnage — the three holdings buckets, `quantity_tons`, `surrendered_tons`, `penalty_deducted_tons`, `shortfall_tons`, and the three `debited_*` amounts — three decimal places. `obligation_tons` and `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` are whole integers, never fractional.
- Availability percentages — `data_availability_pct`, `monitor_data_availability_pct`, `monitor_data_availability_pct_at_substitution` — one decimal place, always between 0.0 and 100.0.
- Calibration error percentages — `zero_error_pct_of_span`, `span_error_pct_of_span` — two decimal places.
- Relative accuracy statistics — `reference_mean`, `cems_mean`, `mean_difference`, `confidence_coefficient` and `bias_adjustment_factor` — four decimal places, with the intermediate sample standard deviation carried to six decimal places. `relative_accuracy_pct` is one decimal place.
- Permit limit quantities — `limit_value`, `limit_value_snapshot`, `observed_value`, `exceedance_magnitude` — four decimal places. `current_rolling_value` is four decimal places for a rate limit, one decimal place for an hourly-average mass limit and three decimal places for a cumulative annual tonnage limit.

Where a quantity is derived by a ceiling rather than by rounding — `obligation_tons` and `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` — the value is raised to the next whole number whenever any fractional part remains, and left unchanged when the value is already integral. A ceiling is never a round.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards

Five write operations are guarded against acting on a stale parent. Each requires a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent row; if the value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is persisted. These are not races: a second write of the same kind is perfectly legitimate product behaviour, so no state precondition rejects it, and the version mismatch is the only signal that the client's view of the parent predates another writer's change.

- Recording a **daily calibration** requires `expected_monitoring_system_version` matching the target monitoring system's current `version`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_MONITORING_SYSTEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This catches the case where an earlier calibration already moved `cal_status` or `consecutive_failed_calibrations`, or drove the out-of-control cascade, so the submitter's view of the QA state is behind.
- Appending a single **operating hour** requires `expected_quarterly_report_version` matching the resolved report's current `version`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Appending **operating hours in bulk** requires the same `expected_quarterly_report_version`, with the same `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on mismatch. This catches the case where the report's totals, `substituted_hour_count` or `status` were already changed by another writer — including by the out-of-control cascade's report recompute.
- Signing a **compliance certification** requires `expected_quarterly_report_version` matching the report's current `version`, again with `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on mismatch.
- Proposing an **allowance transaction** requires `expected_allowance_account_version` matching the target account's current `version`. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), preventing a proposer whose view of holdings, `obligation_tons` and `shortfall_tons` predates a settled movement from acting on it.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.8. Rule Precedence: The Order Checks Are Applied

Multiple rules frequently apply to the same request. The server evaluates them in a **single fixed global order** and returns the first violation it finds; it never reports a later violation in place of an earlier one. The order is banded — authentication and authorization first, then request shape and existence, then state, then operational safety, then fiscal rules — and within a band the sequence below is exact.

**Authentication and authorization band.** (1) `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. (2) `X-User-Role` present and one of the six declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. (3) The role is permitted on this endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — evaluated before any existence lookup. (4) A `FacilityOperator` is acting within its own facility, else `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. (5) On certification signing only, the signer differs from the preparer who submitted, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — deliberately ahead of the version guard, so a self-certification attempt reports the duties conflict even when the client also holds a stale version.

**Shape and existence band.** (6) The request body and query shape are well formed — no unknown enum values, no wrong JSON types, every required field present — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This rule is deliberately blind to the determination-method field families: a syntactically well-formed operating hour that mixes CEMS and Appendix D measurement fields passes this check and is caught much later by `ERR_METHOD_FIELD_MISMATCH`. Shape validation precedes every 404, so a request that both carries an unknown enum value and references a non-existent parent returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). (7) Pagination bounds, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. (8) Every identifier in the request **path** resolves, checked outermost-first, so a unit create under a non-existent facility reports `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` before the body is considered. (9) Every foreign key supplied in the request **body** resolves, checked in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint; this covers `allowance_account_id` and then `counterparty_account_id` on a transaction proposal, `fuel_sample_id` on a single or bulk operating-hour append, and `retest_of_rata_test_id` on scheduling an audit. (10) A referenced child actually belongs to the parent named in the path — a `fuel_sample_id` must belong to the unit in the path, a `retest_of_rata_test_id` must belong to the monitoring system in the path, and a certification named for revocation must belong to its report. A cross-parent reference is reported as the relevant *not found* error rather than as a scope violation, so the caller learns nothing about another parent's data.

**State band.** (11) The version guard of §2.7 — the first rule of this band, and therefore ahead of every state, safety and fiscal rule below it. A stale `expected_allowance_account_version` on an over-drawing surrender returns 412, never `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALLOWANCES`. (12) The requested transition is declared from the entity's current state, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; this also covers terminal-state lockdown, repeat invocation of a one-shot action, dispatching a restricted unit online, activating an outage on a unit that is online, and rescheduling an audit that has not failed. (13) `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD`. (14) `ERR_DUPLICATE_MONITORING_SYSTEM`. (15) `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORISPL_CODE`. (16) `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATING_HOUR`. (17) `ERR_ACCOUNT_TRUE_UP_LOCKED` — ahead of the fiscal sufficiency rule, so a locked account rejects an over-drawing surrender with the lock error. (18) `ERR_UNIT_NOT_ONLINE`. (19) `ERR_MONITOR_OUT_OF_CONTROL`. (20) `ERR_MONITOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` — the weaker of the two monitor-status rules, so an out-of-control system always reports the specific condition rather than the generic one. (21) `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_CURRENT`. (22) `ERR_FUEL_SAMPLE_NOT_ANALYZED`. (23) `ERR_FUEL_ANALYSIS_INVALIDATED`. (24) `ERR_TRUE_UP_BEFORE_DEADLINE` — ahead of the accepted-reports rule, so an early true-up on an incomplete year reports the deadline. (25) `ERR_REPORTS_NOT_ALL_ACCEPTED`. (26) `ERR_RA_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`. (27) `ERR_RETEST_ALREADY_EXISTS`.

**Operational safety band.** (28) `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RATA_RUNS`. (29) `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. (30) `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. (31) `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_IN_PAST` — after the overlap rule, so a window that is both in the past and overlapping reports the overlap. (32) `ERR_CONTROL_OUTAGE_OVERLAP`. (33) `ERR_CONCURRENT_OUTAGE_LIMIT` — after the per-device overlap rule. (34) `ERR_HOUR_OUTSIDE_REPORT_PERIOD`. (35) `ERR_METHOD_FIELD_MISMATCH`. (36) `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. (37) `ERR_DATA_AVAILABILITY_BELOW_FLOOR` — after the monitor-status rules, so an out-of-control monitor reports `ERR_MONITOR_OUT_OF_CONTROL` even though availability has already collapsed. (38) `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTABLE_HOURS`. (39) `ERR_BULK_SIZE_INVALID`.

**Fiscal band.** (40) `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`. (41) `ERR_PENALTY_TRANSACTION_NOT_CLIENT_CREATABLE`. (42) `ERR_INVALID_COUNTERPARTY`. (43) `ERR_VINTAGE_YEAR_INVALID`. (44) `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALLOWANCE_ACCOUNT`. (45) `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_LIMIT`. (46) `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALLOWANCES`. (47) `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED` — evaluated last of all, so a reason-less rejection of a report that is not certified reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` instead.

The numeric range rule at step 36 applies to every creating or modifying request that accepts a numeric input, and enforces these bounds: `op_time_fraction` from 0.0 through 1.0 inclusive; `co2_conc_pct` greater than 0.0 and at most 20.0; `so2_conc_ppm` from 0 through 2000 inclusive; `nox_rate_lb_mmbtu` from 0.0 through 2.0 inclusive; `flow_scfh` strictly greater than 0; `gross_load_mw` at least 0; `sulfur_content_pct` from 0.0 through 10.0 inclusive; `gcv_btu_per_lb` from 1000 through 20000 inclusive; `span_value` strictly greater than 0; `calibration_error_spec_pct` greater than 0.0 and at most 20.0; `limit_value` strictly greater than 0; `max_concurrent_control_outages` from 0 through 10 inclusive; and `quarter` from 1 through 4 inclusive. Violations of any of these report `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and Hierarchical Consistency

Three operations in this system are **four-link cascades**: each link is caused by the previous link's effect rather than by the original request, and every link must be applied before the response is returned. Consistency is immediate; there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in the system.

The **out-of-control cascade** fires when a monitoring system's `qa_status` becomes `OUT_OF_CONTROL`, whether by a calibration verdict or by a declaration. Link one sets the system's QA state and stamps `out_of_control_since_utc`. Link two, caused by the QA state change and not by the calibration itself, retroactively invalidates the unit's affected operating hours. Link three, caused by those hour flips, recomputes the affected quarterly reports and reverts a validated report to draft. Link four, caused by the report recompute, re-evaluates the unit's permit limits and raises or withdraws excess emission events accordingly. §3.2 gives the full detail of each link.

The **true-up cascade** fires when an allowance account is trued up and the waterfall leaves a residual shortfall. Link one drains holdings and records the surrender; link two, caused by the shortfall, creates the punitive deduction against the next vintage; link three, caused by the account now carrying a shortfall, flips the facility's `compliance_posture` to `DEFICIT`; link four, caused by that posture change, restricts dispatch on every unit of the facility. §3.9 gives the detail.

The **compensation cascade** fires when a settled allowance transaction is reversed, and unwinds the true-up cascade symmetrically: holdings are credited back, a reversed true-up surrender releases the account lock and reverses its penalty rows, the facility posture leaves `DEFICIT`, and dispatch restriction is lifted on every unit. §3.9 gives the detail.

Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly one per distinct change applied to it. A parent's `version` moving is what invalidates a client's stale-version guard, which makes the cascades the deliberate serialization points of the system: a client that read a report before an out-of-control cascade cannot append an hour to it afterwards without re-reading.

### 2.10. The Conserved Allowance Quantity

Allowance tonnage is conserved. For every allowance account, at every observable moment, the sum of the three holdings buckets — `holdings_current_vintage_tons`, `holdings_prior_vintage_tons` and `holdings_next_vintage_tons` — plus the total quantity of the account's settled surrenders, plus the total quantity of its settled penalty deductions, minus the total quantity of its settled purchases and transfers in, plus the total quantity of its settled transfers out, equals the account's opening endowment. The system never mints allowances to satisfy an obligation.

The single relief valve is `holdings_next_vintage_tons`, which is the **only** bucket allowed to go negative, and only by a penalty deduction created by the true-up cascade. A negative next-vintage balance represents an encumbrance against allowances the source has not yet received, and it is what keeps the conserved-quantity identity closed instead of destroying the accounting. Client-initiated debits never touch the next-vintage bucket at all.

Reversal restores rather than re-derives. Every settlement stores the exact per-bucket amounts it took in `debited_prior_tons`, `debited_current_tons` and `debited_next_tons`, and a reversal credits back precisely those three stored amounts rather than recomputing a split from the current holdings. Settling and then reversing any transaction therefore returns every bucket to its exact pre-settlement value, however many other movements happened in between.

### 2.11. Path Resolution for Nested Resources

Many paths address a child through its parent. Identifiers in the path are always resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the error. A daily-calibration append against a non-existent monitoring system reports `ERR_MONITORING_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`; an allowance account opened under a non-existent facility reports `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`; every sub-resource operation beneath a unit reports `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` when the unit does not exist. Only once every path identifier resolves does the server examine the body — and body foreign keys are then resolved in declaration order, reporting `ERR_FUEL_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATA_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. The remaining not-found codes — `ERR_DAILY_CALIBRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATING_HOUR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUARTERLY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_LIMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCESS_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOWANCE_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CONTROL_OUTAGE_NOT_FOUND` — follow the same discipline for their own resources.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Generating Station: Facilities, Units, Dispatch and Outage Planning

A `RegulatoryReviewer` brings a station into the system by registering a **Facility** with its plant `name`, its `orispl_code` (the EIA/EPA plant code, between 1 and 8 characters long and globally unique — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORISPL_CODE`), its `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), the legal operating company in `operator_org`, the allowance `programs` the station is subject to, and the `true_up_deadline_utc` before which no allowance true-up may be run. The `programs` array must be non-empty and every member must be one of `ARP_SO2`, `CSAPR_NOX_ANNUAL`, `CSAPR_NOX_OZONE` or `CSAPR_SO2_G1`. An optional `max_concurrent_control_outages` caps how much control equipment the station may have out of service at once; it defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. A new facility begins with `status` of `OPERATING` and a `compliance_posture` of `COMPLIANT`. The reviewer may later revise `true_up_deadline_utc`, `max_concurrent_control_outages` or `status` — the latter moving the facility to `RETIRED` — and each such revision increments the facility's `version` by one.

Either a `RegulatoryReviewer` or the station's own `FacilityOperator` adds generating **Units**. A unit is described by its boiler designation `unit_identifier`, unique among the units of its owning facility; its `primary_fuel`, one of `COAL`, `NATURAL_GAS` or `OIL`; its `nameplate_capacity_mw` and its `max_rated_heat_input_mmbtu_hr`, both strictly positive; and optionally its `control_device`, one of `SCR`, `SNCR`, `FGD` or `NONE` — a unit with no post-combustion control records `NONE`. The unit's owning plant is recorded as `facility_id`. A new unit begins `OFFLINE` in `dispatch_state`, `AVAILABLE` in `control_status`, with `data_availability_pct` of 100.0, `certified_so2_lbs_ytd` of 0.0, and `dispatch_restricted` false.

The dispatch position moves among three values, and exactly four moves are permitted. A `FacilityOperator` brings a unit from `OFFLINE` to `ONLINE`, from `RESERVE_SHUTDOWN` to `ONLINE`, from `ONLINE` to `RESERVE_SHUTDOWN`, and from `ONLINE` to `OFFLINE`, by supplying the desired `dispatch_state`; each move increments the unit's `version`. Any other combination — including going directly from `RESERVE_SHUTDOWN` to `OFFLINE`, or restating the position the unit already holds — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, so a reserve-shutdown unit must be brought back `ONLINE` before it can be taken `OFFLINE`. Two conditions block a move to `ONLINE`, and both surface as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`: `dispatch_restricted` must be false, and no control outage window of the unit may be `ACTIVE` with an `outage_type` of `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE`. The reverse coupling does not exist — a unit going offline never disturbs its outage windows. `control_status`, which may read `AVAILABLE`, `DEGRADED` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, is moved only by outage window transitions and by the dispatch operation.

Compliance planning around control equipment availability is expressed through **Control Outage Windows**. A `FacilityOperator` plans one against a unit with an inclusive `start_utc`, an exclusive `end_utc` that must be strictly later than the start, an `outage_type` of `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE`, `FORCED` or `RATA_TEST_BYPASS`, and the `control_device` being taken out, which must be `SCR`, `SNCR` or `FGD` and must equal the unit's own installed device. A start at or after the end is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Intervals are compared half-open, so an abutting window whose start equals an incumbent's end is accepted, while one second earlier is rejected with `ERR_CONTROL_OUTAGE_OVERLAP`; overlap is scoped per device, so an FGD outage and an SCR outage on the same unit may overlap freely, and `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED` windows never block. A planned window starts in `PLANNED` and leaves the unit's `control_status` untouched.

Activation is where the station-wide ceiling bites. A window moves from `PLANNED` to `ACTIVE` only while the unit's `dispatch_state` is `OFFLINE` or `RESERVE_SHUTDOWN` — attempting it on a unit that is online is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not as a dispatch error — and only if the count of `ACTIVE` windows across **all** units of the facility, counted after this one, would not exceed `max_concurrent_control_outages`; otherwise `ERR_CONCURRENT_OUTAGE_LIMIT`. Because the ceiling is checked at activation and not at planning, a station may plan more windows than it can ever run concurrently and discover the constraint only when it tries to activate one too many. Activation stamps `activated_utc` from the logical clock, increments the window's `version`, and sets the unit's `control_status` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, incrementing the unit's `version`. Closing an `ACTIVE` window stamps `closed_utc`, increments its `version`, and returns the unit's `control_status` to `AVAILABLE` **only** when no other window of the same unit is still `ACTIVE`, leaving it `OUT_OF_SERVICE` otherwise; the unit's `version` increments either way. A `PLANNED` window may instead be cancelled with a `reason`, which is recorded as `cancellation_reason`, moves the window to `CANCELLED`, increments its `version`, and releases the interval for a later window; a cancellation submitted without a reason is rejected with `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.2. Monitoring Systems: Two Independent Status Dimensions

A **Monitoring System** is installed on a unit by a `QAAnalyst` or the unit's `FacilityOperator`, recording the monitored `parameter` — `SO2`, `NOX_RATE`, `CO2` or `FLOW` — the `system_type` (`CEM` for a continuous emission monitor, `FLOW_MONITOR` for a stack flow monitor, `EXCEPTED_APPENDIX_D` for the excepted fuel-analysis methodology), the instrument's `span_value`, and optionally the daily `calibration_error_spec_pct`, which defaults to 2.5 percent of span. The monitored unit is recorded as `unit_id`. A unit may hold only one live system per parameter: a second system for the same `parameter` on the same unit is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MONITORING_SYSTEM` unless the incumbent has been retired, since retirement releases the slot. A new system starts with `qa_status` of `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, `cal_status` of `CAL_EXPIRED`, `bias_adjustment_factor` of 1.0 and `consecutive_failed_calibrations` of 0.

The two status fields are **parallel and independent dimensions of the same row**. `qa_status` is one of `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, `CONDITIONALLY_VALID`, `OUT_OF_CONTROL` or `RETIRED`; `cal_status` is one of `CAL_CURRENT`, `CAL_EXPIRED` or `CAL_FAILED`. A system may legitimately sit at `CERTIFIED` with `CAL_FAILED`, or at `OUT_OF_CONTROL` with `CAL_CURRENT`. Neither axis constrains the other's reachable set — but the classification of an operating hour reads *both*. `RETIRED` is the only terminal value on either axis.

A `QAAnalyst` drives the QA axis with an `action`, optionally accompanied by an `effective_utc` and a `reason`. `CONDITIONALLY_VALIDATE` moves `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` to `CONDITIONALLY_VALID`. `CERTIFY` moves `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` to `CERTIFIED`, `CONDITIONALLY_VALID` to `CERTIFIED`, or `OUT_OF_CONTROL` to `CERTIFIED`, and carries two guards. From `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` or `CONDITIONALLY_VALID`, `cal_status` must not be `CAL_FAILED`; restoring from `OUT_OF_CONTROL` is stricter and requires `cal_status` to be exactly `CAL_CURRENT`. Either failure is `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_CURRENT`. In all three cases, no relative accuracy test audit of this system may be sitting in `FAILED`, else `ERR_MONITOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A successful certification clears `out_of_control_since_utc`, and when restoring from `OUT_OF_CONTROL` it also resets `consecutive_failed_calibrations` to 0. It then adopts a bias adjustment: if at least one audit of this system has `PASSED`, the `bias_adjustment_factor` of the passed audit with the greatest `window_end_utc` — resolving ties by the earlier `created_at` — is copied onto the system; if none has passed, the factor stays at 1.0. Every one of these moves increments the system's `version` by one. Importantly, restoring a system does **not** reclassify operating hours that the earlier cascade already flipped; those stay substituted or unavailable until the substitution procedure is run over them.

`DECLARE_OUT_OF_CONTROL` moves a system from `CERTIFIED` or `CONDITIONALLY_VALID` to `OUT_OF_CONTROL`, stamping `out_of_control_since_utc` from the supplied `effective_utc` and running the full four-link cascade anchored at that instant. `RETIRE` moves a system from `CERTIFIED` to `RETIRED`, increments the `version`, and releases the parameter slot on the unit so a replacement instrument can be installed for the same `parameter`. A system is retired only out of `CERTIFIED`: attempting to retire one standing at `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING`, `CONDITIONALLY_VALID` or `OUT_OF_CONTROL` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, so an instrument must be brought back into certification before it can be decommissioned. `RETIRED` is terminal on the QA axis, and every one of these four actions is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` when the requested move is not one of those listed above.

**Daily calibration** is recorded by a `FacilityOperator` or a `QAAnalyst` against a system, and is where the calibration axis moves. The submission carries the guard field `expected_monitoring_system_version` (see §2.7), the `calibration_utc` instant the check was performed — unique per system, so the same instant cannot be recorded twice — the zero-level reference gas value `zero_reference_value` and the analyser's response `zero_measured_value`, and the upscale pair `span_reference_value` and `span_measured_value`. A calibration may be recorded on any system whose `qa_status` is not `RETIRED`; on a retired system the append is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The server derives two error percentages from the pair readings. `zero_error_pct_of_span` is the absolute difference between `zero_measured_value` and `zero_reference_value`, divided by the system's `span_value`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; `span_error_pct_of_span` is the same computation on the upscale pair. The verdict then reads the **worse of the two percentages** against the system's `calibration_error_spec_pct`, on a ladder evaluated top to bottom where the first match wins. If the worse percentage is **strictly greater than twice** the specification, the `result` is `OUT_OF_CONTROL_TRIGGERED` on this single grossly failed check — a percentage exactly equal to twice the specification does *not* take this branch. Otherwise, if the worse percentage is **strictly greater than** the specification, the check has failed: taking the system's `consecutive_failed_calibrations` as it stood before this call and adding one, a running count that reaches **two** upgrades the `result` to `OUT_OF_CONTROL_TRIGGERED`, and a count of one leaves the `result` at `FAILED`. Otherwise — the worse percentage being at or below the specification — the `result` is `PASSED`. Equality with the specification therefore passes.

The verdict then drives the calibration axis. A `PASSED` result sets `cal_status` to `CAL_CURRENT` from either `CAL_EXPIRED` or `CAL_FAILED`, stamps `last_calibration_utc` from `calibration_utc`, and resets `consecutive_failed_calibrations` to 0; the QA axis is untouched, so a certified system that failed one check is back to `CAL_CURRENT` without any QA action, and an out-of-control system remains out of control. A `FAILED` or `OUT_OF_CONTROL_TRIGGERED` result sets `cal_status` to `CAL_FAILED`, stamps `last_calibration_utc`, and increases `consecutive_failed_calibrations` by one. Every calibration increments the monitoring system's `version` by at least one, and the record reports `monitoring_system_version_after` so the operator can immediately chain another guarded write.

An `OUT_OF_CONTROL_TRIGGERED` result on a system whose `qa_status` is `CERTIFIED` or `CONDITIONALLY_VALID` fires the **out-of-control cascade**, and every link must complete before the response is returned:

- **Link 1.** `qa_status` becomes `OUT_OF_CONTROL`, `cal_status` becomes `CAL_FAILED`, and `out_of_control_since_utc` is set to the new calibration's `calibration_utc`. The system's `version` increments by one.
- **Link 2**, caused by the QA state change rather than by the calibration itself. Every operating hour of this system's unit whose `hour_begin_utc` is at or after `out_of_control_since_utc` truncated to the hour, and whose `data_quality` is `MEASURED`, is set to `data_quality` of `UNAVAILABLE` with its `so2_mass_lbs` cleared to null. The unit's `data_availability_pct` is then recomputed.
- **Link 3**, caused by those hour flips. For each distinct report among the flipped hours, `operating_hour_count`, `measured_hour_count`, `substituted_hour_count`, `monitor_data_availability_pct` and every total are recomputed; a report standing at `VALIDATED` reverts to `DRAFT`, and its `version` increments by one.
- **Link 4**, caused by the report recompute. Every permit limit of the same unit whose `evaluation_status` is not `NOT_EVALUATED` is re-evaluated at its stored `window_evaluated_utc`. A limit that becomes `EXCEEDED` raises a new excess emission event in `DETECTED`; a limit that leaves `EXCEEDED` moves each of its events still in `DETECTED` to `WITHDRAWN`.

A declaration by a `QAAnalyst` runs the identical link two, three and four chain, anchored at the supplied `effective_utc` instead of at a calibration instant.

### 3.3. Relative Accuracy Test Audits, Bias and the Retest Loop

A `QAAnalyst` schedules a **relative accuracy test audit** against a monitoring system by supplying an inclusive `window_start_utc` and an exclusive `window_end_utc` that must be strictly later, optionally an `applicable_standard_value` (the source's emission standard, strictly positive when present), and optionally a `retest_of_rata_test_id` naming the audit this one replaces. The audited system is recorded as `monitoring_system_id`. A zero-length or inverted window is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; a window overlapping another audit of the same system that is still `SCHEDULED` or `RUNS_RECORDED` is `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, with half-open comparison, so a window starting exactly where an incumbent ends is accepted while one second earlier is not, and audits already `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `VOIDED` never block; a `window_start_utc` strictly before the logical clock is `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_IN_PAST`, with equality accepted. A first attempt records `attempt_number` of 1 and leaves `retest_of_rata_test_id` null. When a predecessor is named, it must be sitting in `FAILED` and must not already be referenced by another audit, else `ERR_RETEST_ALREADY_EXISTS`; the predecessor then moves from `FAILED` to `VOIDED` with its `version` incremented, and the new row starts `SCHEDULED` with `attempt_number` one higher than the predecessor's.

Recording the audit's runs advances it from `SCHEDULED` to `RUNS_RECORDED`. The submission carries `runs`, an array of paired measurements each containing a `reference_value` from the reference method and a `cems_value` from the monitor. The array must hold at least 9 and at most 12 pairs, with every value positive, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RATA_RUNS`; 8 pairs and 13 pairs are both rejected, because the statistical table this test relies on is defined only over that range. The system must not be `RETIRED`, and the audited system's unit must be dispatching — `dispatch_state` exactly `ONLINE` — else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_ONLINE`, since an accuracy audit is only meaningful against a generating unit actually producing stack gas.

Recording the runs computes the first half of the statistics. `run_count` is the number of pairs. For each pair, the difference is the `reference_value` minus the `cems_value`, rounded half up to four decimals. `reference_mean` is the arithmetic mean of the reference values and `cems_mean` the arithmetic mean of the monitor values, each rounded half up to four decimals. `mean_difference` is the sum of the per-pair differences divided by the number of pairs, rounded half up to four decimals. The sample standard deviation of those differences — using the count minus one as the denominator — is carried to six decimals, and `confidence_coefficient` is the Student t value for this run count, multiplied by that standard deviation, divided by the square root of the run count, rounded half up to four decimals. The t value is 1.860 for 9 runs, 1.833 for 10, 1.812 for 11 and 1.796 for 12. The audit's `version` increments by one.

Evaluation is a separate step, and it is where the two permitted denominators diverge. The caller supplies `ra_basis`, either `REFERENCE_MEAN` or `APPLICABLE_STANDARD`. Under `REFERENCE_MEAN` — always permitted, and the value a scheduled audit carries by default — the denominator is `reference_mean`, the measured reference method mean of this very run set. Under `APPLICABLE_STANDARD` the denominator is `applicable_standard_value` instead, and that substitution is reserved for genuinely low-emitting sources: it requires `applicable_standard_value` to be present **and** `reference_mean` to be at or below the low-emitter threshold for the system's monitored `parameter` — 0.300 for `SO2`, `NOX_RATE` and `FLOW`, and 5.000 for `CO2`. A request that fails either condition is rejected with `ERR_RA_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`. Because the applicable standard is larger than a low emitter's measured reference mean, this basis always yields a *smaller* relative accuracy and can convert a failing audit into a passing one on identical runs.

`relative_accuracy_pct` is the absolute value of `mean_difference` plus the absolute value of `confidence_coefficient`, divided by the chosen denominator, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to one decimal place. The audit `PASSED` when that percentage is **at or below** 10.0 — the pass threshold is 10.0 for every monitored parameter, and equality passes — and `FAILED` when it is strictly above. Independently of pass or fail, the bias test runs: `bias_test_failed` is true when the absolute value of `mean_difference` is **strictly greater than** the absolute value of `confidence_coefficient`. When the bias test fails, `bias_adjustment_factor` is one plus `mean_difference` divided by `cems_mean`, rounded half up to four decimals; when the bias test passes, the factor is exactly 1.0. Evaluation increments the audit's `version` by one.

The outcome then propagates onto the monitoring system. A `PASSED` audit on a system whose `qa_status` is `CERTIFIED` copies the audit's `bias_adjustment_factor` onto the system and increments the system's `version` by one; a `PASSED` audit on a system in any other QA state leaves the system alone until the next certification adopts the factor. A `FAILED` audit on a system whose `qa_status` is `CERTIFIED` demotes it to `CONDITIONALLY_VALID` and increments the system's `version` by one.

The retest procedure is a genuine cycle **on the same row**: a `FAILED` audit may be rescheduled, returning it to `SCHEDULED` with a fresh `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. Rescheduling revalidates the new interval against the same three window rules — a non-failed audit is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, an inverted interval is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, an overlap with another scheduled or runs-recorded audit of the same system is `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, and a start strictly before the logical clock is `ERR_RATA_WINDOW_IN_PAST`. It clears `runs`, resets `run_count` to 0, nulls `reference_mean`, `cems_mean`, `mean_difference`, `confidence_coefficient` and `relative_accuracy_pct`, sets `bias_test_failed` back to false and `bias_adjustment_factor` back to 1.0, returns `ra_basis` to `REFERENCE_MEAN`, raises `attempt_number` by one, sets `status` to `SCHEDULED`, and increments the audit's `version`. `PASSED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal outcomes; `FAILED` is not terminal, precisely because of this loop.

### 3.4. Fuel Sampling and Laboratory Analysis

Heat input by fuel analysis needs assayed fuel. A `FacilityOperator` records a **Fuel Sample** against a unit with the `sample_utc` instant it was drawn, the `fuel_type` sampled — `COAL`, `NATURAL_GAS` or `OIL` — and the `lot_identifier` of the shipment it represents, unique among that unit's samples. The sampled unit is recorded as `unit_id`. A new sample stands at `PENDING` with no analysis values.

A `QAAnalyst` records the laboratory result by supplying an `outcome`. With an `outcome` of `ANALYZED`, applied to a sample standing at either `PENDING` or `INVALIDATED`, the analyst must supply all three of `gcv_btu_per_lb` (the gross calorific value, between 1000 and 20000 inclusive), `sulfur_content_pct` (the sulfur content as a weight percent, from 0.0 through 10.0 inclusive) and `analysis_method`, one of `ASTM_D5865`, `ASTM_D4239` or `GC_D1945`; any of the three missing is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The sample moves to `ANALYZED`, `analyzed_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, `invalidation_reason` is cleared, and the `version` increments by one. With an `outcome` of `INVALIDATE`, applied to a sample standing at `PENDING` or `ANALYZED`, an `invalidation_reason` is mandatory — omitting it is `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED` — and the sample moves to `INVALIDATED` with its `version` incremented.

Invalidation is deliberately **not** retroactive. Operating hours that already referenced the sample keep the `heat_input_mmbtu` they were stored with, and a report that already reached `VALIDATED` is not auto-reverted. The consequence surfaces only at the next validation attempt, which refuses while any of the report's hours references an invalidated sample. Because `INVALIDATED` is re-openable, a re-analysis returns the sample to `ANALYZED` with fresh values — there is no terminal state on a fuel sample at all — and again there is no retroactive recompute of the hours that referenced it.

### 3.5. Recording Operating Hours: Two Determination Methods

The hourly operating record is the raw material of every emissions number in the system. A `FacilityOperator` or an `EmissionsReportPreparer` appends an hour to a unit, guarded by `expected_quarterly_report_version` (§2.7). The hour itself is identified by `hour_begin_utc`, the start of the clock hour it covers, whose minutes, seconds and milliseconds must all be zero; the pair of unit and `hour_begin_utc` is unique across all quarters, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATING_HOUR`. The reporting unit is recorded as `unit_id`. The client never names the report: **the hour's own timestamp selects it.** The server resolves the report of that unit whose `year` and `quarter` contain `hour_begin_utc` and records it as `quarterly_report_id`; if the timestamp is not exactly on the hour, or if no report standing at `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` exists for that unit and quarter, the append is rejected with `ERR_HOUR_OUTSIDE_REPORT_PERIOD`. The hour is *not* required to be at or before the logical clock — a station may post a projected hour.

Every hour carries `op_time_fraction`, the fraction of the clock hour during which the unit combusted fuel, from 0.0 through 1.0 with two decimals, and optionally `gross_load_mw`, the average gross load during the operating portion, at least 0. It also carries `heat_input_method`, which selects one of two mutually exclusive families of measurement fields and produces materially different numbers from the same physical hour.

`CEMS_FLOW_CO2` derives heat input from measured stack gas. It **requires** `flow_scfh` (stack volumetric flow in standard cubic feet per hour), `co2_conc_pct` (stack CO2 concentration, percent by volume) and `so2_conc_ppm` (stack SO2 concentration in parts per million), and **forbids** `fuel_flow_lbs` and `fuel_sample_id`. `APPENDIX_D_FUEL` derives heat input from the fuel actually burned. It **requires** `fuel_flow_lbs` (the mass of fuel burned in the hour, in pounds) and `fuel_sample_id`, and **forbids** `flow_scfh`, `co2_conc_pct` and `so2_conc_ppm`. `nox_rate_lb_mmbtu`, the NOx emission rate in pounds per mmBtu, belongs to neither family and may accompany either. Any deviation from those two field families — a missing required member, a forbidden member supplied, or a unit that owns no monitoring system whose `system_type` matches the chosen method — is rejected with `ERR_METHOD_FIELD_MISMATCH`. Note that this is *not* a shape error: a request that mixes the families is syntactically well formed, passes the request-shape check, and is caught only here. An `APPENDIX_D_FUEL` hour additionally requires its referenced sample to be standing at `ANALYZED`, since `gcv_btu_per_lb` and `sulfur_content_pct` only exist once the lab has reported; a sample in any other status is `ERR_FUEL_SAMPLE_NOT_ANALYZED`.

Under `CEMS_FLOW_CO2`, the four derived quantities are computed as follows. `heat_input_mmbtu` is `flow_scfh` multiplied by `co2_conc_pct` divided by 100, divided by the carbon-based F-factor of 1800.0 dry standard cubic feet of CO2 per mmBtu, multiplied by `op_time_fraction`, rounded half up to three decimals. `so2_mass_lbs` is the SO2 mass conversion constant 0.000000166 pounds per standard cubic foot per ppm, multiplied by `so2_conc_ppm`, by `flow_scfh`, by `op_time_fraction`, and finally by the `bias_adjustment_factor` of the unit's SO2 monitoring system, rounded half up to one decimal. `nox_mass_lbs` is `nox_rate_lb_mmbtu` multiplied by the hour's already-rounded `heat_input_mmbtu`, rounded half up to one decimal. `co2_mass_tons` is the CO2 mass constant 0.1194 pounds per standard cubic foot, multiplied by `co2_conc_pct` divided by 100, by `flow_scfh` and by `op_time_fraction`, then divided by 2000 pounds per ton, rounded half up to three decimals.

Under `APPENDIX_D_FUEL`, the same four quantities come from the fuel. `heat_input_mmbtu` is `fuel_flow_lbs` multiplied by the referenced sample's `gcv_btu_per_lb`, divided by one million to convert Btu to mmBtu, multiplied by `op_time_fraction`, rounded half up to three decimals. `so2_mass_lbs` is a stoichiometric conversion: `fuel_flow_lbs` multiplied by the sulfur-to-SO2 mass ratio of 2.0, multiplied by the sample's `sulfur_content_pct` divided by 100, multiplied by `op_time_fraction`, rounded half up to one decimal — and **no bias adjustment ever applies to this variant**. `nox_mass_lbs` is again `nox_rate_lb_mmbtu` multiplied by the already-rounded `heat_input_mmbtu`, rounded half up to one decimal. `co2_mass_tons` is the already-rounded `heat_input_mmbtu` multiplied by the fixed emission factor of 205.3 pounds of CO2 per mmBtu, divided by 2000 pounds per ton, rounded half up to three decimals.

The hour's `data_quality` is classified at creation as either `MEASURED` or `UNAVAILABLE`. It is `UNAVAILABLE` when the governing monitoring system — the unit's system for the `SO2` parameter under the CEMS method, or its excepted Appendix D system under the fuel method — has a `qa_status` of `OUT_OF_CONTROL` or `RETIRED`, or a `cal_status` of `CAL_EXPIRED` or `CAL_FAILED`; and, for a CEMS hour, also when `so2_conc_ppm` is simply absent. Otherwise it is `MEASURED`. While an hour is `UNAVAILABLE`, its `so2_mass_lbs` is null. A new hour's `substitution_method` is `NONE`, with `substituted_so2_conc_ppm` and `monitor_data_availability_pct_at_substitution` both null.

Appending an hour recomputes its owning report's counts and totals, reverts that report from `VALIDATED` back to `DRAFT` with its `version` incremented, recomputes the unit's `data_availability_pct`, and records `quarterly_report_version_after` on the hour so the client can chain the next guarded append without re-reading the report.

The **bulk append** exists because a quarter is thousands of hours. It carries `expected_quarterly_report_version` once and an `hours` array whose members each hold exactly the same per-hour fields as a single append. The array must hold at least 1 and at most 200 entries, else `ERR_BULK_SIZE_INVALID`. The operation is strictly all-or-nothing: **any** per-item violation — a duplicate hour, a method field mismatch, an unanalysed sample, an out-of-range value, an hour outside the report period — rejects the entire array, persists nothing, and leaves the report's `version` exactly where it was. Hours are applied in `hour_begin_utc` ascending order regardless of the order they appear in the array, and exactly **one** report recompute runs after all of them are applied, so a successful bulk append advances the report's `version` by exactly one no matter how many hours it carried.

### 3.6. Missing Data Substitution and Monitor Data Availability

**Monitor data availability** is the percentage of a unit's reportable hours in one calendar quarter that carry directly measured data. Scoped to a unit and a calendar quarter, the denominator is the count of that quarter's hours whose `op_time_fraction` is greater than zero, and the numerator is the count of those same hours whose `data_quality` is `MEASURED`. Hours that did not operate at all — `op_time_fraction` of exactly zero — are excluded from **both** numerator and denominator. Hours that are `SUBSTITUTED` and hours that are `UNAVAILABLE` are both in the denominator and neither is in the numerator, which is what makes an unmonitored hour costly. When the denominator is zero the availability is 100.0 by definition; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to one decimal place. This figure is recomputed on every hour append, on every substitution, on link two of the out-of-control cascade, and at report validation, and is stored both as the unit's `data_availability_pct` and as the report's `monitor_data_availability_pct`.

Availability governs how expensively a missing hour is priced. A `FacilityOperator` or an `EmissionsReportPreparer` runs the **missing data substitution procedure** over a unit across a half-open range bounded by `from_hour_utc` inclusive and `to_hour_utc` exclusive. A range whose start is at or after its end is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; a range containing no hour whose `data_quality` is `UNAVAILABLE` is `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTABLE_HOURS`, rather than being silently accepted as a no-op — re-running the procedure over an already-substituted range would let the tier drift as availability changes.

For each target hour the server assembles a **lookback set**: the 20 most recent `so2_conc_ppm` values from hours of the same unit whose `data_quality` is `MEASURED` and whose `hour_begin_utc` is strictly earlier than the target hour, taken in `hour_begin_utc` descending order and resolving ties by the earlier `created_at`, then sorted ascending. The lookback is count-based and may cross quarter boundaries; the availability denominator never does. Substituted hours never enter a later hour's lookback set — only measured hours do — but they *do* enter the availability denominator.

The tier is then selected from the availability figure computed for this hour, which is stored on the hour as `monitor_data_availability_pct_at_substitution`, and the ladder is evaluated top to bottom with the first match winning:

- If the lookback set is empty, `substitution_method` is `T4_MAX_POTENTIAL_CONCENTRATION` and the substitute value is the maximum potential concentration of 400 ppm.
- Otherwise, if availability is **at or above** 95.0 percent, `substitution_method` is `T1_90TH_PERCENTILE` and the value is the 90th percentile of the lookback set.
- Otherwise, if availability is **at or above** 90.0 percent, `substitution_method` is `T2_95TH_PERCENTILE` and the value is the 95th percentile.
- Otherwise, if availability is **at or above** 80.0 percent, `substitution_method` is `T3_LOOKBACK_MAXIMUM` and the value is the largest value in the lookback set.
- Otherwise, `substitution_method` is `T4_MAX_POTENTIAL_CONCENTRATION` and the value is again 400 ppm.

Because every comparison is inclusive, availability of exactly 90.0 percent stays in the 95th-percentile tier rather than dropping to the lookback maximum. Percentiles are **nearest-rank** over the ascending lookback set: for a set of *n* values, the 90th percentile is the value at the one-based position obtained by multiplying 0.90 by *n* and raising the product to the next whole number — leaving it unchanged when it is already whole — and the 95th percentile is the value at the position obtained the same way from 0.95. The 20-hour lookback depth is chosen so that the 90th percentile, the 95th percentile and the maximum are three distinct members of a full set; at 18 or 19 members the 95th percentile and the maximum coincide and the two middle tiers become indistinguishable.

The tier's value is written to `substituted_so2_conc_ppm` as a whole number, taken verbatim from the lookback set rather than interpolated, `data_quality` becomes `SUBSTITUTED`, and `so2_mass_lbs` is recomputed as the SO2 mass conversion constant 0.000000166 multiplied by the substitute concentration, by `flow_scfh` and by `op_time_fraction`, rounded half up to one decimal. **The bias adjustment factor is never applied to a substituted hour.**

The evaluation order is pinned, because the tier depends on availability and availability depends on which hours are substituted. First, availability for a target hour is computed **excluding that hour itself and every hour whose `hour_begin_utc` is at or after it**; this is what breaks the circularity. Second, the target hours are processed one at a time in `hour_begin_utc` ascending order, and each hour's own `substitution_method` is frozen before the next hour's availability is recomputed. Third, a substituted hour never joins a later hour's lookback set but does join its availability denominator. Once the range is processed, the owning reports' counts and totals and the unit's `data_availability_pct` are recomputed.

### 3.7. The Quarterly Emissions Report: From Draft to Acceptance

An `EmissionsReportPreparer` opens a **Quarterly Report** against a unit for a calendar `year` of at least 2000 and a `quarter` of 1, 2, 3 or 4. The reported unit is recorded as `unit_id`. One electronic report exists per unit per calendar quarter, so a second attempt at the same triple is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD` — a rejected report is reopened, never re-created. A new report stands at `DRAFT` with `operating_hour_count`, `measured_hour_count`, `substituted_hour_count` and `resubmission_count` all 0, every total 0, `monitor_data_availability_pct` at 100.0, and `submission_format`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_utc` and `rejection_reason` all null. The lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `VALIDATED` → `SUBMITTED` → `CERTIFIED` → `ACCEPTED`, with two loops back: `CERTIFIED` → `REJECTED` → `DRAFT`, and `VALIDATED` → `DRAFT`. `ACCEPTED` is the sole terminal state.

**Report totals** are rolled up from the report's hours, taken in `hour_begin_utc` ascending order with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. `operating_hour_count` is the number of hours whose `op_time_fraction` is greater than zero. `measured_hour_count` is the number of hours whose `data_quality` is `MEASURED` and `substituted_hour_count` the number whose `data_quality` is `SUBSTITUTED`; both counts are taken over **all** of the report's hours without any operating-time filter, so they are not necessarily subsets of `operating_hour_count`. The aggregation order for the emission totals is **mandatory**: each hour's already-stored, already-rounded value is summed, and the raw unrounded products are never re-summed. `total_heat_input_mmbtu` is the sum of the hours' `heat_input_mmbtu`, rounded half up to three decimals. `total_so2_lbs` is the sum of the hours' `so2_mass_lbs`, counting a null as zero, rounded half up to one decimal. `total_nox_lbs` is the sum of the hours' `nox_mass_lbs`, counting a null as zero, rounded half up to one decimal. `total_co2_tons` is the sum of the hours' `co2_mass_tons`, counting a null as zero, rounded half up to three decimals. `total_so2_tons` is then derived from the **already-rounded** `total_so2_lbs`, divided by 2000 pounds per ton and rounded half up to three decimals — never from a re-sum of per-hour tons. Rounding once at the end instead of per hour produces a figure that can differ by a tenth of a pound, and only the per-hour-then-sum order is accepted. `monitor_data_availability_pct` is recomputed over the report's whole unit-quarter scope.

**Validation** advances a `DRAFT` report to `VALIDATED` and freezes those figures, and it is where the monitoring narrative and the reporting narrative meet. Four business conditions are checked, and in this order: no monitoring system of the report's unit may stand at `OUT_OF_CONTROL` (`ERR_MONITOR_OUT_OF_CONTROL`); every monitoring system of the unit must stand at `CERTIFIED` or `CONDITIONALLY_VALID`, so a system still at `RECERTIFICATION_PENDING` or already `RETIRED` blocks validation (`ERR_MONITOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`); no hour of the report may reference a fuel sample standing at `INVALIDATED` (`ERR_FUEL_ANALYSIS_INVALIDATED`); and the recomputed `monitor_data_availability_pct` must be **at or above** the data availability floor of 80.0 percent, so exactly 80.0 passes and 79.9 does not (`ERR_DATA_AVAILABILITY_BELOW_FLOOR`). The specific out-of-control condition is reported ahead of the generic not-certified condition, and both are reported ahead of the availability floor even when availability has already collapsed. Validation recomputes every count and total, sets the status, and increments the report's `version`.

Any hour appended to a `VALIDATED` report — and any recompute reaching it through link three of the out-of-control cascade — silently reverts it to `DRAFT`, recomputing the counts and totals and incrementing its `version`. Validation is therefore never permanent; it is a checkpoint the preparer must re-establish after any late data.

**Submission** advances `VALIDATED` to `SUBMITTED`. The preparer supplies the electronic `submission_format`, either `XML_ECMPS` or `JSON_ECMPS`, and the server records `submitted_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, stamps `submitted_utc` from the logical clock, and increments the report's `version`.

**Certification** is the segregation-of-duties boundary, and it is exercised by a `DesignatedRepresentative` against a `SUBMITTED` report. The signer supplies `expected_quarterly_report_version` and the `statement_text` of the certification statement, which must be at least 10 characters long. If the signer's `X-User-ID` equals the report's `submitted_by_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409) — and that check runs *ahead of* the version guard, so a representative attempting to certify their own submission sees the duties conflict even when their version is also stale. A successful signature creates a **Compliance Certification** in `SIGNED`, recording `certifier_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, `signed_utc` from the logical clock, `revoked_utc` and `revocation_reason` null, and `certified_so2_lbs_snapshot` captured from the report's `total_so2_lbs` at the moment of signing. It then credits the unit's `certified_so2_lbs_ytd` by that same `total_so2_lbs`, carried to one decimal, incrementing the unit's `version` by one; and it moves the report to `CERTIFIED`, incrementing the report's `version` by one. The unit's `certified_so2_lbs_ytd` is thus a conserved ledger that always equals the sum of `total_so2_lbs` across that unit's reports standing at `CERTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED`.

**Revocation** is what keeps that ledger honest. A `DesignatedRepresentative` revokes a `SIGNED` certification with a mandatory `revocation_reason` — omitting it is `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED`. The certification moves to `REVOKED` (its terminal state) with `revoked_utc` stamped from the logical clock and its `version` incremented; the unit's `certified_so2_lbs_ytd` is debited by **exactly** `certified_so2_lbs_snapshot` — precisely the amount credited at signing, so the ledger returns to its pre-certification value rather than to a re-derived one — with the unit's `version` incremented; and the parent report returns from `CERTIFIED` to `SUBMITTED` with its `version` incremented.

**Disposition** is the regulator's decision on a `CERTIFIED` report, taken by a `RegulatoryReviewer` with a `decision`. `ACCEPT` moves the report to `ACCEPTED` and increments its `version`; from that moment the report's `total_so2_tons`, `total_nox_lbs` and `total_co2_tons` enter the allowance true-up for the matching compliance year. `REJECT` requires a `reason`, else `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED`, records it as `rejection_reason`, moves the report to `REJECTED`, and — symmetrically with an explicit revocation — sets the report's `SIGNED` certification to `REVOKED` and debits the unit's `certified_so2_lbs_ytd` by that certification's `certified_so2_lbs_snapshot`, incrementing the unit's `version`; the report's `version` also increments. The difference between the two revocation paths is only where the report lands: an explicit revocation returns it to `SUBMITTED`, while a rejection leaves it at `REJECTED`.

A `REJECTED` report is **reopened** by an `EmissionsReportPreparer` back to `DRAFT`. Reopening raises `resubmission_count` by one, clears `submission_format`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_utc` and `rejection_reason` back to null, and increments the report's `version`. The preparer then re-validates, re-submits, and a representative re-certifies — potentially a different representative, since the duties check reads whoever submitted this time.

### 3.8. Permit Limits, Rolling Averages and Excess Emission Reporting

A `RegulatoryReviewer` attaches a source-specific **Permit Limit** to a unit, naming the regulated `pollutant` (`SO2`, `NOX_RATE` or `CO2`), the `limit_value` to four decimals and strictly positive, the `limit_units` in which it is expressed (`LB_PER_MMBTU` for a rate limit, `LB_PER_HR` for an hourly-average mass limit, `TONS_PER_YEAR` for a cumulative annual tonnage limit), the `averaging_basis` geometry (`ROLLING_3_HOUR`, `ROLLING_30_DAY` or `ANNUAL`), and `effective_from_utc`, before which no hour is ever admitted into a rolling window. The limited unit is recorded as `unit_id`. A unit may carry several limits on the same pollutant provided the averaging basis or the effective date differs; an exact repeat of unit, pollutant, averaging basis and effective instant is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_LIMIT`. A new limit stands at `evaluation_status` of `NOT_EVALUATED` with `current_rolling_value` null, `window_sample_count` 0 and `window_evaluated_utc` null.

Either a `RegulatoryReviewer` or an `EmissionsReportPreparer` evaluates a limit against an `anchor_utc` instant. The window admits operating hours of the limit's unit whose `hour_begin_utc` is **strictly earlier** than the anchor, **at or after** the anchor minus the basis span, **at or after** `effective_from_utc`, whose `op_time_fraction` is greater than zero, and whose `data_quality` is `MEASURED` or `SUBSTITUTED` — an `UNAVAILABLE` hour is excluded outright. The basis span is 3 hours for `ROLLING_3_HOUR`, 720 hours (thirty days) for `ROLLING_30_DAY`, and 8760 hours (a full year) for `ANNUAL`. `window_sample_count` is the number of hours the window admitted, and `window_evaluated_utc` is set to the anchor.

A window too thin to average is not a compliance judgement: if `window_sample_count` is **below** the minimum for the basis — 3 hours for `ROLLING_3_HOUR`, and 24 hours for both `ROLLING_30_DAY` and `ANNUAL` — the `evaluation_status` becomes `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` and `current_rolling_value` is null. Otherwise the rolling value is computed from the window's pollutant mass, where each hour's contribution is its `so2_mass_lbs` for an `SO2` limit, its `nox_mass_lbs` for a `NOX_RATE` limit, and its `co2_mass_tons` multiplied by 2000 pounds per ton for a `CO2` limit. For `LB_PER_MMBTU` the value is the sum of those masses divided by the sum of the window's `heat_input_mmbtu`, rounded half up to four decimals. For `LB_PER_HR` it is the sum of those masses divided by `window_sample_count`, rounded half up to one decimal. For `TONS_PER_YEAR` it is the sum of those masses divided by 2000 pounds per ton, rounded half up to three decimals. The `evaluation_status` is then `EXCEEDED` when `current_rolling_value` is **strictly greater** than `limit_value`, and `IN_COMPLIANCE` otherwise — a rolling value exactly equal to the limit is in compliance.

An `EXCEEDED` result raises an **Excess Emission Event**. Events are never created by a client request; a limit evaluation is their only origin. The server records `permit_limit_id` for the exceeded limit, denormalises `unit_id` from it so events can be filtered per unit, stamps `detected_utc` from the evaluation's anchor, captures `observed_value` from `current_rolling_value` and `limit_value_snapshot` from `limit_value` at detection, and derives `exceedance_magnitude` as `observed_value` minus `limit_value_snapshot`, rounded half up to four decimals. It then sizes the allowance consequence: the exceedance percent is `exceedance_magnitude` divided by `limit_value_snapshot`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to four decimals, and `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` is the **larger** of one ton and that percent **raised to the next whole number** — a ceiling, never a round, so a percent of exactly 20 gives 20 tons while a percent a hundredth above 20 gives 21. The floor of one ton means even the smallest exceedance carries a consequence. The event opens at `DETECTED` with `return_count` 0 and `narrative`, `corrective_action` and `disposition_reason` all null.

A non-exceeding evaluation is equally consequential in reverse: every event of that limit still standing at `DETECTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, its `version` incremented, and its `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` ceases to enter any allowance obligation. This is the same withdrawal that link four of the out-of-control cascade performs when invalidated hours drop a limit out of exceedance.

An `EmissionsReportPreparer` files the excess emission report on a `DETECTED` event, supplying the `narrative` and the `corrective_action`, which moves it to `REPORTED` with its `version` incremented. A `RegulatoryReviewer` then opens review on a `REPORTED` event, moving it to `UNDER_REVIEW`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer takes one of three dispositions by supplying a `decision`. `RETURN` requires a `reason`, records it as `disposition_reason`, raises `return_count` by one and sends the event **back** to `REPORTED` — a genuine loop, since the same row can be reviewed again. `EXCUSE` also requires a `reason`, records it as `disposition_reason`, and moves the event to the terminal `EXCUSED`. `ENFORCE` requires no reason and moves the event to the terminal `ENFORCEABLE`; from that moment its `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` enters the allowance obligation of every allowance account of this unit's facility whose program regulates the parent limit's `pollutant` and whose `compliance_year` equals the calendar year of `detected_utc`. For that matching, the `ARP_SO2` and `CSAPR_SO2_G1` programs regulate `SO2`, and the `CSAPR_NOX_ANNUAL` and `CSAPR_NOX_OZONE` programs regulate `NOX_RATE`. Each disposition increments the event's `version`. `EXCUSED`, `ENFORCEABLE` and `WITHDRAWN` are the three terminal outcomes; a reason-less `RETURN` or `EXCUSE` is `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED`.

### 3.9. Allowance Holdings, Trading and the Compliance-Deadline True-Up

A `RegulatoryReviewer` opens an **Allowance Account** for a facility, naming the `program` it holds — one of `ARP_SO2`, `CSAPR_NOX_ANNUAL`, `CSAPR_NOX_OZONE` or `CSAPR_SO2_G1`, and necessarily a member of the facility's own `programs` — the `compliance_year` of at least 2000 that it trues up, and the account's opening endowment split across three vintage buckets: `holdings_current_vintage_tons` for allowances whose vintage equals the compliance year, `holdings_prior_vintage_tons` for banked allowances of earlier vintage, and `holdings_next_vintage_tons` for allowances of the following vintage. All three must be at least 0 at creation and are carried to three decimals. The account holder is recorded as `facility_id`. Exactly one account exists per facility, program and compliance year; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALLOWANCE_ACCOUNT`. A new account stands at `OPEN` with `obligation_tons`, `surrendered_tons`, `penalty_deducted_tons` and `shortfall_tons` all zero and `true_up_utc` null. The lifecycle is `OPEN` → `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` or `OPEN` → `CLOSED`, with `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` → `OPEN` reachable only by compensation; `CLOSED` is terminal.

**Proposal is separated from settlement by role.** An `AllowanceTrader` proposes an **Allowance Transaction** and can do nothing else in the system; a `DesignatedRepresentative` records, settles and reverses, and never proposes. A proposal carries `expected_allowance_account_version` (§2.7), the `allowance_account_id` of the account whose holdings move, the `transaction_type`, the `quantity_tons` moved, the `vintage_year` of the allowances moved, and — for transfers only — the `counterparty_account_id`. Five business rules guard the proposal, evaluated in this order: `quantity_tons` must be strictly greater than zero, since direction is carried by `transaction_type` and never by a sign (`ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`); `transaction_type` must not be `PENALTY_DEDUCTION`, because only the true-up cascade may create a penalty and only the cascade may drive the next-vintage bucket negative (`ERR_PENALTY_TRANSACTION_NOT_CLIENT_CREATABLE`); a `TRANSFER_OUT` or `TRANSFER_IN` must carry a `counterparty_account_id` that differs from `allowance_account_id` and belongs to an account of the same `program`, while a `PURCHASE`, `SURRENDER` or `PENALTY_DEDUCTION` must not carry one at all, since allowances never cross program boundaries (`ERR_INVALID_COUNTERPARTY`); and `vintage_year` must fall within the compliance year of the target account minus one through that year plus one, both endpoints accepted, which is exactly what makes the three holdings buckets exhaustive (`ERR_VINTAGE_YEAR_INVALID`). A proposal against an account standing at `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_TRUE_UP_LOCKED`. An accepted proposal stands at `PROPOSED` with `origin` of `MANUAL`, all three `debited_*` amounts 0.0, `reversal_reason` null, and no holdings moved yet; it reports `allowance_account_version_after`. The available transaction types are `PURCHASE`, `TRANSFER_IN`, `TRANSFER_OUT`, `SURRENDER` and `PENALTY_DEDUCTION`, and `origin` reads `MANUAL`, `TRUE_UP_SURRENDER` or `TRUE_UP_PENALTY`.

A `DesignatedRepresentative` drives the transaction with an `action` and an optional `reason`. `RECORD` moves a `PROPOSED` transaction to `RECORDED` and increments its `version`; no ownership comparison against the proposing trader applies, only the role separation. `SETTLE` moves a `RECORDED` transaction to `SETTLED`, and this is where holdings actually move. The account must not be `CLOSED` — nor `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` — else `ERR_ACCOUNT_TRUE_UP_LOCKED`; and for a `SURRENDER` or `TRANSFER_OUT` the `quantity_tons` must not exceed `holdings_prior_vintage_tons` plus `holdings_current_vintage_tons`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALLOWANCES`, with equality accepted since draining an account to exactly zero is legitimate. That sufficiency check runs at settlement rather than at proposal, because holdings can move in between — and it loses both to the account lock and to the version guard.

Settlement selects a bucket by comparing `vintage_year` with the account's `compliance_year`: a vintage earlier than the compliance year addresses `holdings_prior_vintage_tons`, a vintage equal to it addresses `holdings_current_vintage_tons`, and a vintage later than it addresses `holdings_next_vintage_tons`. A `PURCHASE` or `TRANSFER_IN` adds `quantity_tons` to that bucket and records all three `debited_*` amounts as 0.0. A `SURRENDER` or `TRANSFER_OUT` drains **oldest vintage first**: `debited_prior_tons` is the smaller of the quantity still to be taken and `holdings_prior_vintage_tons`; the remainder is then taken as `debited_current_tons`, the smaller of what is left and `holdings_current_vintage_tons`; if anything still remains the settlement is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ALLOWANCES`; `debited_next_tons` stays 0.0, because a client-initiated debit never touches the next-vintage bucket. The two drained buckets are reduced by exactly those recorded amounts, and for a `TRANSFER_OUT` the counterparty account's bucket for the same `vintage_year` is credited by the full `quantity_tons`, incrementing that account's `version` by one. A `PENALTY_DEDUCTION` — which only the cascade creates — records `debited_next_tons` as the full `quantity_tons` and reduces `holdings_next_vintage_tons` by it, which **may drive that bucket negative**, with the other two `debited_*` amounts 0.0.

Settlement then refreshes the account's derived figures: `surrendered_tons` becomes the total `quantity_tons` across the account's `SETTLED` `SURRENDER` rows, `penalty_deducted_tons` becomes the total across its `SETTLED` `PENALTY_DEDUCTION` rows, and `shortfall_tons` becomes `obligation_tons` minus `surrendered_tons`, rounded half up to three decimals and floored at 0.0 so it never goes negative. The account's `version` increments by one, the facility's `compliance_posture` is recomputed and the facility's `version` increments by one when the posture changes, and the transaction moves to `SETTLED` with its own `version` incremented.

**The true-up** is run by a `RegulatoryReviewer` against an account standing at `OPEN`, and it converts a year of accepted emissions into an integral allowance obligation. Two preconditions gate it, in this order: the logical clock must be **at or after** the facility's `true_up_deadline_utc`, else `ERR_TRUE_UP_BEFORE_DEADLINE`; and every quarterly report of every unit of that facility for the account's `compliance_year` must stand at `ACCEPTED`, so a single report left at `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` blocks the year, else `ERR_REPORTS_NOT_ALL_ACCEPTED`.

The waterfall then runs. Emitted tonnage is the sum of `total_so2_tons` across every report standing at `ACCEPTED` for a unit of this facility whose `year` equals the `compliance_year`, rounded half up to three decimals. Event tonnage is the sum of `penalty_allowance_debit_tons` across every excess emission event standing at `ENFORCEABLE` on those units whose `detected_utc` falls in the compliance year. `obligation_tons` is the emitted tonnage **raised to the next whole number** plus that integral event tonnage — a ceiling on the emissions, so a fractional ton of SO2 obliges a whole allowance. Holdings are then drained banked-first: the amount taken from `holdings_prior_vintage_tons` is the smaller of `obligation_tons` and that bucket's balance; the amount taken from `holdings_current_vintage_tons` is the smaller of what remains and that bucket's balance. `surrendered_tons` is the sum of the two amounts taken, rounded half up to three decimals, and `shortfall_tons` is whatever obligation is still uncovered, rounded half up to three decimals. Both buckets are reduced by exactly the amounts taken, `true_up_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, and a `SETTLED` transaction with `origin` of `TRUE_UP_SURRENDER` and `transaction_type` of `SURRENDER` is created recording those two amounts verbatim in `debited_prior_tons` and `debited_current_tons`.

If the shortfall is zero, the account moves straight to `CLOSED`, `penalty_deducted_tons` stays 0.0, no penalty transaction is created, the facility's `compliance_posture` is recomputed without reaching `DEFICIT` on account of this account, dispatch is left untouched, and the account's `version` increments by one.

If `shortfall_tons` is greater than zero, the account moves to `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` with its `version` incremented, and the remaining three links of the **true-up cascade** run:

- **Link 2**, caused by the shortfall rather than by the true-up request itself. A second `SETTLED` transaction with `origin` of `TRUE_UP_PENALTY` and `transaction_type` of `PENALTY_DEDUCTION` is created for `shortfall_tons` multiplied by the penalty ratio of 2.0 — twice the uncovered tonnage — rounded half up to three decimals. That quantity is debited from `holdings_next_vintage_tons`, **which may go negative**, `penalty_deducted_tons` is refreshed, and the account's `version` increments by one again.
- **Link 3**, caused by the account now carrying a shortfall. The facility's `compliance_posture` is recomputed and becomes `DEFICIT`, incrementing the facility's `version` by one.
- **Link 4**, caused by the posture becoming `DEFICIT`. Every unit of the facility has `dispatch_restricted` set to true, and every unit standing at `ONLINE` is moved to `RESERVE_SHUTDOWN`. Each affected unit's `version` increments by one. From here no unit of the station can be brought back `ONLINE` until the deficit is cleared.

Because the account is now locked, no new proposal and no further settlement against it will be accepted — the lock error precedes even the sufficiency error. The one deliberate exception is **reversal**, which is exactly how a locked account is released. `REVERSE` requires a `reason`, recorded as `reversal_reason`, else `ERR_REASON_REQUIRED`. Reversing a `RECORDED` transaction simply sets `reversal_reason`, moves it to `REVERSED` and increments its `version`; no holdings move, because nothing was ever debited. Reversing a `SETTLED` transaction runs the four-link **compensation cascade**:

- **Link 1.** Exactly `debited_prior_tons`, `debited_current_tons` and `debited_next_tons` are credited back to their three respective buckets — the stored amounts, not a re-derived split. `reversal_reason` is set, the transaction moves to `REVERSED` (its terminal state) with its `version` incremented, and the account's `version` increments by one.
- **Link 2**, caused by the holdings credit-back. When the reversed row has `origin` of `TRUE_UP_SURRENDER`, the account transitions from `TRUE_UP_LOCKED` back to `OPEN` and the true-up is **voided rather than re-derived**: `obligation_tons` returns to 0, `surrendered_tons`, `penalty_deducted_tons` and `shortfall_tons` all return to 0.0, `true_up_utc` returns to null, and the account's `version` increments by one. Every `SETTLED` transaction of that account with `origin` of `TRUE_UP_PENALTY` is moved to `REVERSED` with its `debited_next_tons` credited back to `holdings_next_vintage_tons`. Reversing any *other* row of a locked account leaves the account `TRUE_UP_LOCKED`, and only `surrendered_tons`, `penalty_deducted_tons` and `shortfall_tons` are refreshed.
- **Link 3**, caused by the account leaving shortfall. The facility's `compliance_posture` is recomputed and leaves `DEFICIT`, incrementing the facility's `version` by one.
- **Link 4**, caused by the posture leaving `DEFICIT`. Every unit of the facility has `dispatch_restricted` set back to false, incrementing each unit's `version` by one.

**Facility compliance posture** is the station-level rollup that link three and link four hang on, and it is recomputed on transaction settlement and reversal, on true-up, on report submission, certification and disposition, and when an excess emission event is enforced. If **any** allowance account of the facility carries `shortfall_tons` greater than zero, `compliance_posture` is `DEFICIT`, regardless of holdings. Otherwise the system looks forward: for each account, the projected obligation is the sum of `total_so2_tons` across the facility's reports standing at `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `ACCEPTED` whose `year` equals that account's `compliance_year`, rounded half up to three decimals and then raised to the next whole number; the covered tonnage is that account's `holdings_current_vintage_tons` plus `holdings_prior_vintage_tons`. If the covered tonnage is **strictly less than** the projected obligation for any account, the posture is `WATCH` — the first such account settles it. If no account is short, the posture is `COMPLIANT`, and coverage exactly equal to the projection is `COMPLIANT`, not `WATCH`. A report standing at `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` contributes nothing to the projection, which is why rejecting a report after certification lowers the projection and can move a station from `WATCH` back to `COMPLIANT`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — registering a facility, adding a unit, installing a monitoring system, recording a calibration, scheduling an audit, drawing a fuel sample, opening a report, signing a certification, appending operating hours singly or in bulk, adding a permit limit, opening an allowance account, proposing an allowance transaction, planning an outage window — return `201 Created`. Every operation that modifies an existing resource, drives a state transition, or performs a computation over existing rows returns `200 OK`.
