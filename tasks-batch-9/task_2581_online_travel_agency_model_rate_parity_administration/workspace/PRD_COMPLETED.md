# OTA Model & Rate Parity Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An online travel marketplace does not sell a single product on a single set of terms. For one hotel chain the marketplace is the merchant of record: it collects from the traveller, grosses the supplier's net rate up to a sell price, and carries most of the payment risk. For the airline listed two rows below it the marketplace is an agency: the supplier publishes the sell rate, collects the money, and pays a commission. That one choice — merchant versus agency — reaches into pricing arithmetic, settlement timing, chargeback liability, fraud exposure and the shape of every refund. This document specifies the administrative backbone that makes the choice explicit, auditable and enforceable per supplier and per date range, rather than leaving it encoded in tribal knowledge and spreadsheets.

Two pressures converge on that backbone. The first is commercial: suppliers negotiate bespoke commission and markup terms, buy preferred placement, and expect the ranking to honour those deals without letting paid boosts run unbounded. The second is regulatory. A marketplace must display a total price in the way each jurisdiction demands, must disclose the criteria by which it ranks and sorts results, must label paid placement as paid, and must be able to show — after the fact, from stored data — exactly which weights produced a given result set. Rate parity sits between the two pressures: when a supplier undercuts the marketplace on its own direct channel, the commercial team wants mediation, not an immediate takedown, and the compliance team wants a documented ladder of escalation that ends in a defensible action.

The system described here therefore models markets, suppliers, commercial model agreements, negotiated overrides, certification of content quality and connectivity, dated rate and inventory offers, direct-channel parity observations and the mediation cases they open, preferred-placement deals, governed ranking runs with their frozen weights, air-plus-hotel packages with opaque bundle discounts, bookings with independent money and inventory dimensions, dispute mediation with two liability variants, and periodic supplier scorecards. Every deadline, expiry and cutoff is evaluated against a server-controlled logical clock so that the whole domain is deterministic and testable. All paths in this specification are mounted at the service root; there is no additional API prefix. State is held in process memory and every boot starts completely empty: nothing is seeded, no market, supplier or agreement pre-exists, and every row a client can read was created through the endpoints of §4 during that same process lifetime.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Marketplace Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — read or write — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capability set it acts under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header that is present but empty counts as absent, and the role name is compared exactly and case-sensitively, so `platformadmin` is not a role. `X-User-ID` is otherwise never inspected at this stage: any non-empty value authenticates, including — when the role is `SupplierUser` — one that names no existing supplier, which simply fails the scope comparison below on every write while still reading freely. If both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear on the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before any other, in that order.

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `CommercialManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ParityAnalyst`, `SupplierUser` and `BookingAgent`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the marketplace operator. It registers markets, onboards and offboards suppliers, approves paid-slot purchases, and owns the two test-driving capabilities (logical-clock control and acquirer-initiated chargeback simulation). It deliberately holds no commercial or compliance powers: a `PlatformAdmin` cannot draft or activate a model agreement, cannot adjudicate a certification, cannot approve a commission override and cannot publish a ranking run. A `CommercialManager` is the supply-side commercial owner: it proposes model agreements, negotiated overrides and preferred-placement deals, and maintains rate plans, offers and packages on the supplier's behalf. It proposes but never approves — every artifact it proposes is activated by a different identity. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the regulatory and approval authority: it activates model agreements, adjudicates and revokes certifications, reinstates suspended suppliers, approves or revokes overrides, publishes ranking runs with their disclosure version, waives parity cases, resolves disputes and finalises scorecards. A `ParityAnalyst` monitors rate parity: it records direct-channel observations, works the parity case ladder and suppresses out-of-parity inventory, but cannot waive a case and cannot touch commercial terms. A `SupplierUser` is the supplier acting on its own account. A `BookingAgent` is the demand-side booking channel: it assembles and prices packages, takes bookings, captures payment and cancels stays, but cannot publish anything and cannot resolve a dispute it filed.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare the resource's supplier reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SupplierUser` write is admitted only when `X-User-ID` equals the owning supplier of the target resource — the `supplier_id` of a `Certification`, the `supplier_id` of a `RatePlan`, the plan owner behind an `Offer`, the plan owner behind a `ParityCase`, the supplier of the booked inventory behind a `Booking`, or the `supplier_id` of a `DisputeCase`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Where the booked inventory spans more than one supplier — a package booking whose air and hotel legs belong to different suppliers — the write is admitted when `X-User-ID` matches any one of the suppliers behind the booking's component offers, and that is the rule for confirming a booking, completing a booking and filing a dispute against one. If the owning supplier cannot be determined at all, the same `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` is returned. Read endpoints are not scope-restricted: any authenticated role may read any resource. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `CommercialManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ParityAnalyst` or `BookingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `proposed_by_user_id`, `activated_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `opened_by_user_id`, `closed_by_user_id`, `observed_by_user_id`, `published_by_user_id`, `created_by_user_id`, `booked_by_user_id`, `filed_by_user_id`, `resolved_by_user_id` or `finalized_by_user_id` — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, freshly randomised on every response.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one and only value in this API read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it advances between two calls made at the same logical instant and must never be used as a domain timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints. They are present only there: the `meta` of a single-object response, of an empty-data response and of an error response carries `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` and nothing else. A list response always sets `response_type` to `"array"` and carries a JSON array in `data`, even when the page is empty; every other success response sets `response_type` to `"object"`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer, or is below 1, or above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is below 0, are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` has no upper bound, and an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is a well-formed request that returns an empty page with `has_more` false. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and its default applies; anything else that is not the plain decimal spelling of an integer — a fractional value such as `10.5`, a padded value such as `010`, a signed value such as `+5`, or any non-numeric text — is rejected, while surrounding whitespace is tolerated. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. The default ordering for a collection is `created_at` ascending. A number of collections are governed by a domain-specific ordering instead, and where that is the case the ordering is fixed rather than client-selectable: markets and ranking runs are returned newest first, that is by `created_at` descending; model agreements are ordered by `effective_from` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; placement agreements by `window_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; direct-rate observations by `observed_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; scorecards by `period_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; and ranking entries by `rank_position` ascending. Suppliers, certifications, rate plans, offers, parity cases, commission overrides, packages, package components, bookings and dispute cases all use the default `created_at` ascending order. Because the logical clock only moves when it is explicitly set, rows written in the same test step routinely share an identical `created_at`, so every ordering above carries an implicit final tie-break: rows that tie on the declared key or keys are returned in insertion order, that is in the order the server created them. The two descending orderings reverse the composite key including that tie-break, so of two rows written at the same logical instant the later-written one is returned first. Filter query parameters are all optional and are matched by exact equality; one supplied as an empty string is treated as absent. A filter whose value is not a member of the declared enumeration, a boolean filter whose value is not one of `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case, and a date filter not written as `YYYY-MM-DD` are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), whereas an identifier-valued filter is never checked for existence — one naming a row that does not exist is a valid request that returns an empty page with `total_count` 0, not a not-found error.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Immutable Rows

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was written, taken from the logical clock of §2.5. Alongside the declared fields the server keeps two purely internal ones — the insertion sequence that breaks the ordering ties of §2.3, and the pointer identifying which certification currently backs a supplier's standing — and neither is ever serialised into a response body.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. This applies to `Market`, `Supplier`, `ModelAgreement`, `Certification`, `RatePlan`, `Offer`, `ParityCase`, `CommissionOverride`, `PlacementAgreement`, `RankingRun`, `Package`, `Booking`, `DisputeCase` and `SupplierScorecard`. Writing a computed field counts as a mutation: when a certification decision copies scores onto its supplier, or when a booking cancellation rewrites the retained money components, the touched row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented.

Three record types are append-only evidence rather than mutable state, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them. Those three are `DirectRateObservation` (a captured direct-channel price is never edited; a later capture is a new row), `RankingEntry` (a ranked row is frozen at run construction and is never rewritten, not even when the placement deal behind it is later cancelled) and `PackageComponent`. A `PackageComponent`'s own snapshot fields are rewritten by repricing, but the row itself exposes no `updated_at` and no `version`; the mutation is accounted for on the owning `Package`, whose `version` advances instead.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Expiry Sweeps

Nothing in this system's domain reasoning reads the host's wall clock; the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` is the sole exception, as §2.2 notes. All temporal reasoning — certification expiry, override and placement windows, refund cutoffs, travel-date horizons, scorecard period boundaries, arrival instants — is evaluated against a server-held logical UTC clock, and every server-stamped timestamp is a rendering of that clock. Until a `PlatformAdmin` sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. The clock may be moved forward or backward to any explicit instant supplied as `current_utc`, which must be a parseable ISO 8601 instant or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); a trailing `Z` and an explicit numeric UTC offset are both accepted, an instant carrying no zone designator at all is read as UTC, and any offset is normalised to UTC before anything else happens. Setting the clock returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than a clock resource. Only `created_at` and `updated_at` are guaranteed to carry three fractional digits; every other stored instant — `activated_at`, `expires_at`, `escalated_at`, `published_at`, `priced_at`, `arrival_at`, `cancelled_at` — is rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC form but carries a fractional part only when the underlying instant has non-zero milliseconds, so a clock set to a whole second yields, for example, `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

Moving the clock forward runs three sweeps, in this exact order; the clock is advanced to the new instant before the sweeps run, so every row a sweep touches carries that new instant as its `updated_at`. Each sweep visits the candidate rows of its own collection in insertion order. First the certification expiry sweep: every `Certification` whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `PROVISIONAL` and whose `expires_at` is non-null and at or before the new instant moves to `EXPIRED`, which — and only when that package is the one currently backing the supplier's standing, exactly as for a revocation in §3.1 — cascades its owning supplier from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, which in turn suppresses that supplier's `LIVE` offers. The certification itself always expires; a supplier that is not `ACTIVE` at that moment is left untouched and none of its offers are suppressed, and an expiring package that no longer backs the supplier's standing expires silently. Second the override expiry sweep: every `CommissionOverride` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` falls strictly before the new logical clock date moves to `EXPIRED`. Third the placement expiry sweep: every `PlacementAgreement` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `window_end` is at or before the new logical clock date moves to `EXPIRED`. Every row the sweeps touch has its `version` incremented by 1. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and runs no sweep at all, and so is setting the clock to the instant it already holds: the sweeps run only when the supplied instant is strictly later than the current one, and in both other cases the clock is still stored and HTTP 200 still returned. The two date-based sweeps compare against the calendar date of the new instant, so a forward move within a single day still re-runs them and simply finds nothing newly expired.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this domain is an integer number of minor currency units — cents — denominated in the `currency` recorded on the governing rate plan. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in responses. Percentages and rates are decimals: a `base_commission_pct` of 0.12 means twelve percent.

Three distinct rounding disciplines are used, and which one applies is never a matter of taste. The default is round half up to a whole cent: it governs tax and fee computation, agency commission, refund shares, mediation fees and liability splits. The merchant gross-up is the single exception on the upward side — it rounds up to the next whole cent (ceiling), never half up. Discount arithmetic on packages rounds down (floor), both at the bundle level and per component, with the leftover cents redistributed by a largest-remainder rule described in §3.7. Scores that are not money — `price_competitiveness`, `organic_score`, `final_score` and `composite_score` — are carried to exactly 2 decimal places using round half up. How such a number is then rendered in JSON follows one rule: `price_competitiveness`, `organic_score` and `final_score`, like every stored rate or fraction — `discount_pct`, `mediation_fee_pct`, `cancellation_refund_pct` and the entries of a run's `weights_snapshot` — are emitted as a plain integer whenever the rounded value is whole, so the cheapest ranked candidate's price competitiveness appears as `100` and a zero discount as `0`, whereas `composite_score` alone always keeps a fractional part and appears as `100.0`. Parity breach measurement is an integer number of basis points, rounded half up, where one basis point is one hundredth of a percent.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Rate Plans and Packages

Two write operations act on a parent whose state a client may have observed some time earlier, and both are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency token that the client must supply.

Ingesting a dated offer through `POST /rate-plans/{id}/offers` requires the non-optional field `expected_rate_plan_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `RatePlan` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because rate ingestion legitimately supersedes the previous offer for the same `travel_date`: a writer holding a stale view of the plan would silently replace inventory it never saw.

Pricing a package through `POST /packages/{id}/price` requires the non-optional field `expected_package_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Package` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the package's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because repricing overwrites the frozen component snapshots: a writer that has not observed a newly attached component would publish an allocation computed over the wrong component set.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Every endpoint not named above processes its mutation without any optimistic-concurrency comparison.

### 2.8. Nested Addressing and Path Resolution Order

Several resources are addressed beneath their parent — a certification, a rate plan or a scorecard beneath a supplier; an offer or a parity observation beneath a rate plan; a component beneath a package; a dispute beneath a booking. Where a path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. The catalogue of 404 identities is deliberately fine-grained so the failing segment is unambiguous: `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RANKING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RANKING_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. The same catalogue is reused for identifiers supplied in a request body: when a create payload names a market, supplier, rate plan, offer, package or booking that does not exist, the matching not-found identity is returned. Body-reference resolution is required on supplier onboarding, rate-plan creation, override proposal, placement proposal, ranking-run creation, package creation, component attachment, booking creation and chargeback simulation.

Routing precedes the validation ladder of §2.10, so a request that never reaches a declared endpoint is answered without any authentication check: a path matching no declared route at all is rejected with the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) rather than one of the fine-grained identities above, a path that exists under a different method is rejected with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unexpected server fault is reported as `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500); all three use the standard error envelope of §2.2. A trailing slash is insignificant, so `/markets` and `/markets/` address the same endpoint.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

This marketplace separates the identity that proposes from the identity that blesses. Six operations therefore compare `X-User-ID` against a stored proposer, opener or filer and reject a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): activating a model agreement (compared against `proposed_by_user_id`), approving a commission override and rejecting a commission override (both compared against the override's `proposed_by_user_id`), approving a placement agreement (compared against its `proposed_by_user_id`), resolving a dispute case (compared against `filed_by_user_id`), and closing a parity case with the `WAIVED` outcome (compared against `opened_by_user_id`, and additionally requiring that `X-User-Role` be `ComplianceOfficer`). Closing a parity case with the `COMPLIANT` outcome carries no such comparison, so a `ParityAnalyst` may close a case it opened as compliant but may never waive one. Each comparison is an exact string match on `X-User-ID`; where the stored proposer, opener or filer happens to be null, nothing can match it and the operation proceeds.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several checks could reject the same request, the outcome must be predictable, so all validation in this system is evaluated in one global ascending priority order. The first check that fails determines the response; later checks are not evaluated and cannot mask an earlier failure. The ladder is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles, on every endpoint, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Authorisation — the acting role appears on the endpoint's role list, on every endpoint, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Supplier scoping — a `SupplierUser` write targets a resource its own supplier owns, on every `SupplierUser` write endpoint (read endpoints are unrestricted), otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This check outranks path existence only where the path identifier is itself the supplier — submitting a certification or creating a rate plan beneath `/suppliers/{id}` — so a `SupplierUser` naming a supplier that is not itself gets the 403 even when that supplier does not exist. On every other scoped write the owning supplier can only be learned by reading the addressed row, so the path identifier is resolved first and an unresolvable one yields its 404 ahead of any scope comparison.
4. Path existence — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, on every endpoint that has path identifiers, otherwise the matching not-found identity (404).
5. Body reference existence — every foreign reference in the request body resolves, on supplier onboarding, rate-plan creation, override proposal, placement proposal, ranking-run creation, package creation, component attachment, booking creation and chargeback simulation, otherwise the matching not-found identity (404). This check only engages when the reference actually arrives as a non-empty string; a reference that is absent, null, empty or of some other JSON type is not a resolution failure but a shape failure, and falls through to check 6 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. Request shape — every required field present, every enum value a declared member, every integer an integer, every percentage inside its declared range, on every endpoint, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The shape rules are uniform across every endpoint. A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON is treated as an empty JSON object, so such a request fails on its first missing required field rather than on a parse error; a body that parses to a JSON value that is not an object is itself rejected. An explicit `null` is treated exactly as an absent key, so an optional or defaulted field sent as `null` takes its default and a required field sent as `null` is rejected. A required string must be non-empty, while an optional string is only type-checked and may be the empty string. A field declared as an integer rejects a boolean and rejects any float-typed value even when it is integrally valued, such as `5.0`; a field declared as a percentage, rate or other number accepts an integer or a float but likewise rejects a boolean. Declared minimum and maximum bounds are inclusive on both ends. A date field must be exactly the ten characters of a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, so `2026-8-1` is rejected, and an instant field must be a non-empty string that parses as an ISO 8601 instant under the rules §2.5 states for `current_utc`. Enum membership is an exact, case-sensitive comparison. And keys in the body that the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, so an unknown field never changes an outcome.
7. Pagination shape — `limit` an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` an integer of 0 or more, on every list endpoint, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A malformed filter value of §2.3 carries the same identity and status, so the two are indistinguishable to a client.
8. Optimistic concurrency — `expected_rate_plan_version` equals the plan's `version` on offer ingestion, and `expected_package_version` equals the package's `version` on package pricing, otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
9. State precondition — the target sits in a state the requested transition declares as its origin, on every state-transition endpoint, otherwise the transition-specific 409 identity listed in §5.
10. Segregation of duties — the six comparisons of §2.9, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
11. Ranking disclosure — a run being published in a market that requires disclosure carries a `disclosure_version`, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` (422).
12. Opaque-package disclosure — supplier names stay hidden on an opaque package, otherwise `ERR_OPAQUE_DISCLOSURE_FORBIDDEN` (422).
13. Supplier standing — the offer's supplier is certified and visible on offer publication, and the named supplier's `visibility_status` is `ACTIVE` on placement approval, otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422).
14. Model-agreement window disjointness on activation, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_AGREEMENT_OVERLAP` (409).
15. Paid-slot capacity on placement approval, otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
16. Travel-date horizon — on offer ingestion, package creation and ranking-run creation, otherwise `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).
17. Scorecard period closure on finalisation, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (409).
18. Parity notification round cap, otherwise `ERR_PARITY_ROUND_LIMIT` (409).
19. Certification remediation round cap, otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_LIMIT` (409).
20. Dispute mediation round cap, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` (409).
21. Package component mix — on both package pricing and package publication, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_COMPONENT_MIX_INVALID` (422).
22. Market code uniqueness on market registration, otherwise `ERR_MARKET_CODE_TAKEN` (409).
23. Booking target exclusivity, otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_TARGET_INVALID` (422).
24. Ranking candidate pool non-empty on ranking-run creation, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_OFFERS` (422).
25. Paid-entry placement still active on ranking publication, otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (422).
26. Component offers still live — on both package pricing and package publication, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_LIVE` (422).
27. Component date and market agreement on component attachment, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DATE_MISMATCH` (422).
28. Governing model agreement coverage — on offer ingestion, on offer publication and on commission-override approval, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (422).
29. Override type compatible with the governing model on override approval, otherwise `ERR_OVERRIDE_MODEL_MISMATCH` (422).
30. Chargeback precondition on dispute filing, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CHARGED_BACK` (409).
31. Quoted price agreement on booking creation, otherwise `ERR_PRICE_CHANGED` (409).
32. Allotment availability on booking creation, otherwise `ERR_ALLOTMENT_EXHAUSTED` (409).
33. Something left to dispute on dispute filing, otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_TO_DISPUTE` (422).

The practical consequences of this ordering are worth naming. On booking creation the target-exclusivity check at position 23 fires before the price check at 31 and the allotment check at 32, so a payload naming both an offer and a package is rejected as an invalid target rather than as a price mismatch. On package pricing the optimistic-concurrency check at position 8 fires before the component-mix check at 21 and the component-liveness check at 26, so a stale token is reported even when the component set is also invalid. On placement approval the segregation-of-duties comparison at position 10 fires before the capacity computation at 15. And on ranking publication the disclosure check at 11 fires before the paid-entry check at 25.

### 2.11. Cascade Propagation

A single compliance action in this domain can travel four hops through the graph, and each hop must be applied in full. The canonical chain begins when a certification is revoked or expires. Hop one drives the owning supplier from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, clears its derived certification standing and records why. Hop two suppresses every `LIVE` offer of that supplier. Hop three withdraws every `PUBLISHED` package that holds one of those offers as a component — recording `COMPONENT_SUPPRESSED` as the package's `withdrawal_reason` — and withdraws every `PUBLISHED` ranking run that holds a ranked entry for one of them, recording that same `COMPONENT_SUPPRESSED` value as the run's own `withdrawal_reason`. Hop four returns every `PRICED` package holding a suppressed offer to `DRAFT` and clears its frozen pricing, which means nulling the package's `components_subtotal_cents`, `discount_pct`, `discount_cents`, `package_total_cents` and `priced_at` and nulling the `display_total_snapshot_cents`, `discount_alloc_cents` and `allocated_cents` of every one of its components; the components carry no `version`, so that clearing is accounted for on the package alone. This is the same clearing routine that a leg attachment runs against an already-priced package in §3.7, and it is why a component's snapshot is null between the invalidation and the next pricing run. A package or run reached by two different suppressed offers is withdrawn or de-priced once only, on the first hop that arrives, so its `version` advances by exactly 1; a package that is merely `DRAFT` is left alone entirely. The parity chain is the same shape with a different head: escalating a parity case holds the rate plan, the hold suppresses that plan's `LIVE` offers, and those suppressions withdraw or de-price the dependent packages and runs.

Two properties of these cascades are deliberate and must not be "improved" upon. First, cascades never run backwards: reinstating a supplier does not republish its offers, and releasing a parity hold does not republish the plan's offers — each offer must be published again individually. Second, every row a cascade touches is a mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, including rows several hops away from the originating request. Once a supplier reaches `OFFBOARDED`, it is terminal: any write against that supplier, or against one of its rate plans, offers, certifications, model agreements, commission overrides or parity cases, or against a placement agreement being proposed or approved, is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_OFFBOARDED` (HTTP 409); the operations that reach the supplier only indirectly — every booking, package, component, ranking-run, dispute and scorecard operation, and cancelling a placement agreement — carry no such guard and proceed normally. The guard's place in the ladder of §2.10 is fixed: it is evaluated after every request-shape and optimistic-concurrency check, and on supplier, rate-plan, offer, override, placement and parity-case writes it precedes the transition's own state precondition at priority 9; on model-agreement and certification writes it follows that state precondition instead, so a wrongly-stated agreement or certification belonging to an offboarded supplier reports its own 409 identity rather than `ERR_SUPPLIER_OFFBOARDED`. The offboarding close-out of §3.1 is not itself a suppression and does not enter this chain: its offers move straight to `CLOSED`, and no package or ranking run that quoted them is withdrawn or de-priced by that act.

### 2.12. Regulated Disclosure and Total-Price Display

Two regulatory obligations are configured per market and enforced throughout. The first is the total-price display regime, recorded as `price_display_mode` on the market and taking one of two values: `TOTAL_UPFRONT`, meaning the traveller must be shown a single all-in figure, or `EXCL_TAXES`, meaning taxes and fees may be quoted separately. Whichever regime applies, every offer stores its decomposition — a pre-tax `gross_cents`, a `taxes_fees_cents` component, and a `display_total_cents` that is their sum — so the same stored row can serve either presentation without recomputation.

The second obligation is ranking transparency. A market whose `ranking_disclosure_required` flag is true (which is the default when the flag is omitted at registration) will not allow a ranking run to reach travellers unless the run carries a `disclosure_version` naming the published statement of ranking criteria. Independently of that, every ranked row records whether it was boosted by a commercial deal and carries a `disclosure_label` that is exactly one of `ORGANIC`, `SPONSORED` or `PREFERRED_PARTNER`; the latter two are the two labels a supplier may buy, and an unboosted row is always `ORGANIC`. Each run also freezes the four scoring weights it used into `weights_snapshot`, under the keys `price`, `content`, `parity` and `conversion`, so a published ordering can be reconstructed and defended long after the weights themselves have been retuned.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Markets, Supplier Onboarding and Certification

Everything in this marketplace is scoped to a **Market**, a jurisdiction the platform sells into. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one with a `market_code` — 3 to 12 characters of uppercase letters and hyphens, unique across all markets, with a duplicate rejected as `ERR_MARKET_CODE_TAKEN` — a human-readable `name`, a `paid_slot_capacity` of zero or more, a `price_display_mode`, and optionally the `ranking_disclosure_required` flag. A market's `status` begins at `ACTIVE` and its only other value is `CLOSED`; no endpoint in §4 moves a market between the two, so a registered market stays `ACTIVE` for the life of the process while `CLOSED` remains an accepted value of the list filter that nothing can ever match.

Suppliers arrive next. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `CommercialManager` onboards one with a `legal_name`, a `supplier_type` of either `HOTEL` or `AIR`, a `home_market_id` naming the market it is domiciled in, an operations `contact_email`, and optionally a `conversion_index` between 0 and 100 which defaults to 50 and later feeds the ranking score. A fresh supplier is not sellable: its `visibility_status` starts at `PENDING_CERT`, its `certification_level` at `NONE`, its `current_model` at `NONE`, its `content_score` and `connectivity_score` at 0, and its `parity_strikes` at 0, with `suspension_reason` null. Over its life a supplier's `visibility_status` moves among `PENDING_CERT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and the terminal `OFFBOARDED`.

Becoming sellable means passing content-quality and connectivity certification. A `SupplierUser` or a `CommercialManager` submits a certification package carrying a self-declared `content_score_claimed` between 0 and 100, a `connectivity_protocol` of `OTA_2011B`, `JSON_V2` or `GDS_EDIFACT`, and a `sample_payload_ref` pointing at the sample rate payload for review. The package is created in `SUBMITTED` with `remediation_rounds` at 0 and its `content_score`, `connectivity_score`, `decision`, `expires_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `revocation_reason` all null. Nothing limits a supplier to one package at a time: a fresh submission is always accepted whatever the status of any package the supplier already holds. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens review, moving the package `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW`; the same action moves a resubmitted package `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW`. Opening review on a package in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CERT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`.

Adjudication is the pivotal act. The reviewing `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `decision` of `CERTIFY`, `PROVISIONAL` or `REJECT` together with the adjudicated `content_score` and `connectivity_score`, each between 0 and 100, and may add a `note`. Only a package in `IN_REVIEW` may be decided; anything else is rejected with `ERR_CERT_NOT_DECIDED`. A `CERTIFY` decision requires both adjudicated scores to clear their pass marks — the content score must be at least the content pass mark and the connectivity score must be at least the connectivity pass mark — but both pass marks are zero, so every score the shape check of §2.10 admits already clears them and no adjudicated score ever blocks a `CERTIFY` decision. That decision moves the package `IN_REVIEW → CERTIFIED`, stamping `reviewed_by_user_id` and setting `expires_at` to the logical-clock instant of the decision plus a full year of validity, which is 365 days. A `PROVISIONAL` decision moves the package `IN_REVIEW → PROVISIONAL` with the same stamps but a shorter runway: `expires_at` becomes the decision instant plus 90 days. Both of those outcomes drive the supplier from `PENDING_CERT` to `ACTIVE`, set its `certification_level` to `CERTIFIED` or `PROVISIONAL` respectively, copy the adjudicated `content_score` and `connectivity_score` from the certification onto the supplier's own `content_score` and `connectivity_score`, and make this package the one that backs the supplier's standing, repointing that internal pointer away from whichever package held it before; the certification's `version` and the supplier's `version` each increment by 1. Only the `visibility_status` move is conditional on the supplier actually sitting in `PENDING_CERT`: a supplier that is already `ACTIVE`, or one sitting in `SUSPENDED`, keeps the status it has, so a suspended supplier that gets a fresh package certified has its `certification_level` and scores refreshed and its backing pointer repointed but must still be reinstated separately before anything of its can be published. A `REJECT` decision moves the package `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stores the scores, the `REJECT` decision and `reviewed_by_user_id`, sets no expiry, and leaves the supplier's `certification_level` exactly as it was.

A rejected supplier gets a bounded second chance. A `SupplierUser` — and only the owning one — resubmits with a fresh `content_score_claimed` and `sample_payload_ref`, moving the package `REJECTED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, replacing the stored `sample_payload_ref` and incrementing `remediation_rounds` by 1. Remediation is only available from `REJECTED`, otherwise `ERR_CERT_NOT_REJECTED`, and only while `remediation_rounds` is still below three, otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_LIMIT`. The cycle `REJECTED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW → REJECTED` may therefore run at most three times before the supplier is stuck. Remediation rewrites only `content_score_claimed`, `sample_payload_ref`, `remediation_rounds` and `status`: the `content_score`, `connectivity_score` and `REJECT` `decision` written by the previous adjudication stay on the row and are overwritten only by the next one.

Certification can also be taken away. A `ComplianceOfficer` revokes a `CERTIFIED` or `PROVISIONAL` package for cause, supplying a `revocation_reason` that is stored on the row, and the package moves to the terminal `REVOKED`; a package in any other status — `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REJECTED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, or already `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` — is rejected with `ERR_CERT_NOT_DECIDED`, the same identity a premature adjudication carries. The same head applies when the logical clock passes a package's `expires_at`, in which case the package moves to the terminal `EXPIRED`. Both outcomes trigger the full four-hop cascade of §2.11: the supplier moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `CERTIFICATION_REVOKED` for a revocation or `CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` for an expiry, its `certification_level` resets to `NONE` and its `content_score` and `connectivity_score` both reset to 0; every `LIVE` offer of that supplier moves to `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`; every `PUBLISHED` package holding one of those offers moves to `WITHDRAWN` and every `PUBLISHED` ranking run holding one of them moves to `WITHDRAWN`; and every `PRICED` package holding one returns to `DRAFT`. The suspension cascade only fires when the revoked or expired package is the one currently backing the supplier's `certification_level`; revoking a historical package that no longer backs the supplier's standing does not suspend anybody. Nor does a revocation touch a supplier that is not `ACTIVE` at that moment — one already `SUSPENDED`, or still `PENDING_CERT` — exactly as in the expiry sweep of §2.5: the package still reaches `REVOKED` and stores its reason, while the supplier, its derived standing and its offers are all left as they are.

Reinstatement is a separate, deliberate act. A `ComplianceOfficer` lifts a suspension, optionally recording a `note`; this moves the supplier `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and clears `suspension_reason`. It requires the supplier's `certification_level` to be `PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED` at the current logical clock and the supplier's `visibility_status` to be `SUSPENDED`; a supplier that is not suspended, or one with no live certification, is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_SUSPENDED`. Reinstatement never un-suppresses offers.

Offboarding ends the relationship. A `PlatformAdmin` supplies a `reason` — a required non-empty string that is validated but never persisted, so a supplier offboarded out of `SUSPENDED` keeps whatever `suspension_reason` it already carried — and the supplier moves to the terminal `OFFBOARDED` from whichever status it holds: there is no origin-status precondition, so `PENDING_CERT`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` are all valid origins, and only a second attempt against an already-offboarded supplier fails, on the terminal guard of §2.11 with `ERR_SUPPLIER_OFFBOARDED`. The one precondition is inventory: no booking that consumed an offer of any of that supplier's rate plans — for a package booking, any one of its component legs — may still sit in the `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` fulfilment state, and one that does rejects the request with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED` (HTTP 409), the catalogue's nearest booking-state identity. The transition archives every rate plan of the supplier and closes out every offer beneath those plans that sits in `PENDING`, `LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED`, incrementing the `version` of the supplier and of every touched plan and offer by 1.

### 3.2. Choosing the Commercial Model: Merchant versus Agency

The **ModelAgreement** is where the marketplace's relationship with a supplier is actually decided. A `CommercialManager` drafts one against a named `supplier_id`, choosing a `model` of either `MERCHANT` — the marketplace is merchant of record, grosses the supplier's net rate up to a sell price and collects from the traveller — or `AGENCY`, where the supplier publishes the sell rate and collects, and the marketplace earns a commission out of it. The draft carries a window: `effective_from` is the first calendar date covered, written as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date and inclusive, and `effective_to` is the last date covered, also inclusive; when `effective_to` is omitted or null the agreement is open-ended and is treated for every window computation as if it ended on 9999-12-31. A supplied `effective_to` must not precede `effective_from`, and an inverted pair is rejected as a shape failure with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); the two dates being equal is accepted and describes a legitimate single-day window.

Economics travel with the window. Under `AGENCY` the draft supplies `base_commission_pct`, the commission share of the supplier-published sell rate, expressed as a decimal from 0 through 0.5. Under `MERCHANT` it supplies `base_markup_pct` instead, the target margin share of gross used to gross the net rate up, likewise a decimal from 0 through 0.5 — the ceiling on a markup rate is fifty percent of gross. Exactly one of the two is meaningful per model, and the irrelevant one must be omitted or sent as null. Independently of the model, every agreement fixes three risk and settlement terms: `chargeback_liability_pct_marketplace`, the marketplace's share of a dispute settlement total as a decimal from 0 through 1; `fraud_liability_cap_cents`, a hard cap of zero or more cents on the marketplace's share whenever the dispute is a fraud claim; and `settlement_terms_days`, the net settlement days owed to the supplier, an integer from 1 through 90. The draft records `proposed_by_user_id` from the acting identity, starts in `DRAFT`, and leaves `activated_at`, `activated_by_user_id` and `termination_reason` null.

Activation belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer`, and it is where the interesting constraints live. The agreement must still be in `DRAFT` — anything else is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT` — the supplier must not be `OFFBOARDED`, and the activating `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `proposed_by_user_id`. The window check is the subtle one. Treating each agreement's coverage as the closed date interval running from `effective_from` through `effective_to` (with an open-ended agreement closing at 9999-12-31), no other agreement of the same supplier that is already `ACTIVE` may share a single date with the candidate. Because the intervals are closed on both ends, two windows that merely touch — where the candidate's `effective_from` equals an existing agreement's `effective_to`, or vice versa — do overlap, and activation is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_AGREEMENT_OVERLAP`. There is exactly one exemption: an existing `ACTIVE` agreement whose `effective_from` is the same date as the candidate's `effective_from` is not treated as a conflict. Instead that predecessor is moved `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments by 1. Same-start replacement is the only route to `SUPERSEDED`; every other pair of windows must be genuinely disjoint.

A successful activation moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and `activated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and then recomputes the supplier's derived `current_model` from whichever `ACTIVE` agreement covers the current logical-clock date, yielding `MERCHANT`, `AGENCY`, or `NONE` when no such agreement exists. The agreement's `version` increments by 1, the supplier's `version` increments by 1, and any superseded sibling's `version` increments by 1 as well.

Two exits close the loop. A `CommercialManager` may withdraw an agreement that never went live, optionally recording a `note`; this moves it `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, a terminal status, and increments its `version` by 1. Withdrawal is only available from `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. A `CommercialManager` may also terminate one that did go live, supplying a `termination_reason` that is stored on the row; this moves it `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, another terminal status, recomputes the supplier's `current_model` (which becomes `NONE` when no remaining `ACTIVE` agreement covers today's logical date), and increments both the agreement's and the supplier's `version` by 1. Terminating an agreement that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE_STATE`. Crucially, termination reprices nothing: an offer's economics are pinned at the moment the offer was ingested, and no lifecycle event on the agreement ever rewrites an already-ingested offer's price.

### 3.3. Negotiated Overrides and How a Rate Is Selected

Base rates are a starting point; real supply deals are negotiated. A `CommercialManager` proposes a **CommissionOverride** naming a `supplier_id` and, optionally, a `rate_plan_id`. When `rate_plan_id` is supplied the override is scoped to that single plan; when it is null the override is supplier-wide. Both are body references under priority 5 and are resolved in the order written here, so a payload naming a non-existent supplier and a non-existent plan at once reports `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`; the plan is never required to belong to the named supplier. The `override_type` names which lever the negotiated number replaces: `COMMISSION_PCT` replaces the agency commission rate, `MARKUP_PCT` replaces the merchant markup rate, and `FLAT_FEE_CENTS` replaces the percentage arithmetic altogether with a fixed per-unit fee. The `value` field carries the negotiated number — a decimal fraction for the two percentage types, a whole number of cents for the flat fee — and must be zero or greater. Zero is its only bound: no ceiling is imposed on any of the three types, so a `MARKUP_PCT` or `COMMISSION_PCT` override may legitimately carry a value above the 0.5 that §3.2 imposes on a base rate, and the field is type-checked as a number rather than as an integer even for `FLAT_FEE_CENTS`, whose fractional part the sell-price build of §3.4 truncates toward zero when it spends the value as cents. `effective_from` and `effective_to` bound the travel dates the override applies to, both `YYYY-MM-DD`, both inclusive and both required; unlike a model-agreement window the two are never compared with each other, so an inverted pair is accepted and simply never covers a travel date. A `justification` string records the commercial rationale for audit. The proposal is created in `PROPOSED`, stores `proposed_by_user_id`, and leaves `approved_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` null.

Approval is a `ComplianceOfficer` act and carries four conditions. The override must still be `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PENDING`. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`. A model agreement of that supplier with status `ACTIVE` must cover the override's `effective_from` date — only that single date is tested, never the rest of the override's window — and when none does, the approval is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422), the same identity offer ingestion uses for the same absence. And the `override_type` must be compatible with that agreement's `model`: a `COMMISSION_PCT` override requires an `AGENCY` agreement, a `MARKUP_PCT` override requires a `MERCHANT` agreement, and a `FLAT_FEE_CENTS` override is accepted under either. An incompatible pairing is rejected with `ERR_OVERRIDE_MODEL_MISMATCH`. On success the override moves `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, stores `approved_by_user_id`, and its `version` increments by 1.

Approval also has teeth, and this is the one place where an already-created offer is repriced. When an override becomes `ACTIVE`, every offer that is still in `PENDING`, whose rate plan falls inside the override's scope, and whose `travel_date` falls on or after `effective_from` and on or before `effective_to`, is repriced from scratch by the sell-price build of §3.4. Each such offer has its `applied_override_id`, `applied_rate_pct`, `gross_cents`, `taxes_fees_cents`, `display_total_cents`, `marketplace_margin_cents` and `supplier_payable_cents` rewritten, and its `version` incremented by 1. Offers that have already reached `LIVE` keep the price they were ingested at; only `PENDING` offers reprice, whatever the status of the rate plan beneath them. The sweep visits candidate offers in the order the server created them, and an offer whose `travel_date` is no longer covered by any `ACTIVE` model agreement is skipped silently — it keeps the price it already carries, its `version` does not move, and the approval still succeeds. Repricing re-runs the whole override-selection rule stated below rather than simply imposing the override just approved, so an offer that a higher-ranked override already governs is rewritten with that same override's terms; the write is unconditional, so even an offer whose recomputed figures are identical has its `version` incremented by 1.

The exits mirror the entrance. A `ComplianceOfficer` may reject a `PROPOSED` override, moving it to the terminal `REJECTED`, or revoke an `ACTIVE` one, moving it to the terminal `REVOKED`; both take a `rejection_reason` which is stored, both require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, both increment the override's `version` by 1, and an override in any other status is rejected with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PENDING`. Neither rejection nor revocation reprices anything — offers keep the price they were ingested at. Separately, the logical-clock sweep of §2.5 moves an `ACTIVE` override to the terminal `EXPIRED` once its `effective_to` falls strictly before the new logical-clock date, incrementing its `version` by 1.

When an offer is priced, more than one override may be eligible, so the selection rule is fixed and total. The candidate pool is every override of the offer's supplier whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose scope covers the offer's rate plan (either the override names that exact plan or it is supplier-wide with a null plan reference), whose date window covers the offer's `travel_date` on or after `effective_from` and on or before `effective_to`, and whose `override_type` is compatible with the governing agreement's `model` under the compatibility rule above. Overrides in `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` are never candidates. Candidates are then ranked by three criteria applied strictly in this order: first, a plan-scoped override outranks a supplier-wide one; second, within the same scope, a higher `value` outranks a lower one; third, if two candidates still tie, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins, and two candidates sharing an identical `created_at` — routine under the logical clock — are separated by insertion order, so the earlier-written one wins. The row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. The single highest-ranked candidate wins, and only it applies — override effects never compound.

### 3.4. Rate and Inventory Ingestion, and the Sell-Price Build

A **RatePlan** is the ingestion channel through which a supplier feeds rates and inventory into one market. A `SupplierUser` or a `CommercialManager` creates one beneath a supplier, supplying the `market_id` it sells into, a `name` shown on the listing, a `currency` in which every cents amount on the plan is denominated, nominally an ISO 4217 code but validated only as a required non-empty string and never checked for length, case or membership of any code list, a `cancellation_policy_code` of `FLEX`, `MODERATE`, `STRICT` or `NON_REF` which every booking on the plan inherits, a `free_cancel_cutoff_hours` from 0 through 720 (the outer bound is thirty days before arrival), a `tax_fee_pct` from 0 through 0.5 giving the taxes-and-fees rate applied to gross for the market's total-price display, and optionally `parity_monitored`, which defaults to true. A plan starts in `ACTIVE` with `parity_hold_case_id` null; over its life it may sit in `ACTIVE`, `PARITY_HOLD` — which blocks publishing offers — or the terminal `ARCHIVED`. Setting `parity_monitored` to false does not stop observations being recorded; it stops them opening a case.

A `CommercialManager` may archive a plan, optionally with a `note`, moving it `ACTIVE → ARCHIVED`; the same transition is reached by the supplier-offboarding cascade. Only an `ACTIVE` plan may be archived: a plan sitting in `PARITY_HOLD`, or already `ARCHIVED`, is rejected with `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ON_HOLD` — the plan's own state identity, reused for both directions of the status check. Archiving then requires that no offer of the plan is currently `LIVE`, and one that is rejects the request with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_LIVE` (HTTP 409); that inventory check is evaluated after the plan's own status check. On success it closes out every offer of the plan sitting in `PENDING`, leaves offers already `SUPPRESSED`, `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` exactly as they are, and increments the plan's `version` and the `version` of every touched offer by 1.

An **Offer** is one dated rate on one plan. A `SupplierUser` or a `CommercialManager` ingests one with a `travel_date` (the stay or departure date, `YYYY-MM-DD`), a `net_rate_cents` of at least 1 — the supplier's net rate under a merchant agreement, or the supplier-published sell rate under an agency agreement — an `allotment_total` of at least 1 giving the units released for that date, and the concurrency token `expected_rate_plan_version` described in §2.7. The `travel_date` must fall on or after the current logical-clock date and no later than 500 days after it; anything outside that horizon is rejected with `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. On creation the offer's `allotment_held` and `allotment_sold` are both 0 and its `allotment_remaining` equals `allotment_total`; the invariant that `allotment_total` always equals `allotment_remaining` plus `allotment_held` plus `allotment_sold` holds from that moment onward. The offer starts in `PENDING` with `suppression_reason` null.

Ingesting a newer offer for a `travel_date` that already has one supersedes the predecessor. If the prior offer sits in `PENDING`, `LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` and has both `allotment_held` and `allotment_sold` at 0, it moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments by 1. The successor always starts in `PENDING` and must be published separately — supersession does not inherit liveness. The plan's own `version` also increments by 1 on every ingestion, which is precisely why the concurrency token is required.

The sell price is computed at ingestion, in four steps whose order is pinned. **Step one resolves the governing agreement.** The system takes the model agreement of the plan's supplier whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose window covers the offer's `travel_date` — that is, whose `effective_from` falls on or before the travel date and whose `effective_to` (or 9999-12-31 when open-ended) falls on or after it. Because activation guarantees `ACTIVE` windows are disjoint, at most one such agreement exists. If none exists, ingestion is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; otherwise the offer's `pricing_model` is set to that agreement's `model`.

**Step two resolves the rate.** The override selector of §3.3 runs against the offer's supplier, plan and `travel_date`. If no candidate override survives, the offer's `applied_override_id` stays null and its `applied_rate_pct` is taken from the agreement's own base rate — `base_markup_pct` under a `MERCHANT` agreement, `base_commission_pct` under an `AGENCY` one — and the effective lever is treated as a markup percentage under `MERCHANT` and a commission percentage under `AGENCY`. If a candidate wins, the offer's `applied_override_id` is set to that override's `id`, `applied_rate_pct` is set to its `value`, and the effective lever is that override's `override_type`.

**Step three computes gross and margin, gross first and margin second.** Under a `MERCHANT` agreement with a flat-fee lever, `gross_cents` is the `net_rate_cents` plus the flat fee taken as a whole number of cents, and `marketplace_margin_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `net_rate_cents`. Under a `MERCHANT` agreement with a percentage lever, the marketplace grosses up **on the sell price**, not on the net: `gross_cents` is the `net_rate_cents` divided by the quantity one minus `applied_rate_pct`, and that quotient is rounded up to the next whole cent; `marketplace_margin_cents` is again `gross_cents` minus `net_rate_cents`. Multiplying the net by one plus the rate is the wrong reading and produces a different number. Under an `AGENCY` agreement the supplier has already published the sell rate, so `gross_cents` equals `net_rate_cents` exactly; with a flat-fee lever `marketplace_margin_cents` is the flat fee as a whole number of cents, and with a percentage lever `marketplace_margin_cents` is the `net_rate_cents` multiplied by `applied_rate_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. A flat fee is spent exactly as negotiated, with no clamp against the rate it applies to: an agency flat fee larger than the supplier-published `net_rate_cents` drives `marketplace_margin_cents` past `gross_cents` and leaves `supplier_payable_cents` negative, and nothing floors it at zero.

**Step four builds the regulated display.** `taxes_fees_cents` is `gross_cents` multiplied by the plan's `tax_fee_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. `display_total_cents` is `gross_cents` plus `taxes_fees_cents`. `supplier_payable_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `marketplace_margin_cents`. The identity that must hold on every stored offer is that `display_total_cents` equals `supplier_payable_cents` plus `marketplace_margin_cents` plus `taxes_fees_cents`; under the merchant percentage path `supplier_payable_cents` comes out exactly equal to `net_rate_cents`.

Publication makes an offer bookable. A `SupplierUser` or a `CommercialManager` publishes, optionally with a `note`, moving the offer `PENDING → LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED → LIVE`; on the second of those the `suppression_reason` is cleared. Four cross-entity conditions guard both paths equally: the supplier's `certification_level` must be `PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED`, the supplier's `visibility_status` must be `ACTIVE`, the plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a model agreement of the supplier with status `ACTIVE` must cover the offer's `travel_date`. Failing the certification or visibility condition yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_CERTIFIED`; failing the agreement condition yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; an offer that is neither `PENDING` nor `SUPPRESSED`, or whose plan is not `ACTIVE`, yields `ERR_OFFER_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`. Each publication increments the offer's `version` by 1. Because suppression and publication share the same two states, `LIVE → SUPPRESSED → LIVE` is a genuine cycle.

Suppression is the compliance lever. A `ParityAnalyst`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `CommercialManager` supplies a `suppression_reason`, which is stored, and the offer moves `LIVE → SUPPRESSED`; suppressing an offer that is not `LIVE` is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_LIVE`. The same transition is reached by the supplier-suspension cascade with reason `SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` and by the parity-hold cascade with reason `PARITY_HOLD`. Whichever the trigger, suppression propagates: every `PUBLISHED` ranking run holding a ranked entry for this offer moves to `WITHDRAWN`, every `PUBLISHED` package holding it as a component moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and every `PRICED` package holding it returns to `DRAFT` with its frozen pricing cleared. The `version` of every touched row increments by 1.

Close-out retires an offer permanently. A `SupplierUser` or a `CommercialManager` closes out, optionally with a `note`, and the offer moves to the terminal `CLOSED` from `PENDING`, from `LIVE` or from `SUPPRESSED`; an offer already `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_LIVE`. Closing out from `LIVE` or from `SUPPRESSED` additionally requires `allotment_held` to be 0, so inventory a traveller is currently holding cannot be retired underneath them, and held inventory is rejected with that same `ERR_OFFER_NOT_LIVE` identity rather than a distinct one; closing out from `PENDING` carries no allotment condition. The same terminal status is reached by the rate-plan archival cascade for `PENDING` offers and by the supplier-offboarding cascade for `PENDING`, `LIVE` and `SUPPRESSED` offers. Existing bookings against a closed-out offer are unaffected, and the offer's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.5. Parity Monitoring, Case Mediation and Rate-Plan Holds

Rate parity is measured by comparison, not by assertion. A `ParityAnalyst` posts a **DirectRateObservation** against a rate plan, supplying the `travel_date` observed, the `channel` the price was captured from — one of `SUPPLIER_WEB`, `SUPPLIER_APP` or `META` — the `observed_at` instant as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, and the `direct_total_cents`, which is the total price including taxes and fees as shown on the supplier's own channel and must be at least 1. The rate plan's own `status` is never examined, so an observation may be posted against a plan that is `ACTIVE`, one sitting on `PARITY_HOLD` or even an `ARCHIVED` one. The observation is immutable evidence: it records `observed_by_user_id` from the acting identity and is never edited.

Posting an observation runs the breach measurement. The marketplace side of the comparison is the offer on the same rate plan and the same `travel_date` whose `status` is `LIVE`; the observation's `compared_offer_id` records which offer was used. Because ingestion only supersedes a same-date predecessor that holds no allotment, as §3.4 describes, two `LIVE` offers can legitimately coexist for one plan and travel date; where they do, the comparison uses the one with the latest `created_at`, and of two written at the same logical instant the later-written one. If no `LIVE` offer exists for that plan and date, `compared_offer_id`, `breach_bps` and `parity_case_id` are all null, `severity` is `NONE`, and the observation is stored as evidence without opening anything. Where a comparison is possible, `breach_bps` is computed as the marketplace's `display_total_cents` minus the observed `direct_total_cents`, multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the observed `direct_total_cents`, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. A positive figure therefore means the marketplace is dearer than the supplier's direct channel, which is the direction that constitutes a breach.

The breach is banded, and every band boundary is inclusive on its lower edge and exclusive on its upper edge. A breach below one percent — below 100 basis points — is `NONE`. From 100 basis points up to but not including 300 it is `MINOR`. From 300 up to but not including 800 basis points it is `MAJOR`. At 800 basis points, that is eight percent or more, it is `SEVERE`. The band is stored on the observation as `severity`.

Case attachment happens only when the plan's `parity_monitored` flag is true and the derived `severity` is not `NONE`. The system looks for a **ParityCase** on the same `rate_plan_id` and `travel_date` whose `status` is neither `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT` nor `WAIVED`. If there is none, a new case is opened in `OPEN`, its `travel_date` copied from the observation, its `opening_observation_id` set to this observation, its `severity` set to the observed band, its `peak_breach_bps` set to this observation's `breach_bps`, its `notify_rounds` at 0, its `opened_by_user_id` taken from the posting analyst, and its `supplier_response_code`, `closed_by_user_id`, `close_outcome` and `escalated_at` left null. If a non-terminal case already exists, the observation attaches to it instead: `peak_breach_bps` becomes the greater of the case's existing peak and this observation's `breach_bps`, and the case's `severity` is then re-derived from that new peak using the same bands, so severity only ever ratchets upward. Either way, the observation's `parity_case_id` back-links to the case, and the case's `version` increments by 1. At most one non-terminal case exists per rate plan and travel date.

The mediation ladder is a negotiation, not a takedown. A `ParityAnalyst` notifies the supplier, optionally with a `note`, moving the case `OPEN → SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED` or `SUPPLIER_RESPONDED → SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED` and incrementing `notify_rounds` by 1. Notifying a case that is in neither of those two origins — one already `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED`, or one `ESCALATED` — reuses the `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_NOTIFIED` identity rather than introducing one of its own, and that state check is evaluated before the round cap, so an `ESCALATED` case that has also exhausted its rounds reports the state failure. Notification is bounded: it is available only while `notify_rounds` is still below three, and a fourth attempt is rejected with `ERR_PARITY_ROUND_LIMIT`. The owning `SupplierUser` answers, supplying a `supplier_response_code` of `RATE_CORRECTED`, `DISPUTED` or `NO_ACTION` and optionally a `note`; this stores the code and moves the case `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED → SUPPLIER_RESPONDED`. Responding to a case that is not `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED` is rejected with `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_NOTIFIED`, and the responding `X-User-ID` must be the supplier that owns the case's rate plan. The pair `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED → SUPPLIER_RESPONDED → SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED` is thus a bounded negotiation cycle. Any transition attempted on a case already sitting in `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED` is rejected with `ERR_PARITY_CASE_CLOSED`.

Escalation is the enforcement action. A `ParityAnalyst` or a `ComplianceOfficer` supplies an `escalation_reason` and the case moves `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED → ESCALATED` or `SUPPLIER_RESPONDED → ESCALATED`; a case in any other status is rejected with `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_ESCALATABLE`. Escalation additionally requires the case's `severity` to be `MAJOR` or `SEVERE` — a merely `MINOR` breach can be mediated but never escalated — and that severity condition reuses the same `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_ESCALATABLE` identity, evaluated after the status condition, so a `MINOR` case sitting in `OPEN` reports the status failure. On success `escalated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the cascade runs: the supplier's `parity_strikes` counter increments by 1, the case's rate plan moves `ACTIVE → PARITY_HOLD` with `parity_hold_case_id` set to this case, every `LIVE` offer under that plan moves to `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `PARITY_HOLD`, and those suppressions in turn withdraw every `PUBLISHED` package and ranking run that quoted them and return every `PRICED` such package to `DRAFT`. The `version` of every touched row — case, supplier, plan, offers, packages, runs — increments by 1. The hold hop is conditional on the plan actually being `ACTIVE`: escalating a second case against a plan already sitting on `PARITY_HOLD`, or against an `ARCHIVED` one, still moves the case to `ESCALATED`, still stamps `escalated_at` and still increments the supplier's `parity_strikes`, but leaves the plan's `status` and `parity_hold_case_id` exactly as they were and suppresses no offer.

Closing a case takes an `outcome` and optionally a `note`. The `COMPLIANT` outcome is available to both a `ParityAnalyst` and a `ComplianceOfficer` and may be applied from `OPEN`, from `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED`, from `SUPPLIER_RESPONDED` or from `ESCALATED`, moving the case to the terminal `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT`. The `WAIVED` outcome is narrower in every dimension: it is restricted to a `ComplianceOfficer` — a `ParityAnalyst` asking for it is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and because that role condition is read off the payload it is evaluated ahead of the case's own terminal and state conditions, so an analyst waiving an already-closed case still gets the 403. It requires an `X-User-ID` different from the case's `opened_by_user_id`, and it may only be applied from `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED`, moving the case to the terminal `WAIVED`; waiving from any other state is rejected with `ERR_PARITY_CASE_NOT_NOTIFIED` and waiving by the opener is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either outcome stores `close_outcome` and `closed_by_user_id` and increments the case's `version` by 1.

Closing an escalated case does not by itself lift the hold. Releasing a hold is a separate act by a `ParityAnalyst` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, optionally with a `note`, moving the plan `PARITY_HOLD → ACTIVE` and clearing `parity_hold_case_id`. It requires the plan to actually be in `PARITY_HOLD` and requires the case named by `parity_hold_case_id` to have reached `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT` or `WAIVED`; a plan that is not held, or one whose opening case is still open, is rejected with `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_ON_HOLD`. Release increments the plan's `version` by 1 and, as with reinstatement, republishes nothing: `ACTIVE → PARITY_HOLD → ACTIVE` is a cycle for the plan, but each suppressed offer must be published again individually.

### 3.6. Preferred Placement, Ranking Governance and Paid-Placement Disclosure

Suppliers can buy visibility, but only inside a capacity the marketplace controls. A `CommercialManager` proposes a **PlacementAgreement** naming a `supplier_id` and a `market_id`, reserving `slots` — an integer from 1 through 10 — on every day of a window, granting `boost_points` from 0 through 15 to be added to that supplier's organic ranking score, charging a flat `fee_cents` of zero or more for the window, and declaring the `disclosure_label` that any boosted row must carry, which is either `SPONSORED` or `PREFERRED_PARTNER`. Both identifiers are body references under priority 5 and are resolved in the order written here, so a payload naming a non-existent supplier and a non-existent market at once reports `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, and the supplier need not have its home market in the named market. The window is expressed as `window_start`, the first calendar date covered and inclusive, and `window_end`, which is **exclusive** — the window is the half-open range that begins at `window_start` and stops just before `window_end`. Like the override dates of §3.3, and unlike a model-agreement window, the two are never compared with each other, so an inverted or equal pair is accepted at proposal and simply covers no date at all — which also means it consumes no capacity and passes the approval test of the next paragraph vacuously. The proposal is created in `PROPOSED`, records `proposed_by_user_id`, and leaves `approved_by_user_id` and `cancellation_reason` null.

Only a `PlatformAdmin` may approve, optionally with a `note`. Approval requires the agreement to still be `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_PROPOSED`; requires the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from `proposed_by_user_id`; requires the supplier's `visibility_status` to be `ACTIVE`, rejecting a `PENDING_CERT`, `SUSPENDED` or `OFFBOARDED` supplier with the nearest catalogued identity, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422), rather than a placement-specific one — an `OFFBOARDED` supplier having already been caught by the earlier `ERR_SUPPLIER_OFFBOARDED` guard of §2.11; and requires paid-slot capacity to be available. The capacity test walks every calendar date from `window_start` up to but not including `window_end`, and for each such date sums the `slots` of every placement agreement in the same market whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose own half-open window covers that date. If that sum plus the candidate's own `slots` would exceed the market's `paid_slot_capacity` on any single date, approval is rejected with `ERR_PLACEMENT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The comparison is strictly greater-than, so filling the market's capacity exactly is permitted; only pushing past it fails. Agreements in `PROPOSED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` never consume capacity. Note the deliberate asymmetry with model-agreement windows: because placement windows are half-open, an agreement whose `window_start` equals another's `window_end` shares no date with it and never competes for a slot, whereas the closed intervals of §3.2 treat exactly that touching case as an overlap. On success the agreement moves `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, stores `approved_by_user_id`, and its `version` increments by 1.

Cancellation covers both ends of the lifecycle. A `CommercialManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` supplies a `cancellation_reason`, which is stored; a `PROPOSED` agreement moves to the terminal `REJECTED` and an `ACTIVE` one moves to the terminal `CANCELLED`, freeing its slots on every day of its window. Anything else is rejected with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_PROPOSED`, and the agreement's `version` increments by 1. Cancelling an `ACTIVE` agreement does not rewrite history: ranking rows already produced keep their `boost_points_applied` and their `disclosure_label` exactly as computed. A separate expiry sweep, described in §2.5, moves an `ACTIVE` agreement to the terminal `EXPIRED` once its `window_end` is at or before the new logical-clock date.

A **RankingRun** is a governed, reproducible result set. A `CommercialManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` builds one for a `market_id` and a `travel_date`, choosing a `sort_profile` of `BEST_VALUE` or `PRICE_ASC` and optionally attaching a `disclosure_version` naming the published ranking-criteria statement. As with offer ingestion, the `travel_date` must be on or after the current logical-clock date and no more than 500 days after it, otherwise `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The run is created in `DRAFT` with `published_at`, `published_by_user_id` and `withdrawal_reason` null.

Run construction begins with an eligibility filter that is a conjunction of six explicit conditions. A candidate offer must have `status` `LIVE`; its `travel_date` must equal the run's `travel_date`; its rate plan's `market_id` must equal the run's `market_id`; that plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; the plan's supplier must have `visibility_status` `ACTIVE`; and that supplier's `certification_level` must be `PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED`. Offers that are pending, suppressed, closed or superseded never enter the pool, and neither do offers of suspended or uncertified suppliers. If the resulting pool is empty, the whole run is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_OFFERS`.

Each surviving candidate becomes one immutable **RankingEntry** carrying its `ranking_run_id` and `offer_id`. Scoring proceeds in four movements. First, price competitiveness: the system takes the smallest `display_total_cents` across the whole candidate pool, and for each candidate computes one hundred multiplied by the quantity one minus the fraction whose numerator is that candidate's `display_total_cents` minus the pool minimum and whose denominator is the pool minimum, then rounds the result half up to 2 decimal places and stores it as `price_competitiveness`. The cheapest candidate therefore scores exactly 100.00 and dearer candidates score progressively lower, possibly below zero. Second, parity health: the system counts the parity cases on any rate plan of this candidate's supplier whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED`, `SUPPLIER_RESPONDED` or `ESCALATED`, multiplies that count by ten penalty points, subtracts the product from one hundred, and floors the result at zero so parity health is never negative. Third, the weighted organic score: `organic_score` is the sum of four weighted terms — the rounded `price_competitiveness` weighted at forty percent, the supplier's `content_score` weighted at twenty-five percent, the parity-health figure weighted at twenty percent, and the supplier's `conversion_index` weighted at fifteen percent — with the sum rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Note the rounding order: `price_competitiveness` is rounded to 2 decimal places **before** it is weighted.

Fourth, paid placement. The system gathers every placement agreement of this candidate's supplier in the run's market whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose half-open window covers the run's `travel_date` — that is, whose `window_start` is on or before the travel date and whose `window_end` is strictly after it. `boost_points_applied` is the sum of `boost_points` across all of those agreements, capped at fifteen points; the cap is on the total, so several concurrent deals cannot stack past it. The single winning agreement is the one with the highest `boost_points`, with the earliest `created_at` breaking a tie; when `boost_points_applied` is greater than zero the entry's `placement_agreement_id` is set to that winning agreement's `id`, `is_paid_placement` is true, and `disclosure_label` is copied from the winning agreement. When `boost_points_applied` is zero, `placement_agreement_id` is null, `is_paid_placement` is false, and `disclosure_label` is `ORGANIC`. Finally `final_score` is `organic_score` plus `boost_points_applied`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

The governed ordering is the same whichever `sort_profile` the run declared — the profile is recorded on the run for disclosure and never changes how entries are ordered or scored — and it sorts entries by `final_score` from highest to lowest; ties are broken by `organic_score` from highest to lowest; remaining ties are broken by the offer's `created_at` from earliest to latest. The row's `id` is never consulted. `rank_position` is the one-based index in that order. The run then records `entry_count` as the number of entries produced, `paid_entry_count` as the number of those with `is_paid_placement` true, and `weights_snapshot` as the four weights actually used, keyed `price`, `content`, `parity` and `conversion`.

Publication is a `ComplianceOfficer` act, optionally with a `note`, moving the run `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and stamping `published_at` from the logical clock and `published_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. A run that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_RUN_NOT_DRAFT`. Two further conditions apply, in this order: when the market's `ranking_disclosure_required` is true the run's `disclosure_version` must be present and non-empty — a run created with the field omitted, sent as `null`, or sent as the empty string that the optional-string rule of §2.10 admits all fail this check identically — otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`; and every entry with `is_paid_placement` true must reference a placement agreement whose `status` is still `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. That second condition is the reason a cancelled placement deal is fatal to an unpublished run even though it never rewrites the frozen entry. Publication increments the run's `version` by 1.

Withdrawal takes a `withdrawal_reason`, which is stored, and moves a run from `DRAFT` or from `PUBLISHED` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, incrementing its `version` by 1; a run in any other status is rejected with `ERR_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `CommercialManager` may withdraw, and the same transition is reached automatically when a ranked offer is suppressed. Ranked entries themselves are read-only artifacts and can be retrieved individually or as a list filtered by run, offer, `placement_agreement_id` or `is_paid_placement`.

### 3.7. Air-plus-Hotel Packages and Opaque Bundle Pricing

A **Package** bundles a flight and a stay for a single date into one merchandised product. A `CommercialManager` or a `BookingAgent` opens one for a `market_id` and a `travel_date` — the common date every component offer must carry — with a merchandising `name` and an `opaque` flag, the latter a required boolean that is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it is absent, `null` or of any other JSON type — it has no default. An opaque package withholds the component suppliers' identities from the traveller until after booking, which is what allows the bundle to be discounted below the sum of its publicly visible parts. The package is created in `DRAFT` with `component_count` at 0, `reveal_supplier_names` at false, `created_by_user_id` taken from the acting identity, and `components_subtotal_cents`, `discount_pct`, `discount_cents`, `package_total_cents` and `priced_at` all null. The `travel_date` is subject to the same horizon as offer ingestion: on or after the current logical-clock date and no more than 500 days after it, otherwise `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Legs are attached one at a time. A `CommercialManager` or a `BookingAgent` supplies an `offer_id` and a `component_type` of `AIR` or `HOTEL`, creating an immutable **PackageComponent** that records its `package_id`. The offer must currently be `LIVE`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_LIVE`; its `travel_date` must equal the package's `travel_date` and its rate plan's `market_id` must equal the package's `market_id`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DATE_MISMATCH`. Attachment snapshots the offer's `display_total_cents` into the component's `display_total_snapshot_cents`, increments the package's `component_count` by 1, and increments the package's `version` by 1. Attaching to a package that is neither `DRAFT` nor `PRICED` is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_DRAFT_OR_PRICED`. Attaching a leg to a package that had already been priced invalidates the price: the package moves `PRICED → DRAFT` and its `components_subtotal_cents`, `discount_pct`, `discount_cents`, `package_total_cents` and `priced_at` are cleared, and the identical clearing routine of §2.11 runs over its components, so the `display_total_snapshot_cents` of every component is nulled alongside its `discount_alloc_cents` and `allocated_cents` — including the leg just attached, whose freshly taken snapshot is nulled again by the same sweep, leaving every snapshot on the package null until the next pricing run refreshes them. `PRICED → DRAFT → PRICED` is therefore a cycle, traversed every time the component set changes.

Pricing is run by a `CommercialManager` or a `BookingAgent` and carries the `expected_package_version` token of §2.7. It is available from `DRAFT`, in which case the package moves `DRAFT → PRICED`, and from `PRICED`, in which case it stays at `PRICED` and the previous snapshots are simply overwritten; a package in any other status is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_PRICED`. Pricing requires at least two components including at least one `AIR` leg and at least one `HOTEL` leg, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_COMPONENT_MIX_INVALID`, and requires every component offer to still be `LIVE` with a `travel_date` equal to the package's, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_LIVE`.

The pricing arithmetic has a pinned rounding order, and getting the order wrong breaks a stored invariant rather than merely shifting a cent. Components are processed in ascending order of their offer's `created_at` — never in identifier order. Each component's `display_total_snapshot_cents` is refreshed from its offer's current `display_total_cents`, and `components_subtotal_cents` is the sum of those snapshots. The `discount_pct` is then chosen from four tiers by taking the highest tier whose threshold is less than or equal to the subtotal: a subtotal of at least 600,000 cents (six thousand currency units) earns twelve percent, at least 300,000 cents earns nine percent, at least 100,000 cents earns six percent, and anything below that earns zero percent. `discount_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by `discount_pct`, **rounded down** to a whole cent at the package level.

The package-level discount is then allocated across components by floor-plus-largest-remainder. For each component the system forms the exact product of that component's `display_total_snapshot_cents` and `discount_pct`, takes its whole-cent floor, and retains the fractional part that the floor discarded. It then computes the shortfall as the package-level `discount_cents` minus the sum of all the component floors; that shortfall is at least zero and strictly less than the number of components. One extra cent is handed to each of the components with the largest retained fractional parts, that many of them, with the earlier offer `created_at` winning any tie between equal fractions. Each component's `discount_alloc_cents` is its floor plus one if it received a remainder cent and its floor otherwise, and its `allocated_cents` is its `display_total_snapshot_cents` minus its `discount_alloc_cents`. Finally `package_total_cents` is `components_subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents`, and `priced_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Two invariants must close exactly: the sum of the components' `allocated_cents` equals `package_total_cents`, and the sum of their `discount_alloc_cents` equals `discount_cents`. Rounding each component's raw share half up, or prorating an already-rounded package total back over the components, breaks both invariants by one or more cents and is the wrong reading. Pricing increments the package's `version` by 1.

Publication is restricted to a `CommercialManager`, who may pass `reveal_supplier_names` and optionally a `note`. The package must be `PRICED`, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_PRICED`; it must still satisfy the two-leg mix condition, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_COMPONENT_MIX_INVALID`; and every component offer must still be `LIVE`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_LIVE`. The opacity rule is absolute: when the package's `opaque` flag is true, `reveal_supplier_names` must be false, and an attempt to publish an opaque package with supplier names revealed is rejected with `ERR_OPAQUE_DISCLOSURE_FORBIDDEN`. `reveal_supplier_names` defaults to false when the flag is omitted. On success the package moves `PRICED → PUBLISHED`, the supplied `reveal_supplier_names` is stored, and the `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal takes a `withdrawal_reason` and is available to a `CommercialManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, moving the package `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, a terminal status, resetting `reveal_supplier_names` back to false and incrementing the `version` by 1. A package that is not `PUBLISHED` is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The same transition arrives automatically when any component offer is suppressed, as described in §2.11.

### 3.8. Booking, Capture, Confirmation and Refund Pass-Through

A **Booking** is taken by a `BookingAgent` against exactly one target: either an `offer_id` naming a single `LIVE` offer, or a `package_id` naming a `PUBLISHED` package. Supplying neither, or supplying both, is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_TARGET_INVALID`; booking an offer that is not `LIVE` is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_LIVE` and booking a package that is not `PUBLISHED` with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The payload carries a `traveler_reference`, an opaque record locator supplied by the channel; a `quoted_total_cents`, the figure the channel actually displayed to the traveller; and a `payment_token` referencing the tokenised payment instrument. The quoted figure must equal the server's own current charged total — the offer's `display_total_cents` for a single-offer sale, or the package's `package_total_cents` for a package sale — and any discrepancy is rejected with `ERR_PRICE_CHANGED`, which is how a stale price on a channel's screen is prevented from becoming a booked liability. Both fields are body references under priority 5 and are resolved in the order written here, so a payload naming a non-existent offer and a non-existent package at once reports `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`. Every booked offer must also have `allotment_remaining` of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_ALLOTMENT_EXHAUSTED`. Beyond that allotment test a package sale does not re-examine its component offers' own statuses — only the package's `PUBLISHED` status is checked — so a leg closed out without suppression, an act that §3.4 says withdraws nothing, remains bookable through the bundle.

On creation the server writes the money decomposition it will carry for life: `charged_total_cents` from the offer's `display_total_cents` or the package's `package_total_cents`, and the three retained components `taxes_fees_cents`, `marketplace_margin_cents` and `supplier_payable_cents`. On a single-offer sale all three are copied straight from that offer's own decomposition. On a package sale `taxes_fees_cents` is the sum of the component offers' current `taxes_fees_cents` and `supplier_payable_cents` is the sum of their current `supplier_payable_cents`, both read live from the offers rather than from the components' frozen snapshots, and `marketplace_margin_cents` is the residual: `charged_total_cents` minus those two sums. The bundle discount of §3.7 is therefore absorbed entirely by the marketplace's margin, which is the only split under which the conserved identity stated later in this section closes exactly on a package booking as it does on a single offer. It sets `collection_model` from the pricing model of the booked inventory, which is `MERCHANT` when the governing agreement made the marketplace merchant of record and `AGENCY` when the supplier collects — those two values are the only ones the field ever takes. On a package sale the governing leg is the `HOTEL` component, the first of them in attachment order should there be more than one, and only when no `HOTEL` leg resolves at all does the first component in attachment order govern; that is the same governing leg the cancellation policy is read from below. It computes `arrival_at` as the booked inventory's `travel_date` at 15:00 UTC — the arrival hour is fixed at hour fifteen of the day. `refunded_cents`, `last_refund_clawback_cents`, `last_refund_supplier_debit_cents` and `last_refund_tax_cents` all start at 0, and `cancellation_refund_pct`, `cancelled_at` and `supplier_confirmation_code` start null. `booked_by_user_id` records the acting agent. On every booked offer, `allotment_remaining` decrements by 1 and `allotment_held` increments by 1, and each touched offer's `version` increments by 1.

A booking's state is two independent dimensions, and keeping them independent is the point. The money dimension, `payment_status`, starts at `AUTHORIZED` and moves among `CAPTURED`, `REFUNDED_PARTIAL`, `REFUNDED_FULL` and `CHARGEBACK`. The inventory dimension, `fulfillment_status`, starts at `RESERVED` and moves among `CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED`. A booking's initial combined state is `AUTHORIZED` together with `RESERVED`, and its terminal combinations are `REFUNDED_FULL` with `CANCELLED`, `CAPTURED` with `COMPLETED`, and `CHARGEBACK` with `COMPLETED`. Capture touches only the money side; confirmation touches only the inventory side.

A `BookingAgent` captures the authorised payment, optionally with a `note`, moving `payment_status` `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED` and leaving `fulfillment_status` untouched. Capture requires `fulfillment_status` not to be `CANCELLED`, otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_CANCELLED`, and that condition is tested first, so capturing an authorised booking whose stay has already been cancelled under a non-refundable policy reports the cancellation rather than a payment failure; it then requires `payment_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. The booking's `version` increments by 1. A `PlatformAdmin` may simulate the acquirer-initiated chargeback that no business actor can trigger, naming a `booking_id` and a `reason_code`: this moves `payment_status` `CAPTURED → CHARGEBACK` while leaving the fulfilment dimension exactly as it was, so a traveller whose stay is `CONFIRMED` or even `COMPLETED` may still be in chargeback. A booking whose `payment_status` is not `CAPTURED` is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED`, and the booking's `version` increments by 1.

The owning `SupplierUser` confirms the reservation by supplying a `supplier_confirmation_code`, which is stored, moving `fulfillment_status` `RESERVED → CONFIRMED` and leaving `payment_status` untouched. Confirmation requires `fulfillment_status` to be `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED`, requires `payment_status` to be `AUTHORIZED` or `CAPTURED`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the supplier that owns the booked inventory. On every booked offer, `allotment_held` decrements by 1 and `allotment_sold` increments by 1, so the sum of held, sold and remaining still equals `allotment_total`. The booking's `version` and the `version` of every touched offer each increment by 1. Later, the same `SupplierUser` marks the stay consumed, optionally with a `note`, moving `fulfillment_status` `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`; this requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the booking's `arrival_at` and then requires `payment_status` to be `CAPTURED`, `CHARGEBACK` or `REFUNDED_PARTIAL`, rejecting anything else — an uncaptured `AUTHORIZED` booking, or one already `REFUNDED_FULL` — with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED`. Completing a booking that is not `CONFIRMED`, or completing one before the clock reaches `arrival_at`, is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and those two conditions are both tested ahead of the payment condition.

Cancellation is where the cancellation policy of the rate plan is actually passed through to the traveller, and where the marketplace's own margin is put at risk alongside the supplier's payable. A `BookingAgent` cancels with a `cancellation_reason`, moving `fulfillment_status` `RESERVED → CANCELLED` or `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` additionally requires that `payment_status` is not `CHARGEBACK`. A booking already `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED`, or a `CONFIRMED` one in chargeback, is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_CANCELLED`; the chargeback condition attaches only to the `CONFIRMED` origin, so a booking still merely `RESERVED` cancels normally even when its payment has been charged back.

The refund percentage is decided by distance from arrival. The system computes the hours remaining as the interval from the current logical-clock instant to the booking's `arrival_at`, expressed in hours as an exact rational quantity rather than a truncated integer, and compares it against the cutoff, which is the `free_cancel_cutoff_hours` of the booked inventory's rate plan — for a package booking, the rate plan of the `HOTEL` component. Under a `FLEX` policy the refund is the whole amount when the hours remaining are at or above the cutoff, and half otherwise. Under a `MODERATE` policy the refund is the whole amount at or above the cutoff, half when it is below the cutoff but still at or above twenty-four hours, and nothing when it is below twenty-four hours. Under a `STRICT` policy the refund is half at or above the cutoff and nothing below it. Under `NON_REF` the refund is nothing regardless of timing. In every band the comparison against the cutoff is inclusive, so a cancellation landing exactly on the cutoff receives the more generous band. The chosen fraction is stored as `cancellation_refund_pct`, written as the plain decimal fraction the band selected — `1` for a whole refund, `0.5` for a half one and `0` for none — with a whole-valued fraction rendered as the integer rather than as `1.00`.

The money movement is then decomposed into three shares and one residual. The cash returned to the traveller, `refund_cents`, is the booking's `charged_total_cents` multiplied by the refund fraction, rounded half up to a whole cent, and it is added to `refunded_cents`. The margin clawback is the booking's current `marketplace_margin_cents` multiplied by the same fraction, rounded half up, and it is subtracted from `marketplace_margin_cents`. The supplier debit is the booking's current `supplier_payable_cents` multiplied by the same fraction, rounded half up, and it is subtracted from `supplier_payable_cents`. The tax portion is deliberately **not** rounded independently: it is the residual, computed as the cash returned minus the margin clawback minus the supplier debit, and it is subtracted from `taxes_fees_cents`. Making the tax share a residual is what keeps the conserved identity exact: before and after any cancellation, `charged_total_cents` equals `supplier_payable_cents` plus `marketplace_margin_cents` plus `taxes_fees_cents` plus `refunded_cents`. The three amounts are also recorded as `last_refund_clawback_cents`, `last_refund_supplier_debit_cents` and `last_refund_tax_cents` so that a later reversal can replay them exactly.

The payment dimension then follows the fraction: `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED_FULL` when the refund fraction is exactly one, becomes `REFUNDED_PARTIAL` when the fraction is above zero and below one, and is left exactly as it was when the fraction is zero — a non-refundable cancellation cancels the stay without touching the money. Inventory is returned in the same transaction: a booking cancelled from `RESERVED` has `allotment_held` decremented by 1, a booking cancelled from `CONFIRMED` has `allotment_sold` decremented by 1, and either way `allotment_remaining` increments by 1. Finally `cancelled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the booking's `version` and the `version` of every touched offer each increment by 1.

### 3.9. Dispute Mediation, Liability Allocation and Supplier Scorecards

When a refund is not passed through, a chargeback lands, or fraud is alleged, a **DisputeCase** is filed against the booking. A `ComplianceOfficer`, a `SupplierUser` or a `BookingAgent` may file, supplying a `dispute_type` of `REFUND_PASSTHROUGH`, `CHARGEBACK` or `FRAUD`, a `claim_cents` of at least 1, and a `narrative` recorded for audit. Filing a `CHARGEBACK` dispute requires the booking's `payment_status` to actually be `CHARGEBACK`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CHARGED_BACK`. There must also be something left to argue about: the booking's `charged_total_cents` minus its `refunded_cents` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_NOTHING_TO_DISPUTE`. The case resolves `supplier_id` from the booked inventory — from the same governing leg §3.8 names, so a package booking whose legs belong to different suppliers denormalises the `HOTEL` leg's supplier even though any leg's `SupplierUser` was entitled to file, and it is that one supplier the case is later scored against and the one whose `X-User-ID` may answer the notice — and denormalises it onto the row for later scorecard rollups, records `booking_id` and `filed_by_user_id`, starts in `FILED` with `mediation_rounds` at 0, and leaves `supplier_position`, `outcome` and `resolved_by_user_id` null.

The settlement total is computed at filing in four pinned steps, because the fee is otherwise circular — the total contains the fee, and the fee rate is chosen from a tier. Step one: `refund_due_cents` is the smaller of `claim_cents` and the booking's `charged_total_cents` minus its `refunded_cents`, so a claim can never exceed what is still at stake. Step two: `mediation_fee_pct` is the highest of three tiers whose threshold is less than or equal to the step-one figure — a `refund_due_cents` of at least 200,000 cents selects five percent, at least 50,000 cents selects three and a half percent, and anything below that selects two percent. The tier is keyed on `refund_due_cents` and never on the settlement total; keying it on a total that already contains the fee can select a higher tier and produce a different, order-dependent answer. Step three: `mediation_fee_cents` is a flat base of 2,500 cents plus the product of `refund_due_cents` and `mediation_fee_pct` rounded half up to a whole cent. Step four: `settlement_total_cents` is `refund_due_cents` plus `mediation_fee_cents`. No other case participates in this arithmetic.

Liability over that settlement total is allocated twice, from two different agreements, and the difference between the two is the whole point of the design. The **filing agreement** is the model agreement of the booked supplier with status `ACTIVE` whose window covers the logical-clock **date at filing**; if no `ACTIVE` agreement covers that date, the most recently activated `ACTIVE` agreement is used instead, choosing by latest `activated_at` and breaking a tie by earliest `created_at`. Its identifier is stored as `filing_agreement_id`. The **binding agreement** is the model agreement of the booked supplier whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED` and whose window covers the **booked travel date** — the `travel_date` of the booked offer for a single-offer sale and the package's own `travel_date` for a package sale; because windows are disjoint at most one matches, and when none matches the filing agreement is used. Its identifier is stored as `binding_agreement_id`. Both identifiers are resolved and written at filing time.

The preliminary split is stamped at filing and is a forward-looking estimate. `preliminary_marketplace_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` multiplied by the filing agreement's `chargeback_liability_pct_marketplace`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `preliminary_supplier_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` minus that figure. The preliminary variant ignores the dispute type entirely — no fraud cap is applied — and ignores any outcome, because none has been decided. Where no filing agreement resolves at all, because the booked supplier could not be determined or has no `ACTIVE` agreement of any kind, the marketplace's share is treated as zero percent, so `preliminary_marketplace_cents` is 0 and `preliminary_supplier_cents` carries the whole settlement total.

Mediation runs as a bounded exchange. A `ComplianceOfficer` puts the supplier on notice, optionally with a `note`, moving the case `FILED → RESPONSE_REQUESTED` or `MEDIATION → RESPONSE_REQUESTED` and incrementing `mediation_rounds` by 1; it is available only while `mediation_rounds` is below three, and a fourth attempt is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT`. Requesting a response on a case that is neither `FILED` nor `MEDIATION` is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE`. The owning `SupplierUser` answers with a `supplier_position`, which is stored, moving the case `RESPONSE_REQUESTED → MEDIATION`; answering from any other status is likewise rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE`, and the responding `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `supplier_id`. `RESPONSE_REQUESTED → MEDIATION → RESPONSE_REQUESTED` is the mediation cycle, bounded at three rounds. Any transition attempted on a case already `RESOLVED` is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.

Resolution belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` differs from `filed_by_user_id`, and it may be reached from `FILED`, from `RESPONSE_REQUESTED` or from `MEDIATION`, moving the case to the terminal `RESOLVED`. The request supplies an `outcome` of `SUPPLIER_LIABLE`, `MARKETPLACE_LIABLE`, `SPLIT` or `REVERSE_REFUND`, and optionally a `note`. The binding split is then computed against the **binding** agreement. The effective marketplace share is zero percent for `SUPPLIER_LIABLE`, one hundred percent for `MARKETPLACE_LIABLE`, the binding agreement's own `chargeback_liability_pct_marketplace` for `SPLIT`, and zero percent for `REVERSE_REFUND`. `uncapped_marketplace_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` multiplied by that effective share, rounded half up to a whole cent. Then, and only when `dispute_type` is `FRAUD`, the cap bites: `binding_marketplace_cents` is the smaller of `uncapped_marketplace_cents` and the binding agreement's `fraud_liability_cap_cents`. For every other dispute type `binding_marketplace_cents` equals `uncapped_marketplace_cents`. The cap is applied after rounding, never before, and it clamps only the marketplace share; when no binding agreement resolved at filing there is no cap to apply, so a `FRAUD` case is left uncapped, and a `SPLIT` outcome then reads its effective share as zero percent. `binding_supplier_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` minus `binding_marketplace_cents`. Resolution stores `outcome` and `resolved_by_user_id` and increments the case's `version` by 1. Because the two variants read different agreements, the same settlement total can split differently at filing and at resolution — that divergence is expected and both stamps are retained.

The `REVERSE_REFUND` outcome additionally unwinds the booking's refund as a compensating action. It requires the booking's `payment_status` to be `REFUNDED_PARTIAL` or `REFUNDED_FULL` and its `refunded_cents` to be greater than zero; a booking with nothing refunded to reverse is rejected with the nearest catalogued identity, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED` (HTTP 409), rather than an outcome-specific one, and that condition is evaluated after the case's own state precondition and after the segregation-of-duties comparison, so it is the last thing resolution checks. The booking's `payment_status` moves `REFUNDED_PARTIAL → CAPTURED` or `REFUNDED_FULL → CAPTURED`; `refunded_cents` returns to 0; `last_refund_clawback_cents` is added back to `marketplace_margin_cents`, `last_refund_supplier_debit_cents` is added back to `supplier_payable_cents`, and `last_refund_tax_cents` is added back to `taxes_fees_cents`; and only then are all three of those last-refund fields reset to 0. Replaying the stored amounts rather than recomputing them is what makes the reversal exact. The booking's `version` increments by 1.

The **SupplierScorecard** rolls all of this up into one number per period. A `CommercialManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` opens one beneath a supplier with a `period_start` and a `period_end`, both `YYYY-MM-DD` and both inclusive; the two are never compared with each other, so an inverted pair is accepted and simply admits no row into either population, and neither date is constrained relative to the logical clock. It is created with `variant` at `PROVISIONAL` and `finalized_by_user_id` null, and the composite is computed immediately in the provisional variant.

Scoring works from two populations. The parity population is every parity case whose rate plan belongs to this supplier and whose `created_at` date falls inside the inclusive period. Each counted case carries a severity weight: one point for `MINOR`, two for `MAJOR`, four for `SEVERE`. Under the **provisional** variant every in-period case counts at its current severity weight, and any case still sitting in a non-terminal status — `OPEN`, `SUPPLIER_NOTIFIED` or `SUPPLIER_RESPONDED` — adds one further projected escalation point on top of its weight, because it may yet escalate. Under the **final** variant only cases whose status is `RESOLVED_COMPLIANT`, `WAIVED` or `ESCALATED` count at all; a `WAIVED` case counts zero points; and cases still non-terminal are excluded entirely and roll into the next period. The total is stored as `parity_penalty_points`, and `parity_component` is one hundred minus eight points of penalty per point accumulated, floored at zero so it is never negative. `cases_counted` records how many parity cases the active variant actually counted.

The dispute population is every dispute case whose `supplier_id` is this supplier and whose `created_at` date falls inside the period. The provisional variant counts all of them and values the supplier's exposure at `preliminary_supplier_cents`; the final variant counts only those whose `status` is `RESOLVED` and values the exposure at `binding_supplier_cents`. The gross disputed amount is the sum of `settlement_total_cents` over the counted disputes and the supplier-liable amount is the sum of the per-variant supplier share. `dispute_component` is one hundred minus the supplier-liable percentage of the gross disputed amount, where that percentage is one hundred multiplied by the supplier-liable sum divided by the gross disputed sum, rounded half up, then capped at one hundred; when the gross disputed sum is zero the divisor is treated as one, so a supplier with no disputes scores a full one hundred on this component.

The remaining two components are read straight off the supplier at computation time: `content_component` is the supplier's `content_score` and `connectivity_component` is its `connectivity_score`. The `composite_score` is the weighted sum of the four components — the parity component at thirty percent, the content component at twenty-five percent, the connectivity component at twenty percent and the dispute component at twenty-five percent — with each component computed at full precision first and the single weighted sum then rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Finalisation is a `ComplianceOfficer` act, optionally with a `note`. It requires the scorecard's `variant` to still be `PROVISIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_FINAL`, and requires the logical-clock date to be strictly after `period_end`, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` — a period that is merely ending today is not yet closed. Finalisation moves the scorecard `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`, re-runs the composite under the final variant and overwrites every computed field it produces — `parity_penalty_points`, `parity_component`, `dispute_component`, `composite_score`, `cases_counted` and also `content_component` and `connectivity_component`, which are re-read from the supplier as it stands at finalisation and so can differ from the figures the provisional pass stored — stores `finalized_by_user_id`, and increments the scorecard's `version` by 1. Because the two variants differ only in which rows they admit, the same period routinely scores differently before and after closure, and both figures are meaningful.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a brand-new resource into existence — registering a market, onboarding a supplier, drafting an agreement, submitting a certification, creating a rate plan, ingesting an offer, posting a parity observation, proposing an override or a placement deal, building a ranking run, opening a package, attaching a component, taking a booking, filing a dispute or opening a scorecard — return `201 Created`. Every operation that modifies an existing resource, including every state transition, every pricing re-run and both test-driving capabilities, returns `200 OK`. Read operations return `200 OK`. Error responses carry the status code listed against their identity in §5. The two endpoints in the `/_` namespace exist to make time and acquirer behaviour deterministic for testing and are restricted to `PlatformAdmin`. The clock endpoint returns an empty `data` object, while the chargeback endpoint returns the updated booking. The chargeback simulation resolves its `booking_id` reference as a body reference under priority 5, requires a `reason_code` string that it validates but does not persist, and requires the booking's `payment_status` to be `CAPTURED`, rejecting anything else with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED` (HTTP 409); it moves `payment_status` to `CHARGEBACK`, leaves the fulfilment dimension untouched and increments the booking's `version` by 1.
