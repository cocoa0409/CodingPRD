# Public Access TV Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A community media center is a small organisation carrying an unusually heavy compliance load. It must admit residents as certified producers, teach them to operate broadcast equipment safely, accept whatever programming they submit while refusing only the narrow set of content categories the law leaves unprotected, hand out scarce airtime on assigned cable channels without favouring one voice over another, lend expensive cameras and edit suites against modest deposits, staff live broadcasts with unpaid volunteers, and account to the municipality for every cent of franchise fee revenue it receives. Today all of this is tracked in a mixture of spreadsheets, paper indemnification forms and a whiteboard schedule, and the seams show: a producer whose certification lapsed still walks out with a field kit, two shows are promised the same 8 p.m. Tuesday, and the quarterly franchise-fee report is restated twice because two staff members were editing the same figures from different snapshots.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those artefacts. The service is deliberately narrow in scope — it is a system of record and a rules engine, not a playout automation system or a video transcoder. What it must get exactly right is the *bookkeeping of obligations*: which competencies a producer actually holds, which attestations are currently in force, which series inherits seniority from which predecessor, how much of a deposit may lawfully be forfeited, and how much of a franchise fee is capital versus operating money. Every one of those questions has a single defensible answer, and the service is the authority for it.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, **time is explicit**: the service never reads the wall clock. It carries a logical UTC instant that an operator sets, so that session windows, checkout lead times, airtime, indemnification terms and retention deadlines are all reproducible. Second, **editorial neutrality is structural, not aspirational**: a reviewer may refuse a program only by citing one of the enumerated legally unprotected categories, may never annotate an approval with a finding, and may never decide a review that another reviewer claimed. Where a decision carries money or a sanction, the person who created the record and the person who blesses it are required to be different people.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Studio Staff, Producers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control operation must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. When either header is missing or empty on an operation that requires it, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), and this check precedes every other check in the system — an unauthenticated call naming a non-existent resource still returns 401, never 404. When both headers are present but the named role is not one of the roles the target operation permits, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Role authorisation outranks payload validation: a wrong role submitting a malformed body receives 403, not 400.

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `Producer`, `TrainingCoordinator`, `ContentReviewer`, `SchedulingCoordinator`, `FacilitiesManager`, `FinanceOfficer`, and `CenterAdmin`.

`Producer` is the only entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `Producer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Producer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Producer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a producer may only enroll themselves, withdraw their own enrollment, propose a series they own, create and attest and submit and withdraw their own program, request and cancel their own reservation, and confirm or release a crew assignment whose volunteer is themselves. Where the acting `X-User-ID` is not the `producer_id` (or, for crew work, the `volunteer_producer_id`) that owns the target record, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Staff roles are exempt from this ownership rule; it constrains only `Producer`.

The remaining six roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `TrainingCoordinator`, `ContentReviewer`, `SchedulingCoordinator`, `FacilitiesManager`, `FinanceOfficer`, or `CenterAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a program claimed by a `ContentReviewer`, as `filed_by_user_id` on a damage assessment opened by a `FacilitiesManager`, as `waived_by_user_id` on an assessment waived by a `FinanceOfficer`, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a funding period reported by a `FinanceOfficer`, or as `decided_by_user_id` on a funding period decided by a `CenterAdmin` — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

Duties are segregated where money or sanction is at stake. A `FinanceOfficer` may not waive a damage assessment whose `filed_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and a `CenterAdmin` may not accept or return a funding period whose `submitted_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; either attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A parallel but differently-shaped rule governs review: the `ContentReviewer` who claims a program is the only one who may decide it, and a decision from any other reviewer is refused as an impermissible state transition rather than as a duty conflict.

The logical-clock control operation is the single exception to header handling: it accepts any of the seven roles and does not require authentication headers at all, because test harnesses and operators need to position the clock before any membership exists.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Collection reads are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows share a `created_at`, the entities that carry an explicit monotone counter (`sequence_number` on a series, an attestation or a playback slot) resolve the order deterministically; record identifiers are never used as an ordering key anywhere in this system.

Every list response reports `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. The four pagination fields live in `meta`, never in `data`, and they are absent from single-resource reads.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Clients never supply either.

Most resources also carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the caller did not directly request, such as a cascade that cancels a reservation or releases a crew assignment. The `Attestation` resource is the exception: attestations are an append-only legal log, never edited after they are written, so an attestation carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither is ever emitted for it. Where a later attestation supersedes an earlier one, the earlier row is closed by stamping its `effective_until` rather than being rewritten in place — the closing stamp is the one exception to attestation immutability and it does not create a `version`.

The `version` integer is not decorative. It is the value a client must echo back on the two operations that use optimistic concurrency control (§2.7), and it is the observable evidence that a cascade touched a row the caller was not looking at.

### 2.5. The Logical Broadcast Clock

The service holds a single logical current UTC instant, and every temporal comparison in this specification reads it: whether a training session has ended, whether a withdrawal window is still open, whether indemnification is still in force, whether a checkout is inside its lead window, whether a slot's airtime has arrived, and whether an archive asset's retention window has elapsed. Before any operator sets it, that instant is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

An operator in any of the seven roles may reposition the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, the new instant in ISO 8601 form; the response reports the instant now in force as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock never retroactively rewrites stored timestamps — an attestation's `effective_from`, a slot's `aired_at`, an asset's `published_at` and a reservation's `checked_out_at` are all frozen at the instant that was current when they were stamped.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Money is stored and transmitted exclusively as integer cents. No monetary field in this system is a decimal or a floating-point number, and no monetary field is ever negative.

Rates are expressed in basis points — hundredths of a percent — so that every intermediate calculation stays in integer arithmetic. Wherever a basis-point rate is applied, the rule is: multiply the cent basis by the basis-point figure, divide by ten thousand, and round the quotient half up to a whole cent. Where a calculation is built from several independently rated lines, **each line is rounded to a whole cent before the lines are summed**; summing the basis points first and rounding once is explicitly not the rule, and the two approaches differ by a cent on realistic inputs. Where a per-unit fee is multiplied by a unit count, the per-unit fee is rounded once and the product is then taken exactly; the product itself is never rounded again.

Exactly one figure in the system is not an integer: `community_impact_index` is a JSON number carried to two fraction digits, rounded half up. Timestamps computed by the service — a slot's `end_at`, an attestation's `effective_until`, an asset's `retain_until` — are ISO 8601 UTC instants at second precision, and no rounding is applied to them.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Hold Plans and Fiscal Restatements

Two operations in this system legitimately supersede their own prior result, so neither can be refused on state grounds alone. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent row's `version`.

Replacing a series' hold plan requires `expected_series_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Series`. A hold plan replaces whatever plan the series previously carried, so a second plan against the same series is legitimate business behaviour; what is not legitimate is authoring a plan against a series state the client has not seen. If `expected_series_version` differs from the series' current stored `version` — because the series has since been activated, lapsed, re-planned, or had hold slots consumed by scheduling — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the client is expected to re-read the series and try again.

Restating a funding period's franchise-fee figures requires `expected_funding_period_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `FundingPeriod`. Restating twice is ordinary municipal practice, so the second restatement is not an error; it is an error only when the officer's figures were derived from a view of the period that a prior restatement or a report decision has already advanced. If `expected_funding_period_version` differs from the period's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUNDING_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other operation in this system takes a version field, and no other operation may be refused for a version mismatch. In particular, program review decisions, reservation confirmations, slot bookings and archive publication carry no expected-version input; their safety comes from state guards, not from concurrency tokens.

### 2.8. Refusal Order: Which Rule Reports the Error

Any single request may violate several rules at once. The service evaluates rules in a strict ascending priority order and reports **only the first violation it finds**; later violations on the same request are invisible. Forty-two rules occupy twelve priority bands, and the ordering below is normative.

1. **Authentication (priority 1)** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every operation except the logical-clock control operation.
2. **Role authorisation (priority 2)** — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403. Applies to every operation.
3. **Request schema (priority 3)** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every operation with a request body or query parameters. A required input field is missing or null; a field carries the wrong JSON type; an enum field carries a value outside its declared set; an integer field is negative where non-negative is required; a reservation's `end_at` is at or before its `start_at`; a course's `session_end_at` is at or before its `session_start_at`; a channel's `daily_window_end_minute` is at or below its `daily_window_start_minute`; `practical_score` falls outside 0 through 100 inclusive; `measured_households` is negative; `quarter` falls outside 1 through 4 inclusive; or a pagination parameter is out of the range stated in §2.3.
4. **Path existence (priority 4)** — the addressed entity's own not-found error, 404. Applies to every operation with a path parameter. Path identifiers are resolved left to right and the leftmost missing segment decides which error is reported.
5. **Body foreign-key existence (priority 5)** — the referenced entity's own not-found error, 404. Applies to enrollment creation, series creation, program creation, playback-slot creation, reservation creation and crew-assignment creation. Body foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are listed for that operation in §4.
6. **Ownership (priority 6)** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403. Applies to enrollment creation, enrollment withdrawal, program creation, attestation append, program submit, program withdraw, reservation creation, reservation cancellation, crew-assignment confirm and crew-assignment release.
7. **Segregation of duties (priority 7)** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to damage-assessment waiver and funding-period report decision.
8. **Optimistic concurrency (priority 8)** — `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FUNDING_PERIOD_VERSION`, 412. Applies to hold-plan replacement and funding-period restatement. It is evaluated before any state check, so a caller holding an outdated view is told to re-read rather than being told the state is wrong.
9. **Transition legality (priority 9)** — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409. Applies to every action operation that drives a state machine. Raised when the target's current state has no transition for that trigger, including any attempt from a terminal state and any second application of a one-shot action.
10. **Owning producer active (priority 10)** — `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. Applies to enrollment creation, series activation, program submit, reservation confirmation, reservation checkout and crew-assignment confirm.
11. **Program approved for scheduling (priority 11)** — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED`, 409. Applies to playback-slot creation, and it runs before any window or conflict check on the same request.
12. **Program clearance complete (priority 12)** — `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE`, 409. Applies to program submit and playback-slot creation.
13. **Series active (priority 13)** — `ERR_SERIES_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. Applies to playback-slot creation when a `series_id` is supplied, and to hold-plan replacement.
14. **Resource available (priority 14)** — `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE`, 409. Applies to reservation confirmation.
15. **Required competency held (priority 15)** — `ERR_COMPETENCY_NOT_HELD`, 422. Applies to reservation confirmation.
16. **Live certification sufficient (priority 16)** — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`, 422. Applies to program creation when `is_live` is true, and to crew-assignment confirm.
17. **Course tier prerequisite (priority 17)** — `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`, 422. Applies to enrollment creation.
18. **Seats available (priority 18)** — `ERR_COURSE_FULL`, 409. Applies to enrollment creation.
19. **Slot staffed before air (priority 19)** — `ERR_SLOT_UNDERSTAFFED`, 409. Applies to slot airing.
20. **Resource has no open reservations (priority 20)** — `ERR_RESOURCE_HAS_OPEN_RESERVATIONS`, 409. Applies to a resource status change whose target `status` is `RETIRED`.
21. **Finding category permitted (priority 21)** — `ERR_FINDING_CATEGORY_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422. Applies to a program review decision.
22. **Revision rounds remaining (priority 22)** — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to program submit.
23. **Session ended before attendance (priority 23)** — `ERR_COURSE_SESSION_NOT_ENDED`, 409. Applies to attendance recording.
24. **Withdrawal window open (priority 24)** — `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, 409. Applies to enrollment withdrawal.
25. **Indemnification in force (priority 25)** — `ERR_INDEMNIFICATION_EXPIRED`, 422. Applies to program submit.
26. **Slot inside channel window (priority 26)** — `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_CHANNEL_WINDOW`, 422. Applies to playback-slot creation.
27. **Airtime free or preemptible (priority 27)** — `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to playback-slot creation.
28. **Series hold slots remaining (priority 28)** — `ERR_SERIES_HOLD_EXHAUSTED`, 409. Applies to playback-slot creation when a `series_id` is supplied.
29. **Resource capacity and turnaround (priority 29)** — `ERR_RESOURCE_OVERBOOKED`, 409. Applies to reservation confirmation.
30. **Checkout window open (priority 30)** — `ERR_CHECKOUT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, 409. Applies to reservation checkout.
31. **Airtime reached (priority 31)** — `ERR_AIRTIME_NOT_REACHED`, 409. Applies to slot airing.
32. **Retention elapsed (priority 32)** — `ERR_PERMANENT_RETENTION` or `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_ELAPSED`, both 409. Applies to archive purge.
33. **Volunteer not double-booked (priority 33)** — `ERR_CREW_TIME_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to crew-assignment confirm.
34. **Series owned by the program's producer (priority 34)** — `ERR_SERIES_NOT_OWNED_BY_PRODUCER`, 422. Applies to program creation and playback-slot creation.
35. **Channel tier eligibility (priority 35)** — `ERR_CHANNEL_TIER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 422. Applies to playback-slot creation.
36. **Linked slot belongs to the reserving producer (priority 36)** — `ERR_SLOT_PRODUCER_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to reservation creation.
37. **No duplicate active enrollment (priority 37)** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`, 409. Applies to enrollment creation.
38. **Natural key uniqueness (priority 38)** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`, 409. Applies to course creation, channel creation, studio-resource creation and funding-period creation.
39. **Licence reference present (priority 39)** — `ERR_LICENSE_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to attestation append.
40. **Producer deposit cap (priority 40)** — `ERR_DEPOSIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 402. Applies to reservation confirmation.
41. **Allocable base positive (priority 41)** — `ERR_ALLOCABLE_BASE_NOT_POSITIVE`, 402. Applies to funding-period report submission.
42. **Capital grant within base (priority 42)** — `ERR_CAPITAL_GRANT_EXCEEDS_BASE`, 402. Applies to funding-period creation and funding-period restatement.

Two orderings deserve particular attention because they are easy to get backwards. On reservation confirmation, the resource-state check at priority 14 outranks the fiscal deposit-cap check at priority 40: when a resource has been moved into `MAINTENANCE` **and** the producer's held deposits would breach the cap, the reported error is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409), not `ERR_DEPOSIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. On crew-assignment confirm, the certification check at priority 16 outranks the overlap check at priority 33: offering a `DIRECTOR` role on a live slot to a volunteer who is not `LIVE`-certified **and** who already holds a confirmed assignment on an overlapping slot yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422), not `ERR_CREW_TIME_CONFLICT`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Counter Reconciliation

A great many fields in this system are derived rather than supplied, and the specification treats them as invariants that must hold after every operation, not as counters that happen to be incremented in the happy path. When a cascade reaches a row, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes exactly as if it had been mutated directly.

The derived quantities and their defining sums are as follows. A producer's `deposit_held_cents` is the sum of `deposit_hold_cents` over that producer's reservations whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_OUT` or `DAMAGE_REVIEW`. A producer's `deposit_forfeited_total_cents` is the lifetime sum of `deposit_forfeited_cents` over that producer's `SETTLED` reservations. A producer's `volunteer_hours_tenths` is the sum of `credited_hours_tenths` over that producer's `COMPLETED` crew assignments, in tenths of an hour as a whole number. A producer's `confirmed_crew_commitments` is the count of that producer's crew assignments whose status is `CONFIRMED`. A producer's `suspension_points` is the running penalty total contributed by `FINALIZED` damage assessments, less any credit granted at reinstatement. A studio resource's `open_reservation_count` is the count of its reservations whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_OUT` or `DAMAGE_REVIEW`. A channel's `scheduled_seconds` is the sum of `occupied_seconds` over its `SCHEDULED` slots and its `aired_seconds` is the same sum over its `AIRED` slots. A course's `enrolled_count` is the count of its enrollments whose status is `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED` — a withdrawal releases the seat, a failure does not — and its `passed_count` counts only `PASSED` enrollments.

Cascades in this system are always synchronous and always complete before the response is written; there is no eventual consistency and no background reconciliation job. When a producer is suspended or revoked, when a program is withdrawn or rejected, when a slot is cancelled, withdrawn or preempted, or when a damage assessment is finalised, every downstream row named in §3 has already moved by the time the caller sees 200.

### 2.10. Path Resolution for Addressed Resources

Collections in this system are flat: producers, training courses, course enrollments, series, programs, attestations, channels, playback slots, studio resources, reservations, damage assessments, crew assignments, funding periods and archive assets each live at a top-level collection, and relationships between them are expressed by identifier fields in payloads rather than by nesting. The two exceptions are reverse-lookup style writes — appending an attestation to a program and replacing a hold plan on a series — which are addressed underneath their parent.

Where an address contains more than one identifier segment, segments are resolved strictly left to right and the leftmost segment that names no row decides the reported 404. Appending an attestation to a program whose identifier names no program reports `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` regardless of anything else in the payload.

### 2.11. Enumerated Vocabularies

The following vocabularies are closed sets; a value outside the set is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Producer membership status is `APPLICANT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`. Producer certification is `NONE`, `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` or `LIVE`. Curriculum tier is `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` or `LIVE`. Equipment competency codes are `STUDIO_FLOOR`, `EDIT_SUITE`, `ENG_CAMERA`, `AUDIO_BOARD` and `LIVE_SWITCHER`. Enrollment status is `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED`, `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Series status is `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` or `RETIRED`, and series cadence is `WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY` or `MONTHLY`. Program review status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and program clearance status is `INCOMPLETE` or `CLEARED`. Attestation kinds are `INDEMNIFICATION`, `COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE` and `CONTENT_POLICY`. Channel tier is `PUBLIC`, `EDUCATIONAL` or `GOVERNMENT`. Playback slot status is `SCHEDULED`, `AIRED`, `CANCELLED`, `PREEMPTED` or `WITHDRAWN`; booking type is `FIRST_COME` or `SERIES_HOLD`; staffing status is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `UNDERSTAFFED` or `READY`. Crew roles are `DIRECTOR`, `CAMERA`, `AUDIO`, `SWITCHER` and `FLOOR_MANAGER`, and crew assignment status is `OFFERED`, `CONFIRMED`, `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED`. Studio resource class is `STUDIO_A`, `FIELD_CAMERA_KIT`, `AUDIO_KIT` or `EDIT_SUITE`, and resource status is `AVAILABLE`, `MAINTENANCE` or `RETIRED`. Reservation status is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_OUT`, `DAMAGE_REVIEW`, `SETTLED` or `CANCELLED`. Damage severity is `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `TOTAL_LOSS`, and assessment status is `FILED`, `FINALIZED` or `WAIVED`. Funding basis is `ACCRUAL` or `CASH`, and municipal report status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. Archive retention class is `STANDARD_24M`, `EXTENDED_60M` or `PERMANENT`; on-demand status is `PENDING`, `PUBLISHED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `PURGED`; and the impact variant marker is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`.

One vocabulary is reserved for editorial findings and is discussed again in §3.3. The legally unprotected finding categories a reviewer may cite are exactly `OBSCENITY`, `DEFAMATION`, `TRUE_THREAT`, `INCITEMENT_TO_IMMINENT_LAWLESS_ACTION`, `CHILD_EXPLOITATION`, `COMMERCIAL_ADVERTISING`, `COPYRIGHT_INFRINGEMENT` and `TECHNICAL_STANDARDS_FAILURE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Membership Admission and the Certification Curriculum

Membership begins with a registration performed by a `CenterAdmin`, who records the applicant's `legal_name` and their `email`. The e-mail address is a natural key: no two producers may share one, and a second registration on the same address is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (409). A freshly registered producer sits in `APPLICANT` status with `residency_verified` false, `certification_level` `NONE`, an empty `competency_codes` list, `suspension_points` at 0, `reinstated_count` at 0, a null `indemnification_expires_at`, a null `revocation_reason`, and every derived money and volunteer counter — `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_total_cents`, `volunteer_hours_tenths` and `confirmed_crew_commitments` — at 0. Producers may be listed with optional `status` and `certification_level` filters.

Admission to membership is the `APPLICANT → ACTIVE` transition, and only a `CenterAdmin` may drive it, by recording the residency document as `document_reference`. The transition is available only while the producer is in `APPLICANT` status *and* `residency_verified` is still false; the pair of conditions together make this a one-shot action, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). On success `residency_verified` becomes true, `certification_level` is re-derived, and the producer's `version` increments by 1. Re-derivation at this point leaves the level at `NONE`, because admission is not training: nothing about a residency document grants equipment competency.

Competency comes from the curriculum, which a `TrainingCoordinator` publishes. A course carries a `course_code` — a catalog key unique across all courses, whose reuse is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` — plus a `title`, a `tier` of `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` or `LIVE`, a non-empty `grants_competency_codes` list drawn from the five equipment competency codes, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1, and a session window given by `session_start_at` and `session_end_at`, where the end must be strictly after the start. A published course opens with `enrolled_count` and `passed_count` both at 0. Courses may be listed with an optional `tier` filter.

Enrolling consumes a seat. Either the producer themselves or a `TrainingCoordinator` may create an enrollment by supplying `producer_id` and `course_id`; when the acting role is `Producer` the `producer_id` must be the acting user's own. Four business rules guard enrollment beyond the ownership check, and they are evaluated in this order: the owning producer must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); the tier prerequisite must be satisfied, otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (422); a seat must remain, otherwise `ERR_COURSE_FULL` (409); and the producer must not already hold a live enrollment on the same course, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409). The tier prerequisite reads: an `ADVANCED` course requires that the producer already holds a `PASSED` enrollment on some `BASIC` course, and a `LIVE` course requires a `PASSED` enrollment on some `ADVANCED` course; a `BASIC` course has no prerequisite. "Already holds a live enrollment" means an existing enrollment on that same course whose status is `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED` or `PASSED` — a `FAILED` or `WITHDRAWN` enrollment does not block re-enrolment. A successful enrollment is created in `ENROLLED` status with a null `practical_score`, a null `attended_at` and a null `graded_at`, and it raises the course's `enrolled_count` by 1. Enrollments may be listed filtered by `producer_id`, `course_id` or `status`.

Withdrawal is the producer's escape hatch, available to the enrollee or to a `TrainingCoordinator`, and it is bounded by time: the `ENROLLED → WITHDRAWN` transition is permitted only while the logical clock is strictly before the course's `session_start_at`. At or after the session's start instant the request is refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). A withdrawal releases the seat, lowering `enrolled_count` by 1.

Once the session has finished, a `TrainingCoordinator` records attendance by supplying the boolean `attended`. The session must genuinely be over — the logical clock must have reached or passed `session_end_at`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_COURSE_SESSION_NOT_ENDED` (409). When `attended` is true the enrollment moves `ENROLLED → ATTENDED` and `attended_at` is stamped with the current logical instant. When `attended` is false the enrollment moves `ENROLLED → FAILED`, no competency is granted, and — this is the deliberate asymmetry with withdrawal — the course's `enrolled_count` is **not** decremented, because a no-show consumed the seat.

Grading closes the enrollment. A `TrainingCoordinator` supplies `practical_score`, an integer from 0 through 100 inclusive; anything outside that range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A score of at least 80 drives `ATTENDED → PASSED`; a score below 80 drives `ATTENDED → FAILED`. Both branches stamp `graded_at` with the current logical instant and record the `practical_score`. Only the passing branch has consequences beyond the enrollment row: the course's `passed_count` rises by 1, the producer's `competency_codes` gains every code in that course's `grants_competency_codes`, the producer's `certification_level` is re-derived, and both the enrollment's `version` and the producer's `version` increment by 1. On the failing branch only the enrollment's `version` increments, no competency is granted, and `certification_level` is untouched.

The `competency_codes` list is a derived set, not an append log. It is the union of `grants_competency_codes` across every `PASSED` enrollment the producer holds, de-duplicated, and ordered by the `graded_at` of the enrollment that granted each code, earliest first.

`certification_level` is likewise derived, and it is gated by membership status before anything else. A producer who is `APPLICANT`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` holds `NONE` irrespective of course history. Otherwise the level is read off the set of tiers the producer has passed: if that set contains all three of `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` and `LIVE`, the level is `LIVE`; otherwise, if it contains both `BASIC` and `ADVANCED`, the level is `ADVANCED`; otherwise, if it contains `BASIC`, the level is `BASIC`; otherwise `NONE`. Only `PASSED` enrollments feed that tier set — `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED`, `FAILED` and `WITHDRAWN` enrollments contribute nothing. Because the derivation always reads the surviving enrollments, suspension is a suppression rather than an erasure: a suspended producer's level collapses to `NONE`, and reinstatement restores exactly the level the passed courses support.

Reinstatement and revocation both belong to the `CenterAdmin`, and both are discussed where their consequences live: reinstatement in §3.7, alongside the damage penalties that caused the suspension, and revocation in §3.7 as well, because unwinding a membership touches reservations, crew, series and clearance all at once.

### 3.2. Series Lineage, Seniority and Hold Plans

A **series** is the vehicle for scheduling priority. Either the owning producer or a `SchedulingCoordinator` proposes one by supplying `producer_id`, a `title`, a `cadence` of `WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY` or `MONTHLY`, a `first_air_date` naming the historic anchor claimed for this series' own run, and optionally a `predecessor_series_id` identifying the earlier series this one continues. A series with no predecessor is an original series and carries a null `predecessor_series_id`. When a `Producer` proposes the series, the `producer_id` must be their own, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Every proposed series is stamped with a `sequence_number` drawn from a monotone centre-wide counter — 1, 2, 3 and upward in creation order — and this number is the system's deterministic tie-break whenever two series present identical seniority. A new series opens in `PROPOSED` status with a null `seniority_at`, and with `hold_plan_slots_granted`, `remaining_hold_slots` and `missed_windows` all at 0. Series may be listed filtered by `producer_id`, `predecessor_series_id` or `status`.

Activation is the `SchedulingCoordinator`'s act and serves both the `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and the `LAPSED → ACTIVE` edges. Two conditions on the owning producer gate it: the producer's status must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), and the producer's `certification_level` must be something other than `NONE` — an uncertified owner cannot hold airtime priority, and such an activation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Activation from `PROPOSED` computes `seniority_at`. Activation from `LAPSED` additionally resets `missed_windows` to 0 and restores `remaining_hold_slots` to whatever `hold_plan_slots_granted` currently records, and it recomputes `seniority_at` from scratch, because the predecessor chain may have changed while the series was dormant. Either way the series' `version` increments by 1.

**Seniority is inherited, but a retired lineage does not lend it.** `seniority_at` is computed by walking backwards along the predecessor chain. The walk starts by taking the series' own `first_air_date` as the running seniority and marking the series itself as visited, then follows `predecessor_series_id` from node to node. At each predecessor the walk stops immediately if that predecessor has already been visited — this is the cycle guard, and it is what keeps a mis-entered circular lineage from looping forever — and it also stops immediately if that predecessor's status is `RETIRED`, without counting that predecessor's date. Otherwise the predecessor is marked visited, the running seniority becomes whichever is earlier of the running value and that predecessor's `first_air_date`, and the walk continues to that predecessor's own `predecessor_series_id`. When the chain runs out, the running value is stored as `seniority_at`, at second precision with no rounding. Consider a lineage of three: an original series anchored in March 2024, a continuation anchored in June 2025 whose predecessor is the original, and a third anchored in September 2026 whose predecessor is the continuation. Activating the third yields a `seniority_at` of March 2024. If the original is retired first, the walk halts at it without counting it, and the third series' `seniority_at` becomes June 2025 — the earlier of its own September 2026 anchor and the continuation's June 2025 anchor.

A **hold plan** is the series' stock of priority booking slots, and only a `SchedulingCoordinator` may set one. The request supplies `expected_series_version` and `hold_plan_slots_granted`; on success both `hold_plan_slots_granted` and `remaining_hold_slots` are set to the granted figure — a new plan replaces the old one outright rather than adding to it — and the series' `version` increments by 1. The series must be `ACTIVE` when the plan is written, and a plan against a `PROPOSED`, `LAPSED` or `RETIRED` series is refused with `ERR_SERIES_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Because a hold plan legitimately supersedes its predecessor, the concurrency guard described in §2.7 is what protects it: an `expected_series_version` that does not equal the series' stored `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` (412), and that refusal is evaluated **before** the `ACTIVE`-status check, so a coordinator working from a stale read is told to re-read rather than being told the series is in the wrong state.

Hold slots are consumed by scheduling and restored by unscheduling, and the two forms of unscheduling are not equivalent. A series-hold slot that a coordinator cancels returns its hold slot **and** counts as a missed window: `remaining_hold_slots` rises by 1 and `missed_windows` rises by 1. A series-hold slot withdrawn because the underlying program was withdrawn or rejected returns its hold slot **without** touching `missed_windows`, because the series is not penalised for an editorial outcome. Once `missed_windows` reaches 2 the series lapses on the spot: the `ACTIVE → LAPSED` transition fires as a system effect of that cancellation, `remaining_hold_slots` is forced to 0 so that no further priority booking is possible while the series is dormant, `hold_plan_slots_granted` is retained untouched so that a later reactivation can restore it, and the series' `version` increments by 1.

Retirement is terminal. When a producer's membership is revoked, every series they own whose status is `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` moves to `RETIRED`, `remaining_hold_slots` is forced to 0, the series' `version` increments by 1, and every series whose `predecessor_series_id` points at the newly retired series recomputes its own `seniority_at` — which now stops at its own `first_air_date`, because the retired ancestor no longer transfers seniority forward. A `RETIRED` series never leaves that state.

### 3.3. Program Submission, Attestation Clearance and Content Review

A **program** is one playable piece of content, and only its own producer may create it. Creation supplies `producer_id`, a `title`, a `runtime_seconds` of at least 60, the boolean `is_live`, and `required_crew_roles`, the list of distinct technical roles a live broadcast needs, each drawn from `DIRECTOR`, `CAMERA`, `AUDIO`, `SWITCHER` and `FLOOR_MANAGER`. Two further fields are supplied only for episodic content: `series_id` names the series the episode belongs to, and `episode_number` gives its ordinal within that series and must be at least 1. A one-off program carries a null `series_id` and a null `episode_number`. The crew list and the live flag must agree: `required_crew_roles` must be empty whenever `is_live` is false, and a non-empty list on a non-live program is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Live production is gated on certification. A producer whose `certification_level` is anything other than `LIVE` may not create a program with `is_live` true, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Where a `series_id` is supplied, that series must belong to the same producer as the program; a cross-owner reference is refused with `ERR_SERIES_NOT_OWNED_BY_PRODUCER` (422). A program is born with `review_status` `DRAFT`, `clearance_status` `INCOMPLETE`, `revision_round` at 0, `attestation_sequence_high` at 0, a null `reviewed_by_user_id`, a null `finding_category`, a null `finding_notes` and a null `withdrawn_reason`. Programs may be listed filtered by `producer_id`, `series_id`, `review_status` or `clearance_status`.

**Two independent state dimensions live on one program row.** `review_status` tracks the editorial pipeline, and `clearance_status` tracks the legal paperwork. They evolve independently: clearing the paperwork never advances the review, and a review decision never touches clearance. Both are visible on every read of the program, and the operations described below each move exactly one of them.

Clearance is earned by attestation. The producer appends attestations one at a time, each naming an `attestation_kind` of `INDEMNIFICATION`, `COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE` or `CONTENT_POLICY`, a `statement_version` identifying the version of the signed statement text, the boolean `third_party_material` declaring whether the program embeds material the producer did not create, and — where such material exists — a `license_reference` giving the licence or public-domain citation. A `COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE` attestation that declares `third_party_material` true while leaving `license_reference` absent or empty is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_REFERENCE_REQUIRED` (422). Each appended attestation records the `program_id` it covers and the `signed_by_producer_id` who signed it, which must be the program's own producer.

Attestations are an append-only log. Each new one is stamped with a `sequence_number` equal to the program's `attestation_sequence_high` plus 1 — so the counter is per-program, starting at 1 — and that high-water mark advances with it. Its `effective_from` is the current logical instant. Its `effective_until` is set only for the `INDEMNIFICATION` kind, at 365 days after its own `effective_from`; for `COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE` and `CONTENT_POLICY` attestations `effective_until` is null. Appending an attestation also closes the previous attestation of the same kind on that program by stamping that earlier row's `effective_until`. An `INDEMNIFICATION` attestation additionally refreshes the signing producer's `indemnification_expires_at` to 365 days after the current logical instant — the producer-level expiry is what later gates submission, and it is a producer-wide term, not a per-program one. Attestations may be read individually and listed filtered by `program_id`, `signed_by_producer_id` or `attestation_kind`.

Three kinds are required for clearance: `INDEMNIFICATION`, `COPYRIGHT_CLEARANCE` and `CONTENT_POLICY`. After every append, `clearance_status` is re-derived by looking, for each of those three kinds, at the *latest* attestation of that kind on the program — latest meaning the greatest `created_at`, and where two share a `created_at`, the greater `sequence_number`. When all three of those latest attestations carry a `statement_version` equal to the current statement version, which is `2026.1`, the program moves `INCOMPLETE → CLEARED` and its `version` increments by 1; `review_status` is untouched by that move. Clearance is revocable by the same mechanism: appending an attestation of some required kind that carries a `statement_version` other than `2026.1` supersedes the compliant one and drives `CLEARED → INCOMPLETE`, again incrementing `version` and again leaving `review_status` alone. Revoking the owning producer's membership has the same effect on every one of their programs whose `review_status` is not `REJECTED` and not `WITHDRAWN`: `clearance_status` is re-derived to `INCOMPLETE`.

Submission is the producer's own act and drives both `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and the revision-loop edge `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Five conditions must hold. The producer's status must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE` (409) — this is the refusal a producer sees when a required kind is missing or when the latest attestation of a kind carries a superseded statement version. Where the program belongs to a series, that series must be `ACTIVE`; a submission behind a `PROPOSED`, `LAPSED` or `RETIRED` series is refused with `ERR_SERIES_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The producer's `indemnification_expires_at` must be non-null and strictly later than the current logical instant, otherwise `ERR_INDEMNIFICATION_EXPIRED` (422). And the revision budget must not be spent: at most two rounds of requested changes are tolerated, so a program whose `revision_round` has grown beyond 2 can no longer be resubmitted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) — after the third refusal the only remaining path is withdrawal. A successful submission clears `reviewed_by_user_id` back to null, so that any reviewer may claim the next round, and increments the program's `version` by 1.

Review is a two-step act by design, because the same person must do both halves. A `ContentReviewer` first claims the program, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording their own `X-User-ID` in `reviewed_by_user_id`. The reviewer then decides it, supplying `decision` — one of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `REJECT` — together with an optional `finding_category` and an optional `finding_notes`. A decision is accepted only from the reviewer whose `X-User-ID` matches the program's stored `reviewed_by_user_id`; a decision from anyone else, or a decision on a program not in `UNDER_REVIEW`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

Editorial neutrality is enforced through `finding_category`, and the rule cuts both ways. A `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `REJECT` decision **must** cite a `finding_category`, and that category must be one of the eight legally unprotected categories listed in §2.11; a refusal that omits the category or names anything outside that set is rejected with `ERR_FINDING_CATEGORY_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). An `APPROVE` decision must **not** carry a `finding_category` at all, and supplying one is rejected with the same `ERR_FINDING_CATEGORY_NOT_PERMITTED` (422) — the center does not annotate approvals with editorial commentary.

The three decision branches diverge sharply in their consequences. `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` clears both `finding_category` and `finding_notes` back to null and increments the program's `version` by 1; nothing else moves, and the program is now bookable for airtime. `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` raises `revision_round` by 1, records the supplied `finding_category` and `finding_notes`, and increments `version` by 1; the producer may now resubmit, which is the cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` records the `finding_category` and `finding_notes`, increments `version` by 1, and additionally unwinds scheduling: every playback slot carrying this program whose status is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → WITHDRAWN`, with exactly the same downstream effects as the withdrawal cascade described next. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Withdrawal is available to the producer themselves or to a `CenterAdmin`, requires a `withdrawn_reason`, and reaches further than any other single operation in the system. From `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, withdrawal simply sets `review_status` to `WITHDRAWN`, records the `withdrawn_reason` and increments `version` by 1 — no slots can exist from those states, so nothing cascades. From `APPROVED`, four linked effects fire in sequence. First, `review_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` and `withdrawn_reason` is recorded. Second, every playback slot carrying this program whose status is `SCHEDULED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; each such withdrawal lowers that channel's `scheduled_seconds` by the slot's `occupied_seconds`, and where the slot's `booking_type` is `SERIES_HOLD` it raises the owning series' `remaining_hold_slots` by 1 **without** raising `missed_windows`. Third, every crew assignment on a slot withdrawn by the second effect whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `PROGRAM_WITHDRAWN`, and each such slot's `staffing_status` is re-derived. Fourth, every volunteer touched by the third effect has their `confirmed_crew_commitments` re-derived. Beyond those four, every archive asset for this program whose `on_demand_status` is `PUBLISHED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawal is available from any state other than `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, both of which are terminal, and an attempt from either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

### 3.4. Channel Airtime: Window Fit, First Come, and Series Priority

Channels are defined by a `CenterAdmin`. A channel carries a `call_sign` unique across all channels — a reuse is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (409) — a `tier` of `PUBLIC`, `EDUCATIONAL` or `GOVERNMENT` marking its PEG designation, and a daily broadcast window given as `daily_window_start_minute` and `daily_window_end_minute`, both counted in minutes after 00:00 UTC. The start minute must lie between 0 and 1439 inclusive, the end minute must be strictly greater than the start minute and at most 1440, the start boundary is inclusive and the end boundary is exclusive. `inter_program_buffer_seconds` gives the interstitial seconds appended to every slot on the channel and must not be negative. `preemption_enabled` defaults to true; when it is set false, a series-priority challenger can never displace an incumbent on that channel. A new channel opens with `scheduled_seconds` at 0, `aired_seconds` at 0 and `slot_sequence_high` at 0. Channels may be listed with an optional `tier` filter.

Booking airtime is the `SchedulingCoordinator`'s exclusive act. The request supplies `channel_id`, `program_id`, `start_at` — the playback start instant in UTC — and optionally `series_id`, which is supplied only when the booking is to be claimed against that series' hold plan. A booking with no `series_id` is an ordinary first-come booking. Slots may be read individually and listed filtered by `channel_id`, `program_id`, `series_id`, `preempted_by_slot_id` or `status`.

Before anything temporal is examined, two state gates apply to the program. Its `review_status` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED` (409), and this state check runs ahead of every window and conflict check on the same request — a slot request that is both for an unapproved program and in a full window reports the approval failure. Its `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_ATTESTATION_INCOMPLETE` (409). Where a `series_id` is supplied, the named series must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SERIES_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).

**Footprint and window fit.** A slot's footprint is not merely the program's runtime: `occupied_seconds` is the program's `runtime_seconds` plus the channel's `inter_program_buffer_seconds`, and it is frozen on the slot at creation so that a later change to either input cannot retroactively move the schedule. The slot's `end_at` is its `start_at` advanced by `occupied_seconds`, and the slot owns the half-open interval that begins at `start_at` and ends immediately before `end_at` — a slot beginning exactly when another ends does not overlap it. To test window fit, take the requested `start_at`, convert its clock time into minutes after UTC midnight by multiplying its hour by 60 and adding its minute; this start minute must be at or after the channel's `daily_window_start_minute`. Then take the same start expressed in seconds after UTC midnight — the start minute multiplied by 60, plus the start instant's seconds — and add `occupied_seconds`; the result must be at or below the channel's `daily_window_end_minute` multiplied by 60. If either comparison fails, the booking is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_CHANNEL_WINDOW` (422). All of this arithmetic is in whole seconds with no rounding anywhere. Note that it is often the buffer that pushes a slot out: a half-hour program with a two-minute buffer occupies 1920 seconds, and starting it at 22:45 UTC on a channel whose window closes at minute 1380 overruns by 1020 seconds even though the runtime alone would have fitted.

**Conflict resolution.** Once the footprint fits, the candidate is compared against the existing slots on the same channel whose status is `SCHEDULED`; slots that are `AIRED`, `CANCELLED`, `PREEMPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` never block a booking. An existing slot overlaps the candidate when that slot's `start_at` is strictly before the candidate's `end_at` and the candidate's `start_at` is strictly before that slot's `end_at`. When nothing overlaps, the booking is accepted outright.

When something does overlap, only a series-priority challenger can proceed, and the checks run in a fixed order. If no `series_id` was supplied, the booking is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). If the channel's `preemption_enabled` is false, the booking is likewise refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). If the named series is not `ACTIVE`, the refusal is `ERR_SERIES_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). If the series' `remaining_hold_slots` is below 1, the refusal is `ERR_SERIES_HOLD_EXHAUSTED` (409).

**Preemption is all-or-nothing.** Every overlapping incumbent must be individually displaceable, and if even one is not, the whole request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) and no incumbent is touched. An incumbent is displaceable only when its `booking_type` is `FIRST_COME`; an incumbent holding a `SERIES_HOLD` booking can never be displaced. For each displaceable incumbent, form its priority key: where the incumbent itself names a series, the key is that series' `seniority_at`; where the incumbent is an unaffiliated first-come booking, the key is the incumbent slot's own `created_at`. The challenger wins outright when its series' `seniority_at` is strictly earlier than that key. Where the two are exactly equal, the challenger still wins — but only when the incumbent also names a series and the challenger series' `sequence_number` is strictly lower than the incumbent series' `sequence_number`; that monotone counter is the documented tie-break, and record identifiers are never used for it. In every other case the strictly-earlier requirement is unmet and the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Timestamp comparisons here are at second precision.

Consider a channel carrying a first-come incumbent created on 2 August 2026 and occupying 18:00 to 18:32 on 10 August, with preemption enabled. A challenger series whose `seniority_at` is March 2024 and whose `remaining_hold_slots` is 2 wins: the booking is accepted as a `SERIES_HOLD`, the incumbent becomes `PREEMPTED` with `preempted_by_slot_id` pointing at the new slot, and the challenger's `remaining_hold_slots` falls to 1. Had the challenger's `seniority_at` been September 2026, the comparison would have failed, the request would have been refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and the incumbent would have remained `SCHEDULED`.

Two referential gates round out the booking rules. The series named on a booking must belong to the same producer as the program, otherwise `ERR_SERIES_NOT_OWNED_BY_PRODUCER` (422). And channel tier is rationed by certification: a producer whose `certification_level` is `BASIC` may occupy only a `PUBLIC` channel, and booking an `EDUCATIONAL` or `GOVERNMENT` channel for that producer's program is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_TIER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422); only `ADVANCED` and `LIVE` producers reach those tiers.

**What a successful booking writes.** The slot is created in `SCHEDULED` status. Its `occupied_seconds`, `end_at` and `required_crew_roles` are frozen — the crew list is copied from the program as it stands at that moment — and its `sequence_number` is set to the channel's `slot_sequence_high` plus 1, with `slot_sequence_high` advancing to match. Its `booking_type` becomes `SERIES_HOLD` when a `series_id` was supplied, in which case that series' `remaining_hold_slots` falls by 1; otherwise `booking_type` is `FIRST_COME`. The channel's `scheduled_seconds` rises by the slot's `occupied_seconds`, and the channel's `version` increments by 1. `staffing_status` is initialised to `NOT_REQUIRED` when the frozen `required_crew_roles` list is empty, and to `UNDERSTAFFED` otherwise. `measured_households` stays null until the slot airs, `aired_at` and `cancellation_reason` stay null, and `preempted_by_slot_id` stays null unless and until this slot is itself displaced. Finally, every authorised preemption target moves `SCHEDULED → PREEMPTED`.

A displaced incumbent's own transition is more than a status change. `preempted_by_slot_id` is set to the challenger slot's identifier, the channel's `scheduled_seconds` falls by that incumbent's `occupied_seconds`, every crew assignment on the displaced slot whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `SLOT_PREEMPTED`, the displaced slot's `staffing_status` is re-derived, each affected volunteer's `confirmed_crew_commitments` is re-derived, and the displaced slot's `version` increments by 1. A `SERIES_HOLD` incumbent is never displaced, so no hold slot is ever returned by preemption.

Cancellation is the coordinator's deliberate unscheduling and requires a `cancellation_reason`. It is available only from `SCHEDULED` and drives `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. The channel's `scheduled_seconds` falls by the slot's `occupied_seconds` and the reason is recorded. Where the slot's `booking_type` is `SERIES_HOLD`, the owning series' `remaining_hold_slots` rises by 1 **and** its `missed_windows` rises by 1, and the series lapses immediately if that brings `missed_windows` to 2. Every crew assignment on the slot whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `SLOT_CANCELLED`, after which the slot's `staffing_status` and each affected volunteer's `confirmed_crew_commitments` are re-derived. Reservations that named this slot as their `linked_slot_id` and are still `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` keep their own status — studio time is deliberately not auto-released, because a producer whose airing was cancelled may still want the edit suite. The slot's `version` increments by 1. `AIRED`, `CANCELLED`, `PREEMPTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal for a slot, and any action against a slot already in one of them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

### 3.5. Volunteer Crew Staffing and Going to Air

Live programming is staffed by volunteers drawn from the producer community, and the `SchedulingCoordinator` is the only role that may extend an offer. An offer names the `slot_id` being staffed, the `volunteer_producer_id` filling the role, and the `crew_role` — one of `DIRECTOR`, `CAMERA`, `AUDIO`, `SWITCHER` or `FLOOR_MANAGER`. The assignment is created in `OFFERED` status with `credited_hours_tenths` at 0 and a null `release_reason`, and creating it does **not** change the slot's `staffing_status`, because an offer nobody has accepted covers nothing. Assignments may be read individually and listed filtered by `slot_id`, `volunteer_producer_id` or `status`.

Acceptance is the `OFFERED → CONFIRMED` transition, performed either by the volunteer themselves or by a `SchedulingCoordinator`; when the acting role is `Producer` the assignment's `volunteer_producer_id` must be the acting user. Four conditions gate it. The slot must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise the transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). The volunteer's own membership must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Then certification: the roles `DIRECTOR` and `SWITCHER` are the two positions that demand top-tier training, so confirming either of them on a slot whose program has `is_live` true requires the volunteer's `certification_level` to be `LIVE`, and anything less is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Finally the volunteer must be free: if they already hold a `CONFIRMED` assignment on some other slot whose half-open interval from `start_at` to `end_at` overlaps this slot's interval, the request is refused with `ERR_CREW_TIME_CONFLICT` (409). The certification check outranks the overlap check, so a `BASIC`-certified volunteer offered a `DIRECTOR` role who is also double-booked hears about the certification, not the clash. A successful confirmation re-derives the slot's `staffing_status`, raises the volunteer's `confirmed_crew_commitments` by 1, and increments the assignment's `version` by 1.

Release requires a `release_reason` and is available to the volunteer or to a `SchedulingCoordinator`. From `OFFERED` it simply records the reason, re-derives the slot's `staffing_status` and increments the assignment's `version` by 1. From `CONFIRMED` it does the same and additionally lowers the volunteer's `confirmed_crew_commitments` by 1; releasing a confirmed volunteer is exactly how a slot falls back from `READY` to `UNDERSTAFFED`. A confirmed assignment may not be released once its slot has aired, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). The same `RELEASED` outcome is reached without any operator request whenever a cascade demands it, and the `release_reason` records which cascade did it: `SLOT_CANCELLED` for a cancelled slot, `PROGRAM_WITHDRAWN` for a withdrawn or rejected program, `SLOT_PREEMPTED` for a displaced slot, and `VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED` for a suspended volunteer. `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal for an assignment.

**Staffing readiness** is derived, never supplied. When a slot's frozen `required_crew_roles` list is empty, its `staffing_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` and it can never become `UNDERSTAFFED`. Otherwise, gather the set of `crew_role` values held by that slot's assignments whose status is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`; the slot is `READY` when every role in `required_crew_roles` appears in that set, and `UNDERSTAFFED` otherwise. `OFFERED` and `RELEASED` assignments contribute nothing to coverage, and two volunteers confirmed into the same role add no more coverage than one. A slot requiring a director, a camera operator and an audio engineer therefore sits at `UNDERSTAFFED` after two confirmations, reaches `READY` on the third, and drops back to `UNDERSTAFFED` the moment any one of the three is released.

Going to air is the `SchedulingCoordinator`'s act and carries `measured_households`, the metered household count observed at air time, which must not be negative. Four conditions gate the `SCHEDULED → AIRED` transition: the slot must be `SCHEDULED`; the logical clock must have reached or passed the slot's `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_AIRTIME_NOT_REACHED` (409); the program must still be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED` (409); and the slot's `staffing_status` must be either `READY` or `NOT_REQUIRED` — an `UNDERSTAFFED` slot cannot air and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SLOT_UNDERSTAFFED` (409).

Airing settles four things at once. The slot's `aired_at` is stamped with the current logical instant and `measured_households` is recorded from the request. On the channel's airtime ledger, `scheduled_seconds` falls by the slot's `occupied_seconds` and `aired_seconds` rises by the same figure, so the two counters together always account for every booked second exactly once. Every crew assignment on the slot whose status is `CONFIRMED` moves to `COMPLETED` and is credited with volunteer time: `credited_hours_tenths` is the slot's `occupied_seconds` divided by 360 and rounded **up** to a whole number of tenths of an hour — six minutes of occupied airtime buys one tenth, and any part-tenth rounds up to a full tenth. Each such volunteer's `volunteer_hours_tenths` rises by that credit and their `confirmed_crew_commitments` falls by 1, and each completed assignment's `version` increments by 1. Finally, one archive asset is created for the airing, in `PENDING` on-demand status and carrying the provisional community impact score described in §3.9. The slot's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.6. Equipment, Studio Reservations and the Deposit Hold

The equipment room is the `FacilitiesManager`'s domain. Registering a bookable asset supplies a `resource_code` unique across all resources — reuse is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (409) — a `resource_class` of `STUDIO_A`, `FIELD_CAMERA_KIT`, `AUDIO_KIT` or `EDIT_SUITE`, and a `required_competency_code` drawn from the five equipment competency codes, naming the training a producer must hold before the asset leaves the building. `capacity_slots` records how many overlapping reservations the asset can host before it is fully booked and must be at least 1 — a studio floor that hosts three simultaneous edit sessions is capacity 3, a single camera kit is capacity 1. `turnaround_minutes` is the mandatory clearance gap appended to **both** sides of every reservation interval on that asset and must not be negative. `base_deposit_cents` is the basis every deposit and fee line for the asset is computed from, and `replacement_value_cents` is the ceiling on both the deposit hold and any assessed repair; the replacement value must be at least the base deposit. A registered asset opens `AVAILABLE` with `open_reservation_count` at 0 and a null `status_reason`. Resources may be listed filtered by `resource_class` or `status`.

Asset availability is a small three-state machine driven by a `FacilitiesManager` supplying a target `status` and a `status_reason`. `AVAILABLE → MAINTENANCE` and `MAINTENANCE → AVAILABLE` are both freely available and both simply record the reason and increment the asset's `version` by 1; existing reservations keep their own status across a maintenance move, but no `REQUESTED` reservation on the asset can be confirmed while it sits in `MAINTENANCE`. Retirement is reachable from either `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE`, but only when the asset's `open_reservation_count` is exactly 0; a retirement attempt against an asset with any open reservation is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_HAS_OPEN_RESERVATIONS` (409). `RETIRED` is terminal — a retired asset can never return to service, and any further status change is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

Reservations are requested either by the producer themselves or by a `FacilitiesManager`, supplying `producer_id`, `resource_id`, a `start_at` and an `end_at` strictly after it, and optionally a `linked_slot_id` naming the live playback slot this booking supports. An ordinary booking carries a null `linked_slot_id`. Where a `linked_slot_id` is supplied, the program behind that slot must belong to the same producer as `producer_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SLOT_PRODUCER_MISMATCH` (422). A new reservation is created in `REQUESTED` status with `risk_bp`, `deposit_hold_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `assessed_repair_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `uncovered_liability_cents` all at 0, with `checked_out_at`, `returned_at`, `condition_notes` and `cancellation_reason` all null. Requesting a reservation holds no money whatsoever. Reservations may be listed filtered by `producer_id`, `resource_id`, `linked_slot_id` or `status`.

Confirmation is the `FacilitiesManager`'s act and is where money first moves. Five conditions gate `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, and their relative order matters: the asset's status must be `AVAILABLE`, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409); the producer must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); the asset's `required_competency_code` must appear in the producer's `competency_codes`, otherwise `ERR_COMPETENCY_NOT_HELD` (422); the asset must have room for the interval, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_OVERBOOKED` (409); and the producer's aggregate deposit exposure must stay within its ceiling, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402). The asset-state check is the highest of the five and the deposit ceiling is the lowest, so an asset in `MAINTENANCE` belonging to a producer who is also at their deposit ceiling reports `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

**Capacity and turnaround.** Room for the interval is judged against the asset's other reservations whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_OUT` or `DAMAGE_REVIEW`. Two reservations overlap when the first's `start_at` is strictly before the second's `end_at` and the second's `start_at` is strictly before the first's `end_at` — the interval is half-open, so a booking that starts exactly when another ends does not overlap it. The asset is full when the count of such overlapping reservations has already reached `capacity_slots`. Separately, and even where no overlap exists, the request is blocked when its `start_at` falls inside the `turnaround_minutes` window that follows the `end_at` of one of those reservations; a kit with a thirty-minute turnaround cannot be handed to the next producer twenty minutes after it came back. Either condition yields `ERR_RESOURCE_OVERBOOKED` (409).

**The deposit hold is built from three independently rounded lines, in a pinned order.** Step one computes `risk_bp`, the producer's risk loading in basis points: multiply their current `suspension_points` by 1500 basis points per point, then take whichever is smaller of that product and the cap of 4500 basis points — so one outstanding point loads the deposit by 15 percent, two points by 30 percent, and three or more points are capped at 45 percent. Only points that are still outstanding count; points already credited back by a reinstatement do not. Step two computes the duration loading: take the reservation's length from `start_at` to `end_at` in seconds, divide by 3600 and round **up** to a whole number of started hours, subtract 1, floor the result at 0, and multiply by 500 basis points — the first started hour is free and every further started hour adds 5 percent. Step three computes the live loading: where the reservation names a `linked_slot_id` whose program has `is_live` true, the live loading is 2000 basis points, otherwise it is 0.

Steps four through six turn those three loadings into three cent amounts, each rounded on its own. The risk line is the asset's `base_deposit_cents` multiplied by ten thousand plus `risk_bp`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent — note that this line carries the whole base deposit plus its risk loading, not just the loading. The duration line is `base_deposit_cents` multiplied by the duration loading in basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. The live line is `base_deposit_cents` multiplied by the live loading in basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. Step seven sums the three rounded lines. Step eight clamps the sum to the asset's `replacement_value_cents`, taking whichever is smaller, and stores the result as `deposit_hold_cents`. **Rounding each line separately is normative**: for a base deposit of 12345 cents with one suspension point, a three-hour window and a live link, the risk line is 14197, the duration line is 1235 and the live line is 2469, for a hold of 17901 cents — whereas summing the loadings to 14500 basis points and rounding once would give 17900, a cent adrift. A clean comparison case, with no suspension points, a two-hour window and no live link, gives the base 12345 plus a duration line of 617, for a hold of 12962 cents.

The deposit ceiling is per producer, not per reservation: the producer's existing `deposit_held_cents` plus the newly computed hold must not exceed 500000 cents. Breaching it is `ERR_DEPOSIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402), and no hold is written.

A successful confirmation freezes both `risk_bp` and `deposit_hold_cents` on the reservation — neither is ever recomputed afterwards, so a later suspension does not retroactively raise an existing hold — raises the producer's `deposit_held_cents` by the hold amount, raises the asset's `open_reservation_count` by 1, and increments the reservation's `version` by 1.

Cancellation is available to the producer or to a `FacilitiesManager` and requires a `cancellation_reason`. From `REQUESTED` it records the reason and increments `version` by 1; because no deposit was ever held, no money moves. From `CONFIRMED` it is a compensating action: `deposit_refunded_cents` is set to the full `deposit_hold_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents` is set to 0 and `uncovered_liability_cents` is set to 0, while `deposit_hold_cents` itself is retained for audit rather than zeroed. The producer's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the hold amount, restoring the invariant that it sums the holds of `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_OUT` and `DAMAGE_REVIEW` reservations only; the asset's `open_reservation_count` falls by 1; and the reservation's `version` increments by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Checkout is the `FacilitiesManager` handing the equipment over, optionally recording `condition_notes` describing the state it left in. The `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_OUT` transition requires the producer to still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), and it is bounded on both sides: the logical clock must have reached the reservation's `start_at` less a one-hour lead — equipment may be collected up to 3600 seconds early, but no earlier — and must still be strictly before the reservation's `end_at`. Outside that window the request is refused with `ERR_CHECKOUT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409). A successful checkout stamps `checked_out_at` with the current logical instant, records the `condition_notes`, and increments the reservation's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. Returns, Damage Review, Suspension and Reinstatement

The return call is the fork in the reservation lifecycle, and the `FacilitiesManager` decides which way it goes by supplying the boolean `damage_observed`, optionally alongside `condition_notes`. Both branches leave `CHECKED_OUT` and both stamp `returned_at` with the current logical instant.

**The clean branch** fires when `damage_observed` is false and drives `CHECKED_OUT → SETTLED` directly. The full settlement waterfall runs with an assessed repair of 0: `late_fee_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `uncovered_liability_cents` are all computed and stored. The producer's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the reservation's `deposit_hold_cents` and their `deposit_forfeited_total_cents` rises by whatever was forfeited; the asset's `open_reservation_count` falls by 1. No damage assessment is created and no suspension point is applied. The reservation's `version` increments by 1.

**The damaged branch** fires when `damage_observed` is true, in which case both `severity` and `repair_estimate_cents` must also be supplied, along with an optional `damage_notes` describing what happened. This branch drives `CHECKED_OUT → DAMAGE_REVIEW`. Only the late fee is computed at this point; the deposit stays on hold, so the producer's `deposit_held_cents` is unchanged and the asset's `open_reservation_count` is unchanged as well, because a reservation under damage review still counts as open. One damage assessment is created in `FILED` status, recording the `reservation_id`, the `severity`, the operator's raw `repair_estimate_cents` and the `damage_notes`, with `assessed_repair_cents` at 0, `suspension_points_assessed` at 0, `filed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and both `waived_by_user_id` and `waiver_reason` null. The new assessment is not returned by the return call itself; it is discovered by listing damage assessments filtered by `reservation_id`, and assessments may also be filtered by `status` or `severity`. The reservation's `version` increments by 1.

**The settlement waterfall** is a fixed six-step drain of the held deposit. Step one measures lateness: subtract the reservation's `end_at` from its `returned_at`, floor the result at 0 seconds, then divide by 3600 and round **up** to a whole number of started late hours — an on-time or early return yields 0 started late hours and therefore no fee at all, while a return one hour and five minutes late counts as two started hours. Step two prices those hours: the per-hour fee is the asset's `base_deposit_cents` multiplied by 750 basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent — seven and a half percent of the base deposit per started late hour — and `late_fee_cents` is that rounded per-hour fee multiplied by the number of started late hours. The rounding happens once, on the per-hour figure, and the product is never rounded again. Step three totals the charges as `late_fee_cents` plus the assessed repair. Step four sets `deposit_forfeited_cents` to whichever is smaller of `deposit_hold_cents` and those total charges. Step five sets `deposit_refunded_cents` to `deposit_hold_cents` less `deposit_forfeited_cents`. Step six sets `uncovered_liability_cents` to the total charges less `deposit_hold_cents`, floored at 0. Two identities therefore hold on every settled reservation: the hold always equals the forfeited amount plus the refunded amount, and the forfeited amount plus the uncovered liability always equals the late fee plus the assessed repair. On a hold of 12962 cents returned one hour and five minutes late against a base deposit of 12345 with no damage, the per-hour fee is 926, the late fee is 1852, the forfeiture is 1852, the refund is 11110 and the uncovered liability is 0. The same reservation carrying an assessed repair of 120000 cents forfeits the whole 12962, refunds nothing, and leaves 108890 cents of uncovered liability the center must pursue outside this system.

A filed assessment leaves `FILED` by exactly one of two routes, and the reservation's `DAMAGE_REVIEW → SETTLED` transition is the join point where both routes reconverge.

**Finalization** is the `FacilitiesManager`'s route and requires the assessment to be `FILED` with its reservation still in `DAMAGE_REVIEW`. It computes `assessed_repair_cents` as whichever is smaller of the filed `repair_estimate_cents` and the asset's `replacement_value_cents` — the center never charges more than the equipment is worth — and it converts `severity` into `suspension_points_assessed`: a `MINOR` incident is worth 1 point, a `MAJOR` one 2 points and a `TOTAL_LOSS` 3 points, with one extra point added whenever the assessed repair reaches 50000 cents. An estimate of 49999 cents on a minor incident therefore assesses 1 point, while 50000 cents on the same incident assesses 2; a total loss estimated at 1200000 cents against a replacement value of 900000 is assessed at 900000 cents and 4 points. The assessment's `version` increments by 1.

Finalization then cascades. The reservation moves `DAMAGE_REVIEW → SETTLED`, taking the assessment's `assessed_repair_cents` onto its own `assessed_repair_cents` field and re-running the settlement waterfall from step three onwards so that `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `uncovered_liability_cents` reflect both the late fee and the repair. The producer's `deposit_held_cents` falls by the reservation's `deposit_hold_cents`, their `deposit_forfeited_total_cents` rises by the forfeited amount, and the asset's `open_reservation_count` falls by 1. The producer's `suspension_points` rises by `suspension_points_assessed`.

**Suspension is the third link in that cascade.** When the producer is `ACTIVE` and the new `suspension_points` total reaches 3, the producer moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` on the spot, and that transition has consequences of its own. Their `certification_level` collapses to `NONE`, because the derivation gates on membership status. Every reservation of theirs whose status is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `PRODUCER_SUSPENDED`; each formerly `CONFIRMED` one releases its hold, setting `deposit_refunded_cents` to the full `deposit_hold_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` to 0, and lowers that asset's `open_reservation_count` by 1. Every crew assignment where they are the `volunteer_producer_id` whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose slot is still `SCHEDULED`, moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `VOLUNTEER_SUSPENDED`. Each slot touched by those releases re-derives its `staffing_status`, and the producer's own `confirmed_crew_commitments` and `deposit_held_cents` are re-derived. The producer's `version` increments by 1. A producer already `SUSPENDED` when a further assessment finalizes simply accumulates more points; the suspension transition does not fire twice.

**Waiver** is the `FinanceOfficer`'s route and is the compensating alternative to finalization. It requires the assessment to be `FILED`, the reservation to be in `DAMAGE_REVIEW`, a `waiver_reason`, and an acting `X-User-ID` different from the assessment's `filed_by_user_id`; the same person filing and forgiving is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). A waiver leaves `assessed_repair_cents` at 0 and `suspension_points_assessed` at 0, records `waived_by_user_id` and `waiver_reason`, and increments the assessment's `version` by 1. The reservation still reaches `SETTLED` through the same join point, but with an assessed repair of 0, so only the late fee can be forfeited; the producer's `deposit_held_cents` falls, `deposit_forfeited_total_cents` rises by that late-fee forfeiture, and the asset's `open_reservation_count` falls by 1. The producer's `suspension_points` is unchanged and they never leave `ACTIVE` on this path. `FINALIZED` and `WAIVED` are both terminal, and a second attempt at either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

**Reinstatement** is the `CenterAdmin`'s remedy, supplying `suspension_points_credited` and a `reason`. The `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` transition requires the producer to be `SUSPENDED`, requires `suspension_points_credited` to be greater than 0 and no greater than the producer's current `suspension_points`, and requires the remainder after the credit — the current `suspension_points` less `suspension_points_credited` — to be strictly below 3, since reinstating a producer who would immediately re-cross the suspension threshold is incoherent. On success the credit is subtracted from `suspension_points`, `reinstated_count` rises by 1, `certification_level` is re-derived from the surviving `PASSED` enrollments and therefore returns to whatever `BASIC`, `ADVANCED` or `LIVE` level those courses support, and the producer's `version` increments by 1. Reinstatement never revives the reservations the suspension cancelled nor the crew assignments it released — those are gone, and the producer must request them again.

**Revocation** is the terminal sanction, likewise the `CenterAdmin`'s, and requires a `revocation_reason`. It is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and the side-effect set is identical from both. `certification_level` is forced to `NONE` and the `revocation_reason` is recorded. Every reservation of theirs whose status is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`, with confirmed holds released and each affected asset's `open_reservation_count` lowered. Every crew assignment where they are the volunteer whose status is `OFFERED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`, after which each affected slot re-derives its `staffing_status`. Every series they own whose status is `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` moves to `RETIRED`, after which every series whose `predecessor_series_id` names one of those newly retired series re-derives its own `seniority_at`. Every program of theirs whose `review_status` is neither `REJECTED` nor `WITHDRAWN` has its `clearance_status` re-derived to `INCOMPLETE`. The producer's `version` increments by 1. `REVOKED` is terminal: a revoked producer is never reinstated, and any further membership action is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).

### 3.8. Franchise Fee and Capital Funding Administration

Franchise revenue arrives quarterly and must be accounted for to the municipality. A `FinanceOfficer` opens a reporting period by supplying `fiscal_year` — at least 2000 — and `quarter`, an integer from 1 through 4 inclusive, together with the `basis` on which the period will be recognised, either `ACCRUAL` or `CASH`. Three raw figures accompany them: `gross_franchise_fee_cents`, the whole fee billed to the cable operator for the period; `service_period_earned_cents`, the portion earned within the service period, which is the allocable base under `ACCRUAL`; and `remittance_received_cents`, the cash actually remitted within the period, which is the allocable base under `CASH`. `capital_grant_cents` records any PEG capital grant and defaults to 0. None of the four money figures may be negative. The pair of `fiscal_year` and `quarter` is a natural key: a second period for the same quarter of the same year is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` (409). A new period opens in `DRAFT` report status with `revision_round` at 0, with `allocable_base_cents`, `admin_reserve_cents`, `capital_allocation_cents`, `operating_allocation_cents` and `total_program_funds_cents` all at 0, and with `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason` all null. Periods may be listed filtered by `fiscal_year` or `report_status`. Already at creation, and again on every restatement, the capital grant is sanity-checked against the money it sits on top of: a `capital_grant_cents` exceeding the basis-selected allocable base is refused with `ERR_CAPITAL_GRANT_EXCEEDS_BASE` (402).

**The allocation is a two-by-two branch matrix**, and the same raw figures produce four different answers depending on which basis is in force and which reserve tier the recognised amount lands in. Step one selects the recognised amount: `allocable_base_cents` becomes `service_period_earned_cents` when the `basis` is `ACCRUAL`, and `remittance_received_cents` when the `basis` is `CASH`. The figure the basis did not select is retained on the record but plays no part in the allocation. Step two chooses the administrative reserve rate from a two-row tier table: the rate is 500 basis points — 5 percent — when `allocable_base_cents` is at or above 5000000 cents, and 800 basis points — 8 percent — below that threshold. Step three computes `admin_reserve_cents` by multiplying `allocable_base_cents` by the chosen basis-point rate, dividing by ten thousand, and rounding half up to a whole cent. Step four splits what remains: the distributable amount is `allocable_base_cents` less `admin_reserve_cents`; the capital share is that distributable amount multiplied by 3500 basis points, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up; `capital_allocation_cents` is that capital share plus `capital_grant_cents`; and `operating_allocation_cents` is the distributable amount less the capital share, taken as an exact residual so that no cent is created or lost. Step five sets `total_program_funds_cents` to `allocable_base_cents` plus `capital_grant_cents`. Whatever branch was taken, the three allocations always sum back to that total: `admin_reserve_cents` plus `capital_allocation_cents` plus `operating_allocation_cents` equals `total_program_funds_cents`.

The divergence between the two bases is worth seeing concretely. Take a period with `service_period_earned_cents` of 5000000, `remittance_received_cents` of 4800000 and a `capital_grant_cents` of 100000. Under `ACCRUAL` the allocable base is 5000000, which reaches the high tier, so the reserve rate is 5 percent: the administrative reserve is 250000, the distributable amount is 4750000, the capital share is 1662500 giving a capital allocation of 1762500, the operating allocation is 3087500, and the total is 5100000. Under `CASH` the allocable base is 4800000, which falls below the threshold, so the reserve rate is 8 percent: the administrative reserve is 384000, the distributable amount is 4416000, the capital share is 1545600 giving a capital allocation of 1645600, the operating allocation is 2870400, and the total is 4900000.

Submitting the municipal report is the `FinanceOfficer`'s act and drives `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. The basis-selected allocable base must be strictly positive; a period whose selected base is 0 or negative cannot be reported and is refused with `ERR_ALLOCABLE_BASE_NOT_POSITIVE` (402). Submission recomputes `allocable_base_cents`, `admin_reserve_cents`, `capital_allocation_cents`, `operating_allocation_cents` and `total_program_funds_cents` in the pinned order above, records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the period's `version` by 1.

Deciding a submitted report belongs to a `CenterAdmin`, who supplies `decision` — either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` — and, for a return, a `reason`. The decider may not be the submitter: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the period's `submitted_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` records `decided_by_user_id` and freezes the period's figures permanently; `ACCEPTED` is terminal and no further revision is accepted. `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` requires the `reason` to be present, records both `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason`, and sends the period back for rework. Either decision increments the period's `version` by 1.

Restatement closes the cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → DRAFT`. A `FinanceOfficer` restates a period by supplying `expected_funding_period_version` together with any subset of `basis`, `service_period_earned_cents`, `remittance_received_cents` and `capital_grant_cents`; whatever is supplied replaces the stored figure and whatever is omitted is left alone. Restatement is available both from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, where it drives the period back to `DRAFT`, and from `DRAFT` itself, where it is a pre-submission correction that leaves the period in `DRAFT`. Both forms raise `revision_round` by 1, re-run the whole allocation under the effective basis — which may itself have changed with this request — and increment the period's `version` by 1. The `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` form additionally resets `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, so the next submission records whoever actually submits it. Because a restatement legitimately supersedes the previous one, the concurrency guard of §2.7 is the only thing standing between two officers working from different snapshots: an `expected_funding_period_version` that does not equal the period's stored `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_FUNDING_PERIOD_VERSION` (412), and that check runs before the state check, so an officer holding a stale read is told to re-read rather than being told the period is in the wrong state.

### 3.9. Archive Retention, On-Demand Distribution and Community Impact

Archive assets are never created by a client. Exactly one is minted by the system when a slot airs, recording the `program_id` of the aired program and the `source_slot_id` of the airing it was captured from. At that moment three figures are frozen onto it: `measured_households` is copied from the slot, `volunteer_hours_tenths` is the sum of `credited_hours_tenths` over that slot's `COMPLETED` crew assignments, and `community_impact_index` is scored in its provisional form. The asset opens in `PENDING` on-demand status with `impact_variant` marked `PROVISIONAL`, with `on_demand_views` at 0, and with `retention_class`, `retain_until`, `published_at` and `purged_at` all null — a pending asset has no retention deadline at all. Assets may be read individually and listed filtered by `program_id`, `source_slot_id`, `on_demand_status` or `retention_class`.

**The community impact index** blends three normalised components, each capped at its own ceiling and clipped at 1. The reach component is `measured_households` divided by 1000, or 1 if that quotient exceeds 1. The demand component is `on_demand_views` divided by 500, again clipped at 1. The volunteer component is `volunteer_hours_tenths` divided by 100 — that is, ten volunteer hours saturate it — again clipped at 1. The index is 100 multiplied by the weighted sum of the three components, with the reach component weighted 0.5, the demand component 0.3 and the volunteer component 0.2, and the result rounded half up to two decimal places and emitted as a JSON number.

The two variants differ only in which inputs are live. The `PROVISIONAL` variant is what the system computes the instant a program airs, when no on-demand view has yet been recorded and no volunteer credit has yet been read: it forces both `on_demand_views` and `volunteer_hours_tenths` to 0, so only the reach component contributes. The `FINAL` variant recomputes with all three stored values and therefore rises for the same broadcast. A slot airing with 640 measured households, an occupied footprint of 1920 seconds and a single confirmed volunteer — who completes with a credit of 6 tenths of an hour — scores 32.00 provisionally. Recomputed as `FINAL` at publication, with views still at 0 and 6 volunteer tenths in hand, it scores 33.20. Once 250 on-demand views are recorded, it scores 48.20.

Publication is the `CenterAdmin`'s act and drives `PENDING → PUBLISHED`. It requires the asset to be `PENDING`, the program to be `APPROVED`, and the source slot to be `AIRED`. The caller supplies `retention_class`, choosing `STANDARD_24M`, `EXTENDED_60M` or `PERMANENT`. Publication stamps `published_at` with the current logical instant, computes `retain_until`, recomputes `community_impact_index` under the `FINAL` variant, sets `impact_variant` to `FINAL`, and increments the asset's `version` by 1.

**Retention is counted in whole calendar months, not in fixed-length days.** A `STANDARD_24M` asset retains for 24 calendar months and an `EXTENDED_60M` asset for 60; a `PERMANENT` asset has no retention months at all and its `retain_until` stays null. Where a month count applies, `retain_until` is `published_at` advanced by that many calendar months, keeping the same day-of-month and the same time-of-day; where the target month is too short to contain that day-of-month, the day clamps to that month's last day. An asset published on 6 August 2026 under `STANDARD_24M` therefore retains until 6 August 2028; the same publication under `EXTENDED_60M` retains until 6 August 2031.

Recording on-demand demand is likewise the `CenterAdmin`'s act: the caller supplies `on_demand_views` as the cumulative view total, which replaces the stored figure rather than adding to it, and the `FINAL` community impact index is immediately recomputed from it. This is accepted only while the asset is `PUBLISHED`; once the asset has moved to `WITHDRAWN` or `PURGED` it stops accepting view records and the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Each accepted recording increments the asset's `version` by 1.

Withdrawal of an asset is never requested directly. It arrives as a cascade when the underlying program is withdrawn: a `PUBLISHED` asset moves `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, stops accepting view records, and has its `retain_until` and `community_impact_index` frozen at their last values, with `version` incremented by 1.

Purging is the end of the line and is available to a `CenterAdmin` from either `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Two conditions gate it, and they carry different errors. An asset whose `retention_class` is `PERMANENT` can never be purged, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PERMANENT_RETENTION` (409). Otherwise the retention window must have elapsed, and the boundary is inclusive: the asset becomes purgeable at the `retain_until` instant itself, so a purge at one second before `retain_until` is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_ELAPSED` (409) while a purge at exactly `retain_until` succeeds. A successful purge stamps `purged_at` with the current logical instant, freezes `on_demand_views` and `community_impact_index` at their last values, and increments the asset's `version` by 1. `PURGED` is terminal.
