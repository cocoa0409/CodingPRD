# Thesis Format Review Deposit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every graduate degree in a research university passes through a narrow gate at the very end of the candidate's programme. Between the successful defense and the line that finally appears on a transcript sits a chain of clerical acts that nobody enjoys but everybody depends on: the examining committee has to be composed legally and approved by the dean, the defense has to be publicly announced far enough in advance for the announcement to mean anything, the manuscript has to survive a formatting review that is often iterated more than once, third-party figures and previously published chapters have to be documented, a plagiarism screen has to come back below the institution's tolerance, an embargo has to be ruled on where patents or publishers are involved, and only then may the document be deposited into the institutional repository with a citable handle and a complete metadata record. Graduate schools run this gauntlet on spreadsheets, email threads and paper routing slips, and the failure mode is always the same: a candidate is told in October that a permission letter was never filed in June, and the degree slips a whole term.

This document specifies the Thesis Format Review & Deposit API, the system of record the graduate school uses to run that gate. The API is mounted under `/graduate-school`. Its central design commitment is that **graduation clearance is derived, never asserted** — no operator ever sets a candidate's clearance status by hand. Clearance is recomputed from the underlying evidence on every mutation that could possibly change it, and the projection deliberately behaves differently depending on whether the term's deposit window is still open (in-flight evidence is credited so candidates can see where they stand) or already closed (only terminal evidence counts, so the registrar posts degrees against facts rather than intentions). The same discipline governs money and time: late fees, revision windows, announcement leads and embargo release dates are all computed from a single logical clock, not from the server's wall clock, so the whole term can be replayed deterministically.

A second commitment is segregation of duties. The graduate school's own staff are the ones who both judge and record, so the specification enforces that the analyst who signed the accepting format review may not be the analyst who performs the repository deposit, and the dean who approved a committee may not rule on that thesis's embargo. Where two writers can legitimately race on the same thesis — additional permission records, a replacement embargo request — the API demands that the client prove it read the current state of the thesis before it writes.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and revision semantics, the order in which a request is admitted or rejected, arithmetic precision, and the cross-cutting invariants that no single workflow owns.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles named below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not listed for the requested operation in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Student`, `FacultyMember`, `GradSchoolAnalyst`, `GraduateDean`, `IntegrityOfficer` and `Registrar`. A `Student` is the degree candidate: they own the thesis record, the committee proposal, format submissions, permission documentation and embargo requests. A `FacultyMember` serves on committees, and the confirmed chair is the one who records the defense outcome. A `GradSchoolAnalyst` is the graduate school's format analyst: they announce defenses, run format review cycles and plagiarism screenings, review permission documentation and perform the repository deposit. A `GraduateDean` rules on committee composition and on embargo duration. An `IntegrityOfficer` refers flagged screenings and dismisses or upholds referrals. A `Registrar` owns terms, rosters, rooms, the logical clock, the closing of the deposit window and the posting of degree conferral.

Two of the six roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Student` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In practice that means the acting student's identifier must equal the `student_id` of the thesis the request targets, whether the thesis is named directly in the body or reached through the `Committee`, `CommitteeMember`, `FormatReview` or `EmbargoRequest` named in the path; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `FacultyMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FacultyMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Recording a defense outcome additionally requires that this identifier be the `faculty_id` of the `CONFIRMED` `CHAIR` seat of the `APPROVED` committee of the thesis being defended; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_COMMITTEE_CHAIR` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `GradSchoolAnalyst`, `GraduateDean`, `IntegrityOfficer` or `Registrar`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `decided_by_user_id` on a `FormatReview` or an `EmbargoRequest`, as `approved_by_user_id` on a `Committee`, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `PermissionRecord`, as `referred_by_user_id` or `resolved_by_user_id` on an `IntegrityScreening`, or as `depositor_user_id` on a `RepositoryDeposit` — and it later compares those recorded strings against each other for the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.8, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100 is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list endpoint documents a different ordering in §4, results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Revision Counters and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — `Term`, `Student`, `FacultyMember`, `DefenseRoom`, `Thesis`, `Committee`, `CommitteeMember`, `DefenseEvent`, `FormatReview`, `EmbargoRequest`, `PermissionRecord`, `IntegrityScreening`, `RepositoryDeposit` and `GraduationClearance` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by a client. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or fell out of a cascade. Whenever a mutation writes any field of a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant and its `version` is incremented; a row that a cascade merely read but did not change keeps both.

All temporal reasoning in this API reads a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Deadlines, revision windows, business-day counts, announcement leads, late-fee lateness and every `*_at` timestamp are evaluated against it. Before the first time it is set, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `Registrar` may move it with the test-driving capability described in §4, which takes a single `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; setting the clock mutates no entity and fires no cascade.

### 2.5. Request Admission Order

When several rules could reject the same request, the answer the client receives is fixed: rules are evaluated in the order below, and the first one that fails determines the response. Text order in §3 does not imply evaluation order — this ladder does.

1. **Header presence and role legality** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization for the operation** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.
3. **Payload shape** — every required body field present, every value of the declared type, every enum value a legal member, every integer respecting its stated minimum, and `limit` inside 1 through 100: otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Path identifier resolution**, validated left to right along the URL so that the leftmost unresolvable segment is the one reported: `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THESIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORMAT_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMBARGO_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMISSION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND` are each HTTP 404.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution**, validated in the order the fields appear in the operation's input list, reporting `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THESIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACULTY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` according to which referenced record could not be found. This stage applies to the eight creating operations that carry a foreign key in the body: opening a thesis record, opening a committee record, seating a committee member, scheduling a defense, documenting a permission record, requesting an embargo, recording a screening, and creating a repository deposit.
6. **Student ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on the eleven operations a candidate may perform: opening a thesis record, opening a committee record, seating a committee member, releasing a committee seat, submitting the composition for approval, scheduling a defense, cancelling a defense, submitting a format review cycle, documenting a permission record, requesting an embargo, and supplementing an embargo request.
7. **Committee-chair identity** — `ERR_NOT_COMMITTEE_CHAIR`, on the recording of a defense outcome.
8. **Stale parent version** — `ERR_STALE_THESIS_VERSION`, as specified in §2.6.
9. **Deposit window still open** — `ERR_TERM_DEPOSIT_CLOSED`, as specified in §2.9.
10. **Term state matches the requested term transition** — `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`.
11. **Entity state preconditions**, one per governed entity: `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_COMMITTEE_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_DEFENSE_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_FORMAT_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_EMBARGO_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_PERMISSION_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`.
12. **Integrity gates** — first `ERR_SCREENING_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`.
13. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_DUTY_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION`.
14. **Domain business constraints**, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_THESIS_FOR_TERM`, `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_SEAT_CONFLICT`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_COMPOSITION_INVALID`, `ERR_ROOM_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_ROOM_BOOKING_CONFLICT`, `ERR_FACULTY_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.
15. **Temporal constraints**, in this order: `ERR_DEFENSE_START_IN_PAST`, `ERR_DEFENSE_AFTER_TERM_DEADLINE`, `ERR_ANNOUNCEMENT_LEAD_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_REVISION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
16. **Remaining constraints, in this order**: `ERR_DEFECT_SCORE_REQUIRES_REVISIONS`, `ERR_PERMISSION_EVIDENCE_MISSING`, `ERR_FAIR_USE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_EMBARGO_DURATION_EXCEEDS_CAP`, `ERR_PERMISSIONS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_EMBARGO_UNDECIDED`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_ALREADY_EXISTS`, `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`, and finally the fiscal check `ERR_LATE_FEE_MISMATCH`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are frequently guessed wrong. A repository deposit attempted while the thesis is under an integrity hold reports the integrity hold rather than the late fee, because stage 12 precedes stage 16. And a deposit attempted by the same analyst who accepted the format review reports the duty-segregation violation rather than any downstream metadata or fee problem, because stage 13 precedes stage 16.

### 2.6. Stale-Thesis Guards on Candidate Attachments

Two creating operations attach a new child row to a thesis without any state precondition that would notice a concurrent writer, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent `Thesis`.

A `POST /graduate-school/embargo-requests` request requires the non-optional input field `expected_thesis_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Thesis`. If that value does not equal the thesis's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_THESIS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because a new embargo request legitimately supersedes any undecided request for the same thesis; without the check, a client could bury a request it never saw.

A `POST /graduate-school/permission-records` request likewise requires the non-optional input field `expected_thesis_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Thesis`, and a value that does not equal the thesis's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_THESIS_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Multiple permission records per thesis are entirely legitimate, so no state precondition would ever block an additional create; the stale-version check is the only thing stopping a client from documenting third-party material against an outdated view of `documented_permission_count` and `third_party_item_count`.

No other operation in this API takes a version field. Both guarded creates increment the parent `Thesis.version` by one on success, which is precisely why a client that fires two of them back to back must re-read the thesis in between.

### 2.7. Business-Day Arithmetic, Precision and Rounding

A great deal of this specification is expressed in business days, and the definition is exact. A calendar date is a business day when it falls on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday and is not one of the institutional holidays, which are `2026-11-26`, `2026-11-27`, `2026-12-24`, `2026-12-25` and `2027-01-01`. All dates are interpreted in UTC, and the time-of-day component never affects a business-day count.

Two helpers are used throughout. **Counting business days between two instants** yields the number of business days strictly after the calendar date of the earlier instant and up to and including the calendar date of the later instant; if the later instant's calendar date is the same as or before the earlier instant's calendar date, the count is zero. **Advancing an instant by a number of business days** walks the calendar date forward one day at a time, counting only business days, until the requested number of business days has been counted, and returns an instant on that date preserving the original instant's time-of-day.

Rounding is always half-up, and each computed quantity has its own declared precision. The defect-density factor of a format review is carried to four decimal places; each severity subtotal and the final weighted defect score are carried to two decimal places. Adjusted similarity percentages are carried to two decimal places. The repository metadata completeness score is carried to four decimal places. Late fees are whole integer cents. Business-day counts and all committee, chair-load and room-minute counters are whole integers. Instants derived by arithmetic are carried to whole seconds. Where a threshold comparison is stated as "at least", exact equality passes; this is true of the announcement lead, of every committee composition minimum, of the metadata score floor and of both late-fee grace tiers.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties Inside the Graduate School

Because the graduate school both judges and records, two pairs of acts are forbidden to the same operator, and both are enforced with `ERR_DUTY_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The analyst who performs a repository deposit must not be the analyst whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `decided_by_user_id` on the `ACCEPTED` `FormatReview` of that thesis. The dean who rules on a thesis's embargo request must not be the dean whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `approved_by_user_id` on that thesis's `Committee`. Both comparisons are on the opaque operator strings described in §2.1, and both are evaluated at stage 13 of the admission ladder — before any metadata, duration or fee arithmetic runs.

### 2.9. The Term Envelope

A `Term` is the envelope inside which a cohort graduates, and it supplies every deadline a thesis obeys. Its `code` is unique across terms and matches a four-digit year followed by `SP`, `SU` or `FA`. `announcement_lead_business_days` is the minimum number of business days that must separate the announcement instant from the defense start; it defaults to 10 and must be at least 1. `defense_deadline` is the latest defense start allowed for theses in the term, inclusive. `format_review_deposit_deadline` is the base deposit deadline, on top of which grace business days are added when the late fee is computed. `degree_conferral_date` is the anchor instant used by the conferral-anchored embargo variant. `status` is one of `OPEN`, `DEPOSIT_CLOSED` or `CONFERRED`, begins at `OPEN`, and doubles as the selector for the graduation-clearance projection variant. `conferred_count` starts at 0 and records how many clearances reached `CONFERRED` during the term's conferral run.

While a term is `OPEN`, candidate-side work proceeds. Once it leaves `OPEN`, eighteen operations are refused with `ERR_TERM_DEPOSIT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409): opening a committee record, seating a committee member, releasing a committee seat, submitting the composition, ruling on the composition, scheduling a defense, announcing a defense, recording a defense outcome, cancelling a defense, submitting a format review cycle, deciding a format review, documenting a permission record, reviewing a permission record, requesting an embargo, ruling on an embargo, supplementing an embargo request, recording an integrity screening, and creating a repository deposit. Note carefully that **opening the thesis record itself is not on that list** — a candidate may still create a `Thesis` row against a term whose window has closed, and will simply find every subsequent step blocked.

The two term transitions are themselves state-guarded, and a mismatch is `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409): closing the deposit window requires `status` to be `OPEN`, and posting conferral requires `status` to be `DEPOSIT_CLOSED`. `CONFERRED` is terminal.

### 2.10. Derived Counters and Cascade Reconciliation

Six counters in this system are never written by a client; they are recomputed from scratch, from their source rows, every time anything that feeds them changes. `Committee.member_count` and `Committee.external_member_count` are recomputed on every seat creation or release and on every composition submission or ruling. `FacultyMember.active_chair_load` is recomputed on the same events plus the committee-dissolution cascade. `DefenseRoom.booked_minutes_total` is recomputed whenever a defense is scheduled, cancelled, or concluded, and whenever the committee-revert cascade cancels an occupation. `Thesis.format_cycle_count`, `Thesis.documented_permission_count` and `Thesis.analyst_turnaround_bdays_total` are recomputed on the events described in §3.5 through §3.7. `Term.conferred_count` is written by the conferral run. Recomputation is always a full re-derivation from the counted rows, never an increment applied to a stale value; that is what keeps the counters correct after a cascade has moved several rows at once.

Two of these counters are conserved quantities with compensating action pairs, and the system's consistency can be audited against them. Summing `active_chair_load` over every faculty member must always equal the number of `CONFIRMED` chair-or-co-chair seats whose committee is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; seating a chair and releasing that seat are exact inverses. Likewise, a room's `booked_minutes_total` after a schedule-then-cancel pair must equal what it was before the schedule.

Cascades in this system are immediate and complete within the request that triggers them. Where a single operation moves a parent and its dependents, every touched row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, and every derived counter downstream of those rows is recomputed before the response is written. Two cascades are deep enough to deserve their own narrative, and are specified in §3.3 (a membership change revoking a granted committee approval) and §3.6 (an upheld integrity finding terminating a thesis).

### 2.11. Two Independent State Dimensions on a Thesis

A `Thesis` row carries two state fields that evolve independently, and this is the single most important structural fact about the domain. `workflow_state` is the primary lifecycle dimension. It begins at `DRAFT` and takes one of `DRAFT`, `COMMITTEE_PENDING`, `COMMITTEE_APPROVED`, `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED`, `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`, `DEFENSE_PASSED`, `DEFENSE_PASSED_WITH_REVISIONS`, `DEFENSE_FAILED`, `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, `FORMAT_ACCEPTED`, `DEPOSITED`, `CONFERRED` or `TERMINATED`. Of these, `CONFERRED`, `DEFENSE_FAILED` and `TERMINATED` are terminal. The dimension is not a straight line: `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW` and `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED` form a revision cycle that may repeat, and both `COMMITTEE_APPROVED` and `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED` can revert to `COMMITTEE_PENDING` when the committee's composition changes.

`integrity_state` is the second dimension on the same row. It begins at `NOT_SCREENED` and takes one of `NOT_SCREENED`, `SCREEN_CLEARED`, `SCREEN_FLAGGED`, `UNDER_INTEGRITY_REVIEW`, `INTEGRITY_CLEARED` or `INTEGRITY_UPHELD`, with `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` terminal. It advances only through integrity screening events and is fully independent of `workflow_state`: a thesis can be screened while it is `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, `FORMAT_ACCEPTED` or `DEPOSITED`, and the two dimensions are observed, asserted and reported separately. The one place they couple is the integrity-upheld cascade, which drives `integrity_state` to `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` and simultaneously forces `workflow_state` to `TERMINATED`.

### 2.12. Nested Reads and Reverse Reachability

Mutating operations in this API address flat collections; nesting appears almost exclusively on the read side, where a parent path segment is used to reach the children that reference it. A nested read validates its path segments left to right, so a request naming a nonexistent thesis reports `ERR_THESIS_NOT_FOUND` before anything about the child collection is considered. Reverse-reachability reads exist for a thesis's committee, its format review cycles, its embargo requests, its permission records, its integrity screenings and its repository deposit, and for a student's graduation clearance; the single-row cases return an array of zero or one element rather than a bare object. Committee seats and defense events are reached instead through filtered flat lists, whose filters are described alongside the workflows that produce those rows.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a candidate's document from the registrar's term setup through to the transcript notation, and the rules that govern each step are stated where that step happens rather than collected into a schema block.

### 3.1. Registrar Groundwork: Terms, Rosters and Rooms

Before any candidate can act, a `Registrar` populates the reference data. Creating a graduation term takes its `code`, its `defense_deadline`, its `format_review_deposit_deadline` and its `degree_conferral_date`, and optionally an `announcement_lead_business_days` override; when that override is omitted the term is created with a lead requirement of 10 business days, and any supplied value must be at least 1. The `code` must be unique across all terms and must consist of a four-digit year immediately followed by `SP`, `SU` or `FA` — `2026FA` is well-formed, `26FA` and `2026WI` are not. A newly created term is `OPEN`, and its `conferred_count` starts at 0.

Registering a degree candidate takes `full_name`, `email`, `degree_level` and `program_code`. The address in `email` must be unique across all students. `degree_level` is either `MASTERS` or `DOCTORAL`, and it is load-bearing twice over: it drives the committee composition minimums and it halves the embargo duration caps. A freshly registered candidate has `graduation_clearance_state` of `BLOCKED`, `degree_conferred` false and `transcript_notation` null — the notation stays null until either a conferral or an integrity withhold writes it.

Registering a faculty member takes `full_name`, `email` and `appointment_type`, plus an optional `graduate_faculty_certified` flag that defaults to false. Faculty `email` addresses are likewise unique across faculty members. `appointment_type` is one of `TENURED`, `TENURE_TRACK`, `ADJUNCT` or `EXTERNAL`. A new faculty member begins with `active_chair_load` of 0.

Registering a bookable room takes `code`, `building` and `seat_capacity`, and optionally `turnover_buffer_minutes` and `status`. Room `code` values are unique across rooms; `seat_capacity` must be at least 1 and represents the seats available for the public announcement. `turnover_buffer_minutes` is the minimum clear gap that must separate two occupations of that room; it defaults to 30 and may be 0 or more. `status` is either `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE` and defaults to `AVAILABLE`. A new room begins with `booked_minutes_total` of 0.

### 3.2. Opening a Thesis or Dissertation Record

A candidate opens their document record by naming their own `student_id`, the `term_id` of the term they intend to graduate in, the `title`, the `document_type`, and the `page_count`. `document_type` is `THESIS` for a `MASTERS` candidate and `DISSERTATION` for a `DOCTORAL` candidate. `page_count` must be at least 1 and reappears twice later: it drives the defect density factor of every format review, and a long document earns extra revision time. The optional `third_party_item_count` declares how many included third-party items the candidate expects to document; it defaults to 0, may not be negative, and is what the permission-completeness gate later compares `documented_permission_count` against.

A candidate may hold only one live document per term. If the acting student already has another `Thesis` in the same term whose `workflow_state` is anything other than `DEFENSE_FAILED`, `TERMINATED` or `CONFERRED`, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_THESIS_FOR_TERM` (HTTP 409); a failed or terminated attempt therefore leaves the candidate free to start again in the same term.

On success the row is born with `workflow_state` `DRAFT` and `integrity_state` `NOT_SCREENED`, with `format_cycle_count`, `documented_permission_count` and `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total` all at 0, and with `accepted_at` null — that instant is written only when the document reaches `FORMAT_ACCEPTED`. If the candidate has no `GraduationClearance` row yet, one is created for them in `BLOCKED` with `projection_variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `outstanding_items` empty, `cleared_at`, `conferral_posted_at`, `transcript_notation` and `withheld_reason` all null.

The thesis list is filterable by `student_id`, `term_id` and `workflow_state`, and paginated with `limit` and `offset`; it doubles as the reverse-reachability read from a student or a term to the documents that reference them.

### 3.3. Composing the Examining Committee and Winning the Dean's Approval

Committee work begins with a single container row created against the document's `thesis_id`; at most one committee may exist per thesis. It is born `DRAFT`, with `member_count` and `external_member_count` at 0 and with `approved_by_user_id`, `approved_at` and `rejection_note` all null. Its `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `DISSOLVED`, the last of which is terminal and reachable only through a failed defense.

Seating a faculty member on the committee names the `faculty_id` of the person and the `role` they will occupy, with the `committee_id` taken from the path segment of the request. A seat's `role` is one of `CHAIR`, `CO_CHAIR`, `MEMBER`, `EXTERNAL_MEMBER` or `GRADUATE_REPRESENTATIVE`, and the seat is created with `status` `CONFIRMED` and `withdrawal_reason` null. Seats are soft-deleted rather than destroyed: releasing a seat sets its `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, records the supplied `withdrawal_reason`, and leaves the row queryable forever. A release is legal while the seat is `CONFIRMED` and the committee is `DRAFT`, `REJECTED`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; a seat that is already `WITHDRAWN`, or a committee that has reached `DISSOLVED`, is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The same identifier guards the other three committee operations whose source status is constrained: seating a member is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_STATE_INVALID` when the committee is `DISSOLVED`, submitting the composition when the committee is not `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, and the dean's ruling when the committee is not `SUBMITTED`. Seats are listed through a filtered collection accepting `committee_id`, `faculty_id` and `status` together with `limit` and `offset`; when no `status` filter is supplied, `WITHDRAWN` seats are excluded from the result.

Chairing is a privilege, not a default. A `CHAIR` or `CO_CHAIR` seat may only be given to a faculty member whose `appointment_type` is `TENURED` or `TENURE_TRACK`, whose `graduate_faculty_certified` flag is true, and whose current `active_chair_load` is strictly below four; violating any of the three is `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). That load is itself derived: a faculty member's `active_chair_load` is the number of `CommitteeMember` rows naming them whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, whose `role` is either `CHAIR` or `CO_CHAIR`, and whose committee's `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. Committees that are `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `DISSOLVED` contribute nothing, which is why a chair seated on a `DRAFT` committee carries no load at all until the composition is submitted.

Three further seat rules are enforced together and all report `ERR_COMMITTEE_SEAT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409): the same faculty member may not hold two `CONFIRMED` seats on one committee, a committee may not gain a second `CHAIR` seat, it may not gain a second `GRADUATE_REPRESENTATIVE` seat, and an `EXTERNAL_MEMBER` seat may be given only to a faculty member whose `appointment_type` is `EXTERNAL`.

Composition minimums are counted over `CONFIRMED` seats only — `WITHDRAWN` seats are excluded from every count. `member_count` is the number of `CONFIRMED` seats on the committee, and `external_member_count` is the number of those whose `role` is `EXTERNAL_MEMBER`. For a `DISSERTATION` the committee needs at least five members, at least one external member, exactly one `CHAIR` seat and exactly one `GRADUATE_REPRESENTATIVE` seat. For a `THESIS` the committee needs at least three members, exactly one `CHAIR` seat, and no `GRADUATE_REPRESENTATIVE` seat at all. Each minimum is inclusive: a five-member dissertation committee satisfies the five-member floor. A composition that misses any of these is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_COMPOSITION_INVALID` (HTTP 422), and that check runs twice — once when the candidate submits the composition, and again when the dean approves it, because seats can move in between.

Submitting the composition is legal while the committee is `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` and the document is `DRAFT` — a document in any other `workflow_state` is refused with `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A resubmission of a rejected composition leaves the previous `rejection_note` in place for audit. Submission moves the committee to `SUBMITTED` and advances the document from `DRAFT` to `COMMITTEE_PENDING`, recomputes `active_chair_load` for every `CONFIRMED` chair and co-chair seat — the committee has just entered the counted status set — and recomputes the candidate's clearance.

The dean's ruling supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `rejection_note`, and is legal only while the committee is `SUBMITTED`. An `APPROVE` moves the committee to `APPROVED`, captures the acting dean's `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id` and the current logical instant as `approved_at`, advances the document from `COMMITTEE_PENDING` to `COMMITTEE_APPROVED`, and recomputes clearance — under both projection variants the committee-approval item is now satisfied. A `REJECT` moves the committee to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied note as `rejection_note`, sends the document back from `COMMITTEE_PENDING` to `DRAFT`, recomputes `active_chair_load` for every `CONFIRMED` chair and co-chair seat because `REJECTED` has left the counted set, and recomputes clearance. The pair `COMMITTEE_PENDING → DRAFT → COMMITTEE_PENDING` is a genuine cycle: a rejected composition can be reworked and resubmitted as often as necessary.

Seat changes while a decision is pending are unremarkable. On a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` committee, adding or releasing a seat simply recomputes `member_count` and `external_member_count`, and chair load stays at zero because those statuses lie outside the counted set. On a `SUBMITTED` committee, adding or releasing a seat recomputes `member_count`, `external_member_count` and `active_chair_load` for every affected chair or co-chair, and the committee remains `SUBMITTED` — no revert cascade fires, because there is no granted approval to revoke.

Seat changes on an `APPROVED` committee are a different matter, and this is the deepest cascade in the system. When a seat is added to or released from an `APPROVED` committee while the document is `COMMITTEE_APPROVED`, `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED` or `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`, four things happen in order. First, the committee falls back from `APPROVED` to `SUBMITTED` and both `approved_by_user_id` and `approved_at` are cleared to null — the dean's grant is void because the body it applied to no longer exists. Second, because the committee is no longer `APPROVED`, the document reverts to `COMMITTEE_PENDING`. Third, because the document has left `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED` or `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`, every `DefenseEvent` for it whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ANNOUNCED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to the fixed string `COMMITTEE_COMPOSITION_CHANGED`. Fourth, because those occupations have left the counted set, `booked_minutes_total` is recomputed for every affected room. The candidate's clearance and the `active_chair_load` of every chair and co-chair seat are recomputed as part of the same request, and because the document is once again in a pre-approval state the committee-approval clearance item is no longer satisfied.

### 3.4. Booking, Announcing and Holding the Defense

Once the dean has approved the composition, the candidate — or an analyst acting on their behalf — books the room and fixes the date. The request names the `thesis_id` of the document, the `room_id` of the room to occupy, and the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` instants of the occupation. Scheduling is legal only while the document is `COMMITTEE_APPROVED` and its committee is `APPROVED`; a document in any other `workflow_state` is refused with `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID`, and a committee in any other status with `ERR_COMMITTEE_STATE_INVALID`.

Six substantive checks then run in the order given here. The room must not be in `MAINTENANCE`, and the occupation length — `scheduled_end` minus `scheduled_start` — must be at least 30 and at most 240 whole minutes; either failure is `ERR_ROOM_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). The room must be free, which in this institution means more than merely non-overlapping: a room's occupations must be separated by a clear turnover gap of at least its `turnover_buffer_minutes`. Concretely, the new occupation conflicts with an existing one when the new start is earlier than the existing end advanced by the buffer **and** the existing start is earlier than the new end advanced by the buffer. A gap of exactly the buffer is therefore legal, while a gap one minute shorter conflicts; a conflict is `ERR_ROOM_BOOKING_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Only occupations whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ANNOUNCED`, `HELD_PASS`, `HELD_PASS_WITH_REVISIONS` or `HELD_FAIL` are considered — a `CANCELLED` occupation never conflicts with anything.

The faculty availability check uses deliberately weaker arithmetic than the room check, and the difference is intentional rather than an oversight. Every faculty member holding a `CONFIRMED` seat on the new document's committee is examined, and for each of them the system looks at all defense events whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ANNOUNCED` and whose document's committee also seats that faculty member as `CONFIRMED`. Those intervals are treated as half-open with no buffer at all: a conflict exists when the new start is earlier than the existing end **and** the existing start is earlier than the new end. Exact adjacency is consequently legal — a faculty member may finish one defense at eleven o'clock and begin another at eleven o'clock — whereas the same pair of intervals in the same room would violate the buffered room rule. A faculty conflict is `ERR_FACULTY_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Two temporal guards close the set. `scheduled_start` must be strictly after the logical clock, so a defense may not be booked into the past or at the present instant; otherwise `ERR_DEFENSE_START_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). And `scheduled_start` must be no later than the term's `defense_deadline`, which is inclusive — a defense starting exactly at the deadline instant is accepted, one starting a second later is refused with `ERR_DEFENSE_AFTER_TERM_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422).

A successful booking creates the event in `status` `DRAFT`, with `announced_at`, `announcement_lead_bdays`, `outcome_note` and `cancellation_reason` all null, advances the document from `COMMITTEE_APPROVED` to `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED`, recomputes the booked room's `booked_minutes_total`, and recomputes the candidate's clearance. That room counter is the sum, in whole minutes, of the durations of all events on that room whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ANNOUNCED`, `HELD_PASS`, `HELD_PASS_WITH_REVISIONS` or `HELD_FAIL`; cancelled occupations are excluded. Defense events are listed through a filtered collection accepting `thesis_id`, `room_id` and `status` with `limit` and `offset`, ordered by `scheduled_start` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending.

Publishing the announcement is an analyst's act and carries no body at all. It is legal while the event is `DRAFT` and the document is `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED`; an event in any other status is refused with `ERR_DEFENSE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The gate is the announcement lead, measured as the number of business days between the current logical instant and the event's `scheduled_start`, and the announcement is refused when that count is fewer than the term's `announcement_lead_business_days`; a lead of exactly the required number of business days passes. A short lead is `ERR_ANNOUNCEMENT_LEAD_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). On success the event moves `DRAFT → ANNOUNCED`, `announced_at` is set to the current logical instant, the computed lead is frozen onto the event as `announcement_lead_bdays`, the document advances `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED → DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`, and clearance is recomputed — an announced but unheld defense satisfies the defense-outcome item under the provisional projection but not under the final one.

The defense itself is recorded by the committee chair, who supplies an `outcome` and an optional `outcome_note`. The act is legal only while the event is `ANNOUNCED` and the document is `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`, and only once the logical clock has reached or passed the event's `scheduled_end`; an early recording, like any illegal source status, is `ERR_DEFENSE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The `outcome` forks into three branches whose side effects genuinely differ.

- With `outcome` of `PASS`, the event moves `ANNOUNCED → HELD_PASS` and the document moves `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED → DEFENSE_PASSED`. The committee stays `APPROVED`, no chair load changes, and clearance is recomputed with the defense-outcome item now satisfied under both projection variants.
- With `outcome` of `PASS_WITH_REVISIONS`, the event moves `ANNOUNCED → HELD_PASS_WITH_REVISIONS` and the document moves `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED → DEFENSE_PASSED_WITH_REVISIONS`. The committee stays `APPROVED`, no chair load changes, and clearance is recomputed with the defense-outcome item satisfied under both variants exactly as in the `PASS` branch. The two branches differ only in the state the document carries forward, which both the final clearance projection and the transcript record surface.
- With `outcome` of `FAIL`, the event moves `ANNOUNCED → HELD_FAIL` and the document moves `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED → DEFENSE_FAILED`, which is terminal. This is the only branch that touches two further entities: the committee is moved from `APPROVED` to `DISSOLVED`, and because a dissolved committee has left the counted status set, `active_chair_load` is recomputed for every `CONFIRMED` chair and co-chair seat on it. Clearance is then recomputed; since the document is terminal, the clearance settles at `PENDING_ITEMS` with the format-acceptance, integrity and deposit items still outstanding.

Cancelling a defense is available to an analyst or to the candidate and requires a `cancellation_reason`. It is legal while the event is `DRAFT` or `ANNOUNCED` and the document is `DEFENSE_SCHEDULED` or `DEFENSE_ANNOUNCED`; cancelling an event that is already `CANCELLED` or already held is refused with `ERR_DEFENSE_STATE_INVALID`. Either source pair produces the same result: the event moves to `CANCELLED` with the supplied reason stored, the document falls back to `COMMITTEE_APPROVED`, the room's `booked_minutes_total` is recomputed now that the occupation has left the counted set, and clearance is recomputed. Cancellation is the exact compensating action for booking, which is why the room counter returns to precisely the value it held before the booking.

### 3.5. The Format Review Cycle, the Permission Dossier and the Revision Loop

A candidate who has passed the defense — in either the `DEFENSE_PASSED` or the `DEFENSE_PASSED_WITH_REVISIONS` sense — submits the manuscript for format review. The submission carries no body: the document is identified by the path and everything else is derived. It is legal only while the document is `DEFENSE_PASSED`, `DEFENSE_PASSED_WITH_REVISIONS` or `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED` — any other `workflow_state` is refused with `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the governing term is still `OPEN`. Each submission opens a new `FormatReview` row in `status` `PENDING_REVIEW` whose `cycle_number` is one greater than the document's current `format_cycle_count` — so the first submission is cycle 1 — and whose `opened_at` is the current logical instant. Any existing `FormatReview` for the same document whose `status` is `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, keeping its recorded score and turnaround for audit. The document's `format_cycle_count` is then recomputed as the number of its review rows, which is always the `cycle_number` of the newest one, the document moves to `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, and clearance is recomputed — a merely `PENDING_REVIEW` cycle satisfies the format-acceptance item under the provisional projection but not under the final one. A new cycle is born with `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decision`, `revision_due_at` and `turnaround_bdays` all null, with `critical_defect_count`, `major_defect_count` and `minor_defect_count` at 0, and with `severity_weighted_defect_score` at 0.00. Review cycles are read back for a document in ascending `cycle_number` order.

When the document is in `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, resubmission carries one extra condition: the logical clock must be at or before the `revision_due_at` of that document's `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` cycle. A resubmission after that instant is refused with `ERR_REVISION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409), which is what makes the `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW → FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED → UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW` loop bounded rather than infinite; `cycle_number` strictly increases with every turn of it, so the loop is always observable in the record.

Before an analyst can accept anything, the permission dossier has to be complete. Each included third-party item is documented separately, and the create names the `thesis_id`, the `material_type`, the `rights_basis`, a `page_reference` locating the material in the document, and the `expected_thesis_version` described in §2.6; `excerpt_word_count` and `document_uri` are optional. `material_type` is one of `FIGURE`, `TABLE`, `EXCERPT`, `PREVIOUSLY_PUBLISHED_CHAPTER` or `DATASET`. `rights_basis` is one of `WRITTEN_PERMISSION`, `FAIR_USE_ASSERTION`, `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` or `OPEN_LICENSE`. `excerpt_word_count` defaults to 0 and may not be negative; `document_uri`, which points at the signed permission letter, defaults to null. Two evidence rules apply at creation. When the claimed `rights_basis` is `WRITTEN_PERMISSION`, `document_uri` must be a non-empty string, or the create is refused with `ERR_PERMISSION_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (HTTP 422). When the claimed basis is `FAIR_USE_ASSERTION`, the reused `excerpt_word_count` may not exceed 400 words, or the create is refused with `ERR_FAIR_USE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Documentation is accepted while the document's `workflow_state` is anything other than `DEFENSE_FAILED`, `DEPOSITED`, `CONFERRED` or `TERMINATED`; those four are refused with `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID`. A new record is born `SUBMITTED` with `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` null, increments the parent document's `version` by one, and triggers a recompute of `documented_permission_count` and of clearance.

An analyst then rules on each record with a `decision` and an optional `review_note`. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves the record to `ACCEPTED`; `DEFICIENT` moves it to `DEFICIENT`; `WAIVE` moves it to `WAIVED`. `ACCEPT` and `WAIVE` are legal from either `SUBMITTED` or `DEFICIENT`, whereas `DEFICIENT` is legal only from `SUBMITTED` — marking an already-deficient record deficient again, or reviewing a record that has reached the terminal `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, is refused with `ERR_PERMISSION_RECORD_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Every ruling captures the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`, then recomputes the document's `documented_permission_count` — the count of its permission records whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, so a `DEFICIENT` record contributes nothing and a `WAIVED` one counts as fully documented — and recomputes clearance.

The analyst's format decision is where the arithmetic lives. The request supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN_REVISIONS`, the three defect tallies `critical_defect_count`, `major_defect_count` and `minor_defect_count` — each a whole number of zero or greater — and an optional `review_note`. It is legal only while the target cycle is `PENDING_REVIEW`; any other cycle status is `ERR_FORMAT_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The tallies feed a **severity-weighted defect score** whose evaluation order is pinned, because rounding at a different point produces a different number. First, a density factor is obtained by taking the greater of the document's `page_count` and 50, dividing it by 100, and rounding the quotient half-up to four decimal places — so a very short document is never credited with a density below one half, and a 100-page document has a factor of exactly 1.0000. Second, three severity subtotals are formed **and each is rounded half-up to two decimal places on its own**: the critical subtotal is the `critical_defect_count` multiplied by 3.4 and then by the density factor, the major subtotal is the `major_defect_count` multiplied by 1.65 and then by the density factor, and the minor subtotal is the `minor_defect_count` multiplied by 0.35 and then by the density factor. Third, the three already-rounded subtotals are added and the sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places to give `severity_weighted_defect_score`. Rounding once at the end instead of per severity is not the sanctioned order and yields a different published score.

A `RETURN_REVISIONS` decision needs nothing beyond a valid `PENDING_REVIEW` cycle. It moves the cycle `PENDING_REVIEW → REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, records `decision` as `RETURN_REVISIONS`, sets `decided_at` to the current logical instant and `decided_by_user_id` to the acting analyst's `X-User-ID`, writes the computed `severity_weighted_defect_score`, writes `turnaround_bdays` as the number of business days between `opened_at` and `decided_at`, recomputes the document's `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total`, moves the document `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW → FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, and recomputes clearance. It also computes the deadline the candidate must beat. The base revision window is 10 business days for cycle 1, 7 business days for cycle 2, and 5 business days for cycle 3 or any later cycle; a long document — one whose `page_count` is at least 300 — earns 2 additional business days on top of whichever base applies. The decision instant is advanced by the sum of the base window and the long-document bonus, and `revision_due_at` becomes the **earlier** of that result and the term's `format_review_deposit_deadline`. The comparison is on the full instant, so when the term deadline is the earlier of the two the stored `revision_due_at` carries the term deadline's time-of-day rather than the decision's.

An `ACCEPT` decision is guarded four ways beyond the cycle status, and the guards are evaluated in the admission order of §2.5. The document must have been screened at all: an `integrity_state` of `NOT_SCREENED` is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). The document must not be under an integrity cloud: an `integrity_state` of `SCREEN_FLAGGED`, `UNDER_INTEGRITY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), which leaves `SCREEN_CLEARED` and `INTEGRITY_CLEARED` as the only admissible integrity states. Every permission record for the document must have reached `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, and `documented_permission_count` must be at least `third_party_item_count`; a still-`SUBMITTED` or `DEFICIENT` record, or a shortfall against the declared item count, is refused with `ERR_PERMISSIONS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). And the computed `severity_weighted_defect_score` must be strictly below 12.00 — a score of exactly 12.00 already mandates revisions — otherwise the acceptance is refused with `ERR_DEFECT_SCORE_REQUIRES_REVISIONS` (HTTP 422). When all four pass, the cycle moves `PENDING_REVIEW → ACCEPTED` with `decision` recorded as `ACCEPT`, `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` written, the score and `turnaround_bdays` written, `revision_due_at` left null, `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total` recomputed, the document advanced `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW → FORMAT_ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` set to the current logical instant, and clearance recomputed with the format-acceptance item satisfied under both variants.

The `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total` on a document is the sum of `turnaround_bdays` over every one of its review cycles that has a non-null `decided_at` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` or `SUPERSEDED`. Because a superseded cycle keeps the turnaround it was decided with, the total is a running measure of how long the graduate school itself took across all decided cycles — and that total is what buys the candidate grace against the deposit deadline in §3.8. Cycles that are still `PENDING_REVIEW`, and cycles voided by a termination cascade, contribute nothing.

### 3.6. Plagiarism Screening, Integrity Referral and the Termination Cascade

Screening runs on a document that is already in the format-review pipeline. An analyst records a result by naming the `thesis_id` and the raw `similarity_pct` reported by the screening tool, optionally together with `excluded_quoted_pct`, the share of that similarity attributable to correctly quoted matter. Both percentages are carried to two decimal places and must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive; `excluded_quoted_pct` defaults to 0. Recording is legal only while the document's `workflow_state` is `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, `FORMAT_ACCEPTED` or `DEPOSITED` — note that a deposited document may still be screened, which is exactly how a post-deposit finding becomes possible. Recording is additionally refused with `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) when any screening for that document is already `REFERRED` or `UPHELD`: an open or concluded referral must be resolved by the integrity office rather than papered over with a fresh screen.

The **adjusted similarity** is the raw `similarity_pct` less `excluded_quoted_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and floored at 0.00 so that an over-generous exclusion can never produce a negative figure. The institution's tolerance is 18.00 per cent: the screening resolves to `FLAGGED` when `adjusted_similarity_pct` is at least 18.00 and to `CLEARED` when it is below, so exactly 18.00 flags. The row is created in the transient `status` `SCREENED` and auto-resolves to `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` inside the same request — no client ever observes a `SCREENED` row — with `screened_at` set to the current logical instant and `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note` all null. Any earlier screening for the same document whose `status` is `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, so exactly one screening ever carries the governing result. The document's `integrity_state` then follows: `SCREEN_CLEARED` on a cleared result, `SCREEN_FLAGGED` on a flagged one. Because re-screening after revision is permitted, this dimension is itself cyclic — `NOT_SCREENED → SCREEN_FLAGGED → SCREEN_CLEARED` after a successful rewrite, and `SCREEN_CLEARED → SCREEN_FLAGGED` when a later screen crosses the threshold, are all legal paths. Every screening recomputes the candidate's clearance: a cleared state satisfies the integrity item under both projection variants, a flagged state satisfies it under neither. Screenings are read back for a document in ascending `created_at` order.

The integrity office acts on screenings with an `action` and an optional `resolution_note`, which is stored on the row for any of the three actions. An `action` of `REFER` is legal only while the screening is `FLAGGED` and the document's `integrity_state` is `SCREEN_FLAGGED`; it moves the screening `FLAGGED → REFERRED`, writes `referred_at` as the current logical instant and `referred_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, drives `integrity_state` to `UNDER_INTEGRITY_REVIEW`, and recomputes clearance with the integrity item now unsatisfied. An `action` of `DISMISS` is legal only while the screening is `REFERRED`; it moves the screening `REFERRED → DISMISSED`, writes `resolved_by_user_id`, drives `integrity_state` to `INTEGRITY_CLEARED`, and recomputes clearance with the integrity item satisfied again. Any action attempted from a status that does not admit it is `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`.

An `action` of `UPHOLD` is the gravest act in the system. It is legal only while the screening is `REFERRED` and the document's `workflow_state` is not already one of the terminal `CONFERRED`, `DEFENSE_FAILED` or `TERMINATED`; in practice the document will be `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED`, `FORMAT_ACCEPTED` or `DEPOSITED`. It cascades in four linked steps, all inside the one request.

1. The screening moves `REFERRED → UPHELD` with `resolved_by_user_id` set to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`.
2. Because the finding stands, the document's `integrity_state` becomes the terminal `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` and its `workflow_state` becomes the terminal `TERMINATED`, whichever of the admissible source states it was in.
3. Because the document is now `TERMINATED`, every one of its format review cycles whose `status` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` is moved to `VOID`, and any `RepositoryDeposit` for it whose `status` is `DEPOSITED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN`. A voided `PENDING_REVIEW` cycle keeps its null `decided_at`, so it never contributes to `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total`; a voided `REVISIONS_REQUIRED` cycle retains the `decided_at` and `turnaround_bdays` it had already recorded. A withdrawn deposit keeps its `handle` and `embargo_release_date` for audit but stops satisfying the deposit clearance item. This is the only path by which an existing deposit is ever withdrawn.
4. Because the deposit was withdrawn and the document terminated, the candidate's `GraduationClearance` moves to `WITHHELD` with `withheld_reason` set to the fixed string `ACADEMIC_INTEGRITY_FINDING` and `transcript_notation` set to the fixed string `Degree withheld pending academic integrity resolution`, and `cleared_at` is reset to null if the clearance had previously been `CLEARED`. The change is then mirrored onto the candidate: `graduation_clearance_state` becomes `WITHHELD`, `transcript_notation` is set to the clearance's notation, and `degree_conferred` is set back to false.

### 3.7. Embargo Requests, Duration Caps and Release Anchoring

A candidate whose work is patentable or under a publisher's agreement asks for the document to be withheld from open access. The request names the `thesis_id`, a `reason_code`, the `requested_months` and a narrative `justification`, together with the `expected_thesis_version` guard of §2.6. It is legal only while the document's `workflow_state` is `DEFENSE_PASSED`, `DEFENSE_PASSED_WITH_REVISIONS`, `UNDER_FORMAT_REVIEW`, `FORMAT_REVISIONS_REQUIRED` or `FORMAT_ACCEPTED`; anything earlier or later is `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID`. `reason_code` is one of `PATENT_PENDING`, `PUBLISHER_AGREEMENT`, `SENSITIVE_DATA` or `HUMAN_SUBJECTS`, and `requested_months` is a whole number of months of at least 1.

The statutory basis selects the ceiling, and the candidate's `degree_level` halves it. For a `DOCTORAL` candidate the caps are two years for `PATENT_PENDING`, one year for `PUBLISHER_AGREEMENT`, three years for `SENSITIVE_DATA` and one year for `HUMAN_SUBJECTS` — that is, 24, 12, 36 and 12 months respectively. For a `MASTERS` candidate each cap is halved and any fraction discarded, giving 12, 6, 18 and 6 months. A `requested_months` above the applicable cap is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_DURATION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422); a request for exactly the cap is accepted.

A new request is created in `status` `REQUESTED`, with `approved_months` null, `info_request_count` at 0, and `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `release_date`, `release_anchor` and `decision_note` all null. Creating it moves every existing request for the same document whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `MORE_INFO_REQUIRED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and each superseded row keeps its accumulated `info_request_count` for audit. The create increments the parent document's `version` by one and recomputes clearance. Requests are read back for a document in ascending `created_at` order.

The dean rules with a `decision`, an optional `approved_months` and an optional `decision_note`, and every ruling is subject to the segregation rule of §2.8 — a dean whose `X-User-ID` matches the committee's `approved_by_user_id` for that document is refused with `ERR_DUTY_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION`. All four decisions require the request to be `REQUESTED`; a decision on a request in any other status is `ERR_EMBARGO_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

- `APPROVE` moves the request to the terminal `APPROVED`, sets `approved_months` to the `requested_months` the candidate asked for, writes `decided_at` as the current logical instant and `decided_by_user_id` as the acting dean's `X-User-ID`, and recomputes clearance with the embargo-decision item satisfied.
- `PARTIALLY_APPROVE` moves the request to the terminal `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` and sets `approved_months` from the body. The body value must be present, at least 1, and strictly less than `requested_months` — a "partial" approval for the full duration, for zero months, or for a longer duration is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_DURATION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422). `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` are written and clearance is recomputed exactly as in the `APPROVE` branch.
- `DENY` moves the request to the terminal `DENIED` and sets `approved_months` to 0, writes `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id`, and recomputes clearance; a denied request no longer blocks the deposit.
- `REQUEST_INFO` moves the request `REQUESTED → MORE_INFO_REQUIRED`, increments `info_request_count` by one, records the supplied `decision_note`, and deliberately leaves `decided_at` null because nothing has been decided. Clearance is recomputed and the embargo-decision item remains outstanding under both projection variants.

The candidate answers an information request by supplying a replacement `justification`, which is legal only while the request is `MORE_INFO_REQUIRED` — a supplement against a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_STATE_INVALID` — and returns it to `REQUESTED` with the old narrative overwritten. That closes the information loop, and the loop may repeat: each further push-back increments `info_request_count` again, so the record shows how many rounds the ruling took.

Whether an approved embargo actually restricts access, and until when, is settled when the document is deposited. The **governing request** for a document is the one whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` with the greatest `created_at`, ties broken by the higher `version`. Where a governing request exists, its `release_date` and `release_anchor` are written at deposit time from a month-arithmetic projection, and the anchor differs by branch. When the governing request was fully `APPROVED`, the release date is the deposit instant advanced by `approved_months` calendar months and `release_anchor` is recorded as `DEPOSIT` — the clock starts the moment the document lands in the repository. When the governing request was only `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, the release date is instead the term's `degree_conferral_date` advanced by `approved_months` calendar months and `release_anchor` is recorded as `CONFERRAL`, on the reasoning that a shortened embargo is measured from the public conferral date; identical `approved_months` and an identical deposit instant therefore yield a *different* release date under the two branches. Advancing an instant by whole months adds the months to the calendar month, carrying into the following year each time the running month index passes December, and clamps the day of month to the last day of the target month when the original day does not exist there — so a 31 January anchor advanced one month lands on 28 or 29 February. The time-of-day of the anchor is preserved to whole seconds. When no governing request exists, no release date is projected at all.

### 3.8. Repository Deposit, Handle Assignment and Fee Settlement

Deposit is the act that puts the accepted document into the institutional repository, and it is performed by an analyst other than the one who accepted it. The request names the `thesis_id`, the `abstract_word_count`, the `subject_keywords` array and the `late_fee_paid_cents` the candidate is tendering, and may additionally supply `requested_access_level`, `advisor_name` and `department_code`. The document must be `FORMAT_ACCEPTED`, or the attempt is `ERR_THESIS_STATE_INVALID`.

Seven substantive gates then apply, in the admission order of §2.5. An `integrity_state` of `NOT_SCREENED` is `ERR_SCREENING_REQUIRED`, and an `integrity_state` of `SCREEN_FLAGGED`, `UNDER_INTEGRITY_REVIEW` or `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` is `ERR_INTEGRITY_HOLD_ACTIVE`. A depositing analyst whose `X-User-ID` equals the `decided_by_user_id` recorded on that document's `ACCEPTED` format review is refused with `ERR_DUTY_SEGREGATION_VIOLATION` — the same person may not both accept and deposit. An incomplete permission dossier, meaning any permission record not yet `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, or a `documented_permission_count` below `third_party_item_count`, is `ERR_PERMISSIONS_INCOMPLETE`. An embargo request for the document still sitting at `REQUESTED` or `MORE_INFO_REQUIRED` is `ERR_EMBARGO_UNDECIDED` (HTTP 409) — the repository will not publish a document whose access posture is unsettled. A document that already has a deposit with `status` `DEPOSITED` is `ERR_DEPOSIT_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409); because an integrity finding withdraws rather than deletes a deposit, a `WITHDRAWN` row does not block a later attempt. Finally the metadata score and the fee are checked, in that order.

The **metadata completeness score** is a weighted count of the six metadata elements the repository wants, expressed as a fraction of the total weight of 1.00. The abstract contributes 0.30 when `abstract_word_count` is at least 150 words. The keywords contribute 0.20 when `subject_keywords` holds at least three entries. The advisor contributes 0.15 when `advisor_name` is a non-empty string, and the department contributes 0.15 when `department_code` is a non-empty string. The candidate's `degree_level`, read from the student record, always contributes 0.10, and the document's `document_type`, read from the thesis record, always contributes 0.10. The contributions are summed, divided by the total weight of 1.00, and rounded half-up to four decimal places to give `metadata_completeness_score`. The repository requires at least 0.8500, so exactly 0.8500 passes and anything lower is refused with `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Since degree level and document type are always present, the floor is met by supplying the abstract and keywords plus at least one of advisor or department, or by supplying advisor and department plus the abstract.

The **late fee** is settled at the same moment, and its evaluation order is pinned because the grace allowance looks circular: the grace is earned from the graduate school's own turnaround, it shifts the deadline, and the tempting second reading also subtracts it from the resulting lateness. Grace is applied exactly once, at the point where it moves the deadline. First, the document's `analyst_turnaround_bdays_total` is taken. Second, a grace allowance is selected from it: 5 business days when the total is at least 15 business days, otherwise 3 business days when the total is at least 10 business days, otherwise none. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a total of exactly 15 lands in the higher tier. Third, an effective deadline is obtained by advancing the term's `format_review_deposit_deadline` by the grace allowance in business days. Fourth, the days late are counted as the number of business days between the effective deadline and the current logical instant when the clock is strictly past the effective deadline, and zero otherwise. Fifth, the chargeable days are exactly those days late — the grace was already consumed in shifting the deadline and is **not** subtracted a second time. Sixth, the fee is the chargeable days multiplied by 2,500 cents per day, capped at 50,000 cents, which is reached at twenty chargeable days. The tendered `late_fee_paid_cents` must equal that computed figure exactly; any other amount, high or low, is refused with `ERR_LATE_FEE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 402).

A successful deposit creates the row with `status` `DEPOSITED`, `deposited_at` set to the current logical instant, `depositor_user_id` set to the acting analyst's `X-User-ID`, and the computed `metadata_completeness_score` and `late_fee_cents` stored on it. Its `handle` is the citable repository identifier, formed as the term's `code`, a slash, and a five-digit zero-padded sequence number; the sequence is assigned in ascending `created_at` order over the `DEPOSITED` rows whose document belongs to the same term, ties broken by the lower `version`, so the first deposit of `2026FA` receives `2026FA/00001`. The `access_level` is `EMBARGOED` whenever an `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` embargo request exists for the document, and otherwise takes the `requested_access_level` supplied on the request, which defaults to `OPEN_ACCESS` when omitted; the three legal values are `OPEN_ACCESS`, `CAMPUS_ONLY` and `EMBARGOED`. Where the level is `EMBARGOED`, the governing request's `release_date` and `release_anchor` are written as described in §3.7 and the release date is copied onto the deposit as `embargo_release_date`; where it is not, `embargo_release_date` stays null. The document then advances `FORMAT_ACCEPTED → DEPOSITED`, the room and committee counters are untouched, and clearance is recomputed — the deposit item is now satisfied, so if no other outstanding item remains the clearance becomes `CLEARED` with `cleared_at` set to the current logical instant.

Candidates and analysts can rehearse all of this without writing anything. A read-only readiness projection replays the clearance evaluation and the late-fee arithmetic against the current logical clock and reports `thesis_id`, the current `workflow_state`, the `projection_variant` that would apply, the `outstanding_items` array, the `projected_late_fee_cents` a deposit would incur right now, and `next_blocking_error_id` — the identifier of the error the next deposit attempt would raise, or null when the deposit would succeed. It mutates nothing and fires no cascade.

### 3.9. Graduation Clearance, Closing the Window and Degree Conferral

`GraduationClearance` is the one entity in this system nobody edits. There is at most one row per candidate, keyed by `student_id`, and its `status`, `outstanding_items`, `projection_variant` and `cleared_at` are rewritten from scratch by a projection every time anything the projection reads changes — which is to say, on every mutation described in §3.2 through §3.8, plus the two term-level runs described below. When a candidate has more than one document, the projection reads the one with the greatest `created_at`, ties broken by the higher `version`, together with that document's committee, defense events, permission records, format reviews, embargo requests, integrity screenings and repository deposit.

The projection tests seven items and emits into `outstanding_items` only those that are **unsatisfied**, always in the fixed order `COMMITTEE_APPROVAL`, `DEFENSE_OUTCOME`, `PERMISSIONS`, `FORMAT_ACCEPTANCE`, `EMBARGO_DECISION`, `INTEGRITY`, `DEPOSIT` — never sorted by any other key. `COMMITTEE_APPROVAL` is satisfied when the committee is `APPROVED`. `DEFENSE_OUTCOME` is satisfied when a defense event with `status` `HELD_PASS` or `HELD_PASS_WITH_REVISIONS` exists. `PERMISSIONS` is satisfied when the document's `third_party_item_count` is zero, or when the number of its permission records whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED` is at least that count. `FORMAT_ACCEPTANCE` is satisfied when a format review with `status` `ACCEPTED` exists. `EMBARGO_DECISION` is satisfied when no embargo request for the document is `REQUESTED` or `MORE_INFO_REQUIRED`. `INTEGRITY` is satisfied when the document's `integrity_state` is `SCREEN_CLEARED` or `INTEGRITY_CLEARED`. `DEPOSIT` is satisfied when a repository deposit with `status` `DEPOSITED` exists.

Which evidence counts depends on the term, and this is the dual-variant heart of the design. The `projection_variant` is `PROVISIONAL` while the term is `OPEN` and `FINAL` once the term is `DEPOSIT_CLOSED` or `CONFERRED`. Under `PROVISIONAL`, in-flight evidence is credited: a `SUBMITTED` committee also satisfies `COMMITTEE_APPROVAL`, an `ANNOUNCED` defense also satisfies `DEFENSE_OUTCOME`, permission records still in `SUBMITTED` also count toward the permission total, a `PENDING_REVIEW` format review also satisfies `FORMAT_ACCEPTANCE`, and an `integrity_state` of `NOT_SCREENED` also satisfies `INTEGRITY`. `EMBARGO_DECISION` and `DEPOSIT` are tested identically under both variants. Under `FINAL`, only terminal evidence counts: the committee must be `APPROVED`, the defense `HELD_PASS` or `HELD_PASS_WITH_REVISIONS`, the permission records `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`, the format review `ACCEPTED`, and the integrity state `SCREEN_CLEARED` or `INTEGRITY_CLEARED`. For identical data the provisional list is therefore always a subset of the final list, and a candidate who looked `CLEARED` all term can legitimately fall back to `PENDING_ITEMS` the moment the window closes.

`status` is then assigned by a ladder in which the first matching branch wins. It is `WITHHELD` if the document's `integrity_state` is `INTEGRITY_UPHELD` or its `workflow_state` is `TERMINATED`; otherwise `CONFERRED` if the document's `workflow_state` is `CONFERRED`; otherwise `CLEARED` if `outstanding_items` is empty; otherwise `BLOCKED` if the document's `workflow_state` is `DRAFT` or `COMMITTEE_PENDING`; otherwise `PENDING_ITEMS`. Each recompute mirrors the resulting status onto the candidate's `graduation_clearance_state`. `cleared_at` is set to the current logical instant at the moment `outstanding_items` first becomes empty and is reset to null whenever a previously satisfied item regresses — including the regression caused by the variant switching to `FINAL`, and including the fall from `PENDING_ITEMS` back to `BLOCKED` when a revert cascade pushes the document back into `DRAFT` or `COMMITTEE_PENDING`. The five legal statuses are `BLOCKED`, `PENDING_ITEMS`, `CLEARED`, `CONFERRED` and `WITHHELD`, `CONFERRED` being terminal, and the candidate's mirrored `graduation_clearance_state` takes the same five values.

Closing the deposit window is a registrar act, legal only while the term is `OPEN`. It moves the term `OPEN → DEPOSIT_CLOSED` and then re-runs the projection with the `FINAL` variant for every clearance whose candidate owns a document in that term, processing them in ascending order of the documents' `created_at`. Each such clearance has its `projection_variant` set to `FINAL` and its `outstanding_items` and `status` rewritten from the final evaluation, and each affected candidate's `graduation_clearance_state` is refreshed. Because the variant is a function of the term's status, this run is the reconciliation point of the whole dual-variant design: everything provisional becomes final at one instant, in one deterministic order.

Posting conferral is the last registrar act and is legal only while the term is `DEPOSIT_CLOSED`. Every document in the term is processed in ascending `created_at` order, ties broken by the lower `version`, and for each one the projection is re-run under the `FINAL` variant. Where the result is `CLEARED`, the clearance moves `CLEARED → CONFERRED`, the document moves `DEPOSITED → CONFERRED`, the candidate's `degree_conferred` becomes true, `conferral_posted_at` is set to the current logical instant, and the conferral notation is written to the clearance's `transcript_notation` and mirrored onto the candidate's `transcript_notation`. Where the document is `TERMINATED` or its `integrity_state` is `INTEGRITY_UPHELD`, the clearance moves to `WITHHELD` instead, carrying the withhold reason and notation. Where neither applies, the clearance is left as the final projection computed it. The term's `conferred_count` is then set to the number of clearances that reached `CONFERRED` in this run, and the term itself moves `DEPOSIT_CLOSED → CONFERRED`, which is terminal. The run answers with the `term_id` together with `conferred_count`, `withheld_count` and `pending_count` — the three tallies of candidates conferred, withheld and left with items outstanding.

The transcript notation itself is an exact string, built two different ways. On conferral, it begins with the document's `document_type` — the literal `THESIS` or `DISSERTATION` — followed by a space, the word `deposited`, a space, and the calendar date of the deposit's `deposited_at` rendered as four-digit year, hyphen, two-digit month, hyphen, two-digit day in UTC. If the deposit carries a non-null `embargo_release_date`, a semicolon and a space are appended, then the phrase `access restricted until`, a space, and that release date rendered in the same year-month-day form; if it does not, the notation ends after the deposit date. So a doctoral candidate whose embargoed dissertation was deposited on 3 September 2026 with a release date of 3 September 2028 receives `DISSERTATION deposited 2026-09-03; access restricted until 2028-09-03`, while an unembargoed one receives `DISSERTATION deposited 2026-09-03`. On an integrity withhold the notation is instead the fixed sentence `Degree withheld pending academic integrity resolution`, as specified in §3.6.
