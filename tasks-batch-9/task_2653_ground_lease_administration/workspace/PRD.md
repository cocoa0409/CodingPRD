# Ground Lease Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An institutional ground lessor holds land for a century at a time and rents it to developers who build on it. Almost nothing about that relationship is static: the base rent is re-struck every few years against an open market, a share of the tenant's turnover flows back as percentage rent, the tenant borrows against its leasehold and its lender acquires protective rights of its own, buildings are altered and redeveloped inside a fixed development envelope, interests are assigned and sublet for a fee, fires and highway takings redirect insurance money and condemnation awards through a settled order of priority, and at the very end the improvements revert to the landowner against a condition standard agreed decades earlier. Every one of those events is currently tracked in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and a filing cabinet of paper amendments, which is why arrears reconciliations disagree between the asset-management and accounting desks and why an estoppel certificate handed to a lender occasionally certifies a rent figure that was superseded the previous afternoon.

This document specifies the Ground Lease Administration API, the system of record that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is mounted under `/portfolio`, with a single test-driving clock endpoint outside that mount. It is deliberately not a document management system: it models the *economic and procedural state* of each ground lease — two independent status axes per lease, a negotiation state machine per revaluation, protective-rights state per leasehold mortgage, and a strict ordering of who may do what and in what sequence. Because ground leases are adversarial instruments, the service also enforces segregation of duties: the administrator who serves a notice of default may not be the one who terminates on it, and the arbitrator who fixes a rent may not have appraised it.

Three design commitments run through the whole specification. First, **money and area are conserved**: the deposit ledger, the development envelope and the proceeds waterfall each maintain an exact invariant, and every compensating action restores the prior figures precisely rather than approximately. Second, **time is explicit**: every deadline, lapse and expiry is evaluated against a server-side logical clock, never against wall-clock time, so a lender diligencing a portfolio can reproduce any figure. Third, **stale reads are refused rather than silently accepted**: writes that certify or price against a lease's current figures must pin the lease version they observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Parties, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent, empty, or carries a role string that is not one of the six defined roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The same code is returned when the role is well-formed but is not listed among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called.

The six roles are `LessorAdmin`, `AssetManager`, `LeaseAccountant`, `GroundTenant`, `MortgageeAgent` and `Arbitrator`, spelled exactly as shown.

A `LessorAdmin` is the lessor's lease-administration desk. It papers lessees, leases and written amendments, records leasehold mortgages, opens rent revaluations, exchanges appraisals, opens further negotiation rounds, settles a revaluation at the midpoint, refers a revaluation to arbitration, drives the administration sweep and sets the logical clock; it shares with others the ability to lodge the lessor-side appraisal, file insurance endorsements and issue estoppel certificates, and it may read every resource. An `AssetManager` owns the consent and condition side of the portfolio: deciding alteration requests, revoking alteration approvals, deciding transfer requests, adjusting loss events, opening reversion assessments, recording reversion inspections and closing reversions are its alone; it shares the ability to serve default notices, terminate a lease on an uncured default, report loss events and apply reversion holdbacks, and it may read every resource. A `LeaseAccountant` owns the ledger: posting rent charges, assessing percentage-rent statements provisionally, auditing those statements, allocating loss proceeds and rescinding an allocation are exclusive to it; it shares default service, termination on an uncured default and holdback application, and it may read lease, statement, transfer, notice and loss-event resources. A `GroundTenant` is the tenant of record: filing percentage-rent statements, applying for alterations, requesting transfer consent, paying transfer fees, curing its own default and making the casualty restoration-or-termination election are exclusive to it; it shares lodging the lessee-side appraisal, filing insurance endorsements and reporting loss events, and it may read its own lease's resources. A `MortgageeAgent` services a leasehold mortgagee and exclusively elects and completes a mortgagee cure and elects a new lease after termination; it shares estoppel issuance and may read lease, mortgage, notice, loss-event and certificate resources. An `Arbitrator` is the neutral appointed on a referred revaluation: issuing the arbitration award is its only write, and it may read the lease, the revaluation, its appraisal submissions and the proceeding.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks depend on them. When `X-User-Role` is `GroundTenant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Lessee`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Lessee` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a `GroundTenant` acting on a lease whose `lessee_id` is not its own `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. When `X-User-Role` is `LessorAdmin`, `AssetManager`, `LeaseAccountant`, `MortgageeAgent` or `Arbitrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `served_by_user_id` and `terminated_by_user_id` on a default notice, as `decided_by_user_id` on an alteration request, as `inspected_by_user_id` on a reversion assessment, as `issued_by_user_id` on an estoppel certificate, and as `appraiser_user_id` on an appraisal submission — but it never compares those values to any entity's `id`.

Two further authorisation refusals are role-shaped rather than endpoint-shaped. Segregation of duties yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when the `X-User-ID` terminating a lease on a default notice is the same identifier that served that notice, when the `X-User-ID` applying a reversion holdback equals the `inspected_by_user_id` already recorded on that assessment, or when a revaluation is referred to an `arbitrator_user_id` that matches the `appraiser_user_id` of any appraisal already lodged on that revaluation. And because the two sides of a revaluation must be represented by their own appointees, lodging an appraisal whose `appointed_by` is `LESSOR` while acting as `GroundTenant`, or whose `appointed_by` is `LESSEE` while acting as `LessorAdmin`, is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_SIDE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection reads are paginated with two query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when absent, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless an endpoint's description states another ordering, every list endpoint returns rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the leasehold-mortgage list, which reflects recording priority and is therefore ordered by `priority_rank` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending.

List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and carry the four pagination fields in `meta`: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is still below `total_count`). Single-resource reads set `response_type` to `"object"` and omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Version Counter

Every resource the service creates is addressed by `id`, a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. `created_at` records the instant of creation and `updated_at` the instant of the most recent mutation; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

One entity is deliberately leaner than the rest. An `AppraisalSubmission` is immutable once lodged — an opinion of value is never revised, only superseded by a later round — so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its response payload. Every other entity in the system — `Lessee`, `Lease`, `LeaseholdMortgage`, `RentRevaluation`, `ArbitrationProceeding`, `PercentageRentStatement`, `AlterationRequest`, `TransferRequest`, `DefaultNotice`, `LossEvent`, `ReversionAssessment` and `EstoppelCertificate` — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Because a single lease is the hub of the model, its `version` moves often. It is incremented by exactly 1 whenever the lease row itself is written: by a terms amendment, by filing an insurance endorsement, by posting a rent charge, by a resolved revaluation writing back the new base rent, by a statement filing and by each of the two assessment passes, by an alteration approval or approval revocation moving the envelope, by a transfer activating, by a default notice being served or cured, by a termination, by a loss-proceeds allocation or its rescission, by a reversion holdback, by a reversion close, by an estoppel issuance, and by the sweep expiring the lease. Implementers should treat "the lease was written" and "the lease `version` advanced by one" as the same statement.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Deadline Arithmetic

Nothing in this service reads wall-clock time for business purposes. A server-side **logical clock** supplies "now" to every deadline, lapse, expiry, window and chronology comparison described in this document. Before any client has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host clock. A test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and sets the clock to it, echoing back `{ current_utc }`; setting the clock performs no state transitions of its own, so moving time forward does not by itself lapse a cure window or expire a lease — the scheduled administration sweep does that work.

Deadline comparisons are exact and their inclusivity is fixed. Cure deadlines and the new-lease election deadline are **inclusive**: an action attempted at exactly the deadline instant still succeeds, and only an action attempted strictly after the deadline is refused. The sweep's lapse rules are the mirror image and are **strict**: a window is lapsed only when now is strictly after the deadline, so at exactly the deadline instant the window is still open. Comparisons are made on the full ISO 8601 UTC instant, never on the calendar date, and whole-day arithmetic means adding that number of 24-hour days to the origin instant.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points, Areas and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer **cents**; there are no fractional cents and no floating-point money anywhere in a request or a response. All rates, shares and scores expressed as proportions are integer **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is one whole. Areas are integer square feet. A request carrying a negative money amount, a negative area, a basis-point figure below 0 or above 10,000, or a condition score outside the inclusive range 0 to 100 is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) before any business rule is consulted.

Every division performed anywhere in this specification rounds **half up** to the nearest integer of the target unit — to whole cents for money, to whole basis points for proportions. Where a computation sums a series of per-row figures, the rounding happens **on each row first and the rounded figures are then added**; summing raw values and rounding once is a different number and is wrong. Where a computation both derives a figure and then caps or floors it, the cap or floor is applied at the step the workflow narrative names and not earlier.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Lease-Derived Writes

Two writes in this system exist purely to record what the client believed the lease said at the moment it acted, and both therefore require the client to pin the lease version it observed. Filing a percentage-rent statement requires `expected_lease_version` as a non-optional field of the request body. If that value does not equal the target lease's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard is necessary because a new statement for a period legitimately supersedes an earlier statement still in `SUBMITTED` for the same period, so no state precondition stops the second filing — but the base-rent offset credit is derived from the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents`, and a stale view of the lease would assess the wrong figure.

Issuing an estoppel certificate carries the identical guard: `expected_lease_version` is a non-optional field of the request body, and a value that does not equal the lease's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Issuance is legitimately repeatable — a fresh certificate supersedes the prior one for the same lease and mortgage — so nothing in the state machine stops a second issuance. But a certificate's entire purpose is to certify the figures the requester observed, so a lease whose version has advanced since the client last read it must fail rather than certify superseded rent and arrears. Note that both of these writes themselves increment the lease `version`, which means a client issuing two certificates in succession must re-read the lease between them.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field of any kind. Where the specification says an operation re-checks a condition "in force at decision time", that is a re-evaluation against live data, not a version comparison.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Many operations are guarded by several rules at once, and which error a caller sees is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed priority order, and the **first** rule that fails determines the response; later rules are not consulted.

1. **Referential existence.** Every foreign key carried by a write must name an existing row: `lessee_id`, `lease_id`, `revaluation_id`, `mortgage_id`, `parent_transfer_id`, `predecessor_lease_id`, `requested_by_mortgage_id`, `transferee_lessee_id`, `new_lessee_id`, and every `{id}` path segment. Each parent type has its own code — `ERR_LESSEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MORTGAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALTERATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOSS_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND` — each carrying HTTP 404. This check runs on every write that carries a foreign key, and on every read addressed by an identifier.
2. **Authorisation.** The role in `X-User-Role` must appear in the endpoint's authorised-role list; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This applies to every endpoint with a non-empty authorised-role list, which is every endpoint in the system.
3. **Body shape.** Nothing else is read until the payload is well-formed: unknown enum values, negative money or area, condition scores outside 0 to 100, basis points outside 0 to 10,000, malformed ISO 8601 instants and missing required fields all yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This applies to every write endpoint.
4. **Version pinning.** `expected_lease_version` must equal the lease's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION`. This applies to exactly two endpoints: percentage-rent statement filing and estoppel certificate issuance.
5. **Uniqueness.** `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD` (HTTP 409) guards seven creates: one appraisal submission per revaluation, side and round; one open revaluation per lease; one arbitration proceeding per revaluation; one reversion assessment per lease; one `premises_unit_code` among a lease's active subleases; one `priority_rank` per lease's mortgages; and a globally unique `Lessee.legal_name`. The endpoints guarded are appraisal lodgement, revaluation opening, arbitration referral, reversion-assessment opening, transfer-request creation, mortgage recording and lessee registration. A lease's `premises_code` is likewise unique across every lease whose `lease_status` is not `TERMINATED` and not `REVERTED`, and a colliding execution is refused with the same code.
6. **State.** The source entity must sit in a state for which the operation is declared. Every action endpoint carries this check, and a violation yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — including a repeat of an action already applied and any attempted move out of a terminal state.
7. **Lease operativeness.** A lease whose `lease_status` is `TERMINATED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVERTED` accepts no new revaluations, statements, alteration requests, transfer requests, mortgages or default notices; those six creates are refused with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_OPERATIVE` (HTTP 409). Only reversion assessments, loss-event allocation and estoppel certificates remain available on a non-operative lease.
8. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` guards three operations: terminating a lease on a default notice, applying a reversion holdback, and referring a revaluation to an arbitrator who has already appraised it.
9. **Cross-entity guards.** `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409) guards four operations. A revaluation may not be referred to arbitration while the lease's `lease_status` is `IN_DEFAULT`. A transfer request may not be consented to while the lease's `rent_status` is `IN_ARBITRATION`. An alteration may not be approved while a `CASUALTY` loss event on the lease sits in `REPORTED` or `ADJUSTED`. An estoppel certificate may not be issued while any revaluation on the lease sits in `ARBITRATION_REFERRED`.
10. **Interval integrity.** `ERR_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) guards four operations — alteration-request creation, alteration decision, transfer-request creation and statement filing — and is detailed in §2.9.
11. **Chronology.** `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422) guards revaluation opening, statement filing and lessee cure, and is detailed in §2.9.
12. **Cure windows.** `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) guards lessee cure, mortgagee cure (both the election and the completion) and the mortgagee's new-lease election.
13. **Development envelope.** `ERR_GFA_ENVELOPE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) guards the alteration decision.
14. **Sublet area.** `ERR_SUBLET_AREA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) guards the transfer decision.
15. **Money reconciliation.** `ERR_AMOUNT_NOT_RECONCILED` (HTTP 422) guards loss-proceeds allocation, the reversion holdback and transfer-fee payment.
16. **Terms coherence.** `ERR_TERMS_INCOHERENT` (HTTP 422) guards lease execution and terms amendment.
17. **Permitted use.** `ERR_USE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422) guards alteration-request creation and the alteration decision.
18. **Insurance sufficiency.** `ERR_INSURANCE_DEFICIENT` (HTTP 409) guards estoppel issuance, the alteration decision and the adjustment of a casualty loss event.

Where a single endpoint appears at several priorities — the alteration decision appears at 9, 10, 13, 17 and 18 — the ordering above is what a caller observes. An alteration approval on a lease with an open casualty, an overlapping window and an insufficient builders-risk limit reports `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED`, because that check sits highest.

### 2.9. Windows, Periods and Chronology

Three kinds of interval appear in the model and they share one convention: **every interval requires its start to be strictly before its end**, and a start that is at or after its end is a chronology violation reported as `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION`.

Construction windows on alteration requests are treated as **half-open** — the interval runs from `construction_window_start` inclusive up to `construction_window_end` exclusive. Two windows on the same lease that merely touch, where one window's end instant equals the next window's start instant, do **not** overlap and are both permitted; any genuine intersection between a proposed window and an already-approved window on the same lease is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Sublease occupancy is checked the same way: a sublease term runs from `term_start` up to `term_end` for overlap purposes, touching endpoints are allowed, and two sublease terms on the same lease and the same `premises_unit_code` may not intersect. The turnover buffer the lessor requires between successive subleases of one unit is zero days, so back-to-back subleases of the same unit are permitted with no gap. Statement periods run from `period_start` up to `period_end` and may not intersect the period of any other statement on the same lease that is not `VOID`.

Four chronology rules are enforced beyond the start-before-end requirement. A revaluation's `valuation_date` must be at least 180 days after the logical clock's current instant; a valuation date inside that 180-day advance-notice window is refused. A statement's `period_end` must not be after the logical clock's current instant — a tenant cannot file for a period that has not closed. A statement's `period_start` must be strictly before its `period_end`. A lessee cure must carry a `payment_at` at or after the notice's `served_at`; a payment instant preceding service is refused. Finally, a sublease's term must sit inside the lease term, and a sublease term that escapes the lease's `commencement_date`-to-`expiration_date` span is refused with the same chronology code.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities

Four quantities in this system are invariants: they are exact, they never drift, and every compensating action restores them precisely.

The **development envelope** of a lease satisfies `gfa_allocated_sqft` plus `gfa_unallocated_sqft` equal to `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` at all times. `gfa_allocated_sqft` is the sum of `requested_gfa_sqft` over the lease's alteration requests in status `APPROVED`; `gfa_unallocated_sqft` is `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` less `gfa_allocated_sqft` and is re-derived, never stored independently, whenever either term moves.

The **security deposit** of a lease satisfies `deposit_held_cents` plus `deposit_applied_cents` plus `deposit_refunded_cents` equal to `security_deposit_cents` at all times. At execution the whole deposit sits in `deposit_held_cents`, with `deposit_applied_cents` and `deposit_refunded_cents` both zero; a reversion holdback moves cents from held to applied, and a reversion close moves whatever remains from held to refunded.

The **proceeds pool** of a loss event satisfies `allocated_arrears_cents` plus `allocated_mortgagee_cents` plus `allocated_lessee_cents` plus `allocated_lessor_cents` equal to `distributable_pool_cents` exactly, with no rounding remainder unaccounted for, because the lessor's residual bucket absorbs every cent the first three buckets did not take.

The **transfer fee** of a consented transfer satisfies `total_transfer_fee_cents` equal to `transfer_fee_gross_cents` plus `admin_charge_cents` plus `late_surcharge_cents`, and a transfer only activates once `transfer_fee_paid_cents` has reached `total_transfer_fee_cents`.

### 2.11. The Two Status Axes of a Lease, and Cascade Discipline

A `Lease` carries two independent state machines on the same row. The **tenancy axis** is `lease_status`, which begins at `ACTIVE` and moves among `ACTIVE`, `IN_DEFAULT`, `TERMINATED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVERTED`, with `TERMINATED` and `REVERTED` terminal. The **rent-setting axis** is `rent_status`, which begins at `BASE_ONLY` and moves among `BASE_ONLY`, `REVALUATION_DUE`, `REVALUATION_OPEN`, `IN_ARBITRATION`, `RESET_APPLIED` and `FROZEN`, with `FROZEN` terminal.

The two axes evolve independently. A lease may sit in `IN_DEFAULT` on the tenancy axis while its rent axis sits in `IN_ARBITRATION`, and curing the default returns the tenancy axis to `ACTIVE` without touching the rent axis at all. The single point of coupling runs one way only: when the tenancy axis reaches `TERMINATED` or `EXPIRED`, the rent axis is driven to `FROZEN` from whichever of `BASE_ONLY`, `REVALUATION_OPEN`, `IN_ARBITRATION` or `RESET_APPLIED` it was occupying. Nothing on the rent axis ever moves the tenancy axis.

Cascades are stated exhaustively in the workflow narratives of §3 and must be applied in full. Two general rules govern them. First, when an operation mutates more than one entity, every affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by one — the cascade is not a silent write. Second, a cascade that recomputes an aggregate always recomputes it from the current member set rather than adjusting it incrementally: when a sublease leaves the active set, the lease's `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` is recomputed over whatever subleases remain active, which for an emptied set means zero.

### 2.12. Nested Paths and Resource Resolution

Several endpoints address a child through its parent. Path parameters are validated **left to right**: the outer `{id}` is resolved first, and if it names no row the request fails with that parent's not-found code before any inner segment or body field is examined. A revaluation opened under a lease that does not exist reports `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, not a revaluation error.

Parent-child membership is also checked, not merely existence. An estoppel certificate whose `requested_by_mortgage_id` names a real mortgage that encumbers a *different* lease is refused with `ERR_MORTGAGE_LEASE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and a mortgagee cure whose `mortgage_id` names a mortgage that is not on the notice's lease is refused with the same code. Where a resource is reachable both as a nested create under its parent and as a flat read at its own collection, the two views describe the same row: a revaluation opened beneath a lease is subsequently read, filtered and acted on at the revaluation collection, and the same is true of alteration requests, default notices, loss events, leasehold mortgages and reversion assessments.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Papering the Estate: Tenants, Terms, Insurance and the Rent Ledger

Before any land can be let, the counterparty must exist as a record. A `LessorAdmin` registers a ground tenant or a prospective transferee by supplying its registered `legal_name` and the `notice_email` that becomes its address of record for lease notices, optionally together with a `credit_rating_code`. That grade is the lessor's own internal credit scale — `AAA`, `A`, `BBB` or `SPECULATIVE` — and it defaults to `BBB` when the registration omits it. Legal names are globally unique across the tenant register, so a second registration of a name already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. The tenant register can afterwards be listed and narrowed by `credit_rating_code`.

Executing a ground lease is the single densest write in the system, because it fixes economics that will govern for decades. The `LessorAdmin` supplies the tenant as `lessee_id`, the ground parcel as `premises_code`, the term as `commencement_date` and `expiration_date`, the opening rent as `initial_annual_base_rent_cents`, the revaluation cadence as `revaluation_interval_years`, the two collar bounds as `reval_collar_min_uplift_bps` and `reval_collar_max_uplift_bps`, the turnover rent terms as `percentage_rent_rate_bps` and `percentage_rent_breakpoint_cents`, the use clause as `permitted_use_code`, the development envelope as `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`, the two agreed valuations as `improvements_value_cents` and `reversion_land_value_cents`, the end-of-term condition standard as `reversion_standard_code`, and the deposit as `security_deposit_cents`. The opening rent must be at least 1 cent and the revaluation interval at least 1 whole year. The collar floor must be at least 0 basis points and the collar ceiling must be at least as large as the floor; the percentage-rent rate and its breakpoint must each be at least 0; the envelope must be at least 1 square foot; and the improvements value, the reversion land value and the deposit must each be at least 0.

Four coherence rules are then applied to the terms as a set, and any failure is reported as `ERR_TERMS_INCOHERENT`: `reval_collar_min_uplift_bps` must be **strictly** less than `reval_collar_max_uplift_bps`, `percentage_rent_rate_bps` must fall between 100 and 2,000 basis points inclusive, `expiration_date` must be strictly after `commencement_date`, and `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` must be at least 1. A `premises_code` may be reused only once the earlier lease on that parcel has been fully wound up: it must be unique across every lease whose `lease_status` is neither `TERMINATED` nor `REVERTED`.

On acceptance the lease is seeded deliberately. `current_annual_base_rent_cents` is set equal to `initial_annual_base_rent_cents`. `next_revaluation_date` is set to `commencement_date` advanced by `revaluation_interval_years` whole years. `deposit_held_cents` takes the full `security_deposit_cents` while `deposit_applied_cents` and `deposit_refunded_cents` are both 0. `gfa_allocated_sqft` starts at 0 and `gfa_unallocated_sqft` therefore equals `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`. `arrears_balance_cents` and `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` both start at 0. The lease opens on the tenancy axis at `ACTIVE` and on the rent axis at `BASE_ONLY`. Insurance starts out non-compliant: `insurance_status` is `LAPSED`, `property_all_risk_limit_cents` and `builders_risk_limit_cents` are both 0, `lessor_additional_insured` is false, and `insurance_expires_at` is null until an endorsement is filed. Both lineage pointers, `predecessor_lease_id` and `origin_mortgage_id`, are null on a lease executed directly; they are written only on a lease minted by a mortgagee's new-lease election, and the lease list can be narrowed by either of them as well as by `lessee_id`, `lease_status` and `rent_status`.

Terms move over the life of a lease by written amendment, which is a `LessorAdmin` capability. An amendment may carry any subset of `permitted_use_code`, `reval_collar_min_uplift_bps`, `reval_collar_max_uplift_bps`, `percentage_rent_rate_bps`, `percentage_rent_breakpoint_cents`, `revaluation_interval_years`, `expiration_date`, `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`, `improvements_value_cents`, `reversion_land_value_cents` and `reversion_standard_code`. Only the fields actually supplied are overwritten; the rest are left exactly as they were. Because the envelope total may have moved, `gfa_unallocated_sqft` is re-derived from the new `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` less the unchanged `gfa_allocated_sqft`, and the amendment increments the lease `version` by one. The same four coherence rules are re-applied to the post-amendment terms, so an amendment that inverts the collar or pushes the percentage-rent rate outside the 100-to-2,000 basis-point band is refused with `ERR_TERMS_INCOHERENT`. Amendments have consequences elsewhere: an amendment that changes the use clause can retroactively invalidate an alteration request that was compliant when submitted, and an amendment that shrinks the envelope narrows the room available to future alterations.

Insurance compliance is filed rather than inferred, and either a `LessorAdmin` or the `GroundTenant` may file it. The filing carries `property_all_risk_limit_cents`, `builders_risk_limit_cents`, `lessor_additional_insured` and an `expires_at` instant. All four are written straight onto the lease, with `expires_at` landing in `insurance_expires_at`. `insurance_status` then becomes `ACTIVE` when `expires_at` is **strictly after** the logical clock's current instant, and `LAPSED` otherwise — filing an endorsement that has already expired records the endorsement but leaves the lease non-compliant. The filing increments the lease `version` by one.

The ledger side is the `LeaseAccountant`'s. Posting a rent or other monetary charge carries an `amount_cents` figure and an optional free-text `memo`, and adds `amount_cents` to the lease's `arrears_balance_cents`, incrementing the lease `version` by one. Arrears are debited by charges and by percentage-rent assessments, and credited by cure payments and by the arrears bucket of a proceeds allocation; the balance is the single figure a monetary default is measured against. A computed rent-summary read exists for lenders and for internal diligence: it reports `current_annual_base_rent_cents`, `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents`, `arrears_balance_cents`, `gfa_allocated_sqft`, `gfa_unallocated_sqft`, `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents`, an `active_sublease_count` giving the number of subleases on the lease currently in status `ACTIVE`, and an `open_mortgage_principal_cents` giving the summed `outstanding_principal_cents` of the lease's mortgages that have not been extinguished or replaced by a new lease.

### 3.2. Leasehold Mortgages, the Protective Register and Estoppel Certification

Developers finance ground leases by mortgaging the leasehold estate, and the lease grants those lenders rights the lessor must honour. A `LessorAdmin` records a leasehold mortgage against a lease by supplying the lender's `mortgagee_legal_name`, the `notice_email` at which protective notices will be served, the `original_principal_cents` advanced at recording, the recording `priority_rank`, and the `cure_extension_days` the mortgagee enjoys beyond the tenant's own cure deadline. The principal must be at least 1 cent, the priority rank at least 1 and unique among that lease's mortgages, and the cure extension at least 1 whole day. On recording, `outstanding_principal_cents` is seeded from `original_principal_cents`, the mortgage's status starts at `RECORDED`, and both `mortgagee_notified_at` and `new_lease_election_deadline` are null. A mortgage cannot be recorded against a lease that is no longer operative. The mortgage register can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id` and by status, and it is returned in recording priority — `priority_rank` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — because that is the order in which money reaches lenders.

The notice address is not decoration. A mortgage without a `notice_email` earns no protective notice, and service of a default on the lease is refused unless every mortgage on that lease still in `RECORDED` carries a non-empty notice address; because the address is a mandatory field at recording, a well-formed register always satisfies this.

Protective status cycles. When a default notice is served on the encumbered lease, every mortgage sitting in `RECORDED` with a notice address moves `RECORDED → NOTICE_SERVED` and has `mortgagee_notified_at` stamped with the notice's `served_at`. From there the two cure branches diverge in a way worth stating precisely. If the **tenant** cures, every mortgage on the lease in `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` returns to `RECORDED`, and on the `NOTICE_SERVED → RECORDED` leg `mortgagee_notified_at` is **cleared back to null** — the tenant cured before the lender ever had to act, so the register carries no trace of a served notice. If instead the **mortgagee** elects to cure, the electing mortgage moves `NOTICE_SERVED → CURING`, and on completion it moves `CURING → RECORDED` with `mortgagee_notified_at` **left in place as the historical record** of the notice it acted on. The distinction is deliberate: the cleared field means "never engaged", the retained field means "engaged and cured".

Termination and expiry end the cycle in opposite ways. When the lease is terminated on an uncured default, every mortgage in `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` moves to `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE` and has its `new_lease_election_deadline` set to the termination instant advanced by 30 whole days — that is the window in which the lender may claim a replacement lease. When the lease instead reaches the end of its term, every mortgage still in `RECORDED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` moves straight to `EXTINGUISHED`, because there is no estate left to secure. A `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE` mortgage that lets its election window pass is moved to `EXTINGUISHED` by the scheduled sweep. `NEW_LEASE_ELECTED` and `EXTINGUISHED` are both terminal.

Estoppel certificates are the instrument by which a lender's counsel obtains a warranted statement of the lease's economics, and either a `LessorAdmin` or a `MortgageeAgent` may cause one to be issued. The request names the lease as `lease_id`, the financing it supports as `requested_by_mortgage_id`, and the lease version the requester observed as `expected_lease_version`. The named mortgage must actually encumber the named lease; one that sits on another lease is refused with `ERR_MORTGAGE_LEASE_MISMATCH`. Two substantive blocks apply. A certificate may not be issued while any revaluation on the lease sits in `ARBITRATION_REFERRED`, because the rent is genuinely unsettled — that refusal is `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED`. And a certificate may not be issued while the lease's `insurance_status` is `LAPSED`, nor while its `insurance_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock's current instant, which is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_DEFICIENT`.

An accepted issuance opens in status `ISSUED` and freezes four figures. `certified_annual_base_rent_cents` snapshots the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents`, `certified_arrears_cents` snapshots its `arrears_balance_cents`, `certified_rent_status` snapshots its `rent_status`, and `certified_lease_version` records the lease's `version` as it stands *after* the issuance's own increment — a certificate pinned against version 5 therefore certifies a `certified_lease_version` of 6. `issued_at` records the logical clock's instant of issuance and `issued_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Any certificate for the same lease-and-mortgage pair that was still in `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`, so at most one live certificate exists per financing. Certificates are also revoked collaterally: when the lease is terminated, every certificate still in `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `REVOKED` are terminal. The certificate collection can be narrowed by `lease_id`, by `requested_by_mortgage_id` and by status.

### 3.3. Rent Revaluation: Appraisal Exchange, Midpoint Settlement and Baseball Arbitration

Ground rent is re-struck to market on a fixed cadence, and `next_revaluation_date` is the lease's own diary entry for the next reset. At execution it is `commencement_date` advanced by `revaluation_interval_years` whole years, and it is pushed forward by exactly one further interval every time a reset is actually applied. When that date arrives the scheduled sweep flags the lease by moving its rent axis from `BASE_ONLY → REVALUATION_DUE` or from `RESET_APPLIED → REVALUATION_DUE`, which changes no rent figure and merely signals that a revaluation is now overdue.

A `LessorAdmin` opens the revaluation itself, supplying the `valuation_date` as of which the appraisers must value. Three preconditions gate the opening. The lease's rent axis must currently sit in `BASE_ONLY`, `REVALUATION_DUE` or `RESET_APPLIED`; its tenancy axis must be `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT`; and the logical clock must have reached the point 180 days before `next_revaluation_date`, which is the advance-notice runway the lease grants the parties. A lease already carrying an open revaluation cannot open a second one, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. Separately, the supplied `valuation_date` must itself be at least 180 days after the logical clock's current instant — a valuation date inside that advance-notice window is refused with `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION`. A lease that is no longer operative accepts no revaluation at all.

Opening the revaluation creates a negotiation record in status `OPEN_FOR_APPRAISALS` with `round_number` at 1, and snapshots the collar base: `prior_annual_base_rent_cents` takes the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents` as it stands at that moment and is never re-read afterwards, even if the lease's live base rent moves for another reason. The five outcome fields — `lessor_appraised_rent_cents`, `lessee_appraised_rent_cents`, `divergence_bps`, `resolution_method` and `resolved_annual_base_rent_cents` — all begin null. The lease's rent axis moves to `REVALUATION_OPEN`; the transition behaves identically whether the lease was sitting in `BASE_ONLY`, in `REVALUATION_DUE` or in `RESET_APPLIED`, with the same three preconditions and the same side effects in each case. Revaluations can afterwards be listed and narrowed by `lease_id` and by status.

Each side then lodges one appraisal for the current round. A `LessorAdmin` lodges the lessor's appointee's opinion and the `GroundTenant` lodges its own appointee's, each supplying `appointed_by` (`LESSOR` or `LESSEE`), the `appraised_annual_rent_cents` opinion of annual fair market ground rent, and the `valuation_method_code` methodology asserted — `COMPARABLE_LAND`, `RESIDUAL_LAND` or `INCOME_CAP`. The opinion must be at least 1 cent. Lodging a `LESSOR`-side appraisal while acting as `GroundTenant`, or a `LESSEE`-side appraisal while acting as `LessorAdmin`, is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_SIDE_MISMATCH`. Each submission copies the revaluation's current `round_number` at the moment of lodgement and records the submitter's `X-User-ID` in `appraiser_user_id`; that identifier is what the arbitrator-independence rule is later checked against. Exactly one submission is permitted per combination of revaluation, appointed side and round number, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. Submissions are immutable once lodged, which is why they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`. Because a superseded round's submissions are retained but excluded from every later computation, the submission list — narrowable by `revaluation_id`, by `appointed_by` and by `round_number` — is the only channel through which the history of earlier rounds can be discovered.

Once both sides are in, the `LessorAdmin` exchanges them, which requires no request body. The exchange demands exactly one `LESSOR` submission and one `LESSEE` submission whose `round_number` equals the revaluation's current `round_number`; an exchange attempted without both is refused with `ERR_APPRAISALS_INCOMPLETE`. The two figures are copied onto the revaluation as `lessor_appraised_rent_cents` and `lessee_appraised_rent_cents`, and the spread between them is recorded in `divergence_bps`: take the larger of the two appraisals, subtract the smaller, multiply the difference by 10,000, divide by the **smaller** of the two appraisals, and round the result half up to a whole basis point. The revaluation moves `OPEN_FOR_APPRAISALS → PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED`.

Where the gap is wide the parties may take one more run at agreement rather than going straight to a neutral. A `LessorAdmin` opens a further negotiation round with no request body, which is permitted only while `divergence_bps` still exceeds 1,000 basis points — a ten per cent spread — and only while `round_number` is still below 2, the maximum number of negotiation rounds the lease allows. A further round attempted once the divergence has narrowed to 1,000 basis points or less is refused with `ERR_DIVERGENCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, and a third round is refused with `ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_REACHED`. Opening the round increments `round_number` by 1, clears `lessor_appraised_rent_cents`, `lessee_appraised_rent_cents` and `divergence_bps` back to null so that the new round's submissions must be lodged and exchanged afresh, and returns the revaluation `PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED → OPEN_FOR_APPRAISALS`. This is the one cycle in the revaluation machine, and it can run at most once.

A revaluation that has reached `PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED` can be resolved in exactly two ways, and both end at the same place — a collar-clamped figure written into the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents` — but they select the pre-clamp figure differently, and for the same pair of appraisals they routinely produce different rents.

**The evaluation order is pinned and is the same for both routes.** Step one already happened at exchange, when `divergence_bps` was computed from the two current-round appraisals. Step two selects an *indicated* rent, and this is the only step that differs between the two routes. Step three derives the collar bounds from the snapshot the revaluation took when it opened: the collar floor is `prior_annual_base_rent_cents` plus that same figure multiplied by `reval_collar_min_uplift_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents; the collar ceiling is `prior_annual_base_rent_cents` plus that same figure multiplied by `reval_collar_max_uplift_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The collar base is always the snapshot, never the lease's live `current_annual_base_rent_cents`. Step four clamps: the indicated rent is first raised to the collar floor if it falls below it, and the result is then lowered to the collar ceiling if it exceeds it. The clamp is applied **last**, to the already-rounded indicated rent, and never before the indicated rent has been selected.

The first route is **midpoint settlement**, available to a `LessorAdmin` with no request body and permitted at any level of divergence, however wide or narrow. Its indicated rent is the arithmetic midpoint of the two exchanged appraisals: add `lessor_appraised_rent_cents` to `lessee_appraised_rent_cents`, divide by 2, and round half up to whole cents. The arbitrator's independent value plays no part, because there is no arbitrator. Settlement additionally requires the lease's tenancy axis to be `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT`. The revaluation moves `PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED → RESOLVED` with `resolution_method` recorded as `MIDPOINT_SETTLEMENT` and `resolved_annual_base_rent_cents` holding the clamped figure, and the lease's rent axis moves `REVALUATION_OPEN → RESET_APPLIED`.

The second route is **baseball arbitration**, and it begins with a referral by a `LessorAdmin` naming the neutral as `arbitrator_user_id` and recording the scheduled `hearing_date`. A referral is only available where the parties are genuinely far apart: `divergence_bps` must **exceed** 1,000 basis points, and a referral attempted when the divergence is at or below that threshold is refused with `ERR_DIVERGENCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. The nominated neutral must be independent — `arbitrator_user_id` must not equal the `appraiser_user_id` of any appraisal lodged on that revaluation, in any round — and a referral naming an appraiser is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A referral is also blocked while the lease's tenancy axis sits in `IN_DEFAULT`, which is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED`, and only one proceeding may exist per revaluation, a second being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. The referral creates a proceeding in status `REFERRED`, moves the revaluation `PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED → ARBITRATION_REFERRED`, and moves the lease's rent axis `REVALUATION_OPEN → IN_ARBITRATION`.

The neutral then awards. Acting as `Arbitrator`, the appointed party supplies `independent_annual_rent_cents`, its own opinion of value, which must be at least 1 cent; the proceeding must be in `REFERRED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the proceeding's `arbitrator_user_id`, with any other arbitrator refused as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Baseball arbitration then selects, rather than averaging: measure the absolute gap between `lessor_appraised_rent_cents` and the independent value, and the absolute gap between `lessee_appraised_rent_cents` and the independent value. If the lessor's appraisal is strictly nearer, the indicated rent is `lessor_appraised_rent_cents` and `award_basis` is recorded as `CLOSEST_LESSOR`. If the lessee's appraisal is strictly nearer, the indicated rent is `lessee_appraised_rent_cents` and `award_basis` is `CLOSEST_LESSEE`. If the two gaps are exactly equal, the tie goes to the tenant: the indicated rent is `lessee_appraised_rent_cents` and `award_basis` is `TIE_TO_LESSEE`. The selected figure is stored in `awarded_annual_rent_cents` **before** any clamping, the proceeding moves `REFERRED → AWARDED`, and the revaluation moves `ARBITRATION_REFERRED → RESOLVED` with `resolution_method` recorded as `ARBITRATION_AWARD` and `resolved_annual_base_rent_cents` holding the awarded figure after the collar clamp has been applied. The lease's rent axis moves `IN_ARBITRATION → RESET_APPLIED`. Proceedings can be listed and narrowed by `revaluation_id` and by status; `AWARDED` is terminal, as `RESOLVED` is for the revaluation.

Whichever route resolved the revaluation, an identical cascade fires against the lease and it must fire completely. The lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents` is overwritten with `resolved_annual_base_rent_cents`. `next_revaluation_date` is advanced by exactly one further `revaluation_interval_years` interval, so the diary entry moves on rather than being recomputed from the commencement date. The lease `version` is incremented by one. And because the base rent in force has changed, every percentage-rent statement on that lease still sitting in `SUBMITTED` has its `base_rent_offset_credit_cents` recomputed against the new base rent — a statement filed before the reset lands must not be assessed against the superseded figure.

One asymmetry deserves stating. If the lease is terminated or reaches the end of its term while a revaluation is still live, the rent axis is driven to `FROZEN` from `REVALUATION_OPEN` or from `IN_ARBITRATION`, and the revaluation itself is left exactly where it stood, in `OPEN_FOR_APPRAISALS`, `PROPOSALS_EXCHANGED` or `ARBITRATION_REFERRED`. It is not cancelled and it is not resolved — but it can never be settled again, because settlement requires the tenancy axis to be `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT`, and the frozen lease is neither.

### 3.4. Percentage Rent: Filing, Provisional Assessment and Audit

Above a contractual sales breakpoint the lessor takes a slice of the tenant's turnover. The tenant self-reports, the lessor assesses provisionally on that self-report, and the lessor later audits and reconciles — three steps, two of which run the same seven-step computation with different inputs and deliberately produce different numbers from identical database rows.

A `GroundTenant` files a statement for a **closed** period, naming the lease as `lease_id`, the period as `period_start` and `period_end`, the self-reported turnover as `reported_gross_sales_cents`, and the lease version it observed as `expected_lease_version`. Reported sales must be at least 0. `period_start` must be strictly before `period_end`, and `period_end` must not be after the logical clock's current instant — filing for a period that has not yet closed is refused with `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION`. A statement whose period intersects the period of any other statement on the lease that is not `VOID` is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, with one deliberate exception: a filing whose `period_start` and `period_end` exactly match those of an existing statement is treated as a **supersession** and is accepted. In that case the earlier statement, if it is still in `SUBMITTED`, moves `SUBMITTED → VOID`, and because nothing had yet been posted to the ledger for it there is no ledger movement to reverse. A lease that is no longer operative accepts no statement at all.

The accepted statement opens in `SUBMITTED`, its `base_rent_offset_credit_cents` provisioned from the base rent in force at the moment of filing, and every other computed figure — `audited_gross_sales_cents`, `included_sublease_rent_cents`, `gross_percentage_rent_cents`, `amount_due_cents`, `underreport_penalty_cents` and `assessment_variant` — left null. The filing increments the lease `version` by one. Statements can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id`, by status and by `assessment_variant`.

The assessment computation proceeds in seven pinned steps.

**Step one — the sublease inclusion base.** Ground leases fold a share of sublease income into the turnover figure so that a tenant cannot escape percentage rent by subletting instead of trading. Build the inclusion set from the transfer requests on the same lease whose `transfer_type` is `SUBLEASE` and whose term overlaps the statement period — that is, whose `term_start` is at or before the statement's `period_end` and whose `term_end` is at or after the statement's `period_start` — restricted to the statuses the variant admits, and ordered by `created_at` ascending. For each row in that set, take its `annual_sublease_rent_cents`, multiply by 5,000 basis points, divide by 10,000 — in other words, take exactly half of it — and round half up to whole cents. Add the rounded per-row figures together; the sum is `included_sublease_rent_cents`. Rounding each sublease line to whole cents **before** adding is mandatory: summing the raw rents and rounding once yields a different number, differing by one cent for each pair of odd rents, and is wrong. An empty inclusion set yields 0.

**Step two — adjusted sales.** Add `included_sublease_rent_cents` to the gross sales figure the variant selects.

**Step three — the excess over the breakpoint.** Subtract the lease's `percentage_rent_breakpoint_cents` from the adjusted sales figure; if the result is negative, the excess is 0. The breakpoint is **strict**: adjusted sales landing exactly on the breakpoint produce no excess and therefore no percentage rent.

**Step four — the gross percentage rent.** Multiply the excess by the lease's `percentage_rent_rate_bps`, divide by 10,000, and round half up to whole cents. That figure is `gross_percentage_rent_cents`.

**Step five — the base-rent offset credit.** The tenant is credited with a quarter of the base rent it already pays: multiply the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents` by 2,500 basis points, divide by 10,000, and round half up to whole cents. That figure is `base_rent_offset_credit_cents`, and it is taken from the base rent **in force at assessment time**, which is why a revaluation reset that lands while a statement is still in `SUBMITTED` forces the credit to be recomputed.

**Step six — the amount due.** Subtract `base_rent_offset_credit_cents` from `gross_percentage_rent_cents`; if the result is negative, `amount_due_cents` is 0. The offset credit never produces a refund.

**Step seven — the underreporting penalty, on the audit pass only.** Compare the audited turnover against the reported turnover with a five per cent tolerance: if `audited_gross_sales_cents` multiplied by 10,000 is strictly greater than `reported_gross_sales_cents` multiplied by 10,500, the tenant underreported materially. In that case `underreport_penalty_cents` is the shortfall — `audited_gross_sales_cents` less `reported_gross_sales_cents` — multiplied by the lease's `percentage_rent_rate_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents. Otherwise `underreport_penalty_cents` is 0.

The **provisional** pass is run by a `LeaseAccountant` with no request body, on a statement in `SUBMITTED` whose lease's tenancy axis is `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT`. It uses `reported_gross_sales_cents` as the gross sales figure, and its inclusion set admits only subleases whose status is `ACTIVE`. It writes `included_sublease_rent_cents`, `gross_percentage_rent_cents`, `base_rent_offset_credit_cents` and `amount_due_cents`, records `assessment_variant` as `PROVISIONAL`, does **not** write `underreport_penalty_cents`, and moves the statement `SUBMITTED → PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED`. The assessed `amount_due_cents` is added to the lease's `arrears_balance_cents` and the lease `version` is incremented by one.

The **audited final** pass is run by the same `LeaseAccountant` on a statement in `PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED`, supplying `audited_gross_sales_cents`, which must be at least 0. It uses that audited figure as the gross sales input, and — this is the substantive difference beyond the sales figure — its inclusion set **widens** to admit subleases whose status is `ACTIVE` or `CONSENT_GRANTED`, so consented-but-not-yet-paid-for subleases enter the base on audit even though they were excluded provisionally. Identical database rows therefore yield a different `included_sublease_rent_cents` and a different `amount_due_cents` on the audit pass than on the provisional pass. Every figure is rewritten, `underreport_penalty_cents` is written per step seven, `assessment_variant` becomes `AUDITED_FINAL`, and the statement moves `PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED → FINAL_ASSESSED`. The ledger movement is a reconciliation, not a second charge: add the newly computed `amount_due_cents`, subtract the provisional `amount_due_cents` previously posted, and add `underreport_penalty_cents`, applying the net figure to the lease's `arrears_balance_cents` — so a downward audit genuinely reduces arrears. The lease `version` is incremented by one.

Statements are also voided collaterally by termination, and the two branches differ in their ledger consequences. A statement in `SUBMITTED` when the lease is terminated moves `SUBMITTED → VOID` with no ledger movement, because nothing had been posted for it. A statement in `PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED` when the lease is terminated moves `PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED → VOID`, and the provisional `amount_due_cents` already posted to `arrears_balance_cents` is **left in place** — the tenant still owes what it was assessed. `FINAL_ASSESSED` and `VOID` are both terminal.

### 3.5. Alterations, Redevelopment and the Development Envelope

A ground lease grants the tenant the right to build, but only within an envelope and only for the use the lease permits. `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` is that envelope; `gfa_allocated_sqft` is the part of it already committed to approved work; `gfa_unallocated_sqft` is what remains and is always the difference between the two.

A `GroundTenant` applies for consent by describing the work: the `scope_code` classifying it as `COSMETIC`, `STRUCTURAL_ALTERATION` or `REDEVELOPMENT`; the `proposed_use_code` the completed work will serve, drawn from `RETAIL_MIXED`, `INDUSTRIAL_LOGISTICS`, `RESIDENTIAL_RENTAL` and `LIFE_SCIENCE_LAB`; the `requested_gfa_sqft` the work will consume, which must be at least 0; the site occupation window as `construction_window_start` and `construction_window_end`; and the `estimated_cost_cents` hard-cost estimate, which must also be at least 0. The use clause is checked at application: a `proposed_use_code` that differs from the lease's `permitted_use_code` is refused with `ERR_USE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The window must start strictly before it ends, and it is validated half-open against every other window already approved on that lease, with a genuine intersection refused as `ERR_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` and touching endpoints permitted. A lease that is no longer operative accepts no application. The accepted request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `decided_by_user_id` null, and the request list can be narrowed by `lease_id`, by status and by `scope_code`.

An `AssetManager` then decides, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and optionally a free-text `conditions_note`. The two branches are not mirror images and each must be implemented in full.

On `APPROVE`, five substantive conditions are re-examined against the state in force at decision time, not at application time. The lease's tenancy axis must be exactly `ACTIVE` — a lease in default cannot have new work consented. The `proposed_use_code` must still equal the lease's `permitted_use_code`, which means an intervening terms amendment can invalidate a request that was perfectly compliant when submitted; that refusal is again `ERR_USE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Where the `scope_code` is `STRUCTURAL_ALTERATION` or `REDEVELOPMENT`, builders-risk cover must be genuinely in place: the lease's `insurance_status` must be `ACTIVE`, `lessor_additional_insured` must be true, `builders_risk_limit_cents` must be at least `estimated_cost_cents`, and `insurance_expires_at` must be at or after `construction_window_end` so that cover does not run out mid-build; any of these four failing is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_DEFICIENT`. Cosmetic work carries none of those insurance conditions. The construction window must still not overlap another window on the lease that is in `APPROVED`. And the envelope must accommodate the work: `requested_gfa_sqft` may not exceed the lease's `gfa_unallocated_sqft` — equivalently, the sum of `requested_gfa_sqft` across the lease's already-approved requests plus this one's may not exceed `total_permitted_gfa_sqft` — with the refusal `ERR_GFA_ENVELOPE_EXCEEDED`. It is entirely possible for each of several requests to fit individually while the collection exceeds the envelope, which is exactly what this check catches. Approval is additionally blocked while any `CASUALTY` loss event on the lease sits in `REPORTED` or `ADJUSTED`, refused with `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED`, because the lessor will not consent to new work while the consequences of a fire are still unsettled. An accepted approval moves the request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records the deciding `X-User-ID` in `decided_by_user_id`, adds `requested_gfa_sqft` to the lease's `gfa_allocated_sqft`, re-derives `gfa_unallocated_sqft`, and increments the lease `version` by one.

On `REJECT`, the only precondition is that the request currently sits in `SUBMITTED`. The request moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and `decided_by_user_id` records the deciding `X-User-ID` exactly as on the approval branch. Nothing else moves: the envelope is untouched, no lease field changes, and the lease `version` does not advance. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Approvals are reversible by a compensating action. An `AssetManager` revokes a granted approval by supplying a `reason_code`, which is permitted while the request sits in `APPROVED` and the lease's tenancy axis is `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT`. The request moves `APPROVED → APPROVAL_REVOKED`, `requested_gfa_sqft` is subtracted from the lease's `gfa_allocated_sqft`, `gfa_unallocated_sqft` is re-derived, and the lease `version` is incremented by one. The restoration is exact: the envelope returns to precisely its pre-approval figures, and because the revoked request is no longer in `APPROVED`, its construction window is released and another request may now be approved across the same dates. `APPROVAL_REVOKED` is terminal.

### 3.6. Assignment, Subleasing and Transfer-Fee Pricing

The tenant may hand the whole leasehold to someone else or let part of the premises to a subtenant, but never without consent, and consent carries a price. Both cases travel the same three-step path — application, consent decision, fee payment — and diverge sharply in how the fee is computed and in what activation does to the lease.

A `GroundTenant` applies by naming the lease as `lease_id`, the kind of transaction as `transfer_type` (`ASSIGNMENT` or `SUBLEASE`), the term as `term_start` and `term_end`, and the counterparty as `transferee_legal_name`. Six further fields are conditional. An `ASSIGNMENT` requires `transferee_lessee_id`, the registered tenant that will step into the lease, and `net_consideration_cents`, the consideration paid for it. A `SUBLEASE` requires `premises_unit_code`, the demised unit whose occupancy must not collide, together with `annual_sublease_rent_cents` and `sublet_area_sqft`. Omitting any field its transfer type requires is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_FIELDS_MISMATCHED` (HTTP 422). Optionally the application may carry `parent_transfer_id`, naming the sublease this sub-sublease sits beneath; `transferee_credit_rating_code`, which defaults to `BBB` and takes the same `AAA`, `A`, `BBB`, `SPECULATIVE` scale as the tenant register; and `consent_deposit_cents`, a deposit tendered up front that defaults to 0.

Two fields are derived at application and never re-derived afterwards. `submitted_late` is set to true when the logical clock's current instant is strictly after `term_start` — the tenant applied for consent to a term that had already begun — and false otherwise; it is what later drives the late surcharge. `tier_depth` is 1 when `parent_transfer_id` is null and otherwise one more than the parent transfer's `tier_depth`; the lease permits a maximum sublease tier depth of 2, so a direct sublease and one layer of sub-sublease beneath it are allowed and a third layer is not.

Three structural checks run at application. A `premises_unit_code` already taken by one of the lease's active subleases is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. A sublease term that intersects the term of another sublease of the same unit on the same lease is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, and because the lessor requires no turnover gap between successive subleases of one unit, terms that merely touch are permitted. A sublease term that escapes the lease's own term — beginning before `commencement_date` or ending after `expiration_date` — is refused with `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION`. A lease that is no longer operative accepts no application. The accepted request opens in `SUBMITTED` with every priced field null, and the request list can be narrowed by `lease_id`, by `parent_transfer_id`, by `transferee_lessee_id`, by `transfer_type` and by status.

An `AssetManager` decides consent, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and optionally a `denial_reason_code`. On `DENY` the only precondition is that the request sits in `SUBMITTED`; the request moves `SUBMITTED → CONSENT_DENIED`, no fee is assessed, every fee field is left null, and the `consent_deposit_cents` already tendered is **not** credited anywhere. `CONSENT_DENIED` is terminal.

On `APPROVE` five conditions are examined. The lease's tenancy axis must be exactly `ACTIVE`. The lease's rent axis must not be `IN_ARBITRATION`, because the lessor will not price a transfer against a rent that is under award; that refusal is `ERR_CROSS_ENTITY_BLOCKED`. The `transferee_credit_rating_code` must not be `SPECULATIVE` — a speculative counterparty is refused consent outright, whether the transaction is an `ASSIGNMENT` or a `SUBLEASE`, with `ERR_CREDIT_RATING_REFUSED` (HTTP 422). For a `SUBLEASE`, no other transfer request on the same lease and the same `premises_unit_code` whose status is `CONSENT_GRANTED` or `ACTIVE` may occupy the proposed term, again with a zero-day turnover buffer between successive occupancies. And `tier_depth` must not exceed 2. One aggregate check also applies: summing `sublet_area_sqft` across the lease's subleases in `ACTIVE` and `CONSENT_GRANTED`, including the one under decision, may not exceed the lease's `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`, and a breach is refused with `ERR_SUBLET_AREA_EXCEEDED`.

Granting consent prices the transfer, in five pinned steps.

**Step one — the gross fee.** For an `ASSIGNMENT`, select the rate tier from `net_consideration_cents`: 300 basis points where the consideration is at most 50,000,000 cents ($500,000), 500 basis points where it is above that but at most 200,000,000 cents ($2,000,000), and 700 basis points above that. The selected rate applies to the **whole** consideration — this is a flat-by-tier scale, not a marginal one, so a consideration one cent above a ceiling is charged the higher rate on every cent of it. Multiply the consideration by the selected rate, divide by 10,000, round half up to whole cents, and then take whichever is larger of that figure and the minimum assignment fee of 500,000 cents ($5,000). For a `SUBLEASE`, first apportion base rent to the sublet area: multiply the lease's `current_annual_base_rent_cents` by `sublet_area_sqft`, divide by the lease's `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`, round half up to whole cents, and store that as `allocable_base_rent_cents`. The sublease profit is `annual_sublease_rent_cents` less `allocable_base_rent_cents`, floored at 0. The gross fee is that profit multiplied by 5,000 basis points, divided by 10,000 — a fifty per cent share of the profit — and rounded half up to whole cents. There is **no** minimum fee on a sublease. Either way the result is `transfer_fee_gross_cents`.

**Step two — the administrative charge.** Multiply `transfer_fee_gross_cents` by 250 basis points, divide by 10,000, round half up to whole cents, and then take whichever is smaller of that figure and the cap of 250,000 cents ($2,500). The result is `admin_charge_cents`.

**Step three — the base total.** Add `transfer_fee_gross_cents` and `admin_charge_cents`.

**Step four — the late surcharge.** Where `submitted_late` is false, `late_surcharge_cents` is 0. Where it is true, the surcharge rate tier is selected on the **base total from step three, which excludes the surcharge itself** — this is the pinned resolution of what would otherwise be a circular dependency, since the surcharge is a percentage of an amount it would otherwise form part of. The rate is 250 basis points where the base total is at most 1,000,000 cents ($10,000) and 500 basis points above that. Multiply the base total by the selected rate, divide by 10,000, and round half up to whole cents.

**Step five — the total.** `total_transfer_fee_cents` is the base total plus `late_surcharge_cents`. The deposit already tendered is credited at consent, so `transfer_fee_paid_cents` is set to `consent_deposit_cents`.

The request then moves `SUBMITTED → CONSENT_GRANTED`. If the credited deposit already covers the whole fee — if `transfer_fee_paid_cents` has reached `total_transfer_fee_cents` — the row continues immediately to `ACTIVE` within the same request and fires the full activation side-effect set described next, without waiting for a separate payment.

Otherwise the tenant pays the balance. A `GroundTenant` supplies `amount_cents`, which must equal `total_transfer_fee_cents` less `transfer_fee_paid_cents` **exactly**; a part payment or an overpayment is refused with `ERR_AMOUNT_NOT_RECONCILED`. The payment is added to `transfer_fee_paid_cents`, bringing it to the full fee, and the request moves `CONSENT_GRANTED → ACTIVE`. Activation then does different work per transfer type, and both branches increment the lease `version` by one. For an `ASSIGNMENT`, the lease's `lessee_id` is rewritten to the request's `transferee_lessee_id` — the assignee becomes the tenant of record, and from that moment ownership checks on the lease compare against the new tenant's identifier. For a `SUBLEASE`, the lease's `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` is recomputed: take every transfer request on that lease whose `transfer_type` is `SUBLEASE` and whose status is `ACTIVE`, at any tier depth, ordered by `created_at` ascending; for each, take half of its `annual_sublease_rent_cents` — multiply by 5,000 basis points and divide by 10,000 — rounded half up to whole cents; and add those per-row figures together. Rows in `SUBMITTED`, `CONSENT_GRANTED`, `CONSENT_DENIED` or `TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION` are excluded, and an empty set yields 0. The per-row rounding happens before the addition, never after.

That same recomputation fires whenever a sublease **leaves** the active set. When the lease is terminated or reaches the end of its term, every transfer request on it in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION`, and because those subleases have left the active set the lease's `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` is recomputed over whatever remains — which, when every sublease has been swept out, is 0. `TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION` is terminal.

### 3.7. Default, Cure Windows, Termination and the Replacement Lease

Terminating a ground lease destroys an enormous amount of value, so the instrument surrounds it with procedure: a written notice, a tenant cure window, a longer lender cure window, and a segregation-of-duties bar on the person who pulls the trigger. This subsection is the procedural spine of the whole system.

Either an `AssetManager` or a `LeaseAccountant` serves a notice of default against a lease, supplying the `default_type` and, where money is claimed, a `claimed_amount_cents` figure that defaults to 0. The five grounds are `MONETARY_RENT_ARREARS`, `PERCENTAGE_RENT_UNDERPAYMENT`, `INSURANCE_LAPSE`, `UNPERMITTED_USE` and `UNAUTHORIZED_TRANSFER`. Service requires the lease's tenancy axis to be exactly `ACTIVE`. On the two monetary grounds — `MONETARY_RENT_ARREARS` and `PERCENTAGE_RENT_UNDERPAYMENT` — the `claimed_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than the lease's `arrears_balance_cents`; the lessor cannot claim more than the ledger shows. On `INSURANCE_LAPSE` the lease's `insurance_status` must actually be `LAPSED`. And, as noted earlier, every mortgage on the lease still in `RECORDED` must carry a non-empty `notice_email`, since the notice must reach every protected lender. A lease that is no longer operative accepts no notice.

**Cure deadlines are computed once, at service, and never recomputed.** `served_at` takes the logical clock's current instant, and `served_by_user_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID` — or the literal string `SYSTEM` where the notice was raised automatically by the administration sweep rather than by a person. The tenant's window length depends on the ground: 10 whole days for either of the two monetary grounds, 15 whole days for `INSURANCE_LAPSE`, and 30 whole days for `UNPERMITTED_USE` or `UNAUTHORIZED_TRANSFER`. `lessee_cure_deadline` is `served_at` advanced by that number of days. The lender's window is then derived from the register: assemble every mortgage on the lease whose status is `RECORDED` and whose `notice_email` is non-empty; if that set is empty the lease is unencumbered and `mortgagee_cure_deadline` is **null**; otherwise `mortgagee_cure_deadline` is `lessee_cure_deadline` advanced by the **largest** `cure_extension_days` among those noticed mortgages — the widest extension in the register governs, not the sum and not the narrowest. Mortgages already in `NOTICE_SERVED`, `CURING`, `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE`, `NEW_LEASE_ELECTED` or `EXTINGUISHED` are excluded from that assembly.

Service opens the notice in `SERVED`, drives the lease's tenancy axis `ACTIVE → IN_DEFAULT` and increments the lease `version` by one. It also stamps `mortgagee_notified_at` on the notice with the service instant, and moves every mortgage on the lease from `RECORDED → NOTICE_SERVED`, each with its own `mortgagee_notified_at` set to the same instant. Notices can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id`, by status and by `default_type`, and because the sweep serves notices without any client involvement, that list is the only channel through which auto-served notices can be discovered.

The tenant cures first. A `GroundTenant` supplies an optional `payment_at` instant and an optional `cure_amount_cents`, on a notice still in `SERVED`. The window is inclusive: a cure at exactly the `lessee_cure_deadline` instant succeeds, and only a cure attempted strictly after it is refused with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Any `payment_at` supplied must be at or after the notice's `served_at`, with an earlier instant refused as `ERR_CHRONOLOGY_VIOLATION`. On the two monetary grounds the cure must clear the ledger completely: `cure_amount_cents` must be at least the lease's `arrears_balance_cents`, the payment is applied to the ledger first, and the lease's `arrears_balance_cents` must end at 0 — a partial payment does not cure. On `INSURANCE_LAPSE` the cure requires the lease's `insurance_status` to have been restored to `ACTIVE`, which means the tenant must have filed a fresh endorsement before attempting the cure. An accepted cure moves the notice `SERVED → LESSEE_CURED` with `cured_by` recorded as `LESSEE`, subtracts `cure_amount_cents` from the lease's `arrears_balance_cents` on a monetary ground with the result floored at 0, returns the lease's tenancy axis `IN_DEFAULT → ACTIVE`, increments the lease `version` by one, and returns every mortgage on the lease in `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` to `RECORDED` with `mortgagee_notified_at` cleared. Curing the default leaves the lease's rent axis entirely untouched.

If the tenant does nothing, the sweep lapses its window: a notice in `SERVED` whose `lessee_cure_deadline` the logical clock has passed **strictly** moves `SERVED → LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED`, and at exactly the deadline instant it stays in `SERVED`. That lapse opens the lender's branch.

A `MortgageeAgent` then acts, supplying an `action` of `ELECT` or `COMPLETE`, the `mortgage_id` it acts for, and — on completion of a monetary cure — a `cure_amount_cents`. Both actions are refused once the logical clock is strictly after `mortgagee_cure_deadline`, with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and both require the named mortgage to sit on the notice's own lease, with a mismatch refused as `ERR_MORTGAGE_LEASE_MISMATCH`. On `ELECT` the notice must be in `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED` and the named mortgage must be in `NOTICE_SERVED`; the notice moves `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED → MORTGAGEE_CURING` and the mortgage moves `NOTICE_SERVED → CURING`. On `COMPLETE` the notice must be in `MORTGAGEE_CURING`, and on the monetary grounds the same clearance rule applies as for the tenant: `cure_amount_cents` must be at least the lease's `arrears_balance_cents`, the payment lands first, and the balance must end at 0. Completion moves the notice `MORTGAGEE_CURING → MORTGAGEE_CURED` with `cured_by` recorded as `MORTGAGEE`, returns the acting mortgage `CURING → RECORDED`, subtracts the payment from `arrears_balance_cents` floored at 0, returns the lease's tenancy axis `IN_DEFAULT → ACTIVE`, and increments the lease `version` by one.

The lender's window lapses in two ways, both by the sweep and both strictly. A notice in `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED` whose `mortgagee_cure_deadline` is non-null and has been passed moves `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED → MORTGAGEE_CURE_LAPSED`. A notice in `MORTGAGEE_CURING` whose deadline has been passed moves `MORTGAGEE_CURING → MORTGAGEE_CURE_LAPSED`, and the mortgage that had elected to cure **stays in `CURING`** rather than reverting — it is reclassified only when termination arrives.

Termination is available to an `AssetManager` or a `LeaseAccountant`, requires no request body, and requires the lease's tenancy axis to be `IN_DEFAULT`. There are exactly two routes into it, and they differ in their precondition. From `MORTGAGEE_CURE_LAPSED` the notice is terminable outright: both windows have run. From `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED` the notice is terminable only where the lease is **unencumbered** — that is, where no mortgage on the lease sits in `RECORDED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` — since an encumbered lease must give its lender the mortgagee window first. In both routes the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the notice's `served_by_user_id`, and an attempt by the server of the notice is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The termination cascade is the largest single side-effect set in the system and every part of it fires. The notice moves to `TERMINATION_EFFECTED` and records `terminated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. The lease's tenancy axis becomes `TERMINATED` and its rent axis becomes `FROZEN`, and the lease `version` is incremented by one. Every transfer request on the lease in `ACTIVE` becomes `TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION`, and because the active sublease set is now empty the lease's `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` is recomputed to 0. Every mortgage on the lease in `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` becomes `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE`, each with `new_lease_election_deadline` set to the termination instant advanced by 30 whole days. Every estoppel certificate on the lease in `ISSUED` becomes `REVOKED`. And every percentage-rent statement on the lease in `SUBMITTED` or `PROVISIONAL_ASSESSED` becomes `VOID`, with the provisional amounts already posted to arrears left standing.

The lender's last protection is the replacement lease. Within its election window a `MortgageeAgent` may claim a fresh lease on the same parcel, supplying `new_lessee_id` — a registered tenant that must exist, failing which `ERR_LESSEE_NOT_FOUND` — and optionally a `premises_code` where the parcel identifier is being restated. The mortgage must be in `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE`; the logical clock must be at or before `new_lease_election_deadline`, with a strictly later attempt refused as `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and the terminated lease's `arrears_balance_cents` must be exactly 0, so the lender must have cleared the arrears before electing, with an unpaid ledger refusing the election as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The election mints a brand-new lease: `predecessor_lease_id` points at the terminated lease, `origin_mortgage_id` points at the electing mortgage, `lessee_id` takes `new_lessee_id`, `premises_code` is carried across, `current_annual_base_rent_cents` is carried across, `commencement_date` is the logical clock's current instant while `expiration_date` is carried across unchanged, and the collar, percentage-rent, envelope and reversion terms are all copied. The replacement opens at `lease_status` `ACTIVE` and `rent_status` `BASE_ONLY`, with `insurance_status` `LAPSED` because no endorsement has been filed for it, `gfa_allocated_sqft` at 0, `arrears_balance_cents` at 0 and every deposit figure at 0. The electing mortgage moves `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE → NEW_LEASE_ELECTED`. Leases minted this way are discoverable by filtering the lease list on `predecessor_lease_id` or on `origin_mortgage_id`.

### 3.8. Casualty, Condemnation and the Proceeds Waterfall

Fires and highway takings both put a lump of money on the table, and the ground lease dictates who gets it and in what order. A `GroundTenant` or an `AssetManager` reports the event, naming `event_type` as `CASUALTY` or `CONDEMNATION`, `scope_code` as `TOTAL`, `PARTIAL` or `TEMPORARY`, the `occurred_at` instant of the fire or of the taking order, and optionally the `damage_estimate_cents` and `damaged_area_sqft`, each of which defaults to 0. The event opens in `REPORTED`, with `distributable_pool_cents`, `allocation_method`, `rent_abatement_bps` and `lessee_share_bps` all null, all four allocated buckets at 0 and `allocation_detail` an empty list. Loss events can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id`, by `event_type` and by status.

An `AssetManager` then adjusts the event, supplying the `distributable_pool_cents` — insurance proceeds on a casualty, the award total on a condemnation — together with the `allocation_method` that will govern the split, and optionally a `rent_abatement_bps`. The event must be in `REPORTED`. A pool of 0 or less is refused with `ERR_AMOUNT_NOT_RECONCILED`. On a `CASUALTY` two further conditions apply: the lease's `insurance_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the pool may not exceed the lease's `property_all_risk_limit_cents` — you cannot collect more than the policy carries — with either failure refused as `ERR_INSURANCE_DEFICIENT`. Adjustment writes all three supplied values and moves the event `REPORTED → ADJUSTED`. Once fixed at adjustment, the pool is not revised.

A casualty then carries an election that a condemnation does not, because a taking is involuntary and leaves nothing to elect. The `GroundTenant` supplies an `election` of `RESTORE` or `TERMINATE` on an event in `ADJUSTED`; the same request against a `CONDEMNATION` event is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`. On `RESTORE` the tenant keeps the lease alive and earns a rent abatement proportionate to the floor area it has lost: multiply `damaged_area_sqft` by 10,000, divide by the lease's `total_permitted_gfa_sqft`, and round half up to whole basis points, storing the result in `rent_abatement_bps`. The event moves `ADJUSTED → RESTORATION_ELECTED`, and the lease's tenancy axis is left completely untouched — this is the branch that preserves the tenancy. On `TERMINATE` the loss must be severe enough to justify walking away: `damage_estimate_cents` multiplied by 10,000 must be at least the lease's `improvements_value_cents` multiplied by 5,000 — in plain terms, the damage estimate must be at least half of the agreed improvements value — and an election below that threshold is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Where it is met, `rent_abatement_bps` stays null, the event moves `ADJUSTED → TERMINATION_ELECTED`, the lease's tenancy axis becomes `TERMINATED`, its rent axis becomes `FROZEN`, and the **identical** termination cascade described for the default route fires in full: active transfers become `TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION` with the sublease base recomputed, `NOTICE_SERVED` and `CURING` mortgages become `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE` with a 30-day election deadline, issued estoppel certificates become `REVOKED`, and open statements become `VOID`. `TERMINATION_ELECTED` is terminal.

Allocation is the `LeaseAccountant`'s and requires no request body. It is available from `ADJUSTED` on a `CONDEMNATION`, from `RESTORATION_ELECTED` on a `CASUALTY`, and again from `ALLOCATION_RESCINDED` on either. In each case `distributable_pool_cents` must be non-null and greater than 0. The waterfall then runs in four buckets, each drawing from what the previous buckets left.

**Bucket one — the lessor's arrears.** `allocated_arrears_cents` is whichever is smaller of the remaining pool and the lease's live `arrears_balance_cents`. That amount is subtracted from the remaining pool, and the lease's `arrears_balance_cents` is reduced by the same figure.

**Bucket two — mortgage payoff, in recording priority.** Assemble the mortgages on the lease whose status is `RECORDED`, `NOTICE_SERVED`, `CURING` or `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE`, ordered by `priority_rank` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; mortgages in `EXTINGUISHED` or `NEW_LEASE_ELECTED` are excluded. Walk that list in order, paying each mortgage whichever is smaller of the remaining pool and its `outstanding_principal_cents`, appending a record of `mortgage_id` and `amount_cents` to `allocation_detail` **in the order paid**, and subtracting each payment from the remaining pool. Each mortgage's `outstanding_principal_cents` is reduced by the amount it received, and a mortgage whose outstanding principal reaches 0 becomes `EXTINGUISHED`. `allocated_mortgagee_cents` is the sum of the `amount_cents` figures in `allocation_detail`. Note that a low-priority mortgage can legitimately receive an entry of 0 cents where the pool ran dry before reaching it.

**Bucket three — the lessee's improvements share.** The share is computed on the **full** pool, not on what bucket two left, and only then capped by what remains. Multiply `distributable_pool_cents` by `lessee_share_bps`, divide by 10,000, round half up to whole cents; `allocated_lessee_cents` is whichever is smaller of that indicated figure and the remaining pool, and it is then subtracted from the remaining pool. Which `lessee_share_bps` applies depends on the `allocation_method` fixed at adjustment. Under `BONUS_VALUE` the share is a pure value split that ignores how much term is left: multiply the lease's `improvements_value_cents` by 10,000, divide by the sum of `improvements_value_cents` and `reversion_land_value_cents`, and round half up to whole basis points. Under `PRO_RATA_VALUE` the share is a pure term split that ignores relative values: count the whole days from the lease's `commencement_date` to its `expiration_date` as the total term, count the whole days from the logical clock's current instant to `expiration_date` as the remaining term, multiply the remaining days by 10,000, divide by the total days, and round half up to whole basis points; day counts use a 365-day year basis. For the same pool and the same ledger the two methods return different bucket-three figures, which is exactly why the method is recorded on the event.

**Bucket four — the lessor's residual.** `allocated_lessor_cents` takes every cent still remaining, including any rounding remainder from bucket three. Because bucket four is a pure residual, the four buckets always sum to `distributable_pool_cents` exactly, with no drift.

Allocation moves the event to `ALLOCATED` and increments the lease `version` by one.

An allocation posted in error is reversed by rescission, a compensating action available to a `LeaseAccountant` on an event in `ALLOCATED`, supplying a `reason_code`. Rescission adds `allocated_arrears_cents` back to the lease's `arrears_balance_cents`; restores each mortgage named in `allocation_detail` by adding its `amount_cents` back to that mortgage's `outstanding_principal_cents`, **replaying the list in reverse order** so that any mortgage driven to `EXTINGUISHED` by the allocation returns to the status it held before it; returns all four of `allocated_arrears_cents`, `allocated_mortgagee_cents`, `allocated_lessee_cents` and `allocated_lessor_cents` to 0; clears `lessee_share_bps` and empties `allocation_detail`; and increments the lease `version` by one. The event moves `ALLOCATED → ALLOCATION_RESCINDED` and the ledger is left byte-identical to its pre-allocation values. From there the waterfall may be re-run against the current ledger — `ALLOCATION_RESCINDED → ALLOCATED` — and the re-run may legitimately use a different `allocation_method` and therefore produce a different split.

### 3.9. Expiry, Reversion Close-Out and the Scheduled Administration Sweep

Deadlines do not enforce themselves. Setting the logical clock forward changes no state; instead a `LessorAdmin` runs the deferred lease-administration sweep, which takes no request body and executes **five phases in this fixed order**, processing the rows within each phase by `created_at` ascending. The ordering is contractual, because earlier phases create work that later phases act on.

**Phase one — insurance.** Every lease whose tenancy axis is `ACTIVE` and whose `insurance_expires_at` is non-null and at or before the logical clock's current instant has its `insurance_status` set to `LAPSED`, has a default notice of `default_type` `INSURANCE_LAPSE` served against it with `served_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM` and the 15-day tenant cure window computed exactly as a hand-served notice would be, and has its tenancy axis driven `ACTIVE → IN_DEFAULT`. A lease whose `insurance_expires_at` is still null has never filed an endorsement and is **not** lapsed by this phase.

**Phase two — cure windows.** Notices in `SERVED` whose `lessee_cure_deadline` the clock has passed strictly move to `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED`. Notices in `LESSEE_CURE_LAPSED` whose `mortgagee_cure_deadline` is non-null and has been passed strictly, and notices in `MORTGAGEE_CURING` whose `mortgagee_cure_deadline` has been passed strictly, both move to `MORTGAGEE_CURE_LAPSED`.

**Phase three — revaluation diary.** Leases whose rent axis is `BASE_ONLY` or `RESET_APPLIED` and whose `next_revaluation_date` is at or before the clock move to `REVALUATION_DUE`. No rent figure changes.

**Phase four — election windows.** Mortgages in `NEW_LEASE_ELIGIBLE` whose `new_lease_election_deadline` the clock has passed strictly move to `EXTINGUISHED`, and the lender's protective rights are gone for good.

**Phase five — expiry.** Leases whose tenancy axis is `ACTIVE` or `IN_DEFAULT` and whose `expiration_date` is at or before the clock reach the end of their term. The tenancy axis becomes `EXPIRED`, the rent axis becomes `FROZEN`, and the lease `version` is incremented by one. A reversion assessment is created in `PENDING_INSPECTION` with its `required_standard_code` snapshotting the lease's `reversion_standard_code`. Every transfer request on the lease in `ACTIVE` becomes `TERMINATED_BY_REVERSION` and the lease's `percentage_rent_sublease_base_cents` is recomputed over the emptied active set. Every mortgage on the lease in `RECORDED`, `NOTICE_SERVED` or `CURING` becomes `EXTINGUISHED`. Where the lease expired out of `IN_DEFAULT` rather than out of `ACTIVE`, the consequences are identical with one addition worth stating: the open default notice is **left exactly where it stands** and is no longer terminable, because termination requires the tenancy axis to be `IN_DEFAULT` and the lease has moved past that.

The run reports itself. `run_at` echoes the logical instant of the run, `insurance_lapses_raised` counts the phase-one lapses, `cure_windows_lapsed` counts the phase-two lapses, `revaluations_flagged` counts the phase-three flags, `mortgages_extinguished` counts the phase-four extinguishments, `leases_expired` counts the phase-five expiries, and `affected_lease_ids` lists the identifiers of every lease any phase touched. The sweep is idempotent within one logical instant: a second run at the same clock value moves nothing and returns zero counts.

Reversion is where a ground lease finally settles up. A reversion assessment normally arrives automatically with phase-five expiry, but an `AssetManager` may also open one against a lease directly, with no request body; either way `required_standard_code` snapshots the lease's `reversion_standard_code` at the moment of creation and is never re-read, so a later terms amendment to the lease's standard does not move an assessment already open. Only one assessment may exist per lease, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD`. The assessment opens in `PENDING_INSPECTION` with `structural_condition_score`, `envelope_condition_score`, `systems_condition_score`, `inspected_by_user_id`, `weighted_score_bps`, `deficiency_bps`, `holdback_cents` and `deposit_released_cents` all null. Assessments can be listed and narrowed by `lease_id` and by status.

An `AssetManager` records the inspection, supplying `structural_condition_score`, `envelope_condition_score` and `systems_condition_score` — three integers each in the inclusive range 0 to 100, with anything outside that range refused as a malformed request. The weighting depends on the condition standard the assessment snapshotted. Under `SHELL_CONDITION` the structural component carries 6,000 basis points, the envelope 3,000 and the building systems 1,000, and the assessment passes at 7,000 basis points. Under `FULLY_FITTED` the structural component carries 4,000, the envelope 3,000 and the systems 3,000, and the assessment passes at 8,500 basis points. Under `BROOM_CLEAN` the structural component carries 7,000, the envelope 2,000 and the systems 1,000, and the assessment passes at 6,000 basis points. `weighted_score_bps` is then computed component by component: multiply each component score by its weight in basis points, divide by 100, round half up to a whole basis point, and add the three rounded component figures together — the per-component rounding precedes the addition. `deficiency_bps` is 10,000 less `weighted_score_bps`. The inspection also records the acting `X-User-ID` in `inspected_by_user_id`, which the holdback's segregation-of-duties check will read, and moves the assessment `PENDING_INSPECTION → INSPECTED`.

Where the premises fall short of the standard, the lessor takes a holdback out of the deposit. Either a `LeaseAccountant` or an `AssetManager` applies it, supplying `acknowledged_holdback_cents`. The assessment must be in `INSPECTED`, and `weighted_score_bps` must be **strictly below** the passing score for the snapshotted `required_standard_code`; at exactly the passing score no holdback may be applied and closing is the only available move, with the attempt refused as `ERR_STANDARD_MET_NO_HOLDBACK`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `inspected_by_user_id`, with the inspector's own attempt refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The holdback itself is computed as whichever is smaller of the lease's `deposit_held_cents` and the deficiency-weighted improvements value — the lease's `improvements_value_cents` multiplied by `deficiency_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents — and the supplied `acknowledged_holdback_cents` must equal that computed figure **exactly**, with any discrepancy refused as `ERR_AMOUNT_NOT_RECONCILED`. Application writes `holdback_cents`, adds it to the lease's `deposit_applied_cents`, subtracts it from the lease's `deposit_held_cents` so that held plus applied plus refunded still equals `security_deposit_cents`, increments the lease `version` by one, and moves the assessment `INSPECTED → HOLDBACK_APPLIED`.

Closing the reversion is an `AssetManager` action taking no request body, and it is reachable from two states with different preconditions. From `INSPECTED` it is available only where `weighted_score_bps` is at or above the passing score for `required_standard_code` — the premises met the standard and no holdback was needed. From `HOLDBACK_APPLIED` it is available unconditionally, the shortfall having already been dealt with. Both branches do the same work: `deposit_released_cents` is set to whatever the lease's `deposit_held_cents` currently is — which, on the holdback branch, is the figure remaining after the holdback was taken — that amount is added to the lease's `deposit_refunded_cents`, and `deposit_held_cents` becomes 0, preserving the deposit invariant exactly. The lease's tenancy axis moves `EXPIRED → REVERTED`, the lease `version` is incremented by one, and the assessment moves to `CLOSED`. `REVERTED` and `CLOSED` are both terminal, and the ground lease is finally at rest.
