# Railcar Manufacturing & Lease Fleet Conversion API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Trinity-style railcar manufacturing is a capital-intensive business in which the same physical asset can be a customer order, a revenue-earning leased unit, a regulatory liability, or a secondary-market disposal — sometimes within a single fiscal quarter. This document specifies the API that runs that entire conversion chain: taking orders against a production catalogue of car types, sourcing wheelsets, castings and coatings whose supplier lead times routinely exceed the customer's order-to-delivery window, scheduling line changeovers across shared production families, and deciding at the moment of completion whether each finished car is sold to the customer or retained in the lease fleet where it will earn per-diem instead of margin.

The lease side of the business is where the accounting complexity concentrates. A retained car carries an acquisition cost that must be capitalized against a contract, depreciated daily, and eventually reconciled against either a retrofit program that extends its regulatory life or a sale that realizes a gain or loss against its depreciated book value. Contracts themselves are living documents: rates and maintenance liability can be amended, and an amendment restates the accruals of every live placement under that contract. Cars move between lessees, come back off placement into a repair queue, and settle mileage true-ups that land on the lessee's ledger balance.

Because regulatory mandates, shop capacity, and credit exposure all impose hard limits that cannot be negotiated at runtime, the API treats them as first-class rejections rather than warnings. Every rule in this document — lead-time feasibility, component shortage, fleet caps, credit ceilings, shop-slot collisions, regulatory life expiry — is enforced deterministically at the moment of the write, and every rejection carries a stable machine-readable error identifier so that upstream planning systems can react without parsing prose.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. Both are mandatory. A request missing either header, or naming a role that is not one of the seven roles declared below, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is declared but not permitted for the requested operation is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403); authorization is evaluated against both the role matrix in this section and the per-endpoint role list in §4.

The platform recognizes exactly seven roles, spelled as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `SupplyChainCoordinator`, `ProductionPlanner`, `LeaseAdministrator`, `PortfolioManager`, `RegulatoryRetrofitist`, and `Lessee`.

`PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator with unconditional override and owns the logical clock; when `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity — the server may record it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same opaque treatment applies to `SupplyChainCoordinator` (who owns the component catalogue, purchase orders and yard receipts), `ProductionPlanner` (who owns the order backlog, the production line schedule and shop work), `LeaseAdministrator` (who owns lease contracts, placements and the return desk, and acts as the second-person approver for substitution endorsements), `PortfolioManager` (who owns secondary-market sales, scrapping and return settlement), and `RegulatoryRetrofitist` (who owns retrofit mandates, their covered car types and program closure). For each of these six roles, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it on the resources the role creates, but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

`Lessee` is the exception. When `X-User-Role` is `Lessee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Lessee`, and where an endpoint does perform an ownership check the resource's foreign-key field is compared to `X-User-ID`. A `Lessee` reads the endpoints listed in §4 on the same terms as every other role — no read is filtered to the resources that name it; the outstanding-balance read is the one exception, and it is available to a lessee only for its own `id`, an attempt on any other `id` being rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

Two duties are deliberately separated. First, clock control is exclusive to `PlatformAdmin`: no other role may advance the logical clock. Second, substitution endorsements are subject to a segregation-of-duties rule — the operator who created an endorsement may not be the operator who approves it, and an approval attempted by the creating `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Several operations are shared rather than exclusive: creating a car type is open to both `ProductionPlanner` and `RegulatoryRetrofitist`; raising a substitution endorsement and scheduling a retrofit work order are open to `ProductionPlanner` and `RegulatoryRetrofitist` alike; and every role may perform the read operations listed in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. A request that names a path the API does not mount, or that uses a method an otherwise matching path does not accept, is answered in this same envelope: an unmapped path yields `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a wrong method yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying the method-rejection's own HTTP status (405).

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, and its smallest permitted value is 1 and its largest is 100; a value outside that band, and a value that is not an integer at all, is a request-shape failure rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). `offset` defaults to 0 and counts the number of matching records to skip before the first returned record; a negative or non-integer `offset` is rejected the same way. A parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent rather than as a value. List endpoints accept their documented filters alongside these two parameters; filters that name a status, a car type, a component, a contract, a car, a production order, a purchase order, a settlement status, a shop line, a program or a superseding order narrow the result set before pagination is applied.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and records whose timestamps are identical — the stamps carry millisecond precision, so records written in one burst can share one — keep the order in which they were inserted. This ordering is the default for every list endpoint and is not overridable by the caller; where a list is derived from a parent resource — the orders that superseded a given production order, for example — the same ascending creation ordering applies within that filtered set.

The `meta` object of a list response carries all four pagination fields: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter across the whole result set, not the number in the current page, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records returned is less than `total_count`. Single-resource reads and writes return the `meta` object without the pagination fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Nested Retrieval and the Production Catalogue

Every persisted resource in this API carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — a `CarType` and a `Component` are distinguishable only by the endpoint that returned them, never by the shape of the identifier. Every resource also carries `created_at` and `updated_at`, both rendered as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, and a `version` integer. `version` starts at 1 for a newly created resource and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation that the state machines in §3 prescribe, with `updated_at` refreshed in the same write. All sixteen resource types in this system declare the common field set, so every one of them exposes `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`; there is no resource in this catalogue that omits `updated_at` or `version`. A write that does not change a resource does not increment its `version`; the cascade writes of §3 are the exception in practice, since each of them versions every resource it visits whether or not that resource's own figures moved — a car re-flagged by a program start, for instance, is versioned even when its flag already stood true.

Nested paths are resolved from left to right. A request such as the listing of the orders that superseded a given production order carries a path parameter `id` for the parent production order, and a request that schedules work on a production line carries a path parameter `line` naming the line. The server resolves each templated identifier in the order it appears in the path and stops at the first one that does not correspond to a persisted row, returning `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A child collection is never consulted before its parent has resolved, so an unresolvable parent masks any problem with the child segment.

The production catalogue has three resources. A `CarType` describes what the factory can build: its `model_code` is a manufacturer code that is unique across the catalogue, its `car_class` is one of `BOXCAR`, `TANK`, `HOPPER`, `FLATCARS` or `GONDOLA`, its `build_hours` is the standard shop time to build one car and is at least 1, its `regulatory_life_years` is the service life granted from the car's build date and is at least 10, and its `line_count_family` names the production line family the type runs on, so that several types in the same family share a line. A car type's `status` is `ACTIVE` while the type is offered and `RETIRED` once it is withdrawn; a newly created type starts `ACTIVE`.

A `Component` describes a purchased part. Its `part_number` is unique across the component catalogue, its `component_class` is one of `WHEELSET`, `CASTING` or `COATING`, its `unit_cost_cents` is the standard cost of one unit in whole cents and may not be negative, and its `lead_time_days` is the supplier's order-to-receipt time in whole days and may not be negative. Two quantity fields track stock. `on_hand_qty` is system-maintained: it starts at 0 when the component is registered unless the caller supplies an opening quantity, is increased only by a receipt, and is decreased only by consumption against a reservation or by a retrofit work order's consumption of its program's required kit — it is never set directly by a client after creation, and no floor is applied to it, so a retrofit consumption larger than the stock on hand drives the figure below zero. `reserved_qty` is derived, not stored input: it starts at 0 and is recomputed from scratch by the component allocation rule of §3.2 on every event that changes commitments, so it is never patched incrementally.

Beyond the catalogue, the system persists nine more resource types, each introduced in §3 and each carrying the common field set described above. A `ProductionOrder` is the backlog unit for a batch of cars of one type; a `SubstitutionEndorsement` is the offer of a replacement component for a short one; a `LeaseContract` is the commercial agreement under which cars are placed; a `LeaseFleetCar` is a car owned by the lease fleet; a `CarAssignment` is the placement of one fleet car with one lessee; a `CarReturn` is the closing record of such a placement; a `RetrofitWorkOrder` is a unit of regulatory shop work on one car; a `ComponentPurchaseOrder` is a supplier order for component units; and a `LeaseFleetSale` is a secondary-market disposal of a fleet car. One further resource is a singleton rather than a collection: the `PortfolioLedger`, which holds the fleet's committed capital, realized gains and net book value and is always present in status `OPEN`.

A `CarTypeComponentRequirement` is the bill-of-material row binding one component to one car type. It names the `car_type_id` and the `component_id`, and states `qty_per_car`, the units of that component consumed per finished car, which is at least 1. The pair of car type and component is unique — a second row for the same pair is a duplicate, not an additional requirement. Together these rows are the authoritative list of what an order for a given car type must consume, and they are the input set for both the price quote and the lead-time feasibility gate described in §3.1.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

This API never reads wall-clock time. All time-dependent behaviour is evaluated against a logical clock that the platform operator drives explicitly, which makes every dated rule in this document reproducible. On server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`; it holds that value until it is set, and it never falls back to the host's system time under any circumstance. A read of the clock returns the current instant as `current_utc`, echoing the instant exactly as it was last set — the startup default therefore reads back as `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` verbatim — and setting the clock rejects a missing or unparseable `to_iso8601` with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Advancing the clock is an operator-only capability: a `PlatformAdmin` supplies a target instant in the `to_iso8601` field, and the clock thereafter reads that instant. Once the clock has moved, every date-dependent rule in the system is evaluated against the new instant — order lead-time feasibility, retrofit compliance deadlines, regulatory life expiry, per-diem accrual windows, depreciation, and fleet net book value all shift together, so a scenario can be driven forward deterministically by a single clock call and no rule may consult any other source of "now".

Business dates (`order_date`, `required_delivery_date`, `term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `return_date`, `built_date`, `regulatory_life_end_date`, `compliance_deadline_date`, `scheduled_start_date`, `scheduled_end_date`, `receipt_date`, `expected_receipt_date`, `sale_date`) are ISO 8601 calendar dates. Where a business date is compared against the logical clock, the clock's date component is used.

Two interval conventions recur throughout the specification and must be applied consistently. First, an end date is exclusive: a placement whose `end_date` falls on a given day leaves the car free from that day onward, and a retrofit shop window that ends on a given day occupies its shop line only up to, but not including, that day. Second, when two half-open intervals are compared, a window that ends exactly when another begins does not collide with it — touching at a boundary is legal, overlapping by any amount is not. The one exception is the placement buffer rule of §3.6, which deliberately requires a gap wider than the half-open convention alone would demand.

### 2.6. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary value in this API is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no endpoint accepts or returns a decimal money value. The monetary fields are `unit_cost_cents`, `unit_price_cents`, `per_diem_rate_cents`, `per_diem_accrued_cents`, `mileage_charge_cents`, `mileage_overage_cents`, `maintenance_cost_cents`, `maintenance_parts_cents`, `acquisition_cost_cents`, `capital_allocation_cents`, `program_cost_cents`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `credit_ceiling_cents`, `sale_price_cents`, `net_book_value_cents`, `writeback_liability_cents`, `gain_loss_cents`, `committed_capital_cents`, `realized_gain_cents`, `fleet_nbv_cents`, `oldest_basis_cents`, `newest_basis_cents`, `drift_cents`, `settled_charges_cents` and `credits_applied_cents`.

Rounding is not left to the implementation's discretion; each calculation in §3 names its own treatment, and the following rules apply wherever those calculations are evaluated. Rounding half-up is never used: every rounding step in this system is either a ceiling to a whole unit or a floor to a whole unit. The price quote rounds each bill-of-material line up to the next whole dollar — that is, up to the next multiple of one hundred cents — before the lines are summed, so the order of operations is per-line ceiling first, summation second, and the sum is never rounded again; the change-fee cap in the same calculation is floored to a whole cent. The capital allocation floors the lessor equity at the final step, and the per-diem accrual floors the utilization credit. The mileage true-up ceilings the number of thirty-day allowance blocks, the feasibility gate ceilings each gated component's derived lead time, the fleet reconciliation ceilings the net book value, and the forecast price variant ceilings both of its uplifted unit costs. The sale gain-or-loss computation applies no rounding step at all, because every operand is already an integer number of cents.

Several derived quantities are clamped rather than rounded. The lessee's outstanding balance is floored at zero after aggregation, so a ledger that would otherwise go negative reports zero. A component's shortfall quantity is zero whenever commitments do not exceed stock, and the overage mileage on a placement is zero whenever the placement stayed within its allowance — an overage of exactly zero produces a charge of exactly zero. In the fleet reconciliation, the disposal proceeds term is floored at zero, and each car's depreciated value is floored at zero individually before the fleet total is taken, so a fully depreciated car contributes nothing rather than a negative amount.

Quantities, distances and durations are whole numbers in their natural units: `qty_per_car`, `qty_ordered`, `qty_received`, `quantity`, `completed_qty`, `component_qty_consumed`, `on_hand_qty`, `reserved_qty` and `accumulated_mileage` are whole units or whole miles; `lead_time_days`, `build_hours`, `planned_shop_days`, `shop_days`, `required_shop_days_per_car` and every date difference are whole days or whole hours; `utilization_threshold_pct` is a whole percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive. Two rates are expressed in basis points rather than percent — the retained-car equity share and its ceiling — and basis points are converted by dividing by ten thousand, not by one hundred. The condition-grade parts table is the one place where an enumerated grade maps directly to a monetary amount, and that mapping is fixed in §3.7.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this system are guarded by an explicit version precondition, because each of them legitimately supersedes an earlier write of the same kind and the only way to tell a supersede from an overwrite is the version the client last observed. Both guards use the same rejection: when the supplied expected version does not equal the parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and no part of the request is applied.

The first guard protects contract amendment drafting. A `POST /lease-contracts/{id}/amendments` request must carry `expected_lease_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of that contract. If the contract's version has advanced since the client read it, the draft is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a contract that is already in `AMENDMENT_DRAFTED` may legitimately be redrafted before ratification — a second draft supersedes the first under the product semantics of §3.5 — so the two writes are distinguished only by the version the writer observed. `expected_lease_version` is a required field of that request, and the same field is required again when the drafted amendment is ratified, so ratification also fails with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) if the contract moved between drafting and ratification.

The second guard protects substitution-endorsement creation. A `POST /substitution-endorsements` request must carry `expected_production_order_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of that order. If the order's version has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the serialization point for endorsement creation: a newly created endorsement for the same original component legitimately supersedes the previously `PENDING` endorsement on that order, so the two writes are distinguished only by the version the writer observed, and the stale writer is prevented from superseding an endorsement it never saw.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version precondition. Endpoints that are not named in this section must not require an `expected_*_version` field, and a client that supplies one to an unguarded endpoint is supplying an unrecognized field rather than a precondition. The endorsement PATCH additionally accepts an optional `expected_substitution_endorsement_version`, which a client may use to guard a rejection or a return-to-pending against the endorsement's own `version`; because it is optional, omitting it leaves that write unguarded.

### 2.8. Regulatory Life, Compliance Windows and Shop Capacity

A lease fleet car's `regulatory_life_end_date` is derived from its build date and its car type: it is the car's `built_date` advanced by the car type's `regulatory_life_years` in whole calendar years, a build date of 29 February landing on 28 February when the target year is not a leap year. Because a completed retrofit resets the car's `built_date` to the completion date, the derived end date is recomputed at that moment and the car's regulatory clock restarts. A car's `retrofit_required` flag is a stored boolean whose derived test is enrollment: it is true while the car is enrolled in a retrofit program whose status is `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `compliance_deadline_date` has not yet passed — the deadline day itself still counts as unexpired, so a program whose deadline equals the current clock date still enrolls — and false otherwise. The flag is written directly by the §3.8 lifecycle — a program start and a work-order scheduling write set it true, and a program completion or voiding sets it false — so it can read true ahead of that test; it is re-derived to the test whenever a car's derived fields are recomputed — at a return, at the repair-queue release, at retrofit work completion, and for the cars a retained order creates — and on every move of the logical clock.

Regulatory life is a hard operating boundary, not a warning. A car whose `regulatory_life_end_date` is on or after the operation date remains eligible for the normal operations that need it — sale, placement, or retrofit scheduling — but a car at or past the end of its regulatory life may only be scrapped. An attempt to sell, place or schedule retrofit work on a car that has passed its life end is rejected with `ERR_REGULATORY_LIFE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); that rejection guards the sale-creation, placement-creation and retrofit-work-order-creation writes. The boundary is inclusive of the end date itself: a car whose `regulatory_life_end_date` equals the current clock date is still eligible for retrofit, and the scrapping decision described in §3.8 treats it the same way.

Retrofit work must fit inside the mandate's compliance window. A shop window is acceptable only if it ends on or before the program's `compliance_deadline_date`; scheduling work whose window would run past the deadline is rejected with `ERR_RETROFIT_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422), and the same deadline test guards both the creation of a work order and the starting of a retrofit program.

Shop lines have exclusive occupancy. The window from `scheduled_start_date` up to but not including `scheduled_end_date` may not collide with any other retrofit work order on the same `shop_line`; a colliding window is rejected with `ERR_SHOP_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because the interval is half-open, a window that ends exactly when another begins is allowed, while any true overlap is not. The same test guards both work-order creation and the starting of scheduled work, and it counts every work order on the line except a `CANCELLED` one — a cancelled window releases its slot, while a `COMPLETED` window still occupies the line it ran on. A work order's derived `shop_days` — the difference between its scheduled end and start — must equal the program's `required_shop_days_per_car`, so the shop occupancy a mandate demands and the occupancy a work order books cannot diverge; a window whose width fails that test is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and a window whose end is not after its start is rejected the same way.

Two upstream prerequisites guard the retrofit path. A work order may only be raised for a car whose car type is covered by the named program through a `RetrofitProgramCarType` row; a car type that is not covered is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Separately, a program that covers no car type at all cannot start, and an order whose car type has no bill-of-material rows cannot reserve components; both situations are rejected with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409), because in each case a required upstream aggregate is missing rather than merely inconsistent.

### 2.9. Ledger, Credit and Capital Principles

Two ledgers run in parallel. The lessee ledger tracks what a customer owes; the portfolio ledger tracks what the fleet is worth and what it has earned. They are never mixed, and a write that touches one never silently adjusts the other.

A lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of its settled net charges minus the credits applied to it, and it is floored at zero — a lessee that has over-credited its account reads a balance of zero rather than a negative one. The balance moves only through the events enumerated in §3: a settled return's mileage true-up, a completed order whose disposition is `SOLD`, a settled secondary-market sale's written-back liability, and the credit that a `RETAINED` order applies back to the balance. A lessee is registered with a `credit_ceiling_cents`, which is the maximum exposure the business will carry for that account, and with a `utilization_threshold_pct`, the utilization percentage at or above which the lessee qualifies for the full per-diem credit described in §3.6; the threshold is a whole number from 0 to 100. A lessee's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`, and a newly registered lessee starts `ACTIVE`.

The credit ceiling is enforced before a charge is booked, not after. When a return would add a projected mileage charge to the balance, or when a placement is created, the lessee's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the projected charge must stay at or below `credit_ceiling_cents`; a balance exactly equal to the ceiling passes, and only an amount strictly above it is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Both the return-recording write and the placement-creation write are guarded by this ceiling. On the placement write the projected charge is the placement's own accrual over its window computed with its recorded mileage standing at zero — the window's day count times the snapshotted rate, less the utilization credit a zero-mileage placement earns at the lessee's threshold — and on the return write it is the mileage true-up recomputed with the placement's already-recorded mileage increased by the `mileage_since_last` the request reports.

Fleet size is capped per contract. A contract carries a `fleet_car_cap`, and the number of live cars in the fleet — those in `HELD`, `ASSIGNED` or `UNDER_REPAIR` — plus the cars an order would add must stay at or below that cap. The cap is checked when a retained order is released, and a breach there is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402); at completion the same overflow is compensated rather than rejected, exactly as §3.4 describes. A contract's `capital_allocation_cents` records the capital that has moved into the lease fleet for `RETAINED` cars under that contract; it starts at 0 and is re-derived by the allocation rule of §3.4 rather than adjusted incrementally.

The portfolio ledger is a singleton row that is always in status `OPEN` and is never created or deleted by a client. It carries three derived figures: `committed_capital_cents`, the capital committed to the fleet through retained orders; `realized_gain_cents`, the cumulative gain or loss realized on settled sales; and `fleet_nbv_cents`, the current net book value of the live fleet. The ledger's `version` increments on every write that touches it, and `realized_gain_cents` moves only on settlement — voiding a sale must leave it exactly where it stood before that sale was created. A sale settles only if it is internally balanced: when a sale's `writeback_liability_cents` exceeds its gain over book value, the settlement cannot balance and is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_UNBALANCED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Cascading Effects and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Almost no write in this system touches a single row. A reservation moves component stock and re-prices an order; a release applies endorsements, re-allocates contract capital and re-derives reserved quantities; a completion creates fleet cars, moves the lessee's balance and advances the portfolio ledger; a ratification restates every live placement under a contract; a sale moves the car, the ledger, the lessee balance and the fleet net book value. The full cascade for each operation is narrated in §3, and the following principles govern all of them.

Every resource mutated by a cascade has its own `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed. A cascade never borrows the parent's version counter, and a resource that is read but not modified is never versioned. Where a state machine declares a side effect that mutates a second entity, that second entity's version and timestamp move in the same logical write as the first.

Derived fields are recomputed from scratch, never patched incrementally, so that any sequence of operations converges on the same value. This applies to a component's `reserved_qty` and its `shortfall_qty`, a car's `regulatory_life_end_date`, `accumulated_mileage` and `retrofit_required`, a contract's `capital_allocation_cents`, a program's `program_cost_cents`, a placement's `per_diem_accrued_cents`, `mileage_charge_cents` and `maintenance_cost_cents`, a lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents`, a sale's `net_book_value_cents` and `gain_loss_cents`, and the portfolio ledger's `fleet_nbv_cents`. A recomputation that produces the same value leaves the field unchanged and does not by itself force a version increment beyond the one the triggering write already applied to the owning resource.

Ownership links move through workflow actions rather than through direct field edits. A fleet car's `lease_contract_id` is stamped when a placement is created against a contract and cleared when the car returns to the fleet through the repair queue or is sold; a car created by a retained order carries the producing order's `production_order_id`, while a car acquired on the secondary market carries no producing order. A component's stock is only ever moved by the four events §3.2 enumerates — reservation, release-time substitution, receipt, and retrofit consumption — and the on-hand figure is authoritative while the reserved figure is derived from it.

Where an operation is a compensating action — voiding a sale, cancelling a reservation, returning an endorsement to pending — it must restore the affected derived fields to the values they would hold had the original write never occurred. Compensation is not a new business event; it is an erasure, and the ledger figures it touches must land exactly where they stood before.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

A request can fail more than one check at once, and the error a client receives must be the one that belongs to the earliest failing check in the order below — not an arbitrary member of the failing set. The checks are evaluated in this fixed sequence, and the first failure short-circuits the rest of the evaluation. One practical refinement applies: a write parses and range-checks the body fields its later gates read before those gates run, so a missing or malformed such field is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even where an earlier-numbered gate would also have failed — the placement, return and retrofit-work-order creates and the changeover write all read their dates and quantities up front, the endorsement PATCH validates the requested `status` before its state and version checks, and the bill-of-material create validates `qty_per_car` before its duplicate-pair test.

First, authentication. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must both be present and must name a declared role; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check applies to every endpoint without exception, including the reads. Second, authorization: the acting role must be permitted for the operation by the matrix in §2.1 and by the endpoint's own role list in §4, and a role that is declared but not permitted is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Third, path resolution: every templated resource identifier in the path must resolve to a persisted row, validated from left to right, and a failure is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); this check applies to every path that carries an `id` or a `line` segment. Fourth, body referential integrity: every foreign key named in the request body must resolve to a persisted row, and a failure is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). This fourth check is not limited to component references despite its name — it covers `lease_fleet_car_id`, `lease_contract_id`, `lessee_id`, `component_id`, `car_type_id`, `production_order_id` and `retrofit_program_id` alike — and it guards the creation of production orders, car assignments, lease contracts, substitution endorsements, retrofit work orders and component purchase orders.

Fifth, state legality: the target resource must be in a state from which the requested action is legal per §3, and an illegal source state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); this check applies to every action endpoint. Sixth, terminality: a resource that has already reached a terminal state accepts no further transitions, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409); this check guards order completion, sale settlement and retrofit-program start. Seventh, version currency: a supplied expected version must equal the parent's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412); this check guards amendment drafting and substitution-endorsement creation.

Eighth, regulatory life: the car's `regulatory_life_end_date` must be on or after the operation date, because a car at or past the end of its regulatory life may only be scrapped; a breach is rejected with `ERR_REGULATORY_LIFE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), and it guards sale creation, placement creation and retrofit work-order creation. Ninth, compliance deadline: a retrofit shop window must end on or before the program's `compliance_deadline_date`, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_RETROFIT_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422) on work-order creation and program start. Tenth, shop-slot exclusivity: the half-open window from `scheduled_start_date` to `scheduled_end_date` must not collide with another work order on the same `shop_line`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_SHOP_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) on work-order creation and work-order start. Eleventh, placement buffering: the half-open placement interval must not collide with another placement of the same car, and because a one-day buffer is required between placements, a `start_date` equal to a prior `end_date` plus zero days or plus one day is also rejected — all of these with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) on placement creation. Twelfth, line exclusivity: the half-open changeover window must not collide with another changeover on the same `line_count_family`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Thirteenth, component sufficiency: for every bill-of-material row of the order's car type whose `component_class` is `WHEELSET` or `CASTING`, the component's `on_hand_qty` must be at least the effective per-car quantity multiplied by the order's `quantity`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHORTAGE` (HTTP 409) on reservation and on endorsement approval. Fourteenth, lead-time feasibility: the order-to-delivery horizon must be at least the worst gated component lead time derived in §3.1, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_INFEASIBLE` (HTTP 422) on order creation and reservation. Fifteenth, material endorsement closure: no endorsement on the order with `decides_material` true may remain in `APPROVED` status when the order is released, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_MATERIAL_ENDORSEMENT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) on release. Sixteenth, declared uniqueness: `model_code` on car types, `part_number` on components, `legal_name` on lessees, `mandate_code` on retrofit programs, and the car-type-and-component pair on bill-of-material rows must each be unique, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROW` (HTTP 409) on the corresponding create endpoints. Seventeenth, request shape: required fields must be present, enum members must be legal, and numeric ranges must hold — `quantity` at least 1, `priority_rank` at least 1, `planned_shop_days` at least 1 and equal to the car type's `build_hours` multiplied by `quantity`, `term_end_date` strictly after `term_start_date`, `completed_qty`, `mileage_allowance`, `mileage_since_last`, `per_diem_rate_cents`, `credit_ceiling_cents`, `fleet_car_cap`, `sale_price_cents`, `writeback_liability_cents` and `net_book_value_cents` at least 0, and `qty_ordered`, `qty_received`, `component_qty_consumed`, `substitute_qty_per_car` and `required_shop_days_per_car` at least 1 — with a breach rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) on every write endpoint.

Eighteenth, credit exposure: the lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the projected mileage charge must stay at or below the lessee's `credit_ceiling_cents`, with equality permitted, and an excess is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) on return recording and placement creation. Nineteenth, fleet cap: the live car count plus the cars an order would add must stay at or below the contract's `fleet_car_cap`, and an excess on release is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402); an excess on completion is compensated instead of rejected, exactly as §3.4 describes. Twentieth, settlement balance: a sale whose `writeback_liability_cents` exceeds its gain over book value may not settle, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_UNBALANCED` (HTTP 409). Twenty-first, car availability: the car must be in `HELD` status for every operation that requires a free car, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_CAR_NOT_HELD` (HTTP 409) on sale creation, placement creation, retrofit work-order creation and scrapping. Twenty-second, lessee correspondence: the lessee named in the request must be the lessee of the referenced contract, or of the sold car's terminated contract, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_LESSEE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) on placement creation and sale creation. Twenty-third, return closure: every return of a car must be `SETTLED` before the car may leave `UNDER_REPAIR`, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_UNSETTLED_RETURNS` (HTTP 409) on the settle-returns action. Twenty-fourth, program coverage: the car's car type must be covered by the retrofit program through a `RetrofitProgramCarType` row, and a car type that is not covered is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) on work-order creation.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Order Intake, Quoting and Lead-Time Feasibility

Backlog management begins with the catalogue. A `ProductionPlanner` or a `RegulatoryRetrofitist` registers a car type by supplying its `model_code`, `car_class`, `build_hours`, `regulatory_life_years` and `line_count_family`; a `SupplyChainCoordinator` registers a component with its `part_number`, `component_class`, `unit_cost_cents` and `lead_time_days`, optionally opening the stock position with an `on_hand_qty`; and a `ProductionPlanner` binds the two together by adding a bill-of-material row carrying `car_type_id`, `component_id` and `qty_per_car`. All three creates are subject to the declared uniqueness rules — a repeated model code, part number, or car-type-and-component pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROW` (HTTP 409), and a range violation such as a `build_hours` below 1 or a `regulatory_life_years` below 10 is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Taking an order is where the two halves of feasibility meet. The planner supplies `car_type_id`, `quantity`, `planned_shop_days`, `disposition`, `priority_rank`, `order_date` and `required_delivery_date`, and may supply a `unit_price_cents`, a `lessee_id`, a `retention_reason`, and a `supersedes_production_order_id` when the order is being raised to replace a previously cancelled one. A supplied `unit_price_cents` is never used: the order's price is always the quote the algorithm below produces, at intake and at every later re-quote. The order starts in `DRAFT`. Two checks run before it is accepted. The request shape must hold — `quantity` at least 1, `priority_rank` at least 1, `planned_shop_days` at least 1 and equal to the car type's `build_hours` multiplied by `quantity` — or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). And the order must be feasible against supplier lead times: the horizon from `order_date` to `required_delivery_date` must be at least the worst gated lead time derived below, or the order is rejected with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_INFEASIBLE` (HTTP 422). The `disposition` chosen at intake — `SOLD` for a customer sale, `RETAINED` for the lease fleet — determines which branch of the completion workflow the order will eventually take, and `priority_rank` fixes its place in the backlog: lower values schedule first, ties are broken by the earlier `order_date`, and remaining ties by the earlier creation timestamp.

Pricing is quoted, not negotiated, and it is quoted on two bases. The committed basis applies once the order has been component-reserved or released, at which point contract prices and the endorsed composition are locked. The forecast basis applies while the order is still `DRAFT` and the current date is more than the lock horizon of 120 days past `order_date`. Both bases run the same line-item algorithm; the forecast basis first re-prices two classes of input, as described below.

The algorithm proceeds as follows. Take every bill-of-material row of the order's car type and order them by the component's `part_number` in ascending order, breaking ties by the row's `created_at` in ascending order. For each row, the effective quantity is the `substitute_qty_per_car` of the endorsement on this order whose `original_component_id` matches the row's `component_id` and whose status is `APPROVED` or `APPLIED`, if such an endorsement exists — and when more than one endorsement matches the row, the most recently created of them governs — otherwise the effective quantity is the row's own `qty_per_car`. Endorsements in `PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` status are excluded from that substitution set entirely and never affect the quote. The line amount for the row is then the effective quantity multiplied by the `unit_cost_cents` of the component standing in for that row — the substitute's own cost when an `APPROVED` or `APPLIED` endorsement covers the row, the row's own component's cost otherwise — plus a line surcharge of 25,000 cents, divided by one hundred, rounded up to the next whole number, and multiplied back by one hundred — that is, each line is rounded up to the next whole dollar before it is summed. The component subtotal is the sum of those line amounts, in the row order established above. The labor charge is the car type's `build_hours` multiplied by the labor rate of 45,000 cents per build hour. The unrounded total is the component subtotal plus the labor charge. The change-fee cap is 8 percent of the unrounded total, floored to a whole cent. The raw change fee is 15,000 cents multiplied by the number of endorsements on this order whose status is `APPROVED` or `APPLIED`. The change fee actually charged is the smaller of the raw fee and the cap, and the order's `unit_price_cents` is the unrounded total plus that change fee. Evaluation order is pinned: per-line ceilings first, summation second, the cap and the fee third, and no further rounding after the final addition.

The forecast basis differs from the committed basis in exactly two substitutions, both applied to the input costs before the line loop begins and both confined to an order that is still `DRAFT`; only the first of them is conditioned on the 120-day horizon, so the coating uplift below applies to a draft order of any age whose coating lead time exceeds its threshold. First, for every row whose `component_class` is `WHEELSET` or `CASTING` and where the current date is more than 120 days past the order's `order_date`, the component's `unit_cost_cents` is replaced by that cost multiplied by 110 and divided by 100, rounded up to a whole cent — the locked contract price has lapsed. Second, for every row whose `component_class` is `COATING` and whose component `lead_time_days` exceeds 60 days, the component's `unit_cost_cents` is replaced by that cost multiplied by 104 and divided by 100, rounded up to a whole cent. Because both substitutions only raise costs, the resulting `unit_price_cents` is strictly greater than the committed variant's whenever at least one row is affected.

Lead-time feasibility is evaluated on the gated classes only. The horizon is the number of days from the order's `order_date` to its `required_delivery_date`. The gated rows are those bill-of-material rows of the order's car type whose component belongs to the `WHEELSET` or `CASTING` class; `COATING` rows are excluded, because coatings are procured against a service contract and never gate release. For each gated row, the derived lead time is the component's `lead_time_days` divided by the row's `qty_per_car`, rounded up to a whole day, and the worst gated lead time is the largest such figure across all gated rows. An order whose car type has no gated rows at all takes a worst gated lead time of zero by convention, so it is always feasible on this axis. The order passes the gate exactly when the horizon is greater than or equal to the worst gated lead time; anything shorter is rejected with `ERR_LEAD_TIME_INFEASIBLE` (HTTP 422).

### 3.2. Component Sourcing, Reservation and Receipts

Once an order is on the books, the planner commits stock to it. Reservation is legal only from `DRAFT`, and it transitions the order to `COMPONENT_RESERVED`. Before any stock moves, the order must actually have a bill of materials: an order whose car type has no bill-of-material rows cannot reserve anything and is rejected with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409). Reservation then walks every bill-of-material row of the order's car type. For rows in the `WHEELSET` and `CASTING` classes, the component's `on_hand_qty` must be at least the effective per-car quantity multiplied by the order's `quantity`, where the effective quantity is the endorsed substitute quantity when an `APPROVED` or `APPLIED` endorsement covers that row and the row's own `qty_per_car` otherwise; a shortfall on any covered row rejects the whole reservation with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHORTAGE` (HTTP 409), and the same shortage test guards the approval of a substitution endorsement. `COATING` rows are exempt from the on-hand test — they are procured to contract and commit through the purchase-receipt path instead — but they are not exempt from the lead-time gate of §3.1.

A successful reservation commits the wheelset and casting stock: for each covered row, the component's `on_hand_qty` is decremented by the effective per-car quantity multiplied by the order quantity, and the component's `reserved_qty` is recomputed by the allocation rule below. The order is then re-quoted on the committed basis, its status becomes `COMPONENT_RESERVED`, and its `version` and `updated_at` move. There is one automatic branch inside this write: if a substitution endorsement for this order is still `PENDING` and names the very component the order is short of, and the substitute's own stock test passes — the substitute's `on_hand_qty` is at least its `substitute_qty_per_car` multiplied by the order quantity — then the endorsement is approved by the system as part of the reservation, and the reservation commits the substitute's stock instead of the original's for that requirement row. The manual approval path described in §3.3 is the operator's alternative to this automatic endorsement.

Reserved quantity is derived, never patched. For a given component, the reservation term is the sum, over every bill-of-material row that names that component, of the row's `qty_per_car` multiplied by the total quantity of production orders that build that row's car type and are in `RELEASED` status; orders in `DRAFT`, `COMPONENT_RESERVED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. The retrofit term is the sum of `component_qty_consumed` over every retrofit work order that is in `IN_PROGRESS` status and whose program names this component as its `required_component_id`; work orders in `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. The component's `reserved_qty` is the reservation term plus the retrofit term, and its `shortfall_qty` is the amount by which that reserved figure exceeds `on_hand_qty`, or zero when it does not. The evaluation order is pinned: `on_hand_qty` is authoritative and read first, and `reserved_qty` is re-derived from scratch on every event rather than incremented or decremented in place. A read taken after a receipt has landed therefore shows the same reservations term with a larger on-hand figure, and consequently a lower shortfall.

Because wheelsets, castings and coatings are bought on lead times that routinely exceed the order-to-delivery window, the purchasing path runs in parallel with the order. A `SupplyChainCoordinator` raises a purchase order naming the `component_id`, the `qty_ordered`, the agreed `unit_cost_cents`, the `order_date` and the `expected_receipt_date`; the purchase starts in `PLACED`. The expected receipt date is always supplied by the caller: the server never derives it from the supplier lead time, and a purchase whose `qty_ordered` is below 1 or whose `unit_cost_cents` is negative is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A placed purchase can be confirmed by its supplier, moving it to `CONFIRMED`, and it can be cancelled while it is still `PLACED`, moving it to `CANCELLED`; once confirmed it can no longer be cancelled. Both the confirmation and the cancellation are supply-chain actions, and neither moves stock.

Stock moves only on receipt. A coordinator books a `ComponentReceipt` against the purchase with the `component_purchase_order_id`, the `component_id`, the `qty_received` and the `receipt_date`; the two identifiers are resolved independently and are not cross-checked against one another, so it is the receipt's own `component_id` whose stock the later receiving action moves. Booking the receipt alone does not change stock. Applying it — the receiving action on that receipt — is what moves the goods: the parent purchase order must be `CONFIRMED`, and the receipt's `receipt_date` must not be before the purchase order's `order_date`, or the write is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On success the purchase order becomes `RECEIVED`, the component's `on_hand_qty` is increased by `qty_received`, and `reserved_qty` and `shortfall_qty` are recomputed by the allocation rule above. The component's `version`, the purchase order's `version` and the receipt's own record all move together. Throughout this path, a body that names a component, a purchase order or any other foreign key that does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before any state check runs, and an action attempted from the wrong source state — confirming a purchase that is not `PLACED`, receiving against one that is not `CONFIRMED`, or cancelling one that is not `PLACED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.3. Substitution Endorsements: Segregation of Duties and the Retry Cycle

When a required component cannot be sourced, a `ProductionPlanner` or a `RegulatoryRetrofitist` may raise a substitution endorsement offering a different part in its place. The creating write names the `production_order_id` awaiting the endorsement, the `original_component_id` the order is short of, the `substitute_component_id` being offered, the `substitute_qty_per_car` that will override the junction row's quantity while the endorsement is `APPROVED`, and the `decides_material` flag, which is true when the substitution can change the material composition of a car because the substitute's component class differs from the original's and which defaults to false when the request omits it. The write must also carry `expected_production_order_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the production order; if the order has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. This is the serialization point that stops a stale writer from superseding an endorsement it never saw, because a newly created endorsement for the same original component legitimately supersedes the previously `PENDING` endorsement on that order. A successful create starts the endorsement in `PENDING`, supersedes any earlier `PENDING` endorsement for the same original component on that order, and increments the production order's `version`.

Approval is a second-person action and belongs to a `LeaseAdministrator`. It is legal only from `PENDING`, that status test running ahead of the segregation-of-duties test so an endorsement in any other status reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) regardless of who is acting. It also carries a segregation-of-duties precondition: the acting `X-User-ID` must not be the `X-User-ID` that created the endorsement, and an approval attempted by the creator is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). On success the endorsement becomes `APPROVED`, the production order's `unit_price_cents` is recomputed on the committed basis of §3.1 with the endorsed substitute standing in for the original component, and the endorsement's own `version` and `updated_at` move — the refreshed quote is written onto the order row, but the order's own `version` and `updated_at` are left where they stood. Approving one whose substitute cannot cover the order is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHORTAGE` (HTTP 409) by the same stock test that guards reservation.

An endorsement can also reach `APPROVED` without an operator: as described in §3.2, a reservation that finds a `PENDING` endorsement naming the exact component the order is short of, with sufficient substitute stock on hand, approves it automatically and commits the substitute's stock in place of the original's for that requirement row. The manual approval path exists for the cases where the planner wants the substitution decided explicitly rather than at reservation time.

Approval is not the end of the story — the substitution must be applied before the order ships. Releasing a component-reserved order applies every `APPROVED` endorsement on it, moving each to `APPLIED`. Applying an endorsement replaces the original component's committed stock with the substitute's for the endorsed requirement row and recomputes `reserved_qty` for both components by the allocation rule of §3.2, and it re-quotes the order on the committed basis; an endorsement whose substitute the reservation already committed under the automatic branch of §3.2 is not swapped a second time — it is marked `APPLIED` without a further stock move. One precondition couples the endorsements on an order together: any endorsement on the same order that is `APPROVED` and has `decides_material` true blocks the release outright: the release is rejected with `ERR_MATERIAL_ENDORSEMENT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) and the endorsement is left `APPROVED`, so a material substitution is never applied by a release. This is the check that keeps the material composition of a car from changing after the order has been released to the line.

Rejection and retry run on the same editing write, which is authorized for both the `LeaseAdministrator` and the `ProductionPlanner` and which accepts a `status` field plus an optional `expected_substitution_endorsement_version`. Either authorized role may drive either direction — the role does not restrict which of the two statuses may be requested, and a `status` outside those two is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A `LeaseAdministrator` uses it to reject a `PENDING` endorsement, which moves it to `REJECTED` and increments its version. A `ProductionPlanner` uses it to return a `REJECTED` endorsement to `PENDING` so the substitution can be attempted again, and that write clears any committed quantity recorded for the substitution on the production order and increments the endorsement's version. The retry is bounded: an endorsement that has already been rejected once may be returned to `PENDING` one time, and the third rejection is terminal — an attempt to return it to `PENDING` a second time is rejected with `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_RETRY_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). The cycle `PENDING → REJECTED → PENDING → REJECTED` is therefore allowed exactly once, and the second return to `PENDING` is the last retry the endorsement will ever get. An edit attempted from a status the editing write does not accept is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and `APPLIED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal: an endorsement that reaches any of them accepts no further transitions.

### 3.4. Release, Line Changeover and Completion: Sold versus Retained Cars

An order that has its components committed is ready to be released to the backlog. Release is legal from `COMPONENT_RESERVED` and requires that every bill-of-material row of the order's car type has committed supply: for wheelsets and castings, the stock released at reservation counts as the commitment, while for coatings a purchase order must exist for that component with status `CONFIRMED` or `RECEIVED` and an `expected_receipt_date` on or before the order's `required_delivery_date`. The release also requires that no other order on the same `line_count_family` is already `IN_PRODUCTION` with a higher-priority backlog position that this release would preempt; a release that would preempt such an order is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a coating requirement with no `CONFIRMED` or `RECEIVED` purchase order whose `expected_receipt_date` falls on or before the order's `required_delivery_date` is rejected with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409). A release that would leave a material endorsement in flight is rejected with `ERR_MATERIAL_ENDORSEMENT_PENDING` (HTTP 409), and a release that would push the fleet past its contract cap is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) — that cap test being applied to a `RETAINED` release only, and only when the release body names a `lease_contract_id`, so a retained release that names no contract passes the cap test untouched.

A successful release moves the order to `RELEASED`, applies every `APPROVED` substitution endorsement on it, and re-quotes the order on the committed basis. When the order's `disposition` is `RETAINED`, the release also records the order's `retention_reason` — the stated justification for keeping the cars in the fleet rather than selling them, a value supplied on the release body overwriting any recorded at intake — attributes the order to the named contract's lessee when the order itself names none, and re-derives the capital allocation for the lease contract named in the release body, which the request accepts as an optional `lease_contract_id` for exactly this purpose. The order's `version` and `updated_at` move with the write.

Capital allocation is an aggregation over the cars an order has actually delivered into the fleet. The retained-car set is every fleet car whose `production_order_id` points at this order and whose status is one of the three live fleet states, `HELD`, `ASSIGNED` or `UNDER_REPAIR`; cars that have been sold or scrapped are excluded, which is why scrapping a car re-derives a smaller capital figure for its former contract. The capital figure is the size of that set multiplied by the order's `unit_price_cents`. The lessor equity derived from that capital applies the fleet share inside the cap so that the evaluation order is pinned: the fleet share is 700 basis points, the ceiling is 900 basis points, and the equity is the capital multiplied by the smaller of those two figures, divided by ten thousand, and floored to a whole cent. The capital figure — not the lessor equity — is what is written to the named contract's `capital_allocation_cents`; the equity is a derived quantity that is not a field of any resource. On the `SOLD` basis the capital never enters the fleet at all: `capital_allocation_cents` keeps whatever value it previously held, and the order's value is accrued against the lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` instead.

Production starts at a line changeover, and the changeover is also the scheduling act. The `line` path segment names a production line family: it resolves when at least one persisted car type carries that `line_count_family`, and a segment matching no car type's family is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); an order whose car type runs on a different family is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `ProductionPlanner` supplies the `production_order_id` to be started together with the `scheduled_start_date` and `scheduled_end_date` of the changeover window. The order must be `RELEASED`, and it must be the head of the backlog schedule for its car type's `line_count_family` — the order with the lowest `priority_rank`, ties broken by the earliest `order_date` and then by the earliest creation timestamp. The half-open window from `scheduled_start_date` up to but not including `scheduled_end_date` must not collide with any other changeover on the same line family, because the line is exclusive-occupancy; a collision is rejected with `ERR_CHANGEOVER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). On success the order moves to `IN_PRODUCTION`, its `planned_shop_days` is set to the width of the changeover window — which must still equal the car type's `build_hours` multiplied by the order's `quantity` — and its `version` and `updated_at` move.

Completion closes the order and decides the car's economic fate. It is legal only from `IN_PRODUCTION`, and it completes the remaining cars in one step: the `completed_qty` supplied on the request, plus the quantity already finished, must equal the order's `quantity`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The request also accepts an optional `completion_date`, which is the date the cars are deemed finished — when it is omitted the logical clock's current date is taken — and an optional `lessee_id` used on the sold branch. An order that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` accepts no further transition and is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409); the fleet cap is re-checked here, but an overflow on this write is compensated rather than rejected, exactly as the next paragraph describes.

On the sold branch the lessee the completion settles against is the lessee named on the request, or, when the body omits `lessee_id`, the lessee the order itself names, or, when it names none, the lessee of the only `LESSOR`-liable contract in `ACTIVE` status; when no candidate resolves, the completion still succeeds and no lessee is charged. The resolved lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` is debited by the order's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the order's `quantity`, the charge being carried into the balance by the reconciliation rule of §3.9 rather than by a direct decrement. On the retained branch, completion creates one fleet car per car built, each starting in `HELD` with its `production_order_id` pointing at the producing order, its `acquisition_cost_cents` equal to the order's `unit_price_cents`, and its `built_date` set to the completion date; each new car's `regulatory_life_end_date` and `retrofit_required` are then derived, and the contract's capital allocation is re-derived by the rule above. The completion also advances the portfolio ledger: its `committed_capital_cents` is increased by the order's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the number of cars completed, and its `fleet_nbv_cents` is re-derived by the reconciliation rule of §3.9. The order itself ends as `COMPLETED` with `completed_qty` equal to `quantity`, and its `version` and `updated_at` move.

One compensating path exists inside the retained branch and must be honoured exactly. If completing a retained order would push the fleet's retained-car count above the cap the contract allows, the cars are still created but the overflow car is born scrapped rather than held: creating three fleet cars while three cars remain in the retained backlog of a contract that can accept only two more results in the third car being created with status `SCRAPPED` instead of `HELD`, and the order still reports `COMPLETED` with `completed_qty` equal to `quantity`. The order is never blocked by this overflow; only the car's initial state changes.

An order that has not yet been released can be abandoned. Cancellation is legal from `DRAFT` or `COMPONENT_RESERVED`, and the request accepts an optional `reason`. Cancelling a draft changes nothing but the status. Cancelling a component-reserved order restores the `on_hand_qty` of every wheelset and casting row of the order's car type — coating rows were never decremented and are not restored — at the effective per-car quantity the reservation committed, meaning the endorsed substitute's quantity where an endorsement covers the row, and it leaves each component's `reserved_qty` unchanged, and it supersedes every endorsement of that order that is still in `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `APPLIED` status — a cancelled order can never ship a substitution. Either cancellation moves the order to `CANCELLED` and increments its `version`.

### 3.5. Lease Contracts: Drafting, Activation, Amendment and Termination

A lessee account is opened before any contract names it. A `LeaseAdministrator` registers the account with its `legal_name`, its `credit_tier` — one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, with `A` the strongest standing — its `utilization_threshold_pct` between 0 and 100, and its `credit_ceiling_cents`, the maximum exposure the business will carry. A duplicate legal name is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROW` (HTTP 409). The account starts `ACTIVE` with an `outstanding_balance_cents` of 0.

Contracts are then written against that account. The drafting write names the `lessee_id`, the `lease_type`, the `per_diem_rate_cents`, the `liability_bearer`, the `term_start_date`, the `term_end_date` and the `fleet_car_cap`. The `lease_type` is either `FULL_SERVICE`, in which the daily rate bundles maintenance into the rate, or `NET`, in which it does not. The `liability_bearer` is either `LESSOR` or `LESSEE` and decides who pays for maintenance and regulatory retrofit on cars under the contract. The term must end strictly after it starts, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and a lessee or contract identifier that does not resolve is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A drafted contract starts in `DRAFT` with an `amendment_seq` of 0 and a `capital_allocation_cents` of 0.

A contract is put into force by activation, which is legal only from `DRAFT`. Two preconditions guard it: the contract's `term_end_date` must be later than the logical clock's current date, so a lapsed term can never be activated — that attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and no placement referencing the contract, whatever its status, may hold a date interval that overlaps another's under the plain half-open test of §2.5, with no placement buffer applied to this comparison; a colliding pair is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Activation moves the contract to `ACTIVE` and increments its `version`. `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` are the contract's terminal states, and a contract that reaches either accepts no further transitions.

The amendment cycle is the contract's revision loop: `ACTIVE → AMENDMENT_DRAFTED → ACTIVE`. Drafting an amendment is legal from `ACTIVE` or from `AMENDMENT_DRAFTED`, is authorized for the `LeaseAdministrator`, and carries the new `per_diem_rate_cents` and `liability_bearer` together with a required `expected_lease_version`. The expected version must equal the contract's current `version`; if the contract has advanced since the client read it, the draft is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. This guard exists because a contract that is already `AMENDMENT_DRAFTED` may legitimately be redrafted before ratification, so a second draft supersedes the first and the two writes are distinguished only by the version the writer observed. A successful draft stores the proposed rate and bearer on the contract row itself — the contract's `per_diem_rate_cents` and `liability_bearer` become the drafted values, so a second draft supersedes the first — moves the contract to `AMENDMENT_DRAFTED`, and increments its `version`. The contract view carries the drafted terms a second time as `draft_per_diem_rate_cents` and `draft_liability_bearer`, both null until an amendment is drafted and thereafter mirroring the drafted values.

Ratification applies the drafted terms and restates everything the contract governs. It is legal only from `AMENDMENT_DRAFTED`, and it likewise requires `expected_lease_version` to match the contract's current `version`, rejecting a stale attempt with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). On success the contract returns to `ACTIVE`, its `amendment_seq` is incremented by one, and two cascades run. First, the newly ratified `per_diem_rate_cents` is refreshed onto every placement referencing this contract whose status is `ACTIVE`, and each such placement's `per_diem_accrued_cents` is recomputed by the accrual rule of §3.6 — which is why the ratify path produces a restated accrual rather than a continuation of the old one. Second, `maintenance_cost_cents` is recomputed on every placement of this contract by the maintenance allocation rule of §3.7 under the newly ratified `liability_bearer`, reading the condition grade from the placement's latest recorded return and attributing a zero parts cost to a placement that has no return at all, so flipping the bearer from `LESSOR` to `LESSEE` moves the maintenance burden off the lessor's books for every live placement at once. Every placement referencing the contract is versioned by this write, whether or not its figures changed, and the contract's `version` and `updated_at` move with the ratification.

Termination ends the contract, and it does not silently strand live placements. It is legal from `ACTIVE`. The request accepts an optional `cascade_assignment_ids` list, and the precondition is that no placement referencing this contract may be in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status unless the body enumerates every such placement in that list — enumerating them is therefore mandatory whenever any live placement exists, a shortfall being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and a `cascade_assignment_ids` that is not a list at all being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On success the contract becomes `TERMINATED`, and for each placement of this contract in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status the placement is set to `COMPLETED` and its parent car is returned to `HELD` — so a terminated contract leaves no car still flagged as placed. Termination does not reach into the lessee ledger at all: no balance, charge or credit moves on this write, whatever the closed placements held. The contract's `version` and `updated_at` move with the write.

Two contract-scoped limits bite elsewhere in the lifecycle and are worth restating here because the contract is where they live. The `fleet_car_cap` bounds the live fleet a retained order may grow into, and it is checked at release and at completion with a breach rejected as `ERR_FLEET_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). The `capital_allocation_cents` figure is not client-writable at all: it is re-derived from the retained cars that point at orders under this contract, so it only ever moves as cars complete, are scrapped, or are released under it.

### 3.6. Placements: Assignment, the Per-Diem Meter, Suspension and Reassignment

A placement is created by a `LeaseAdministrator` and is the only route by which a car leaves the free fleet for revenue service; there is no separate activation endpoint for a car. The creating write names the `lease_fleet_car_id`, the `lease_contract_id` that will govern the placement, the `lessee_id` being served, the `start_date`, the `end_date` and the `mileage_allowance`. The end date is exclusive: the car is free from that date onward. Four conditions must hold. The car must be in `HELD` status — an operation that requires a free car and finds one that is not held is rejected with `ERR_CAR_NOT_HELD` (HTTP 409). The target contract must be `ACTIVE`. The lessee named on the request must be the lessee of the referenced contract, or the write is rejected with `ERR_LESSEE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). And the car must not be enrolled in any retrofit program whose status is `IN_PROGRESS`, because a car under an active mandate cannot be placed into service, that attempt being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A car past the end of its regulatory life may not be placed either — the test compares the car's `regulatory_life_end_date` against the placement's own `start_date` rather than against the current clock date — and such an attempt is rejected with `ERR_REGULATORY_LIFE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Placements of the same car are buffered, not merely non-overlapping. The half-open interval from `start_date` up to but not including `end_date` must not collide with any other placement of the same car, and because the fleet requires a one-day buffer between placements, a `start_date` that equals a prior `end_date` plus zero days or plus one day is also rejected. Every one of those cases is rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The credit ceiling is checked on the same write: the lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the projected charge must stay at or below `credit_ceiling_cents`, with equality permitted, or the write is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

On success the car moves to `ASSIGNED`, the car's `lease_contract_id` is stamped with the governing contract, and the placement's `per_diem_rate_cents` is snapshotted from the contract's rate at that moment. That snapshot matters: every later accrual reads the rate stored on the placement, never the contract's current rate, so a later amendment only affects placements through the explicit restatement cascade of §3.5. The placement starts in `ACTIVE` with `mileage_used`, `mileage_charge_cents` and `maintenance_cost_cents` all at 0, and its `per_diem_accrued_cents` is computed by the accrual rule below. The car's `version` and the placement's own record move together.

Per-diem accrual is a chronological computation with a utilization credit. The accrual window ends at the placement's `end_date` when the placement is `COMPLETED` or `REASSIGNED`, and at the logical clock's current date otherwise. The number of days is that end minus the `start_date`, with the end date exclusive, and the gross accrual is those days multiplied by the placement's snapshotted `per_diem_rate_cents`. The utilization percentage is the placement's `mileage_used` divided by one hundred and floored to a whole number, so every full one hundred miles adds one point. The credit is the gross accrual multiplied by 12 and divided by 100, floored to a whole cent, but only when the lessee's `utilization_threshold_pct` is at or below the computed utilization percentage; otherwise the credit is exactly zero. The placement's `per_diem_accrued_cents` is the gross accrual minus the credit. An open placement evaluated in this way is the `current_open` basis, and it ends its window at the current clock date.

A restated accrual is produced whenever the placement history is rewritten rather than merely continued — after a contract amendment, after a reassignment, or at a return. The restated basis walks every placement of the same car in `start_date` ascending order, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, clips each interval to the governing contract's `term_start_date` up to but not including its `term_end_date`, and clips it again to the window from 365 days before the current clock date up to but not including the current clock date. The clipped day counts are summed and multiplied once by the snapshotted rate. The utilization credit applies to the restated basis exactly as it does to the open basis: the gross restated accrual is reduced by the 12-percent credit whenever the lessee's `utilization_threshold_pct` is at or below the computed utilization percentage, and the difference is what the placement's `per_diem_accrued_cents` stores. This restated figure differs from the open basis whenever a placement was suspended, reassigned, or clipped by a contract term bound, which is precisely why ratifying an amendment produces a new accrual rather than an additive one.

The pause loop is `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Suspension is legal when the placement is `ACTIVE`, the parent car is `ASSIGNED` and the governing contract is `ACTIVE`; the request accepts an optional `suspend_date` that fixes the date the meter stops. On success the placement becomes `SUSPENDED`, per-diem accrual stops, the parent car returns to `HELD`, and the placement's `per_diem_accrued_cents` is recomputed as of the suspension date by the accrual rule above — when the request omits `suspend_date`, that recomputation is taken as of the current clock date instead; both the placement's and the car's versions move. Resumption is legal from `SUSPENDED` or from `REASSIGNED`, and it requires the governing contract to still be `ACTIVE` and the parent car to be `HELD`; it returns the placement to `ACTIVE`, restarts the meter, puts the car back to `ASSIGNED`, and increments both versions, leaving the placement's stored `per_diem_accrued_cents` exactly where the suspension left it — the figure is not recomputed by the resumption itself, only by the next restating event.

Reassignment moves a placement to a different car without ending the commercial relationship. It is legal from `ACTIVE`, and the request names the `target_lease_fleet_car_id` and accepts an optional `effective_date`. The target car must be `HELD` and must carry the same `lease_contract_id` as the source car, so a placement can never hop between contracts through this route; a target that fails either test is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the source placement becomes `REASSIGNED` and its car is returned to `HELD`, and a replacement placement is created on the target car with a `start_date` equal to the source placement's `end_date`, an `end_date` equal to that same date — a zero-width window — a `mileage_allowance` copied from the source placement, and a `per_diem_rate_cents` snapshotted from the same contract; the source placement's, the source car's and the target car's versions move. An `effective_date` supplied on the request is accepted but not used: the replacement always begins at the source placement's `end_date`. The reassignment loop is `ACTIVE → REASSIGNED → ACTIVE`: a placement may move to another car and later be resumed, and resuming a reassigned placement restores it to `ACTIVE` on its new car.

### 3.7. Returns, Mileage True-Up and the Repair Queue

A return is the mirror of a placement: it closes the commercial interval and starts the physical one. A `LeaseAdministrator` records the return with the `lease_fleet_car_id`, the `car_assignment_id` being closed, the `return_date`, the `mileage_since_last` the car ran on this placement, and a `condition_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`. The placement being closed must be in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status; the `return_date` must be strictly later than the placement's `start_date`, a breach being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); and no other placement of the car named on the request may hold a date interval that would overlap the interval being closed — that interval running from the placement's `start_date` up to but not including the `return_date`, compared against the other placements' own half-open intervals under the plain §2.5 test with no buffer applied — a conflict being rejected with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The car named in the body is used for the physical transition and is not cross-checked against the placement's own car. The lessee's credit ceiling is checked here too: the current balance plus the projected mileage charge must stay at or below `credit_ceiling_cents`, with equality permitted, or the return is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

On success the placement becomes `COMPLETED`, the parent car moves to `UNDER_REPAIR`, and the car's `accumulated_mileage` is recomputed as the total miles recorded across every placement and return for that car. The car's `retrofit_required` flag is re-derived at the same time, and both the placement's and the car's versions move. The return record itself starts with a `settlement_status` of `PENDING`.

The mileage true-up is charged at return, and it is computed from the placement's own allowance rather than the contract's. The number of days in the placement is the placement's `end_date` minus its `start_date`. That figure is divided by thirty and rounded up to a whole number to give the number of allowance blocks; the included miles are those blocks multiplied by the placement's `mileage_allowance`. The overage miles are the placement's `mileage_used` minus the included miles, or zero when that difference is negative — an overage of exactly zero produces a charge of exactly zero. The charge is the overage miles multiplied by the overage rate of 12 cents per mile, and it is written to the return's `mileage_overage_cents` and to the placement's `mileage_charge_cents`. This is the `monthly_prorated` basis, in which the allowance is re-granted for every thirty-day block.

One variant overrides the block treatment. When the governing contract is a `NET` lease whose term is shorter than the placement itself, the allowance is granted once for the whole term rather than per block: the included miles become the placement's `mileage_allowance` alone. The resulting charge is therefore strictly greater than the prorated basis would produce whenever the placement ran longer than thirty days and the miles used exceeded the allowance, which is exactly the case this variant exists to cover.

Maintenance burden is allocated by condition grade and liability bearer. The parts cost for the return is read from the condition grade: grade `A` maps to 0 cents, grade `B` to 200,000 cents, grade `C` to 400,000 cents and grade `D` to 600,000 cents, and that figure is written to the return's `maintenance_parts_cents`. The split of that cost between lessor and lessee is then decided by the contract's `liability_bearer`, with the cap applied inside the share computation so the evaluation order is pinned. When the bearer is `LESSOR`, the lessor absorbs the entire parts cost and the lessee's share is zero. When the bearer is `LESSEE`, the lessor's share is the parts cost multiplied by the smaller of the lessee parts share of 1 percent and its ceiling of 5 percent, divided by one hundred and floored to a whole cent, and the lessee absorbs the remainder — the total minus the lessor's share. The placement's `maintenance_cost_cents` stores the lessor's share, so a `LESSOR`-liable return attributes the full cost to the placement and a `LESSEE`-liable return attributes only the capped sliver. The two bases differ for every non-zero parts cost.

Settlement moves the charge into the ledger. A `PortfolioManager` settles a return, which is legal only while its `settlement_status` is `PENDING`; the settlement sets the status to `SETTLED` and recomputes the lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` by the reconciliation rule of §3.9, and it increments the return's `version`. Until a return is settled, its mileage overage does not count toward the lessee's balance, because the reconciliation aggregates only settled returns.

The repair queue is closed by a separate action once the money side is finished. A `ProductionPlanner` may release a car from `UNDER_REPAIR` back to `HELD` only when every return of that car has `settlement_status` equal to `SETTLED`; a car with any outstanding return is rejected with `ERR_UNSETTLED_RETURNS` (HTTP 409). The release sets the car to `HELD`, clears its `lease_contract_id`, re-derives its `retrofit_required` flag, and increments the car's `version` and `updated_at`. A car that is not in `UNDER_REPAIR` cannot take this action at all and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.8. Retrofit Mandates, Shop Work and the Scrap-versus-Retrofit Decision

A regulatory mandate is opened as a program. A `RegulatoryRetrofitist` creates it with a unique `mandate_code`, a `description`, the `compliance_deadline_date` by which every enrolled car must be retrofitted, the `required_shop_days_per_car` the work consumes, and an optional `required_component_id` naming the coating or casting kit consumed per retrofit car — null when the mandate consumes no component. A duplicate mandate code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROW` (HTTP 409). The program starts in `PLANNED` with a `program_cost_cents` of 0. Coverage is then declared by adding `RetrofitProgramCarType` rows, each naming the `retrofit_program_id` and the `car_type_id` it covers; a program covers nothing until at least one such row exists. A coverage row may be added while the program stands in any status, and a second row for a program-and-car-type pair already covered is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROW` (HTTP 409).

Starting the program enrolls the fleet. The start action is legal only from `PLANNED`, and it requires the program to have at least one covered car type — a program with no coverage is rejected with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409), because the upstream aggregate it depends on is missing. The compliance deadline is checked on this write as well: a start that cannot be satisfied before the mandate's `compliance_deadline_date` is rejected with `ERR_RETROFIT_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). On success the program becomes `IN_PROGRESS`, the `retrofit_required` flag is set to true on every fleet car of a covered car type whose status is `HELD` or `UNDER_REPAIR`, and the program's `program_cost_cents` is re-derived by the cost rule below. The program's `version` and the `version` of every enrolled car move with the write. A program that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` accepts no further transition and is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).

Program cost is an aggregation over completed shop work, and it is derived on two bases. On the scheduled estimate used while the program is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, every work order of the program that is not in `CANCELLED` status contributes. On the completed actual used once the program is `COMPLETED`, only work orders in `COMPLETED` status contribute — so a work order cancelled in the middle of a program reduces the program cost rather than leaving an estimate behind. Each contributing work order adds its `shop_days` multiplied by the shop-day rate of 185,000 cents, plus its `component_qty_consumed` multiplied by the unit cost of the program's required component when a `required_component_id` is set. A program with no required component contributes only the shop-day term.

Shop work is scheduled per car. A `ProductionPlanner` or a `RegulatoryRetrofitist` raises a retrofit work order naming the `retrofit_program_id`, the `lease_fleet_car_id`, the `shop_line` the work occupies, the `scheduled_start_date` and `scheduled_end_date` of the window, and the `component_qty_consumed`. The car must be in `HELD` status, or the write is rejected with `ERR_CAR_NOT_HELD` (HTTP 409), and the car's car type must be covered by the program through a `RetrofitProgramCarType` row, or the write is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). There is no separate test of the car's existing retrofit obligations on this write: the `HELD` precondition is what keeps a car already in shop work — a car the scheduling write itself leaves `UNDER_REPAIR` — from taking a second work order. The window must end on or before the program's `compliance_deadline_date` — otherwise `ERR_RETROFIT_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422) — must not collide with another work order on the same `shop_line`, or `ERR_SHOP_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) applies, and the car must not be past its regulatory life — that test comparing the car's `regulatory_life_end_date` against the current clock date, so a car whose life ends today remains schedulable — or `ERR_REGULATORY_LIFE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) applies. The derived `shop_days` — the difference between the scheduled end and start — must equal the program's `required_shop_days_per_car`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

A successful scheduling write moves the car to `UNDER_REPAIR`, sets its `retrofit_required` flag to true, and increments the car's `version`; the work order itself starts in `SCHEDULED`. Starting the work is legal only from `SCHEDULED`, requires the half-open window not to overlap any other work order's window on the same `shop_line` — a collision being rejected with `ERR_SHOP_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) — and requires the parent car to be `UNDER_REPAIR`, a car that is not being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the work order becomes `IN_PROGRESS`, and — when the program names a `required_component_id` — the component's `on_hand_qty` is decremented by the work order's `component_qty_consumed` with no sufficiency test standing in front of it, and its `reserved_qty` and `shortfall_qty` are re-derived by the allocation rule of §3.2. The work order's `version` moves.

Completing the work returns the car to service with a fresh regulatory clock. Completion is legal from `IN_PROGRESS` and requires the work order's shop window not to be past the program's `compliance_deadline_date`. On success the work order becomes `COMPLETED`, the parent car returns to `HELD`, its `built_date` is set to the completion date — the write takes no completion date of its own, so this is the logical clock's current date — and its `regulatory_life_end_date` is re-derived from that new build date, and the program's `program_cost_cents` is re-derived by the cost rule above. The car's status is not re-tested on this write; only its existence is. The work order's and the car's versions both move. A scheduled work order that has not started may instead be cancelled, which sets it to `CANCELLED`, increments its `version`, and re-derives the program cost; the car it named is left `UNDER_REPAIR` with its `retrofit_required` flag standing. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are the work order's terminal states.

Program closure and voiding both release the fleet's obligation. The program completes only from `IN_PROGRESS`, and only when every covered car type has at least one work order in `COMPLETED` status — the work order counting toward a type being one whose car is of that car type — and every work order of the program is in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` status; a program that fails either test is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the program becomes `COMPLETED`, and `retrofit_required` is set to false on every fleet car of a covered car type. Voiding is the abandonment path: it is legal from `IN_PROGRESS` and from nowhere else, so a void attempted on a completed or already-voided program is a state-legality rejection with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than a terminality one; it accepts an optional `reason`, sets the program to `VOIDED`, sets every work order of the program that is not already `CANCELLED` to `CANCELLED`, and sets `retrofit_required` to false on every enrolled car. Both closures increment the program's `version`.

The scrap-versus-retrofit decision is what the mandate economics come down to. For a car and the program covering its car type, the first test is regulatory life: when the current clock date is strictly later than the car's `regulatory_life_end_date`, the decision is `SCRAP` regardless of retrofit economics — a car whose life end date equals the current clock date is still inside its life and is not caught by this test. Otherwise the retrofit allowance is 2,500,000 cents, and the retrofit cost is the program's `required_shop_days_per_car` multiplied by the shop-day rate of 185,000 cents, plus, when the program has a required component, that component's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by the work order's `component_qty_consumed`. When the retrofit cost exceeds the allowance, the decision is `SCRAP` on cost grounds even though the car could still legally run — this is the economic write-off case. When the cost is within the allowance and the car's `retrofit_required` flag is true, the decision is `RETROFIT`; otherwise the decision is `RETAIN`. No endpoint returns this decision — it is an internal computation over the mandate economics rather than a client-facing read.

### 3.9. Secondary-Market Sales, Scrapping and the Portfolio Ledger

A fleet car leaves the fleet either by sale or by scrap, and the two paths have different economics. A `PortfolioManager` sells a car by naming the `lease_fleet_car_id`, an optional `lease_contract_id` for the contract that had to be terminated first, the `buyer_name`, the `sale_price_cents`, an optional `net_book_value_cents`, the `writeback_liability_cents`, and the `sale_date`; a supplied `net_book_value_cents` is validated for shape but not used, the stored figure always being the derived book value. The car must be in `HELD` status — otherwise `ERR_CAR_NOT_HELD` (HTTP 409) applies — and the car's `regulatory_life_end_date` must be on or after the sale date, consistent with the inclusive boundary of §2.8, or the sale is rejected with `ERR_REGULATORY_LIFE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The contract recorded on the sale is the body's `lease_contract_id` when it is supplied and otherwise the car's own `lease_contract_id`, and a `lessee_id` may be supplied as well; where both a named lessee and a resolved contract exist they must agree, or the write is rejected with `ERR_LESSEE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409), and where no contract resolves the correspondence test is skipped. On success the car moves to `SOLD`, its `lease_contract_id` is cleared, and the sale's `net_book_value_cents`, `writeback_liability_cents` and `gain_loss_cents` are computed by the rule below. The car's `version` and `updated_at` move. The sale itself starts in `PENDING_SETTLEMENT`.

The realized gain or loss is a chronological computation against a daily depreciation. The car's age in days is the sale date minus the car's `built_date`. The book value is the car's `acquisition_cost_cents` minus that age multiplied by the daily depreciation of 2,500 cents. The sale's `gain_loss_cents` is the `sale_price_cents` minus that book value minus the sale's `writeback_liability_cents`, and the associated balance delta is the gain or loss minus the writeback liability again. No rounding step applies, because every operand is already a whole number of cents. On a full-service sale the `writeback_liability_cents` is zero, so the gain or loss is simply the sale price minus the book value. On a net-lease sale the accrued lessee liability is written back and subtracted, so the gain or loss is strictly lower by exactly the writeback liability — the two bases diverge by precisely that amount on the same car at the same price.

Settlement is what moves the ledger. It is legal only while the sale is `PENDING_SETTLEMENT`, and it requires the contract recorded on the sale to be `TERMINATED` or null, so a car still under a live contract cannot be settled out from under it — a sale that records no contract at all passes this test — and a contract that is still live is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A sale that has already reached `SETTLED` or `VOIDED` accepts no further transition and is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The settlement must also be internally balanced: a sale whose `writeback_liability_cents` exceeds its gain over book value cannot settle and is rejected with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_UNBALANCED` (HTTP 409), the test being the sign of the sale's stored `gain_loss_cents`, which is negative in exactly that case. On success the sale becomes `SETTLED`, the portfolio ledger's `realized_gain_cents` is adjusted by the sale's `gain_loss_cents`, and — when the terminated contract's `liability_bearer` was `LESSEE` — the `outstanding_balance_cents` of the lessee recorded on the sale, whether that lessee was named on the request or inherited from the terminated contract, is credited by the `writeback_liability_cents`. The ledger's `fleet_nbv_cents` is re-derived by the reconciliation rule below, and the sale's and the ledger's versions both move.

Voiding is the compensating action and it must be exact. It is legal only from `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` and accepts an optional `reason`. On success the sale becomes `VOIDED` and the car is restored to `HELD` — with no `lease_contract_id` put back, that link having been cleared when the sale was written — and the portfolio ledger's `realized_gain_cents` is left exactly as it stood before this sale was created — the void is an erasure, not a loss, and it must leave the ledger where it would have been had the sale never been written.

Fleet capital is reconciled on two bases that are expected to diverge. The disposal proceeds term is the sum of `sale_price_cents` over every sale in `SETTLED` status, floored at zero; sales that are `VOIDED` or still `PENDING_SETTLEMENT` contribute nothing. The committed basis is the ledger's `committed_capital_cents` minus those disposal proceeds — the historic cost basis, which charges the full proceeds against committed capital and applies no depreciation. The newest basis is the live fleet's depreciated book value: for every car in `HELD`, `ASSIGNED` or `UNDER_REPAIR`, the car's `acquisition_cost_cents` minus the number of days from its `built_date` to the current clock date multiplied by the daily depreciation of 2,500 cents, floored at zero per car so a fully depreciated car contributes nothing, summed across the fleet and rounded up to a whole cent. The drift is the committed basis minus the newest basis. The reconciliation read reports both bases and the drift together, as `oldest_basis_cents`, `newest_basis_cents` and `drift_cents`, alongside the ledger's `committed_capital_cents` and `realized_gain_cents`; the two bases diverge by exactly the drift once a car is sold.

The lessee ledger is reconciled from the same family of events. A lessee's `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of the `mileage_overage_cents` of its returns whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`, plus the sum of `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity` over its production orders that are `COMPLETED` with disposition `SOLD`, plus the sum of `writeback_liability_cents` over its sales that are `SETTLED`, minus the sum of `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the car count over its orders with disposition `RETAINED` (an order counts for the lessee named on it, or, when it names none, for the lessee of the contract it was released against; the count is the number of fleet cars the order has produced, whether or not those cars are still live). The whole figure is then clamped at zero. Only settled returns and settled sales accrue, and a retained order credits the balance back, so retaining cars instead of selling them lowers the lessee's balance. The outstanding-balance read exposes the components as well: it reports `outstanding_balance_cents`, `settled_charges_cents` and `credits_applied_cents`, the two component figures being the raw sums before the zero clamp is applied to the balance, and a lessee may read it only for its own `id`.

Scrapping is the other exit and it reverses capital rather than realizing it. A `PortfolioManager` scraps a car from `HELD` status, supplying an optional `scrap_reason`; a car that is not in `HELD` status is rejected with `ERR_CAR_NOT_HELD` (HTTP 409) rather than the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The scrap sets the car to `SCRAPPED`, re-derives its former contract's `capital_allocation_cents` by the allocation rule of §3.4 — which now excludes this car, so the contract's capital figure shrinks; the contract re-derived is the one the car's producing order was released against, falling back to the car's own `lease_contract_id` only when the order names none — and clears the car's retrofit obligations: every retrofit work order of this car that is still `SCHEDULED` is deleted, and every other work order of this car is set to `CANCELLED`. The car's `version` and `updated_at` move. `SOLD` and `SCRAPPED` are the car's terminal states; a car that reaches either accepts no further transitions, with the single exception of the void path, which returns a sold car to `HELD` when its sale is still pending settlement.

The portfolio ledger itself is a singleton that is always `OPEN` and has no terminal state. Two events write to it. Settling a sale increments its `realized_gain_cents` by the sale's `gain_loss_cents` and re-derives `fleet_nbv_cents`. Completing a production order whose disposition is `RETAINED` increases its `committed_capital_cents` by the order's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the number of cars completed, and re-derives `fleet_nbv_cents` by the same rule. Both writes increment the ledger's `version` and refresh its `updated_at`; a bare refresh of `fleet_nbv_cents` that is neither of those — the refresh a sale creation or a sale void performs — rewrites the stored figure without advancing the ledger's `version`. `fleet_nbv_cents` is a time-dependent derived figure and is re-derived from the current live fleet and the current clock on every read that reports it, so a clock move alone changes the reported net book value without any write touching the ledger. The ledger's own `OPEN → OPEN` transitions are internal bookkeeping rather than client-driven actions, and the reconciliation read is available to every role.
